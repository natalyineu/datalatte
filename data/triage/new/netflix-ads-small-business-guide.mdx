A local business cannot realistically buy Netflix ads through a direct deal. Reported minimums run from $10,000 to $25,000+ ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/netflix-advertising-small-business), secondary), and another vendor cites $15,000 per month. A July 2026 report says Netflix inventory opened to buyers on The Trade Desk with no minimum spend, but that route needs a demand-side platform account. Small advertisers usually buy streaming bundles through self-serve platforms, where Netflix may be part of the delivery mix and the advertiser cannot pick a show.

## Can a local business buy Netflix ads directly?

Direct sales run through Netflix's sales team or agency partners. One vendor guide puts the typical minimum at $10,000 or more, with a stated range of $10,000 to $25,000+ and CPMs of $40 to $60 ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/netflix-advertising-small-business), secondary). Another vendor says Netflix has reportedly lowered its entry point to a minimum of $15,000 per month ([Awarity](https://awarity.com/blog/what-to-know-before-you-advertise-on-netflix-in-2025), secondary).

The two figures do not match, and neither is a published Netflix rate card. One is a range with no billing period. The other is monthly and hedged with "reportedly." Both come from companies that sell competing streaming ads, so they work as ballparks only. Ask a sales rep for current terms before relying on either.

For scale, take an assumed $600 monthly marketing budget. A $15,000 minimum equals 25 months of that spend. Adwave's own guide says direct Netflix advertising is "simply not an option" for a local business with a $500 monthly budget.

## Did Netflix drop its minimums through The Trade Desk?

One outlet, PPC Land, reported on July 20, 2026 that Netflix inventory became available to every buyer using The Trade Desk's Sellers and Publishers 500+ marketplace. The Trade Desk is quoted as saying there is "no minimum spend or change to your workflow required" ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/netflix-opens-250m-user-inventory-to-trade-desk-buyers-drops-spend-minimums/), secondary). That is a single-source report relaying a Trade Desk platform page. The article quotes no Netflix statement on the change.

Before the change, buyers had two routes: programmatic guaranteed deals and 1:1 private marketplace arrangements. Both typically needed scale commitments that smaller advertisers could not meet. The report says Netflix kept those private options and added a third, lower-friction path.

The change removes a Netflix-side floor, not a platform-side one. The sources do not document what a demand-side platform charges, whether it accepts very small accounts, or whether CPMs moved after July 2026. The CPM figures in this article come from a January 2026 vendor page. Ask for current rates rather than assuming they have shifted in either direction.

## What are the routes to Netflix ads, and what do they cost?

The reported minimums differ by route. Where two sources give different numbers, both appear.

| Route | Reported minimum | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|
| Direct deal with Netflix sales or an agency | $10,000 to $25,000+ (Adwave, secondary); $15,000 per month (Awarity, secondary) | National brands and large regional chains |
| Programmatic guaranteed or 1:1 private marketplace | No figure given; described as needing scale commitments (PPC Land, secondary) | Agencies and large advertisers with account relationships |
| Always-on marketplace in The Trade Desk (Sellers and Publishers 500+) | No minimum spend, reported by one outlet relaying The Trade Desk | Buyers who already hold a Trade Desk account |
| Amazon DSP, where Netflix has been listed since Q4 2025 | No stated minimum for self-service; agencies recommend about $10,000 monthly; managed service starts at $50K ([Canopy](https://canopymanagement.com/amazon-streaming-tv-mid-market/), secondary) | Mid-market brands with an agency or in-house buyer |
| Self-serve streaming bundle on a CTV platform | $50 to start (Adwave, secondary); another Adwave page says $30 a day | Local businesses testing streaming TV |

The Amazon row combines two sources: PPC Land reports Netflix in Amazon DSP, and Canopy gives the DSP budget guidance. Canopy's figures describe Amazon DSP in general, not Netflix specifically.

Adwave pages give two starting figures, $50 and $30 a day. Both are vendor claims, so confirm the current minimum on the platform itself.

## Is Netflix's audience big enough to matter?

Nielsen's The Gauge put Netflix at 7.8% of all US TV viewing in April 2026, second among individual streaming services behind YouTube at 13.4%. Total streaming reached 47.6% ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/netflix-tv-viewing-share-q2-2026), secondary, citing Nielsen). Netflix ran closer to 9% in January 2026 on the Stranger Things finale, so its share moves with its release calendar.

Netflix's May 2026 upfront put its ad-supported reach at more than 250 million monthly active viewers, up from 190 million in late 2025. More than 60% of new sign-ups in ad-plan countries choose that plan (same Adwave source).

Older vendor pages show lower numbers. Awarity cites 70 million monthly active users on a page first dated November 2024, and Adwave's guide cites over 40 million "as of 2024." These are earlier snapshots, not competing counts, so they should not be blended.

One caveat applies: Nielsen delayed its March report over a methodology dispute, and a recalibrated version is expected in the fall. The same source says that could trim streaming shares on paper without any real change in viewing.

## What can a small advertiser control on Netflix?

Less than on search or social. Adwave lists geographic, demographic, genre and daypart targeting. It states that advertisers cannot target individual titles or use detailed interest targeting ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/netflix-advertising-small-business), secondary). The same guide describes ad load as roughly 4 to 5 minutes per hour, which is a vendor estimate.

For a local business, geographic targeting carries most of the value. A salon can limit delivery to its metro area instead of paying for viewers who will never book a chair.

Through a programmatic bundle, delivery is spread across services. Adwave names Hulu, Peacock and Tubi alongside Netflix, with no exclusivity and no control over when or where the ad appears. A request to see a placement report by service is a fair question for any platform. Whether Netflix actually receives a share of a given small campaign is not documented in these sources.

## What do small advertisers do instead?

Most buy a streaming bundle and let the platform spread impressions. Adwave quotes $15 to $25 CPMs on free ad-supported services such as Tubi and Pluto TV, against $35 to $50 for programmatic Netflix access. The page's text says AVOD reaches three to four times as many viewers per dollar. The page's own ranges imply roughly 1.4 to 3.3 times as many (35 divided by 25, and 50 divided by 15). The two statements disagree, so treat the ratio as unsettled.

A 70/30 split between AVOD and Netflix is Adwave's suggestion for a $1,000 monthly budget. For broader price context, see [real CTV CPM data](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data) and the [platform comparison](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026). Amazon's ad-supported Prime Video, reported at 175+ million monthly viewers by Awarity, is another premium option, covered in the [Prime Video guide](/blog/amazon-prime-video-ads-small-business).

Creative also affects cost. Canopy notes that video quality, not media spend, is often the real floor for mid-market brands. Amazon's spec calls for MP4, 6 to 45 seconds and a 15 Mbps minimum bitrate. The [creative specs guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-creative-specs-guide-2026) covers what to prepare.

## What does a $600 salon test look like?

This worked example uses assumptions throughout. A salon wants to test streaming TV for one four-week window. Assumption inputs: budget $600; one metro area; four impressions per household; each new client worth $300 in first-year revenue. The CPMs are picked from the ranges quoted above: $40 within Netflix's $35 to $50, and $20 within AVOD's $15 to $25.

| Scenario (assumption) | Spend and CPM | Impressions | Households at 4 each |
|---|---|---|---|
| All Netflix-priced inventory | $600 at $40 | 15,000 | 3,750 |
| All AVOD-priced inventory | $600 at $20 | 30,000 | 7,500 |
| 70/30 split | $420 at $20 plus $180 at $40 | 21,000 plus 4,500 = 25,500 | 6,375 |

Impressions equal spend divided by CPM, times 1,000. The $40 case matches Adwave's own math: $200 to $400 buys about 5,000 to 10,000 impressions at that price.

Break-even is simple: $600 divided by $300 is 2 new clients. The 70/30 row only applies if the platform lets the advertiser set splits by service. Many bundles do not, so the real mix may differ. This example shows delivery, not results, and no source documents conversion rates for salon streaming campaigns.

## What is the two-week checklist to a first test?

The plan below fits a business with no streaming ads running yet.

### Week 1: prepare

- Day 1: set one goal, such as new-client bookings from a defined ZIP code radius, and a test budget the business can afford to lose.
- Day 2: confirm the offer and a single landing page or phone number to track.
- Days 3 and 4: prepare a 15 or 30 second video that meets the [creative specs](/blog/ctv-advertising-creative-specs-guide-2026) of the platform chosen.
- Day 5: install conversion tracking following the [GA4 guide](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business).
- Days 6 and 7: shortlist two platforms using the [platform comparison](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026). Ask each for current CPMs, the minimum, whether Netflix is in the delivery mix, and whether a placement report is available.

### Week 2: launch and read

- Day 8: choose the platform and set geographic targeting to the service area.
- Day 9: launch at the minimum daily budget so the full budget lasts the whole test.
- Days 10 to 12: check delivery and spend without changing anything; early swings are noise.
- Day 13: pull the placement report and compare actual CPM with what the platform quoted.
- Day 14: record bookings, calls and site visits against spend, then decide whether to continue, adjust or stop. The [small-budget CTV guide](/blog/is-ctv-advertising-worth-it-small-budget-2026) helps frame that decision.

## FAQ

### Can an ad run only on Netflix?

Not through a typical self-serve bundle. Adwave describes delivery across several services with no Netflix exclusivity, and no targeting of individual shows. Exclusive Netflix placement points to the direct route and its reported minimums.

### Is the no-minimum Trade Desk access confirmed?

It is reported by one outlet, PPC Land, citing a Trade Desk platform page dated July 20, 2026. Confirm with the platform or agency before budgeting around it. The sources also do not say what a demand-side platform itself charges.

### Will Netflix ads get cheaper?

No source documents future Netflix pricing. Awarity reports a reportedly lowered entry point, but that is not a forecast. Ask any platform for current CPMs on the day of purchase.

### How much does a first test need?

Adwave cites $50 as a platform start and $200 to $400 as a test phase, buying roughly 5,000 to 10,000 impressions at a $40 CPM. These are vendor figures. Platform minimums vary, so check before committing.
