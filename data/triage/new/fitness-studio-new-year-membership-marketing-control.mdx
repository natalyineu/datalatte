New year gym membership marketing works best when a studio sells early, holds its price in January, and spends the following weeks keeping the people who joined. The sources behind this article contain no national 2026 sign-up totals, so the plan leans on older industry figures and local reports, each flagged for age and origin. The sequence: presale in December, a commitment-based January offer, class-first onboarding with referrals, and weekly tracking through Feb 14.

## How much of the year's sign-ups really land in January?

The most cited figure comes from IHRSA's 2018 report, relayed in a 2019 [Glofox article](https://www.glofox.com/blog/6-new-years-resolution-gym-statistics-you-need-to-know/) (secondary): 12% of all sign-ups happen in January, more than any other month. An even spread would be about 8.3% per month (100 divided by 12), so January is elevated, not dominant.

Every number in this space has an age or an origin problem. The table sorts them.

| Figure | Where it comes from | Type | How far to trust it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12% of sign-ups in January | IHRSA 2018, via Glofox, 2019 | Industry report retold by a software vendor | Directional only; old and secondhand |
| 79% more new customers than an average month at Planet Fitness; 53% more at Pure Barre | [Second Measure](https://secondmeasure.com/datapoints/at-some-gyms-the-new-years-set-keep-memberships-longest/), January 2017 data | Transaction panel, vendor blog (secondary) | Old; the publisher says its panel changed in 2022 and advises against historic comparisons |
| 44% of adults likely to make a resolution; 13% of those chose exercise | NPR/Marist poll 2018, via Glofox | Poll, retold | Old |
| About 60% of Americans say their resolution is fitness-focused | [ABC News 4](https://abcnews4.com/news/local/story/january-gym-surge-brings-new-faces-and-a-push-for-consistency), Jan 2026, citing a health-and-fitness group | Local TV retelling | Wording and base unknown |

The 13% and 60% figures conflict. The bases differ (resolution-makers versus all Americans), as do the years and pollsters, so they cannot be reconciled. KGUN adds that a YouGov survey ranks exercising more as the top 2026 resolution, but gives no percentage.

## Is the January rush still happening in 2026?

Local January 2026 reports point in different directions. A Planet Fitness employee in Pittsfield told a [local radio site](https://wupe.com/ixp/920/p/new-year-fitness-pittsfield/) that sign-ups ran at about a quarter of the prior year, adding that most sign-ups now happen online and are hard to gauge until month-end reports. That is one person at one location.

Other outlets reported the opposite. [ABC News 4](https://abcnews4.com/news/local/story/january-gym-surge-brings-new-faces-and-a-push-for-consistency) in Charleston described gyms filling with new members, and a [KGUN](https://www.kgun9.com/news/community-inspired-journalism/eastside-news/starting-the-new-year-with-a-new-gym-membership-heres-how-to-stay-on-track-with-those-goals-until-december) report from Tucson quoted a gym owner calling January the biggest time of year. Headlines from Kelowna, Canada, and Milwaukee also reported a surge, though no counts were available.

None of these is a measurement. The reliable benchmark is the studio's own sign-up dates from last January, by week. The Tucson owner adds a boutique-relevant point: a crowded space is uncomfortable for existing members, so presale and class capacity limits matter as much as lead volume.

## Do January joiners quit faster than other members?

The claim that 80% of January joiners quit within five months appears in the Glofox article, but it is attributed to a third-party statistics site, so the original research is unknown. Treat it as folklore until a primary source appears.

The only transaction data in the sources points the other way. Second Measure found January joiners at some gyms were as likely as other joiners to keep memberships, if not more so. At Planet Fitness, 66% of the January 2017 cohort was still active at ten months, against 59% for the preceding 11 cohorts.

Two cautions apply. That gym charges $10 a month and, per the article, required in-person or letter cancellation, so "active" may mean "still paying". And a My Protein survey of several hundred people, retold by KGUN, found 34% had stopped using memberships by December.

Boutique data is weaker: over 80% of boutique customers in Second Measure's panel did not spend in the last quarter of year one, while ClassPass averaged 39% quarterly retention against 16% for single boutique brands. Billing retention and attendance are different things, so a studio should track both.

## Which January offers protect margin?

Glofox advises against defaulting to free trials, which can encourage trial hopping and devalue the brand, and recommends incentives that give value but require commitment. It also says that, capacity permitting, no discount at all can be reasonable in January, with price competition reserved for slower months. No source tests these tactics, so the table below is a set of planning suggestions, not proven results.

| Studio type | Offer structure to consider | Margin logic | Watch-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boutique class studio | December founding-member presale: membership with a fixed first-month start date plus a non-price perk such as guest passes | Perk costs less than a percentage cut across every renewal | Cap spots to protect class capacity |
| Yoga | Beginner series that converts to a membership or class pack after the last session | Commitment up front; the series is the onboarding | Single-class buyers may never convert, so collect contact details at the first visit |
| Personal training | Assessment-first start with limited trainer slots, then a package | Trainer hours are the fixed constraint, so scarcity is real | Do not discount below the hourly rate that covers the trainer |

Paid reach can support the presale. The [CTV ads for fitness studios](/blog/ctv-ads-for-fitness-studios) guide covers that channel. Whether December or January ad rates differ from other months is not documented in the sources, so ask platforms for current quotes before budgeting.

## What is the week-by-week plan from Dec 7 to Feb 14?

Dates assume Monday starts in the 2026-27 season. The [12-month local marketing calendar](/blog/local-business-marketing-calendar-week-by-week-12-months) shows where this block fits in the full year.

- **Dec 7:** Pull last January's sign-ups by week from the booking system. Update holiday hours and offers on the [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).
- **Dec 14:** Open the founding-member presale to the existing email and text lists. The [SMS platform comparison](/blog/sms-marketing-platforms-small-business-compared) helps choose a tool.
- **Dec 21:** Send a second presale message to the waitlist and past trial attendees. Keep it light around the holidays.
- **Dec 28:** Close the presale. Confirm class capacity, staffing and the booking system's load before January 4.
- **Jan 4:** Launch the public offer with the commitment structure. Every new member gets a booked first class before leaving the page or desk.
- **Jan 11:** Check week-one attendance. Message anyone who has not yet attended.
- **Jan 18:** Send the referral invitation to members who have attended at least three classes.
- **Jan 25:** Staff check-ins with every January joiner (a second contact this month).
- **Feb 1:** Count members active in the last 30 days. Compare with last year's own cohort.
- **Feb 8 to 14:** Run a group-class challenge for new members, review results, and plan the next offer from real numbers.

## How do referrals and group classes keep January joiners?

The sources support the class-first approach more than any other tactic. Glofox, citing IHRSA's 2018 report, says class attendees are 56% less likely to cancel than members who train alone on equipment. It also cites a Penn State group-exercise study in which 98.8% completed a 30-week program, against about 40% dropout in solo studies. Both are secondhand and for gyms generally, not boutique studios specifically.

Onboarding contact has its own evidence, again via Glofox. Dr. Paul Bedford's research found as few as four sessions in the first weeks could lift six-month retention by as much as 17%. Separately, two staff-member interactions per month were associated with a 33% lower cancellation risk, according to IHRSA's 2018 report.

Trainers quoted by ABC News 4 recommend small, attainable goals over dramatic ones, which fits a "first four classes" onboarding path.

No source offers referral performance data, so referral steps are untested. A reasonable test: invite members after their third class, and reward the referrer only once the friend attends, not at sign-up.

## What does a January offer look like in a worked example?

This example is for a boutique class studio. Every input is an assumption, not a benchmark.

Assumptions:
- Membership price: $150 per month
- New January members: 60
- Ad and promotion spend: $1,500
- Option A: 20% off for the first 3 months
- Option B: no price cut, plus a welcome perk costing the studio $20 per member
- Retention at month 6: 40% in a weak case, 55% in a stronger case

Option A gives away $30 per member per month. That is $30 × 60 × 3 = $5,400 in discounts. Option B costs $20 × 60 = $1,200. The perk route keeps $4,200 more over the first three months, before counting any difference in sign-ups, which neither option's inputs predict.

Acquisition spend works out to $1,500 ÷ 60 = $25 per new member.

Retention changes more than the offer does. At 40%, 24 of the 60 remain, or $3,600 a month. At 55%, 33 remain, or $4,950 a month. Nine extra members are worth $1,350 monthly, which is why onboarding and class habits deserve as much attention as the sign-up page.

## What should a studio track through February?

Tracking needs few numbers, defined the same way every week.

- Leads and sign-ups by source (presale list, Google Business Profile, ads, referrals), using a unique code or landing page per channel.
- Share of new members who attend a first class within seven days.
- Classes attended in each member's first four weeks.
- Members active in the last 30 days at Feb 1 and Feb 14, as a share of the January cohort.
- Discount and perk cost per member, from the table above.
- Referrals sent, referrals who attended, and referrals who joined.

Boutique billing can be irregular, as Second Measure notes, because class packs and drop-ins do not renew monthly. A single definition of "active" avoids misreading billing gaps as churn.

## FAQ

### Should a studio discount memberships in January?

Glofox suggests that, capacity permitting, no discount can be reasonable in January, and advises commitment-based incentives over free trials. That is vendor advice, not test data. Run the worked-example math above with the studio's own price and margin before choosing.

### Are January joiners less loyal?

The sources conflict. A widely repeated 80% five-month quit figure has no traceable primary source. Second Measure's 2017 Planet Fitness data showed January joiners retained as well or better, but at a low-cost gym with difficult cancellation. A 2022 brand survey found 34% had stopped using memberships by December.

### When should the presale start?

No source tests an ideal date. The plan above starts Dec 7 so a list is warmed before January 4, and closes Dec 28 so staff and capacity are ready. Adjust it using last January's sign-up dates.

### Are there national 2026 sign-up numbers?

Not in the sources used here. Only local reports and older industry figures exist, and they disagree. A studio's own booking and billing data is the more reliable guide.
