FAST (free ad-supported streaming TV) is scheduled, TV-style programming delivered over the internet at no cost to viewers and paid for entirely by ads. Tubi, Pluto TV and The Roku Channel are the three largest US platforms, and [EMARKETER forecasts 131.4 million US FAST users in 2026](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-fast--how-free-streaming-tv-reshaping-ad-market-2026). For a local business, FAST is a TV entry point where ads are typically bought programmatically, not through a dedicated small-business desk.

## What is FAST, and how does it differ from other streaming ads?

FAST channels work like cable: viewers tune into a genre or themed channel and watch whatever is airing. No account or payment is required. [EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-fast--how-free-streaming-tv-reshaping-ad-market-2026) lists The Roku Channel, Tubi and Pluto TV as the major platforms, and notes that FAST's linear format supports traditional mid-roll ad breaks.

AVOD (ad-supported video on demand) is different: viewers pick a specific title from a library. Many platforms blend both. Tubi and Pluto TV run linear FAST channels next to on-demand libraries. Ad tiers on Netflix, Hulu and Amazon Prime Video count as AVOD, not FAST.

Scale of choice is large. Gracenote counted nearly 1,870 FAST channels globally in mid-2025, per EMARKETER, and [NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/07/24/fast-viewing-surged-55-last-year-but-discovery-and-metadata-havent-kept-up/) (secondary) later cited more than 2,100. The Roku Channel alone offers 550+ live linear channels and more than 80,000 free movies and shows, according to [Roku Advertising](https://advertising.roku.com/learn/resources/everything-advertisers-want-to-know-about-fast-tv-answered) (secondary, vendor).

Audience figures in the sources do not line up, because each uses a different base. Treat them as a range, not a single answer.

- EMARKETER: 131.4 million US FAST users in 2026, which is 54% of CTV users.
- Parks Associates, cited by Roku (secondary): 46% of US internet households regularly use FAST.
- Hub Entertainment Research, via [NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/07/24/fast-viewing-surged-55-last-year-but-discovery-and-metadata-havent-kept-up/) (secondary): 55% of viewers have used at least one FAST service.
- Roku (secondary): 64% of US Roku households stream FAST.

Platform counts also differ. EMARKETER forecasts 97.3 million US viewers for The Roku Channel, 92.5 million for Tubi and 68.6 million for Pluto TV. [Strategus](https://www.strategus.com/blog/free-ad-supported-tv-fast-advertising) (secondary, vendor) says Tubi has 80 million monthly users without a date or method. The sources do not reconcile these.

Growth numbers vary too. Wurl, cited by EMARKETER, reports monthly active FAST households up 12% year over year. Comscore, cited by Roku, shows FAST hours up 43%. Amagi measured global FAST viewing hours up 55%. Different measures, same direction: up.

## Who actually watches FAST?

The old assumption was that FAST audiences are cost-conscious cord cutters who skip paid streaming. Hub's 2026 research, reported by [NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/07/24/fast-viewing-surged-55-last-year-but-discovery-and-metadata-havent-kept-up/) (secondary), points the other way. FAST users reported about 24 hours of TV a week versus 22 for non-FAST viewers. They estimated spending about $75 a month on TV services versus $84. Sixty percent use FAST alongside paid subscriptions, not instead of them.

Age skews broad. Nielsen data cited by EMARKETER shows adults 18 to 49 spend 63.8% of TV time with ad-supported content, and FAST captured growing attention from viewers 35 to 64. Deloitte, cited by Roku (secondary), found more than two-thirds of younger viewers stream at least one FAST channel.

Geography matters for local buyers. Roku (secondary) reports free TV is most prevalent in the South, at 60% of total TV time. Nielsen found 28% of Gen Xers frequently buy products they encounter through streaming TV ads (cited by EMARKETER).

## How are FAST ads bought, and can a small business get in?

Buying is typically programmatic. [Strategus](https://www.strategus.com/blog/free-ad-supported-tv-fast-advertising) (secondary, vendor) describes FAST ads sold through auctions involving supply-side platforms, demand-side platforms and data providers. A bid request fires when an ad break approaches, carrying device type, location and demographic indicators, and the winning ad is inserted in milliseconds.

For a small business, that usually means going through a DSP or a managed-service partner. None of the sources documents a direct self-serve desk for small advertisers on Tubi or Pluto TV, so confirm access before planning.

Roku's side is better documented. Roku (secondary) lists ZIP-code targeting, age and gender targeting, and programmatic access through Amazon Ads and The Trade Desk. It says the June 2025 Amazon partnership covers over 80% of US connected TV households.

Questions for any provider: which FAST platforms and channels are in the buy, whether ZIP targeting is available, what the minimum spend is, and what the final report shows.

## Why can FAST be a lower-cost way into TV?

[EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-fast--how-free-streaming-tv-reshaping-ad-market-2026) says viewer growth is outpacing advertiser demand on FAST, which has produced lower ad rates and widely available premium inventory. Wurl, cited there, adds that ad fill rates keep falling as content supply exceeds advertiser investment. EMARKETER frames this as a window for early-mover advertisers.

Strategus (secondary, vendor) also describes FAST CPMs as cost-efficient. No source in this article gives an advertiser-side CPM for Tubi, Pluto TV or The Roku Channel, so no price is quoted here. Whether rates will move later is not documented. Ask providers for current rates in writing, and compare them against the benchmarks in [how much CTV advertising costs](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data).

Ad load is lighter than cable. Roku and Horizon Futures Group research, reported by Roku (secondary), puts The Roku Channel at about 8 minutes of ads per hour versus 14 to 16 on linear cable. That is vendor data, but it matches the premise that FAST ads face less clutter.

## What can go wrong on FAST?

EMARKETER lists four risks. Fragmentation: with so many channels, deciding where to place a buy is hard. Weak metadata: only 9.2% of CTV advertisers prioritize program-level contextual targeting, and 54% say better metadata would raise spending (Gracenote, cited by EMARKETER). Churn: FAST platforms lack exclusive content, so viewers move freely. Measurement: reporting standards vary across platforms.

Brand safety needs a check on news channels. Wurl data in [NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/07/24/fast-viewing-surged-55-last-year-but-discovery-and-metadata-havent-kept-up/) (secondary) found 35.7% of news scenes fully brand safe across IAB-aligned categories. A salon or groomer may prefer lifestyle and entertainment channels, and the provider should say which channels the buy includes.

Small budgets add a separate question. Read [is CTV advertising worth it on a small budget](/blog/is-ctv-advertising-worth-it-small-budget-2026) before committing.

## What does a 4-week FAST test look like?

This plan is a suggested structure, not a sourced benchmark. Creative formats are covered in the [CTV creative specs guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-creative-specs-guide-2026).

**Week 1: setup**
- Record new clients per week for the last four weeks as a baseline.
- Pick one offer, such as a first-visit discount, with a unique code or landing page.
- Choose a ZIP list around the business.
- Send the provider the questions from the buying section and get rates in writing.

**Week 2: launch**
- Upload creative that follows the provider's specs.
- Confirm which platforms and channels the buy covers.
- Ask for a frequency cap and a placement report.

**Week 3: check**
- Compare delivery against the plan.
- Count code redemptions and answers to "how did you hear about us?"
- Change nothing unless the ad is broken or running outside the target ZIPs.

**Week 4: decide**
- Compare new clients with the baseline.
- Divide total spend by new clients to get cost per new client.
- Request the final report by platform and channel, then continue, adjust or stop.

## What does a worked example look like for a pet groomer?

Every input below is an assumption for illustration, not a benchmark.

- Test budget: $1,200 for four weeks (assumption). Whether a provider accepts that size is a question for the quote.
- Average grooming ticket: $75 (assumption).
- Visits per new client in the first year: 6 (assumption), so $450 revenue per client.
- Contribution margin after supplies and labor: 60% (assumption), so $270 per client.

Break-even is $1,200 divided by $270, which is 4.4, so 5 new clients are needed in the first year to cover the test.

Three outcome scenarios, all assumptions:

- 3 new clients: $810 contribution, a $390 shortfall.
- 5 new clients: $1,350 contribution, $150 above cost.
- 8 new clients: $2,160 contribution, $960 above cost.

The math ignores clients who would have booked anyway and clients who leave after one visit. That is why the baseline in week 1 and the intake question matter. The same logic fits a gym; see [CTV ads for fitness studios](/blog/ctv-ads-for-fitness-studios) for a studio-specific version.

## FAQ

### Is FAST the same as CTV?

No. FAST is a type of content delivered over connected TV devices, smart TVs and mobile apps, according to EMARKETER. CTV is the broader screen category and also includes on-demand and subscription ad tiers.

### Can a small business advertise on Tubi or Pluto TV directly?

The sources describe programmatic buying through demand-side platforms, and Tubi's documented targeting runs through a partnership with Viant. A direct small-business sales desk is not documented. Ask any provider whether the buy covers Tubi, Pluto TV or The Roku Channel.

### How much do FAST ads cost?

No advertiser-side CPM appears in the sources, so a price cannot be stated here. EMARKETER reports lower ad rates than demand would suggest. Ask for current rates in writing.

### Are FAST viewers mostly budget cord cutters?

Hub's 2026 data suggests not. FAST users watch about 24 hours of TV a week, and 60% use FAST as a complement to paid subscriptions (NewscastStudio, secondary).
