A local business can buy Peacock ad inventory through three routes: a self-service platform, a direct NBCUniversal plan, or a programmatic deal through an eligible demand-side platform. The route you choose decides which inventory you get, how you target, what you can report and what you pay. Peacock's own page says it generates more than 15 million hours of ad-supported viewing every day, but it does not publish a price per thousand views or a minimum for local advertisers. Before you commit, confirm that Peacock is included in your route, ask for written terms, and work out your break-even cost per customer.

## Can a Local Business Buy Peacock Ads?

Yes, but the route determines what you can do. NBCUniversal describes Peacock as a premium streaming home with live events, original series and films, and says its ad products include Spotlight, contextual Pause Ads and Engagement Ads ([NBCUniversal Together](https://together.nbcuni.com/network/peacock/)). Those are formats, not self-serve buying options. Availability depends on the product, the content window and the commercial agreement.

Self-service access is the most realistic starting point for a small budget. The Ad Geeks guide describes Universal Ads as a self-service platform with no platform minimum spend and says it gives access to NBCUniversal and other premium publishers ([Ad Geeks](https://adgeeks.agency/news/news-how-to-advertise-on-peacock), secondary). That same guide warns that specific publisher access may need an account representative, so Peacock availability must be confirmed before you build the media plan.

## What Does Peacock Say About Its Audience?

NBCUniversal's own figures are strong, but they are vendor claims measured by NBCU or its partners. Its page states that 64% of Peacock viewers are not reached on competitors' ad-supported tiers, citing Nielsen streaming platform ratings averaged over December 2025 to February 2026 ([NBCUniversal Together](https://together.nbcuni.com/network/peacock/)). It also reports a 36% brand impact lift and a 22% search activity lift versus CTV norms, drawn from NBCU's aggregated campaign data.

Treat these as reasons to ask questions, not as proof for your town. National audience numbers say nothing about how many households within your trade area will see your spot. The same page reports 1.7x higher return on ad spend for its ad innovations compared with standard media, which is again NBCU's own measurement.

## Which Buying Route Fits a Local Budget?

The Ad Geeks guide sets out four routes: Universal Ads self-service, NBCUniversal direct or One Platform, programmatic guaranteed or private marketplace deals, and managed CTV buying ([Ad Geeks](https://adgeeks.agency/news/news-how-to-advertise-on-peacock), secondary). For a local advertiser, the first two matter most.

Universal Ads suits smaller and mid-market advertisers that want hands-on control of objective, audience, location, bid, budget, schedule and standard video creative. It offers two campaign types. Performance+ optimizes delivery across eligible premium publishers toward awareness or conversion goals. Content Select lets you choose genres or allowlisted networks for more placement control.

Direct NBCUniversal planning usually needs a larger commitment and more coordination. It can open premium content, live moments and custom formats, but it tends to suit brands buying across the NBCU portfolio rather than a single local business. Programmatic routes depend on the deal itself: Peacock may not be present in an open auction or in every DSP, so ask for the deal ID and confirm whether it is Peacock-specific.

## What Targeting Can You Actually Use?

Targeting depends on the route and the data permissions attached to it. Universal Ads documents location controls at state, DMA and ZIP code level, with inclusion and exclusion options ([Ad Geeks](https://adgeeks.agency/news/news-how-to-advertise-on-peacock), secondary). Location is the most useful layer for a local business because it shows whether your spot can stay near your store or service area.

The same guide recommends starting with one or two targeting layers and allowing time for delivery to stabilize. Each extra layer narrows the audience, so check forecasted scale after every change. Ask the seller whether a tighter geographic area raises the price, because the answer changes what a test costs.

NBCU's announcement of its self-serve Peacock Ad Manager, reported in trade coverage, describes precision audience and geo targeting ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/convergent-tv/nbcu-courts-smaller-performance-focused-advertisers-at-one23-ad-tech-event/), secondary). Which geographic level you can buy, and at what price, still needs written confirmation.

## What Should You Ask a Seller Before Spending?

Start with availability. Ask the seller to confirm in writing that Peacock inventory is included in your plan, not just "NBCUniversal inventory". NBCU's broader portfolio can include other networks, so ask for the network, app, content and device detail your decision depends on.

Then ask about cost and reporting. Request the minimum for a one-month test, whether it applies per campaign or per flight, and the CPM or flat price for your geography. Ask what share of your targeted households will see ads, since Peacock's own help material says its top plan is ad-free with limited exceptions. Finally, ask how delivery will be proven and whether you will receive household or ZIP-level reports.

Put every answer in writing. If a seller cannot confirm availability and a price, you do not yet have an offer you can compare.

## How Do You Work Out Break-Even?

Break-even is the cost per new customer that your business can afford. Use gross profit per customer, not revenue, because that is the money left to pay for the ad. The table below uses assumptions throughout, so treat it as a way to judge a quote rather than a forecast.

| Input or result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget (assumption) | $3,000 |
| CPM, cost per 1,000 views (assumption) | $40 |
| Impressions | 75,000 |
| Views per household (assumption) | 4 |
| Households reached | 18,750 |
| Households that become customers (assumption) | 0.05% |
| New customers | 9.4 |
| Cost per new customer | $320 |
| Gross profit per customer (assumption) | $150 |

In this example the test loses money on average, because $320 per customer is more than $150 of gross profit. A quote that brings cost per customer below your gross profit would justify a test. Frequency matters too: four views to 18,750 homes is usually a sounder local test than one view to 75,000 homes, although that is a judgment, not a measured result.

Compare the result with search. If your own Google Ads account already acquires customers at a lower cost, CTV has to justify itself through awareness, referrals and later search volume, not last-click conversions alone. The [Google Local Service Ads guide](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) is a useful benchmark for paid local leads.

## Who Should Test Peacock and Who Should Wait?

A test makes most sense for a business with high customer value, a trade area large enough for a metro-scale buy, and working search and Business Profile listings. Home services, dental practices, med spas, gyms and auto repair fit that profile better than a single café or salon with a small radius and a low ticket.

If your service area is one city, look first at local streaming guides for that market, such as [CTV advertising in Phoenix](/blog/ctv-advertising-phoenix-az-local-business-guide), [CTV advertising in Minneapolis](/blog/ctv-advertising-minneapolis-mn-local-business-guide) or [CTV advertising in Detroit](/blog/ctv-advertising-detroit-mi-local-business-guide). Each shows how a metro buy compares with a tightly targeted one.

## What Should Your Creative Do?

Streaming spots play on a large screen with sound on and no click. Lead with your business name and what you do in the first few seconds, show your real location and people, and keep one message and one call to action. Put a short web address or QR code on screen long enough to use.

Check specifications with the seller before production. The Ad Geeks guide says Universal Ads requires exact-duration 16:9 video and that platform and publisher review takes time, so leave room in your schedule for approval ([Ad Geeks](https://adgeeks.agency/news/news-how-to-advertise-on-peacock), secondary).

## How Should You Measure a Peacock Test?

Streaming ads are rarely clicked, so build tracking before launch. Use a vanity URL and a dedicated phone number for the campaign, add a "how did you hear about us" question at booking, and compare branded search and Business Profile calls in your area with the four weeks before the test. A holdout, where one area or period runs without the ad, gives you a baseline.

Do not accept the platform's attribution as the only evidence. Use it alongside your own sales records and search data.

## FAQ

### Can a small business advertise on Peacock?
Yes in principle. NBCUniversal has described a self-serve Peacock Ad Manager aimed at smaller advertisers, and self-service and programmatic routes exist. Current minimums and availability in your market must be confirmed directly with the seller.

### How much do Peacock ads cost?
NBCUniversal does not publish a price per thousand views in the sources reviewed here. Ask for a written quote, then test it against your break-even cost per customer.

### Does everyone on Peacock see ads?
No. Peacock's help material says its top plan carries no ads, with limited exceptions such as live sports and events. Ask the seller what share of the households you target will see ads.

### Can I target my city or ZIP codes?
Universal Ads documents state, DMA and ZIP code controls ([Ad Geeks](https://adgeeks.agency/news/news-how-to-advertise-on-peacock), secondary). Whether those controls apply to Peacock inventory, and at what cost, must be confirmed in writing.

### Is Peacock better than other streaming platforms for a local business?
The sources reviewed do not show that. Compare written quotes on cost per new customer against your gross profit per customer.
