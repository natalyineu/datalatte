A hair salon's Facebook ad costs what it costs per new client, and that number only means something when you set it against what a client is worth. Cost per client equals ad spend divided by new clients, and new clients equal leads multiplied by the share of leads who actually come in. Work backwards from gross profit per client: the most you can pay per lead is that profit multiplied by your lead-to-client rate. Everything else in this guide is about moving the three inputs you control: the offer, the follow-up and the tracking.

This guide is for salon and barbershop owners. It covers tracking setup, local targeting, offers, budget and the arithmetic that tells you whether to keep spending. The worked example uses clearly labelled assumptions, so swap in your own figures before you rely on it.

## What Does a Hair Salon Facebook Ad Really Cost Per Client?

Platform reports count leads, not clients. A lead might be a form fill, a message or a booking click. Only your front desk knows which of those leads sat in a chair and paid.

That gap is why a cheap cost per lead can still be an expensive cost per client. If one lead in five becomes a client, a $30 lead costs $150 per client. If half of them become clients, the same $30 lead costs $60. Your conversion rate from lead to attended appointment matters as much as the ad's price.

Track both numbers from the first week. Cost per lead tells you whether the ad attracts attention. Cost per client tells you whether it pays.

## How Do You Set Up Tracking Before Spending?

If you cannot see which ads produce bookings, you cannot improve them. Set up tracking before the first pound, dollar or dollar equivalent goes into an ad set.

Install the Meta Pixel on your website, using your site builder's built-in integration if it has one. Add an event for a completed booking. If your booking happens on a third-party platform, track a click on the booking button instead, and accept that it is a weaker signal.

Meta's developer documentation covers the Conversions API, a server-side connection that works alongside the Pixel. It needs deduplication so one booking is not counted twice, which is done with a shared event ID. Your website platform or developer should handle this, so ask them to confirm it is set up before you launch.

After a test booking, open Events Manager and check the event appears once. Then add a "How did you hear about us?" question to your booking form and give each ad its own offer code. Those two steps catch what the platform misses.

## How Local Should Your Targeting Be?

Most salon clients come from a short drive away. Pull the postcodes or zip codes of your last 50 new clients and look at where they cluster. Use that spread to set a radius around your address in Ads Manager.

Check which location settings your chosen objective supports, because options change over time. Keep age and interest targeting broad. The creative does the filtering: a photo of balayage attracts people who want balayage.

If you have a client list and permission to use it, build a custom audience from it. Exclude current clients from new-client offers, because they deserve a different message, and a new-client discount shown to a regular can feel like a loss.

A county-wide radius usually spreads spend across people who will never drive that far for a haircut. A tighter radius concentrates budget where bookings are realistic.

## Which Offer Should You Test First?

The offer usually moves results more than the audience does. Test one offer at a time so you know which change caused which result.

A new-client consultation with a stylist is low commitment and builds trust, but it needs a calendar that can be booked quickly. A free add-on, such as a gloss or scalp treatment with a first colour, protects your headline price, though it costs product and time. A service-led ad for balayage, extensions or a bridal trial reaches high-ticket buyers, but only if you have real photos of that exact service.

A quiet-slot offer, such as weekday mornings, fills empty chairs. Be careful: if regulars learn to wait for deals, the offer trains them to delay full-price bookings.

Use your own work for the creative: before-and-after photos, a short video of a finished style, and a stylist introducing herself. Get client consent for every image. State what the offer includes, the price and when it ends. Keep one idea and one button per ad.

## How Much Should You Spend and What Is the Maximum Cost Per Lead?

Start with a budget you can afford to lose during a fair test, and set a rule for what happens afterwards. Running one or two ad sets is easier to read than ten, because each one gets enough bookings to judge.

Use this formula to set your ceiling. Maximum cost per lead equals gross profit per client multiplied by your lead-to-client rate. If a client is worth $128 in gross profit over six months and four in ten leads attend, the ceiling is about $51 per lead. Above that, every lead loses money over the period you chose.

If tracking is not ready, run traffic to your booking page and fix tracking in week one. Do not treat traffic results as booking results, because they cannot tell you what each click became.

## How Do You Follow Up Leads and Retarget?

An ad that generates leads can still lose money if nobody replies. Reply to every enquiry on the same day, and aim for within an hour during opening hours, as a working target. If you take online bookings, show the first available slot on the landing page. If you take bookings by phone, make sure someone answers the number in the ad.

Most people do not book the first time they see an ad. With the pixel running, build an audience of people who visited your booking page or engaged with your page. Show them a different message: a client review, a before-and-after of the service they looked at, or a note about remaining appointments that week.

Rotate the creative so the same image does not follow the same person around. Frequent repetition tends to annoy people rather than persuade them.

## Which Numbers Should You Read Each Week?

Once a week, open Ads Manager and look at three things in order. First, cost per lead or booking, the one that matters most. Second, which ad and audience produced the most bookings. Third, click-through rate, which tells you whether the creative is getting attention.

Pause the weakest ad, keep the best one and test one new idea. Do not judge on likes or comments, and do not change the budget, audience and creative all at once, because you will not know what worked.

Give each ad a fair window before deciding. As a working assumption, one to two weeks is enough if you get a steady number of leads. If you only get a handful, wait longer and judge on booking cost instead.

## Worked Example: Does the Math Work?

Take a $600 monthly ad budget. Every input below is an assumption for illustration, not a benchmark. Replace each one with your own records before you use the result.

| | Tough | Middle | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend (assumption) | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per lead (assumption) | $80 | $50 | $30 |
| Leads | 7.5 | 12 | 20 |
| Lead to attended client rate (assumption) | 25% | 40% | 50% |
| New clients | 1.9 | 4.8 | 10 |
| Ad cost per new client | $320 | $125 | $60 |
| Average ticket (assumption) | $85 | $85 | $85 |
| Visits in first six months (assumption) | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 50% | 50% | 50% |
| Gross profit per client over six months | $128 | $128 | $128 |
| Gross profit minus ad spend | -$358 | +$12 | +$675 |

Read the Middle column as a break-even case. At these assumptions, the ads roughly pay for themselves over six months, and nearly all of the profit comes from the second and third visit. That is why rebooking at checkout matters as much as the ad.

The Tough column loses money because the lead-to-client rate is low, so the fix is usually the follow-up and the booking page, not more spend. The Strong column is possible but depends on assumptions you have not yet tested, so do not budget around it.

## What Mistakes Waste Facebook Ad Budget?

Targeting a whole county wastes money on people who will not travel for a haircut. Running ads with no tracking leaves you arguing about results instead of measuring them. Sending traffic to a slow or confusing booking page loses people before they book, so test the page on a phone using mobile data.

Judging a creative on reach or likes rather than bookings favours attractive posts that do not sell. Discounting too deeply cuts into margin, and a free add-on often protects your pricing better than a large percentage reduction. Turning ads off after one quiet week, before a fair test has run, can end a campaign that was about to work.

## What Should You Do This Week?

Pull the postcodes of your last 50 new clients and set your ad radius from them. Work out gross profit per new client over the period you care about, then calculate your maximum cost per lead using the formula above.

Check that your booking link works on a phone, then install the Meta Pixel and a booking event. Ask your developer or site builder to confirm the Conversions API is set up with deduplication.

Write one specific new-client offer using real photos from your salon, and add an offer code or a "how did you hear about us" question. Launch one campaign with one or two ad sets, and write down the number you will use to decide whether to continue or stop before the first ad goes live.

## FAQ

### How much does a Facebook lead cost for a hair salon?

The figure depends on your area, offer and landing page, and no benchmark in this guide's source list gives a verified number for salons. Work out your own cost per lead from your first test and compare it with your maximum cost per lead.

### Do I need the Meta Pixel to run salon ads?

You can run ads without it, but Meta cannot optimise for bookings and you cannot see which ads produce them. Meta's documentation recommends running the Conversions API alongside the Pixel, with deduplication.

### How long should I run an ad before deciding?

As a working assumption, one to two weeks if you get a steady number of leads. If you get only a few, wait longer and judge on cost per booking instead of cost per lead.

### Should I boost posts or use Ads Manager?

Use Ads Manager for booking campaigns. Boosts give you less control over the objective and tracking.

### Can I use the same ads on Facebook and Instagram?

Meta ads can run on both from the same account. Test placements together first, then compare results by placement so you can see where bookings actually come from. For more on local reach, see the [hair salon local SEO guide](/blog/hair-salon-local-seo-guide).
