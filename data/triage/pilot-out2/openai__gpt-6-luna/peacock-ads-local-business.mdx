## What should a local business verify first?

Start with access to Peacock inventory, not with an assumed price. A platform or seller may offer NBCUniversal inventory without guaranteeing that a campaign will run on Peacock specifically. Ask which service, app, content, devices, and deal are included, and request a written answer.

There are several possible buying routes. The current NBCUniversal page describes Peacock’s advertising products and ad innovations, while an independent 2026 guide describes self-service, direct, and programmatic buying options. The guide is a secondary source, so treat its route descriptions as a starting point for questions rather than a guarantee of access or terms.

Before comparing offers, clarify whether the objective is local awareness, visits, calls, or online leads. That choice affects what targeting and measurement matter. A campaign aimed at broad awareness may use different inventory and reporting from one designed to prompt a local booking.

## Which buying route might fit?

A self-service route may suit a business that wants to manage campaign setup and standard video creative directly. The secondary guide describes Universal Ads as a self-service option and says advertisers should confirm whether Peacock is available in the account or forecast. Because access and features can change, verify the current platform and Peacock-specific inventory before building a plan around it.

A direct NBCUniversal conversation may be more suitable when the campaign needs a premium content association, a live event, a custom placement, or discussion of cross-platform inventory. NBCUniversal’s Peacock page features products such as Arrival Ad, Live in Browse, Spotlight, and ad innovations. These are not interchangeable standard placements; ask whether a particular product is available to the business and what agreement applies.

Programmatic buying can make sense when a business or its agency already works through a demand-side platform. The secondary guide describes private marketplace and programmatic guaranteed routes, but emphasizes that access depends on the deal. Ask the seller and platform to identify the deal type, inventory, pricing basis, and whether delivery is Peacock-only or broader NBCUniversal inventory.

Do not compare routes solely by the label attached to them. Request the same core information from each seller: placement, geographic reach, cost, delivery reporting, audience controls, creative specifications, and cancellation or change terms. A quote without clear inventory details is not a sound basis for comparing options.

## What can Peacock’s published performance figures tell you?

NBCUniversal reports audience and campaign comparisons on its advertising page. It says that 64% of Peacock viewers are not reached on competitors’ ad-supported tiers, and reports a 36% brand impact lift and 22% search activity lift versus CTV norms. The page identifies sources and methodology for those comparisons, including NBCUniversal aggregated campaign data for the reported brand and search outcomes.

These figures are useful as context for the platform’s sales proposition, but they are not guaranteed results for an individual advertiser. The published comparison does not give a local business’s expected reach, cost per lead, sales lift, or return on ad spend. Do not insert the figures into a forecast as if they were promises.

The same distinction applies to scale. More than 15 million hours of ad-supported viewing per day indicates substantial platform activity, but viewing hours do not equal unique local households. A business needs a forecast for the geography it serves and a clear explanation of how the seller estimates delivery.

## How can a business check local targeting?

Ask the seller what geographic controls are available for the specific buying route. The secondary guide describes location targeting such as state, designated market area, and ZIP code in Universal Ads, while NBCUniversal’s public Peacock page does not set out the exact location controls available to every advertiser or product. Confirm the options in the actual account or proposal.

Then ask how location is applied. Does the campaign target people based on a household location, a device signal, or another method? Can the seller exclude places outside the service area? What is the forecasted reach after geographic restrictions are applied? The answer should distinguish targeting capability from the number of eligible viewers.

A small trade area can create a practical mismatch with a broad streaming buy. If a seller can only offer a larger region, some impressions may reach people who cannot realistically become customers. Decide which locations are commercially relevant before assessing the quoted audience size.

Targeting must also be appropriate for the business and compliant with applicable requirements. Keep the campaign focused on geography and relevant audiences, and ask the platform or seller to explain available controls rather than assuming a targeting option exists.

## How should a business evaluate a test?

Use a break-even calculation built from the business’s own economics. The example below is illustrative only: every input is labelled an assumption, not a Peacock rate, forecast, or expected result.

**Worked example — all inputs are assumptions.** Assume a business receives a written campaign quote of $2,000 (assumption). Assume its gross profit from one new customer is $250 (assumption). Assume the campaign leads to eight new customers (assumption). The campaign cost per new customer would be $250 (assumption: $2,000 divided by eight), matching the assumed gross profit per customer. Under those assumptions, the campaign reaches break-even before other costs.

That calculation is not evidence that the campaign will generate eight customers. Replace the assumed cost with the actual written quote, and replace gross profit with the business’s own figure after variable costs. Then assess whether the attributed customers can be measured credibly and whether the campaign supports a broader objective such as awareness.

Set up tracking before delivery begins. Depending on the business, that may include a dedicated landing page, a campaign phone number, a booking question about how the customer heard about the business, and a record of leads and sales. Agree with the seller on what delivery and outcome reports will be supplied, and how those reports will distinguish Peacock from any other inventory.

A local business should compare campaign performance with its own customer acquisition economics, not with a platform-wide lift figure. Keep the attribution method consistent and avoid treating an increase in branded searches or calls as proof of causation unless the measurement design supports that conclusion.

## What should the seller answer before launch?

Ask for the current minimum commitment, any fees, and the pricing basis for the specific Peacock placement. NBCUniversal’s public Peacock page does not publish a local business minimum or CPM, so a seller’s written offer is necessary to assess cost.

Ask whether the inventory is Peacock-only or part of a wider NBCUniversal package. Confirm the geography, available targeting controls, expected delivery, and any restrictions on content or devices. Request a clear description of the reporting included and whether outcome measurement has an additional cost.

Confirm creative requirements before production. Ask for the accepted aspect ratio, duration, file specifications, safe areas, audio requirements, and review process. The secondary guide advises advertisers to confirm exact-duration 16:9 video and allow time for review; verify those requirements with the current seller before creating an asset.

Finally, clarify who controls campaign changes, how pacing is managed, and what happens if availability or terms change. Keep the answers in writing so the business can compare proposals on consistent terms.

## Is Peacock a sensible fit for every local advertiser?

Peacock may be worth investigating when a business can serve the proposed geography, has a clear advertising objective, and can evaluate the cost against customer value. A business with a narrow service area should pay particular attention to whether the available targeting and forecast match its actual customer footprint.

A business should wait if the seller cannot identify Peacock inventory, explain the geography, or provide a clear price and measurement plan. That does not mean Peacock is unsuitable; it means the offer is not yet specific enough to assess. A written proposal lets the business compare the opportunity with other channels, including [Microsoft Advertising for local businesses](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business).

The decision should follow the business’s unit economics and available evidence. National platform figures can provide context, but local reach and customer outcomes need to be evaluated using the actual campaign terms and the business’s own tracking.

## FAQ

### Can a small business advertise on Peacock?

A small business can ask about Peacock through self-service, direct sales, or a programmatic route, but availability depends on the current account, seller, and deal. Confirm that Peacock inventory is included before planning a campaign.

### How much do Peacock ads cost?

The NBCUniversal Peacock advertising page does not list a local business minimum or CPM. Request a written quote that identifies the placement, geography, pricing basis, and any additional costs.

### Can a campaign target a city or ZIP code?

Targeting options depend on the buying route and deal. A secondary guide describes location controls in Universal Ads, but confirm the exact controls and forecast available for the current campaign.

### Do NBCUniversal’s reported lifts predict local results?

No. NBCUniversal reports comparisons for platform and campaign data, not a guaranteed outcome for an individual local advertiser. Use the figures as context and evaluate a campaign using its written terms and business-specific measurement.
