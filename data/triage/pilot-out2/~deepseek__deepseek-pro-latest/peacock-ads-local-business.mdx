Peacock generates more than 15 million hours of ad-supported viewing every day, and its ad innovations drive 1.7x higher ROAS than standard media, according to NBCUniversal. Yet neither figure answers the two questions a local café, salon, or plumbing company asks first: what is the minimum spend, and what is the cost per thousand views? NBCUniversal publishes no CPM for Peacock in any public source opened for this article.

That gap defines the buying process. A small business can buy Peacock through self-service, programmatic, or direct routes, but pricing and local reach remain unverified until a written quote arrives. This guide maps the verified facts, the routes in, a break-even calculator built on assumptions, and the questions to send a seller before committing anything.

## What Does NBCUniversal Verify About Peacock?

NBCUniversal publishes performance claims for Peacock that frame what advertisers can expect. The platform delivers [15 million hours of ad-supported viewing daily](https://together.nbcuni.com/network/peacock/). Its campaigns show a 36% brand impact lift and 22% search activity lift compared with CTV norms, based on aggregated data across hundreds of campaigns. These numbers describe national platform health, not local delivery.

The 64% figure comes from Nielsen Streaming Platform Ratings, comparing Peacock viewers against competitors' ad-supported tiers over a three-month average. That means nearly two-thirds of Peacock's ad-supported audience is exclusive to the platform among AVOD services. For a local advertiser, this implies incremental reach beyond cheaper CTV options, but it says nothing about how many of those viewers live within your service area.

Peacock's ad products include Contextual Pause Ads, which show 23% higher memorability than pause ad norms, and Engagement Ads with 114% higher interaction when paired with NBCU IP. These innovations are available through direct NBCUniversal plans, not necessarily self-service routes. A local advertiser should ask which formats are available at which budget level.

Two cautions apply. The 15 million daily viewing hours are national, and your customers live within a few miles of your door. A rising platform does not guarantee local reach. Second, NBCUniversal's impact lifts come from aggregated campaign performance across brands that may spend far more than a local test budget. Treat these as upper-bound context, not a promise for a $5,000 flight.

## What Are the Actual Buying Routes for Peacock?

Universal Ads is Comcast's self-service TV platform with no documented minimum spend. It offers two campaign types: Performance+, which optimizes delivery across eligible premium publishers, and Content Select, which lets advertisers choose genres or publisher networks. Whether Peacock is included in either route depends on account approval and current inventory, so confirm it in writing before committing a media plan.

NBCUniversal's One Platform brings linear and streaming inventory into a broader planning framework. This route suits advertisers who need live sports, sponsorships, or custom formats like Spotlight. Direct plans require negotiated minimums and separate reporting for Peacock delivery versus other NBCU inventory. Ask for impression, spend, and outcome data at the lowest available level.

Programmatic access uses private marketplace or programmatic guaranteed deals through an eligible DSP seat. Availability is deal-specific. An open auction does not guarantee Peacock inventory. Before building a line item, request the DSP seat eligibility, seller, deal ID, pricing model, device coverage, and whether the deal covers Peacock only or broader NBCU inventory. A managed partner can compare direct, self-service, and programmatic routes before money moves.

## What Targeting Can a Local Business Expect?

Targeting depends on the buying route and data permissions. [Universal Ads currently documents location controls that include state, DMA, and ZIP code inclusion or exclusion](https://adgeeks.agency/news/news-how-to-advertise-on-peacock). This matters for local targeting: a business serving three ZIP codes can exclude the rest of a metro. However, narrowing geography often reduces forecasted scale and may raise effective CPMs.

Demographic controls include age, gender, household income, education, homeownership, family status, and shopper behaviors. Each layer reduces reach, so a local advertiser should start with location and one or two high-signal layers. Viewership behavior targeting uses signals like sports, news, comedy, and food viewing to reach people based on what they watch, not the content beside the ad. Genre or contextual alignment targets the content itself.

The critical question for a local business is whether geographic targeting reaches only its service area or forces a broader buy. Universal Ads lists ZIP-level control, but actual delivery within those ZIPs depends on Peacock's household coverage there. A seller should provide forecasted impressions for your specific geography before you commit.

## What Does a Break-Even Calculation Look Like?

NBCUniversal publishes no CPM, so every number in this worked example is an **assumption**. Use it to evaluate a written quote, not to predict results.

A $3,000 test budget at an assumed $40 CPM buys 75,000 impressions. At four views per household, that reaches 18,750 households. At a 0.05% household-to-customer rate, it produces about 9.4 new customers, at roughly $320 each. At an assumed $25 CPM, the same budget reaches 30,000 households at frequency four, yielding about 15 customers at $200 each. At an assumed $60 CPM, households drop to 12,500, customers to 6.3, and cost per customer to $480.

The break-even rule is simple. If a new customer leaves you less gross profit than the cost per customer, the flight loses money on direct response. A salon with $120 of profit per first-year client cannot justify the $40 CPM scenario. A dental practice with $1,500 profit per implant patient can. Frequency matters more than reach for local awareness: four views to 18,750 nearby homes beats one view to 75,000 metro-wide households for most local objectives.

Compare with your own search cost per customer. If Google Ads delivers customers at $60, CTV must justify itself through awareness, later search volume, and referrals, not last-click conversions. For a wider CTV cost picture, see [real CTV CPM data](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026).

## What Questions Should a Seller Answer in Writing?

1. What is the minimum for a one-month test, and is it per campaign or per flight?
2. What is the CPM for my specific ZIP codes, and does narrowing geography raise it?
3. Which shows, sports, and channels will my ad run in, and which can I exclude?
4. What share of targeted households will see ads rather than a no-ads plan?
5. What creative specs do you require, and who pays for production?
6. How will you prove delivery, and do I get household or ZIP-level reports?
7. What changes if the buying platform changes ownership or is renamed mid-flight?

NBCUniversal documents [Peacock ad innovations such as Contextual Pause Ads and Engagement Ads](https://together.nbcuni.com/network/peacock/), but availability depends on the commercial agreement. A direct plan may open these formats; a self-service route likely does not. Ask which innovations apply at your budget. The same page describes Spotlight, a first-standard-ad-break placement for 24 hours, which requires a negotiated direct plan.

Get answers in writing. If a seller cannot answer the first two questions, no offer exists yet. NBCUniversal's public page describes [arrival ads on the profile selection page and live-in-browse placements](https://together.nbcuni.com/network/peacock/), both premium formats outside typical local budgets. Knowing they exist helps you gauge whether a seller is offering standard pre-roll or something different.

## How Should Creative Be Built for a Living-Room Screen?

A streaming ad plays on a large screen with sound on and no click. Lead with your business name and service in the first five seconds. Show real people, real space, and a single message. Keep one action: a web address, phone number, or QR code held on screen long enough to use. Confirm length and technical specs with your seller before production, since a 16:9 exact-duration video is typically required.

Do not repurpose a square social ad. A vertical TikTok clip stretched to a television looks broken. If you need production guidance, [this CTV creative guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-detroit-mi-local-business-guide) covers formats and checks for small budgets.

## How Should a Local Business Measure a Peacock Test?

Streaming ads rarely produce clicks, so measurement must be designed before launch. Use a vanity URL and a dedicated phone number for the campaign. Add a "how did you hear about us" question at booking or checkout. Track branded search volume and Business Profile calls in your service area, compared with the four weeks before the flight. Run a holdout: an area or period without the ad to isolate its effect.

Platform attribution alone is insufficient. NBCUniversal reports search activity lift of 22% versus CTV norms, but your own call tracking and booking data are the only proof that matters for a local budget. If search is your primary channel, review [how search and CTV interact](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) before splitting budget.

## Who Should Test Peacock, and Who Should Wait?

Reasonable fit includes businesses with high customer value and a trade area large enough to justify a streaming buy: home services, dental, med spa, gyms, and auto dealers. These businesses can absorb a $200 to $480 cost per customer from the worked example if gross profit per customer exceeds it.

Wait if the business has a small radius and low ticket. A single café serving a two-mile radius will struggle to see enough households even with ZIP targeting. The same logic applies to a boutique salon with an average ticket under $100. For those businesses, a self-serve CTV option or stronger local SEO returns more per dollar. Compare [Roku's self-serve route](/blog/ctv-advertising-minneapolis-mn-local-business-guide) or [Bing Ads for local](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business) before committing to Peacock.

The platform's 64% exclusive reach figure matters most to advertisers already running competitor CTV. If your campaign already covers Hulu or Roku, Peacock adds households those platforms miss. If no CTV spend exists yet, start with the cheapest effective route for your geography, not the most premium.

## FAQ

### Can a small business advertise on Peacock?

Yes. Universal Ads provides self-service access with no documented minimum, NBCUniversal direct plans serve negotiated commitments, and programmatic deals offer DSP workflow. Peacock inclusion is not automatic in any route, so verify it in writing.

### How much do Peacock ads cost?

NBCUniversal does not publish a CPM. The worked example in this article assumes $25 to $60 per thousand views. A written quote from a seller is the only way to know your actual price.

### Does everyone on Peacock see ads?

No. Peacock offers an ad-free Premium Plus tier, and NBCUniversal does not publish the share of subscribers on ad-supported plans. Ask your seller what percentage of your targeted households will actually see an ad.

### Can a local business target its own ZIP codes?

Universal Ads documents ZIP code inclusion and exclusion. Whether Peacock delivery matches those ZIPs at sufficient scale requires forecasted impressions for your specific geography from the seller.

### Is Peacock better than Hulu or Roku for local business?

No verified public source says so. NBCUniversal claims strong platform-wide lifts, but local performance depends on your geography, budget, and creative. Compare written quotes on cost per customer before choosing.
