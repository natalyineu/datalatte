A coffee shop can run Facebook and Instagram ads by targeting a small radius around the shop, promoting one clear offer with real photos or short video, spending a modest test budget and tracking redemptions with a code. The aim is to reach people who live or work nearby, turn them into first-time visitors, and then into regulars. Before spending anything, set one goal, work out what each new customer is worth, and check that your Google listing and menu are current, because ads send people straight to them.

## How should a coffee shop set up its first Facebook ad campaign?

Start with the foundations rather than the ad itself. A campaign needs a Facebook Page, an Instagram account linked to it, Meta Business access, an ad account with a payment method, and a landing point such as a Google Business Profile or a menu page. That landing point should load quickly on a phone. The [coffee shop conversion techniques](/blog/coffee-shop-cro-techniques) guide covers what visitors look for once they arrive.

Add tracking before launch. A unique code, a QR coupon or a staff prompt shows which customers came from the ads. Without one, a campaign only produces reach and clicks, with no evidence that anyone walked in.

Meta's Business Help Center page, listed in the sources, returned a 404 error when checked, so this guide takes no figures from it. Check Meta's live help center for current budget, billing and objective rules before setting up billing.

## What goal should a coffee shop pick for its first campaign?

Pick one goal per campaign. Each goal needs a different offer and a different measure of success, so the table below matches them up. Campaigns that try to do everything at once make it impossible to tell which part worked.

| Goal | Offer that fits | Measure by | Common trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New customers | Free add-on with a first drink | Redemptions at the till | Offer too generous to protect margin |
| New drink or season | Limited-time drink | Orders of that drink | Running it long after the launch |
| Loyalty or email sign-ups | Sign-up bonus | Sign-ups compared with spend | Sign-ups who never visit |
| Quiet-time filler | Mid-afternoon deal | Covers in that time window | Deal left running all day |
| Opening or event | Invitation with a date | Event attendance | Vague date or location |

Run a single offer toward a single objective, such as traffic to your menu or sign-ups for a loyalty card. Judge the campaign on that one result.

## How much time and budget should a coffee shop plan for?

Ad spend is only part of the workload. Bellwether Coffee's guide to social media for coffee shops gives rough organic time estimates: about 30 minutes a day for Instagram, 1 hour a day for TikTok, 15 minutes a day for Facebook and 5 minutes a day for Google Business. The same guide says Facebook still drives the most local-event and community engagement, especially where the audience skews older ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)).

These are organic time figures, not ad budgets. No standard ad budget exists, so set the number from your own margin. Let a campaign run for several days before judging it, because delivery needs time to settle, and change one thing at a time.

## Which local targeting settings work for a cafe?

Local targeting is the biggest advantage a single-site cafe has. Bellwether notes that "neighborhood discovery" moves the needle more than national reach for a single-location café ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)). Set a radius that matches how far your customers actually travel. Then check redemption data to see where visitors come from, and narrow or widen the radius to match.

Keep age and interest targeting broad at first, since narrow filters can limit delivery and raise costs. If the offer is for first-timers, exclude existing customers using an uploaded list, where consent allows. Think about who passes your door, such as commuters, students, office workers or parents, and write the ad for the group you most want to reach.

## What creative should a coffee shop run?

Coffee is visual, so lead with real images. Close-ups of drinks, latte art and steam work well, as does a short clip of a barista at work, the storefront, and staff or regulars shown with permission. Bellwether found that authenticity matters more than production value, and that Stories and Reels consistently outperform polished static posts ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)). For shot ideas, see the [coffee shop Instagram Reels ideas](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-reels-ideas-that-get-views) guide.

For copy, Fresh Cup's breakdown of coffee ads argues against talking about yourself. It suggests letting the ad show the benefit to the customer and keeping "we" language for the About page ([Fresh Cup](https://freshcup.com/a-breakdown-of-6-coffee-ads-and-why-they-work/), secondary). Name the place and the offer, such as a free pastry with a first drink this week, and give one clear call to action, such as showing the ad at the counter.

## How do you track whether the ads brought customers in?

Use one code or phrase per campaign, such as a spoken code at the till or a QR coupon, so each redemption maps to a specific campaign. Add a simple question for new customers about how they heard of you. Then compare redemptions with spend, not just clicks with spend.

The worked example below uses assumed figures for illustration. Replace every input with your own till data.

Inputs (all assumptions): ad spend of $300 per month; 40 tracked first-visit redemptions; average first-visit ticket of $6; gross margin on that ticket of 60%.

Cost per new customer = $300 ÷ 40 = $7.50.
Gross margin per first visit = $6 × 0.60 = $3.60.
Visits needed to recover the ad cost = $7.50 ÷ $3.60 ≈ 2.1, so three visits in total.

On these assumptions, the campaign pays off only if a new customer comes back at least twice after the first visit. If tracking shows fewer repeat visits, cut the spend or change the offer. ## What mistakes should a coffee shop avoid?

Several common errors waste money. Boosting posts without a goal gives less control than Ads Manager. A radius that is too wide sends ads to people who will not cross town for a coffee. Dim, cluttered photos drag results down, and running ads without tracking means no one can tell what worked.

Promotions need discipline too. Bellwether warns against constant discounting, which devalues the brand, and against promotions that cost more than they return ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)). Restaurant HQ lists coupons among coffee shop marketing tactics, so treat them as one tool rather than the whole plan ([The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/), secondary). Where possible, schedule ads around opening hours so you do not pay for clicks you cannot serve.

## What should a coffee shop do after the first test?

Review the test against the one goal you set at the start. Keep the offer that brought redemptions at a cost your margin supports, and pause the rest. Then check the listing that ads send people to, since a weak profile wastes paid clicks. The [Google Business Profile tips for coffee shops](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips) guide explains which details to check first.

Only then change one variable at a time, such as the radius or the image. For a second opinion on your listing and ad setup before spending more, start with a [free audit](/free-audit).

## FAQ

### How much should a coffee shop spend on Facebook ads?

Set the figure from your own numbers. Use the margin and repeat-visit method in the worked example to find the most you can afford per new customer, then test within that limit. Check Meta's current help pages for how daily and lifetime budgets work.

### Should a coffee shop use Facebook ads or Google Ads?

They do different jobs. Facebook and Instagram reach people who are not searching yet, while Google reaches people looking for coffee now. Many shops get the Google listing right first and then add Meta ads for awareness.

### How long does a test take before results show?

Allow at least two weeks for a first test, with tracking running throughout, so redemptions can be compared with spend. Judge the result on one offer and one goal.

### Are boosted posts good enough?

They are quick to set up but offer fewer controls over goals, radius and placements. Ads Manager gives more precise targeting and measurement.
