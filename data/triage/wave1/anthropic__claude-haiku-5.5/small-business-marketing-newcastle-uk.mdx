## What should a Newcastle business fix on Google Business Profile first?

Google Business Profile is the first place to work, because it costs nothing and appears in local map results. A listing that names the business, its categories and its service area gives Google more to match against nearby searches. Make the profile complete and accurate before spending money on ads, since paid clicks often lead people straight to it.

1. Confirm the business name, address and phone number match your website and other directories exactly.
2. Choose a primary category and add secondary categories for each service you sell.
3. Write a description that names Newcastle and the neighbourhoods you serve, such as Jesmond, Gosforth or Heaton, without stuffing keywords.
4. Add current photos of the shopfront, interior, team and products, and refresh them every few months.
5. Set opening hours, including bank holiday changes, and keep them updated.
6. Reply to every review, thanking positive reviewers and answering concerns from negative ones in a calm, specific way.
7. Post an update at least once a week, such as a seasonal offer or a new menu item.

## How should Google Ads be set up for a Newcastle postcode area?

Start with a tight geographic radius rather than a city-wide or national campaign. A coffee shop or studio mainly serves people who can reach it on foot, by bike or on the Metro, so a radius around the premises or a short list of Newcastle postcodes is usually the right scope. Broad national keywords spend budget on people who will never visit.

Build ad groups around one service each, such as "coffee shop Jesmond" or "pilates studio Gosforth", and send each group to a matching page on your website. Use location-specific wording in headlines and include a clear next step, such as booking or a call button. Negative keywords should remove searches for jobs, courses and unrelated places, which are common sources of wasted clicks for local services.

Review search terms every week for the first month. Add irrelevant queries as negatives, and move the strongest terms into their own ad groups. Pause ideas that produce clicks without enquiries. The [Google Ads service page](/services/google-ads) covers account structure in more detail.

## Where do Meta Ads and Instagram fit for local services?

Meta works best for retargeting people who already know the business, such as website visitors, past customers or people who watched a video. Cold audiences are harder to justify with a small budget, because the business has to earn the first visit and the next visit at the same time. A retargeting campaign with a specific offer, such as a booking discount, gives the ad a reason to act.

Creative should look like the place it advertises. Real photos of the shopfront, staff at work and the actual product usually feel more credible than stock images. Keep the message short and name the neighbourhood, so people recognise the business as local.

Consistent posting matters for Instagram and Facebook, but managing several accounts by hand takes time. A scheduling tool can keep the calendar full, and the comparison in [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social for local businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) explains which features matter for a small team.

## Which channel suits which goal?

Each channel has a different cost to start, a different speed to results and a different main risk. Use the table to pick one paid channel and one free channel for the first month, rather than running everything at once.

| Channel | Cost to start | Time to first results | Best use | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Free | Several weeks of consistent work | Map visibility, reviews, opening hours | Thin or outdated information |
| Google Ads (search) | Set daily budget | Days, once live | People already searching for a service | Wasted clicks from broad or national terms |
| Meta Ads (retargeting) | Set daily budget | Days, once an audience exists | Past visitors and customers | Small audience size for new businesses |
| Email and SMS | Software plus staff time | Weeks, as the list grows | Repeat visits and rebookings | Sending to people who never opted in |

The "time to first results" column is a general pattern for planning, not a sourced figure. Judge each channel against your own bookings after the test period.

## How can email and SMS bring customers back?

Repeat visits are often cheaper than new customers, so a basic list is worth building from the first month. Collect contact details at the till, on the booking form or at the front desk, and only email or text people who have agreed to hear from you. Explain what they will receive, such as a monthly list of events or a loyalty reward.

Appointment-based businesses can use reminders to reduce missed bookings. Automated texts or emails before a visit, plus a rebooking prompt after it, keep the calendar full without extra staff time. Field service and booking software often includes these features, and the differences are compared in [Jobber vs ServiceTitan for local businesses](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business).

Keep the list segmented by neighbourhood or service where possible. A message about a Jesmond event is less useful to a customer in Gosforth, and separating groups helps keep unsubscribe rates lower.

## What mistakes do Newcastle owners make with reviews and targeting?

The most common mistake is spreading a small budget across too many channels at once. Two channels run well for a month are more useful than five run badly, because the owner cannot tell which one produced bookings. Pick one paid channel and one free channel, then review results weekly.

Reviews are the second common gap. A business with a few old reviews and no replies looks less active than a competitor with recent, specific responses. Ask happy customers for a review at the point of service, and reply within a day or two.

Targeting too broadly is the third. National keywords and wide radius settings spend money on people who cannot visit. A neighbourhood-level focus usually produces fewer clicks but better enquiries for a local business.

## FAQ

### Is Google Business Profile enough on its own for a Newcastle business?

It is a strong starting point, but most businesses benefit from a website with service pages and reviews. The profile supports local map visibility, while the website gives people detail before they book or visit.

### How long should a Google Ads test run before judging it?

Run the test for at least two to four weeks, so there is enough click and booking data to compare. Judge it against your own booking value and margin, not a generic benchmark.

### Should a small Newcastle business use Meta Ads or Google Ads first?

Google Ads captures people already searching for a service, so it is usually the first paid channel to test. Meta Ads suits retargeting and awareness, and works better once there are website visitors or past customers to target.

### How much should a local business spend on marketing each month?

The sources reviewed give no benchmark for this. Set a fixed test budget, such as the £20 a day in the worked example, and increase it only when the return on spend supports it.
