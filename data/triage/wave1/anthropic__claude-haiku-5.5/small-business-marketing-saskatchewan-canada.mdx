The core pieces are an accurate Google Business Profile, local search terms, consistent listings, a steady flow of reviews, and a small paid budget you measure carefully. ## What should small business marketing in Saskatchewan include?

A local digital strategy goes beyond adding a city name to an ad. Directwest describes the core work as targeted ads based on location, local keywords that residents actually search, updated listings on local platforms such as [Mysask411](https://mysask411.com/), and messaging that feels relevant to the community ([Directwest, secondary](https://directwest.com/articles/the-power-of-local-why-saskatchewan-businesses-need-a-targeted-digital-marketing-strategy/)).

Start with four basics: a fast, mobile-friendly website with current hours and contact details, a complete Google Business Profile, a review request process, and one paid channel you can track. Add channels only after these four work. Directwest also makes the point that approaches built for Toronto or Vancouver do not always connect in Saskatchewan, so local wording and local references matter.

## Why does local matter to Saskatchewan customers?

Saskatchewan customers research before they visit. Directwest names three priorities: trust built through reviews, speed in finding clear information on mobile or search, and consistency between your website and your listings. A business that shows outdated hours, or a website that does not match its listing, can lose the customer before the first conversation.

Salesforce reports that 66% of customers expect companies to understand their unique needs ([Salesforce CA, primary](https://www.salesforce.com/ca/small-business/marketing/smb-marketing-guide/?bc=OTH)). For a local service firm, that means your listing, reviews, and ads should name the neighbourhood, the service area, and the problem you solve. For a wider view of the national picture, read the [Canada local business marketing guide](/blog/local-business-marketing-canada-guide-2026).

## How do you set up a Google Business Profile that gets found?

WareSpace lists the profile as the first local marketing tactic. Its advice is to keep business information accurate, add good photos, encourage reviews, respond promptly to every review, and update hours and services whenever they change ([WareSpace, secondary](https://warespace.com/articles/small-business-guides-tips/10-local-marketing-strategies-for-small-businesses)).

Directwest warns that businesses often forget seasonal hours and holiday closures on Google, and that these gaps frustrate customers and cost sales ([Directwest, secondary](https://directwest.com/articles/the-power-of-local-why-saskatchewan-businesses-need-a-targeted-digital-marketing-strategy/)). Set a calendar reminder to check hours before each major holiday and each seasonal change, such as a summer patio opening or a winter closure for a rural job site. ## How much should a Saskatchewan business spend on Google Ads?

No cost-per-click figure for Saskatchewan appears in the sources, so the example below uses assumptions. Replace every assumption with your own numbers from a 30-day test. The purpose is to check whether the campaign pays back before you increase spending.

Example: a coffee shop in Saskatoon with a 5 km targeting radius.

| Line | Input | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Daily budget | CA$20 (assumption) | CA$600 per 30 days |
| Average cost per click | CA$1.50 (assumption) | 400 clicks |
| Share of clicks that become a purchase | 8% (assumption) | 32 customers |
| Cost per customer | CA$600 divided by 32 | CA$18.75 |
| Average first-month spend per customer | CA$45 (assumption) | CA$1,440 revenue |
| Return on ad spend | CA$1,440 divided by CA$600 | 2.4x |

If your real numbers show a return below 1x after 30 days, change the offer or the landing page before you raise the budget. If the return is above 1x, extend the test for another 30 days to confirm it holds. Keep the test to one ad group at first, so you can see which keywords bring the clicks that convert. ## Which social and paid channels fit a local budget?

WareSpace suggests paid local advertising that geo-targets specific neighbourhoods, and SMS marketing once a customer has given permission ([WareSpace, secondary](https://warespace.com/articles/small-business-guides-tips/10-local-marketing-strategies-for-small-businesses)). Choose one paid channel first, then measure it against the cost per customer in the table above.

## What mistakes should you avoid?

Several common errors cost local businesses the most.

- Leaving hours, holidays, or service areas out of date on Google and the website.
- Ignoring reviews, or never asking happy customers for one.
- Targeting the whole province when customers come from a few kilometres away.
- Running ads with no specific offer, so the message blends into everything else.
- Collecting email or phone numbers without clear permission.

Salesforce frames marketing as a process of trial and error that should show where the budget works best ([Salesforce CA, primary](https://www.salesforce.com/ca/small-business/marketing/smb-marketing-guide/?bc=OTH)). Record one number for each channel each month, even if it is rough. Over time, the record shows which channel earns its place in the budget.

## FAQ

### What is the best first step for small business marketing in Saskatchewan?

Complete and verify your Google Business Profile, then make sure your hours, services, and service area match your website. Both are free and affect whether local customers find you at all.

### How many reviews does a local business need?

The sources do not give a review count threshold. Focus on a steady flow of recent reviews, and respond to each one promptly and professionally, as Directwest recommends.

### Should I run Google Ads or Meta Ads first?

Start with the channel that matches how your customers search. Customers looking for a service usually search, which favours Google Ads. Test one channel for 30 days using the worked example above before adding another.

### How do I know if my marketing is working?

Track cost per customer and return on ad spend for each channel every month. Compare those numbers to your assumptions, and change one variable at a time.
