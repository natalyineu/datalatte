Xiaohongshu, also called RED or RedNote, is a lifestyle search and shopping app with over 300 million monthly active users, according to [Digital Business Lab](https://digital-business-lab.com/xiaohongshu-marketing/). For a local business, it works best as a content channel for Chinese-speaking visitors and residents who research before they buy. The sources reviewed here do not confirm which accounts and ad tools a business outside China can use, so check the current rules in the app before paying an agency or buying any promotion.

## Is Xiaohongshu Worth It for a Local Business?

Digital Business Lab reports over 300 million monthly active users and more than 100 million monthly content creators. It describes the creator base as 70% women, 35% Gen Z and 50% Millennials, with half of users in Tier 1 and Tier 2 cities. Those figures describe a large Chinese-speaking audience, not a general local one.

The platform is worth a test if Chinese-speaking students, tourists or residents make up a real share of a business's customers. If recent customers cannot be named who came from Chinese-language search or reviews, time is better spent on channels that reach them first. A test costs little, but it still takes staff hours, so a limit should be set before it starts.

## How Does the Platform Work for Brands?

OOm describes Xiaohongshu as a mix of Instagram, Pinterest and Amazon, where users share content and shop inside the app. Its guide says the app depends on user-generated content, with shoppers reading reviews, watching tutorials and asking for recommendations from real users. That makes reviews and notes the core of the channel, not just paid placement.

Digital Business Lab separates three content types: brand-generated content (BGC) from the business itself, key opinion leader content (KGC) from influencers, and user-generated content (UGC) from customers. It argues the three feed each other. It also mentions boosting high-performing posts through Xiaohongshu's native ad platform, Juguang. That is an agency claim, so confirm who can buy Juguang ads before planning around it.

## What Should a Local Business Post?

Digital Business Lab recommends using popular Xiaohongshu expressions and culturally relevant themes, which it says helps avoid translation problems. A literal translation of an English slogan usually reads poorly, so new Chinese copy should be written and checked by a native speaker. The speaker should confirm the note title as well as the body.

For a local business, the most useful notes answer what a visiting customer wants to know before arriving: the service, the price range, the address, opening hours and how to pay. Photos of a real menu or price list tend to be more useful to a searcher than a brand slogan. Each note works best when it covers one service or one location.

## FAQ

### Can a business outside China use Xiaohongshu?

The sources here describe the platform's reach and content model, but they do not confirm account rules for overseas businesses. Registration and business verification steps should be checked inside the app.

### Are Juguang ads available to overseas businesses?

Digital Business Lab describes boosting posts through Juguang, but the sources do not say who can buy those ads. Xiaohongshu or the chosen agency should confirm eligibility before a budget is planned.

### Do I need to write in Chinese?

Notes are read by Chinese speakers, so the main copy should be in Chinese. A native speaker should write or check it, since literal translations often read poorly.

### How big is the audience?

Digital Business Lab reports over 300 million monthly active users, with half of users in Tier 1 and Tier 2 cities. That figure is useful context for a business's own customer mix, but it is not a forecast of results.
