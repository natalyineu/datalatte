## Can a Small Business Afford Big-Game Season Advertising?

Yes, if the plan is built around a fixed monthly amount instead of a single large purchase. The sources reviewed for this guide do not list broadcast spot prices, so this plan works from per-click and click-through benchmarks. Those benchmarks show what a small amount of spend can buy in clicks and where the money goes.

The practical approach is to choose one channel, run it for four weeks, and scale only what produces leads. A $500 test is a workable size for many local businesses because it generates enough clicks to compare cost per click, click-through rate, and cost per lead. A test that small will not prove long-term results, but it can show whether an offer is worth expanding.

## What Do Google Ads Benchmarks Say About Search and Display Costs?

The benchmarks show a clear gap between search and display. The average display cost per click across industries is $0.63, compared with $2.69 for search ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks), secondary). Display clicks are cheaper, but the average display click-through rate of 0.46% is well below the 3.17% search rate, so the cost of attracting attention and the cost of earning a visit are different problems.

Industry changes the numbers. Legal advertisers show the highest average search cost per click in the benchmark table at $6.75, while consumer services average $6.40 ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks), secondary). A business in a high-cost category should expect fewer clicks for each dollar and should set its test budget with that in mind.

## How Does the Display Network Reach Local Viewers?

Google's documentation says Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190), primary). Optimized targeting is the default setting, and Google states that Display campaigns optimize within days. Responsive display ads accept several headlines, images, and logos, and Google selects the combinations to serve.

For a local business, the practical control is geography. Set the campaign area to the places you can actually serve so that spend stays close to customers who can visit. When using Target CPA bidding, the Pay for conversions option lets the advertiser pay for actions rather than clicks, which ties spending to the outcomes that matter ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190), primary).

## How Do You Build a $500 Big-Game Test?

The worked example below uses benchmark figures for cost and click-through, and assumption-labelled inputs for the budget and lead rate. Replace the assumptions with your own numbers before drawing conclusions.

**Inputs**
- Monthly test budget: $500 (assumption)
- Search CPC: $2.69 (benchmark)
- Display CPC: $0.63 (benchmark)
- Search CTR: 3.17% (benchmark)
- Display CTR: 0.46% (benchmark)
- Share of clicks that become leads: 5% (assumption)

**Calculation**
- Search clicks: $500 ÷ $2.69 ≈ 186 clicks
- Implied search impressions: 186 ÷ 0.0317 ≈ 5,870
- Display clicks: $500 ÷ $0.63 ≈ 794 clicks
- Implied display impressions: 794 ÷ 0.0046 ≈ 172,600
- Cost per lead on search: $2.69 ÷ 0.05 = $53.80 (assumption-based)

These are averages, not forecasts. Results depend on the offer, the landing page, and the location. The most useful figure is cost per lead. If a lead is worth more than $53.80 to the business (assumption), the search test covers its cost on this basis.

## What Is the Game-Day Checklist?

Use this sequence before the event window opens:

1. Set a fixed monthly test budget and write down the highest cost per lead you will accept.
2. Confirm the campaign geography covers only the area you serve.
3. Build one offer page with a single call to action, such as a table reservation or a visit.
4. Upload responsive display assets and at least two headline variations.
5. Set up conversion tracking and test it with a real submission.
6. Plan event posts in advance with a scheduling tool, such as one compared in [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social for local businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business).
7. Review results weekly and move money only toward the channel with the lowest cost per lead.

## How Do You Measure Whether Big-Game Ads Worked?

Measurement starts before launch. Pick a conversion goal such as a booking, a call, or a form submission, and confirm it is tracked. Give each campaign its own landing page so that visits can be traced to their source. The Pay for conversions option mentioned above can align bidding with the actions you track ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190), primary).

Record results in the same system used for jobs and revenue. A plumbing business, for example, can compare booked jobs against ad spend using the framework in [Jobber vs ServiceTitan for small trades](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business). A service business can match paid invoices to campaign dates with a tool from [FreshBooks vs QuickBooks for local businesses](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business). Matching revenue to campaign dates is what turns a click count into a cost per customer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a small business run big-game ads with a $500 budget?

Yes, as a test. At the benchmark average search CPC of $2.69, $500 buys roughly 186 clicks ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks), secondary). That is enough to learn whether an offer converts, but not enough to judge long-term performance.

### Which channel should a local business test first?

Search suits businesses whose customers already look for the service. Display suits awareness, since Google reports reach across 35 million websites and apps ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190), primary). Start with the channel that matches how customers currently find the business.

### How long does a Display campaign need before results are judged?

Google states that Display campaigns optimize within days ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190), primary). Judge the test after at least one full campaign cycle rather than after the first day of results.

### What is a realistic cost per lead?

It depends on the industry and the conversion rate. In the worked example, a 5% lead rate at a $2.69 CPC gives about $53.80 per lead (assumption). Replace the 5% with a tracked rate before deciding whether the channel pays off.
