Local businesses can buy ads around Thursday Night Football on Prime Video in three ways: through Amazon's advertising team, through Amazon DSP with a partner, or through a programmatic platform with access to Prime Video inventory. Live NFL inventory is premium, so costs usually run well above ordinary streaming ads. Before committing, test whether the cost per customer beats a search ad, and get a written quote with the CPM and minimum spend.

The useful question is not whether a TNF spot can run. It is whether the cost per customer beats what a search ad would deliver for the same money. The worked example below shows how to test that before spending.

## What Are the Three Ways to Buy TNF Ads on Prime Video?

The first route is Amazon's own advertising team. This suits large advertisers, and minimum commitments for live sports are typically high, so it rarely fits a single-location business.

The second route is Amazon DSP through a partner agency. Access terms differ by partner, and some agencies may offer lower thresholds than going direct. Ask for current minimum spend, fees and whether live sports inventory is available at your budget. The [Amazon ads guide for local business](/blog/amazon-ads-for-local-business-complete-guide) covers how these buying routes compare.

The third route is a programmatic platform that can buy Prime Video inventory alongside other connected TV supply. This is usually the most flexible option for smaller budgets. You may not get Amazon's full audience data, but you can often target by geography. For a wider view of how live sports buys work for small firms, see [live sports advertising for local business](/blog/live-sports-advertising-local-business).

Ask every provider the same four questions:

1. Is live Thursday Night Football inventory actually available to me, or only general Prime Video ads?
2. What is the minimum budget and the CPM range for live sports?
3. Can I target by DMA or ZIP code?
4. What reporting will I receive, and can I get store-visit or household-level measurement?

## What Does a TNF Ad Cost a Local Business?

No reliable single price is published for this inventory, and costs vary by game, platform and buying method. Treat any figure seen online as unverified. The only sound approach is a written quote with CPM, minimum spend, and the fee structure of the buying route.

To budget, decide how many impressions you want in your market, divide by 1,000 and multiply by the quoted CPM. Then divide impressions by your target frequency to estimate reach. Four impressions across 25,000 households reaches far fewer people than one impression across 100,000, so frequency matters as much as raw volume.

Add production costs. If you do not already have a 15 or 30-second video, budget for one before committing media. Check the provider's file specifications early, since format, resolution and audio requirements differ. The [CTV creative specs guide for 2026](/blog/ctv-advertising-creative-specs-guide-2026) lists common requirements to compare against.

If your monthly ad budget is only a few hundred dollars, live NFL inventory is probably not the best use of it. Capture existing search demand first, then test TNF once you have a stable baseline.

## What Break-Even Math Should You Run First?

The example below uses assumptions, not Amazon prices, market averages or client results. Replace every assumption with the CPM from your written quote and your real ticket size. The Google Ads cost per click is the all-industry search average from WordStream, a secondary source with older data, so check your own account numbers before relying on it.

Assumptions: budget $3,000, quoted CPM $30 (assumption), target frequency 4 (assumption), average customer spend $40 (assumption), search click cost $2.69 (WordStream average), and 5% of clicks becoming customers (assumption).

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| TNF impressions bought | $3,000 / $30 x 1,000 | 100,000 |
| TNF households reached | 100,000 / 4 | 25,000 |
| Customers to cover spend on revenue | $3,000 / $40 | 75 |
| Share of households that must buy | 75 / 25,000 | 0.3% |
| Same budget in search ads | $3,000 / $2.69 = about 1,115 clicks x 5% | about 56 customers, about $54 each |
| Share of households needed to match search | 56 / 25,000 | about 0.2% |

Under these assumptions, search delivers about 56 customers for the same budget, so the TNF buy needs about 0.2% of reached households to buy just to match it. Covering the spend on revenue alone requires 0.3%. Break-even on revenue is a crude test, since it ignores margin and repeat purchases, but it sets a floor.

If the required response rate seems unbelievable, do not buy. A brand or game-day bet can still be justified, but price it as one and decide in advance what result would justify a repeat.

## Is TNF Targeting Precise Enough for a Local Area?

Targeting on connected TV is coarser than search. Through a programmatic or Amazon DSP route, delivery can often be limited by geography and sometimes by content category or shopping interest. Confirm exactly which layers the route supports, because available audiences and minimums change.

Shopping-interest layers can suit some local businesses. A restaurant might look for food delivery and grocery shoppers, an auto shop for car parts buyers, and a gym for fitness equipment shoppers. Treat these as hypotheses to test, not guaranteed results.

Neighborhood-level targeting on CTV tends to waste impressions. These campaigns work best for businesses that serve a whole metro area, such as a regional restaurant group, a delivery service, or a sports-adjacent retailer. A business serving a few blocks is usually better served by search, map listings and social.

## What Cheaper Ways Reach the Same Fans?

If live TNF inventory is out of reach, football fans can still be reached around game day. Geo-targeted search and display campaigns running on Thursdays are one option. Google describes Display campaigns as reaching people across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail, which gives a wider net than search alone ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190), primary).

Local sponsorships and venue partnerships are another route. A game-day special promoted through a bar, a league or a local team can cost far less than national streaming inventory. The [college football CTV guide for local business](/blog/college-football-ctv-advertising-local-business) covers a similar approach for a different season.

Email and SMS to an existing list with a game-day offer cost very little to run. A Spectrum Reach post on Instagram makes the general point that small and mid-sized businesses can buy sports advertising without a huge budget, but that is vendor marketing, so verify any terms directly ([Spectrum Reach on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/DWRlrXpjFCV/), vendor).

## How Do You Measure a Game-Night Campaign?

Streaming campaigns are harder to measure than search, so plan measurement before launch. Compare sales and foot traffic on Thursdays in the test market against the same weekdays in previous weeks. Use a unique URL, QR code or promo code tied only to the campaign.

Watch branded search volume and calls to the business during the flight. Ask customers how they heard about the business, and log the answers. Shifts in branded search are a weak signal alone, but they help when combined with promo code redemptions.

Set the success threshold before the first game. If the results do not clear it, cut spend on the next flight rather than extending the test on hope.

## FAQ

### How do I advertise on Amazon Thursday Night Football?

Buyers can work through Amazon's advertising team, through Amazon DSP with a partner, or through a programmatic platform with Prime Video access. Ask each route about live sports availability and minimum spend, since these vary by provider.

### How much does a Thursday Night Football ad cost?

Live NFL inventory is premium and no reliable public price is available. Request a written quote with CPM and minimum spend, and run the break-even table with those numbers before committing.

### Can I target only TNF games?

It depends on the buying route. Managed and direct buys usually offer more control. Programmatic platforms may place ads near sports content rather than guarantee a specific game, so confirm this in writing.

### Do I need to sell on Amazon to advertise on Prime Video?

No. Advertisers can buy streaming inventory through Amazon DSP and similar routes without an Amazon seller account. Confirm the account requirements with the chosen provider.

### Is a TNF campaign worth it for a local business?

It is most likely to fit a metro-area business with a game-day tie-in and a meaningful budget. If the budget is small or the radius is tight, search and local channels usually deliver more for the money.
