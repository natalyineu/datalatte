Meta ads can fill salon chairs when three settings line up: a radius matched to real travel distance, creative that shows the actual work, and a booking objective instead of a likes objective. Pew's 2026 survey found that 70% of US adults use Facebook and 51% use Instagram, so both platforms reach a large local audience. Reach is not bookings, though. Test a fixed daily budget inside the real trade area, judge the test on cost per completed booking, and avoid switching ad sets on and off by hand.

Salons sell trust at short distance. A balayage specialist may draw clients from across a city, while a walk-in barber draws from a few streets. Targeting should copy that reality rather than a default radius that covers a whole metro.

## Which Meta objective should a salon use?

Use the booking or leads objective connected to the real booking system. Traffic and engagement objectives buy cheap taps from people who like hair content, including other stylists. Optimising for completed bookings costs more per result, but it reports the number that pays the rent.

Meta's own guidance is to test deliberately rather than informally. Its A/B testing documentation says that duplicating a campaign copies its budget, and that a fair comparison uses the same budget for both versions. Manually turning ad sets on and off can create overlapping audiences and unreliable results, so the test should be set up once and left to run.

Set up conversion tracking before spending. Without a working pixel or Conversions API connection, Meta has no reliable way to tell a booking from a bounce. ## How tight should salon targeting be?

Start with the distance clients already travel. Pull the last 100 bookings, map their postcodes, and note the area that holds most of them. Most salons find the true trade area is smaller than their ambition, so advertise to that area plus one ring rather than the whole city.

Keep interest layering light. Inside a tight radius, broad targeting with a booking objective often works better than narrow interest stacks, because the auction can find likely bookers from behaviour. Split campaigns by service value so the cheapest click does not drown out the services that earn the most.

## What creative actually books appointments?

Before-and-after video shot in the chair tends to prove the stylist more convincingly than designed graphics. Film the consultation question, the process in two short cuts, and the finished result in natural light. Put the service name and starting price in the caption.

Offers need a limit and a deadline that match real capacity. "Five colour appointments this week" names scarcity the salon actually has. Repeating the same discount for months trains clients to wait for sales, so a permanent discount usually signals that targeting or creative is the problem, not price.

Worked example, with every input an assumption: a salon spends $15 a day for 30 days, or $450. At an assumed $30 cost per booking, that buys 15 bookings. If the assumed average first ticket is $90 and half of those clients rebook once, expected revenue is about $2,025 against $450 of ads. Replace the cost per booking with your own figure after the first $100 of live spend.

## How do you run a test without wasting budget?

1. Confirm the booking event fires on the confirmation page, and test it with a real booking.
2. Set a fixed daily budget and leave it unchanged for at least two weeks.
3. Limit the audience to the trade area from your postcode map.
4. Run one campaign per service group, using the same budget for each comparison.
5. Review cost per completed booking, not clicks, at the end of the test window.
6. Change one variable at a time, such as the radius or the creative, not both.

## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Meta ads?

Choose a fixed daily amount you can sustain for at least two weeks, and judge it only on cost per completed booking. The $15 a day in the worked example is an assumption, not a recommendation. A salon that cannot track bookings should fix tracking before increasing spend.

### Should a salon run Facebook or Instagram ads?

Running both under one Meta campaign lets the platform allocate budget, which is simpler to manage than splitting it by hand. Pew's 2026 data shows Facebook reaches 70% of US adults and Instagram 51%, so the right mix depends on which clients you already serve.

### Do salon ads need a discount to work?

No. A specific service, a named stylist, a real before-and-after and a limited number of slots can convert without training clients to expect sales. Discounts can help launch a new stylist or fill a genuinely empty weekday.

### Why do salon ads get clicks but no bookings?

The usual causes are a radius that includes people who will never travel, an objective that rewards taps instead of bookings, or a booking page that is slow or confusing on mobile. Check the objective and the page before rewriting the creative.
