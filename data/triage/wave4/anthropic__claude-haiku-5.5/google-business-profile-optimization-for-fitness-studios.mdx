To optimize a Google Business Profile for a fitness studio, claim the listing, use the real-world studio name, enter an accurate address or service area, and choose the fewest categories that describe your core business. Google requires one profile per business and asks that the profile reflect the business accurately, or it may be suspended. Business descriptions cannot contain links or promotional prices. Google's guidance also says changes can take a few hours to several months to appear, so judge results over several weeks. The sections below cover naming, categories, content rules, timing and a monthly upkeep budget.

## How should you name and categorize your studio on Google?

Your business name should match the name customers see on your signage, website and stationery. [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say the name must reflect the real-world name as customers know it. If your studio is called Northside Pilates, that is the name to use, not Northside Pilates Classes Open 6am.

Keep class names, taglines, hours and phone numbers out of the name field. The same guidelines list these as prohibited additions, and including them can lead to suspension of the profile. Trademark symbols and fully capitalized words also do not belong in the name.

For categories, [Google advises](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) choosing the fewest categories that describe your overall core business. A studio that focuses on yoga should set a yoga-related primary category rather than adding every service it offers. Add secondary categories only where they describe something you actually deliver as a core service.

Keyword choices should match the same service terms you use in paid search. The [Google Ads keyword guide for fitness studios](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-fitness-studios-in-2026) covers which terms to test, and the [profile optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) lists the fields to review in order.

## Which name and description rules apply to your profile?

The table below compares what Google's guidelines allow with what they prohibit. It is based on the examples in the [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), applied to a fitness studio.

| Profile field | Allowed | Not allowed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business name | Northside Pilates | Northside Pilates Open 6am |
| Business name | Iron Works Gym | IRON WORKS GYM |
| Business name | Riverside Yoga | Riverside Yoga Tel 555 0100 |
| Business description | Services, mission and history | Links of any type |
| Business description | Class types and instructor expertise | Sales, discounts and price offers |
| Categories | One primary plus the few secondary categories that describe core services | Every category that could loosely apply |

The business description is the place to explain what your studio offers and why it is different. Google's guidance asks for content that is relevant and honest, with no gimmicky characters, misspellings or filler. Keep the description factual, and move promotions into posts, where they belong in your marketing plan.

## What should your photos, posts and description avoid?

Google's content guidelines prohibit private or confidential information. Its [prohibited content policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) cover contact information linked to a name, sensitive records and personal details. For a fitness studio, this means you should not publish a client's full name beside a progress photo, a booking record or a personal phone number. Use instructors' professional names and studio-owned contact details instead.

You are allowed to post your own business's contact details, including social handles, email and phone number, on your profile or in replies. The rule covers solicitation of personal information, not the studio's own contact points.

Posts and photos should highlight what makes the studio unique, such as the training approach, the space and the instructors. Check that hours, address and service area match your website after every change, because consistency across signage, website and listings is what Google's guidance asks for.

## How long does it take for profile changes to show?

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says every change takes time to reflect on Google's side. Some changes may take effect in a few hours, while others can take several months. The guide suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change worked, and notes that not all changes will produce a visible result.

For a fitness studio, a single edit should not be judged on its own. Make a set of changes, record the date of each, then review results after several weeks. Changing hours or categories again within days makes it impossible to tell which edit mattered.

Worked example, using assumed figures:

- Assumption: the studio updates its primary category and fixes its hours on day one.
- Assumption: it reviews profile results at week three and at week eight.
- Result: the week-three review shows whether the edits are live. The week-eight review shows whether calls and direction requests moved. Google's guidance supports waiting weeks rather than days before deciding.

## What does a monthly profile check cost in time?

A profile does not need constant work, but it does need a regular check. The budget below uses assumed figures for a single-location studio. The times are planning estimates, not Google requirements.

| Task | Frequency (assumption) | Time per task (assumption) | Monthly time (assumption) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Post a class update or photo | 4 per month | 30 min | 2 h 00 min |
| Check profile insights and accuracy | 4 per month | 15 min | 1 h 00 min |
| Reply to reviews | 10 per month | 5 min | 50 min |
| Total | | | 3 h 50 min (230 min) |

If the studio has several locations, multiply the first two rows and add time for each location's categories and hours. A realistic schedule matters more than a large one. A profile updated consistently for about four hours a month usually serves a studio better than a burst of edits that stops after a week.

Use the monthly check to confirm the name, address, categories and hours still match the real studio. Booking links are worth checking at the same time. ## FAQ

### Do I need a Google Business Profile if most clients come by word of mouth?

Yes. People who hear about your studio often search for it before they call or visit. Google's guidelines say the profile should accurately reflect your business, so an out-of-date listing can give a poor first impression even when referrals bring the customer in.

### How many categories should my fitness studio choose?

Choose the fewest categories that describe your core business. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) do not give a fixed number, so start with one primary category and add secondary categories only where they describe services you deliver regularly.

### Can I put my class times or prices in the business description?

Your description should focus on services, mission and history. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) prohibit special promotions, prices and sales in the description, and they prohibit links of any type. Class schedules belong in your booking system or in posts.

### How long should I wait before judging a profile change?

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) suggests waiting a few weeks, because some changes show within hours and others take several months. Record each change with its date so you can compare results afterwards.
