## How does Google decide which pet groomers show in the map pack?

Google uses automated crawlers to find and organise pages, and local listings are built from the information a business publishes. The [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains that SEO helps search engines understand content, and that sites following Search Essentials are more likely to appear in results. No official guidance promises a top-three placement, so the map pack should be treated as a result of clear, consistent, accurate information.

For a St. Louis groomer, that means three things. The listing must describe a real business at a real place. The website must match it. Service details must be specific enough that Google can connect the business to searches such as "dog groomer near me" or "mobile dog grooming" in the city.

## What should your Google Business Profile say?

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) set the baseline. The business name should match the name used in the real world, as it appears on signage and stationery. Taglines, store codes, phone numbers, and hours do not belong in the name field. There should be only one profile per business, because duplicates can cause problems with how information displays on Google Maps and Search.

Choose the fewest categories that describe the core business. A groomer would typically pick "Pet groomer" first and add secondary categories only where they truly fit. The description should explain services and business history. It should avoid links and should not focus on prices or special offers, which the guidelines do not allow. Promotions belong in timely posts, not in the permanent description.

## How consistent do citations need to be?

Citations are listings of a business name, address, and phone number on other websites. The same guidelines ask for accuracy throughout: the address and any service area must be precise. If the sign reads "Maple Street Pet Spa," a directory should not list "Maple St. Pet Spa & Grooming." Small differences such as "Ave" versus "Avenue" or a different phone format are easy to miss and can confuse search engines about which business is which.

Start by compiling existing listings, then fix errors before adding new ones. Check Google, the website, and any pet-care directories where the business already appears. Local chambers of commerce and neighbourhood groups are worth checking too, but they should be added only after the core listings match.

## How do reviews and posts fit into the plan?

The sources used here do not provide a ranking weight for reviews, so no review count should be treated as a guaranteed threshold. What they do support is accuracy and clarity. Replies should reflect real services and the real location, and the listing should carry current, genuine updates.

A simple routine helps. After each appointment, send a short message asking for feedback. Assumption: a groomer who books 20 appointments a week and sends a message after each one will gather steadier feedback than one who asks in bulk twice a year. Replies should be factual and specific to the service the customer received, without offers or links.

## How long before you see results?

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) states that changes may take effect within a few hours or may take several months. It recommends waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change helped. That range explains why a 90-day plan is a sensible planning window rather than a promise of a specific ranking.

Track progress with Google Business Profile insights, search impressions for the website, and booking records. If a change does not show up after a few weeks, adjust one variable at a time rather than rewriting everything at once.

## FAQ

### Do I need a separate profile for mobile grooming?

Google's guidelines say there should be only one profile per business. A business that travels to customers can be eligible for a profile, but duplicate listings for each van or service area should not be created. Check the current guidelines before setting up a service-area business.

### How many categories should a pet groomer choose?

Choose the fewest categories that describe the core business, according to Google's guidelines. Start with "Pet groomer" and add secondary categories only where they clearly describe a service offered.

### Can a discount go in the Business Profile description?

No. The guidelines say descriptions should not focus on special promotions, prices, or offers, and should not include links. Use the description for services and business history, and use posts for timely updates.

### Are Google Local Service Ads a substitute for SEO?

They are a different channel. The guidance above covers profile and website work. For a comparison of paid options, read [whether Google Local Service Ads are worth it for local businesses](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business).

### Where should a groomer start with limited time?

Fix the profile name, address, hours, and categories first. Those basics take the least time and are the ones the guidelines emphasise. Then correct any citation errors and build a steady review routine.
