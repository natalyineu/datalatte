To advertise on Peacock as a local business, start with the buying route, not the creative. The main options are Universal Ads self-service, a direct NBCUniversal plan, or a programmatic deal through an eligible DSP. Each route changes the inventory you receive, the targeting you can use and the reporting you get. Peacock availability is deal-specific, so confirm Peacock inventory in writing before committing budget. NBCUniversal publishes engagement and ROAS claims but no per-thousand-view price, so base your budget on a written quote.

## Which buying route suits a local business?

For most small advertisers, the self-service route is the practical starting point. [Ad Geeks' 2026 Peacock guide](https://adgeeks.agency/news/news-how-to-advertise-on-peacock) (secondary) reports that Universal Ads, Comcast's self-service TV platform, describes access to NBCUniversal and other premium publishers with no platform minimum spend. It is aimed at smaller and mid-market advertisers who want hands-on setup.

Self-service has a catch. Universal Ads is not a Peacock product, so Peacock may or may not sit in your inventory. The same guide advises checking the platform forecast or getting written confirmation before committing the media plan.

Direct NBCUniversal plans suit brands buying premium content, live moments or sponsorships. They usually need more coordination and a larger commitment. Programmatic deals suit teams already running a DSP, but availability depends on the specific deal. Do not assume Peacock appears in an open auction.

## What do NBCUniversal's own Peacock figures show?

NBCUniversal's [Peacock advertising page](https://together.nbcuni.com/network/peacock/) (primary, vendor) reports that Peacock generates more than 15 million hours of ad-supported viewing every day. It also reports that Engagement Ads show a 114% higher interaction rate for units with NBCU IP than for those without. These are vendor claims, not independent audits, and the page does not define every comparison set behind them.

The same page cites Nielsen data that 64% of Peacock viewers are not reached on competitors' ad-supported tiers. That describes audience overlap across streaming services, not how many people in your town will see your spot.

## How does targeting work for a local buy?

Targeting depends on the route and the data permissions attached to it. [Ad Geeks](https://adgeeks.agency/news/news-how-to-advertise-on-peacock) (secondary) lists location controls including state, DMA and ZIP code inclusion or exclusion, plus demographic and viewing-behavior layers. For a local business, location controls matter most. A dental clinic with a five-mile service area needs ZIP-level control, not a state-wide buy.

The same guide recommends starting with one or two targeting layers so delivery can stabilise. Stacking several filters on a small local budget often leaves too little inventory to deliver at all. If you run a metro-scale campaign, the [Phoenix CTV guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-phoenix-az-local-business-guide) shows how geography choices change the buy.

Check forecasted scale after each layer you add. A filter that cuts your forecast to a few thousand impressions is a budget problem, not a precision gain.

## What is the test budget and break-even?

No public source gives a Peacock CPM, so every input below is an **assumption**. The example is a hypothetical gym with a $120 gross profit per new member. It shows the arithmetic you need before you accept a quote.

| Input or result | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Test budget | $3,000 | Assumption |
| CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions) | $40 | Assumption |
| Views per household | 4 | Assumption |
| Share of reached households who become members | 0.05% | Assumption |
| Gross profit per new member | $120 | Assumption |
| Impressions | 75,000 | Calculated |
| Households reached | 18,750 | Calculated |
| New members | 9.4 | Calculated |
| Cost per new member | $320 | Calculated |
| Gross profit from new members | $1,125 | Calculated |

At these inputs, the test loses $1,875. To break even, you need 25 members, which means about 50,000 households reached at four views each. That is 200,000 impressions, so the break-even CPM is $15 ($3,000 divided by 200 thousand impressions). If the quote is $40, the buy must convert far better than the assumed rate or deliver more value per member.

Check that break-even reach against your market. If your trade area holds 40,000 households, a break-even reach of 50,000 households means most of the spend lands outside the area you can serve.

## How should Peacock compare with search spend?

Compare the cost per new customer from your own Google Ads account before you test streaming. Industry averages are a weak substitute for your own data. The [WordStream benchmark](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks) (secondary, dated) puts the average search CPC across all industries at $2.69, with wide variation by sector. Health and medical averages $2.62 and legal $6.75, so your own numbers will differ.

If your search cost per new customer is well below your Peacock break-even, a streaming test is a weaker use of money. Streaming makes more sense when search is saturated, when you need awareness in a new area, or when you want to lift branded search later. For the search side, see [whether Google Local Service Ads are worth it](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) and how [Bing Ads compares](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business).

## What creative and format details should you confirm?

Ad Geeks reports that Universal Ads standard video needs exact-duration 16:9 files, and that platform and publisher review add time before launch. Build that review window into your schedule. NBCUniversal also lists formats such as Spotlight, Arrival Ad and Live in Browse on its [Peacock page](https://together.nbcuni.com/network/peacock/). Availability depends on the product and the commercial agreement.

Keep the spot simple: business name and service in the first seconds, one clear action, and a short web address or phone number on screen. A streaming viewer cannot click through, so the measurement set-up matters as much as the video. For a sports-led metro buy, the [college football CTV guide](/blog/college-football-ctv-advertising-local-business) covers how live-event inventory changes timing.

## What should you ask a seller before paying?

Put these questions to any Peacock seller or platform rep:

1. Is Peacock inventory included in my plan, in writing?
2. What is the minimum for a one-month test, and is it per campaign or per flight?
3. What is the CPM or flat price for my ZIP codes or DMA, and does narrowing the area raise it?
4. Which networks, apps and content will my ad run in, and can I exclude any?
5. What delivery and outcome reporting will I get, and at what geographic level?
6. What creative specifications and review timelines apply?
7. What happens to my commitment if the product, pricing or deal terms change mid-flight?

If a seller cannot answer the first two questions, the offer is not yet usable.

## FAQ

### Can a small business advertise on Peacock?

Access depends on the route. Universal Ads is self-service with no platform minimum spend, according to [Ad Geeks](https://adgeeks.agency/news/news-how-to-advertise-on-peacock) (secondary). Peacock inventory within that platform must still be confirmed before launch.

### How much do Peacock ads cost?

None of the sources reviewed publishes a Peacock CPM. Get a written quote for your geography and run the break-even calculation above before accepting it.

### Can I target my city or ZIP codes?

Universal Ads documents state, DMA and ZIP code controls, per the Ad Geeks guide. Whether those controls apply to Peacock inventory in your specific deal needs written confirmation.

### Is Peacock a better choice than other streaming platforms?

The sources reviewed do not support a general ranking. Compare written quotes on cost per new customer against your own search benchmarks.
