Call-only campaigns are built to produce phone enquiries, and service businesses such as plumbers, cleaners and locksmiths rely on them. Before rebuilding one, check its current status on [Google's Ads Help pages](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118), because the sources behind this guide do not set out the deadlines for call ads. The steps that carry over regardless are measuring the account correctly, checking Quality Score on the keywords that drive calls, and setting the budget from your own cost per booked job. WordStream's average Search cost per click across industries is $2.69, which is a useful starting range but not a target for your trade.

This guide covers how to plan the rebuild, what to measure and how to work out whether the calls pay.

## What Should You Check Before Rebuilding a Call Campaign?

Start with the status of your existing campaigns. Google's Ads Help pages describe the call ad products, and their current dates should be confirmed there rather than copied from a summary. Note which ad groups depend only on a call ad, because those are the ones that will be affected first.

Next, list the keywords that produced calls over the last few months. Those keywords are the ones worth moving into a Search campaign with a responsive search ad and a call asset, since they already show buying intent. Keep the list in a spreadsheet with the calls, bookings and spend for each keyword, so the decision rests on your own figures.

## How Does Quality Score Affect Call Results?

Quality Score is a diagnostic from 1 to 10, shown at keyword level. It compares your ad and landing page against other advertisers whose ads showed for the exact same search over the last 90 days. A higher score means your ad and landing page are more relevant and useful to someone searching that keyword.

It is built from three components: expected clickthrough rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Each one is rated "Above average," "Average" or "Below average." Google says a rating of "Average" or "Below average" may point to an improvement opportunity, which makes the three components more useful than the single number.

Quality Score is not a key performance indicator and is not an input in the ad auction, so do not optimise your account toward it directly.

## How Do You Read a Quality Score Gap?

Open the Keywords table, add the Quality Score and component columns, and filter for keywords that show a dash. Google uses that dash when there are not enough exact searches to score a keyword. Broadening match types will not change how the score is calculated, but it can help the keyword enter more auctions and gather the data needed for a score.

For keywords with a Below average component, match the problem to the fix. Weak expected CTR usually means the headline and offer need work. Weak ad relevance means the ad text does not mirror the search. Weak landing page experience means the page the call comes from is not useful enough to justify the enquiry.

## Which Trade Categories Are Closest to Service Work?

WordStream's benchmark tables are not split by plumbing, cleaning or locksmith work, so the nearest matches are the broad categories. The figures below come from the dated tables on the page and show how cost per click and click-through rate vary between categories that can sit close to a home service business.

| Industry (WordStream table, secondary) | Average CTR (Search) | Average CPC (Search) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Industrial Services | 2.61% | $2.56 |
| Home Goods | 2.44% | $2.94 |
| Consumer Services | 2.41% | $6.40 |
| Legal | 2.93% | $6.75 |

Consumer Services and Legal cost far more per click than Industrial Services. Use the category that matches your work most closely, then replace the figure with your own account data as soon as you have a month of results.

## Worked Example: A Monthly Call Budget

The inputs below are assumptions for a single trade business. The CPC comes from WordStream's all-industry Search average. The conversion rate and booked share are assumptions to be replaced by your own numbers.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | Given | $1,000 |
| Clicks | $1,000 divided by $2.69 | about 372 |
| Calls counted as conversions (assumption 8%) | 372 times 8% | about 30 |
| Cost per call | $1,000 divided by 30 | about $33 |
| Share of calls booked (assumption 50%) | 30 times 50% | 15 booked jobs |
| Cost per booked job | $1,000 divided by 15 | about $67 |

If an average booked job earns more than about $67 in gross profit, the budget covers its cost on this assumption. If the margin is lower, tighten keywords and the service area before increasing the budget.

## How Should You Measure Calls?

Count only calls that match a real enquiry. Set a minimum call length in your conversion settings so that very short calls do not inflate the count, and choose the length from your own call data rather than from a default.

Where a Google forwarding number is not available in your country, track calls through your phone system or CRM, then import the results so they appear against the campaign. Keep one definition of a conversion for the whole account so that results from different campaigns can be compared fairly.

## Should You Compare Call Ads With Local Services Ads?

Local Services Ads are a separate product with their own eligibility rules, so they cannot simply replace a Search campaign. Check whether your trade and area qualify. The [Local Services Ads guide](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) explains how they differ from Search ads and when they are worth testing.

For most service trades, the Search route works best when you already know which keywords convert. The [Google Ads guide for service businesses](/blog/google-ads-for-service-businesses) covers keyword structure and budget control in more detail.

## What Mix of Campaign Types Makes Sense?

Search campaigns are the main route for calls from people who already want a service. Smart campaigns add automated bidding and can be useful once you have enough conversions to train them, as explained in the [Smart campaigns guide](/blog/google-ads-smart-campaigns).

Display is a weaker fit for direct calls. WordStream's all-industry Display average click-through rate is 0.46%, against 3.17% for Search (secondary), so a call goal rarely suits it. Google describes Display as a way to reach people across its partner sites, apps and properties, which suits awareness more than immediate phone enquiries.

Compare channels on cost per booked job rather than cost per click. The [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads comparison for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) walks through that comparison for local accounts.

## FAQ

### Is the Google Ads call-only campaign still available?

The sources used for this guide do not confirm the current creation status or deadlines. Check Google's Ads Help pages for the live position before planning a migration.

### What does Quality Score measure?

Quality Score is a diagnostic from 1 to 10 at keyword level. It compares your ad and landing page relevance with other advertisers for the same exact search over the last 90 days. It is not a KPI and is not an auction input.

### Why does a keyword show a dash for Quality Score?

A dash means there are not enough exact searches for the keyword to produce a score. Keyword Planner can help you find higher-volume terms, and broader match types can help a keyword gather more data.

### What is a good cost per click for a service business?

WordStream's all-industry Search average is $2.69, but its tables do not list plumbing or locksmith work. Use the closest category as a starting point, then replace it with your own cost per booked job.

### Does changing match type change Quality Score?

No. Google says Quality Score is based on historical impressions for exact searches, so changing match types does not affect the score itself.
