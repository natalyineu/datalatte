UK small businesses can buy connected TV (CTV) ads on streaming services such as ITVX, Sky Go and other apps, either directly from a broadcaster or through a demand-side platform. Most of this inventory is priced by CPM, the cost per thousand impressions. A UK agency puts the common starting CPM at £15, rising to £50 or more, so a £1,500 budget buys roughly 30,000 to 100,000 impressions. A first test should be small, local and tied to one measurable result.

## Where Can a UK Small Business Buy CTV Ads?

The main routes are broadcaster video-on-demand services and programmatic platforms. The TV Agency lists ITVX (formerly ITV Hub), All 4, Sky Go, Samsung, LG, Netflix, Freevee and Pluto TV among the CTV apps advertisers can reach. It describes ITVX as working on a CPM model and says Sky Go has more than 12 million registered users. Sky Advance is Sky's programmatic platform and also uses CPM pricing, according to the same source.

Each platform has its own minimum spend and targeting menu, so compare them before committing. The TV Agency notes that "some suppliers have minimum or recommended spends others do not." Ask each seller for a quote in writing.

Businesses comparing markets can read the [Ireland CTV guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-ireland-small-business-guide), which covers a neighbouring English-speaking market with its own platforms and costs, or the [Canada CTV guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-canada-small-business-guide) for North American buyers. This guide focuses on the UK.

## What Does CTV Advertising Cost Per Thousand Views?

CPM is the price for every 1,000 impressions an ad receives. The TV Agency says the most common model is still CPM, which "can start from £15 going up to £50+." It also notes that programmatic platforms may use automated bidding and real-time pricing, which can change the effective price.

The same source gives a budget example. A £20,000 campaign would reach about 1,333,333 viewers at a £15 CPM, 800,000 at £25, 571,000 at £35 and 400,000 at £50. These are impression counts, not unique people, so a single household may see the ad several times.

The TV Agency adds that a regional test "could be easily less than 10k," while a national campaign "could easily be 50k plus." These are the agency's estimates, not published rate cards, and no UK broadcaster publishes a CPM for small advertisers.

## What Does a £1,500 Test Buy? A Worked Example

The table below uses inputs labelled as assumptions. They are not quotes. Change any input and the outputs move with it.

| Input or output | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | £1,500 |
| CPM (assumption, inside the £15 to £50+ range) | £25 |
| Impressions bought (£1,500 divided by £25, times 1,000) | 60,000 |
| Average views per household per month (assumption) | 4 |
| Households reached (60,000 divided by 4) | about 15,000 |
| Share of households who respond (assumption) | 0.1%, about 15 people |
| Cost per responder (£1,500 divided by 15) | about £100 |

At a £15 CPM the same budget buys 100,000 impressions. At £50 it buys 30,000. The purpose of a first test is to replace the assumed CPM and response rate with real figures from the campaign report.

Track responses with a unique phone number, a dedicated web address or a "where did you hear about us" question at booking. Without one of these, the response row in the table is guesswork.

## Does Targeting Change the Price?

Targeting is one of the main reasons CTV is useful for a local business. The TV Agency lists demographics, interests, location and device as basic options, and notes that Sky Go lets advertisers target by location and device. Some sellers also offer data partners or viewing-based targeting, which the source describes as targeting based on what someone has watched on a smart TV.

The cost warning matters. The TV Agency says that if you add lots of targeting options, "it could increase the cost dramatically." Start with one or two filters, such as location and a single interest group, and add more only if the first test is too small to read.

For a local radius, begin with the postcodes or towns already served. US readers can see the same logic applied to a single city in the [Phoenix CTV guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-phoenix-az-local-business-guide), though the costs there are not UK figures.

## Which Local Businesses Should Test CTV?

CTV is a broad-reach channel, so it fits best when a single customer is worth enough to cover a cost per impression that looks high on paper. Think about lifetime value, not the price of one visit. A business whose typical customer returns several times a year has a stronger case than one selling a single low-price item.

A useful check before booking: divide the planned spend by the number of new customers needed to break even. If the answer is clearly above what a customer is worth, test a smaller budget or a more targeted audience first.

Search and display may be cheaper ways to reach people already looking. In a 2016 WordStream benchmark set, the average Google Ads search cost per click across industries was $2.69 and the display figure was $0.63. These figures are dated, in US dollars and secondary, so use them only as a rough comparison, not as a UK benchmark.

## What Mistakes Should UK Advertisers Avoid?

- Treating one CPM as the price. The TV Agency says CPMs vary by supplier, and expensive suppliers can start much higher. Get quotes from at least two sellers.
- Spreading a small budget thinly across many targeting filters, which can raise cost and shrink reach.
- Launching without a measurement method. Decide how responses will be counted before the first impression runs.
- Judging the test on one week. Give the campaign enough time and impressions to produce a readable result.
- Assuming a search budget and a TV budget will perform alike. They reach different people at different stages of the buying decision.

## How Should You Run a First CTV Test?

1. Set one goal, such as bookings, calls or web visits, and one budget that can be afforded to lose.
2. Ask two or three sellers for a written CPM and minimum spend.
3. Pick one location filter that matches the area already served.
4. Create a unique response channel, such as a tracking number or landing page.
5. Run the campaign long enough to collect a meaningful number of responses.
6. Divide total spend by responses to get cost per response, then compare it with what a customer is worth.
7. Decide whether to scale, change targeting or stop, using the same measure each time.

Google's own display offer shows how broad reach works in practice. Google says its Display campaigns reach people across 35 million websites and apps and on Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail. That is display rather than TV, so it is a different product with a different audience, but it can be a lower-cost complement to a TV test.

## FAQ

### Is there a minimum spend for CTV advertising in the UK?

It depends on the seller. The TV Agency says some suppliers have minimum or recommended spends and others do not. It suggests a regional test could be easily under £10,000 and a national campaign could be £50,000 or more. Ask each platform for its current minimum in writing.

### How much does CTV cost per thousand views in the UK?

The TV Agency says the common CPM can start from £15 and go up to £50 or more, and that it varies by supplier. No broadcaster publishes a rate card for small advertisers, so treat any range as an estimate and read the actual CPM from the campaign report.

### Is CTV better than Google Ads for a local business?

They do different jobs. Search reaches people already looking for a service. CTV reaches people who may not know the business yet. With a small budget, the channel where people are already searching is a sensible starting point, followed by a measured TV test. A paid search review is available through the [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads).

### Can one video be used across TV and social media?

The sources here do not set out UK creative specifications, so check format requirements with each seller before producing a video. Plan for a horizontal screen and a clear call to action, and test whether the same cut works before committing to a full production.
