To manage social media for several locations, use one brand plan, one scheduling tool and a short local layer for each site. Head office publishes shared campaigns to every location, while each site adds its own photos, staff, events and offers. Tool costs depend on whether the vendor charges per seat or per channel, so the total depends on how many people and accounts you run. The worked example below uses labelled assumptions for a five-location group.

## How should multi-location content be split between head office and locations?

Split content into two layers so no location starts from a blank page. The brand layer covers seasonal promotions, new products, brand story and anything that must stay consistent. Head office writes it once and schedules it to every profile. The local layer covers staff introductions, neighbourhood events, regular customers and location-specific offers.

A starting split of about 60% brand and 40% local is an assumption to test, not a benchmark. If your sites differ a lot by neighbourhood, shift toward local. If presentation is strictly controlled, shift toward the brand layer.

The local layer matters because local posts do the most work when they reflect something real at that address. [OnEveryMap](https://oneverymap.com/insights/what-is-multi-location-social-media-management) lists location-specific events, operational facts such as holiday hours, real photos from the site and language that fits the area. A salon group, for example, can use the same pattern described in [hair salon AI-powered social media management](/blog/hair-salon-ai-powered-social-media-management). Keep the template fixed: one photo, one sentence, one call to action.

## Should each location have its own social account?

Often yes for Facebook and Google, and sometimes no for Instagram. Separate location pages help customers find the right address and leave reviews for the right site. Many small accounts, however, each need their own content, which multiplies the workload. A common compromise is one brand account on Instagram plus a separate Facebook page and Google profile per location.

The reach numbers help decide where the effort goes. Pew Research Center's 2026 survey of 5,511 U.S. adults found that 70% use Facebook and 51% use Instagram, while 18% use Nextdoor. Those are U.S. figures only, so check local platform usage in the UK, Australia and Canada before you weight your effort.

Decide by asking whether customers search by brand or by neighbourhood. Coffee shops and salons often get found by neighbourhood, so local pages earn their keep. A chain with near-identical offers everywhere may get enough from one brand account.

## What do social scheduling tools cost?

Pricing changes often, so check each vendor's current page before you budget. The clearest per-seat figures in the sources come from a competitor's 2026 comparison. [SocioLocal](https://sociolocal.io/blog/8-best-multi-location-social-media-management-tools-2026-rankings) reports that Sprout Social starts at $199 per seat per month on its Standard plan and $399 per seat per month on Advanced, with Enterprise custom. Treat these as secondary figures and confirm them with Sprout directly.

The same comparison says Hootsuite's enterprise support sits behind steep premium tiers and that its platform lacks native point-of-sale integration for retail workflows. That makes a per-seat or enterprise tool a poor fit for a small group that only needs scheduling and approvals.

Compare two numbers before you choose: the monthly tool cost and the staff hours it saves. ## How do Google Business Profiles fit into a chain's social plan?

Social posts and Google profiles work together, because customers often read reviews and check a profile before they visit. [OnEveryMap](https://oneverymap.com/insights/what-is-multi-location-social-media-management) cites Google's 2024 finding that a complete Business Profile makes customers 2.7 times more likely to see the business as reputable and 70% more likely to visit. Those are secondary figures, so check Google's own reporting if you rely on them.

The same source cites BrightLocal's 2026 study, which found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses and 54% visit a business website after reading positive reviews. It also reports that 71% of consumers use Google to read local reviews. Keep each location's profile details, hours and photos matching the social pages.

Check accuracy regularly. The OnEveryMap article also cites a SOCi finding that only 68% of business information shown by AI tools matches the Google Business Profile. A monthly check of each site's listing data catches mismatches before customers do.

## How do you keep quality consistent across locations?

Start with a one-page style guide covering tone, spelling, hashtags and what never to post. Name one approver for brand posts and one local owner for each site. Use business-owned logins rather than personal ones, and record who has access to each account.

Review each location's posts monthly, and keep a shared folder of approved photos and logos so local managers stop reusing old images. Reply to comments and messages within a working day.

When you test ad creative for a location, follow a proper method. Meta's help documentation recommends A/B testing rather than switching campaigns on and off by hand, because informal testing can produce overlapping audiences and unreliable results. Use the same budget for both versions so the comparison is fair. For tools that help draft variants, see [AI-powered social media management tools](/blog/ai-powered-social-media-management-tools).

## FAQ

### Do I need a separate account for every location?

Not always. Use separate Facebook pages and Google profiles where customers search by neighbourhood. A single brand account on Instagram can cover many sites if the offers are similar.

### How much does social media scheduling cost for five locations?

It depends on the vendor's pricing model. In the worked example, two Sprout Social Standard seats at $199 each come to $398 a month, using a secondary source price. Confirm current prices on the vendor's site.

### Which platforms should a multi-location business prioritise?

Start with where your customers already are. Pew's 2026 U.S. survey shows Facebook and Instagram are the most widely used among the platforms covered. Check local usage data for your own country before you decide.

### Can I use AI to write location posts?

Yes, for first drafts. Have a person review every post for accuracy, local facts and tone before it goes live.
