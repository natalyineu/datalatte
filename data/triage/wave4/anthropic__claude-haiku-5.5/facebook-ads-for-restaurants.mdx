## Is Facebook Worth It for a Restaurant?

Pew's 2026 survey of 5,511 US adults found Facebook used by 70%, Instagram by 51% and WhatsApp by 33%. Those figures describe reach, not results. They show that most people in a restaurant's catchment area likely have an account on the platform, which makes paid reach practical. They do not show how many of those people will book a table or order a meal.

The survey covers the United States only. Restaurants in the UK, Australia and Canada should check local usage data before setting a budget, since platform use varies by market.

Facebook is not the only paid option for a local venue. Google's help documentation says Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail. Google Search captures people already looking for a meal, while Facebook is better suited to creating demand around a new menu or a quiet weekday. The [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads comparison](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) sets out which platform fits which job.

## Who Should the Ad Reach?

Use a tight radius around the restaurant and describe the audience by occasion rather than by cuisine. A brunch offer and a late-night bar offer reach different people on different days, so each should have its own ad set and its own schedule.

Run one variable at a time. Meta's documentation describes A/B testing as showing each version to a separate segment of the audience so nobody sees both, and it recommends using the same budget for both versions to keep the comparison fair. Meta also advises against informal testing, such as switching ad sets on and off by hand, because it can produce overlapping audiences and unreliable results.

## What Should the Ad Show?

Restaurant Growth, a restaurant marketing agency, reviewed 18 restaurant Facebook ads in 2024. Several worked for clear reasons. A Seahorse Restaurant ad offered a chance to win a $50 gift card with the call to action "Tap The Link & Enter To Win". A Goat & Vine ad led with a free bottle of wine and a "Reserve now" button. A Chicago venue promoted a half-price caipirinha during happy hour and named its city in the copy so local people knew it was nearby.

Each of these ads made one action obvious. Show food the restaurant serves today, state the offer in the text, and include the address and hours. Replace an ad when its click-through rate falls, and keep a record of which version ran and when. ## How Do You Measure Walk-Ins?

Online clicks do not equal tables, so the count has to happen at the door. Three simple methods work for most venues:

1. Create one offer code per campaign and enter it at the till or on the booking form.
2. Add a question to the booking form asking how the guest heard about the restaurant.
3. Compare redemptions with spend every week, not once at the end of the month.

Do not rely on the platform's reported clicks alone. For delivery orders, the margin depends on platform fees, which can change the answer entirely. ## How Much Could $450 a Month Do?

On these assumptions, revenue is about 1.9 times the ad spend before food and labour costs are deducted. That margin may not cover a thin-margin service, so the campaign needs repeat visits to pay. Testing one offer for a slow day before committing a larger budget keeps the downside small.

## What Should You Do Before Spending Money?

Check the Google Business Profile first, since people often search a restaurant's name after seeing an ad. Confirm that the hours, menu link and photos are correct. Then pick one offer, create one code, and set a schedule that matches the slow hours the restaurant wants to fill.

Influencer posts can run alongside paid ads for the same offer. The [influencer marketing guide for restaurants and cafes](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-local-restaurants-and-cafes) explains how to match creators to a local audience.

## FAQ

### How much should a restaurant spend on Facebook ads?

No standard budget exists in the sources reviewed. Start with an amount the business can afford to lose while it learns which offer fills tables, then scale only after the cost per reservation is known.

### Are Facebook ads better than Google ads for restaurants?

They do different jobs. Google Search reaches people already looking for food. Facebook is useful for creating demand around a new menu, an event or a slow day.

### Do restaurant ads need an offer?

The examples reviewed all used a clear reason to act, such as a giveaway, a free item or a discount. An offer gives the restaurant something specific to track.

### How do I know if the ads bring people in?

Use one code per campaign, ask on the booking form, and compare redemptions with spend each week.
