## What Do the Available Sources Say About Liberia's Market?

The sources cover large public and private investment rather than the buying habits of local shoppers. The National Port Authority of Liberia has unveiled a five-year strategic plan for 2025 to 2030 worth US$550 million, aimed at modernising the maritime and logistics sector ([African Trade Chamber via LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/liberia-sets-sail-new-path-maritime-transformation-iegmf), secondary).

The same report says the Freeport of Monrovia is responsible for 40% of the Authority's revenue. That shows where trade activity is concentrated, but it does not show how many people in a given neighbourhood buy online. Treat it as context, not as a demand measure for your product.

## Why Start With a Free Business Profile?

A free presence costs nothing and gives customers something to check before they visit. Create a Facebook Page with the business name, street address, opening hours and a phone number that answers calls. Post real photos of the shop, the products and the prices in local currency.

Customers decide quickly whether a business looks legitimate. A Page with recent posts and visible contact details answers that question without any ad spend. Update it at least weekly, even if the update is a single photo of new stock.

## How Should Customers Pay?

Customers who pay by mobile money need to know which networks you accept before they arrive. List the accepted payment methods in every post, on the Page and on any printed sign at the shop. Confirm each method with your mobile money provider before you advertise it, because payment options can differ between accounts.

Write down how each payment reaches your account, who checks it and how refunds work. This prevents disputes and makes the process easy to repeat when a new employee starts.

## Which Channels Reach Local Buyers?

Use the channel your customers already answer. Some shoppers will respond to a Facebook post, others to a phone call or a message from someone they trust. Ask five or ten recent customers how they first heard about you, and record the answers in a simple list.

Word of mouth and signage matter wherever some customers are offline. Put your phone number and location on the shop front, and ask satisfied customers to tell a neighbour. ## How Do You Test a Small Facebook Ad?

Run one ad with a clear goal, such as messages or calls, aimed at people within reach of your shop. Keep the budget fixed for the whole test. Choose a duration you can afford to lose, then decide whether to continue based on counted results.

Measure only what you can count: messages received, calls made, and customers who say they saw the ad. Ask every new customer how they found you and write the answer on the sale record. An ad that no customer mentions should be judged on its cost per sale, not on its reach figures.

## What Should a Two-Week Plan Look Like?

Use this checklist to run the test without overspending:

1. Set up the free Facebook Page with address, hours, phone number and five real product photos.
2. List accepted payment methods in the About section and in the first three posts.
3. Set a fixed test budget, for example US$50, and a fixed end date.
4. Run one ad with a message objective aimed at people near the shop.
5. Record every message, call and sale in a shared sheet each day.
6. On the end date, compare cost per customer with profit on an average sale.
7. Continue only if the ad covers its cost, then repeat with one change at a time.

## Is Online Advertising Worth It for a Small Shop?

Paid ads work best as a test, not a foundation. If the free Page brings enquiries and walk-in customers arrive, the ad should add to that flow rather than replace it. If customers never mention the ad, the money may do more good on signage, delivery or a staff member who answers messages quickly.

Review the decision each month. Prices and customer habits change, so record the date of any figure you rely on and confirm it directly with the platform or your provider before acting on it.

## FAQ

### Should a small business in Liberia start with paid ads?

Start with a free Facebook Page, clear prices and a list of accepted payment methods. Add a small paid test only once customers are already finding the business.

### How do I know if an ad is working?

Count the messages, calls and sales that come from it, then compare the cost per customer with your profit on an average sale. A test that does not cover its cost should stop.

### Which payment methods should I advertise?

List only the methods you have confirmed with your provider. Do not promise payment options that are not active on your account.
