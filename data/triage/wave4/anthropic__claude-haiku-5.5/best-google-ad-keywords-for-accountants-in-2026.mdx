The best Google Ads keywords for an accountant name a specific service and a buyer or place, such as "small business accountant" plus a city, "bookkeeping services" plus a suburb, or "self-employed tax return help." Group them by service line, sort each one by buying intent, and add negatives for free, DIY, job and training searches before the first click. No source in this review publishes search volume or cost per click for accountant keywords specifically. The closest published figures are general benchmarks for finance and professional services, so treat every budget number here as a starting range to test in your own account.

## Which keyword themes should an accountant start with?

Group keywords by what the searcher wants to buy. Each theme deserves its own ad group and its own landing page, because a bookkeeping retainer and a one-off tax return have different values and different close rates. [Clickem's accountant keyword guide](https://clickem.io/accountant-google-ads-keywords/) organises its lists by service line, including bookkeeping, tax, fractional CFO and advisory, payroll and audit, and it recommends the same split for ad structure.

| Theme | Example phrases | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small business accounting | "small business accountant" plus city | Higher value, ongoing work |
| Bookkeeping | "bookkeeping services" plus suburb, "monthly bookkeeping" | Recurring retainers |
| Business tax | "small business tax accountant," "S corp tax preparation" | Often becomes year-round work |
| Personal tax | "tax preparation near me," "self employed tax accountant" | Usually one-off, seasonal |
| Payroll | "payroll services for small business" | Add-on to bookkeeping |
| Problem-led | "catch up bookkeeping," "back taxes help" | Urgent, narrower |

The SERP-level data in the benchmark section is not split by accounting sub-category, so these themes are structure advice, not volume rankings. Check search volume and suggested bids for your own cities in Google Keyword Planner before you launch.

## How should accountants sort keywords by intent?

The same service word appears at every intent level. "Bookkeeping" can mean someone ready to hire, someone comparing prices, or someone trying to learn the work themselves. The modifier decides which one you are buying, so negatives should target modifiers, not the service name.

Keywords that pair a service with a place or a buyer, such as "near me," "[city]" or "for contractors," signal hiring intent. Keywords with "how to," "what is," "template," "salary" or "course" usually signal the opposite. Keep hiring-intent terms on exact and phrase match with full bids, keep comparison terms like "cost" or "pricing" with a landing page that shows prices or ranges, and exclude learning terms.

## What negative keywords should accountants add first?

Start with four groups: free and do-it-yourself terms ("free," "template," "calculator," "software"), training and jobs ("course," "jobs," "salary," "certification"), services the practice does not offer, and places outside the service area. Clickem reports that Google's help says negative keywords do not match close variants, so "course" will not block "courses" unless both are added. Add plurals and misspellings of every negative by hand.

After launch, review the search terms report at least weekly and move new irrelevant queries into the negative list. Clickem also notes that a responsive search ad can hold up to 15 headlines of 30 characters each, which is enough to write copy that matches each service ad group.

## What do the benchmarks say about accountant click costs?

WordStream's industry benchmark page has no accounting row. The nearest finance and professional categories give a sense of range: Finance and Insurance averages $3.44 per search click with a 2.91% search click-through rate, while Legal averages $6.75 per click. Across all industries, the page reports $2.69 per search click and a 3.17% click-through rate.

The page is dated 2016 with later updates, so these figures are a reference point, not a current price list. Google's Display help page points to Performance Max or Search campaigns for other automated campaign types, so a keyword-led Search campaign is a reasonable starting structure for an accounting practice.

## What could $1,000 a month buy?

The inputs below are assumptions for illustration. The $3.44 cost per click comes from the Finance and Insurance benchmark above. The 3% conversion rate and 25% client rate are assumptions, and an enquiry means a contact form submission or phone call.

| Step | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $1,000 | Assumption |
| Cost per click | $3.44 | WordStream, Finance and Insurance |
| Clicks | about 291 | $1,000 ÷ $3.44 |
| Conversion rate to enquiry | 3% | Assumption |
| Enquiries | about 8.7 | 291 × 3% |
| Cost per enquiry | about $115 | $1,000 ÷ 8.7 |
| Enquiries that become clients | 25% | Assumption |
| New clients | about 2.2 | 8.7 × 25% |
| Cost per new client | about $459 | $1,000 ÷ 2.2 |

If a new bookkeeping or tax client is worth more than about $459 over the relationship, the spend pays back. Replace each assumption with real account numbers after the first month. A higher-value fractional CFO client can justify a higher cost per click, while a one-off personal return may need a lower one.

## How should you keep ads local and time them?

[CPA Site Solutions](https://www.cpasitesolutions.com/cpa-websites/2024/07/accountant-google-ads/) advises accountants to use local keywords, target specific geographic areas, and segment campaigns by the services offered (secondary source). Set the location to the real service area, check where clicks come from, and state on the landing page whether remote clients are served. Search ads for remote-friendly services, such as virtual bookkeeping or outsourced CFO work, can use wider location targeting than in-person tax work.

Seasonality matters more for tax than for bookkeeping. Plan personal and business tax campaigns ahead of local filing deadlines, and keep bookkeeping and small business ads running through the year. The same logic applies to other service businesses, such as [electricians](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-electricians-in-2026) or [cleaning services](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-cleaning-services-in-2026), where demand also follows a predictable pattern.

For professional services with a consultation-led close, compare results against the intent table above, not against a general average. A [chiropractor](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-chiropractors-in-2026) or [personal trainer](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-personal-trainers-in-2026) campaign runs on the same logic: hiring-intent terms first, learning terms excluded.

## FAQ

### What are the best Google Ads keywords for accountants?

Service plus place phrases tied to what the client buys, such as small business accountant, bookkeeping services, tax preparation and payroll services. None of the sources here ranks accountant keywords by search volume or cost, so test each theme in your own market.

### How much does an accountant click cost on Google Ads?

WordStream's benchmark page, dated 2016 with later updates, has no accounting category. Its Finance and Insurance search average is $3.44 per click and its Legal average is $6.75. Use these as a range, not a forecast.

### Should accountants use broad match keywords?

Only with conversion tracking, a firm negative list and enough budget to learn from. With a small budget, phrase and exact match give tighter control over which searches trigger ads.

### How many negative keywords do accountants need?

No source publishes a number. Start with free, DIY, job and training terms, add services the practice does not offer, then add new irrelevant queries from the search terms report each week.

### Does Google Ads work for small accounting firms?

It can, when each service has its own ad group and landing page, and each campaign is judged by booked consultations and client value rather than clicks alone.
