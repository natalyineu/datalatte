To set up Nextdoor ads, claim your free Nextdoor Business Page, fill it in completely, open the ads tool in your account, choose a goal, set the area you want to reach, set a daily budget, and upload one photo-led ad with a single local offer. Nextdoor's menus and options change, so read each step in your own account. Before spending anything, build the page and collect recommendations, because a neighbor who checks your profile after seeing an ad needs something to read.

## What do you need before you set up Nextdoor ads?

Start with the free Business Page. Nextdoor describes it as a way to create an online presence, and its free Business Posts let you engage neighbors locally ([Nextdoor for Small Business](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/small-business/)). Nextdoor also says 1 in 3 US households are on the platform, and that 76% of neighbors have been influenced by a recommendation there ([Nextdoor for Small Business](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/small-business/)). Those are Nextdoor's own figures, so treat them as a platform claim rather than independent research.

Search for your business first. Nextdoor pulls initial details from public data, so correct anything wrong, then complete category, phone, hours, website and photos before you buy any ads. A paid ad that sends people to an empty profile wastes the click.

## What are the 7 steps to set up Nextdoor ads?

### Step 1: Claim the Business Page

Claim your page at business.nextdoor.com and confirm the listing details. Add at least five real photos: exterior, interior, team, and your product or service. Stock images make a local business look generic to people who live nearby.

### Step 2: Open the ads tool

Sign in and open the advertising section of your Nextdoor business account. Look at the goal options, placement types and reporting views before you build anything, because the labels and layouts change over time.

### Step 3: Choose a goal

Pick the goal that matches your next action. If you want bookings or enquiries, choose a goal aimed at visits or messages. If you are promoting a time-limited offer, pick the sale or discount format if your account offers it.

### Step 4: Set the target area

Set the area by radius or by ZIP code, and start tighter than you think. Choose the streets your customers realistically travel from. A cafe or fitness studio usually needs a neighborhood-sized area, not a whole city. Narrow audience filters can shrink a small local audience quickly, so add them only when the test shows a reason.

### Step 5: Set the budget

Use a daily budget you can hold for a full test. A very low budget may not generate enough impressions to judge the ad, while a high one can burn cash before you learn anything. Judge results by cost per new customer, not cost per impression. The break-even example below shows how.

### Step 6: Write the ad

Use one photo, one clear offer and one call to action. Name the business, say what you offer, and mention a nearby landmark so neighbors can place you. Keep the text short, since most of it will be read on a phone.

```
Headline: [Business name]: [one-line value]
Body: Specific offer, plus a nearby street or landmark
Call to action: Book, Learn More or Call, as your account offers
```

Example for a salon (assumption, invented for illustration): headline "Glow Hair Studio: New Clients Welcome", body "First colour service 20% off. Two blocks from the library. Booking open this week."

### Step 7: Run for a month, then review

Two weeks is usually too short to separate a real signal from noise. Run the ad for about 30 days with the same creative, then compare results. Change one variable at a time, such as the photo or the offer, so you know what caused any difference.

## How many new customers must a test earn to break even?

Nextdoor does not publish a price list that this article can confirm, so work backward from what a customer is worth to you. The example below uses invented inputs, so replace them with your own numbers.

| Input or result | Counting first visit only | If a new client books 4 visits a year |
|---|---|---|
| Test spend (assumption: $20 a day for 30 days) | $600 | $600 |
| Profit per visit (assumption: $85 ticket, 50% margin) | $42.50 | $42.50 x 4 = $170 |
| New clients needed to break even | $600 / $42.50 = 14.1, so 15 | $600 / $170 = 3.5, so 4 |
| Clients needed per week of the test | about 3.5 | about 1 |

The honest read is that the answer depends on retention. If most clients never return, the ad must pay back on the first visit. If clients stay for years, a smaller number of new customers may justify the spend. Decide which view you are using before the test starts, not after.

## What does good Nextdoor ad creative look like?

Neighbors on Nextdoor are reading about what is nearby, so ads that feel like a neighbor's recommendation tend to fit better than ads that feel like national campaigns. Use real photos of your place, a specific local reference, and a concrete offer such as a free consultation this week. Only mention recommendations if you have real ones, and use the true number.

Avoid stock photos, vague claims like "best service in town," and ads with no call to action. Check every photo on a phone screen, since the text is often small there.

## How do you track Nextdoor ad results?

Nextdoor attribution will not be as precise as a search ad's, so set up simple tracking before the test starts. A dedicated promo code used only on Nextdoor lets you count redemptions. A tagged link with UTM parameters, such as ?utm_source=nextdoor&utm_medium=paid, shows the visits in your analytics. Asking new clients "how did you hear about us?" will undercount Nextdoor but still gives a useful signal.

An on-and-off test also helps. Run the ads for a month, pause for a month, and compare booking volume. Seasonal changes will affect both periods, so note any unusual weeks.

## How does Nextdoor compare with Google Ads?

Google Ads captures people who are already searching, while Nextdoor reaches neighbors who are reading local news and recommendations. Google's Display campaigns reach people across 35 million websites and apps, including YouTube and Gmail ([Google Ads Display help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)). That reach is broad, but it is not the same as a neighborhood feed.

| Attribute | Nextdoor | Google Ads (search) |
|---|---|---|
| Audience intent | Browsing neighborhood content | Searching for a service now |
| Targeting | Radius or ZIP code | Location and keywords |
| Best for | Trust and awareness nearby | Capturing demand |
| Typical result pattern | Builds with repeated exposure | Often faster to show results |

WordStream's industry benchmarks, a secondary source, put the average Google Ads search cost per click at $2.69 across industries, though that figure is older and varies widely by sector ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks)). Many local businesses use search ads for demand they can already see and Nextdoor for neighborhood awareness. ## FAQ

### How much should I spend on Nextdoor ads?

Spend what you can keep running for a full test, usually about 30 days, and judge the result by cost per new customer. Use the break-even table above with your own ticket size and margin to set the number. Start small if your margin is thin.

### Can I target only homeowners on Nextdoor?

Check your ad account for audience filters before assuming they exist. Home services such as cleaning or lawn care may benefit from homeowner targeting, while salons, cafes and studios often lose too much reach with extra filters.

### Is Nextdoor better than Facebook ads for local businesses?

Neither is better for every business. Nextdoor offers neighborhood trust, while Facebook is usually stronger for visual formats and retargeting. Many local businesses test both with separate budgets. For help writing ad copy and offers quickly, see [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing).

### How do I get more Nextdoor recommendations?

Ask your best local customers directly and link them to your Business Page. Read Nextdoor's current rules before offering any incentive, since recommendation requests should be honest and compliant.

### How long before Nextdoor ads show results?

Nextdoor builds awareness rather than capturing search intent, so plan for several weeks before judging the channel.
