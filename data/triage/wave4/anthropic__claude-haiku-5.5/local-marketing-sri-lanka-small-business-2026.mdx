Local marketing in Sri Lanka works best when a business uses the channels its customers already use: a Facebook Page and Messenger for discovery and inquiries, WhatsApp for bookings, and Google Search for people looking for a service in a named area such as Colombo 7 or Galle. Sinhala, Tamil and English each reach a different audience, so most local content needs at least two of them. This guide compares channel roles, sets out a worked monthly budget built on stated assumptions, and gives a 30-day starter checklist.

## Which channels matter for Sri Lankan local customers?

Most small businesses in Sri Lanka need three channels working together. A Facebook Page holds the business details, opening hours and a clear way to make contact. Messenger handles the questions that follow. WhatsApp is where many customers want to confirm a booking, check stock or ask about delivery, so the WhatsApp number should appear on every page and every ad.

Google covers customers who search before they choose. A salon in Nugegoda, a café in Colombo 3 or a pet groomer in Mount Lavinia can be found through a Google Business Profile and a search ad that names the area. Maps listings matter most for visitors who are new to a city.

Pick one primary channel and one support channel before spending anything. A business without time to reply on Messenger should not run paid Meta ads that send people there, because unanswered inquiries cost money and damage trust.

## How do the main channels compare for a small Sri Lankan business?

The table below sets out what each channel is for, who it suits and what to measure. It is a planning tool, not a benchmark: no verified Sri Lankan figures were available for platform reach or ad prices when this guide was written.

| Channel | Main job | Best suited to | Cost basis | Measure first |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook Page | Business profile and proof of trust | Almost every local business | Free to set up | Page messages per week |
| Messenger | Answer questions and take bookings | Services with varied enquiries | Staff time to reply | Median reply time |
| WhatsApp | Confirm orders and appointments | Bookings, delivery, repeat clients | Staff time | Bookings made via WhatsApp |
| Google Business Profile | Show up in local search and Maps | Any business with a fixed location | Free to set up | Calls and direction requests |
| Google Search Ads | Capture people already searching | Services with clear keywords | Pay per click | Cost per inquiry |
| Meta or TikTok ads | Reach people who are not searching yet | Visual products, food, beauty | Pay per impression or click | Inquiries per LKR spent |
| TikTok organic | Build reach with younger customers | Under-35 audiences | Staff time for filming | Profile visits leading to messages |

Read the table from top to bottom when planning. Start with the free channels, add paid search once replies are fast, and add social ads only after you know which offers get answered.

## How should content split between Sinhala, Tamil and English?

Sri Lanka has three working languages, and each one reaches a different group. Sinhala is the language of the largest community and is the default for mass-market Facebook posts. Tamil matters in Jaffna, Batticaloa and Tamil communities in Colombo, so businesses serving those areas should publish in Tamil or in a bilingual format.

English is the working language of many professionals and of international visitors. That makes it the right choice for tourism-facing businesses in places such as Galle and Ella, where visitors may search in English before they arrive.

A practical starting rule is to write the main Facebook post in the language most of your customers use, then add a short English line for search and for visitors. Keep product names, prices and area names identical across languages so that Google and customers see the same details.

## What does a worked monthly budget look like in LKR?

The figures below are illustrative inputs for a specialty café in Colombo 7. None come from a published Sri Lankan benchmark, so each is labelled as an assumption. Replace them with your own numbers after the first month.

Assumed inputs:
- Total monthly budget: LKR 50,000 (assumption)
- Google Ads budget: LKR 20,000 (assumption)
- Average cost per click: LKR 60 (assumption)
- Share of clicks that become an inquiry: 3% (assumption)

Calculation:
- Clicks per month: 20,000 divided by 60, about 333 (assumption-based)
- Inquiries per month: 333 multiplied by 3%, about 10 (assumption-based)
- Cost per inquiry: 20,000 divided by 10, about LKR 2,000 (assumption-based)

The remaining LKR 30,000 covers Meta and TikTok ads (LKR 18,000) and content production (LKR 12,000). At an assumed rate of 1 USD = LKR 300, the total is about USD 167. Check the exchange rate on the day you set the budget, because it changes.

## What should a new business do in its first 30 days?

Work through these steps in order, because each depends on the one before.

1. Create or claim a Google Business Profile with the exact area name, such as Colombo 7 or Nugegoda, and your opening hours.
2. Build a Facebook Page with the WhatsApp number, a short Sinhala description and one English line.
3. Set an automatic Messenger greeting that asks what the customer needs and states the expected reply time.
4. Write three Sinhala or bilingual posts that show the service, the price range and the location.
5. Start one Google Search ad group with area-based keywords and a small daily cap.
6. Test a WhatsApp booking flow with a colleague or friend before public launch.
7. After 30 days, compare cost per inquiry across channels and move budget toward the cheapest inquiry source.

Step 6 is where most small businesses lose bookings. The [WhatsApp and Meta guide for small businesses in Brazil](/blog/local-marketing-brazil-small-business-whatsapp-meta) covers a similar messaging workflow and is useful for comparing how booking replies are handled.

## Which dates should a Sri Lankan marketing calendar include?

The Sinhala and Tamil New Year is the main cultural occasion for beauty, food and retail businesses, and campaigns should start several weeks before it. Vesak, the Buddhist festival in May, is deeply observed. Avoid overtly commercial messages on the day itself and focus promotions on the days before. Christmas and the New Year matter for businesses with a significant Christian customer base in Colombo.

Lunar and religious holiday dates move each year, so confirm them against an official calendar before planning. Plan one social-first campaign and one search campaign around each date, and schedule posts in advance so that replies can be handled during business hours. ## FAQ

### Is Sinhala required for digital marketing in Sri Lanka?

Sinhala is the most useful single language for mass-market reach, so most local businesses should publish Sinhala content first. English still matters for search, international visitors and professional clients. Tamil is needed where Tamil customers form a large share of your market.

### Can a small business run marketing without a large budget?

Yes. The worked example uses LKR 50,000 per month with most money going to search and social ads, and the amount can be scaled down. Start with one channel and one area, then expand after measuring cost per inquiry.

### Why are platform user counts and ad prices not listed here?

No verified Sri Lankan source with those figures was available when this guide was written. Ad prices vary by keyword and season, so check current data in your own ad account before setting a budget.

### Should a business use TikTok?

TikTok can work for organic content aimed at younger customers, but it is less reliable for direct bookings than Google or WhatsApp. Test it with a small amount of time or budget, and treat it as a support channel until it produces inquiries.
