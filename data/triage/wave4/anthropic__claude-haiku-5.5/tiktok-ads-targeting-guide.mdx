TikTok ads targeting for a local shop should start with two settings: the area you serve and the age range of your customers. Every extra filter narrows who sees the ad, so add interests or advanced options only after you have delivery data. Pew Research Center reports that 37% of US adults used TikTok in 2026. That is a large pool, but a local shop only needs the slice near its door. The rest of this guide shows how to set that slice, run a fair 14-day test, and check the numbers against a simple budget.

## How many local adults are on TikTok?

Pew Research Center surveyed 5,511 US adults between January 30 and June 18, 2026, and found that 37% say they use TikTok. In 2021 the figure was 21%, and it was 33% in 2023 ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)). The trend is rising, which means more of your neighbors are on the app each year.

These figures describe US adults only. Pew does not report results for the UK, Australia or Canada in this source, so a shop in those markets should check local platform data before setting a budget. National usage also says nothing about your town. Use it as context, then measure reach inside your own targeting area in Ads Manager.

## Which location and age settings should you start with?

Set location first. Choose the city or the zip or postal codes where customers actually live or drive from. A barber near a train station and a barber in a quiet suburb need different areas, so draw the boundary from your past booking data, not from a guess.

Set age second, using the age range of the people who already book with you. If most clients are 30 to 50, do not start with 18 to 24 to chase reach. Keep gender and language open unless the service is specific. Add one interest only after the first week of delivery, so you can see whether the interest changes results or simply shrinks the audience.

## Why should you avoid stacking filters early?

Each added filter removes people from the audience. A small shop that picks a neighborhood, a narrow age band, one gender and three interests may find that the ads reach very few people, and the test tells you nothing useful. When results are weak, you cannot tell whether the offer, the creative or the targeting caused the problem.

Build the test in layers. Run the location and age version first. If it delivers clicks at a cost you can accept, add a single interest in a second version and compare the two. This keeps each change visible, and it means a weak result points to one cause.

## How do you run a fair 14-day test?

Meta's guidance on A/B testing offers a useful principle even though it covers Meta Ads, not TikTok. Meta says each version should be shown to a separate segment of the audience so nobody sees both, and that the same budget should go to each version to ensure a fair comparison ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669)). Meta also says informal testing, such as switching ad sets on and off by hand, can produce overlapping audiences and unreliable results.

Apply the same logic on TikTok. Write one hypothesis before you start, for example "a 3-mile radius with ages 25 to 45 will book more visits than the citywide version." Launch both versions at the same time with equal budgets. Do not switch one on after the other, because the second week may have different weather, holidays or competitor activity. Compare cost per click, booking rate and cost per booking when the 14 days end.

## Where does TikTok fit with other channels?

TikTok is one channel among several. For a shop that wants to compare audiences across platforms, the [Amazon ads guide for local businesses](/blog/amazon-ads-for-local-business-complete-guide) explains how paid channels differ when the goal is a sale rather than a visit. Google's Display Network reaches across 35 million websites and apps, according to Google's help page, which gives a sense of scale for a broad display channel, though it is a different product from TikTok ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)).

Canadian readers who want to test video on television-style platforms can compare the [CTV advertising guide for Canada](/blog/ctv-advertising-canada-small-business-guide) before splitting a budget across formats. Track every channel the same way: the same booking form, the same question about how the customer found you, and the same follow-up window.

## FAQ

### Can I target by radius around my shop?

This source set does not confirm that TikTok offers a radius option. Use city, zip or postal code selections, and test which group of codes gives the best booking rate for your shop.

### How long should a targeting test run?

This guide uses 14 days as an assumed test window. A longer run gives more clicks and bookings, which makes the comparison more reliable. Compare results only after both versions have run for the same number of days.

### Should I use broad or narrow targeting?

Start with location and age, then narrow. Narrow settings can reduce the audience a lot, so the test may stop delivering ads before you learn anything.

### Can I run the test with a small budget?

You can run a small test, but the budget must be large enough to produce a meaningful number of clicks. The table above shows how quickly a small test runs out of clicks at a few dollars per day. Plan the budget from your expected cost per click before you start.
