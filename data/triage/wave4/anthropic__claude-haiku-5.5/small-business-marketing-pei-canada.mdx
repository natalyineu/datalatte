A small business in Prince Edward Island markets to two audiences at once: residents who buy all year and visitors who arrive in waves. The island had 5,246 small employer businesses at December 2024 and welcomed 1.87 million non-resident visitors in 2025. The practical plan is a complete Google Business Profile, a steady stream of recent reviews, a small local ad budget tested in peak windows, and a seasonal calendar that follows both groups.

Published provincial and federal figures are used below. No public source gives PEI-specific ad costs, so the cost example uses US benchmarks and is labelled as an assumption.

## How Big Is the PEI Small Business Market?

ISED's [Key Small Business Statistics 2025](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/sme-research-statistics/en/key-small-business-statistics/key-small-business-statistics-2025) counts 5,339 employer businesses on PEI at December 2024. Of these, 5,246 are small (1 to 99 employees), 79 are medium and 14 are large. Small firms make up 98.3% of the total, so almost every competitor you face is a local owner-operated or small team.

The count covers employer businesses only. Sole traders without staff are not included, so the real number of local rivals is higher. Search your own category and town on Google to see how many businesses appear in the results that matter to you. Note the results you see for each search term, because they show which profiles already hold the top local positions in your category.

## How Much Do Visitors Matter?

[Tourism PEI's March 2026 release](https://www.tourismpei.com/news/prince-edward-island-celebrates-record-breaking-2025-tourism-season) reports 1.87 million non-resident visitors in 2025, up 10% on 2024. Visitor spending rose 12.4% according to Destination Canada's initial estimates, compared with 4.4% nationally.

For cafés, shops and personal-service businesses, visitor demand is large relative to the resident base and is not spread evenly across the year. The [Tourism PEI home page](https://www.tourismpei.com/) promoted fall festivals for October 2026, including the Charlottetown Scarecrow Festival and the Summerside Pumpkin Festival. Events therefore create demand outside the summer months, and a calendar should reflect them.

## Which Channels Should a PEI Business Use First?

Start with free and low-cost channels, then add paid ads once the basics work and you can measure what they bring in.

The Google Business Profile comes first. It needs to be verified, show correct hours (including holiday changes), carry current photos, list services and link to a booking page. Reviews come second: ask every customer, and reply to all of them. Google's [review reply guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) says reviews must reflect a genuine experience and that offering rewards for reviews is prohibited.

Paid search comes third, as a small radius campaign around your location. Social ads come fourth, for events, offers and seasonal pushes. An email or SMS list for repeat locals comes last. The [Canada local marketing guide](/blog/local-business-marketing-canada-guide-2026) covers the national picture if you serve more than one province.

## Do Reviews Matter for Visitors?

A [BrightLocal consumer survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) reports that 97% of consumers read local business reviews, 74% consider only reviews from the last three months, and 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. These are US consumer figures from a vendor survey, not PEI data, but visitors search on Google in the same way residents do.

A visitor who has never been to the island judges you from your profile and reviews alone. Ask for reviews year-round rather than only in peak weeks, so the most recent ones always reflect how the business operates now. A review request sent after each visit, and a reply to each review posted, keeps the profile current without a large time cost.

## How Do You Plan the Year?

Build the calendar around three questions: when do locals buy, when do visitors arrive, and which events can you tie into? Check festival dates on the Tourism PEI site each season, because they change.

For each window, plan one offer, update hours on your profile before the window opens, and check the listing on Google the week it starts. Keep a simple log of spend and sales per window. Accounting software such as [FreshBooks](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business) can help separate ad spend by campaign, so the cost-per-customer figure in the table is based on real numbers. If you post event news to several social channels, a scheduling tool like [Hootsuite](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) or Sprout Social can batch the posts ahead of each festival.

## FAQ

### How many small businesses are in Prince Edward Island?
ISED counts 5,246 small employer businesses (1 to 99 employees) at December 2024. That is 98.3% of PEI's employer businesses. Sole traders without staff are not included.

### How many tourists visit PEI?
Tourism PEI reported 1.87 million non-resident visitors in 2025, up 10% on 2024.

### How much do Google Ads cost on PEI?
No PEI figure is published. US benchmarks average $5.42 per click across all industries, so your local cost may be higher or lower.

### Should I advertise to tourists or locals?
It depends on your sales mix. Check which customers actually buy from you, then use your Business Profile and reviews to reach both groups.

### Is paid advertising necessary?
Not at first. A complete profile and recent reviews cost nothing and make every ad you run later more effective.
