## How Much Does a New Massage Client Cost in Google Ads?

The cost of one new client depends on four figures: what each click costs, how many clicks turn into booking requests, how many requests actually attend, and how much your monthly budget buys. Multiply those together and you get the cost per attending client. Each figure can be measured in your own account after a few weeks, which is why the estimate should be replaced with real data as soon as it is available.

Benchmarks for massage specifically are hard to find. The nearest category in the older WordStream dataset is Health & Medical, at an average search CPC of $2.62 and a search CTR of 3.27% ([WordStream benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks), secondary, older data). The same page reports all-industry averages of $2.69 per click and 3.17% CTR for search. Treat these as a starting point for a Keyword Planner check in your own city, not as a forecast for a treatment room.

Local competition, the season and the time of day all move real click prices. A therapist in a dense city centre may pay far more per click than one in a smaller town for the same keyword. The worked example later in this guide shows how to plug in local figures.

## What Does Quality Score Mean for a Massage Practice's Ads?

Quality Score is a diagnostic from 1 to 10, set at keyword level. It is calculated from three components: expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Each is rated "Above average," "Average" or "Below average," compared with other advertisers whose ads showed for the same search over the last 90 days ([Google Ads Help: Quality Score](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118), primary).

Google states that Quality Score is not a key performance indicator and is not an input in the ad auction. It is a tool for spotting where ads, landing pages or keyword choices may need work. Changing a keyword's match type does not change its score, because the score is based on historical impressions for exact searches.

For a massage therapist, the practical levers are these. Ad relevance improves when the exact service appears in the headline, such as "Deep Tissue Massage in [Town]". Landing page experience improves when clicks go to a page about that one service, with price, duration and a booking button, rather than to a general homepage. Expected click-through rate improves when the wording tells the searcher what they will get and where. A keyword showing a dash means there are not enough exact searches to score it yet.

## Which Search Keywords Should a Massage Therapist Start With?

Search ads catch people typing "massage near me" or "deep tissue massage [town]". For most solo practices this is the right first campaign. Group keywords by intent so each ad group can have its own headline and landing page.

| Ad group | Example keywords |
| --- | --- |
| Generic | "massage near me", "massage therapist [town]" |
| Technique | "deep tissue massage [town]", "sports massage [town]" |
| Problem-led | "massage for neck and shoulder tension" |
| Offer-led | "same day massage", "evening massage appointments" |
| Brand | your practice name |

Start with phrase and exact match at a small budget. Broad match can help a keyword enter more auctions and gather the exact-search data a score needs, so use it with care rather than ruling it out. Check the search terms report weekly and add negatives for jobs, courses, equipment and other non-client intent. Keep the list specific to your service so genuine bookings are not blocked.

Keep the copy plain and accurate. The sources used for this article do not cover health-claim rules, so check Google's advertising policies directly before writing any ad that mentions symptoms or outcomes.

## Should a Massage Therapist Use Display Ads?

Display campaigns serve visual ads across the Google Display Network, which Google describes as reaching people across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail ([Google Ads Help: Display ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190), primary). That reach is wide, and it is aimed at building awareness and reaching people who are not yet searching for a treatment.

For a single treatment room, that reach is usually more than a small budget can support. Someone who needs a massage this week is more likely to type a search. Start with Search, measure bookings for a month, then decide whether a small Display test is worth adding. Display campaigns also use AI-driven bidding and optimised targeting by default, which gives less manual control over who sees the ad.

## How Do You Work Out the Break-Even Click Price?

The highest click price you can afford comes from one formula: maximum CPC equals the most you will pay for an attending client, multiplied by the request rate and the show-up rate. The inputs below are assumptions, except the reference click price.

| Step | Figure | Source or label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $600 | Assumption |
| Cost per click | $2.62 | Health & Medical, WordStream, secondary |
| Clicks | 229 | $600 ÷ $2.62 |
| Click to booking request rate | 6% | Assumption |
| Booking requests | 13.7 | 229 × 0.06 |
| Show-up rate | 70% | Assumption |
| New clients who attend | 9.6 | 13.7 × 0.70 |
| Cost per attending client | $62 | $600 ÷ 9.6 |
| Session price | $90 | Assumption |
| Contribution margin after supplies and fees | 80% | Assumption |
| Contribution per visit | $72 | $90 × 0.80 |
| Visits to repay ad cost | 0.9 | $62 ÷ $72 |

At these inputs, one session covers the acquisition cost. If the show-up rate drops to 60%, the cost per attending client rises to about $73. If the target is $70 per client, the maximum click price is $2.94, calculated as 70 × 0.06 × 0.70.

The table ignores time, rent and insurance. It also assumes the client does not rebook, which is a conservative assumption for most massage practices, since repeat visits lower the real cost of each new client.

## How Do You Track Bookings Instead of Clicks?

Clicks are not the result that matters. Set up tracking for the event that becomes income, such as an online booking confirmation, and count it as a conversion in the account. If calls matter to the practice, track calls from the ad and calls from the website separately so it is clear which path produces bookings.

Ask every new client how they found the practice and note the answer against the booking. The platform counts requests and calls, but only the practice can confirm who attended. Compare that log against the break-even table once a month, and replace each assumption with a real figure as soon as one is available.

## What Should You Do First?

Work through these steps in order before turning on a campaign:

1. Pull the last 50 new clients and map their towns or postcodes. Set the ad radius from that map.
2. Fill in the break-even table with the session price, typical visits per client and the target cost per client.
3. Draft one ad group per service, with a landing page for each.
4. Check Keyword Planner for real click prices in the local area and replace the $2.62 reference.
5. Set up booking and call tracking, then launch one Search campaign with a tight negative list.

## FAQ

### Can a massage therapist use Google Ads for free?

No. Google Ads charges per click, so the budget set is the amount spent. The sources here do not give a minimum budget. Start with an amount that can be sustained for at least a month, then adjust based on booking data.

### Does Quality Score change the price paid per click?

Google describes Quality Score as a diagnostic tool that is not an input in the ad auction. Improving ad relevance and landing page experience is still worth doing, because those are the components the score measures and they affect how well ads serve searchers.

### Why does a keyword show a dash instead of a score?

Google shows a dash when there are not enough exact searches for that keyword in the last 90 days to calculate a score. Use Keyword Planner to find higher-volume terms, or broaden the match type so the keyword enters more auctions.

### Is the $2.62 click price accurate for my town?

No. It is the average search CPC for Health & Medical in an older WordStream dataset, and it is not specific to massage. Check Keyword Planner for the local area and replace it in the break-even table.
