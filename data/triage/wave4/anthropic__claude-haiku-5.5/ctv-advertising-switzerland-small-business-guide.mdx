Connected TV (CTV) advertising lets a Swiss local business show video ads on streaming services, targeted by region. Switzerland has German, French, Italian and Romansh-speaking regions, so a campaign usually starts with one language and one canton. The sources behind this guide do not include Swiss broadcaster rates, so no CHF prices are quoted here. Instead, the guide uses published Google benchmarks as reference points and works through a budget example with labelled assumptions. Start with one region, one creative version and a fixed test budget, then measure before expanding.

## How should a Swiss local business choose its language and region first?

Switzerland's four language regions mean that creative language and geographic targeting are linked decisions. A single-location business usually needs one language version, matched to the canton where its customers live. A salon in a French-speaking canton and a café in a German-speaking one need different creative, even if both run on the same platform.

Multi-language campaigns only make sense when a business has locations in more than one language region, or when customers regularly travel across a language border. Border cities deserve a check of where customers actually come from before any budget is committed. A simple customer postcode export from the booking system or point-of-sale tool can show whether the catchment crosses cantonal lines.

Businesses that also sell across a border can compare the approach in the [Germany CTV guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-germany-small-business-guide), which covers a neighbouring German-speaking market. The principle is the same: one audience, one language, one measurable catchment area.

## Which streaming platforms should a local business test?

This guide does not rank Swiss streaming platforms, because the sources used here do not include their rate cards, minimum spends or audience data. Platform offers also change, so any ranking written today could be out of date by launch.

Shortlist the broadcaster streaming services and programmatic platforms that offer canton-level geographic targeting. Then request four things in writing from each seller: a current rate card, the minimum spend, the targeting options available in the chosen canton, and how impressions are verified. Compare the answers side by side before committing any budget.

## How do the Google benchmarks help set expectations?

Google describes Display campaigns as reaching people across 35 million websites and apps, including YouTube and Gmail. That reach figure shows how broad display inventory can be, but it does not show what a Swiss viewer costs to reach.

The WordStream averages answer a different question: whether a click or impression is cheap or expensive relative to typical results. Across all industries, the study reports a 3.17% average search CTR and 0.46% display CTR, with average cost per click of $2.69 for search and $0.63 for display. These are US-dollar averages across markets, not Swiss figures.

The benchmark page is updated periodically and includes industry splits. Check the current version before setting targets for a specific sector, and note the date of any figure used in a internal report. Google's help page explains how the Display network and its AI-driven targeting work, which helps when reading platform reports later.

## What legal checks should a Swiss ad campaign include?

The sources used for this guide do not cover Swiss advertising, privacy or broadcasting rules, so this guide does not state specific legal requirements. Earlier claims about Swiss legislation were not verified against primary sources and have been removed.

Before launch, check three areas with a Swiss adviser or the relevant official body. The first is the accuracy of advertising claims, including any superlatives or comparisons. The second is how audience data is collected and used for targeting. The third is any broadcaster-specific rule on ad placement and timing. Keep each claim specific, and be ready to substantiate it with evidence on file.

## What does a worked monthly budget look like?

The example below shows what the benchmarks imply for a one-canton test. All inputs are assumptions, and the benchmarks are US-dollar averages for search and display, not CTV.

Inputs (all assumptions): monthly test budget of $1,000, one canton, 30-day test period.

Search: $1,000 ÷ $2.69 average CPC ≈ 372 clicks. At a 3.17% CTR, that implies about 11,735 impressions (372 ÷ 0.0317).

Display: $1,000 ÷ $0.63 average CPC ≈ 1,587 clicks. At a 0.46% CTR, that implies about 345,000 impressions (1,587 ÷ 0.0046).

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Search test | $1,000 | $2.69 CPC, 3.17% CTR | About 372 clicks, about 11,700 impressions |
| Display test | $1,000 | $0.63 CPC, 0.46% CTR | About 1,587 clicks, about 345,000 impressions |
| CTV test (assumption) | $1,000 | Platform CPM quote needed | Impressions = budget ÷ CPM × 1,000 |

The CTV row cannot be filled from these sources. Replace the CPM with the quote from the chosen seller, and the formula gives a comparable impression count. Divide the test budget by that CPM, then multiply by 1,000.

## How should a business launch and measure a first CTV test?

Work through these steps in order before spending the full test budget:

1. Pick one canton and one language, and write one creative version for it.
2. Request a written rate card, minimum spend and targeting confirmation from each seller.
3. Set a fixed test budget and run the campaign for at least 30 days (assumption: a common length for a first read on results).
4. Track outcomes with a unique phone number or landing page, so calls, bookings or visits can be tied to the campaign.
5. Compare cost per booking against the search and display benchmarks above, then decide whether to extend, change the creative or stop.

If search or display ads already run for the business, keep the CTV test budget separate so results are not mixed. The [Google Ads services page](/services/google-ads) describes how DataLatte sets up that separation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a minimum budget for CTV advertising in Switzerland?

The sources used here do not give a minimum. Ask each seller for its minimum spend in writing. The worked example above uses an assumed $1,000 test budget, which should be replaced with the business's own figure.

### Can a business target a different canton from where it is based?

It may be possible, depending on the platform's targeting options. Confirm the exact geographic targeting in writing before buying, because this guide does not cover Swiss targeting rules.

### Which language should the creative use?

Match the language of the region where the customers live. A business operating in one language region usually needs one creative version. Multiple languages make sense only when the business has locations or customers in several regions.

### Do Google's benchmarks apply to CTV?

Not directly. The averages cover Google Ads search and display campaigns. Use them to compare cost per click and click-through rate in general, not as CTV prices. A CTV test needs the platform's own CPM quote.
