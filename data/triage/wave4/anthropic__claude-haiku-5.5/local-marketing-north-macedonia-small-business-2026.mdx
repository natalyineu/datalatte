Small businesses in North Macedonia can run effective local marketing on a modest monthly budget by choosing two or three channels, confirming where their own customers already search and message, and logging every enquiry by source. Start with Google Maps and one social page, test one paid line at a time, and compare cost per enquiry after 30 days. The sources checked for this rewrite did not publish North Macedonia market figures, so every number below is a labelled assumption for a worked example.

## Which channels should you test first?

Start with the channels your customers already use to find you and contact you. For a salon or café, that usually means Google Maps, a Facebook or Instagram page, and a messaging app for bookings. Ask five to ten recent customers how they found you and which app they used to get in touch. Write the answers in a simple sheet.

Then test at most two paid channels at a time. Search ads suit people who already type a service into Google, such as a barber in a named district. Social ads suit businesses that need visual reach, such as a new menu or a refit. Running both at once with no tracking makes it impossible to see which one brought the enquiries.

## What does a 30-day checklist look like?

Work through these steps in order before spending the full budget:

1. Complete your Google Business Profile with the Macedonian business name, opening hours, service list and current photos.
2. Build one ad group per service, using the exact words your customers type in search.
3. Set a fixed daily cap so a busy week cannot use up the whole monthly budget.
4. Add a phone or message button that opens the app your customers actually use.
5. Log every enquiry in a shared sheet with its source channel and date.
6. On day 30, compare cost per enquiry by channel and reallocate the weakest line.

Have a native speaker review every ad and post before it goes live. Machine translation can get tone, grammar and local place names wrong, which lowers trust in a service business. Confirm which language your local customers use in search and messages before choosing the ad language.

## How should you plan around local dates and seasons?

Plan content around the holidays your customers observe, and around the tourist season if you serve visitors. Check the national and Orthodox calendars each year, because some dates change. Start a campaign several weeks before a holiday, when people are planning, rather than on the day itself.

Businesses in Ohrid that serve visitors may need pages in more than one language. Test which language returns more enquiries before building a full bilingual site. Keep the seasonal calendar in the same sheet as your enquiry log, so each season has a measured result you can compare the following year.

## FAQ

### Should ads be in Macedonian or Albanian?

Use the language your customers actually search and message in your area. Test both on a small budget and compare enquiries after a month. Do not assume one language covers every neighbourhood or customer group.

### Is a fixed monthly budget enough to test?

Yes, if you split it across no more than two paid lines and log results for at least one full month. The worked example above uses MKD 40,000 as an assumption. Adjust the total to what you can sustain for three months.

### Do I need an agency to run this?

No. The 30-day checklist can be done in-house. A specialist can speed up setup and tracking. If you want an independent review of your current listings and ads first, the [free audit](/free-audit) is a practical starting point.
