Meta Ads can fill a hair salon's calendar when they target a small local radius, send people straight to a booking page, and run on a fixed monthly budget. Facebook has the widest reach: according to Pew Research Center's 2026 survey of US adults, 70% use it and 51% use Instagram. A realistic starting budget for a single-location salon is about $500 a month. The main levers are audience, creative and booking tracking, not click counts.

## Which Meta platform should a salon start with?

Facebook is the sensible first placement for most salons because it has the largest adult audience in the US. [Pew's 2026 fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) puts Facebook at 70% of adults, up from 68% in its 2023 survey, while Instagram rose from 47% to 51% over the same period. YouTube, at 84%, sits outside Meta and is not covered in this guide.

Pew surveyed US adults only. Salons in the UK, Australia or Canada should check the audience estimates shown inside Meta Ads Manager before assuming the same platform ranking applies locally.

Nextdoor at 18% may suit neighbourhood salons, but Pew's data does not show how many of those users live within your radius. Treat it as a test channel rather than a core one. Start with one placement set, then add others only after the first produces bookings you can measure.

## How should a salon set up targeting and budget?

Start with a local radius. The exact distance is an assumption to test, but 5 to 8 miles around a single location is a common starting point. Add service-related interests, such as colour, cuts or styling, and exclude people who already visit your booking page so that existing clients do not absorb spend meant for new ones.

[The US Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/barbers-hairstylists-and-cosmetologists.htm) notes that barbers, hairstylists and cosmetologists mostly work in salons and barbershops, and that schedules often include evenings and weekends. Set ad schedules and open booking slots around those busy periods so that a click can become an appointment on the same day.

When comparing two versions of an ad, [Meta recommends](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669) an A/B test with the same budget for both versions. Informal testing, such as switching ad sets on and off by hand, can create overlapping audiences and unreliable results. Meta's test options include ad images, ad text, audience and placement.

## What does a $500 month look like in numbers?

The table below uses assumed inputs throughout. The cost per click is a planning figure. [WordStream's 2016 benchmark](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks) (secondary, search ads across all industries) was $2.69, so $1.50 assumes a tighter local audience. Replace every assumption with your own figures after the first two weeks.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly budget (assumption) | Fixed spend | $500 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | Planning figure | $1.50 |
| Clicks | $500 ÷ $1.50 | about 333 |
| Click-to-booking rate (assumption) | Planning figure | 2% |
| Bookings | 333 × 2% | about 6.7 |
| Cost per booking | $500 ÷ 6.7 | about $75 |
| Average first-visit ticket (assumption) | Planning figure | $80 |
| First-visit revenue | 6.7 × $80 | about $536 |

At these inputs, first-visit revenue barely covers the $500 spend. The campaign pays off only if clients return, so track rebooking rates alongside first bookings. If cost per booking stays above your average ticket after 60 days, change the offer or the audience before adding budget.

## What checklist should a salon follow each month?

1. Confirm the booking link works on mobile and shows open slots for the next 7 days.
2. Set a fixed monthly budget and a daily schedule. Avoid spending the full month in the first few days.
3. Run two to four creative variations at equal budget for about two weeks.
4. Keep the winning creative, pause the rest, and add a new variation before performance drops.
5. Review cost per booking and repeat-visit rate, not only clicks.
6. Refresh creative on a fixed cycle, such as every 7 to 14 days, and note the date of each change.

## Why do creative and pacing decide results?

Creative usually matters more than audience tweaks once targeting is reasonable. Real photos from the salon, with a specific service named in the headline, tend to give a clearer signal than generic stock imagery. Change one variable at a time, so that any shift in results can be traced to a single cause.

Pacing matters too. A fixed daily amount spreads spend across the month and gives the platform time to learn. Pausing and restarting a campaign resets that learning, so keep the campaign running and change only the creative when results slip.

Influencer content can supply real client footage for paid placements. A [local creator approach for hair salons](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-hair-salons-how-to-get-free-bookings-from-local-creator) shows how that content is sourced. Paid search covers people who already know the service they want, so compare [Google Local Service Ads](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) before committing the full budget to Meta.

## FAQ

### Do I need a booking system before running Meta Ads?

Not strictly, but without one you cannot easily see which clicks become appointments. A direct booking link or a booking platform integration makes it possible to measure cost per booking, which is the number that decides whether the campaign is worth running.

### Should a small salon boost posts or use Ads Manager?

Ads Manager offers control over audience, schedule, creative testing and conversion tracking. A boosted post offers far fewer of these controls, which makes it harder to tell why a campaign worked or failed.

### How long should a test run before judging results?

Two weeks is a practical minimum for comparing creatives at a $500 monthly budget, and four to six weeks gives a clearer view of cost per booking. Judge on bookings and repeat visits rather than early click numbers.

### Is $500 a month enough to learn from?

It is a workable starting point for one location. Below that level, the number of bookings may be too small to compare creatives reliably. The worked example above shows how few bookings a $500 budget can produce at typical assumed costs.
