## What Do the Sources Confirm About Reach?

Google's help page for Display campaigns says they can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail. That is display inventory, not podcast listening. It shows how wide a display network can go, but it says nothing about how many of those people live near a given shop.

WordStream's benchmark report, a secondary source first published in 2016 and updated since, gives averages across twenty industries. The all-industry averages are 0.46% click-through on the display network and $0.63 per click, compared with 3.17% and $2.69 on search. These are US-dollar figures from a US-focused benchmark. They are useful as a rough sense of relative cost, not as a price list for UK audio.

The honest conclusion is that a small UK business has no sourced number for how many local listeners a podcast ad will reach. Any estimate has to be labelled as an assumption and tested.

## How Should a UK Business Test Podcast Ads?

Start with a goal that can be counted. A test that aims for "awareness" cannot be judged against a budget. A test that aims for phone calls, a promo-code redemption or visits to one landing page can. Choose one goal before buying anything, and write it down so that the final review uses the same measure.

Next, decide the geography. If the business serves one town, a national audio campaign will spend most of its money outside the catchment. Ask the platform or show whether the placement can be limited to that area. If the answer is no, the test is still possible, but the cost per local customer will be higher, and the budget should reflect that.

Finally, give the test a fixed length. Several weeks is a sensible minimum for a small budget, because weekly listening habits and ad fatigue can distort early results. Compare the cost per customer at the end with what the same money does in search or on a Google Business Profile. Plays, downloads and impressions show exposure only, so they should sit in a report as context, not as the verdict.

## What Does a Test Budget Look Like?

The worked example below uses assumed inputs throughout. None of the figures come from a UK podcast price source. Replace them with the numbers from a platform quote before deciding anything.

| Input or result | Value | Label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test budget, six weeks | £300 | Assumption |
| Cost per thousand plays (CPM) | £15 | Assumption |
| Total plays bought | 20,000 | Calculated: £300 ÷ £15 × 1,000 |
| Share of plays heard in your town | 10% | Assumption |
| Local plays | 2,000 | Calculated |
| Share of local plays that redeem a code | 0.5% | Assumption |
| Customers from the test | 10 | Calculated |
| Cost per customer | £30 | Calculated: £300 ÷ 10 |

If a customer is worth £120 in gross profit to the business, the test returns £4 for every £1 spent under these assumptions. If the real CPM is double the assumed figure, the same budget buys half the plays and the cost per customer doubles. That sensitivity is why the platform quote matters more than the example itself.

## Which Local Businesses Should Try Podcast Ads?

Podcast advertising suits businesses that sell across a wide area or have a considered purchase, such as a multi-site gym, a salon group, a destination venue or a retailer with both online and physical sales. A single café with a small catchment is a poor fit for a medium that spreads spend across the country.

If the business serves one town, search ads, Google Business Profile activity and local social targeting are usually easier to measure and to control. Audio can still work as a supporting channel, but it should be judged against those options rather than assumed to beat them.

## How Should a Podcast Ad Be Labelled in the UK?

Paid promotion in audio has to be clearly identifiable as an advertisement. The sources behind this rewrite do not set out the exact wording or timing that UK regulators expect, so a recalled phrase should not be relied on. Check the current guidance from the Advertising Standards Authority before recording.

The practical step is to write the disclosure into the script from the start, not to add it after the host has improvised. Decide who reads it, where it sits in the segment and how the host will say it. Then test the script with someone who has not seen the brief and ask whether they could tell it was an ad.

Host-read sponsorships also raise a question of responsibility. If a show's host and the advertiser both have a stake in the message, agree on the approval process in writing before the first episode airs.

## What Is the Step-by-Step Test Checklist?

1. Write one measurable goal, such as calls, code redemptions or visits to a single landing page.
2. Confirm the service area and ask whether the placement can be limited to it.
3. Set a fixed budget and test length, and label the figures as assumptions until the platform quote arrives.
4. Create a unique code, short URL or phone number used only for this test.
5. Write the script with the disclosure built in, and check it against current ASA guidance.
6. Add a "how did you hear about us" question at point of sale or on the booking form.
7. Record weekly results, including cost per customer, not only plays.
8. At the end, decide whether the cost per customer beats the search or social benchmark. If it does not, stop.

## FAQ

### How many people listen to podcasts in the UK?
The sources behind this guide do not include a UK podcast listener figure, so none is quoted. Check current RAJAR or Ofcom published data before planning a national campaign.

### Can a UK business target one town with podcast ads?
The sources here do not confirm postcode or town-level targeting for podcasts. Ask the platform or show directly, and get the answer in writing before paying.

### Is a podcast ad a good replacement for Google Ads?
Not for a single-town business. Search and Maps capture people already looking for a service. Podcast ads create demand at a distance and are harder to measure. Audio works best as a test alongside search, not instead of it.

### How do I know if a podcast test worked?
Compare cost per customer with other channels, using a unique code or landing page for the test. Plays and downloads show exposure, not sales.
