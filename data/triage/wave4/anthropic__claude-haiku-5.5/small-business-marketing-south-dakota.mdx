## What Free Help Does the South Dakota SBDC Offer?

The SBDC describes its consulting as professional, confidential and at no cost to small businesses. Its listed services include start-up counseling, business planning, market research, marketing, small business financing programs, cash flow and management, and business expansion ([University of South Dakota, secondary](https://www.usd.edu/Academics/Colleges-and-Schools/beacom-school-of-business/Small-Business-Development-Center)). Offices are in Sioux Falls, Rapid City, Aberdeen, Watertown, Pierre and Yankton.

For a marketing project, the practical use is planning before spending. If a business cannot yet say what a booked job is worth, a planning session is a sensible place to define that number. A single-location business, such as a plumber, cafe or gym, can settle the job value first and then choose a platform based on that figure.

## What Do the SBDC Annual Report Figures Mean?

The homepage lists four program-level figures: 2,179 new clients, 1,381 jobs created or retained, 102 business starts and $282,868,254 in direct economic impact. The footnote states that the impact figure includes direct capital investment (cash, equity and loans), federal contracts won, new and retained sales, and grant awards.

The $282.9 million is therefore not a measure of marketing spend or revenue for one shop. The figures show that the program is active across the state. They are not a benchmark for the return on a single business's advertising.

## Where Else Can South Dakota Owners Get Help?

Ally Dakota's resources page lists the SBDC alongside the West River Business Service Center, the Rushmore Region Alliance and the U.S. Small Business Administration ([Ally Dakota, secondary](https://allydakota.com/resources/)). The page describes the Rushmore Region Alliance as providing consulting, marketing and other services to businesses in western South Dakota.

Because Ally's page is a lender's summary rather than an official service listing, confirm what each organisation offers directly with that organisation before relying on it for a marketing plan.

## How Do You Build a Test Budget for a Local Ad?

The example below follows a plumber in Rapid City. Every input is an assumption, not South Dakota or national data. After the first month, replace each value with actual figures and keep the structure the same.

| Input | Value (assumption) |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly test budget | $500 |
| Cost per click | $4.00 |
| Clicks | 125 ($500 divided by $4.00) |
| Booking rate from clicks | 8% |
| Bookings | 10 (125 times 8%) |
| Average value of a booked job | $150 |
| Revenue from bookings | $1,500 (10 times $150) |
| Return on spend | 3 times ($1,500 divided by $500) |

The break-even point is the cost per booking: $500 divided by 10 bookings is $50. If the average booked job were worth $40 instead of $150, each booking would cost $50 and earn $40, so the test would lose money. Work out the job value before setting the budget. Tracking jobs in a field-service tool such as [Jobber or ServiceTitan](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business) makes that calculation easier to repeat each month.

## How Should You Split Residents and Visitors?

Run separate campaigns for people who live in the service area and for people planning a trip. A cafe in a tourist town may need both, while a dentist in Sioux Falls usually needs only the first. Name the towns actually served rather than the whole state, so the budget does not spread into areas the business cannot reach.

Check the location setting each time a campaign is created. Review the search terms report every week and add irrelevant searches as negative keywords. This keeps the test focused on people who can book the service.

## What Should You Do This Month?

Work through these steps in order. Each one takes little money but makes the test easier to judge.

1. Book a free consultation with the nearest SBDC office and ask for help defining the value of a booked job.
2. Complete the Google Business Profile with accurate hours, categories and photos, and reply to every review.
3. Set a monthly test budget you can afford to lose, and a daily cap that stops the budget running out early.
4. Track every call, booking and job value in one place. A spreadsheet works without software, and [FreshBooks or QuickBooks](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business) can handle the invoicing side.
5. After the test period, scale only the channel whose cost per booking sits below the average job value.

If you also post to several social networks, compare scheduling tools such as [Hootsuite and Sprout Social](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) before adding paid social.

## FAQ

### Is the South Dakota SBDC really free?

The SBDC states that its consulting is professional, confidential and at no cost to small businesses. Confirm eligibility and any program terms with the nearest office.

### Where are the South Dakota SBDC offices?

The SBDC lists offices in Sioux Falls, Rapid City, Aberdeen, Watertown, Pierre and Yankton.

### How much should a South Dakota business spend on ads?

No South Dakota ad cost figure appears in the sources checked. Start with a capped test you can afford to lose, then scale only if the cost per booking is below the average job value.

### Does the economic impact figure apply to my business?

No. The $282,868,254 figure is program-level and includes capital investment, federal contracts, sales and grant awards, so it does not describe one business.
