## How Large Is CTV Growth Across Asia-Pacific?

Campaign Asia, which published a partner article written by the ad-tech firm StackAdapt, cites Pixalate data showing a 43% rise in open programmatic CTV ad spend in APAC in Q1 2024 compared with Q1 2023. The same article cites an eMarketer forecast of 167.8 million sub-OTT users added across APAC between 2023 and 2027, which it describes as 55% of global users. Both are secondary figures, and the article is vendor content, so its claims about reach and attention should be read as positioning.

The article also states that CTV is tracked by IP address and targets users at a household level. That is a useful reminder that a CTV campaign reaches households rather than individuals, so the audience a small business pays for is broader than the person who makes the purchase.

Korea is not broken out in these numbers. They show regional direction, meaning advertisers are shifting budget toward streaming screens, but they do not tell you what a Korean district audience costs. Do not use them as a Korean budget benchmark. ## Can a Small Business Run Video Ads on TV Screens Through Google?

Google's help page states that the Display Network reaches people across 35 million websites and apps, plus Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail. Display campaigns use AI across targeting, bidding and formats, and optimized targeting is the default setting. The page also notes that Display campaigns using Target CPA bidding can be set to pay for conversions instead of clicks. That makes Google a measurable starting point for a small test, since spend and conversions can be seen inside one account.

The help page does not list Korean inventory or a TV-screen device option. Confirm the device, placement and country settings in the account before launch. Set location targeting to your district, and exclude audiences you cannot serve or who fall outside your delivery area. ## How Do You Check Whether a Small CTV Test Pays?

A break-even check shows how many customers a test must win before its cost is covered. All inputs below are assumptions, because no Korean CPM was found in the sources reviewed. Replace them with the quote received.

| Input | Value | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test budget | $1,000 | Assumption |
| CPM | $10 | Assumption |
| Impressions | 1,000 / 10 x 1,000 = 100,000 | Calculated |
| Margin per new customer | $50 | Assumption |
| New customers to break even | 1,000 / 50 = 20 | Calculated |

At these inputs, the test must produce 20 new customers from 100,000 impressions, a conversion rate of 0.02%. If the quote arrives at a $25 CPM instead, the same $1,000 buys only 40,000 impressions. The customer target stays at 20, but the required rate rises to 0.05%, which is much harder to reach in a small district.

Run this calculation with a real margin figure before any call with a sales team. If the required customer count looks unrealistic for the local area, the test is too expensive at that CPM.

The best first choice is the category where margin per customer can be measured and where a visit or booking is easy to track.

## What Should a 30-Day Test Include?

A test should be small enough to stop without damage and clear enough to judge. Use the following sequence before launch:

1. Pick one offer and one district.
2. Get a written CPM and minimum spend from the platform or agency.
3. Calculate the break-even customer count using the real margin.
4. Set a stop rule: pause if spend reaches the agreed test budget without the target number of customers.
5. Track visits, bookings or offer-code redemptions, not views alone.
6. Review the results against the break-even figure, and repeat only if the test met it.

Write the stop rule into the order form or internal notes so it is not decided under pressure at the end of the month.
