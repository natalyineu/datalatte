Waze ads can put a local business in front of drivers near its location, but the route to buying them is less clear than older guides suggest. The only Google-owned source in this review, its Display campaign help page, says Display reaches people across 35 million websites and apps, and it points advertisers toward Performance Max or Search for other automated campaign types. A secondary vendor page describes a Waze Ads manager with budgets up to $30,000 per month, but it is undated. Check what your own Google Ads account offers before you budget for Waze.

## Does Waze advertising still work for local businesses?

The sources in this review do not agree on the current state of Waze advertising, and their ages differ. The DeepReach article describes Waze Ads as a self-serve ad manager that lets brands build local campaigns quickly, with a Waze Ads Starter option for smaller businesses ([DeepReach, secondary](https://www.deepreach.com/media/how-waze-boosts-local-visibility-for-businesses)). It is a vendor page with no publication date in the text, so its product details may be out of date.

Google's Display help page does not mention Waze at all. It describes Display campaigns, Smart Bidding and Performance Max as the automated options to consider ([Google Ads Help, primary](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)). That is the only primary-source guidance in this review.

Treat Waze as an unconfirmed channel until your account shows it. Do not buy a Waze-only package based on a retelling. Ask any provider for the current campaign type, the bidding goal and the exact places the ad would appear, in writing.

## What kinds of Waze ad formats did the vendor describe?

The DeepReach article lists four formats: pins, search, takeovers and arrows ([DeepReach, secondary](https://www.deepreach.com/media/how-waze-boosts-local-visibility-for-businesses)). A pin marks a point of sale and can show opening hours and special offers. Search places the company name in the in-app search results, with logo and address highlighted.

A takeover displays a banner to drivers who have been stopped for at least four seconds, and it can cover part of the screen. Arrows point nearby drivers toward an outlet they can tap to learn more.

These are the formats one secondary source describes, not a confirmed current product list. Before you plan around any of them, confirm in your account which placements exist and how each is billed.

## Which local businesses gain most from on-the-way ads?

The vendor article names restaurants, hotels, tourism and automotive businesses as the strongest fit, especially those with retail outlets or hospitality venues. Its argument is that drive-to-store traffic and holidaymakers are reachable through location-based ads ([DeepReach, secondary](https://www.deepreach.com/media/how-waze-boosts-local-visibility-for-businesses)).

The test is simpler than the list. A driver must be able to pull over, order or buy without planning ahead. Coffee shops, quick-service restaurants, fuel stations and gyms usually pass that test. Appointment-only services, and businesses in walkable centres where most customers do not drive, usually do not.

If you sell a service that requires a call and a quote, the better starting point is search or lead ads. The [Google Local Services Ads review](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) explains that route.

## How many visits does a location test need to break even?

No public price list for Waze ads appears in these sources, so no honest cost per visit can be quoted. What you can do is work out what a test must deliver before you spend. The example below uses invented inputs, labelled as assumptions, so you can swap in your own numbers.

| Step | Input or formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Test budget (assumption) | $15 a day for 28 days | $420 |
| Average spend per visit (assumption) | $7 | Input |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 60 percent | $4.20 per visit |
| Visits to break even | $420 divided by $4.20 | 100 visits |
| Visits per day | 100 divided by 28 | about 3.6 a day |
| With repeat visits (assumption: 1 in 3 customers returns once) | $4.20 plus ($4.20 divided by 3) | $5.60 per customer |
| Customers needed with repeats | $420 divided by $5.60 | 75 customers |
| Customers per day with repeats | 75 divided by 28 | about 2.7 a day |

If you cannot imagine about four extra customers a day from one location, the test will not pay back on the first visit alone. Then the real question is whether repeat visits cover the gap. Scale the budget to your own ticket size and margin before you commit.

## How should you measure location ad results?

Measurement is where small businesses most often lose the thread. An ad that shows on a driver's map does not tell you whether that driver stopped. Build at least three signals into the test before it starts.

A unique offer code that customers say at the counter gives you a direct redemption count. A dedicated phone number or tracking link, used only for this campaign, separates test calls from normal calls. A weekly comparison of calls and direction requests in your Google Business Profile insights shows whether the pattern shifts during the test.

Compare the test period with the four weeks before it started, using the same days of the week. Seasonal shifts can look like ad results if you skip this step.

## What should you do this week?

1. Confirm in your Google Ads account whether any location or store-visit campaign type is available to you, and which placements it lists. Do not rely on a vendor's description alone.
2. Keep your Google Business Profile address, hours and phone number exact, since location campaigns depend on that data.
3. Write one offer in a single sentence, such as a free add-on for a morning order.
4. Fill in the break-even table above with your own ticket size, margin and test budget.
5. Set reviews on day 14 and day 28, and compare redemptions, calls and direction requests with the four weeks before the test.
6. Match ad hours to your open hours, so you do not pay for impressions while the doors are closed.

## How do Waze ads compare with other local channels?

| Channel | Reaches | Best when |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Waze-style location placements | Drivers near your location | Customers can make an on-the-way stop |
| Google Ads search | People searching now | Customers already know what they want |
| Local Services Ads | People hiring a local professional | Service businesses that take calls and quotes |
| Performance Max | Several Google surfaces from one campaign | You want Google to choose placements within one budget |

Start with search and a complete Google Business Profile, then test a location campaign once the basics convert. ## FAQ

### Can small businesses still buy Waze ads?

One secondary source describes a self-serve Waze Ads manager and a Starter tier, but it is undated. Google's Display help page does not mention Waze. Confirm access in your own Google Ads account before planning spend.

### How much do Waze ads cost?

No price is published in the sources reviewed here. The largest option one secondary source mentions is up to $30,000 per month, which is a ceiling and not a typical cost. Set a fixed test budget and work out break-even visits first.

### Which businesses benefit most from location-based driver ads?

Businesses where a driver can stop without planning ahead, such as restaurants, cafes, fuel stations, automotive services and gyms, are the strongest fits. Appointment-only and walk-in city businesses usually fit less well.

### How do you track whether a driver visited your store?

Use a unique offer code, a dedicated phone number or tracking link, and weekly comparisons of Google Business Profile calls and direction requests against the four weeks before the test. Compare the same days of the week.

### Should I choose Waze ads or Local Services Ads?

They solve different problems. Local Services Ads reach people actively searching for a service provider. Location placements reach drivers on the move, which suits walk-in and impulse purchases.
