WhatsApp is the core channel for most local marketing in Brazil. A small business should build an opted-in contact list, send a few relevant broadcasts each month, and take bookings or orders in the same chat. Instagram and TikTok bring new people in, and WhatsApp turns them into repeat customers. Most reach figures for the platform are global, so each business should test its own list. Brazil's LGPD consent rules and native Pix payments also shape how the channel should be set up.

## Why does WhatsApp work better than other channels in Brazil?

The [Crescendo Lab guide](https://blog.cresclab.com/en/whatsapp-marketing) describes WhatsApp messages as more likely to be seen and acted on than emails or SMS, citing a reported open rate of about 98% and users spending up to 17 hours a month on the app. Those are secondary figures, and the underlying data is global rather than Brazil-specific. Use them as direction, not as a forecast for your city.

The more useful question for a local business is whether customers will read a message from you and reply. A salon, gym or restaurant can answer this with its own list. Send one offer to one segment, track replies and bookings for two weeks, then test a second offer against it. Your own response rates will matter more than any platform-wide average.

Crescendo Lab also suggests using WhatsApp as a lightweight CRM, with personalised messages that refer to a customer's past visits. For a Brazilian service business, that means noting which service each contact bought and writing to that segment only.

## What does a compliant WhatsApp broadcast need in Brazil?

Brazil's data law, the LGPD, expects a business to show that customers agreed to receive messages. [Message Central](https://www.messagecentral.com/blog/best-whatsapp-business-api-platform-brazil) lists exportable consent logs, data-subject rights management and an operator contract as platform requirements. Treat these as the minimum. A business should be able to show when and how each contact opted in, and remove anyone who asks.

1. Collect opt-ins at the point of sale, booking or in-store sign-up, and store the date and source with each contact.
2. Put a clear opt-out line in every broadcast, such as a reply word that removes the contact.
3. Segment by service (haircut, colour, membership) so each message matches what the customer bought.
4. Start with a low frequency, then reduce it if opt-outs or muted chats rise. No source here sets a Brazil benchmark.
5. Include a Pix QR code in the broadcast or chat so the customer can pay without leaving the conversation.

Pix support depends on the provider. Message Central states that Twilio's Pix integration is not native, which makes platform choice a payment question as well as a messaging question.

## How much can a Brazilian salon earn from one broadcast?

Take a salon in São Paulo with 800 opted-in WhatsApp contacts (assumption). At the reported 98% open rate (secondary), about 784 contacts open the message. Assume 3% of all contacts reply (assumption), which gives 24 replies. Assume a quarter of those replies become bookings (assumption), giving 6 bookings. At an average ticket of R$150 (assumption), one broadcast brings in about R$900 (assumption).

If the salon sends two broadcasts a month, that is about R$1,800 in attributable bookings (assumption-based) before costs. Subtract the staff time to write and send messages and any platform fee. The result depends entirely on the assumed reply and booking rates, so log them from the first campaign and replace the assumptions with real figures after 60 days.

## How do Instagram and TikTok fit alongside WhatsApp?

Use Instagram and TikTok to find new people, and WhatsApp to keep them. A post or short video can link to a WhatsApp opt-in page. Once a customer messages the business, move the relationship into the chat for booking confirmations, reminders and seasonal offers.

Yes, mainly in payments and data rules. Pix and LGPD push businesses toward platforms that handle messaging and payments together. ## FAQ

### Is WhatsApp marketing effective for small businesses in Brazil?

The available sources give global reach and open-rate figures, not Brazil-specific results. Test one segment for two weeks, track replies and bookings, and compare with a control group before scaling.

### Do I need consent before sending WhatsApp broadcasts in Brazil?

Yes. The LGPD expects a documented basis for using personal data. Keep consent records with dates and sources, offer an easy opt-out, and confirm your provider supplies consent logs and an operator contract.

### Which provider should a small business pick for Pix and WhatsApp?

Shortlist providers that state native Pix integration and Portuguese support, then request written per-message pricing and Meta onboarding timelines. Check vendor claims with a trial before committing.

### How often should a business send WhatsApp broadcasts?

No source here sets a Brazil benchmark. Start with a low frequency for one segment, then increase it only if replies and bookings hold up and opt-outs stay low.
