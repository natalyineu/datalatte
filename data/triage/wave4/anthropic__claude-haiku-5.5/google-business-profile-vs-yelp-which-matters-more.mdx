## Which Platform Do Consumers Use More?

Google still leads by a wide margin. BrightLocal reports that Google's share of consumer review use dipped from 83% in 2025 to 71% in 2026, yet Google remains far ahead of other platforms. The same survey says the average consumer uses six different review sites when choosing a business, so no single profile covers the whole picture.

The survey gives no separate 2026 reading for Yelp, so it is not possible to say how many consumers read Yelp reviews. The excerpt also does not state a country split. Treat the figures as a guide for your own market and confirm them with your own customers.

Consumers are also reading more. BrightLocal reports that 41% of consumers always read reviews in 2026, up from 29% the year before. A growing habit makes a complete, active Google profile more valuable, not less. The [Google Business Profile optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers the setup steps.

## Why Is Google's Share Falling?

BrightLocal links part of the decline to reach. Its SMB research finds that only 35% of small businesses have a Google Business Profile, which leaves many consumers unable to read or write Google reviews for the businesses they want to check.

Other sources are also growing. The survey reports rising use of video platforms, including YouTube, Instagram and TikTok, and names AI tools such as ChatGPT among the most common sources for local recommendations. It also reports that traditional review sites such as Tripadvisor, the Better Business Bureau and Healthgrades are seeing a resurgence.

A falling share is not a reason to skip Google. If a business has no profile, it has no presence in the place most consumers still check first. Claiming the profile comes before any other review platform.

## Does Yelp Matter for Your Category?

Yelp is worth checking, but it is rarely the first profile to build. Yelp's resource center says a free listing can be claimed, and it lists more than 20 free tools for the business page, along with paid options such as Yelp Advertising. The free listing is the part most small businesses need first.

The sources checked do not give Yelp advertising costs or results, so a budget should not be based on this article. If a business is considering paid Yelp placements, it should first track whether Yelp appears in how new customers say they found it. For paid options on Google, the [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads guide](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) compares the main choices before adding a third platform.

Restaurants and cafés may see more Yelp activity than other categories. The [coffee shop Google Business Profile tips](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips) show how one category sets up its profile in detail.

## What Does a Monthly Split Look Like?

The worked example below is an assumption. It shows how to weigh time across two profiles. It is not a benchmark, and the inputs should be replaced with real counts after 60 days.

| Input (assumption) | Google | Yelp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours per month | 3 | 1 |
| New reviews per month | 8 | 1 |
| Replies per month | 8 | 1 |
| Share of new customers who say they found you there | 30% | 5% |

If 40 new customers a month are asked how they found the business, those shares give about 12 from Google (40 × 30%) and 2 from Yelp (40 × 5%). Google takes three hours and produces most of the customers, so it gets most of the time. Yelp gets an hour to keep the listing correct and reply to the odd review.

Use this order to put the split into practice:

1. Complete the Google profile with category, hours, services and photos. Check that the address and phone number match the website.
2. Add a "How did you find us?" question to the booking form or till script, with Google, Yelp, Facebook and referral as options.
3. Claim the Yelp listing, correct the basic details and reply to any reviews there.
4. Review the counts after 60 days. Consider paid Yelp placements only if the counts show Yelp customers arriving.

## How Long Before Profile Changes Show Up?

Google's SEO Starter Guide says changes can take effect in a few hours or take several months. It advises waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change had a beneficial effect in search results. The guide also notes that not every change produces a visible impact.

For this reason, a 60-day tracking window is a sensible test for the counts above. A business that changes its profile should note the date, then compare the "How did you find us?" answers before and after. This turns a guess about the two platforms into a small, repeatable check.

## FAQ

### Is Google Business Profile or Yelp better for a small business?
For most, Google. BrightLocal's 2026 survey shows it is still the review source most consumers use, and it appears in Google Search. Yelp can matter in some categories, so check where your own customers found you.

### Do I need both profiles?
Claim both so the information is correct. Put most of the effort into Google first, then keep the Yelp listing accurate and reply to its reviews.

### Does the 71% figure apply to my country?
The excerpt of BrightLocal's survey does not state a country split. Treat the figure as a guide and confirm it with your own customers.

### How do I know if Yelp is worth paid advertising?
The sources checked give no Yelp advertising costs or results. Start with the free listing, track how new customers find the business for 60 days, and consider paid placements only if Yelp appears in those answers.

### How long before profile changes show up?
Google's guidance says changes can take a few hours to several months to show, and recommends waiting a few weeks before judging a change. Track counts over at least 60 days.
