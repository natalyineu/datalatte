## What does Google require from a coffee shop profile?

Google's guidelines say a business can create a Business Profile if it has a physical location customers can visit, or if it travels to customers. For a Lexington coffee shop, the profile should match the real storefront. The guidelines ask you to represent your business as it is consistently recognised in the real world, across signage, stationery, and other branding. They also ask that your address or service area be accurate and precise.

There should be only one profile per business. Google notes that duplicate profiles can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search. If you have a second location, it needs its own distinct profile, not a copy of the first.

Profiles that break these rules can be changed or removed. Check the guidelines before you edit, because a rejected edit slows down the updates you need most. Source: [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

## How should you name and categorise the business?

Your profile name must match the name on your sign and menus. Google's examples show that taglines, store codes, trademark symbols, phone numbers, URLs, and hours do not belong in the name. A 24-hour shop should enter its name plainly, such as "Shelly's Coffee," and then list "Open 24 hours" in the hours section.

Categories follow the same logic. Google asks you to choose the fewest categories it takes to describe your core business. For most Lexington coffee shops, that means a primary category of "Coffee shop." Add a second category such as "Café" only if it describes a core part of what you sell. Stacking overlapping categories makes the profile less clear, not more visible.

Source: [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

## What belongs in the description, photos, and posts?

The business description should explain your services, products, mission, and history. It should be honest and relevant. Google does not allow low-quality or distracting content, such as misspellings, gimmicky characters, or gibberish. It also does not allow descriptions that focus on promotions, prices, or sales. A description that reads "Best bagels in town for $5!" breaks the rule, so keep prices and discounts out of the description.

The description must not contain links of any type. Put your website address in the website field of the profile, not in the description text.

Photos and posts should stay genuine and tied to the business. Show the real interior, the drinks you serve, and the storefront. Posts should describe what customers can actually get and stay within the content rules. Source: [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

## How do reviews and citations fit in?

Consistency is the common thread. Your business name, address, and phone number should read the same on your profile, your website, and any directory listing. If the storefront says "Main St." and a directory says "Main Street," fix the directory so the record matches the real world. Google's guidance asks for the name to be consistent across storefront, website, and stationery.

Reviews are the customer voice on the profile. Ask customers for honest feedback about what they actually experienced, and reply with a plain, factual tone. Do not offer incentives in exchange for reviews, and do not write reviews for your own shop. Your replies should meet the same accuracy standard as the profile itself.

Before building new listings, audit the ones you already have. Fixing existing mismatches is usually more useful than adding more directories that repeat the same error. Source: [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

## How long before changes show up?

Google's guidance says changes take time to appear. Some take effect in a few hours, while others can take several months. In general, wait a few weeks before deciding whether a change worked. Not every change will show up as a visible result in search, so judge each change on its own timeline. Source: [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

Google's crawler generally views pages from a US location, which matters for a Lexington shop whose customers are local. To check how Google sees your website pages, use the URL Inspection Tool in Search Console. You can also search with the site: operator to confirm whether your pages are in the index. Source: [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

## Which website signals support the profile?

Your website should make it easy for Google and customers to understand what each page covers. Google recommends descriptive URLs, since parts of the URL can appear as breadcrumbs in search results. A path such as /coffee-beans or /espresso-bar tells both users and search engines more than a page named with a number. Source: [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

Google also says it should be able to see your page the same way a user does. If key content is hidden behind scripts or styles that Google cannot access, the page may not show up well. Check key pages with the URL Inspection Tool.

For structured data, Google documents a LocalBusiness type that describes a local business on the web. Use the type that fits your shop, and keep the details identical to your profile. Source: [LocalBusiness structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business). If you embed a map on your contact page, the [Google Maps embed guide](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) explains the setup.

## Should paid search sit alongside the profile?

Organic profile work and paid search do different jobs. The profile and website earn visibility over time and follow Google's rules, while paid ads buy placement during a campaign. A shop that wants to test paid visibility can read the [Google Ads guide for coffee shops](/blog/google-ads-for-coffee-shops-complete-2026-guide) before setting a budget. Either way, the profile name, address, and hours should match across both channels.

## FAQ

### Can a coffee shop in Lexington have more than one Google Business Profile?

Google's guidelines say there should be only one profile per business. A second location should have its own distinct profile with its own accurate address. Duplicate profiles for the same shop can cause display problems on Google Maps and Search. Source: [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

### Can I put my website link in the business description?

No. Google's guidelines say the business description must not display links of any type. Add your website in the website field of the profile instead. Source: [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

### How long should I wait after changing my profile?

Google says changes can take a few hours or several months to be reflected. In general, wait a few weeks before judging whether a change had a positive effect. Source: [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

### Should I include prices or specials in the business description?

No. Google's guidelines say the description should not focus on promotions, prices, or sales. Put offers in posts that follow the content rules, or on your website. Source: [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).
