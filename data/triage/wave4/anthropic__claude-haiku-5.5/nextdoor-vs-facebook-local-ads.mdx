Nextdoor and Facebook both let a local business target nearby customers, but they reach very different numbers of people. Pew Research's June 2026 survey found that 70% of US adults use Facebook, while 18% use Nextdoor. Reach alone does not decide the winner, though. The better platform for a coffee shop, salon, or fitness studio is the one that brings in enquiries at a lower cost per customer in your own area. The practical answer is to run a fixed, equal test on both and compare results.

## Which platform reaches more of your neighbours?

Facebook has the larger audience. Pew's 2026 fact sheet shows 70% of US adults say they ever use Facebook, and only YouTube (84%) is higher among the platforms it tracks. Nextdoor's share is smaller at 18%, but it has grown from 13% in 2021 according to the same series. [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) reports these as shares of all US adults, so they describe the general population rather than your local market.

That distinction matters for a neighbourhood business. A small share of a tightly drawn radius can still be the right customers. Check how many active users sit inside your service area before you judge either platform by its national numbers.

## How do the two platforms differ in what they target?

Nextdoor is built around neighbourhoods, so its ads reach people by where they live. Facebook targets by location as well, but its audience is spread across many interests and purposes. Your ad copy should change to match. Neighbourly, soft-sell messages tend to suit Nextdoor, while Facebook ads usually need a clearer offer and a reason to act now.

Google sits in a different category. Its Display Network reaches people across 35 million websites and apps, and Google's help pages describe Display campaigns as a way to reach people as they browse, not only when they search. Service businesses that need calls from people with an immediate problem often get more value from search-based ads, so treat Google as a separate option rather than a third social network.

## How should you run a fair test?

Meta's own guidance says to use the same budget for both versions of a test so the comparison is fair. Its help page also advises against informal testing, such as switching ad sets on and off by hand, because that can overlap audiences and produce unreliable results. [Meta's A/B testing guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669) explains the approach.

A workable setup for a neighbourhood business looks like this:

1. Choose one goal, such as booking calls or walk-in visits.
2. Set the same budget on each platform and fix the test window in advance.
3. Use separate ad copy written for each audience.
4. Track each enquiry to its source with a call tracking number or a source question on your booking form.
5. Compare cost per enquiry at the end, then move most of the budget to the winner.

If your business is in Australia, the same logic applies, and [local marketing guidance for Australian small businesses](/blog/local-marketing-australia-small-business-2026) covers how neighbourhood and search channels compare there.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I run the same ad on Nextdoor and Facebook?

You can, but the audiences respond to different tones. Write a separate version for each platform. Meta's testing guidance also recommends comparing versions with a fair, shared budget rather than running one copy and hoping it works on both.

### How do I test the two platforms fairly?

Give each platform the same budget, run both for the same number of days, and avoid turning ad sets on and off by hand. Track every enquiry to its source so you can compare cost per enquiry rather than clicks.

### Is Nextdoor worth it for a service business?

Pew's figures show Nextdoor reaches a smaller share of US adults than Facebook, so it is worth testing against a clear enquiry goal. For a service business that depends on people searching for an urgent need, a search-based channel is usually the stronger first step. [Google Ads management](/services/google-ads) can help set that up.

### How long should a test run?

The sources do not give a fixed number of days, so choose a window before you start and keep it. A 30-day test with a set budget gives you enough enquiries to compare, provided you count them consistently.
