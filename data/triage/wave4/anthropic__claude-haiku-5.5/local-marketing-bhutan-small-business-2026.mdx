## Why Start With Your Google Business Profile?

Your Google Business Profile is the first place many local customers look. Local Falcon states that [over 1 billion people use Google Maps every month](https://www.localfalcon.com/blog/effective-local-marketing-strategies-for-smbs), which makes the profile a digital storefront. Keep the business name, address and phone number identical everywhere they appear, post updates and photos regularly, and ask happy customers for reviews.

Search engines decide who appears in local results. [Lyfe Marketing explains](https://www.lyfemarketing.com/blog/local-marketing/) that they look at social media, other local websites and review sites to confirm a business is genuinely local. A Facebook page that matches the profile adds one more consistent signal, so the two should never contradict each other.

Facebook and Messenger support the profile rather than replace it. Use the same opening hours, address and booking link on both, so a customer who finds you on one channel can verify you on the other.

## How Much Traffic Arrives on Mobile?

Most of it, according to the sources. Lyfe Marketing reports that over 50% of website traffic now comes through mobile devices. The same guide, citing HubSpot, says 50% of people who visit a local business on mobile will visit the physical location within 24 hours. Both figures are secondary, so treat them as planning inputs rather than forecasts for Bhutan.

The practical consequence is that the site must load quickly and show the address, opening hours and a tap-to-call button on a phone screen. Lyfe Marketing also notes that each extra second of load time loses visitors, though it gives no figure for that claim. Keep navigation short, because too many choices confuse people.

## Which Channel Does What?

Each channel has a different job. The table below sets out a split that keeps the profile, the site and the messaging inbox consistent. It is a planning structure, not a benchmark.

| Channel | Set up first | Check every week | Main job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Name, address, phone, hours, photos | Reviews and new questions | Be found and verified |
| Website | Fast load, phone number, booking link | Mobile layout and contact clicks | Convert a visit into a booking |
| Facebook page | Matches the profile exactly | Post reach and comments | Show photos, offers and updates |
| Messenger | Greeting and same-day reply routine | Unanswered chats | Turn interest into a booking |
| Paid boost (later) | One offer with one audience | Cost per booking | Add reach only after the offer works |

## What Should You Post on Facebook and Messenger?

Use a short routine rather than a large content calendar. Local Falcon recommends posting regularly with photos, offers and updates, and it lists encouraging reviews as a core tactic. Its nine strategies also include local directories, local content and personalised email, so Facebook is one part of a wider plan.

1. Confirm the Google Business Profile details match the Facebook page exactly.
2. Post photos, a current offer or an update at a fixed rhythm, for example three times a week.
3. Reply to every Messenger message on the same day, so the customer does not go elsewhere.
4. Ask for a review after each completed booking, and send the review link in the reply.
5. Record the source of each enquiry, so you know which channel produced the booking.

## How Would a Four-Week Local Test Work?

All inputs below are assumptions for illustration, not benchmarks from the sources.

- Test budget per month (assumption): 300 local currency units
- Facebook posts a week (assumption): 3
- Messenger reply target (assumption): same day
- Average value of one booking (assumption): 60 local currency units
- Booking rate from Messenger conversations (assumption): 1 in 5

Four weeks of organic posting costs only staff time. If the paid boost produces 20 Messenger conversations, the booking rate gives 4 bookings. At 60 units each, that is 240 units of revenue against 300 units of spend, a loss of 60 units. The result is useful: the offer or the photos need work before the budget grows. Change one variable at a time and run the test for another four weeks.

## FAQ

### What is the most important first step for a local business?

Make the Google Business Profile accurate and complete. Local Falcon's guide lists keeping the name, address and phone number consistent as its first quick tip.

### How many people use Google Maps?

Local Falcon states that over 1 billion people use Google Maps every month. This is a vendor-reported global figure, not a count for Bhutan.

### Does mobile traffic matter for a small business website?

Yes. Lyfe Marketing reports that over 50% of website traffic comes through mobile devices, so the site must load fast and work on a phone.

### Should I spend on ads before organic posting?

Not necessarily. Test organic posts and replies first, measure the enquiries they generate, and add a paid boost only once you know which offer works.
