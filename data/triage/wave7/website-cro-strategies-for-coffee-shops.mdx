For a cafe, a conversion is usually a tap on directions, a call, an online order or a catering enquiry. Fix the top of the page first, then reviews, then the links that send people to your Google Business Profile. Adwave's guide lists a first-visit-to-loyal-customer rate of 20-40% when product and atmosphere are strong, so the first visit is worth getting right.

## What Should a Coffee Shop Website Show Above the Fold?

Above the fold is the content a visitor sees before scrolling. StackAdapt (secondary) describes it as the content immediately visible when a page first loads, so your address, today's hours and one main action belong on that first screen. Seven in 10 Canadians use smartphones to access the internet, according to Statista as cited by [Square's cafe marketing guide](https://squareup.com/ca/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/marketing-plan-for-a-coffee-shop) (secondary), so design the phone layout first.

StackAdapt gives "Learn More," "Shop Now" and "Sign Up" as examples of effective calls to action because they are short and to the point. A cafe can use similar labels such as Order ahead or Get directions. Keep one primary button and one secondary link, and move team photos and the origin story lower down the page.

Square's cafe guide also reports that Dilly Dally Coffee Cafe received its first online order within three minutes of going live on its Square Online store. That is one cafe's experience, not a benchmark, but it shows how quickly a working order path can start to earn.

## Do Reviews Change Whether Walk-Ins Convert?

Adwave states that cafes with under 4.4 stars or fewer than 30 Google reviews see meaningfully lower walk-in conversion. The page does not publish the size of that drop, so treat those thresholds as a planning target rather than a forecast. A visitor who finds a low rating or very few reviews may choose the next cafe on the map.

Per Diem (secondary) recommends answering common questions in your Business Profile Q&A section before customers ask, such as whether you offer free Wi-Fi, dairy-free options or dogs on the patio. Repeat those answers in a short homepage FAQ, so a visitor does not have to leave the site to decide. ## What Conversion Rate Should a Cafe Website Expect?

Cafe-specific conversion benchmarks are not reliably published, so use broad figures only as context. Outbrain (secondary), citing another source, says average website conversion rates across industries hover between 2 and 3%. Benchmark Email (secondary) cites a 2016 Business Insider e-commerce report saying first-time visitors generally max out at 3%.

Adwave defines conversion rate as the percentage of visitors who take the action you want, such as making a purchase or signing up. For a cafe, that action is a tap on directions, a call or an order. Count those taps each week before you change anything, then compare the same count after each fix. ## How Do You Connect the Site to Your Google Business Profile?

Adwave's phased plan puts Google Business Profile optimization, a review generation system, website conversion and tracking into a Foundation phase covering Months 1-2. Working on the profile and the site together makes sense because they share the same visitors. Use the same ordering or booking URL on both, so your tracking shows one clear path.

Adwave says basic local optimization is free but time-consuming, while professional local SEO services run $500-2,000 monthly. ## How Many Loyal Customers Does a $300 Ad Budget Buy?

This example uses assumptions for the inputs. The ranges they sit within come from Adwave.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly paid budget | $300 | Assumption |
| Cost per first visit | $10 | Assumption, inside Adwave's $5-$15 urban range |
| Share of first visitors who become loyal | 30% | Assumption, inside Adwave's 20-40% range |

The arithmetic is $300 divided by $10 gives 30 first visits. Thirty visits at 30% gives 9 loyal customers. Then $300 divided by 9 is about $33.33 per loyal customer, which sits inside Adwave's $25-$80 band.

| Loyal share (assumption) | Loyal customers | Cost per loyal customer |

The 20% and 40% rows match the ends of Adwave's range. With the ad price held constant, moving a visitor's conversion from 20% to 40% halves the cost per loyal customer. ## Which Metrics Should You Track After Each Change?

BoostFloral (secondary) recommends tracking click-through rate, conversion rate and return on ad spend in Google Analytics and Google Ads reports. Adwave's guide suggests click-through rate and conversion rate for campaigns built to drive a response. For a seasonal push, its KPI list adds revenue, order volume and average order value.

Change one element at a time, such as the order button or the review block, and compare equal periods before and after. That keeps the cause of any change clear. ## Should the Site Capture Email or Text Sign-Ups?

Square's Future of Commerce 2025 report, as cited by Square (secondary), says 65% of Canadians like businesses to communicate with them by email, and 39% appreciate text message updates. Those figures are for Canada, so check local preferences if you operate elsewhere. Put a short email sign-up near the order button, not in the footer, and explain what subscribers will receive.
