## What does Google use to choose the local pack for a hair salon?

The local pack rewards the businesses whose profiles Google can read most clearly. Adwave states that analysis of millions of profiles shows the primary category is the number one ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, though it does not name the source of that analysis ([Adwave, category guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones), secondary). Its 2025 benchmark reports that Google Business Profile signals represent 32% of local pack ranking weight ([Adwave, multi-location audit](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit), secondary).

A 2021 BrightLocal survey found 36% of SEOs ranked the Google Business Profile as the most important factor for the map pack ([Ahrefs, Local SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), secondary). Google's AI-driven answers draw on business profile data, website content, and reviews, according to a summary of the transition ([Adwave, Q&A guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a), secondary). Checking the live results is still the best test. Search your service in Philadelphia and note which categories the top three to five businesses use.

## Which Google Business Profile fields should a hair salon fill in first?

Google describes the Business Profile as [free to set up](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/), so cost is not a reason to delay. Start with the fields that must be accurate. Adwave advises making hours, phone number, and address 100% accurate ([Adwave, maps marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary).

Choose a primary category that matches what most of your bookings are for, then add secondary categories where they fit. Adwave's guide lets the top local results set the pattern for your category choice. The description allows 750 characters ([Adwave, beginner's guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary), so name your neighbourhood, core services, and what sets your stylists apart. Keep it readable.

If you cut hair at a home studio or travel to clients, Adwave notes that you can hide your street address and be designated a Service Area Business ([Adwave, beginner's guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary). Google also lets clients book from Search and Maps through Reserve with Google, which a vendor says works in under 30 seconds ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), secondary). For a deeper profile walkthrough, read [how AI tools can support Google Business Profile optimisation](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization).

## How often should a salon post, upload photos, and manage Q&A?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, saying a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls ([Adwave, maps marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Another Adwave guide suggests at least 10, so treat 15 as a target rather than a rule. Useful salon photos include colour results, stylists at work, the interior, and the reception area.

Adwave advises at least one new Google Post a week and describes posts as free mini-billboards on your profile in Search and Maps ([Adwave, maps marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Google reviews each post after publishing. Only posts with the Live status show publicly, and Pending or Not approved posts do not ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after each post.

Google Maps messages can also be handled from the Business Profile dashboard on desktop, not only in the mobile app ([Adwave, messaging guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on), secondary). Use that to answer questions quickly, and seed the Q&A with the questions clients ask most often.

## Which citations and NAP details have to match?

Adwave says your name, address, and phone number must be exactly the same on your website, your Google Business Profile, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places ([Adwave, voice search guide](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses), secondary). The same page treats small punctuation differences as mismatches. Its beginner guide says to match "St." versus "Street" as well ([Adwave, beginner's guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).

Apple Business Connect is worth checking too. Adwave reports that it has captured a 7% share of a maps-only comparison and is growing ([Adwave, Apple Business Connect](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), secondary). Adwave also says 11.1% of profiles are unclaimed, without naming a source, so search for your salon and claim the listing if it is still open ([Adwave, maps marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary).

Your website matters as well. A [Google Maps embed on your location page](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) gives the map and address a second place to confirm. If you run Google Ads, location data comes directly from Google Maps and Business Profile, and corrections made there flow into linked accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)).

## How many reviews does a salon need, and how should it ask?

A third-party analysis by Blogging Wizard of 2 million Google Business Profile pages found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack ([Adwave, review guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business), secondary). Treat 200 as a benchmark from that study, not a guaranteed threshold. Reviews are visible next to your profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)), so they also influence whether clients choose you.

Ask for reviews in person or by text after a visit, and keep the request short. Do not offer incentives that break platform rules. Read the [Denver salon review benchmarks](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026) to see how review counts compare across a different city, then set your own target based on your current count and your competitors.

## What is a worked example of map pack visibility worth?

The example below uses assumed inputs, so replace them with your own figures from Google Business Profile reporting.

Assumption: the salon receives 400 Business Profile views a month. Assumption: 10% of viewers call, request directions, or visit the website. Assumption: 30% of those actions turn into a booking. Assumption: the average ticket is $85.

Calculation: 400 views × 10% = 40 actions. 40 actions × 30% = 12 bookings. 12 bookings × $85 = $1,020 a month.

If a stronger profile lifted views to 600 with the same rates, the figure would rise to 18 bookings and $1,530 a month. That gap is the value of a profile change, and it sits alongside any paid spend. ## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a hair salon?

Google describes its Business Profile as a free profile for Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile, Canada](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). Setup and basic listing management do not require payment.

### How many photos should a salon upload?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, though another of its guides suggests at least 10 ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Aim for 15 and refresh the set with seasonal work.

### Why isn't my Google Business Profile post showing?

Google shows a post publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after publishing.

### Can a salon with a home studio hide its address?

Yes. Adwave says a business that works from home or serves customers at their location can hide its street address and be designated a Service Area Business ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).
