Denver pet groomers grow Google reviews by asking at pickup, replying to every review and keeping the profile current. Google shows reviews next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search, so each one is visible to owners choosing a groomer. BrightLocal's 2026 survey shows reviews are spread across several platforms, and its separate research found that only 35% of small businesses have a Google Business Profile. This guide explains where reviews fit, how to ask within the rules, and a 30-day plan you can run in under an hour a week.

## Why do reviews matter for Denver pet groomers?

Google states that reviews [show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122). That placement puts your feedback in front of an owner at the moment they decide where to book.

Reviews also live outside Google. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 34% of consumers wrote a review on Facebook, 24% on Yelp, 17% on Apple Maps, 16% on Tripadvisor and 16% on the Better Business Bureau ([BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)). The same survey reports Apple Maps usage for reviews nearly doubled from 14% in 2025 to 27% in 2026. A groomer's reviews may therefore sit on several sites, so check the ones your clients actually use.

Trust is the reason to care. A secondary retelling of BrightLocal data says 88% of consumers trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations ([EnvisionNow, secondary](https://envisionnow.com/resources/blog/tips-for-pet-groomers-to-boost-sales/)). Owners handing over a dog want that reassurance before they call.

## Which review platforms should a pet groomer watch?

Google and Yelp differ in ways that change how you should run requests. The table below uses only the facts these sources state.

| Question | Google Business Profile | Yelp |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to set up | Free, according to Google's own page | Check Yelp's current terms directly |
| Rules on asking | Google allows businesses to ask customers for genuine reviews and provides a direct review link | Yelp's guidelines explicitly prohibit offering incentives in exchange for reviews |
| Filtering risk | Not covered in this guide | A sudden spike in reviews, particularly after a marketing campaign, can trigger review filtering |
| Main source | [Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) | [Keepers Digital, secondary](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/) |

Google Business Profile is a free tool that lets businesses manage their presence across Google Search and Maps, which is why it should be your first priority. Yelp still matters for some owners, and the trade-offs are covered in more detail in our [Google Business Profile vs Yelp comparison](/blog/google-business-profile-vs-yelp-which-matters-more).

## How do you ask for reviews without breaking the rules?

Google allows businesses to ask customers for genuine reviews and provides a direct review link to share with them ([DMLY, secondary](https://dmly.io/google-business-profile-automation-guide/)). Requests can go out as a tracked link by WhatsApp, SMS or email, either by hand or automatically after an appointment or payment.

Keep the request honest. Do not offer a discount, a free add-on or any other reward in exchange for a review. Yelp's guidelines explicitly prohibit incentives, and the same caution applies on any platform. Ask every client at pickup, not only the ones you expect to be happy. Specific feedback helps future owners more than a generic five-star rating, so invite people to say what mattered to them rather than scripting the words.

Start with a single sentence at the counter, such as: "If you are happy with how they look, a Google review helps other owners find us." Then send one follow-up text with the link. Repeated reminders can irritate clients, so one message is enough.

## What should you do about an unfair review?

Google says you can report any review, but only reviews that violate Google policies are eligible for removal. It advises against reporting a review just because you disagree with it or dislike it, and review evaluation typically takes several days ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en-GB)).

If a review is unfair but does not break policy, reply rather than argue. Acknowledge the experience, explain what you will check, such as scheduling or handling of anxious pets, and invite the owner to talk offline. Future owners read your reply as much as the review itself, so keep it calm and specific.

## What does a review target look like in numbers?

The worked example below uses assumption-labelled inputs. It is a planning tool, not a forecast, and your real response rate will differ.

Inputs (all assumptions):

- Pickups per week: 40
- Share of owners who leave a review after being asked: 20%
- Starting total of Google reviews: 30
- Staff time per pickup to mention the review link: 30 seconds

Calculation:

- Reviews per week: 40 × 20% = 8
- Reviews over four weeks: 8 × 4 = 32
- Total after 30 days: 30 + 32 = 62
- Staff time per week: 40 × 30 seconds = 20 minutes

If only 10% of owners respond, the same inputs give 4 reviews a week, 16 over four weeks, and a total of 46. The gap between those two outcomes shows why consistent asking matters more than a single campaign.

## What should a 30-day review plan include?

Work through these steps. Each takes a few minutes once the profile is set up.

1. Verify your profile and confirm that the name, address and phone number match your signage and website.
2. Choose a primary category, add services with plain descriptions, and add a booking link or a phone number that is answered during opening hours.
3. Create your review link and print a QR code for the counter and grooming receipts.
4. Ask at pickup with one sentence, and send one follow-up text with the link to clients from the last 30 days.
5. Publish an update, such as holiday booking dates. Google says a post is shown publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, and Pending or Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)), so check the status after publishing.
6. Reply to every review from the last 60 days.
7. In week four, count new reviews against your starting total and check which steps you skipped.

## Can paid campaigns support a pet groomer's reviews?

Paid search can point more people to your profile, but it does not replace reviews. Google's help page on location assets states that data for location assets in Google Ads is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and that corrections made there flow into linked Google Ads accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). Keeping your profile accurate therefore helps your ads as well as your organic listing.

One vendor case study, secondary, describes a mobile pet groomer who advertised on Facebook and Google and reported a 134% increase in impressions and a 696% increase in clicks compared with the previous six months ([Marketing 360, secondary](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/)). That is one business, published in 2023, so treat it as an example of what is possible rather than an expected result. ## FAQ

### How many Google reviews should a pet groomer aim for?

No reliable benchmark for Denver pet groomers is available from these sources. Track your own count each month, ask consistently and compare your progress with your starting total rather than with another groomer's.

### Do reviews show on Google Maps?

Yes. Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)).

### Can I post updates on my profile?

You can, but a post is public only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not show on Search or Maps ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).

### Should I ask for reviews on Yelp too?

You can, but do not offer incentives, since Yelp's guidelines explicitly prohibit them. A sudden spike in reviews, particularly after a campaign, can also trigger filtering on Yelp ([Keepers Digital, secondary](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/)).
