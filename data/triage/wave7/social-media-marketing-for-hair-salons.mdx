A hair salon should start with one main platform and mirror its posts to a second. Every post should show finished work, a price range or starting price, opening hours and a clear booking link. Measure social media by bookings, not likes. [Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) says consumers aged 55 and older account for 30% of the salon market share and spend the most on hair care services, so the audience is not only younger clients. This plan covers platforms, post types, free scheduling tools, and a simple check on whether social media brings bookings.

## Which platforms should a hair salon use in 2026?

No source in this plan ranks platforms by salon results, so choose by where your clients are. Bookeo's figure for clients aged 55 and older suggests a Facebook Page can reach older regulars, while younger clients are more likely to be active on visual platforms. Check your booking records for client ages before deciding.

For paid social, [Bookeo's guide](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) advises targeting women aged 21 to 55 who live nearby and show interest in hair care and beauty. The same guide cites Douglas J. College's figure that 4.9 billion people use social media platforms worldwide, a broad number that says little about your own town.

Start with one platform you can post to every week. Add a second once the first is steady, and copy the same finished-work photos across both.

## What should a hair salon post on social media?

Post what a new client wants to see before booking: finished cuts and colour, prices or a starting price, the team, and how to book. Keep captions short and put the booking link in your profile and pinned post.

[Booksy recommends](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-marketing-ideas) educational clips of about 15 seconds, such as how to maintain a cool blonde at home or how to use a specific styling tool. These are easy to film during a normal appointment with the client's permission.

- Finished cut or colour photos, with the client's permission
- 15-second clips explaining at-home maintenance or a styling tool
- A pinned post with services, prices and the booking link
- Last-minute openings for the coming week

## How do you know social media is bringing bookings?

Count three things: bookings that name social media as the source, link clicks from your profile, and new followers who book. Add a "How did you hear about us?" question to your booking form, because platform analytics rarely show which post led to a chair being filled.

The example below uses assumptions, not published figures, so replace each input with your own numbers.

- Assumption: one stylist-owner spends 1 hour a week batching photos and captions.
- Assumption: that hour is valued at $30, so about $130 a month (4.33 weeks).
- Assumption: the average service earns $85.
- Break-even: $130 divided by $85 is about 1.5, so 2 extra bookings a month ($170) cover the time cost.

Decision rule: after 8 weeks, if fewer than 2 bookings a month name social media, change the content first (more finished work, a clearer booking link) before paying for ads. ## FAQ

### Which platform should a salon start with?

Start with the one your clients use. Bookeo says clients aged 55 and older hold 30% of salon market share, so check your own booking records before choosing between Instagram and Facebook.

### How often should a salon post?

None of the sources set a number. Choose a pace you can keep, such as three posts a week, and review the results after eight weeks.

### Is a paid scheduling tool necessary?

Not always. AdWave says Metricool has a real free plan, but check its limits for your number of accounts. Compare prices on each vendor's pricing page before buying a seat.

### Is an AI agent worth it for one salon?

Only if it saves time you would otherwise spend posting. Prices vary widely: Sintra's Soshie starts at $15.60 a month on its 12-month plan, while HubSpot's Breeze agent starts at $800 a month, according to the vendor comparison.
