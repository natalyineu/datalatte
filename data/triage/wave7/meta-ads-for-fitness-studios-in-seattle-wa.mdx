## What Does Meta Advertising Cost for a Seattle Fitness Studio?

Vendor guides give Meta a monthly range rather than a Seattle price. Adwave's channel comparison puts a standalone Meta (Facebook and Instagram) buy at $300–$1,000 per month, according to its [channel selection guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels). Its [fitness and wellness guidance](https://adwave.com/resources/connected-tv-vs-social-media-ads-comparison) recommends a 45–55% Meta share of total spend.

Cost is usually measured per thousand impressions (CPM) and per click (CPC). Adwave's [small business marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-small-business-guide) puts typical Meta costs at $5–$15 per thousand impressions and $1–$3 per click. Those are broad benchmarks, so replace them with your own account figures after the first few weeks.

Reach is large. Adwave's [Facebook advertising overview](https://adwave.com/resources/facebook-advertising-for-small-business), which cites Meta, says its apps reach an average of 3.56 billion daily active people. Scale is not the constraint for a single studio; relevance and offer are.

## Which Ad Formats Work Best for Fitness Studios?

Start with short video. Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, which says [short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners) than static images on Facebook and Instagram (secondary). Engagement is not the same as bookings, so judge video ads by trial sign-ups.

Competition for attention is high. A [Sprites page](https://www.sprites.ai/gym-static-ads) counts 746 static ads from 81 gyms and studios in the Meta Ad Library, with 705 running now (secondary). Filming real classes and instructors gives your ads a look that stock imagery does not.

Instagram and Facebook run through the same system. Adwave notes that [Instagram ads run through Meta Ads Manager](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business), and one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook, or both. For Stories-specific setups, see the [Instagram Stories ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/instagram-stories-ads-fitness-studios).

Instagram and Facebook also let businesses [add a Book Now button to their profile](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/) (secondary). Point that button at your trial page so the path from ad to booking stays short.

## Who Should You Target in Seattle?

Start with the area where current members already live. Export member postcodes from your booking system before you set a radius, then draw the targeting circle around your studio and widen it only if results justify it. Seattle is a large metro, so a tight radius is easier to test.

Use interest targeting that matches your class type. A yoga studio should target yoga and mindfulness interests, while a strength or HIIT gym should target strength training and fitness interests. A broad "fitness" interest mixes people who will never book your class type with people who will.

Custom audiences work better than interests once you have data. Upload past clients or website visitors, and build a lookalike audience from that list when Meta's help pages say the list is large enough. The [Los Angeles guide](/blog/meta-ads-for-fitness-studios-in-los-angeles-ca) covers another metro and shows how radius choices change in a dense market.

For a wider view of audience setup, the [complete Facebook ads guide for gyms and fitness studios](/blog/facebook-ads-for-gyms-fitness-studios-complete-guide) walks through campaign structure.

## Worked Example: How Far Does a $1,000 Budget Go?

The inputs below are assumptions, not sourced benchmarks. The Meta share is set inside Adwave's 45–55% range, and the CPM sits inside its $5–$15 range. Click, booking, and conversion rates are assumptions to replace with your own data.

| Step | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Total monthly ad budget | $1,000 | Assumption |
| Meta share | 50% = $500 | Assumption, within 45–55% |
| Impressions | 50,000 | $500 ÷ $10 CPM × 1,000 (CPM assumption) |
| Clicks | 350 | 0.7% CTR (assumption) |
| Cost per click | $1.43 | $500 ÷ 350, within $1–$3 range |
| Trial bookings | 35 | 10% of clicks (assumption) |
| New members | 7 | 20% of trials (assumption) |
| Cost per new member | $71 | $500 ÷ 7 |

The result shows why the offer and the follow-up matter as much as the ad. If your trial-to-member rate is 30% instead of 20%, the cost per new member falls to about $48 with the same spend. Recalculate with real numbers after the first campaign.

## Which Offers Work for Seattle-Area Studios?

Seattle-area directory listings show what local competitors put in front of readers. Be Yoga Burien advertises $50 for one month, Hot Yoga Renton $39 for one month, Shefayoga Roosevelt $59 for two weeks, Bouldering Project Poplar $73 for one month, and Bsod Nams Yoga $75 for a private class, according to the [Seattle Yoga News directory](https://seattleyoganews.com/yoga-studios/) (secondary). These are listed prices, not a benchmark for your studio, so check the live listings before using any of them.

Cold traffic rarely signs up for a long contract on the first click. A short, low-commitment trial suits paid ads better than a membership sign-up. Make the ad, the button, and the landing page all promise the same trial.

Keep the offer specific: the class, the duration, and what happens after the trial ends. Vague offers make it harder to tell which ad is working.

## How Should You Set Up Tracking Before Spending?

Work through these steps before the first ad goes live:

1. Install the Meta Pixel on your booking confirmation page and your contact or trial form page.
2. Set a Lead event that fires when someone submits a trial or intro booking form.
3. Add UTM parameters to every ad URL so your analytics tool records Meta traffic separately.
4. Report from Meta Ads Manager, which Adobe Express describes as a [centralized point to run ads across Instagram and Facebook](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising) (secondary).
5. Compare cost per booking after the first weeks, not impressions or reach.

Once bookings start coming in, a follow-up sequence helps trial members convert. ## FAQ

### Is there free training for Facebook and Instagram ads?

A [marketing guide for beginners](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/) describes Meta Blueprint as offering free Facebook and Instagram advertising courses with certification on completion (secondary). Check Meta's current course list before enrolling, since course availability can change.

### What budget should a studio start with?

Adwave's channel comparison puts a standalone Meta buy at [$300–$1,000 per month](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels). The $500 Meta share in the worked example sits inside that range. Choose an amount you can keep running for several weeks, since Adwave notes that Meta [takes weeks to optimize](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-small-business-2026).

### Do I need a Seattle-specific CPM to plan?

No. Use the $5–$15 CPM range as a first assumption, then replace it with your own account data once the campaign has run long enough to show a pattern.

### Can one campaign run on Facebook and Instagram?

Yes. Adwave states that [Instagram ads run through the same system as Facebook ads](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business), and one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook, or both.
