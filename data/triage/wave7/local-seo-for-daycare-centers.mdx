## What does Google use to rank a daycare in local results?

Google's own guidance on local ranking names three main inputs: relevance, distance, and prominence. [Google's local ranking tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) say local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity. Relevance is how well your listing matches what a parent typed, such as "daycare near me" or "infant care in Lincoln Park." Distance is measured from the searcher, so you cannot move your building closer. What you can control is making sure your location details are accurate.

Prominence is the lever most daycare owners overlook. Google defines it as how well known a business is, and says prominent places are more likely to show up in search results. It adds that prominence is also based on how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have.

Google also states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google. Any vendor who promises paid placement inside Google's local results is describing something Google says does not exist. Put that budget toward profile accuracy, reviews, and listing cleanup instead.

## Which Google Business Profile details should a daycare check first?

Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results. For a daycare, complete means exact hours, a phone number that gets answered during those hours, a service description that names the ages you serve, and photos of the actual space. Parents often search late in the evening or on weekends, so hours shown on your profile need to match what happens at the door.

Verification comes first. Google says verifying a business tells it the owner is authorized to represent that business, which makes it more likely to show up in search results. If your profile is unverified, complete that step before anything else on this list.

Choose a primary category that describes what you actually do. Open the category field in your profile and compare the options with the wording parents use in searches. Google changes category options over time, so check its live help page before you edit, rather than relying on a list copied from an old article.

## How do reviews and links affect a daycare's prominence?

Google says prominence is based partly on how many reviews a business has. Reviews also add relevance. When a parent writes about the outdoor play area, pick-up routine, or infant room, that language helps your listing match narrower searches. Ask families for a review in person or in a follow-up message, and never write reviews on their behalf.

Respond to every review, including the negative ones. Keep replies short, factual, and specific. Acknowledge the concern, state what changed if something did, and invite the parent to call. Parents reading your profile judge how you handle problems as much as the rating itself.

Links count toward prominence too. Google says prominence is based on how many websites link to a business. For a daycare, useful links often come from a parent association, a local school, a community calendar, or a park district page that mentions your program. Ask those organizations for a link that describes your business accurately. A link you request from a relevant local partner is worth more than a large batch of unrelated directory pages.

## Why do listing details need to match across the web?

A daycare's name, address, and phone number appear on many sites beyond Google. When those details disagree, parents who check two platforms before calling can end up confused, and the search engines that compare them have less confidence in which version is correct. Clean-up is slow work, but it is one of the few local SEO tasks with a clear, checkable result.

Use this checklist once a quarter, and again after any change to hours, staffing, or phone systems.

| Field | What to match | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| Business name | Same spelling and punctuation everywhere | Google Business Profile, your website footer |
| Address | Exact street format, suite number if used | Google, Apple Maps, Bing Places |
| Phone | One number that is answered during listed hours | All listings, click-to-call test on a phone |
| Hours | Holiday and summer schedule included | Google, Facebook, your homepage |
| Category | Matches what you offer, not a generic term | Google Business Profile |
| Website link | Points to the page you want parents to land on | Every listing |

Some listings will be duplicates or outdated. Mark closed or wrong entries as closed or request removal, then keep one correct record for each location.

## How much is a better Google profile worth? A worked example

The numbers below are assumptions for illustration, not measured results for any daycare. Replace them with your own enrollment data before you act on them.

| Input | Value (assumption) |
|---|---|
| Monthly calls from Google profile before fixes | 40 |
| Share of calls that book a tour | 25% |
| Share of tours that enroll | 30% |
| Monthly tuition per child | $1,000 |
| Calls after profile and review fixes | 60 |
| Your time for the audit, at $30/hour | 3 hours, $90 |

Baseline: 40 calls × 25% = 10 tours. 10 tours × 30% = 3 enrollments. 3 enrollments × $1,000 = $3,000 in new monthly tuition.

After fixes, assuming the same conversion rates: 60 calls × 25% = 15 tours. 15 tours × 30% = 4.5 enrollments on average. 4.5 × $1,000 = $4,500 per month, an increase of $1,500 in monthly tuition.

Against a one-time $90 time cost, the payback is fast if the call increase holds. The weak point is the call increase itself. Track calls from your profile each month so you can tell whether the change worked or the baseline was simply a slow month.

## Do voice searches change what a daycare should publish?

According to [Geotargetly's hyperlocal marketing guide](https://geotargetly.com/blog/what-is-hyperlocal-marketing), over half of consumers say they have used voice to search for things like nearby stores or business hours. Parents asking a phone assistant "is the daycare on Elm Street open now?" need a short, unambiguous answer that matches your listing.

Write hours in plain language on your profile and website, such as "Monday to Friday, 6:30 AM to 6:00 PM," rather than a compressed format that needs interpreting. Name the city and neighborhood in your service description so a spoken query matches your location. Keep the phone number on the page the same as the one on your listings.

If calls about hours and openings are piling up, an automated answering setup can handle routine questions so staff can focus on children. ## How can a daycare track local rankings without guessing?

Ahrefs describes its Rank Tracker as able to track up to 10,000 keyword rankings for regular organic search by country, state, city, and ZIP or postal code. A single daycare does not need that scale. Its value for a small operator is the location filter: you can check how your site appears for a phrase in your own ZIP code rather than nationwide.

Pick a short list of phrases that parents actually type, such as "daycare near me," "infant care [your neighborhood]," and "preschool [your city]." Check them monthly in the same location settings so the comparison stays fair. Also check the live Google Business Profile insights for calls, direction requests, and website clicks, since those show local activity directly.

When you change something on your site, such as a tour landing page, test one change at a time. ## FAQ

### Does a daycare need to pay to rank higher in Google's local results?

No. Google states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google. Improvements come from a complete and accurate profile, verification, reviews, and links from relevant websites. Paid ads appear separately and do not change your local ranking position.

### Does a daycare need to post on its Google Business Profile every week?

Google's local ranking guidance does not set a posting schedule, so there is no official number to follow. Regular updates help parents see that your hours, programs, and photos are current. Check the live help page for the features your profile offers before building a routine around them.

### Is mobile speed important for a daycare website?

Google switched to mobile-first indexing in late 2020, meaning it evaluates the mobile version of a website when determining search rankings, according to a [Grubhub summary of Google's guidance](https://get.grubhub.com/blog/restaurant-seo/). Test your key pages on a phone, make the phone number clickable, and keep the tour request simple. A parent who arrives on a slow page will often call a competitor instead.

### How often should a daycare audit its listings?

A quarterly check works for most centers, plus an immediate update after any change to hours, staff phone lines, or location. Use the table earlier in this article as a template, and keep a single document that records the correct version of every field.
