## What Units Do Chat Tools Charge By?

Chat software usually bills by one of four units: seats (each person who logs in), conversations (each chat thread counted against a quota), visitors (people reached by automation), or outcomes (issues an AI agent resolves). Two tools with similar headline prices can cost very different amounts once you know which unit applies.

Before comparing anything, write down the unit for each plan. A conversation and an AI outcome are not the same thing, so a quota of 50 conversations does not translate directly into 50 outcomes. Our guide on [AI agents versus chatbots](/blog/ai-agent-vs-chatbot-whats-the-difference-local-business) explains the difference in more detail.

Check whether the page separates monthly from annual billing. Many pricing pages show an annual-equivalent figure in large type and the monthly figure in small print, so confirm the cycle before you put a number in a budget.

## How Much Should a Small Business Expect to Spend?

An AdWave customer service article says small businesses can often run better support with existing tools or light software, usually from $0 to $150 per month if they add a shared inbox, chat or ticketing support (secondary source). That range is a useful ceiling for a first budget, although the article does not name Tidio or Intercom.

If your current inbox or booking software already receives website messages, the realistic cost of a new chat tool is the difference between what you pay now and what the new plan adds. A tool that costs nothing but takes a week to set up can still be expensive in staff time.

Chat tools also compete with other marketing spend. If your monthly budget is limited, compare the chat tool against spend on [local SEO](/blog/how-much-does-local-seo-cost-for-a-small-business-honest-pricing-guide) before committing to a multi-year plan.

## Worked Budget: 50 Chats a Month

All inputs below are **assumptions** chosen for illustration. Replace them with your own figures.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Chats received per month | 50 |
| Minutes to answer one chat by hand | 6 |
| Staff cost per hour | $25 |
| Share of chats an AI agent answers | 40% (20 chats) |
| Tool cost, upper end of range | $150 |

Scenario A has no AI and no new tool. The 50 chats take 300 minutes, or 5 hours, which costs $125 in staff time.

Scenario B uses the $150 tool and the AI agent handles 20 chats. The remaining 30 chats take 180 minutes, or 3 hours, costing $75 in staff time. The total is $75 plus $150, or $225 a month.

On these assumptions the tool costs more than it saves in staff time. The result changes if chat volume rises to around 200 a month, because the staff-time saving grows with volume while a flat plan cost does not. Run the same sum with the per-unit prices on the vendor page you are considering.

## How Do You Check Live Pricing Without Errors?

Use the same steps on each vendor's pricing page so the comparison stays fair.

1. Record the plan name, its billing unit (seat, conversation, visitor or outcome) and the quota included.
2. Note whether the price shown is monthly or annual, and what the toggle changes.
3. Find the AI add-on separately and record its quota and price.
4. Write down the trial length and whether a card is needed.
5. Check whether the contract is optional, and for which plans.
6. Multiply your monthly volume by the unit price, then add staff time from the worked budget above.
7. Repeat for the second vendor and compare totals, not headline prices.

Pricing pages change. Treat any figure you copy as dated, and check the live page on the day you buy.

## Why Verify Prices on More Than One Source?

Third-party review sites often disagree with vendors and with each other. A ToolSchool review of Grok lists X Premium+ at $16 per month in its pricing section, yet its FAQ gives the same plan as $40 per month (secondary source, as of December 23, 2025). Neither figure should go into a budget without checking the vendor's own page.

This is the reason the checklist above starts with the vendor page. Use reviews for context, and use the vendor for the number you will pay.

## Check What the Chat Widget Stores

A chat widget or booking tool can also set cookies, and those affect your privacy notice. WhatConverts lists the cookies on its own site. Its pscd cookie lasts 3 months and supports core functionality, visitor attribution and session continuity. Its ajs_group_id cookie, set by Segment, lasts 1 year and tracks visitor usage and events. Its _calendly_session cookie lasts 21 days and lets the Calendly meeting scheduler function on the site.

Those figures belong to WhatConverts and its embedded tools, not to Tidio or Intercom. The point for a local business is to ask each chat vendor which cookies its widget sets, how long they last, and whether your cookie banner needs to list them.

## Which Local Business Should Pick Which?

A single person answering a handful of chats a week usually needs a simple tool with a clear monthly cost, and the worked budget will show whether it is worth paying for. A team with several people sharing an inbox needs seat limits, roles and reporting, and the per-seat math matters more.

If your chats are mostly about opening hours, prices and booking links, an AI agent may pay off. The guide to [AI chatbots for booking and FAQs](/blog/ai-chatbot-for-local-business-booking-and-faqs) covers those use cases. If most of your chats are one-off complaints or custom quotes, a human reply is still the better tool, and the comparison on [AI chatbots versus live chat](/blog/ai-powered-chatbot-vs-live-chat-local-business) explains why.

## FAQ

### Is Tidio cheaper than Intercom?

This article does not state current prices, so it cannot give a verified answer. Run the worked budget using each vendor's live pricing page, with your own monthly chat volume and the billing unit each plan uses.

### How do I compare AI agent costs fairly?

Convert both vendors' prices to the same monthly volume. A conversation quota and a per-outcome charge measure different things, so compare total monthly cost at your expected volume rather than the unit price alone.

### Does a chat widget set cookies?

Often yes. WhatConverts lists cookies for its own site, including pscd for 3 months and ajs_group_id for 1 year. Ask each chat vendor which cookies its widget sets and update your cookie notice to match.

### Should a local business start with live chat?

Not always. If chats are rare, a clear contact form and an accurate Google Business Profile may answer most questions. Add chat once messages arrive often enough that staff time becomes a real cost.

### Can a free plan be enough?

It can be, if your monthly volume fits the plan's quota and one person handles the replies. Check the quota and the trial terms on the live page, then run the budget before you rely on it.
