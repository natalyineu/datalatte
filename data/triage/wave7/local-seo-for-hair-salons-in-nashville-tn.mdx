## How does the Nashville map pack work for hair salons?

The local pack is the block of three listings that appears above organic results when someone searches for a service in a place. Adwave states that the top three businesses shown in the map pack are pulled directly from their Google Business Profile listings ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business), secondary). That makes the profile the first asset to get right, before any website work.

Ranking weight is a debated topic. A BrightLocal survey cited by Ahrefs found 36% of SEOs in 2021 considered the Business Profile the most important ranking factor for the map pack, up from 23% in 2013 ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), secondary). That is a survey of opinion, not a measured weight, so treat it as direction rather than a formula.

Nashville competitor counts and local search volumes are not covered by the sources used here. Check your own market directly: search your main service plus "Nashville" in Google Maps, note the three businesses that appear, and open each profile to see what they show.

## Which Business Profile fields should a Nashville salon fix first?

Start with the category. Adwave recommends reviewing the top three to five businesses in local map results for a service search to see which category they use, and states that the primary category is the number one ranking factor for getting into the local pack, though it does not name the source of that analysis ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones), secondary). Compare your primary category with what the leading salons use.

Next, the description. Adwave's beginner guide notes that a Business Profile description allows 750 characters ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary). Use the space to name your services, your neighbourhood, and what makes your salon different, written for customers rather than search engines.

Then check your details. Google's own help material describes the Business Profile as free to set up ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). Adwave advises making your hours, phone number, and address 100% accurate, and notes that 11.1% of profiles are unclaimed, without naming a source ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). If you cut hair at a client's home or travel to customers, the same guide says you can hide your street address and be designated a Service Area Business.

Google Business Profile Q&A was launched in Maps in 2017 ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a), secondary). Seed it with the questions clients ask most, such as parking, walk-ins, and pricing for consultations. For more on AI-assisted profile work, see [AI for Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization).

## How many photos and posts keep a salon profile active?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, and says a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). A separate Adwave to-do list suggests at least 10, so 15 is the safer target. Good subjects for a salon include before-and-after colour work, stylists at the chair, reception, and the product wall.

Posts are the second habit. Adwave advises at least one new Google Post a week, and describes posts as free mini-billboards that appear on the profile in Search and Maps ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Only posts with a Live status are public. Google's help page says Pending and Not approved posts do not show on Search or Maps ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after you publish, especially for offers.

## How many reviews does a Nashville salon need?

The strongest review figure in these sources comes from a third-party analysis of 2 million Business Profile pages, which found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business), secondary). Most salons are nowhere near that number, so focus on steady growth rather than a one-off push. For a benchmark comparison in another US market, read [Denver hair salon Google Maps review benchmarks](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026).

Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). That makes the review text visible to clients before they choose a salon, so ask happy clients to describe the service they received. Reply to reviews in plain language, and avoid copying the same reply across every review.

## Which citations and booking links should a salon set up?

Citations are mentions of your name, address, and phone number on other sites. Adwave says the NAP must be exactly the same on your website, your Business Profile, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses), secondary). Check the small details too: one source warns that "St." versus "Street" counts as a mismatch ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).

Booking is the next step. Booksy states that Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search or Maps in under 30 seconds ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), secondary). For a salon, that shortens the path from a map result to a booked chair.

Apple Maps matters as well. Adwave reports that Apple Business Connect gives access to over 1.5 billion affluent iOS users, and that Apple Maps Actions let a business plug in booking services so customers can book without leaving the app. It also reports a 7% share for Apple Business Connect in a maps-only comparison, and that Apple Business Connect reports Profile Views ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), secondary). The same article states 88% of people use Google Maps and 12% use Apple Maps, but does not name a source, so treat those shares as unverified. Claim the Apple listing, and keep it identical to your Google details.

If you also run paid ads, note that Google's location asset data is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and corrections made there flow into linked Google Ads accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). For paid search basics, see [Google Ads for hair salons: complete guide](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide).

## What does a 60-day local SEO plan look like?

Use this sequence. It is a planning order, not a guaranteed timeline.

1. Week 1: Claim or verify the profile, confirm the primary category against the top three to five local results, and make the hours, phone, and address exact.
2. Week 2: Write the 750-character description and add services as service items.
3. Week 3: Upload at least 15 photos, including before-and-after work.
4. Week 4: Match name, address, and phone across your website, Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places.
5. Weeks 5 to 8: Publish a post every week, check each post shows as Live, and reply to every new review.
6. Ongoing: Record the six metrics in the tracking section below every month.

Adwave says a business may start ranking for its own name in the Map Pack a few weeks after verification, as a vendor estimate ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).

## How should a salon measure progress?

Adwave lists six local search metrics it calls the useful ones to track: Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift over time ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent), secondary). Record them in a spreadsheet monthly, and add Apple Business Connect Profile Views if you use it.

Rank checks are useful too. Search your top services in a private browser window and note where your listing appears. Track the same searches each week so you can separate real movement from day-to-day changes.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a hair salon?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up and says it helps businesses stand out on Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)).

### How many photos should a salon upload?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos. Its own to-do list suggests at least 10, so 15 gives you more room to rotate fresh images ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary).

### Can a salon take bookings directly from Google?

Booksy reports that Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search or Maps in under 30 seconds. Check which booking partners your salon software supports before you set it up ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), secondary).

### Can a mobile stylist hide their street address?

Yes, according to Adwave. A business that works from home or serves customers at their location can hide its street address and be designated a Service Area Business ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).
