To win back a no-show, send one short, non-judgmental email within 24 hours with a single link to rebook. Send one follow-up about 7 days later, then stop. The 24-hour and 7-day timings are a recommended default, not a tested best time. Published benchmarks for no-show emails are scarce, so this article uses general reactivation figures and salon retention data, and shows how to measure your own results.

## What Is a Win-Back Email for a No-Show?

It is a message to a customer who booked and did not arrive. Its job is to rebook, not to scold. Send it from a real person's name, name the service they booked, and give one link or reply option to pick a new time.

The structure is similar to abandoned-cart follow-up, where one timely message with a clear next step is the core of the sequence. See our [abandoned cart email strategy](/blog/abandoned-cart-email-strategy) for that model. Keep the no-show version even shorter, because the customer already knows they missed the visit.

## What Do the Benchmarks Say?

Cleverly states that roughly 45% of customers who receive a reactivation email open subsequent emails, and gives a 2–5% click-through benchmark for reactivation emails. Cleverly gives no source for the 45% figure, so treat it as a vendor claim. Mailchimp defines click rate as the share of successfully delivered emails that prompt subscribers to click and explore further, which is the number to compare against that range.

Lutily, citing Boulevard's Client Retention Report, says top-performing salons convert 70% of first visits into a second appointment, while average salons convert 45%. Those salon figures measure rebooking in general, not no-shows. They show what is at stake for a new client who misses, not how much an email will recover.

## When Should the Sequence Start and Stop?

Propello advises starting win-back campaigns 30 to 90 days after last customer activity, typically around day 45 for email, and sending 3 to 5 emails spaced every 3 to 5 days. That timing is for lapsed customers. A missed appointment needs a faster response, so the no-show sequence should start much sooner.

Propello also calls those counts starting lines rather than rules. A gym guide from Vertical Impression uses Day 7, Day 30 and Day 60 after sign-up for its retention emails, with a referral ask at Day 60. That is a retention schedule, not a no-show schedule, but it shows one way to space check-ins across two months. ## What Should the Three Emails Say?

| Email | When | Goal | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Within 24 hours of the missed visit | Rebook | "We missed you today." One link to pick a new time |
| 2 | About 7 days later | Gentle nudge | Same link, mention a booking policy if you have one |
| 3 | 30 to 60 days later (optional) | Reactivate | A seasonal reason to return |

Keep each email under 100 words. Do not guilt the customer, and do not offer a discount to everyone who misses, because that rewards no-shows. If you have a fee or deposit policy, state it plainly at booking and check your local rules before charging.

Sample text for email 1: "Hi [first name], we missed you at [time] today. Life happens. Here is a link to pick another time: [link]. If something about the booking did not work for you, just reply and tell us."

For a salon, the same template works with a stylist's name in place of the studio name. A broader look at salon messaging is in our [AI email marketing for hair salons](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-for-hair-salons) guide.

## Which Metrics Show Whether It Works?

Cleverly recommends tracking four core metrics: reactivation rate, revenue per reactivated customer, campaign ROI, and 90-day retention rate after reactivation. It calls 90-day retention the real north star, and says reactivated customers who churn again within two months are the warning sign to watch. Its 5–15% reactivation benchmark is labelled for B2B, so it does not transfer cleanly to a salon or studio.

Worked example. Every input is an assumption: 40 no-shows in a month, 40 first emails sent, 10 people rebook within 14 days (25%), an average service price of $60, and 2 staff hours at $25 per hour to set up and check the sequence. Revenue is 10 × $60 = $600. Cost is 2 × $25 = $50. Net gain is $550, and campaign ROI is $550 ÷ $50, or 11 times the cost. Then check whether those 10 clients are still booking at 90 days.

## When Should You Stop Emailing a No-Show?

Cleverly's tiering model assigns Tier 3 customers, those with low lifetime value who have been dormant 12 months or more, to light-touch email only or suppression. Applied to no-shows, a client who misses twice and does not rebook after two emails should move to light-touch contact, not keep receiving the full sequence.

Check whether your booking tool can trigger an email when an appointment is marked as a no-show. If it cannot, add a weekly manual step: pull the list of no-shows, send email 1 to the new ones, and send email 2 to those from the week before. A fixed weekly routine is easier to keep than an ad hoc one.

## FAQ

### How fast should I email after a no-show?

Within 24 hours is the recommended default in this article, chosen so the missed visit is still fresh. No published test confirms the best hour for your business.

### How many follow-up emails should I send?

Propello's 3 to 5 emails spaced every 3 to 5 days are industry averages, not rules. For no-shows, two messages is a sensible limit, with an optional third after 30 to 60 days.

### Do I need a no-show fee?

That is a policy choice. If you charge one, state it before booking and check your local rules before collecting it.

### What open rate should I expect?

Cleverly says roughly 45% of customers who receive a reactivation email open later emails, but it gives no source. Measure your own open and click rates before relying on that figure.
