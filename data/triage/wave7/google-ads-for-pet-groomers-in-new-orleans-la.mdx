## What Does a Dog Groomer Click Cost?

AdTurbo gives a benchmark of $2–$8 per click for dog groomer Google Ads and warns that a few wasted clicks add up fast. The same page says most dog groomer advertisers spend around $800–$5,000 a month. For a New Orleans salon, that range is only a starting point.

No New Orleans-specific figure is published in the material behind this guide. Check live bids and costs in your own Google Ads account before you set a monthly budget, and compare them against what a booked groom is worth to you.

## Which Keywords Should a New Orleans Groomer Bid On?

AdTurbo lists "dog groomer near me", "dog grooming near me" and "puppy grooming near me" as keywords worth bidding on. Add your city and the neighborhoods you serve, such as "dog grooming New Orleans" or "dog groomer Uptown", then test each one against the calls and bookings it produces. Narrow phrases tied to one service are easier to judge than broad ones.

Negative keywords matter as much as the positive list. GroomerGenie says its proprietary list contains more than 500 groomer negatives, which is a vendor claim worth checking against your own data. Build your own list from the search terms report, and add job seekers, school or course searches, and DIY or supply terms as they appear. Check the platform's live help page for how negatives match, since close variants can behave differently.

## How Wide Should the Radius Be?

Barklead suggests targeting users within a 10–15 mile radius, but that advice is written for Facebook ads, not Google. Use it as a test range only. Start with the distance your bookings actually come from, using your own appointment records.

If most clients live in residential areas such as Uptown or Mid-City, set the radius around those neighborhoods rather than the tourist core. Clicks from visitors who will never book a groom spend budget without adding clients. Review the search terms report each week and narrow the radius if distant clicks do not turn into calls.

## Does Demand Change Through the Year?

Adwave's pet grooming advertising guide, which quotes Groomer to Groomer, notes that November and December are traditionally a very busy time for groomers, sometimes too busy. If your calendar fills up in that stretch, a large budget then will buy clicks you cannot serve.

Plan spend around your capacity. Set a higher budget in quieter months if you need more new clients, and cap it in the busy weeks so you do not turn away bookings you already have.

## What Should a Groomer Count as a Conversion?

AdTurbo names booking a grooming appointment as the primary conversion to track. Make that the main goal in your account. Add calls to the salon as a secondary goal if the phone brings in a meaningful share of bookings, and choose a minimum call length that reflects a real inquiry.

Marketing 360's case study of a mobile pet groomer reports a 696% increase in clicks and a 682% increase in conversions compared with the previous six months. It dates from 2023 and gives no cost figures, so read it as direction, not return on spend. ## What Does a Month of Ads Cost? A Worked Example

Every input below is an **assumption**. The click price is the midpoint of AdTurbo's $2–$8 range, rounded to $5.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | Assumption | $600 |
| Cost per click | Assumption, midpoint of $2–$8 | $5 |
| Clicks | $600 / $5 | 120 |
| Booking rate | Assumption | 10% |
| Booking requests | 120 x 10% | 12 |
| Requests that arrive and pay | Assumption, 50% | 6 new clients |
| Cost per new client | $600 / 6 | $100 |
| Client revenue over a year | Assumption, 6 visits x $70 | $420 |

On these assumptions, a new client brings in $420 of revenue against a $100 acquisition cost. That ignores supplies and your time, so rerun it with your own visit price and repeat rate. Change one input at a time: at $8 a click, the same budget buys 75 clicks, and at the same rates the cost per new client rises to about $160.
