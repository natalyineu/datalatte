## How does the Google Maps pack choose which pet groomer appears?

[Adwave's map pack guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) states that the top three businesses in the map pack are pulled directly from their Google Business Profile listings. That makes the profile the first thing to fix. The same guide cites a figure that GBP accounts for 32% of Map Pack ranking potential, but it does not name a source, so treat that number as a rough guide.

None of the sources here give Google's own weighting. The practical question is which profile fields you control, and which of them a pet owner sees before booking.

Google describes its Business Profile as a free listing on Search and Maps, so setup costs nothing beyond your time ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). For a full walkthrough, see [Google Business Profile for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-for-pet-groomers).

## Which profile fields matter first for a Honolulu groomer?

Start with contact details. Adwave advises making hours, phone number and address "100% accurate" on the profile ([Adwave Google Maps marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers)). The same name, address and phone should appear on your website, your profile and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places ([Adwave voice search guide](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses)).

Next, the description. Adwave's beginner guide notes that a GBP description allows 750 characters. Use that space for the service, the Honolulu neighbourhoods you serve and what sets your grooming apart, such as handling anxious dogs or running a mobile van. Write for the pet owner reading it, not for a search engine.

Posts need a status check. Google says a post is public on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, and Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after each post you publish.

## Does category selection change map pack visibility?

[Adwave's category guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) says that analysis of millions of profiles shows the primary category is the #1 ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, though it does not name the analysis source. The same guide suggests checking the top three to five businesses in the local map results for your main service and noting which categories they use.

Pick one primary category that matches the service you book most often. Secondary categories should describe services you actually deliver, such as mobile grooming. Stacking categories to cover every search tends to make the profile less clear to both Google and the customer.

For paid channels, the Marketing 360 pet groomer case study describes a mobile groomer who chose Facebook and Google to advertise ([Marketing 360](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/)). If you are weighing those two, see [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for local business](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins).

## How many photos, posts and reviews are enough?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, and says a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers)). Its grooming to-do list sets a lower bar of 10 photos ([Adwave map pack guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). Aim for 15 and use the first 10 as your first-week target.

For posts, Adwave advises at least one new Google Post a week to keep the profile fresh. Posts appear on the profile in Search and Maps, so a grooming offer or a seasonal reminder can reach someone already viewing your listing.

Reviews matter in two ways. Google states that reviews appear next to your profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Adwave also cites Blogging Wizard's analysis of 2 million profile pages, which found businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. That is a correlation, so build review flow steadily rather than chasing a fixed count.

## Which citations are worth building in Honolulu?

Citations are listings of your name, address and phone number on other sites. Adwave names Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places as major directories where the details must match exactly ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses)). Its pages also show small differences that count as mismatches, such as "St." against "Street" and the comma before a suite number.

Apple Business Connect is another listing to claim. Adwave says it reaches users across Maps, Siri and Wallet, and that in a maps-only comparison it holds a 7% share and is growing ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring)). The audience figures in that article are not attributed to a source, so use them as a reason to claim the listing, not as a forecast.

Accuracy beats volume. Adwave cites one landscaping firm that cleaned up its NAP data and saw an 18% jump in local pack appearances. That is a single anecdote, but it fits the pattern: fix the listings you have before adding more.

## How should a home-based or mobile groomer handle its address?

Adwave's page says a business that works from home or serves customers at their location can hide its street address on the profile and be designated a Service Area Business ([Adwave map pack guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). Set the service area to the drive time you will actually cover, rather than a radius picked to look large.

Hiding the address does not change the need for consistency. The name and phone number still need to match the other listings exactly.

## How do you tell whether the map pack work is paying off?

Adwave lists six local search metrics worth tracking: GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP and branded search lift over time ([Adwave local search intent guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent)). Review them monthly in the same order each time so changes are easy to compare.

Messages sent to the profile can be handled from the GBP dashboard on a desktop, not only in the Maps app ([Adwave messaging guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on)). Someone answering booking questions from the office computer is less likely to miss them.

Google's AI-driven answers, according to Accrisoft's transition summary cited by Adwave, draw on business profile data, website content and reviews ([Adwave Q&A guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a)). Keep your website's service pages consistent with the profile so both sources tell the same story.

## FAQ

### Can a pet groomer in Honolulu rank in the map pack without a website?

The sources focus on the profile and citations rather than on a website. They do say a website's details must match the profile exactly ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses)), and that Google's AI answers draw on website content ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a)). A simple service page with the same details is the safer setup.

### Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as a free listing on Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). Your costs are time and any ads you choose to run.

### How many Google reviews do I need?

No source gives a single threshold you must reach. Blogging Wizard's analysis, cited by Adwave, found businesses with over 200 reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business)). Use that as a benchmark for where competitors sit, and focus on steady review flow in the meantime.

### Why isn't my new Google post showing?

Google shows a post publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Posts marked Pending or Not approved do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status in your dashboard after publishing.
