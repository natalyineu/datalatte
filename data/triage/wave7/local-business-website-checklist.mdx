A local business website needs six things done well: clear contact details, a page for each service, a fast mobile experience, proof such as reviews, markup that tells search engines who and where you are, and basic accessibility and legal pages. This local business website checklist has 30 items in those six groups. Each item is either backed by a cited figure or labelled as practical advice, so you can tell measured checks from judgement calls. Work through it on your phone. A first pass should take under an hour.

## What Must Visitors Find in the First Few Seconds?

Visitors arrive from a map listing or a search, and they want three answers fast: what you do, where you are, and how to reach you. A launch checklist for a café lists a Google Maps listing among its core digital presence items, which shows how central the map listing is to local discovery ([TGP International, secondary](https://www.tgpinternational.com/blog/the-ultimate-caf%C3%A9-opening-checklist-a-complete-pre-launch-guide)). If your site sits behind a map, check the [Google Maps embed setup](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) so the address on the page matches your listing.

Check each item below on your home page, on a phone.

1. Your business name and what you do, in plain words, at the top.
2. Your town or service area in the first screen.
3. A tap-to-call phone number.
4. Opening hours that match your Google Business Profile.
5. One clear main action, such as Book, Call or Get a quote.

## Does Your Site Work Well on a Phone?

Mobile is where most local visits happen. One checklist cites Studio Mesa for the claim that more than 60% of website traffic now comes from mobile devices ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/mobile-website-design-checklist-for-local-businesses)). The same source cites Mozello's finding that users are 61% more likely to return to sites with a good mobile experience.

Devcart's mobile checklist, also cited there, commonly recommends touch targets of at least 48px for buttons and links. Test that with your thumb, not with a mouse.

6. Same content on mobile and desktop.
7. Text readable without zooming, and buttons at least 48px for easy tapping.
8. No pop-up covering the phone number or the main button.
9. Forms that work with a phone keyboard and show the right input type.
10. Phone numbers and addresses as real text links, not images.

## How Fast Should the Site Load?

Adwave's redesign guidance says Google uses Core Web Vitals, including LCP, FID and CLS, as page-experience signals, citing a third-party claim from Mr Green Marketing ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/website-redesign-checklist-when-and-how-to-refresh-your-site)). The same page recommends testing components in tools like Lighthouse. Check the current metric thresholds on the platform's live help page, because these targets change over time.

Adwave's local SEO audit places its mobile performance check in the "Fix Now" priority group ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today)). Treat speed as urgent, not optional.

11. Largest content element loads quickly on mobile, checked in PageSpeed Insights or Search Console (practical advice).
12. Interactions such as taps and menu opens respond without visible lag (practical advice).
13. Layout does not jump while the page loads (practical advice).
14. Photos resized to the screen, not uploaded at camera size (practical advice).
15. No unused tracking scripts or chat widgets (practical advice).

## Does Google Understand Who and Where You Are?

Adwave's local SEO audit recommends keeping title tags between 50 and 60 characters ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today)). Each page needs its own title, so give every service page a title with the service and town, and keep it inside that range.

The structured data and metadata items below are practical advice. Keep the name, address and phone on your site identical to your Business Profile, because a mismatch confuses both visitors and search engines. A business that works through the [15 local SEO steps](/blog/how-to-improve-local-seo-for-your-small-business-15-steps-that-work-in-2026) will usually find these same mismatches in its audit.

16. A unique title for every page, with the service and town, within 50-60 characters.
17. A short meta description written for people, not a list of keywords.
18. One page per main service, not one page covering everything.
19. LocalBusiness markup with name, address, phone and hours (practical advice).
20. Name, address and phone identical to your Business Profile.

## Does the Site Give Reasons to Trust You?

Proof is the item most often missing from small business sites. Show reviews that are recent and real, and name the source. Never write or pad reviews. Photos of your own premises, team and finished work do more than stock images, and an About page that names who runs the business answers a question many customers have before they call.

Prices or a price range help people decide whether to contact you. If you cannot give a fixed price, explain what it depends on. Customers who want a budget for marketing work can read [what local SEO costs for a small business](/blog/how-much-does-local-seo-cost-for-a-small-business-honest-pricing-guide) before they ask.

21. Recent reviews with the source named.
22. Photos of your own premises, team or work.
23. Prices or a price range where you can give one.
24. An About page naming who runs the business.
25. A short FAQ answering the questions customers ask by phone.

## Is the Site Secure, Accessible and Legal?

Adwave's SSL checklist lists five checks to run this week to catch most SSL issues ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/website-security-basics-ssl)). Start there, then confirm that every page loads over HTTPS, with no mixed-content warnings.

Accessibility and privacy items below are practical advice. Check colour contrast, add alt text to images that carry meaning, and label every form field. Legal requirements depend on where your customers live. If you serve UK or EU visitors, review the [GDPR checklist for local business sites](/blog/local-business-website-gdpr-compliance) for consent and privacy page rules.

26. Text with enough contrast, and alt text on meaningful images.
27. Every form field has a visible label.
28. HTTPS on every page.
29. A privacy page and, where required, cookie consent.
30. A working contact form that sends to an inbox someone reads.

## Should You Keep Old Pages When You Rebuild?

If you are rebuilding rather than fixing, start with the history. Adwave says that for some SMBs, 90 days of website and traffic data is enough to set a performance baseline, but home services, healthcare, legal and retail sites usually need a longer look ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/website-redesign-checklist-when-and-how-to-refresh-your-site)). Record the baseline before you change anything.

The same page advises matching each old URL to the closest new destination, and avoiding sending everything to the homepage. Any page with traffic, rankings, links or conversion history should not be removed without a replacement plan. Redirect those pages first. ## FAQ

### What pages does a local business website need?

At minimum: home, one page per main service, about, contact and a privacy page. A pricing or FAQ page helps if customers regularly ask about cost or policies.

### How often should I review my site?

Check hours, phone and address whenever they change, and run the full checklist at least once a year. No source in this checklist sets a fixed schedule.

### Is a website still needed if I have a Business Profile?

A profile shows hours and reviews, but a site holds your services, prices and proof. Each one supports the other.

### Can I build the site myself?

Yes, if you keep the checklist in mind during the build. Test on a phone at every stage, and do not launch without checking the redirects for any old pages.
