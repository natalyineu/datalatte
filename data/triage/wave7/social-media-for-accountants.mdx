## Which social platform should an accountant start with?

Choose the platform where your existing referral sources already spend time. For many accountants that means LinkedIn, where other professionals check credentials and make introductions. If you serve local consumers, add a Facebook business page. Add anything else only after these two run without falling behind.

Social media does play a role in referrals. An AdWave roundup of referral statistics reports that social media accounts for 28% of referral shares, though it does not break that figure down by profession. For a local practice, [Nextdoor](/blog/hyperlocal-marketing-nextdoor-small-business) can also help you reach neighbourhood clients, so check whether your area's clients already use it.

## What should an accountant post each week?

Aim for posts that answer questions clients already ask. Three formats cover most needs. The first is a question from this week with identifying details removed. The second is a deadline reminder that links to the official source. The third is a plain-language explanation of one term, such as estimated payments or sales tax registration.

Keep each post short and limited to one idea. Avoid advice that depends on someone's personal situation, and end with an invitation to message you rather than a hard sell. A fixed rhythm matters more than volume. A [content calendar for small business](/blog/how-to-create-social-media-content-calendar-small-business) helps you plan a month in one sitting.

Tool costs can stay low. AdWave's scheduling roundup says Metricool offers a real free plan. Turrboo's free plan covers 2 channels and 30 posts a month. Sprout Social's Standard plan starts at $199 per seat per month, according to Sociolocal, which is usually more than a solo practice needs. The [Hootsuite and Sprout Social comparison](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) covers the trade-offs.

## How fast should you reply to social inquiries?

Response speed decides whether a comment becomes a call. AdWave advises assigning a small response pod with specific jobs for the first 24 to 72 hours after a campaign launches. It also recommends tracking First Response Time using the median rather than the average, because a few extreme outliers distort averages.

A solo accountant can run a smaller version of this. Name one person, even a part-time assistant, to check messages each working day. AdWave's guidance says a freelancer is often the better first social media hire when a business needs independent execution, cleaner accountability and faster progress. ## Should accountants run paid ads alongside posts?

Search ads are usually the main paid route for bookkeeping, payroll, CFO and audit work. ClickEm notes that general accounting and bookkeeping are not listed as their own category on Google's Local Services page, so keyword campaigns do most of the work. Start with about 60 negative keywords before adding plurals and misspellings, and review the search terms report weekly. In tax season, check it daily for the first two weeks.

Targeting can stay local. CPA Site Solutions suggests radius targeting around your office or specific zip codes where your ideal clients live. For a first test, AdWave says a few hundred dollars is usually enough to learn something meaningful. The benchmark cost per click for Finance and Insurance, the closest category ClickEm cites to accounting, is $3.39 in the 2026 WordStream and LocaliQ figures.

## What does a referral-first plan return? A worked example

Every input below is an **assumption** unless the table says otherwise. Use these numbers to test your own, not as benchmarks.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Referral-focused posts per month | 8 (assumption) |
| Time per post, including replies | 20 minutes (assumption) |
| Monthly time cost | About 2 hours 40 minutes |
| Inquiries from referrals per month | 8 (assumption) |
| Share of inquiries reaching a first consultation | 25%, low end of AdWave's 25-50% range |
| Share of consultations that become clients | 50% (assumption) |
| Annual fee per new client | $1,500 (assumption) |

Eight inquiries at 25% gives 2 consultations. At a 50% close rate, that is 1 new client a month, or 12 a year. At $1,500 each, first-year fees come to $18,000 for about 21 hours of posting time. If the consultation rate sits at the 50% end of the range, the same inputs give 4 consultations and 2 clients a month.

For a paid test, a $300 budget (assumption) at $3.39 per click buys about 88 clicks. Clicks are not clients, so compare the cost against consultations booked, not impressions. Ask every new client how they found you, and record the answer in your [marketing dashboard](/blog/how-to-create-marketing-dashboard-small-business).

## Which rules apply to accountants marketing to clients?

Texting is the riskiest channel. US Tech Automations' playbook for accounting firms says that under the TCPA you need prior express consent to text clients, and you should keep an auditable record of it. Build that record into your intake process before you send any messages.

Check your state rules and licensing body before you publish fees, testimonials or client stories. This article is not legal advice, and the rules for solicitation and advertising differ by jurisdiction.

## How do you build referral partners in your community?

AdWave advises identifying 8 to 15 centres of influence in your community, such as attorneys, insurance agents, real estate professionals and bankers. Make a short list, comment on their posts for a few weeks, and then send a useful note about a client question you answered. Referral relationships grow from repeated, low-pressure contact, not from a single request.

Pair this with a website and email list so that people who check your name after a recommendation find a clear next step.

## What mistakes should accountants avoid?

- Posting tax law updates nobody asked for.
- Running five platforms at once with half-kept profiles.
- Going quiet for two months and then posting daily.
- Ignoring replies and comments.
- Sharing client details, even disguised, without written permission.

If your current profiles are out of date, AdWave's audit guidance suggests spending the first two weeks on cleanup before adding new content. A [free audit](/free-audit) can show what needs fixing first.

## FAQ

### Is social media worth it for a solo accountant?

It can be, mainly for referrals and credibility. Keep the effort to a fixed weekly rhythm, and track where each new client came from. The worked example above shows how to test the numbers with your own assumptions.

### Which platform should I choose first?

Start with the platform where your referral sources already spend time. For most accountants that is LinkedIn, with a Facebook page added if you serve local consumers. Do not add more until those two run smoothly.

### Can I post client testimonials?

Check your licensing body's rules first, and get written permission from the client before publishing anything. Never imply guaranteed outcomes.

### How do I know if it is working?

Ask every new client how they found you and record the answer. Compare the consultations and clients from each channel against the hours or budget you spent on it.
