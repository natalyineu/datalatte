To show up in the Google Maps pack for a pet groomer in Denver, CO, your Google Business Profile (GBP) needs to be complete, consistent with the rest of the web, and active. The map pack is the [top three business listings in local search results](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today), and Google pulls those listings directly from their profiles. A GBP is free to set up, so the main cost is your time. This guide covers the profile fields to fix first, how photos, posts, citations and reviews feed into visibility, a worked budget example, and the metrics to track.

## How does the map pack choose which pet groomers to show?

The map pack shows three listings, and those listings are built from the business profile. Adwave's article states that in-depth analysis of millions of profiles shows the primary category is the number one ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, although the page does not name the analysis source. Treat category choice as a high-priority field rather than a formality.

A practical check is to search for the service you sell, look at the top three to five businesses on the map, and note which categories they use. [Adwave recommends this step](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) before you pick your own. Reviews also appear beside your profile in Maps and Search, [according to Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), so a strong ranking still depends on what searchers see when they reach your listing.

## Which Google Business Profile fields should a Denver groomer fix first?

Start with the fields that carry the most weight and are easiest to get wrong:

1. Primary category: choose the category that matches your core service, then confirm it against the top local competitors.
2. Description: [the description allows 750 characters](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Name the service, the neighbourhood you serve, and one differentiator in plain language.
3. Hours, phone and address: Adwave says these must be [100% accurate](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Check them after any seasonal schedule change.
4. Service area: if you work from home or travel to clients, you can [hide your street address](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) and be designated a Service Area Business.

The full setup process is covered in our [GBP optimisation guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers). Once the fields are right, move on to content, because an incomplete profile wastes the effort you put into photos and posts.

## How many photos and posts keep a grooming profile active?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos and says a robust gallery [gets far more clicks and calls](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). A separate Adwave beginner guide suggests a minimum of 10, so treat 10 as the floor and 15 as the target. Useful shots include the grooming station, before-and-after results taken with owner consent, and your mobile van if you run one.

Posts are the other ongoing task. Google describes them as [free mini-billboards](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) that appear on your profile in Search and Maps, and Adwave suggests at least one new post a week. Google reviews each post, and only posts with Live status are shown publicly, while pending or not-approved posts stay hidden. [Check the status](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en) after you publish.

## How consistent must your name, address and phone be across citations?

Adwave says your name, address and phone must be [exactly the same](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses) on your website, your GBP, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places. The beginner guide goes further and says the website must match the listing [right down to punctuation](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), including whether you write "St." or "Street".

Keep one master sheet with the exact string for each field, and copy from it every time you update a listing. Check Apple Business Connect as well. It reports Profile Views, which Apple defines as [how many people saw your business card in Apple Maps](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring). If your website shows a map, the [embed guide](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) explains how to keep that map tied to the same address.

## How do reviews change your map pack position?

Adwave reports an analysis by Blogging Wizard of 2 million GBP pages, which found that [businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business) to appear in the local pack. A small groomer may take years to reach that number, so the practical goal is steady review growth. For a sense of how review counts compare in another local niche, see our [Denver hair salon review benchmarks](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026).

Ask for a review after each appointment, and make the request easy with a direct link. Respond to every review. When it fits naturally, a response can mention the service the client booked, because the wording of reviews and replies is visible to searchers and to the platform.

## Does paid search make sense alongside local SEO?

Paid search and local SEO share the same profile data. Google states that [data for location assets in Google Ads comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), and corrections made there flow into linked accounts. A vendor case study of a mobile pet groomer describes choosing [Facebook and Google](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/) as its advertising channels. For a channel comparison, see our [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads guide](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins), and for choosing a manager, see [how to find a Google Ads manager](/blog/best-google-ads-manager-local-business-how-to-find).

The worked example below uses assumed inputs to show how profile traffic translates into bookings. Replace the assumptions with your own numbers.

| Step | Input or result | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly profile views | 800 | Assumption |
| Share who call | 5% (40 calls) | Assumption |
| Share of callers who book | 40% (16 bookings) | Assumption |
| Average booking value | $75 | Assumption |
| Monthly booking value from profile | $1,200 | 16 × $75 |
| Paid campaign cost | $600 per month | Assumption |
| Paid bookings | 10 | Assumption |
| Cost per paid booking | $60 | $600 ÷ 10 |

In this example, a $60 cost per booking sits below the $75 average value, so the campaign pays back on a per-booking basis. Change any assumption and the result changes, which is why you should measure your own call and booking rates before committing budget.

## How should you measure local SEO progress?

Adwave lists six useful local search metrics: [GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent) over time. Review them monthly in the GBP dashboard, and log each change you make, such as a new photo batch, a category edit or a posting schedule, next to the date. That log is what lets you tell which change moved which number.

Messages from customers can be handled from the [desktop dashboard](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on), not only the mobile app, so assign someone to answer them during business hours. If you use AI tools to draft profile content, review the [AI for GBP optimisation guide](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization) first, and check every claim against your real services and prices.

## FAQ

### Do I need a Google Business Profile to appear in the map pack?

The map pack is built from GBP listings, according to [Adwave's analysis of how the top three businesses are chosen](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business). A verified, complete profile is the starting point for local visibility.

### Does a Google Business Profile cost anything?

No. [Google describes its Business Profile as free](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) to set up on Search and Maps.

### How many photos should a pet groomer upload?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos. Another Adwave beginner guide suggests at least 10, so 10 is a reasonable minimum to start with.

### Can a groomer who works from home keep their address private?

Yes. [Adwave's beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) says you can hide your street address and be designated a Service Area Business if you work from home or serve customers at their location.
