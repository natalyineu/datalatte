Pet groomers can start Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) at a daily budget of $10 to $20, according to a [Facebook advertising guide written for groomers](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers) (secondary). Typical Meta costs run $5 to $15 per thousand impressions and $1 to $3 per click, per [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-small-business-guide) (secondary). Target dog and cat owners within 10 to 15 miles of your shop, run one campaign across both apps, and judge it on bookings rather than likes. No Anchorage-specific grooming benchmark is published, so the ranges below are a starting point to check against live estimates in Ads Manager.

## How much should a pet groomer spend on Meta ads each month?

The Barklead guide recommends starting with a daily budget of $10 to $20 and raising spend once you find high-performing ads. Over a 30-day month, that is $300 to $600. [Adwave's channel guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels) (secondary) says a standalone Meta buy often costs $300 to $1,000 per month, so a starting groomer budget sits at the lower end of that band.

Adwave's veterinary page says most veterinary practices can run effective campaigns for $300 to $1,000 per month ([Adwave veterinary advertising](https://adwave.com/resources/veterinary-advertising), secondary). Veterinary care is not grooming, so treat that range as a comparison point, not a benchmark. Your real spend depends on how many bookings you can take each week, and on whether each booking covers the ad cost.

## What does $450 a month actually buy?

The inputs below are assumptions, except the CPM and cost-per-click ranges, which come from Adwave.

Assumption: $15 a day for 30 days, so $450 a month.
Assumption: 5% of clicks become a booking.
Assumption: $80 average grooming ticket.

| Scenario | Impressions | Clicks | Bookings at 5% (assumption) | Cost per booking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low costs: $5 CPM, $1 CPC | 90,000 | 450 | 22 | about $20 |
| High costs: $15 CPM, $3 CPC | 30,000 | 150 | 8 | about $56 |

Each row is a separate scenario, not a sum. Booking counts are rounded. At an $80 ticket, $450 breaks even at about 6 bookings (assumption). The high-cost scenario still clears that line, while the low-cost one leaves room to test a second offer. If your real click-to-booking rate is lower than 5%, the break-even point moves out of reach quickly, which is why tracking matters more than the budget itself.

## Who should see the ads?

Barklead suggests targeting pet owners aged 25 to 60 who are likely to have disposable income, within 10 to 15 miles of the groomer. Treat these as starting settings. If cost per booking rises, narrow the radius before you cut the budget. A mobile groomer and a salon on one commercial street need different radii, so set the radius to match the area you actually serve.

Ads Manager runs both apps from one place. Adobe Express notes that Meta Ads Manager can be used to run ads across Instagram and Facebook ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising), secondary), and Adwave says one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook, or both ([Adwave, Instagram advertising for local business](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business), secondary). For a deeper comparison of the two placements, read [Instagram ads vs Facebook ads](/blog/instagram-ads-vs-facebook-ads).

## Which ad formats should pet groomers use?

Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, which says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram ([Adwave electrician advertising](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners), secondary). For a groomer, that means a short reel of a dog before and after the groom, filmed in your shop. Keep the footage real and unpolished. Planning short-form content is covered in [fitness studio marketing with Instagram Reels](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-instagram-reels), and the same planning applies to grooming.

Campaign goals matter too. A restaurant guide notes that Meta paid campaigns can be set up with goals such as traffic or reservations, across Facebook, Instagram, or both ([The Fork Manager](https://www.theforkmanager.com/en/blog/introduction-paid-ads-restaurants), secondary). For a groomer, a traffic goal pointing at your booking page is the closest match. One mobile groomer in a [Marketing 360 case study](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/) (secondary) chose Facebook and Google as its advertising channels.

## How should you set up the campaign, step by step?

1. Create one campaign in Meta Ads Manager and place it on both Instagram and Facebook.
2. Choose a traffic goal that sends people to your online booking page.
3. Set a starting daily budget of $10 to $20.
4. Target pet owners aged 25 to 60 within 10 to 15 miles.
5. Attach one clear offer. The Barklead sample copy reads: "Book today and get 10% off your first grooming session."
6. Run at least two creative versions, such as a reel and a still photo, so you can compare them.
7. Allow several weeks before judging. Adwave says Meta takes weeks to optimise.

If you want training before you spend, [Meta's Blueprint courses](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/) (secondary) are listed as free Facebook and Instagram advertising courses with certification on completion. Check Meta's own Blueprint site for the current course list.

## How do you tell whether the ads are bringing bookings?

Judge the campaign on bookings, not impressions. Confirm that your Meta Pixel fires on the booking confirmation page, and set a conversion event when someone submits the booking form. Add UTM tags to every ad link so your analytics can separate Meta visitors from other traffic.

Then divide spend by bookings to get cost per booking, and compare it with your average ticket, as in the worked example above. For past clients, retargeting is usually cheaper per impression: Adwave lists a typical Meta retargeting CPM of $5 to $15 ([Adwave retargeting ads](https://adwave.com/resources/retargeting-ads-small-business), secondary). Pair retargeting with [the pet groomer's guide to Instagram marketing](/blog/pet-groomers-guide-to-instagram-marketing) so that organic posts and paid ads point to the same booking page.

## FAQ

### How much should a pet groomer spend on Meta ads per month?

Start at $10 to $20 a day, which is about $300 to $600 over a month. Increase spend once you have identified ads that produce bookings.

### Should pet groomers run ads on Instagram or Facebook?

One campaign can run on both, since Instagram ads run through the same Meta Ads Manager system. Compare results by placement after several weeks of data.

### How long does it take for Meta ads to produce bookings?

Adwave says Meta takes weeks to optimise, so give a new campaign several weeks before you judge it.

### Is there free training for Meta ads?

Meta's Blueprint offers free Facebook and Instagram advertising courses with certification on completion, according to one training guide. Check Meta's own Blueprint site for the current list.
