Above the fold design is the layout of the part of a web page that people see before they scroll. For a local business, that top section should show what you do, where you do it, and the one action you want visitors to take, such as calling or booking. [StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/landing-page-conversion-rate) describes this as the content immediately visible when a page first loads. Attention concentrates in that area, so it is the first place to fix before a larger redesign.

## What counts as above the fold on a local business website?

The fold is not a fixed line. It depends on the screen. A laptop shows more of a page than a phone does, so the same homepage has a different fold on each device. Design for the smallest screen first and check the larger ones second.

For most local businesses, the top section needs five things: the business name, the main service, the town or service area, a phone number or booking button, and a short trust signal such as a rating. Not every item has to fit on screen. The test is simple: can a stranger tell within a few seconds what you offer and how to contact you?

Keep the hero to one headline, one line of supporting text, and one clear next step. Long paragraphs and large decorative images push the action below the first screen, where fewer people will find it.

## Why does the top of the page get so much attention?

According to a [summary of Nielsen Norman Group's fold research on Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/website-hero-section-design) (secondary), the 100 pixels immediately above the page fold were viewed 102% more than the 100 pixels immediately below it. That figure comes from an analysis of 57,453 eye-tracking fixations.

The same NN/g research, from 2010, found users spent 80.3% of their viewing time above the page fold and 19.7% below it. That study is more than a decade old, so treat the exact percentages as a starting point rather than a forecast for your own visitors. The direction still matters: people give most of their attention to the top of the page, so anything a customer needs in order to act should not depend on scrolling.

## Should your phone number and contact form sit above the fold?

Lyfe Marketing's [local marketing guide](https://www.lyfemarketing.com/blog/local-marketing/) (secondary) advises that forms should be above the fold on a local business landing page. For a plumber or dentist, a short form near the top can catch people who already know they need help and want to book now.

A form only works if people can complete it. [Adwave's mobile-first guide](https://adwave.com/resources/mobile-first-website-design) (secondary) notes that every field shows its label above the input in portrait orientation. Keep labels above the fields rather than inside them, because placeholder text disappears as soon as someone starts typing. Ask only for what you need to start a conversation, usually a name, a phone number, and a short request.

## How do mobile visitors change the top section?

A large share of local searches happen on phones, and the top section on a phone is much smaller. [Studio Mesa's small-business checklist](https://adwave.com/resources/mobile-website-design-checklist-for-local-businesses), cited by Adwave (secondary), states that more than 60% of website traffic now comes from mobile devices. That figure is third-party, so check your own analytics for the real split.

Tap targets also need room. Devcart Solutions' mobile checklist, cited on the same Adwave page, commonly recommends at least 48px for buttons and links so they are easy to tap. Mozello's website launch checklist, also cited there, says users are 61% more likely to return to sites that provide a good mobile experience.

A hero with a large image and a two-line headline can push the call button below the first phone screen. Check the mobile view before the desktop view, and move the call or booking button up if it sits too low.

## Where do reviews fit in the top section?

Adwave's guide on [repurposing customer reviews](https://adwave.com/resources/repurposing-customer-reviews-into-marketing-content) (secondary) says that placing reviews above the fold, in the top 300px of the viewport, can increase visibility by 60% and conversion by 25%. The page does not name a source for these figures, so treat them as unverified.

A short test works better than relying on any published number. Show a rating line in the hero for half of your visitors for a month, then compare booking or call rates with the other half. The [A/B testing guide for local businesses](/blog/a-b-testing-for-local-business) explains how to split traffic and read the results without drawing conclusions too early.

## Which call to action belongs in the top section?

Use one primary call to action in the top section. Match it to what a visitor is most likely to do next: call, book an appointment, or request a quote. A second button with a different goal competes with the first and tends to lower the rate at which people act.

Write the button label as the action itself. "Book a Visit" or "Call Now" tells a visitor exactly what happens next, while "Learn More" leaves them to guess. Use a colour that stands out from the background, and check that the text is readable on it.

If you take bookings online, the button can lead to a booking flow. The [AI agent for appointment booking guide](/blog/ai-agent-for-appointment-booking-local-business) covers how that works for small service businesses.

## What is a better hero section worth to a local business?

The figures below are assumptions, not sourced data. They show how to test whether a redesign pays off.

Assumed inputs: 2,000 homepage visitors per month, 3% of visitors book or call through the top section today, an average job value of $180, and a 10% relative improvement from a clearer top section.

Current bookings: 2,000 × 3% = 60 per month. Current revenue: 60 × $180 = $10,800 per month.

With the improvement: 60 × 1.10 = 66 bookings. Revenue: 66 × $180 = $11,880 per month.

Monthly gain: $1,080, or $12,960 per year. If a hero redesign costs $1,500 (assumption), it would take about 1.4 months to pay back at that gain. If the real lift is smaller, the payback period lengthens. Replace each assumption with your own numbers before deciding.

## How do you check your above the fold design?

Work through these steps in order, using the mobile view first:

- Load the homepage on a phone and note what is visible without scrolling.
- Confirm the business name, service, and location appear in the first screen.
- Check that the phone number or booking button is visible and tappable.
- Confirm buttons and links are large enough to tap comfortably; Devcart's checklist commonly suggests at least 48px.
- Open the contact form and check that every field has a label above it.
- Repeat the check on a laptop-sized screen.
- Run an accessibility audit. Adwave cites the 2025 Web Almanac reporting a median Lighthouse Accessibility score above 85 (secondary), a useful benchmark.
- Record a few real visits with a session recording tool and watch where people click or stop.

## FAQ

### Does above the fold design still matter if visitors scroll?

The NN/g figures show most viewing time in 2010 happened above the fold, but the sources used here do not measure current scroll behaviour. Check your analytics for scroll depth and confirm whether your key actions happen on the first screen.

### Do I need a video in the hero section?

The sources used here do not cover video in hero sections. Compare a static image with a video on your own traffic before paying for video production.

### Can I improve above the fold design without a designer?

Yes. Most of the changes in this article are about copy and layout: a clear headline, a visible phone number or booking button, larger tap targets, and labels above form fields. Start with the mobile view, since that is where the top section is most cramped.

### Does above the fold design affect SEO?

The sources used here do not address search rankings. Check the current help documentation for your website platform for guidance on how page layout affects search.
