Website maintenance for a small business usually costs $50 to $500 a month, according to [GoDaddy's 2026 cost guide](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/skills/website-maintenance-cost). The same guide puts simple personal blogs at $5 to $75 a month and ecommerce stores at $300 to $1,000. Most of that spend covers hosting, domain renewal, backups, updates and content edits. The costlier problem is usually a broken contact form that nobody notices. A brochure site sits near the bottom of the small-business range, and a site that takes bookings or sales sits higher.

This guide is for owners of cafés, salons, studios and groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Dollar figures are US-based; for pounds, Australian dollars or Canadian dollars, use local quotes in the same formulas.

## What Does Website Maintenance Cost Per Month?

GoDaddy's 2026 table lists average monthly maintenance costs of $5 to $75 for a personal website or blog, $50 to $500 for a small to medium business site, and $300 to $1,000 for an ecommerce store. Annual costs for small to medium business sites run $600 to $6,000.

A second vendor, [The CSS Agency](https://www.thecssagency.com/blog/website-maintenance-cost-and-packages), gives a similar band of $50 to $500 a month for a small business site, and $200 to $1,500 for a mid-size site. Treat these as ballpark figures from firms that sell maintenance, and ask for the scope in writing before you sign.

## What Do the Individual Parts Cost?

Elementor's [2026 maintenance breakdown](https://elementor.com/blog/website-maintenance-cost/) prices the components. A basic shared hosting plan might cost $2 to $15 a month, a Virtual Private Server $20 to $100 a month, and a domain renewal $15 to $60 a year. Automated backup services can cost $20 to $100 a month or more.

Hosting and domains are cheap. Backups and any paid care are where the bill grows. For a simple local site, the question is whether you need a paid backup service at all, or whether your host already provides one. Check that before you compare quotes, because two quotes that look similar may cover different jobs.

## Where Do Small Business Sites Actually Break?

Sites fail in predictable ways. [Visual Composer](https://visualcomposer.com/blog/how-to-create-a-404-page/) lists three common causes of a 404 error: a visitor mistyping a URL, a page that was deleted, moved or renamed without a redirect while old backlinks still point to it, and a site that is down because of overcapacity, maintenance or a crash.

The second and third causes are the maintenance problems. A renamed service page with no redirect quietly loses visitors who already found you. A crashed site produces no error you will see unless you are monitoring it. Both are cheap to prevent and expensive to discover late.

## What Should a Maintenance Plan Cover?

A plan worth paying for covers hosting, domain renewal, backups, software updates, uptime monitoring and content edits. Ask the provider which of these are included and which cost extra. Hourly edits on top of a plan are a common hidden cost, so check the price of a change to your opening hours or menu before you need it.

For a custom build, [Zeepalm's comparison of studio software](https://www.zeepalm.com/blog/mindbody-vs-glofox-vs-custom) describes the fixed monthly floor as hosting plus maintenance. Any custom or heavily customised site has that floor, and the plan should show it on the invoice.

## What Can You Skip at Small Scale?

Some spend is optional. Premium builder add-ons you do not use, plugins you installed once and forgot, and homemade booking widgets that a hosted tool could replace are all worth cutting. Every plugin is one more thing to update, so fewer moving parts lowers the cost of every other line.

Speed tuning does not always need paid tools. Adwave's [guide to fixing site speed for free](https://adwave.com/resources/website-speed-why-it-matters-and-how-to-fix-it-for-free) notes that for many small businesses a free CDN tier is enough to improve consistency without meaningful cost. SEO is a separate budget; see [how much local SEO costs for a small business](/blog/how-much-does-local-seo-cost-for-a-small-business-honest-pricing-guide) before bundling it into a maintenance plan.

## How Do You Run a 15-Minute Weekly Check?

Put this routine in the calendar every Monday:

1. Submit your own contact form and booking form, and confirm the email reaches your inbox rather than spam.
2. Open the site on your phone over mobile data and tap the call button.
3. Check that opening hours, prices and holiday notices are correct.
4. Glance at the uptime alert history and the latest backup log.
5. Compare last week's form submissions in your analytics with the enquiries you actually received. If forms show 12 submissions and you got 8, something is losing leads.

## Is a Paid Care Plan Worth It?

The table below compares three setups over a year. Every input is an assumption, kept inside the published ranges above so the scale is realistic. Your own time is valued at $30 an hour (assumption). Domain cost is spread across the year.

| Input or result | Lean DIY | DIY plus paid backups | Managed care plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hosting per month (assumption, within $2 to $15) | $10 | $10 | Included |
| Domain per month (assumption, $30 a year) | $2.50 | $2.50 | $2.50 |
| Backup service per month (assumption, within $20 to $100) | $0, host backups assumed | $50 | Included |
| Care plan per month (assumption, within $50 to $500) | None | None | $200 |
| Your time per month, valued at $30 an hour | $30 (1 hour) | $30 (1 hour) | $15 (0.5 hour) |
| Total per month | $42.50 | $92.50 | $217.50 |
| Total per year | $510 | $1,110 | $2,610 |

The cost of a failure is the other half of the ledger. Assume a salon gets 20 enquiries a month, a quarter of them book, and a first booking is worth $60. That is $300 a month in first bookings (all assumptions). A six-week silent outage costs about $450. A weekly check that catches the same fault in one week limits the loss to about $75.

On these numbers, the managed plan costs $2,100 more a year than lean DIY. It pays back only if it prevents roughly five outages of the size above. For a simple brochure site, that rarely happens. For a booking-driven site with higher first-booking value, the calculation shifts quickly, so run the same arithmetic with your own figures. ## What Should You Do This Week?

Start with a short, concrete audit of your current setup:

1. List every plugin, integration and embed on your site, and delete anything you do not use.
2. Confirm that you have an automated backup, and check how a restore works with your host or builder.
3. Submit your contact and booking forms and confirm the emails arrive. Repeat after any change.
4. Set up a free uptime monitor that emails a real inbox.
5. Write down what you pay each month for hosting, domain, tools and help. Compare that total with the table above.

## FAQ

### How much does website maintenance cost per month?

GoDaddy's 2026 guide lists $50 to $500 a month for small to medium business sites, $5 to $75 for personal blogs, and $300 to $1,000 for ecommerce stores. A simple local brochure site usually belongs near the bottom of the small-business band.

### Do hosting and domain costs add up quickly?

Usually not. Elementor's breakdown puts basic shared hosting at $2 to $15 a month and domain renewal at $15 to $60 a year. Backups and paid care cost far more than either.

### Is a paid care plan worth it for a simple site?

It depends on the cost of an outage. The worked example above shows a plan costing about $2,100 more a year than lean DIY. It pays back only if it prevents several outages of the size modelled, so run the numbers with your own enquiry and booking figures.

### What is the most common way a small business site loses visitors?

Pages that are deleted, moved or renamed without a redirect, and sites that go down without anyone noticing. Visual Composer lists both as common causes of 404 errors, so a free uptime monitor and a redirect check are the cheapest protection.
