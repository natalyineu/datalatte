To advertise a dog grooming business, start with free channels that build trust: a complete Google Business Profile, a steady flow of reviews and a booking link on every page. Then add one paid channel, usually a local search campaign, and test one offer at a time. Keep the first budget small enough to test for 60 days. Agency fees and ad spend are often billed separately, so compare the total monthly cost, not the headline fee. The ideas below run in the order worth trying them. ## Which free ways to advertise a grooming business work first?

Start with channels that cost time rather than money. Claim your Google Business Profile and fill every field: hours, services, photos and a phone number that someone actually answers. Gingr lists four customer types for pet grooming: busy professionals, families who spoil their pets, value-driven clients and parents who prioritise the best care. Each group responds to a different message, so write one line for each and use it in your profile and posts.

Ask for reviews every week rather than once a year, and reply to each one. Put a phone link and a booking button at the top of every page so a visitor can book in one tap.

Partner with vets, pet stores and trainers. Leave a stack of cards with a tracked QR code, because partners send customers who already need a groom. Referral offers also work when both sides get something. Gingr's sample campaign is "Refer a Friend, Get 15% Off", and small freebies such as nail trims or bandanas can reward the referrer. Track which codes produce bookings.

## What do paid ads cost, and what is billed separately?

Paid channels need a clear budget and a clear cost per booking. Read the cost structure before you sign anything. TailWerks states that its standalone Essentials plan does not include advertising, SEO, a website or AI phone answering, and that ad spend is paid separately to the platforms. Its Foundation package combines search advertising, local SEO, website work and tracking, while Growth adds Meta advertising, managed social and more content work.

TailWerks also bills hosting, software, phone, SMS, email, registration, AI usage and integration costs separately from its monthly fee. Add those lines together to get your real monthly number.

Search ads with a call button suit groomers because people searching for a groomer nearby are ready to act. Set a sensible radius, add call tracking and check the calls each week. Meta ads can follow once search works. Gingr's sample targets pet owners within 10 miles of the business or "dog lovers" interest audiences. Check any other ad platform's current pricing on its own page before committing.

## Which offline offers get dog owners to notice you?

Flyers and cards with a QR code only work when the code leads to a page with one specific offer. Zeely's salon example points a window-sign QR code to a landing page with a booking button and a small incentive, such as 10% off. A 10% first-visit offer is a reasonable starting test for a groomer, not a proven benchmark.

Give each channel its own code. Zeely's example sets an auto-DM story poll with a code like POLL15 for $15 off, so each channel has a code you can count. Local press can also bring in customers. See [how to get local press coverage for your small business](/blog/how-to-get-local-press-coverage-for-your-small-business) for story ideas such as a rescue fundraiser or a free groom for a shelter adoption.

Sponsoring a youth team or a rescue event puts your name in front of pet families. Give the sponsorship its own phone number or code so you can measure it.

## How do you keep clients booking again?

Repeat visits drive the profit in grooming. Gingr suggests rewarding regulars with a free groom after five visits or discounts on add-on services. Use a stamp card or a simple app count so clients can see their progress.

Cross-promotion with a pet store works well. Gingr's sample is "Buy a new collar, get 10% off your next groom." Ask the store to hand out the offer and use a unique code to track it.

Rebooking reminders are the cheapest retention tool. Send a message when a dog is due for its next groom, based on the interval you set. Email can carry these reminders, and [email marketing ideas for small businesses](/blog/email-marketing-ideas-for-small-businesses-20-campaigns-that-drive-revenue-2026) gives campaign formats you can adapt. Schedule seasonal offers, such as shedding season or holiday slots, two weeks ahead so the diary fills early.

## FAQ

### How much should a dog groomer spend on advertising?

The sources here do not publish a standard for groomers. Start with an amount you can test for 60 days, then judge it by cost per booking rather than cost per click. The worked example above shows how to set up that check.

### Who pays for ad spend if I use an agency?

It depends on the package. TailWerks states that ad spend is paid separately to the platforms and is not included in its management fee. Ask any agency for a written list of every monthly cost before you sign.

### Are social ads worth it for a groomer?

Test them with one clear offer and a defined local radius. Gingr's sample targets pet owners within 10 miles, but no published data here proves results for groomers, so track bookings from the ad.

### How do I track which flyer brought the booking?

Use a different QR code or booking link for each flyer and ask every caller how they found you. Unique codes per channel let you compare results without guessing.
