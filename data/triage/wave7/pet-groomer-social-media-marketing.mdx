A pet groomer should start with Facebook and Instagram, add Google Business Profile posts, and publish real work on a steady schedule instead of spreading thin across every platform. Pet owners are a reachable audience: 60% discover new pet care services through social media, a figure [Love Groomers](https://www.lovegroomers.com/blog/marketing-your-grooming-business-with-social-media/) attributes to Pet Business Magazine. [Barklead](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers) states Facebook has over 2.9 billion active users and suggests targeting people within 10 to 15 miles of your salon.

This guide covers platform choice, what to post, how to target nearby clients and what the time and money add up to. Most published figures come from vendors or general audiences, not grooming salons, so treat them as a starting point and measure your own results.

## Which Social Platforms Should a Pet Groomer Use?

Facebook and Instagram are the default pair for a local service business, mainly because of reach and ad targeting. Barklead suggests targeting pet owners aged 25 to 60 who are likely to have disposable income. Treat that age range as a hypothesis to test in your own ad results, not a verified profile of your clients.

Each channel has a different job, so match the effort to the payoff.

| Channel | Best use for a groomer | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facebook | Local ads, event posts, community pages | Low to medium |
| Instagram | Finished-groom photos and short reels | Medium |
| Google Business Profile | Offers, holiday hours and updates on your listing | Low |
| TikTok or YouTube | Short how-to video, only if you can film weekly | High |

Two channels, posted well, beat four posted once a month. Add video platforms later, once the routine holds.

## What Should a Groomer Post?

Use content only you can make. Finished grooms with the owner's permission, short breed advice, opening hours and holiday closures, and a price guide with "from" prices all work. Reviews quoted from public review sites add trust without extra effort. The [local business social media content creation guide](/blog/local-business-social-media-content-creation) has more formats you can adapt.

First impressions happen fast. CXL Institute research, cited by [EnvisionNow](https://envisionnow.com/resources/blog/tips-for-pet-groomers-to-boost-sales/) (secondary), says a potential client forms an impression of a pet grooming studio in 50 milliseconds. Use a clean, well-lit photo as your cover image on every profile.

Skip invented before-and-after images and stock photos. Customers notice them, and they damage the trust that brings bookings.

## How Do You Target Local Clients Online?

Paid social lets you aim at people near your salon. Barklead suggests a 10 to 15 mile radius, and [Gingr](https://www.gingrapp.com/blog/pet-grooming-advertising-ideas-to-grow-your-business) suggests targeting pet owners within 10 miles and dog-lover interests. A groomer's own 2016 example described customers living within about 12 miles, according to [Groomer to Groomer](https://www.groomertogroomer.com/reaching-more-customers-with-facebook-ads/).

Set the radius to a realistic drive time. A mobile groomer should usually use a tighter radius, because drive time cuts the number of dogs per day. [AA Center](https://www.aacenter.net/blog/how-much-to-charge-for-mobile-dog-grooming) notes mobile groomers usually do fewer dogs per day than a busy salon due to drive time, setup and one-on-one handling.

Barklead's sample ad offers 10% off a first grooming session. A clear first-visit offer gives new clients a reason to book now rather than later.

## How Much Time and Money Does It Take? A Worked Example

All inputs below are **assumptions** for a one-person salon, except the $10 daily starting budget, which comes from Barklead.

| Line | Monthly figure |
| --- | --- |
| Your time: 16 hours at $25 an hour (assumption) | $400 |
| Facebook ads: $10 a day for 30 days (assumption on days) | $300 |
| Total cost | $700 |
| Revenue: 6 new clients at a $70 first visit (assumption) | $420 |
| Net in month one | -$280 |

Time alone roughly breaks even, at $420 against $400. Adding ads lifts the cost to $700, so about 10 first visits at $70 would cover it. With a 10% first-visit discount, the price becomes $63 (assumption), and about 12 visits would cover the same spend. Repeat visits change the picture, so record where each booking came from before you decide to raise the budget.

## How Do You Plan Around Busy Months?

Groomer to Groomer, quoted by [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/pet-grooming-advertising) (secondary), notes that November and December are traditionally very busy for groomers, sometimes too busy. Plan posts for those months in advance rather than writing during the rush.

Capacity matters too. Vanspeed PRO describes an industry standard of 5 to 8 dogs per day for one groomer, and a typical mobile groomer works 20 to 22 days per month on a Monday to Friday schedule. If your diary is full, pause paid ads and post waitlist or booking-window reminders instead. A [content calendar](/blog/how-to-create-social-media-content-calendar-small-business) makes this easier to see weeks ahead.

## Are Google Business Profile Posts Social Media Too?

They work like social posts but sit on your Google listing, where people already search for a groomer. Use them for offers, holiday hours and event dates. Check whether your profile offers a booking button, and link it to your booking page if so.

Google changes its post rules and limits, so check its current Business Profile Help pages before you rely on any older summary. Keep posts accurate and current; stale hours or offers do more harm than having no post at all.

## How Do You Turn Posts Into Bookings?

Put one link in your bio that goes straight to booking or a short enquiry form. Tag that link with UTM parameters so your analytics shows which platform sent each visitor. Use the same source, medium and campaign naming every time, in lowercase, so the reports stay readable.

Reply to comments and messages within a day where you can, because pet owners often ask about prices and availability. Ask happy clients to leave a review and send a direct link. Avoid offering incentives for reviews, since that can break platform rules.

## Do You Need a Paid Scheduling Tool?

Not at first. AdWave's review of scheduling tools states that Metricool offers a real free plan, while Sendible's costs and feature access vary by plan, so check tiers before scaling. Paid enterprise-style tools can cost far more than a salon needs. [sociolocal](https://sociolocal.io/blog/8-best-multi-location-social-media-management-tools-2026-rankings) lists Sprout Social's Standard plan at $199 per seat per month. Compare options in the [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social guide](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) before you commit.

## Can AI Write Your Captions?

AI can draft captions in a few seconds, which cuts the writing step from your weekly hours. Check every draft before it goes live. Confirm prices, hours, holiday closures and breed advice against your own records, because a confident but wrong price is worse than no post. The [AI agent for social media posting guide](/blog/ai-agent-for-social-media-posting-local-business) explains a review workflow that keeps you in control.

## FAQ

### Which social media platform is best for pet groomers?
Facebook and Instagram together cover most starting needs. Facebook offers strong local ad targeting, and Instagram suits photos of finished grooms. Add Google Business Profile posts for your listing.

### How much should a groomer spend on Facebook ads?
Barklead suggests starting with $10 to $20 a day, then increasing spend once you identify high-performing ads. Start at the low end and track every booking's source before raising the budget.

### How often should a groomer post?
Pick a pace you can keep every week. A small set of consistent posts beats a burst that stops after two weeks. Plan extra posts ahead of November and December.

### Can I post customers' pets?
Ask the owner first, and only post a client's pet or family photo with clear permission.
