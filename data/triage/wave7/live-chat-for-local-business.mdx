Live chat for a local business can start at $0 if you already have a shared inbox or chat tool. Adwave reports that small businesses typically pay $0 to $150 a month for shared inbox, chat or ticketing support. The real constraint is speed. Customers expect a reply within 30 seconds, so add chat only if someone can answer during your opening hours. Otherwise, a contact form with a stated reply time is the safer choice.

This guide covers what live chat costs, how fast replies need to be, where the widget should sit, and a worked example you can adapt. Vendor pricing changes often, so check each provider's live pricing page before you commit.

## What Does Live Chat Cost a Small Business?

The subscription is only part of the cost. Adwave's customer service article says most small businesses can run better support with existing tools or light software, usually $0 to $150 a month once they add a shared inbox, chat or ticketing support. That range is a secondary benchmark, not a quote for any one product.

Plan limits matter more than the headline price. Vendors count conversations, seats, customer profiles and AI features differently, so a free plan can run out quickly once traffic grows. Read the definition of each limit before you rely on it.

Staff time is the other cost. Every chat takes minutes to answer, and those minutes come from someone's working day. The worked example later in this guide puts a number on that.

## How Fast Must Your Replies Be?

Replies need to be quick. Adwave's customer service benchmarks, which cite Ringly and Stealth Agents, put the customer expectation for live chat at under 30 seconds. A separate Adwave article citing HubSpot says live chat demands responses in under 48 seconds, compared with 30 minutes on Twitter and 6 hours on Facebook.

Those benchmarks are secondary and written for large support teams. Still, they show the gap between chat and other channels. A chat that gets answered ten minutes later works like a form with extra steps, and the visitor may already have left.

If nobody can reply in time, an [AI agent for customer service](/blog/ai-agent-for-customer-service-local-business) can handle first questions. Check what it can and cannot answer before you let it speak for your business.

## Should You Use Live Chat or a Contact Form?

Use live chat when a person can reply within minutes during opening hours. Use a form when they cannot. Many local businesses run both: chat during open hours and a form out of hours.

Whatever you choose, state the response time clearly. Adwave's guidance is blunt on this point: if replies come in a few minutes, say that instead of implying instant support. A visitor who sees "usually replies within 5 minutes" is less likely to feel misled than one who sees a chat box that never answers.

Squarespace shows the same approach in its own help centre. Its live chat support runs Monday to Friday, 4am to 8pm EDT, and is offered in English only. Publish your hours and language in the same way. For a deeper comparison of automated and human options, read our [chatbot vs live chat guide](/blog/ai-powered-chatbot-vs-live-chat-local-business).

## Where Should the Chat Widget Go?

Place the widget where the visitor is close to a decision. Adwave advises putting live chat on high-intent pages first, especially those tied to quote requests, bookings and service decisions. Its author recommends service pages, quote pages and checkout paths.

Avoid placing it on blog posts or pages with no next step. A widget there gets more clicks from curious readers and fewer enquiries from buyers.

Be careful with conversion benchmarks. One benchmark cited by a local SEO directory reports a 2% to 5% average lead-conversion rate for B2B websites. That figure is for B2B sites, not local service businesses, so do not use it as your target. Our [conversion rate benchmarks guide](/blog/ctr-conversion-rate-benchmarks-local-business) explains which numbers fit a local business.

## Does Live Chat Improve Call Handling?

Some vendors claim that chat reduces phone workload. Grammarly Business states that when integrated with other support technologies, live chat can improve call resolution by 18 percent and decrease call handling time by 14 percent. The page does not name the study behind those figures.

Treat this as a vendor claim. It may describe a large support operation with several tools connected, which is unlike a three-person shop with one inbox. If you already take many calls for simple questions, the useful test is whether chat reduces them in your own data.

To check, run the widget for a set period, count calls and chats, then compare with a matching period before launch. Our [A/B testing guide](/blog/a-b-testing-for-local-business) covers how to split tests cleanly when traffic is small.

## Is Live Chat Worth It? A Worked Example

All inputs below are assumptions for a service business with a small team, except the tool cost, which uses the top of Adwave's $0 to $150 range.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website visitors per month | 1,500 | Assumption |
| Visitors who start a chat | 2% (30 chats) | Assumption |
| Chats that become a booking | 20% (6 bookings) | Assumption |
| Gross profit per booking | $50 | Assumption |
| Monthly gross profit from chat | $300 | 6 × $50 |
| Staff time per chat | 10 minutes (5 hours total) | Assumption |
| Value of one staff hour | $20 | Assumption |
| Monthly staff time cost | $100 | 5 × $20 |
| Tool cost | $150 | Top of sourced range |
| Net monthly result | $50 | $300 − $100 − $150 |

On a $0 tool the net result is $200 a month. If replies arrive after hours and only 2 bookings follow, the same tool at $150 produces a loss of $150 a month: $100 gross profit, minus $100 staff time, minus $150 tool cost. Measure your real reply rate and booking rate before you decide.

## FAQ

### Is live chat free for small businesses?

It can be. Adwave reports that small businesses typically pay $0 to $150 a month for shared inbox, chat or ticketing support, and some setups use existing tools. Check each vendor's plan limits before you rely on a free tier.

### How fast should live chat replies be?

Customers expect a reply within 30 seconds, according to Adwave's customer service benchmarks. A separate Adwave article citing HubSpot puts the live chat response target under 48 seconds. If you cannot meet that, use a form with a stated reply time.

### Should I use live chat or a contact form?

Use live chat when someone can reply within minutes during opening hours. Use a form outside those hours. Many local businesses use both, and they state the reply time and hours on the page.

### Does live chat improve call resolution?

Grammarly Business claims live chat can improve call resolution by 18 percent when integrated with other support tools. The page does not name its source, so test the effect with your own call and chat counts.
