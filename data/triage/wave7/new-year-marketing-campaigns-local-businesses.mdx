New Year marketing works best when the push lands in the week your customers return to their normal routine, and when you plan for the second visit as much as the first. Winback Engine's vendor guide puts conversion for reactivated customers at 15-40%, against 1-3% for new customers. That gap is why January joiners deserve as much attention as the ads that bring them in. This guide covers a six-week plan, offers a small budget can carry, and a worked break-even example you can rerun with your own numbers.

## Why is January a better window for new customers?

January is one of the strongest windows for new customers. Adwave's guidance ranks the August-September routine-reset window as the year's second-best period, after January, for service businesses, education, fitness and healthcare ([Adwave, H1 2026 TV advertising review](https://adwave.com/resources/h1-2026-small-business-tv-advertising-review)). Adwave's TV guide also says post-holiday campaigns can drive January and February sales when acquisition costs often decline and competition decreases ([Adwave, TV advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/tv-advertising-ecommerce-guide)).

Do not assume the discount holds. A 2026 Wellhub survey reported by the Health & Fitness Association found that 82% of gym and studio owners said their customer acquisition costs had risen that year ([Health & Fitness Association](https://www.healthandfitness.org/86-of-gym-and-studio-owners-grew-membership-this-year-even-as-costs-and-competition-rise/)). Plan for rising costs, and make the first month pay its way.

## Is keeping a customer really cheaper than finding one?

Winback Engine says reactivation campaigns typically cost 5-10 times less per converted customer than new acquisition. Its comparison table puts cost per contact at $50-200 for new customers and $5-20 for reactivation. These are vendor figures, so use them as a direction to test rather than a benchmark for your market.

Adwave, citing an Invesp analysis, says acquiring a new customer can cost five times more than retaining an existing one ([Adwave, loyalty program guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-build-a-customer-loyalty-program-on-any-budget), secondary). The 90-day retention rate is the most important measure for reactivation, according to Cleverly ([Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/customer-reactivation-campaign-strategies), secondary), because customers who churn within two months have not really come back.

## What does a six-week New Year plan look like?

Plan backwards from the date your own customers return to routine, which is not necessarily January 1. If some of your customers also mark Lunar New Year, that is a separate calendar, and our [Chinese New Year marketing guide](/blog/chinese-new-year-marketing-local-business) covers it. Use the table below as a skeleton and fill in your own dates.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Early December | Set the offer, daily booking capacity and a spending cap. Update holiday hours on your Google Business Profile. |
| Mid December | Tell your list what is coming and give regulars first access. |
| Last week of December | Open January bookings. Sell gift cards if you take them. |
| Return-to-routine week | Main push by email, text, profile post and ads. |
| Two weeks later | Reminder to people who opened but did not book. |
| Weeks 4 to 6 | Welcome and rebooking sequence for every new customer. |

Holiday hours matter because customers searching for "open now" during the break will see whatever your profile says. Check Google's Business Profile help pages for the current steps.

## Which offer can a small budget carry?

Pick one offer with one action and one end date. Adwave's retention guidance suggests the first loyalty reward should be reachable within 30 to 90 days for many local businesses ([Adwave, retention strategies](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-retention-strategies-that-cost-less-than-new-acquisition)). A reward a customer can earn by their next or second visit keeps the offer tied to the behaviour you want.

Referral offers can also work. Brenton Way reports that Le Collage, a hair salon, offered 20% off haircuts and hair color through referral marketing, and that the approach improved retention and client acquisition ([Brenton Way](https://brentonway.com/blog/beauty-salon-marketing-ideas), secondary). Write the offer copy and welcome messages with help from our [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) list, then check every line against your real margin.

## Which channels fit a New Year budget?

Adwave's estimated customer acquisition costs for local small businesses are $50-200 for Google Ads search, $30-150 for Meta and Instagram ads, $10-50 for Local SEO and Google Business, and $100-300 for direct mail ([Adwave, customer lifetime value guide](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-lifetime-value-small-business)). These are estimates, and your category will move them. Google Ads search suits high-intent buyers actively searching, while Meta suits awareness and retargeting.

Measure every channel the same way. Adwave defines customer acquisition cost as total advertising costs divided by new customers acquired through the campaign ([RMAA Agency](https://russia-promo.com/digital-marketing/paid-search/yandex), secondary). If the number is above what a new customer is worth to you, the channel is a loss no matter how good the creative looks.

## How do you tell whether the campaign paid off?

Adwave recommends a lifetime value to acquisition cost ratio of 3:1 for most businesses and a payback period under 12 months ([Adwave, CAC calculation](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-acquisition-cost-calculation)). It also suggests calculating CAC monthly or quarterly for most small and mid-sized businesses. Recalculate customer lifetime value at least once a year.

The example below is hypothetical. Every input is an assumption, so swap in your own figures.

- Campaign spend: $1,000 (assumption).
- New customers from the campaign: 20 (assumption), so CAC is $50.
- Profit on the first visit after a $20 intro discount: $15 per customer (assumption).
- Profit per repeat visit: $30 (assumption).
- Repeat visits per returning customer in the following six months: 2, 3 or 4 (assumption).

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| New customers (assumption) | 20 | 20 | 20 |
| Cost per new customer (calculated) | $50 | $50 | $50 |
| First-visit profit at $15 each | $300 | $300 | $300 |
| First-visit result after $1,000 spend | -$700 | -$700 | -$700 |
| Share who return (assumption) | 20% | 40% | 60% |
| Extra visits per returner (assumption) | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Repeat profit at $30 per visit | $240 | $720 | $1,440 |
| Net result | -$460 | +$20 | +$740 |

Read the table as a bet on the second visit. Every scenario loses money on the first visit, so the outcome depends on how many customers return. At three extra visits each, break-even needs about 39% of new customers to return (700 divided by 20 customers x 3 visits x $30). The base case clears break-even, but its roughly $51 of profit per customer is well short of the 3:1 ratio Adwave recommends.

To count returns without guesswork, use a system that tags each customer at booking. Our guide to [AI-driven customer insights for local businesses](/blog/ai-driven-customer-insights-for-local-businesses) explains what to track. Winback Engine's 65%+ target for 90-day retention of reactivated customers is a useful second check. Do not borrow its 60-70% rebooking rate for your New Year customers, since that figure describes lapsed customers.

## What should you do in the next seven days?

1. Write your customers' return-to-routine date in a one-page plan, using their calendar rather than January 1.
2. Update holiday hours on your Google Business Profile before the rush starts.
3. Run the break-even example with your own profit per visit, cost per new customer and return rate.
4. Write one offer with one action and one end date, plus a rebooking line for the front desk. Saved replies for common questions make the rush easier, and our guide to a [custom GPT for local business customer service](/blog/custom-gpt-local-business-customer-service) shows one way to build them.
5. Confirm you have permission to message every address or number on your list, and check the consent rules that apply in your country. This article does not cover them.

## FAQ

### When should a local business start New Year marketing?

Plan in early December, open January bookings in the last week of December, and time the main push to your customers' return-to-routine date. Adwave ranks January as the strongest window for service businesses, so there is little reason to wait until February.

### Should I discount for New Year?

Only enough to get someone through the door once. Use the break-even return rate from the worked example to test whether repeat business can cover the discount, and consider a reward the customer can reach within 30 to 90 days, as Adwave suggests.

### How do I know whether January customers stayed?

Count how many return within 60 days and track 90-day retention. Winback Engine treats 65% or more as a target for reactivated customers. Recalculate customer lifetime value at least once a year, or every quarter if you are growing quickly or changing pricing.

### Is January always the best month to acquire customers?

No. Winback Engine and Adwave both show that the economics depend on your category and costs, and the Health & Fitness Association survey shows acquisition costs can rise. Test your own cost per new customer before committing a large budget.
