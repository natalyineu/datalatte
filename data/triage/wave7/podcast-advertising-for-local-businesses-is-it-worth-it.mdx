Podcast advertising can be worth it for a local business when three things line up: the show's listeners live in your service area, the ad uses a short format, and you can connect responses to the ad. Audio buying is growing. A third-party forecast cited by [StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/audio-advertising-show-level-targeting-reporting) puts programmatic podcast ad spending at $389 million in 2026, with 18.8% growth the following year. Those national figures do not give local show rates, so the real test is a host quote and a budget you can track.

## Who Listens to Podcasts, and Does the Audience Fit?

The audience numbers are the strongest argument for podcasts, but they carry dates and caveats. StackAdapt's audio advertising guide says 37% of Americans listen to podcasts monthly and 24% listen weekly, citing a podcast statistics source ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/audio-advertising)). That is a US figure, so it says little about a shop in Manchester, Toronto, or Sydney without local listener data.

Older Edison Research figures, reported in StackAdapt material, describe the listener in a few more ways. A 2019 study found 65% of US podcast listeners use mobile to listen, versus 25% on a desktop or laptop ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/programmatic-audio-ads)). A 2017 Edison finding describes listeners as typically earning a household income above $100k per year ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/programmatic-audio)). Treat these as directional. They are dated and US-focused.

The trust argument is also sourced. The same StackAdapt audio guide says podcast and music listeners have opted in, so they are choosing the content, which gives it an inherent trust ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/audio-advertising)). That helps only if the listeners include people who can walk through your door.

## How Long Should a Local Podcast Ad Run?

Sources give a range rather than a single rule. Matinée describes produced spots as pre-recorded, professionally mixed audio, typically 15 to 30 seconds long ([Matinée](https://matinee.co.uk/blog/podcast-advertising-guide/)). Voices, as cited by StackAdapt, says a fully produced podcast ad can run 15 to 60 seconds and is often more conversational than a radio ad ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/audio-advertising)). Tinuiti reports that Spotify podcast pre-recorded ads typically range from 15 to 60 seconds ([Tinuiti](https://tinuiti.com/blog/tv-audio/spotify-ads/)).

For a local business, the shorter end is a practical starting point. A 15 to 30 second spot leaves room for one offer, one address, and one next step, and it keeps production and testing costs down. Write at least two scripts with different offers so you can see which one brings in customers. Keep the tone conversational, so the ad sounds like a recommendation from the host rather than a radio spot.

## Where Do You Buy Podcast Ad Time?

Most local buying happens by contacting a show directly. The other route is programmatic. StackAdapt's show-level catalog contains more than 530,000 podcast shows that advertisers can search, filter, and add to inclusion lists ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/audio-advertising-show-level-targeting-reporting)). Spotify states that its podcast inventory can be bought through DSPs such as The Trade Desk, Google Display & Video 360, Amazon DSP, and Yahoo DSP via Private Marketplace deals ([Spotify Advertising](https://ads.spotify.com/en-AU/news-and-insights/podcast-advertising-guide/)). Those tools suit media buyers with larger budgets, so a single local business rarely needs them.

Direct outreach is simpler. Find the host's contact details on the show's website, then ask for the listener location breakdown, the ad formats they sell, and a fixed-fee quote for a set number of episodes. Ask whether the host reads the ad live or whether you supply a produced spot, since these are different products with different production steps. Get all terms in writing before the first episode airs.

## What Does the Budget Math Look Like for a Local Shop?

The sources do not publish local show rates, so the example below uses assumptions. Replace them with your own quotes and margins before you decide.

1. Monthly podcast budget: $300 (assumption).
2. Host fee: $150 per episode, two episodes per month, totaling $300 (assumption).
3. Average first sale: $60, with a 50% gross margin, so $30 profit per new customer (assumption).
4. Break-even: $300 divided by $30 equals 10 new customers per month.

The exact figures matter less than the logic. A podcast buy has to clear its cost in new profit, and you only know whether it does if tracking is set up before the first episode airs.

## How Do You Track Podcast Results Without Pixels?

Podcast audio does not carry a browser pixel, so tracking has to be built around the listener's next action. Give each show its own promo code, so each code maps to one buy and one host. Create a UTM-tagged link for the show's landing page, with utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign values that name the show. That lets you see visits in your analytics tool. Finally, train staff to ask every new customer how they heard about you and log the answer.

Compare these results with other paid channels using the same budget. For a broader look at trackable paid options, read [Google Local Service Ads: are they worth it for a local business?](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) The comparison helps you decide whether podcast audio earns a place in your mix or belongs in a test budget.

## When Should a Local Business Skip Podcast Ads?

Skip podcast ads if most of your customers fall outside the listener profile above, if the show's audience is not in your service area, or if you cannot track responses. The sourced listener profile skews toward mobile use and higher household incomes, so businesses serving budget-focused or older customers may see weaker returns. Those are inferences, not guarantees.

## FAQ

### Is podcast advertising worth it for a local business with a small budget?

It can be tested on a small budget, but the sources give no local rate card. Get fixed-fee quotes, set a break-even number using your own margins, and commit only to a test you can track. If the break-even figure is out of reach at your margins, the channel is not worth it at that price.

### How long should a podcast ad be?

Sources range from 15 to 30 seconds for produced spots ([Matinée](https://matinee.co.uk/blog/podcast-advertising-guide/)) to 15 to 60 seconds for fully produced ads ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/audio-advertising)). Start short, then test a longer version only if the first one brings in tracked customers.

### Do podcast listeners all earn high incomes?

No. A 2017 Edison finding describes listeners as typically earning a household income above $100k per year ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/programmatic-audio)). That describes a typical listener, not every listener, so check a specific show's audience data before assuming a match.

### Can I buy directly from a host instead of through an ad network?

The sources do not compare direct and network pricing. Direct outreach is a reasonable first step for a local show because it gives you the host's listener data and lets you negotiate a fixed fee. Confirm the ad format, episode count, and terms in writing.

If your customers, budget, and tracking setup match the examples above, a short test with a clear break-even number is a reasonable next step.
