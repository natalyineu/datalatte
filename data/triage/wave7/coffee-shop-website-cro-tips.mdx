A coffee shop website converts when a visitor on a phone can find your hours, address and menu, then tap to call, get directions or order, without signing up first. Consumers search online for new businesses: a Statista figure cited by Restaurant HQ puts the share at 98%. The fixes below cover what to show first, how to use your Google profile, how reviews fit in, and how to measure results from your own traffic.

This guide does not quote a conversion-rate benchmark for coffee shops. The only cross-industry figure below is a general average, and it should not be read as a café target.

## What Should a Coffee Shop Homepage Show First?

Visitors usually want four things: opening hours, location, what you sell and how to order. Put them in the first screen on mobile, using a short headline, a tap-to-call number, a directions link and an order or menu button. A large slideshow pushes all of that further down the page, where fewer visitors will reach it.

Keep the menu as live text rather than only an image or PDF. Text is easier to read on a small screen, can be indexed by search engines, and is quicker to update when prices change. Check the homepage on a real phone before you change anything else.

## Why Should the Site Be Built for Phones First?

Statista data cited by Square says seven in 10 Canadians use smartphones to access the internet. For a café, that means the phone layout is the version most visitors will see first, so it should be tested first. The same logic applies in other markets; the [mobile-first local marketing guide for Kenya](/blog/local-marketing-kenya-small-business-mobile-first) explains the approach in a market where phones are the main route online.

Square's Future of Commerce 2025 report, as cited on its page, says 65% of Canadians like businesses to communicate with them by email and 39% appreciate text message updates. Offer a simple email or text sign-up on the page, without requiring an account to see the menu or order.

## Should Your Google Business Profile Be Part of the Conversion Path?

Yes. Per Diem's guide says Google verifies a listing before it goes live, usually by mailing a postcard with a code, and that without verification your updates will not show publicly and customers may not find you. Check that verification is complete before spending time on the website.

Per Diem also recommends choosing the "Coffee shop" category or a related term rather than a broad one like "Restaurant". Google lets users message businesses directly through a listing, so check that messaging is set up and answer quickly. The [coffee shop Google Business Profile tips](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips) cover the listing in more detail.

If you take online orders, check the ordering settings in your profile against the platform's live help pages, since those settings change over time. A [Brazil guide on WhatsApp and Meta channels](/blog/local-marketing-brazil-small-business-whatsapp-meta) shows how messaging-led ordering works in a different market, which may help if you are weighing a chat channel.

## Do Reviews Lift Website Conversions?

A 2021 guide from Digital Resource says that featuring reviews or testimonials from real customers can help build trust and increase conversion rates by 270%. The page does not describe how that figure was measured, so treat it as a direction to test rather than a forecast for your shop.

Place a few recent reviews on the homepage and link to your full Google reviews. Do not write or edit testimonials; only show reviews customers actually left.

## Which Search Terms Should Land on Your Site?

Localized search terms usually face less competition than broad ones. seoplus states that a phrase such as "coffee shop downtown Phoenix" is likely to have less competition than "coffee shop", which can lead to a lower cost per click. Salesforce gives "best coffee shop in [city]" as an example of a long-tail, location-specific phrase.

Send paid and map traffic to a page that matches the phrase: the menu for "coffee near me" searches, directions and hours for "coffee shop downtown" searches. A generic homepage for every query usually converts less well.

## How Do You Measure Website Conversions?

Define a conversion before you change anything. For a coffee shop, that is usually an order tap, a call tap or a directions tap. Count these for a few weeks to get a baseline from your own traffic.

BoostFloral recommends tracking click-through rate, conversion rate and return on ad spend in Google Analytics and Google Ads reports. Compare the same days of the week, since weekend traffic often behaves differently from weekday traffic.

## What Should You Fix This Week?

1. Open the homepage on a phone and time how long it takes to see hours, address and an action button.
2. Replace any menu PDF with live text, or add a text version next to it.
3. Add tap-to-call and directions links to the first screen.
4. Remove any step that requires an account before a visitor can see prices or order.
5. Confirm your Google Business Profile is verified and set to the "Coffee shop" category or a close match.
6. Start counting order, call and directions taps for two weeks to set a baseline.
7. Change one element at a time and compare the same weekdays before and after.

## FAQ

### What is a good conversion rate for a coffee shop website?

No verified coffee-shop benchmark is given here. Outbrain cites a general average of 2 to 3% across all industries, which is not a café-specific target. Define your own conversions and improve from your baseline.

### Do I need online ordering to improve conversions?

Not necessarily. If you do not offer ordering, make the menu, hours and directions the main actions. If you do, link ordering from your website and your Google profile.

### Is a PDF menu bad for conversions?

A PDF is harder to read and tap on a phone than live text. Text is also easier to update when prices change.

### Should customers need an account to order?

Forced accounts add a step that can push visitors away. Offer guest ordering where your system allows it, and show the full price before the account prompt.
