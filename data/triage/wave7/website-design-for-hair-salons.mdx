A hair salon website needs a booking button visible without scrolling, a price list, real photos of your work, your address and hours, and a phone number that can be tapped to call. Put the booking path first, keep it to as few steps as your booking tool allows, and track every booking and call link so you can see which pages produce appointments. Speed matters on phones too: a SOASTA study cited by Google found that mobile pages loading one second faster achieved up to a 27% increase in conversion rates. ## What Should a Salon Booking Page Include?

Visitors on a phone want three answers quickly: do you offer their service, what does it cost, and when is the next open slot. The page should answer those before anything else. Place a Book button at the top that opens your booking tool, and repeat it at the bottom so a visitor who has read the prices does not have to scroll back up.

- Services with prices or "from" prices, with a note where prices vary by hair length.
- Photos of real work by your stylists, with their names.
- Address, hours and a map link that match your Google Business Profile.
- A phone number that can be tapped to call.
- Cancellation terms in one or two sentences.

For the wording itself, the guide on [how to write website copy for local businesses](/blog/how-to-write-website-copy-local-business-examples) gives examples. Placement of the booking button matters as much as wording, and the [above the fold design guide](/blog/above-the-fold-design-local-business) explains how to keep it on the first screen.

## How Much Does Speed Affect a Booking Page?

A slow page loses visitors before they see the booking button. The 27% figure comes from a general study cited in Google's mobile site speed playbook, not from salon sites. It is a ceiling for one kind of site, so treat it as a reason to test your own page rather than a forecast.

Salon homepages are usually photo-heavy, so start there. Compress images and size them to the space they fill. Then load the home page on a phone and time how long the Book button takes to appear. Repeat the check after each new batch of photos.

Security affects trust as well. Adwave notes that SSL only protects data in transit and does not stop phishing, server malware, weak passwords or vulnerable plugins. A page can also lose its padlock when images, scripts or forms still call for HTTP resources, a problem known as mixed content.

## Who Books Online, and Do They Come Back?

Online scheduling is common among people booking appointments. In a 2025 Eliciting Insights survey of 384 US adults who had visited a healthcare provider in the previous year, 95% had booked a medical appointment online or said they would if the option were available. That survey covers medical care, not salons, so read it as a sign of expectation rather than salon demand.

Repeat use is the more useful signal. In the same survey, 92% of people who had already booked online said they planned to use online scheduling again. Booksy's data also reports that engaged loyalty members visit salons 2.4 times as often. A salon should make rebooking as easy as the first booking, for example by linking to the booking tool from the confirmation message.

## How Do You Track Booking Page Performance?

Tracking shows which page and which button produce bookings. The Adwave mobile-first standard requires each tap-to-call and booking link to log an event in analytics. Apply the same rule to your site: every Book button and tap-to-call link should record a click, tagged with where it sits (header, service page or footer).

Bookeo defines cost per booking as total monthly social media spend divided by the number of confirmed bookings that came from social media. The same arithmetic works for paid search and for your site. Divide what you spend on ads by the bookings your tracked links recorded, and compare that figure across months rather than trusting a single week.

## Should Your Site Say More Than the Booking Tool?

Yes. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and there is no way to request or pay Google for a better local position. Your site supplies relevance: a page for each main service, your location and your team, with name, address and phone matching your Google Business Profile.

Posting to the profile will not guarantee a higher ranking, because Google does not say that posting every day guarantees a ranking increase. Do not offer a reward for a positive review or tell clients what to write. ## FAQ

### What should a hair salon website include?

A Book button at the top and repeated at the bottom, services with prices, photos of real work, address, hours, a tappable phone number and short cancellation terms.

### Does a faster website help bookings?

A general SOASTA study cited by Google found up to a 27% conversion increase for mobile pages one second faster. It is not a salon-specific figure, so test your own pages.

### Do people book appointments online?

A 2025 Eliciting Insights survey found 95% of US adults who had visited a healthcare provider had booked or would book a medical appointment online. It does not measure salon bookings.

### How do I track booking clicks?

Log each booking button and tap-to-call link as an analytics event, following the Adwave standard, and tag each event with where it appears on the page.

### Can posting to Google Business Profile guarantee a better ranking?

No. Google does not say that posting every day guarantees a ranking increase, and local position cannot be requested or paid for.
