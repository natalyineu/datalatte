Angi Ads and Angi Leads are separate products with different billing. Angi Leads is a pay-per-lead model, so your cost rises with the number of contacts you buy. Angi Ads is an advertising plan, with pricing that starts at around $300 per month and is customized by service and location. A business profile on Angi is free to create. For most local service businesses, the choice comes down to whether you can handle variable lead spend or would rather have a fixed monthly line you can budget against. The sections below compare cost, lead exclusivity and fit, then run a worked budget example.

## What is the difference between Angi Ads and Angi Leads?

Angi Leads sits in the pay-per-lead group. One review places Angi alongside HomeAdvisor and Porch as pay-per-lead companies, [as of 2022](https://k2radio.com/porch-pro-reviews/) (secondary). You pay for the contact itself, not for a booked job, so the number of leads you buy directly drives the bill.

Angi Ads is an advertising product. According to a [home services marketing guide](https://onlinevisibilitypros.com/internet-marketing-for-contractors/homeadvisor-vs-thumbtack-vs-angis/) (secondary), advertising on Angi starts at around $300 per month, with pricing customized by services and location. The guide also states that creating a business profile on Angi is free.

The two products are separate purchases. Setting up a profile costs nothing, and the paid choice is between per-lead billing and an advertising plan. Check Angi's current help pages before you sign anything, because pricing and terms change.

## How much does each option cost in practice?

The advertising plan has a known starting point of around $300 per month. The final figure depends on your service category and market, so ask for a quote for your specific zip codes and trade before you commit.

Per-lead pricing is harder to pin down from published sources. For a useful comparison, look at how Thumbtack bills. Thumbtack uses a credit system in which professionals buy credits to communicate with leads, and it has [no annual flat fee](https://7ten.marketing/angie-vs-thumbtack/) (secondary). Each lead costs a number of credits that varies with the job's complexity, demand and location. That same logic, where price moves with demand, is worth asking Angi about directly.

Since a per-lead price changes with category and location, get the number for your own trade and area in writing. A quote you can compare with your average job value is more useful than any general price range.

## Which model fits your business?

Lead exclusivity is the first question to ask. HomeAdvisor, which is part of the same family of lead platforms, [splits its leads into two types](https://onlinevisibilitypros.com/internet-marketing-for-contractors/homeadvisor-vs-thumbtack-vs-angis/) (secondary): Market Match leads are shared with other service providers in the area, while Exact Match leads are exclusive to the company. Ask Angi which of its lead types you would receive and whether any are shared with competitors.

Billing structure matters just as much. A pay-per-lead model lets you stop buying when your calendar is full, but you pay for every contact that does not turn into a job. A fixed monthly fee costs the same whether leads convert or not, so it only makes sense if the jobs it brings in cover that fee comfortably. For a deeper look at how these two platforms charge, read [Angi vs Thumbtack: how each charges for leads](/blog/angi-vs-thumbtack-for-local-business).

## How should you test Angi before committing a budget?

A short test protects you from a long contract. Work through the steps below in order.

1. Confirm the lead model in writing. Ask whether each lead type is exclusive or shared with other pros.
2. Get the per-lead or monthly price for your exact category and zip codes.
3. Set a spend cap equal to an amount you can afford to lose during the test.
4. Log each lead's source, whether it was contacted, and whether it became a booked job.
5. Divide spend by booked jobs and compare the result with your average job value after materials and labor.

If the cost per booked job is above your margin, reduce the categories you buy or stop. If it sits comfortably below, you have evidence to scale. Either way, the decision rests on your own bookings, not a vendor's claim.

## FAQ

### Is an Angi business profile free?

According to a [home services marketing guide](https://onlinevisibilitypros.com/internet-marketing-for-contractors/homeadvisor-vs-thumbtack-vs-angis/) (secondary), creating a business profile on Angi is free. Paid products such as advertising are separate purchases.

### Does Thumbtack charge an annual fee?

No. Thumbtack's model is pay-as-you-go with [no annual flat fee](https://7ten.marketing/angie-vs-thumbtack/) (secondary). Registration is also free, and contractors pay for leads when potential clients ask for a bid, according to a [comparison guide](https://onlinevisibilitypros.com/internet-marketing-for-contractors/homeadvisor-vs-thumbtack-vs-angis/) (secondary).

### Is Angi Ads a fixed monthly cost?

Advertising on Angi starts at around $300 per month, and pricing varies by service and location. Confirm the contract length and cancellation terms with Angi before you sign, since those details are not covered in the sources used here.

### Are Angi leads shared with other pros?

Angi's own distribution rules are not confirmed by the sources used here. Its sister platform HomeAdvisor splits leads into shared Market Match and exclusive Exact Match types. Ask Angi directly and check its current help pages for the rules that apply to your account.
