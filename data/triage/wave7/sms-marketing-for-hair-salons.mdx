SMS marketing helps hair salons in two main ways: texting clients who have gone quiet, and filling last-minute gaps in the appointment book. Hair salon visits typically fall every 4 to 6 weeks, and one reactivation guide treats a client as lapsed after 90 days inactive, so that gap is where a text belongs. Only message clients who have agreed to receive marketing texts, and check your texting platform's live help page for the consent and opt-out wording you need. ## Which clients should you text first?

Start with clients who are past their normal visit window. The [Winback Engine guide](https://winbackengine.com/blog/customer-reactivation-guide/) lists hair salon visits every 4 to 6 weeks and treats clients as lapsed after 90 or more days inactive. A client who is well past their usual gap is the clearest candidate for a short, relevant reminder.

Demographics add context. [Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) says consumers aged 55 and older account for 30% of the market share and spend the most on hair care services. Its advice to target women aged 21 to 55 who live nearby is written for social ads, so use it to shape your audience, not to decide who receives a text.

For texts, the most useful list is usually nearby clients who have already booked with you. Their consent, visit history and preferred service are all in your booking system, which makes segmenting straightforward. Pair this with your email list, as described in [AI-powered email marketing for hair salons](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-for-hair-salons).

## Do loyalty and referral offers work by text?

Loyalty appears to matter. [Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing) reports, from its own data, that engaged loyalty members visit salons 2.4x more often. A text that rewards a returning client is therefore a reasonable test, though Booksy does not say how much of that gap a text creates.

Offers that have been used in salon marketing include "Prebook now and save 10%" and "Bring a friend, save $20 each" with a shareable code or link, both listed by [Zeely](https://zeely.ai/blog/hair-salon-advertising-ideas/). Booksy's example gives an existing client $20 off their next service and the new friend 20% off their first visit.

Referral offers can also be shared by local creators. If you plan to try that, see [influencer marketing for hair salons](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-hair-salons-how-to-get-free-bookings-from-local-creator). Keep one offer per campaign so you can tell which one brought people back.

## Which offer wording keeps you on the right side of advertising law?

Price claims need to be honest. According to the American Bar Association's summary cited on [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/pool-service-advertising) (secondary), FTC rules prohibit advertising a service for less than it really costs and bait-and-switch tactics. Those rules apply to any business, including salons, so a text should not advertise a price or slot that clients cannot actually book.

In practice, state the full price, the offer's end date and whether the discount applies only to a first visit. Confirm the slot is available before the text goes out. This summary covers one secondary source and is general information, not legal advice; check your local rules if you are unsure.

Avoid vague phrases such as "huge savings" that do not say what the client receives. A specific offer, such as "$20 off your next cut before Friday," is easier to trust and easier to audit later.

## What does a text campaign cost and return? (worked example)

Every input in this example is an assumption. Assume you have 300 clients who have opted in to marketing texts, and 120 of them last visited more than 90 days ago. Assume each text costs $0.04, the platform fee is $40 a month, and your average ticket is $75. Assume the offer is $20 off the next service, and that 6 of the 120 clients rebook.

Campaign cost: 120 texts at $0.04 is $4.80, plus the $40 platform fee, for a total of $44.80. Revenue from six rebooked clients is $75 each before the discount, or $55 each after the $20 offer, so $330 in total. Net result: $330 minus $44.80, or $285.20.

The break-even point is simple. Each rebooked client nets $55 after the offer, so one rebooking covers the $44.80 cost. If you rebook fewer than one client, the campaign loses money in that month. Adwave's illustrative salon example values a client at $75 a visit, 6 visits a year and 4 years of retention, or $1,800 in total ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-lifetime-value-small-business), secondary). That lifetime figure is useful for judging whether a returning client matters, but judge each campaign first on its net result.

## How do you run a reactivation text without breaking trust?

Follow these steps for each campaign:

1. Confirm that every recipient has opted in to marketing texts. If you cannot show a record of consent, do not send to that contact.
2. Pull clients whose last visit was 90 or more days ago, using your booking system's history.
3. Write one offer, with the full price, the end date and any first-visit restriction stated.
4. Include the opt-out wording your texting platform requires, and check its live help page for current carrier and consent requirements.
5. Send once. Given the 4 to 6 week visit cycle for hair salons, a second text inside that window is unlikely to help and may annoy the client.
6. Record how many clients rebook and compare the net result with the cost. Keep the campaign only if the net beats the cost, and test a different offer if it does not.

## FAQ

### Can I text clients from my personal phone?
The sources used for this article do not cover that question. Ask your texting platform what it supports, and check its live help page before you send any promotional message.

### How many promotional texts should I send each month?
No source here sets a number. Start with one campaign, measure rebookings and opt-outs, and decide from there.

### Is there salon-specific evidence that texts bring clients back?
The sources here do not include a salon-specific text test. Measure your own rebooking rate against the assumption in the worked example before you scale up.

### Do loyalty offers really bring more visits?
Booksy reports that engaged loyalty members visit 2.4x more often, based on its own data. Test that with your own clients rather than assuming the same result.
