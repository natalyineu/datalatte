The best Google Ads keywords for a fitness studio are local, class-specific phrases that name the service and the area, such as "pilates classes [city]" or "spin class near me", plus offer terms if you run a trial. Start with phrase and exact match, and judge each keyword group by cost per new member rather than cost per click. Adwave puts fitness keyword clicks at $2 to $8 in competitive metros, so a cheap click may bring no members, while an expensive one may still pay for itself.

## Which keyword groups should a fitness studio bid on?

Group keywords by what the searcher wants, and give each group its own ad group and landing page. Most studios can cover their demand with four groups:

- Ready-to-visit terms: "fitness studio [city]", "fitness studio near me", "boutique gym [neighborhood]".
- Class-type terms: "hiit classes [city]", "pilates classes [city]", "barre classes [neighborhood]", "spin class near me".
- Offer terms: "free trial fitness class [city]" or "first class free [city]", but only if the landing page really makes that offer.
- Brand terms: your studio name, and your studio name plus "schedule".

Class-type terms usually show clearer intent than "fitness studio", because the person already knows what they want to do. Keep one ad group per class type so the ad and landing page can repeat the words they typed. For a yoga-specific list, see [best Google Ads keywords for yoga studios](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-yoga-studios-in-2026).

Informational searches such as "beginner pilates exercises" belong in blog posts and social content, not in a paid campaign.

## How much does a fitness studio click cost?

Adwave's guide says cost per click for fitness keywords in competitive metros often lands in the $2 to $8 range, and is higher for specialised training ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/gym-advertising-increasing-memberships)). Its suggested starting spend is $20 to $40 a day on intent keywords such as "[your category] [your city]" ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-gyms-fitness-centers)).

## Should you use broad, phrase or exact match?

Broad match fits general interest, but it also reaches loosely related searches. Selworthy's guide notes that broad keywords like "fitness" attract general interest, while phrase match keywords such as "affordable gyms in [location]" target a more specific audience ([Selworthy](https://www.selworthy.com/blog-articles/google-ads-for-fitness-centers-gyms-in-2026)).

For a single studio, phrase and exact match give more control over who sees your ad. Use phrase match to learn how people describe what they want, and exact match for proven terms and your brand.

Consider broad match only after conversions are tracked reliably and your negative list has matured. A studio with only a few enquiries a month has little data for any automated bidding to learn from.

## What belongs on your negative keyword list?

A negative keyword stops your ad from showing on a search you do not want. Start with the themes that rarely turn into members:

- Jobs and training: careers, hiring, salary, certification, instructor training.
- At-home and online: home workout, workout videos, free apps, YouTube.
- Equipment and shopping: for sale, used, second hand, rental, supplements.
- Business: franchise, business plan, cost to open, insurance.
- Wrong service: physiotherapy, diet plan, meal plan.
- Sports you do not offer, such as golf or tennis.

Check plural forms yourself, because a negative may not cover them. Handle "free" with care: if you offer a free trial, keep "free trial" as a keyword and block only the searches that do not fit. Remove any negative that matches something you actually sell, such as martial arts if you teach it.

Review the search terms report every week during the first month. Add irrelevant queries as negatives and move good ones into an exact-match ad group.

## How do you budget cost per new member?

Cost per new member depends on four inputs. The formula is: ad spend divided by (clicks multiplied by enquiry rate multiplied by join rate).

The example below uses assumptions, not benchmarks. Replace them with your own figures after 30 days of data.

- Monthly search budget: $900 (assumption, about $30 a day, inside Adwave's suggested range).
- Enquiry rate from click: 5% (assumption).
- Enquiry-to-member rate: 20% (assumption).
- Average cost per click: tested at $2, $5 and $8, inside Adwave's $2 to $8 range.

| Average CPC (assumption) | Clicks | Enquiries | New members | Cost per new member |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $2 | 450 | 22.5 | 4.5 | $200 |
| $5 | 180 | 9 | 1.8 | $500 |
| $8 | 112.5 | 5.6 | 1.1 | $800 |

If one new member is worth more than $800 to your studio, the $8 keyword still works. If not, move budget toward phrases nearer $2 or improve the enquiry and join rates before you raise spend.

For context, Mariana Tek says many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for all marketing ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). At an assumed $20,000 monthly revenue, that range is $1,000 to $2,000, so a $900 search budget would sit inside it.

## Where do clicks go after they land?

Many people check your Google Business Profile before they click. Google's free profile shows business hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos directly in search results, and the Map Pack lists local businesses for queries such as "personal training studios near me" ([Association of Fitness Studios](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google)). Keep the profile accurate, since it works alongside your ads.

Speed matters after the enquiry. Mariana Tek advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades quickly after a pop-up ends ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). The steps for setting that up are in [marketing automation for local fitness studios](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-fitness-studios). For the organic side of the same searches, see [AI-driven local SEO strategies for fitness studios](/blog/ai-driven-local-seo-strategies-for-fitness-studios).

Brand clicks deserve a separate look. Winback Engine lists gyms with 2 to 4 visits a week as lapsed after 30 to 60 days without a visit ([Winback Engine](https://winbackengine.com/blog/customer-reactivation-guide/)), so a brand search from someone who has gone quiet is worth a fast, personal reply.

## FAQ

### What are the best Google Ads keywords for a fitness studio?

Local, high-intent phrases that name your class type and place, such as "pilates classes [city]" or "personal trainer [neighborhood]", plus offer terms if you run a trial. The right mix depends on your market, so test them and judge each one by cost per new member.

### Should I bid on "fitness studio near me"?

Test it in its own ad group. Adwave's range of $2 to $8 per click for competitive fitness terms gives a sense of the cost, so check the cost per new member before you scale it.

### Should I bid on competitor names?

Only with a clearly better offer and a small test budget. Keep their trademarks out of your ad text, and check Google's current advertising policies on the live help pages before you launch.

### How often should I update my keywords?

A reasonable rhythm is a weekly review of the search terms report for the first month, then every two to four weeks. This is a suggested routine, not a platform rule.

### What if the click price is higher than my budget allows?

Narrow the location radius, use more specific phrases, improve the offer and landing page, and answer enquiries faster. If the numbers still do not work, strengthen your Google Business Profile and local search presence first.
