## What should a fitness studio listen for?

Four types of mention are worth tracking, because each one leads to a clear action:

- Your studio name and common misspellings
- Your class names and instructors
- Your neighbourhood plus a service, such as "pilates near" a street or district
- Competitor studio names

Each mention becomes one of four things: a review to answer, a question to answer publicly, a complaint to fix, or a gap in what you offer. Anything that does not lead to an action is noise. The LoyalSnap fitness marketing article cites Smart Insights that more than half of people spend over two hours a day on social platforms, so the conversation about your studio is likely happening even when you are not watching.

To build the competitor list, the Appointy guide suggests starting with about 15-20 businesses or brands you hear about most. Our [fitness studio marketing strategy](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-marketing-strategy) guide covers how to pick which ones to watch.

## How fast should a studio reply to a mention?

Reply speed is the variable most likely to change results. Blue Grid Media, as cited by Now Booked Marketing, reports that contractors who answer within one hour book about 31% of answered calls, while those taking 24 hours or more book about 15%. These figures come from contractors, not studios, so treat them as a direction rather than a forecast for your business.

The same source reports that slow follow-up roughly doubles the cost of each booked job, regardless of which channel the lead came from. Mariana Tek advises studios to follow up with pop-up contacts within 24 hours using an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades fast after a pop-up ends. Tracking response times is easier when you use the reporting described in our [social media analytics guide](/blog/ai-powered-social-media-analytics-for-local-businesses).

## What does the weekly routine cost in time?

The table below splits a 20-minute routine into tasks. All figures are assumptions for a single studio.

| Routine step | Minutes a week (assumption) |
| --- | --- |
| Check search alerts and tagged posts | 7 |
| Answer new reviews and messages | 8 |
| Log competitor and class mentions | 5 |
| Total | 20 |

Twenty minutes a week is about 87 minutes a month (assumption: 52 weeks divided by 12). At a staff cost of $20 an hour (assumption), that is about $29 a month.

Next, assume 20 inquiries a month start from a social mention, and that each new client is worth $40 in first-month revenue (both assumptions). At a 31% booking rate, 20 inquiries give 6.2 bookings. At 15%, they give 3.0. The gap is 3.2 bookings, worth $128 a month (assumption). After the $29 time cost, the net is about $99 a month. The 31% and 15% rates are contractor benchmarks, so your own rates will differ.

## When does a paid listening tool make sense?

Some paid tools bundle listening into a scheduling plan instead of selling it separately. AdWave's scheduling comparison says Later reserves some advanced analytics and listening capabilities for its higher tiers. Sintra's roundup says the Professional plan from Vista Social includes 15 social profiles, 3 users, publishing, reporting, listening tools and AI-powered features.

That means the cheapest route may be a plan you already pay for. Check current prices on each vendor's pricing page, as no prices are quoted here. Using the worked example, a tool's monthly fee has to come out of the $128 gain from faster replies, plus whatever time it saves. ## How do listening findings feed your content?

Listening is most useful when it changes what you post. Mariana Tek describes three to five short-form videos per week as a realistic, sustainable pace for most studios. Appointy recommends creating at least 5-10 posts in each content category in advance, so that busy days do not leave you without anything to publish.

Appointy also notes that Instagram and Facebook let you add a Book Now button to your profile. If listening shows that people ask about a specific class, a post with a booking link answers that question directly. Our [fitness studio social media content](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-content) guide shows how to turn common questions into a post plan. Mariana Tek adds that many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing, which gives a ceiling for any tool spend.

## FAQ

### Is social listening worth paying for at one studio?

Usually not at first. Use the worked example: if the free routine already captures most mentions, a tool fee has to be justified by extra bookings.

### What can a free routine cover?

Search alerts, the review inbox of your Google Business Profile and a manual check of tagged posts and comments. Check each platform's live help page for current limits.

### Do I need to reply to every mention?

Reply to reviews, direct questions and complaints first. Blue Grid Media's contractor data links faster responses with higher booking rates, so prioritise anything that asks for a booking or a price.

### Can listening tell me what content to post?

Yes. Repeated questions about classes or schedules are good candidates for posts. Pair them with a booking link, and batch the posts in advance.
