## Who Uses Instagram, and Why Does It Matter for Flowers?

Adobe Express, citing DataReportal, says at least 1.7 billion people use Instagram and that the platform can reach over 20% of the world's population ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising), secondary). The largest active age group is 25 to 34, followed by 18 to 24. Reported gender split is 48% female and 52% male, with data for other genders unavailable.

Urban shoppers are a strong audience. The Restaurant HQ page, citing Pew Research, says 53% of urban social media users are on Instagram ([The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/), secondary). Bookeo, a booking software vendor, says about 81% of people use Instagram to research a service, though it gives no source for that figure ([Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/)).

These are general audience figures, not florist results. They tell you the platform is large and used for research, which fits a shop that wants buyers to see arrangements before they call or order. Check your own order history to see which age groups and areas already buy from you.

## What Should a Florist Post on Instagram?

Post finished arrangements, but also show the work behind them. A pet store guide lists user-generated content, behind-the-scenes moments, tips and advice, seasonal content, interactive features and product highlights in context as formats that work for retail ([Blackstone Infomatics](https://blackstoneinfomatics.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-pet-shops/), secondary to a florist, but the categories transfer well). For a florist, that means a bouquet being wrapped, a tip on keeping stems fresh, and a seasonal arrangement with its price.

Choose formats for lasting value. Instagram and Facebook stories are temporary by design, so use them for same-day updates like available stems or a last-day preorder reminder, and keep permanent arrangements and prices in the feed ([Associated Hair Professionals](https://www.associatedhairprofessionals.com/updates/blog-posts/power-social-media-your-hairstyling-brand)).

Avoid building the plan around one trend. Infegy reports that GRWM (Get Ready With Me) videos surged in early 2023 on TikTok and Instagram, after which engagement and post volume declined as content moved toward lifestyle storytelling ([Infegy](https://www.infegy.com/blog/three-ways-to-find-beauty-insights)). A format can spike and fade, so check your own reach each month. The guide on [creating social content for local businesses](/blog/local-business-social-media-content-creation) covers a repeatable content mix.

## How Often Should a Florist Post on Instagram?

Hootsuite's research, as summarised by SalonBiz, recommends posting to Instagram two to seven times per week ([SalonBiz](https://salonbizsoftware.com/blog/daily-salon-social-media-marketing-ideas-how-to-engage-your-audience/), secondary). A coffee shop guide from Bellwether recommends three to five feed posts per week plus daily Stories, and puts the Instagram time requirement at about 30 minutes per day ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)). That coffee guide is written for a different business, so treat it as a rhythm to test, not a florist rule.

For a florist, three feed posts a week plus Stories is a practical starting point, with more posting in the two weeks before a holiday. Batch the work: photograph two or three arrangements in one session, then schedule the captions. Sintra's review of social media AI agents claims an agent can build a month of Instagram posts in under an hour; that is a vendor claim, but it is the kind of task covered in [how an AI agent can schedule social posts for a local business](/blog/ai-agent-for-social-media-posting-local-business).

## How Do You Turn Instagram Views Into Orders?

Put the ordering route where people look. Google's Business Profile help page says you can add one social media link per platform, including Instagram, to your profile ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617)). Link your Instagram there, and keep the same delivery rules and cut-off time in your bio.

Instagram and Facebook can also show a Book Now button on a profile, according to Appointy's guide for gyms and studios ([Appointy](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/)). If you take orders through a booking or order tool, check whether it offers this link. Bookeo says Facebook and Instagram can reach audiences and build trust without spending on the platform itself ([Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/)).

Track where orders come from. Use a separate link or code for each campaign, and compare the number of profile visits, message requests and completed orders. The [Instagram analytics guide for local businesses](/blog/ai-powered-social-media-analytics-for-local-businesses) explains how to set this up without a large reporting budget.

## Should a Florist Run Instagram Ads?

Possibly, for a short holiday preorder. Adobe Express says Meta Ads Manager, formerly Facebook Ads Manager, can run ads across Instagram and Facebook from one place ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising), secondary). Turrboo confirms that Meta Ads Manager covers Facebook, Instagram and Messenger ([Turrboo](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media)).

Cost varies. Adobe Express cites AdRoll's 2021 figures for Instagram ads at $0.20 to $2 per click, so check current costs in your own account before you plan a budget ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising), secondary). Targeting matters too. A grooming business example on Groomsoft's blog suggests targeting people within 10 miles who have shown interest in the category. That page's reach and booking figures are hypothetical projections, not measured results ([Groomsoft](https://blog.groomsoft.com/growing-your-grooming-business/)).

## What Does a Small Holiday Ad Test Return?

The example below uses assumptions for every input except the CPC range, which is an outside figure from the $0.20 to $2 range above. Replace each assumption with your own numbers before you spend.

- Budget for the week before a holiday: $200 (assumption)
- CPC: $1.00 (assumption, the midpoint of the $0.20 to $2 range)
- Clicks: $200 divided by $1.00 = 200
- Share of clicks that become orders: 2% (assumption)
- Orders: 200 × 0.02 = 4
- Average order value: $65 (assumption)
- Revenue: 4 × $65 = $260
- Gross margin on flowers: 40% (assumption), so gross profit = $260 × 0.40 = $104
- Result after ad spend: $104 − $200 = −$96
- Break-even orders: $200 ÷ ($65 × 0.40) = 7.7, so about 8 orders

At these assumptions, the test loses money. To break even, you would need about 8 orders from 200 clicks, a lower CPC, or a higher order rate. A simple rule before you spend: work out your break-even order count first, then decide if your offer and audience can reach it. If the numbers look weak, put the budget into posting and your Google Business Profile instead. ## FAQ

### How often should a florist post on Instagram?
Hootsuite's research, as summarised by SalonBiz, suggests two to seven posts per week. Start with three feed posts and daily Stories, then increase before holidays if your reach holds.

### Do Reels or photos sell more flowers?
None of the sources here compare them for florists. Use Stories for timely stock updates, and test whether feed photos or short videos bring more profile visits and orders for your shop.

### Should I show prices on Instagram?
The sources here do not say whether to show prices. Test it, since prices help buyers decide quickly, and compare message volume and orders with and without prices.

### How much should I spend on Instagram ads?
Start with a small, time-limited budget tied to one preorder offer. Work out your break-even order count first, using your own margin and average order value.
