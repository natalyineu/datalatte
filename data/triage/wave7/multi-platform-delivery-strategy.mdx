A multi-platform delivery strategy means sending Glovo, Wolt and your own ordering channel into one system, keeping one master menu, and judging each channel on net profit per order rather than order count. Commission decides most of the result. Delivery platform commissions run 15% to 30% per order, according to [Adwave's restaurant advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/restaurant-advertising-complete-marketing-guide) (secondary). Glovo's published plans range from a 30% fee on dish value to a 15% marketplace fee when the restaurant uses its own couriers, per [Deliverect's Glovo guide](https://www.deliverect.com/en-mx/blog/online-food-delivery/glovo-101-the-essential-guide-for-restaurants) (secondary). The sections below cover the setup order, the fee lines to compare and a worked example you can rerun with your own invoices.

## Should You List on More Than One Delivery App?

Often yes, when the extra reach brings in orders you would otherwise miss and you have the staff to run each channel properly. Wolt says it partners with more than 36,000 restaurant and retail partners, according to a [Smartly case study](https://www.smartly.io/resources/wolt) (secondary). Scale on a marketplace does not guarantee orders on your street or for your cuisine, though. Glovo, per the same Deliverect guide, operates in 26 countries and 200 cities with 5.1 million active users. Local customer habits matter more than headline size.

The cost is complexity: duplicate tablets, menus that drift apart and missed orders. Read each contract for exclusivity terms before you add a listing. The [Glovo vs Wolt comparison](/blog/glovo-vs-wolt-which-is-better) sets out what to ask each platform in writing, and it is a useful starting point before you agree to a second listing.

## How Much Do Delivery Apps Take Per Order?

The rate depends on the plan you sign. Glovo's Delivery with Glovo plan charges a 30% fee on the value of the dish, while its Self-Delivery plan charges a 15% marketplace fee and the restaurant uses its own couriers. Glovo's In-Store Pickup option charges a 15% pickup fee and offers no delivery. These figures come from Deliverect's summary of Glovo's plans, so confirm them against your own offer.

Adwave's how-to guide says third-party commission fees often hit 30% of an order. The same page gives that figure without citing a source, so treat it as a planning ceiling rather than a published rate. Wolt's rates are set in your merchant agreement, and the platform's current help pages are the place to check them.

## How Do You Run One Kitchen Across Apps?

Use a POS integration or a middleware service that receives orders from each app into one screen. Then follow these steps:

1. Confirm that each app's orders can reach your POS or middleware before you go live on it.
2. Keep one master menu in the POS and push it to every app.
3. Set one opening-hours calendar so no app takes orders while the kitchen is closed.
4. Test a single order end to end on each channel before a busy service.
5. Log every missed, refunded or penalised order by channel for the weekly review.

Wolt's ads page describes 24/7 access to its self-service platform, where merchants can track sales. Check [Wolt ads for restaurants in Europe](/blog/wolt-ads-restaurant-guide-europe) for how that reporting fits with your other channels.

## How Do You Keep Menus and Prices Consistent?

Keep one master menu in the POS and push it to every app, so item names, options and availability match across channels. If an app's fee is higher, decide in advance whether you will change prices there. Check each platform agreement and your local rules first, because some terms limit price differences.

Build a shorter delivery menu with items that hold up in transit. The [menu optimisation guide for delivery platforms](/blog/restaurant-menu-optimisation-delivery-platforms) explains which dishes to keep, which to cut and how to present them on each app.

## How Do You Move Repeat Customers to Your Own Channel?

Collect contact details with the customer's consent, then put a card with a direct-order link or QR code in each bag. Keep your Google Business Profile current so people searching for you can order directly. The approach in [abandoned cart email strategy](/blog/abandoned-cart-email-strategy) can guide follow-up messages to customers who started an order but did not finish it.

Some app agreements restrict direct promotion, so read the clauses before you print cards. Paid social can also drive direct orders; the [Facebook ads guide for restaurant delivery orders](/blog/facebook-ads-restaurant-delivery-orders-guide) covers setup. Plan the spend with the [restaurant delivery marketing budget split guide](/blog/restaurant-delivery-marketing-budget-how-to-split).

## What Should You Review Each Week?

Review each channel on the same lines: orders, net revenue after commission, penalties, refunds, rating and preparation time. Use the same sheet for every platform so the comparison stays honest. If a channel's net profit per order stays below your target for two months, renegotiate the terms or drop the channel.

Keep the fee sheet current when platforms change terms, and re-run the worked example with the latest invoices. Small changes in commission can shift which channel earns the most per order.

## FAQ

### Is it worth being on several delivery platforms?
It can be, if each channel earns a profit after commission and you can run them without errors. Compare net profit per order, not revenue.

### How do I manage all delivery orders in one place?
Use a POS integration or a middleware service that receives orders from each app. Confirm that your POS provider supports each platform before you switch on the channel.

### What are the usual delivery commissions?
Delivery platform commissions run 15% to 30% per order, per Adwave. Glovo's published plans are 30% on dish value for Delivery with Glovo and 15% for Self-Delivery. Wolt's rates depend on your agreement, so check its current help pages.

### Can I charge different prices on each app?
That depends on your platform agreement and local rules. Check the terms with each platform before you change prices on one channel.
