Most pet groomers should start with Google Ads search, then add programmatic display or retargeting once bookings arrive consistently. Search reaches people already looking for a groomer. Programmatic display buys ads by software and shows them across websites and apps, so it suits a salon that has a working booking page and a budget to test. AdTurbo gives a benchmark of $2 to $8 per click for dog groomer Google Ads, and says most advertisers spend $800 to $5,000 a month. This guide covers when display helps, how to set a small test and what to measure.

## Should a Pet Groomer Start With Programmatic Ads?

Usually not. A person typing a grooming query is already close to booking, so Search meets demand that exists. Programmatic display works better for reaching people who have not chosen a groomer yet, or for reminding people who visited your booking page and left. AdTurbo names booking a grooming appointment as the primary conversion to track for dog groomer Google Ads, so set that up before adding any channel. For the difference between display and programmatic buying, see [display advertising vs programmatic for local businesses](/blog/display-advertising-vs-programmatic-local-business).

## What Does a Dog Groomer Search Click Cost?

AdTurbo puts dog groomer clicks at about $2 to $8 and warns that a few wasted clicks add up fast. The same page says most advertisers in the category spend around $800 to $5,000 a month. The range is wide, so a small test budget will buy less volume than a full campaign and should be read as a learning exercise. Keywords the page lists include "dog groomer near me," "dog grooming near me" and "puppy grooming near me." Because AdTurbo is a vendor page, check live bids in the platform before setting your budget.

## What Has Worked for a Mobile Groomer?

Marketing 360 published a case study on a mobile pet groomer that advertised on Facebook and Google. Compared with the previous six-month period, the groomer had a 134% increase in impressions and a 696% increase in clicks. The page also reports a 682% increase in conversions. The case was published on June 13, 2023, and covers one business in one period, so it shows what is possible rather than what to expect. A fixed salon with a different service mix will see different numbers.

## Does Seasonal Timing Change the Budget?

Yes. Groomer to Groomer, as summarised by AdWave, notes that November and December are traditionally a very busy time for groomers, sometimes too busy. That means demand and competition can both shift through the year. Start a test before the peak so you can read the results, rather than starting in the middle of a rush when you may not have capacity to take extra bookings. Check your own booking history for your local pattern before you commit a budget.

## How Do You Target a Local Radius?

Barklead, in its guide to Facebook ads for groomers, suggests targeting users within a 10 to 15 mile radius of the groomer's location. That is a secondary suggestion, so test it against your own booking data. Mobile groomers with a defined service area should match the radius to that area. Programmatic and search tools also offer location targeting, but radius options and minimums vary by platform, so check each provider's live help page before choosing a setting. Pull your client postcodes from your booking software first, then set the radius to fit them.

## What Should You Measure Before Adding Display?

Work through these steps in order:

1. Confirm that booking confirmations are tracked as the main conversion.
2. Run the search test for at least one full booking cycle.
3. Record cost per booking and the profit per groom, not just clicks.
4. Only then add retargeting, and limit it to visitors who did not book.
5. Judge display by site visits and later branded searches, since it rarely produces direct bookings.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid?

Do not run display before your booking page and tracking are working. Ads sent to a page that cannot take a booking waste money. Do not target a region far beyond the area your clients actually travel from, because wide targeting spends on people who cannot book.

Do not judge a test after a week. Demand shifts through the year, so one short window tells you little. Finally, do not copy ranking claims from vendors or competitors. Lucky Dog Mobile Groomers calls itself the "#1 rated mobile pet groomer nationwide," but the page gives no cited source for that claim. Treat it as marketing, not evidence.

## FAQ

### How much should a pet groomer spend on programmatic ads?

There is no standard amount. AdTurbo reports that most dog groomer advertisers on Google Ads spend around $800 to $5,000 a month. Set a budget you can measure, and add display only after bookings are tracked.

### Is programmatic better than Google Ads search for groomers?

For people ready to book, search is usually the better first choice because it answers an active query. Programmatic display is better for reminders and wider reach. Use it alongside search rather than instead of it.

### What should a groomer track as the main conversion?

AdTurbo names booking a grooming appointment as the primary conversion to track for dog groomer Google Ads. Set that up before measuring anything else.

### Should a mobile groomer use Facebook as well as Google?

A mobile groomer in Marketing 360's 2023 case study advertised on both Facebook and Google. Test one channel at a time so you can see which one brings bookings.
