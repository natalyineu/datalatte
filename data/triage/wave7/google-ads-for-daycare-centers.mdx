Single-location daycare centers should budget $400-$1,500 per month on Google Ads, according to [AdWave's daycare advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/daycare-advertising). A better way to set the number is from your open seats: multiply the spots you need to fill by the most you will pay to enroll one child, then check that against the cost of a tour request. The worked example below does this with labelled assumptions. Dollar figures are US-based; run the same logic in your own currency.

## How much should a daycare spend on Google Ads each month?

AdWave's childcare guidance gives two ranges. Single-location centers should budget $400-$1,500 per month on Google Ads, and multi-location operators typically spend $2,000-$8,000 per month. The same page says most successful single-location centers spend 4-8% of gross revenue on advertising and marketing.

The revenue range covers all advertising and marketing, not only Google Ads, so do not treat the whole range as your search budget. As a check, assume a center grosses $15,000 a month (assumption). Four to eight percent gives $600 to $1,200 of total advertising and marketing spend.

AdWave's other pages use different ranges. Its tutoring page says learning centers typically spend $1,500-$5,000 per month and 6-10% of revenue, and its gym page puts most gyms at 5-10% of monthly revenue. Those are different businesses, so use them only as context.

## What does a tour cost, and how many enrollments does it produce?

AdWave says cost per tour request on childcare Google Ads typically lands at $35-$120. It also says well-run centers convert 30-55% of tour requests into enrollments. Combining the two gives a rough cost per enrollment, assuming each enrollment comes from one tour request (assumption).

Cost per enrollment equals cost per tour divided by the tour-to-enrollment rate. At the cheapest end, $35 divided by 0.55 is about $64. At the expensive end, $120 divided by 0.30 is $400. These are the edges of the ranges, not expected results for your center.

The useful question is where your own numbers sit. Your tuition, average length of stay and tour process decide which end you land on, so track them rather than assume.

## How do you set a monthly cap from open seats?

Start with the seats. Suppose two infant spots are open (assumption), and you will pay up to $300 to enroll each child (assumption). The cap for that room is 2 x $300, or $600. The monthly budget of $800 (assumption) sits inside AdWave's single-location range.

| Scenario | Cost per tour | Tours from $800 | Enrollments at 30% | Enrollments at 55% | Cost per enrollment at 30% / 55% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low cost per tour | $35 | 22.9 | 6.9 | 12.6 | $117 / $64 |
| High cost per tour | $120 | 6.7 | 2.0 | 3.7 | $400 / $218 |

This table is a calculation, not a forecast. Read it as a warning. At $120 per tour and a 30% close rate, each enrollment costs $400, above your $300 cap. Only the best combinations keep you under the cap at $800 a month.

Until your own tracking shows which scenario you are in, set the monthly budget to the $600 cap for two seats rather than the full $800 range.

## Which campaign type and keywords suit a daycare?

Start with Search. A parent typing "infant care near me" or "toddler daycare" with a neighborhood name is already looking for a place. Keep one campaign per location, and build ad groups by room. If the toddler room is full, do not bid on toddler terms.

Use phrase and exact match on a small budget. Add a brand ad group for your center name. Performance Max can wait until Search produces enough tour requests to learn from, which a single center may take months to reach.

Add negative keywords on day one, such as jobs, hiring, salary, certification, course, degree, training, nanny, dog, pet, free and coloring pages. Review the search terms report weekly and add new negatives as they appear.

## Does a fast fill need a separate campaign?

AdWave suggests a Facebook lead generation campaign with a $20-$40 daily budget, run for 7 days, to fill an empty spot quickly. At those rates, a seven-day test costs $140 to $280 in total (7 x $20 to 7 x $40).

That is a short, separate test, not a replacement for Search. It suits one empty room. Run it only when the spot has a clear start date, and log every lead in the same spreadsheet you use for tours, so you can compare the cost per enrollment against Search.

## Why do reviews matter before parents click?

AdWave states that centers with 4.7+ star ratings on Google and Yelp consistently fill faster than centers with weaker review profiles. Parents compare centers before they call, so a weak rating can waste the clicks you pay for.

Ask families who have had a good experience to leave a review, and reply to every review, including the critical ones. Keep your Google Business Profile hours and room details current. For the local listing side, see [local SEO for daycare centers](/blog/local-seo-for-daycare-centers).

## How should you track tours instead of clicks?

Set the tour request form or booked tour slot as your main conversion, not the click. A click tells you about the ad. A tour tells you about the families who are serious.

Record each inquiry, tour and enrollment in a spreadsheet. Compare your tour-to-enrollment rate with AdWave's 30-55% range. If you sit below it, the problem is usually the tour or the tuition conversation, not the ad. Ask families who toured but did not enroll what held them back.

Answer inquiries quickly. ## What rules apply to ads aimed at parents?

Keyword-targeted Search keeps the audience on parents searching for care. Avoid audience setups that imply you are targeting children, and do not upload children's details or family enrollment records to ad platforms.

Advertising policies change, so read the current versions on the platform's help center before building any audience. Ask a compliance adviser before sharing family data with any ad platform. This is general information, not legal advice.

## FAQ

### How much should a daycare spend on Google Ads per month?

AdWave suggests $400-$1,500 per month for a single-location center and $2,000-$8,000 for multi-location operators. A better method is to multiply open seats by the most you will pay per enrollment, then check the result against those ranges.

### Can I target parents by age or interests?

Keyword-targeted Search is the simplest route. Avoid audience uploads that contain family data, and read the platform's current advertising policies before building any audience.

### What if my ads get clicks but no tours?

Check that the landing page matches the search, loads quickly on a phone and has a visible tour button and phone number. Then check whether tuition is clear, and ask families who did not book what put them off.

### Should I run ads when I have a waitlist?

Usually not for that age group. Pause the ad group for the full room and keep ads running for rooms that still have openings.
