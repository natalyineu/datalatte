## How should a Melbourne salon set up its Google Business Profile?

Your Google Business Profile does the most work for laneway and side-street salons, because clients need to find an entrance that may not face the street. Write the route in the description in plain language, such as "entrance via the laneway off the main street, look for the green door." Name the suburb you serve, whether that is Fitzroy, Brunswick, Collingwood or the CBD, so the listing matches how people search.

Profile accuracy is a quick win. RŌZ says a salon owner can begin correcting common missing or inconsistent profile information in about ten minutes. Check your hours, phone number, service list and category before you spend on ads.

Post offers to the profile as well. Zeely's example is a new-client offer such as "$20 off first color service," promoted monthly. Swap in a dollar figure that fits your AUD price list. The [Google Business Profile posting guide for salons](/blog/hair-salon-marketing-google-my-business-posts) covers the post cadence, and the [local SEO guide for hair salons](/blog/hair-salon-local-seo-guide) covers the citations and reviews that support the listing.

## Which social channels should a Melbourne salon prioritise?

An Australian local marketing guide from Zipzipe, citing Social Media News statistics from February 2023, lists Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn among the most used platforms in Australia. The data is dated, so check current usage before you commit a budget. For most salons, Instagram is the channel that shows work, and Facebook is useful for reaching older clients who already follow local pages.

Short service videos do most of the visual work. Zeely recommends service demos of 15 to 30 seconds that show a treatment in action, and suggests a caption such as "Balayage from $165, book now" as a sample format. Replace that price with your own. Zeely also cites HubSpot for the point that at least 47% of buyers view three to five pieces of content before engaging with a sales rep, which supports a steady rhythm of posts rather than one big campaign.

Stories suit time-limited offers. Zeely's example pairs a story poll with an auto-DM code, POLL15, for $15 off. Associated Hair Professionals describes Instagram and Facebook stories as temporary content formats, so a story promotion needs a clear end date. The [Instagram marketing playbook for hair salons](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-hair-salons) walks through the posting routine.

## How should a salon message clients without breaking opt-out rules?

Bella Booking's two-way messaging is an add-on called Messages Plus. It provides a dedicated number, SMS and email campaigns, and automatic birthday, new-client and follow-up messages, and it is added to any paid plan. The vendor says Messages Plus adds 100 extra SMS credits a month, which never expire, and that email campaigns are unlimited within the add-on, subject to fair use.

Opt-out handling matters. In Bella Booking, a client who replies STOP stops all SMS to that number, while NO PROMOS or STOP MARKETING opts out of marketing messages only. Build your message list so clients who only want appointment reminders are not pulled into promotions. The vendor offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card required, which is enough time to test one reminder flow.

## Where do paid ads fit for Melbourne salons?

Paid ads work best after your profile, photos and reviews are in order. Bookeo's social media guide for beauty salons advises targeting women aged 21 to 55 who live nearby and show interest in hair care and beauty. Use that as a starting audience, then test one service at a time, such as extensions or colour, so you can see which creative drives bookings.

Search phrasing matters too. Trafft gives "hair salon near me," "spa near me" and "nail salon in [city]" as examples of terms people use on Google. For a Melbourne salon, test phrasing that names the suburb, such as "hair salon Fitzroy," alongside the broader local terms, and check the search volumes in Google Ads Keyword Planner for your own area.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a Melbourne salon need to pay for Google visibility?

Not to get a basic listing. RŌZ's comparison table lists Google Business Profile and Google reviews as free. Paid Google Ads is optional, and you can add it once the free profile is complete and accurate.

### Which social platforms do Australians use most?

A Zipzipe guide citing Social Media News statistics from February 2023 lists Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn among the most used platforms in Australia. Check current platform data before you set a long-term budget, since usage changes over time.

### How much does client messaging cost for a salon?

Bella Booking's pricing example gives A$99 per month for a salon with 5 team members on the Professional plan, with two-way messaging, and says there are no per-seat fees and no commission. The Messages Plus add-on is priced separately, so confirm the current total before you commit.

### Should salons in the CBD and Fitzroy run the same ads?

Not necessarily. Test separate messages for each location, since the people you reach from a CBD address and from an inner-north suburb may respond to different offers and different photos. Keep the offer and creative separate so you can see which location is driving bookings.
