Instagram marketing for restaurants means a professional profile with a working booking link, a steady rhythm of food, people, place and offer posts, Reels for reach, and tracking that connects followers to bookings. Instagram is rarely the first step in a diner's decision, but it is a strong second one. In the worked example below, a restaurant spending $580 a month on staff time and boosts needs roughly 48 covers, or about 19 bookings, to break even. The rest of this guide shows how to set that up and measure it.

Most restaurant owners never run that calculation. They post, count likes and hope.

## Where Does Instagram Fit in the Diner's Decision?

Instagram is usually a checkpoint, not the first one. A diner often finds a restaurant through search or a map, then checks photos before booking. The Restaurant HQ, citing Pew Research, states that 53% of urban social media users are on Instagram ([secondary](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/)). Bookeo, a booking software vendor, says about 81% of people use Instagram to research a service, but it gives no sample ([Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/)).

Reach is also large. Adobe Express, citing DataReportal, says at least 1.7 billion people use Instagram, and the largest age group is 25 to 34, followed by 18 to 24 ([secondary](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising)). Use these figures to judge whether your guests are on the app, not to predict your own results.

Your Instagram therefore has to serve two audiences: the stranger who finds a Reel, and the person who already found you elsewhere and is checking you out.

## How Do You Set Up the Profile Before Posting?

Start with the profile, because every Reel, tag and story sends people there. Put your cuisine and area in the name field, write a one-line bio that says what you serve and gives one reason to visit, and add a single link to your booking page. Keep the name, address and hours identical to your Google Business Profile, since diners compare them.

Google's Business Profile Help says you can add one social media link per platform, including Instagram, so add your handle there ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617)). Appointy, a booking software vendor, notes that Instagram and Facebook let a business add a Book Now button to its profile ([Appointy](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/)). Check Edit profile in your own app to see which action buttons your country offers, because availability varies.

Add highlights for the menu, find us, parking, events and private dining. These answer the questions people otherwise send by direct message, and a clear highlight saves replies.

## What Should a Restaurant Post Each Week?

Cadence advice varies. Hootsuite's research, as quoted by a salon software blog, recommends posting to Instagram two to seven times a week ([secondary](https://salonbizsoftware.com/blog/daily-salon-social-media-marketing-ideas-how-to-engage-your-audience/)). Bellwether Coffee's guide recommends three to five feed posts a week plus daily Stories for a coffee shop ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)). Choose a rhythm you can keep for months. A burst you abandon after three weeks does more harm than steady posting.

Sort content into four buckets so the feed stays varied.

| Bucket | Examples | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | A cheese pull, a pour, plating, the pass at 7pm | Create appetite |
| People | The baker at 5am, your longest-serving server | Build trust |
| Place | Patio at golden hour, the bar, the private room | Set expectations |
| Offers and news | New menu, event night, holiday hours | Trigger a booking |

Scheduling tools can cut the admin. A vendor article on AI agents claims a small business that once spent a Sunday planning a month of Instagram posts can build the same calendar in under an hour, which is a vendor claim worth testing. For batch planning, see [AI agents for social media posting for local business](/blog/ai-agent-for-social-media-posting-local-business) and [local business social media content creation](/blog/local-business-social-media-content-creation).

## Are Reels Worth the Effort?

Reels are the format most likely to reach people who do not follow you yet, which makes them useful for restaurants that need new diners. Good options include a 10 to 20 second plating or pour, the opening routine from lights on to first order, the signature dish being made, and a short walk through the room before service.

Show the most appetising frame in the first second. Add captions, because many viewers watch with the sound off. Finish with one clear action, such as "book via the link in bio", so the viewer knows what to do next.

Phone photos still matter for static posts. Shoot near a window with the flash off, wipe the plate rim before each shot, and avoid heavy filters that set expectations the plate cannot meet. Guests notice when the food looks different in person.

## How Much Time and Money Does Instagram Take?

Bellwether Coffee's guide estimates that Instagram needs about 30 minutes a day for a coffee shop, which is a useful planning figure for staff time. Bookeo reports that Instagram users average 33.1 minutes a day on the platform, but it does not name its source. Treat both as rough estimates, not targets.

Paid ads are a separate cost. Adobe Express, citing AdRoll's 2021 figures, says Instagram ads range from $0.20 to $2 per click. Those numbers are dated and vary by market. Meta Ads Manager lets you run ads across Instagram and Facebook from one place ([secondary](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising)), and restaurants can set up campaigns aimed at traffic or reservations ([The Fork Manager](https://www.theforkmanager.com/en/blog/introduction-paid-ads-restaurants)).

Start by boosting a post that already performed well organically, aim it at a radius around your restaurant, and judge it on bookings. Projections from ad examples are not results. A grooming business's illustrative example estimates 10,000 to 15,000 reached pet owners and 200 to 300 new clients, but it labels these as hypothetical ([GroomSoft](https://blog.groomsoft.com/growing-your-grooming-business/)).

## What Is the Break-Even Math?

All inputs below are assumptions for a neighbourhood restaurant. Replace them with your own numbers before deciding anything.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Staff time: 5 hrs/week x 4.3 weeks x $20/hr (assumption) | $430 | $430 | $430 |
| Boost budget (assumption) | $150 | $150 | $150 |
| Monthly cost | $580 | $580 | $580 |
| Bio-link taps per month (assumption) | 150 | 300 | 600 |
| Tap-to-booking rate (assumption) | 5% | 8% | 10% |
| Bookings | 7.5 | 24 | 60 |
| Covers per booking (assumption) | 2.5 | 2.5 | 2.5 |
| Extra covers | 19 | 60 | 150 |
| Contribution per cover after food and direct costs (assumption) | $12 | $12 | $12 |
| Contribution | $225 | $720 | $1,800 |
| Net after Instagram costs | -$355 | +$140 | +$1,220 |

Break-even is $580 divided by $12, which is about 48 covers. At 2.5 covers per booking, that is roughly 19 bookings a month. Two factors move this number fastest.

First, count only extra covers. A regular who would have booked anyway was not won by Instagram. Second, the tap-to-booking rate depends heavily on the booking page. Open your bio link on your own phone, on mobile data, and time how long it takes to reach a bookable table. If it takes more than a few taps, fix that before posting anything new.

## How Do You Track Bookings Instead of Likes?

Tag the bio link with UTM parameters, such as `utm_source=instagram` and `utm_medium=social`, so analytics can separate Instagram traffic from other sources. Add a `utm_campaign` value for each event or offer. Check your analytics platform's live help page for the current tagging rules.

Ask guests how they heard about you when they book, and ask walk-ins for a month. Each month, review which five posts drove the most bio-link taps and make more posts like them. ## What Mistakes Waste Instagram Effort?

Common errors include posting only discounts, leaving direct messages unanswered (people ask "do you have a table at 8?"), skipping the call to action on Reels, going quiet for weeks after a burst, and buying followers. Purchased followers do not book tables and distort every number in your monthly review.

## FAQ

### How often should a restaurant post on Instagram?

There is no official figure. Sources vary, from two to seven posts a week, so pick a rhythm you can keep for months. A common small-team pattern is two to four feed posts or Reels a week, plus Stories on service days.

### Do I need a professional photographer?

Not to start. Natural light, a clean plate and a modern phone are enough for most daily posts. A professional shoot of hero dishes and the dining room once or twice a year can still be worth the cost.

### Are hashtags worth using?

Use a few specific tags for cuisine and neighbourhood. Captions with plain place and cuisine words, a location tag and strong content matter more than tag volume.

### Should I focus on Instagram or Google Business Profile first?

If you must choose, start with Google, since that is where many people search for a table nearby. Keep both consistent, and treat Instagram as the place where diners check the food and atmosphere before they book.

### How do I turn followers into reservations?

Make the next step obvious. Use one booking link, a link sticker on Stories, a call to action in every Reel, and fast replies to table requests in direct messages.
