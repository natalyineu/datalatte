## How Much Should a Philadelphia Hair Salon Spend on Meta Ads?

The only salon-specific figure in this guide is Bookeo's suggested starting range of $750 to $1,500 per month. Adwave's guides for other single-location businesses give wider bands. Its restaurant guide puts Meta ads at [$500 to $2,000 per month for most single-location restaurants](https://adwave.com/resources/restaurant-advertising-complete-marketing-guide), and its dental guide uses the same band. Its channel comparison for standalone Meta buys [often lands between $300 and $1,000 per month](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels).

For a salon, starting at the lower end of the salon range is a reasonable test. A test budget shows whether the ads produce booking-form submissions and bookings at a cost you can sustain. None of these sources publishes Philadelphia-specific pricing, so check the live cost per click and reach figures inside Meta Ads Manager once your campaign is running. Those in-account numbers are the ones to plan from.

Treat the first month as data collection. Raising spend before you know which ad converts is the most common way a small budget turns into a wasted one.

## Which Ad Creative Works Best for Salon Ads?

Video is the format to lead with. Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, which says [short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners). The source is a vendor summarising Meta's report and covers all verticals, not salons alone, so use it as a direction rather than a promise.

Stories are a second option. One hairstyling industry article notes that [Instagram and Facebook stories are temporary content formats](https://www.associatedhairprofessionals.com/updates/blog-posts/power-social-media-your-hairstyling-brand) that creators use to share snapshots of their work. That temporary quality suits limited-time offers and same-week openings.

Ads perform better when they match what the salon already posts. Bookeo says [most salon owners find three to four posts weekly on Instagram and one to two on Facebook sustainable](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/). A steady organic feed gives you finished clips to turn into ads. The [Instagram marketing guide for hair salons](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-hair-salons) covers how to build that feed.

## How Do You Set the Radius and Audience for Salon Ads?

Start with the radius Bookeo recommends: [5 to 10 miles around the salon for Facebook and Instagram ads](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/). A radius that is too tight limits reach. A radius that is too wide spends budget on people who will never book a cut at your location.

Next, check when your followers are online. Bookeo advises looking at [peak times in the Instagram or Facebook Insights tab, which requires a business account](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/). Schedule ads and posts around those windows once you have enough data.

Meta lets advertisers choose a goal such as traffic or reservations, and one restaurant marketing guide notes that [campaigns can be set up with specific goals across Facebook, Instagram or both](https://www.theforkmanager.com/en/blog/introduction-paid-ads-restaurants). For a salon, the goal should match the next step you want: a booking page visit or a booking form submission. The [Facebook ads guide for hair salons](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons) goes deeper on audience settings.

## How Do You Set Up Ads Manager and Tracking?

Meta Ads Manager is the central place to run ads. Adobe describes it as [a centralised point to run ads across Instagram and Facebook](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising). Turrboo lists [Facebook, Instagram and Messenger](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media) among the placements it covers. Adwave explains that [Instagram ads run through the same system as Facebook ads because Instagram is owned by Meta](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business), so one campaign can run on both.

Before spending, work through this checklist:

1. Confirm the Instagram account is a business account, so Insights data is available.
2. Create one campaign with a clear goal and a single offer.
3. Set the radius to the salon's service area, starting within 5 to 10 miles.
4. Install tracking on the booking confirmation page, and check the current setup in Meta's help centre.
5. Add tracking parameters to every ad link so the booking system shows where each booking came from.
6. Review results after four weeks before changing the budget.

Meta also offers free training. One guide notes that [Meta's Blueprint certification covers Facebook and Instagram advertising at no cost](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/). Check the Blueprint site directly for current course details.

## How Should Meta Ads Fit With Organic Social and Email?

Paid ads work best as one part of a system. Bookeo claims that [Facebook and Instagram can be used to reach audiences and build trust without spending a dollar on the platform itself](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/). That is a vendor statement about organic reach, not a measured result for your salon.

Bookeo also claims that a client who has seen the salon's brand on Facebook five to ten times converts at two to three times the rate of a cold searcher. The page gives no source for that figure, so treat it as a hypothesis to test, not a benchmark.

Once a person books, email and automation keep them coming back. The [marketing automation guide for hair salons](/blog/marketing-automation-for-hair-salons) shows how to send follow-ups after an appointment, and the [email marketing guide for hair salons](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-for-hair-salons) covers rebooking reminders.

## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Meta ads per month?

Bookeo suggests starting at $750 to $1,500 per month for Facebook and Instagram ads. Begin at the lower end, measure bookings for at least four weeks, and then decide whether to increase spend based on your own cost per booking.

### What radius should a salon use for Facebook and Instagram ads?

Bookeo recommends a 5 to 10 mile radius around the salon. Narrow the radius if the budget is small, and widen it only after you see which areas book.

### Do Meta ads cost the same in Philadelphia as in other US cities?

None of the sources in this guide publishes Philadelphia-specific pricing. Check the cost per click and reach in Meta Ads Manager during your campaign, since those live figures reflect your own auction.

### Is video better than static images for salon ads?

Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, as cited by Adwave, says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images. Test video against your best static photo before committing the full budget.
