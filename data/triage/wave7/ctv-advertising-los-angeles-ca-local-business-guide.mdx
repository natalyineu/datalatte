Connected TV (CTV) advertising places a video ad inside streaming content on internet-connected televisions. According to Paramount Ads Manager, these ads are non-skippable and typically run 15 or 30 seconds, full-screen. Programmatic systems buy and serve them automatically. Streaming services such as Hulu and Peacock are among the places where this inventory can appear. This guide explains the format, how targeting and budgets work, and how to run a small test that you can measure honestly.

## What Is CTV Advertising for a Los Angeles Business?

Connected TV is a television that connects to the internet and lets viewers stream content, according to [Simpli.fi's CTV explainer](https://simpli.fi/latest/what-is-ctv). The same article describes CTV advertising as video ads delivered through streaming content on smart TVs and devices such as Roku, Fire TV and gaming consoles.

The buying side is largely automated. [Paramount Ads Manager](https://adsmanager.paramount.com/insights/what-is-ctv-advertising-definitions) explains that programmatic advertising uses automated technology to buy and serve ads in real time, with the platform purchasing inventory that matches criteria the advertiser sets. [Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/connected-tv-advertising) notes that its streaming TV ads are currently purchased the same way.

For a Los Angeles business, the practical point is that the ad appears on a TV screen in a household, not on a phone. That is a different environment from search or social, and it needs a different creative plan.

## How Should a Local Business Set Up Targeting?

Targeting options differ by platform, so start by reading each platform's live targeting documentation. Location-based methods for local ads are covered in [geofencing advertising for local businesses](/blog/geofencing-advertising-local-business-guide), which explains the logic behind matching ads to places.

Programmatic buyers set criteria, and the platform purchases inventory that fits them. Amazon's guide notes that CTV programmatic pricing draws on metrics such as video completion rate (VCR) and cost per completed view (CPCV). Choose the metric that matches your goal before you launch, and keep the same metric when you compare campaigns.

Do not assume that a national audience figure applies to your postcodes. Use the platform's own reporting to see where your ads ran and how many households were reached.

## What Should a 15-Second CTV Ad Include?

Paramount Ads Manager recommends a strong opening hook within the first 3 to 5 seconds, because viewers on streaming TV are not waiting for an ad to start. Amazon notes that the VCR of CTV ads tends to be very high because many streaming services do not allow ads to be skipped, so your message has a real chance to be seen in full.

Creative specifications differ by platform. A [BidWaves page](https://www.bidwaves.com/what-we-do/small-business-digital-marketing-services/programmatic-advertising) is not a source for specs, but a [Beasley page](https://advertiseintampa.com/services/advertising/ctv-ott/) notes that streaming giants such as Amazon and Apple each set their own rules for resolution, frame rate, bitrate and format. Confirm the spec sheet with each platform before you upload.

Use this checklist to build a first ad:

1. Put the hook in the first 3 to 5 seconds, so the viewer knows what the ad is about.
2. Name the business early and keep the logo visible.
3. Show one benefit or service clearly, not several.
4. End with one call to action, such as a phone number, address or web address.
5. Export the file to each platform's current specification.

## What Does a Monthly CTV Test Budget Buy?

The example below uses assumption-labelled inputs so you can check the arithmetic against your own numbers. The $40 CPM is borrowed from an [Adwave example](https://adwave.com/resources/netflix-advertising-small-business), which describes a $200 to $400 monthly test at $40 programmatic CPMs delivering about 5,000 to 10,000 impressions. It is a vendor example, not a market rate for Los Angeles.

Inputs (all assumptions):
- Monthly test budget: $400
- Programmatic CPM: $40
- Share of impressions completed: 80%

Calculation:
- Impressions = $400 ÷ $40 × 1,000 = 10,000 impressions
- Completed views = 10,000 × 80% = 8,000 completed views
- Cost per completed view = $400 ÷ 8,000 = $0.05

Replace each assumption with your platform's quoted CPM and your measured completion rate after the first flight. The formula stays the same, and the result tells you whether the test is priced in a range you can afford to repeat.

## How Do You Measure CTV Results?

CPCV and VCR tell you about delivery: how much of each ad was watched. They do not show whether customers came in. CTV usually works through recall, so measure outcomes that a viewer might take later, such as calls, visits or branded searches. [How to track marketing ROI for a local business](/blog/how-to-track-marketing-roi-local-business-guide) sets out a method for linking spend to outcomes.

Keep a simple baseline before the test starts. Record weekly calls, walk-ins and branded search volume for at least four weeks, then compare the same measures during and after the flight. Ask new customers how they heard about you, and log the answers consistently. Check the platform's live help page for the reporting it provides.

## FAQ

### Are CTV ads skippable?

Paramount Ads Manager describes CTV ads as non-skippable video advertisements. Amazon adds that many streaming services do not allow ads to be skipped, which is why completion rates are high.

### Is streaming TV the same as CTV?

They are closely related but not identical terms. Amazon describes streaming TV as the digital distribution of video content across devices with an internet connection rather than a cable signal. Simpli.fi describes CTV as the internet-connected television itself.

### What is a FAST channel?

A free ad-supported streaming TV channel is free for viewers and supported entirely by advertising. Paramount Ads Manager gives Pluto TV as an example.

### Are CTV ads bought programmatically?

Amazon states that its streaming TV ads are currently purchased programmatically, meaning they are bought and sold digitally by an automated system. Other platforms may use different buying paths, so check each one's current options.
