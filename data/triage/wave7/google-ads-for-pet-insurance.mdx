## How Much Should a Pet Insurance Agency Spend on Google Ads?

Adwave recommends that single-agent offices budget $1,500-$4,500 per month on Google Ads. The same vendor suggests $300-$800 per month if you hire outside help for Google Business Profile work. Focus Digital's 2025 guidelines list $5,800 as the minimum viable monthly budget for Insurance, which sits above the vendor range.

The sources use different assumptions and neither is specific to pet insurance, so treat these figures as a range to test rather than a target. A one-producer agency with a single state licence can start at the lower end, then move up only if cost per quote and cost per policy justify it.

If you outsource account management, compare the fee against the spend it manages. The [guide to finding a Google Ads manager](/blog/best-google-ads-manager-local-business-how-to-find) covers what to ask before signing.

## Is the Pet Insurance Market Large Enough to Advertise To?

The best-known published figure is from the UK. An Association of British Insurers estimate, cited by ManyPets on a [Think with Google UK page](https://business.google.com/uk/think/search-and-video/growth-automation-performance-max/), says only 45% of UK cat and dog owners hold insurance policies for their pets. That means most UK owners are uninsured, which is a reason to explain cover clearly.

This is a UK estimate, not a US sizing figure. Do not use it to forecast a US campaign. Before you set a budget for US searches, check current US industry data directly from the publisher, and use your own search volume from Keyword Planner to size demand in your states.

Most people searching for pet cover are comparing options for the first time, so the landing page has to do more explaining than a typical quote page.

## What Compliance Checks Apply to Leads and Remarketing?

Insurance advertising is regulated by state, so check your state insurance department's rules before you launch in any state. That is the first compliance step, and it applies regardless of the channel you use.

Data handling is a second layer. Adwave explains that [CAN-SPAM is a U.S. law focused on commercial email](https://adwave.com/resources/can-spam-and-gdpr-compliance-what-small-business-owners-must-know). It regulates how a business sends promotional messages, not every way it collects or stores customer data. The same page notes that CAN-SPAM penalties can be assessed on a per-email basis, so a follow-up sequence with many sends carries more exposure than a single email.

GDPR is the other rule to check. The law took effect on May 25, 2018, and the page says a lead form, remarketing audience, CRM entry or follow-up campaign can fall in scope if it involves data from EU residents. A survey cited by Mailforge found over 50% of European small businesses felt uncertain about key compliance requirements in 2019, so this is not an edge case.

Adwave's [cold email compliance guidance](https://adwave.com/resources/cold-email-compliance) recommends assigning separate owners for DNS and authentication, the suppression process, and template and jurisdiction review. Even a small agency can split those tasks between two people.

## Which Searches and Ad Claims Should You Use?

Start with searches from people who are ready to compare or buy, then add the pet type, breed or cover type. Keep the match types tight at first. Exact and phrase match give you more control over who sees the ad, while broad match can bring in loosely related searches that cost money without producing quote requests.

Add negative keywords for job seekers, free-cover seekers and existing policyholders who need a claim, so the budget goes to people who can buy. Negatives match only the exact terms you add, so include plurals and common misspellings yourself.

Ad copy must match what the policy actually covers. If a policy has exclusions or waiting periods, say so on the landing page rather than leaving them for the policy document. Drafting variants with an AI tool speeds up the work, but a licensed person should check every claim. The [ChatGPT prompts guide for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) shows how to brief a tool without letting it invent terms.

## How Do You Calculate Cost per Policy?

Cost per click tells you little on its own. Cost per policy is the figure to manage. The worked example below uses assumptions for every input except the $1,500 monthly budget, which sits at the bottom of Adwave's single-agent range.

| Input or result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (from Adwave range) | $1,500 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $4.00 |
| Clicks | 375 |
| Quote request rate (assumption) | 3% |
| Quote requests | 11.25 |
| Cost per quote request | $133.33 |
| Quote-to-policy rate (assumption) | 25% |
| Policies | 2.81 |
| Cost per policy | $533.33 |
| Commission-relevant value per policy over its life (assumption) | $450 |

At these inputs, each policy costs $533 to acquire and returns an assumed $450, so the account loses money. The $450 is a placeholder, not a published figure. Replace every assumption with your own cost per click, quote rate, close rate and commission data before deciding on a budget.

The useful test is simple. If your real cost per policy is above your real value per policy, improve the landing page and the quote follow-up before raising spend. Whether a person or an [AI tool](/blog/ai-vs-human-marketing-local-business-honest-comparison) writes the copy, the same cost test decides whether it pays.

## FAQ

### How much should a single-agent pet insurance office budget for Google Ads?

Adwave recommends $1,500-$4,500 per month for single-agent offices. Start within that range, then adjust based on your own cost per quote and cost per policy.

### Does the 45% insured figure apply to the US?

No. The 45% figure is an Association of British Insurers estimate for UK cat and dog owners, cited by ManyPets. Check current US data from the publisher before using any US market figure.

### Do GDPR rules apply to a US pet insurance agency?

They can. Adwave states that GDPR becomes relevant when the data belongs to an EU resident, regardless of where the business is located. A US agency with no EU residents in its lead data is less likely to be affected, but it should confirm this for its own list and remarketing audiences.

### Can a groomer or vet advertise pet insurance?

Only if they are licensed to sell it in the state where they advertise. Check your state insurance department before running any ads.

### Is $1,500 a month enough to test pet insurance ads?

It is the bottom of Adwave's single-agent range, so it is a reasonable test budget for one producer. Whether it is enough depends on your cost per policy, which the worked example above shows how to calculate.
