To rank in the Google Maps pack in Denver, a coffee shop needs a verified Google Business Profile, the right category, steady reviews and consistent business details across the web. Verification comes first, because an unverified profile does not show publicly. The steps below follow that order, and a worked review example near the end shows how a routine adds up over a month.

## Why does the Google Business Profile matter most?

A 2021 BrightLocal survey, reported by Ahrefs, found that 36% of SEOs considered the Google Business Profile the most important ranking factor for the map pack. In 2013 the share was 23% ([Ahrefs local SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), secondary). That survey shows a trend among SEO professionals, not a fixed Denver ranking rule.

Google describes its Business Profile as a free tool for managing a business's presence across Google Search and Maps ([Keepers Digital](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/), secondary). Creating a listing requires a Google account, which can be registered for free, according to BrewRound's 2020 guide ([BrewRound](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/), secondary).

## How does verification work, and why does it come first?

Per Diem states that without verification, updates will not show publicly and customers may not find the business ([Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide), secondary). Verification methods change over time, so check [Google's verification help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en) before you start. BrewRound's 2020 guide describes a code sent by letter, which may no longer be the only option.

Until verification is complete, none of the other work reaches searchers. Finish this step before you spend time on posts, photos or citations.

## Which category and Q&A answers help a coffee shop?

Per Diem recommends choosing "Coffee shop" or a closely related term rather than a broad category such as "Restaurant" (secondary). BrewRound notes that you can select one category when creating the listing and add more later (secondary, 2020). Choose the most specific primary category first, then add secondary terms once the listing is live.

The Q&A section is also worth filling in early. Per Diem suggests answering common questions before customers ask them, such as whether you offer free Wi-Fi, dairy-free options or dogs on the patio (secondary). For a fuller walkthrough of profile settings, see [the coffee shop profile optimization guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization). Google also lets users message businesses directly through the listing, so check who answers those messages and how quickly.

## How should Denver coffee shops collect reviews?

Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), primary). The sources here do not give a sourced review threshold for Denver, so treat any fixed number as a target you set for your own shop.

A steady routine usually matters more than a single burst of requests. The example below uses assumed inputs to show how the numbers work.

Worked example (all inputs are assumptions):
1. Assumption: the shop asks 6 customers per week for a review at the counter.
2. Assumption: 1 in 4 asked customers leaves a review.
3. Requests per month: 6 × 4.33 weeks ≈ 26. Reviews per month: 26 × 0.25 ≈ 6.5.
4. Assumption: the shop has 20 reviews today and a target of 50.
5. Months to reach 50: (50 − 20) ÷ 6.5 ≈ 4.6, or about 5 months.

At 3 asks per week, the same shop would need roughly 9 months. The ask rate is the input you control most directly.

## Do citations and the website still matter?

Citations need the same name, address and phone number everywhere the business appears. The sources for this guide do not provide a statistic on citation impact, so keep the advice to consistency. Check each directory listing against the Google profile before building new ones. A [Google Maps embed on the contact page](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) is a simple way to reinforce your location on your own site.

Paid search can use the same location data. Google's help page explains that data for location assets in Google Ads comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and corrections made there flow into linked accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), primary). If you add paid search later, the [coffee shop ad keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-coffee-shops-in-2026) covers keyword choices.

## What is the five-step checklist for Denver?

1. Verify the profile and confirm the listing is publicly visible before any other work.
2. Set the primary category to "Coffee shop", with related terms added as secondary categories.
3. Answer the questions customers ask most in the Q&A section, and check messages regularly.
4. Publish posts and confirm each one shows as Live. Google shows posts on Search and Maps only when their status is Live, and Pending or Not approved posts do not appear ([Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), primary).
5. Ask for reviews on a fixed weekly routine, and keep name, address and phone identical across every listing.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up ([Google Business Profile, CA](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/), primary). Creating a listing requires a Google account, which can be registered for free.

### How long does verification take?

The sources here do not give a timeframe. Check [Google's verification help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en) for the current process and expected steps.

### Can a coffee shop have more than one category?

Yes. BrewRound's 2020 guide says you can select one category at creation and add more later (secondary). Start with "Coffee shop" as the primary category.

### Do reviews alone decide map pack position?

No source here gives a rule like that. Google's help page confirms that reviews show next to the profile in Maps and Search, which makes them visible to searchers, but it does not set a ranking threshold.

### Is the 36% survey figure still current?

Treat it as dated. The figure comes from a 2021 BrightLocal survey reported by Ahrefs, so it indicates a trend among SEO professionals rather than a current Denver ranking rule.
