Google's map pack lists relevant local businesses for searches such as "personal training studios near me", so a studio missing from that set is missing when a prospect is ready to book. ## Why does the Google map pack matter for a Houston fitness studio?

The map pack is the block of local listings that appears above regular results for local queries. Google's Business Profile shows hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos directly in search results, so a prospect can decide to call or visit without clicking through to your website ([How to Make Your Fitness Business Stand Out on Google](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google), secondary; [Yelp vs Google Business Profile](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/), secondary).

Industry opinion points the same way. A BrightLocal survey cited by Ahrefs found that 36% of SEOs in 2021 considered the Business Profile the most important ranking factor for the map pack, up from 23% in 2013 ([Ahrefs Local SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), secondary). That is a survey of practitioners, not a measure of Houston searches, so treat it as direction rather than proof.

Houston-specific search volumes are not published in this guide. Check current volumes in your keyword tool before you commit budget to any term.

## Which Google Business Profile settings should a Houston studio complete first?

Work through these in order. Each step is free to complete.

1. Confirm your primary category, then add each class type you run as a service, such as HIIT, yoga, Pilates or personal training.
2. Write a description that names your service and your Houston neighbourhood in plain sentences a customer would read aloud. Avoid repeating keywords.
3. Upload at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes in session, and coaches. One guide recommends this number for fitness businesses ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), secondary).
4. Publish posts and confirm each one reads Live. Google shows a post on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, so Pending and Not approved posts stay hidden ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).
5. Seed the Q&A with the questions prospects ask most, such as trial class access, parking and class schedules.
6. Check that your hours, phone number and address match your website and your signage.

## How do you keep name, address and phone consistent across Houston citations?

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address and phone number, such as a directory listing. Consistency is the point. "Street" and "St.", or a phone number written with and without brackets, count as different entries to a reader comparing records.

Start by auditing the listings you already have before adding new ones. Correct the existing records first, then add directories relevant to fitness, such as Apple Maps, Bing Places and Facebook. Adding new listings that carry the old details spreads the problem.

Corrections matter beyond search. Location data used in Google Ads comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, so a fix made there flows into linked ad accounts ([About location assets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)).

One landscaping guide reports that a firm which cleaned up its NAP data saw an 18% jump in local pack appearances. That is a single anecdote from a secondary source, so use it as a reason to audit, not as an expected result.

## How many reviews does a fitness studio need to compete?

A landscaping-focused page claims that businesses with more than 50 reviews and a 4.7+ average rating dominate the top map pack spots, without citing a source ([AI Google Business Profile Ranking for Landscapers](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), secondary). Treat 50 reviews and a 4.7 average as a working benchmark to track against your local competitors, not as a guaranteed threshold.

Reviews appear next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search, so each review is also a visible sales statement ([Google review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Ask for reviews shortly after a trial class or first session, when the experience is fresh. Reply to every review, and mention the class type a reviewer describes in your reply.

Avoid offering incentives for reviews. Ask every customer on the same terms and let the review describe their own experience.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a fitness studio?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up for showing on Google Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)).

### Why does my Google post not appear?

Google reviews each post after publishing. Only posts with Live status appear publicly on Search and Maps. Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).

### How many photos should a fitness studio upload?

One guide recommends at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes and staff ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), secondary). Use real photos from your studio rather than stock images.

### Do reviews show in Google Maps?

Yes. Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)).
