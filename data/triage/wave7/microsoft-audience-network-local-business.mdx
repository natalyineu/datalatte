## How Big Is Microsoft's Network for a Local Business?

Microsoft's reach figure covers more than 1 billion people in 187 markets. That number describes the whole network, not the people near your shop. A global total tells you little about how many customers in your service area will see an ad, so the location settings in your account matter more than the headline reach.

If you already run Bing search, read the [Microsoft Advertising guide for local businesses](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business) before adding any non-search placements. Search data shows which services and locations already convert. Prove that search works first, then decide whether extra reach is worth paying for.

Google's display network is easier to compare because Google publishes its inventory size. Its help page says Display campaigns reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail. Microsoft gives no equivalent site count in the material used for this guide.

## Is the $500 Microsoft Advertising Credit Worth Claiming?

The offer is for new US customers. You earn the $500 credit when you spend $250 on advertising charges, excluding taxes, and Microsoft says the offer expires June 30, 2027. Read the terms before you sign up, because the credit has a time limit of its own.

Any part of the credit not used within 90 days of the award expires. It cannot be carried over or restored, even if the account is later reactivated. In practice, a $250 spend only makes sense if you were already planning to test Microsoft ads. Do not spend money to chase the credit alone.

Check the promotion against your plan. If your budget for the next three months is well above $250, the credit reduces the cost of a test. If not, the offer is a small discount on a test you would not run anyway.

## How Does Microsoft Compare With Google Display?

The table below sets out what the cited pages say. Where a page is silent, the cell says so and points to the live help page, rather than filling the gap with guesses.

| Feature | Microsoft Advertising network | Google Display campaigns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stated reach | More than 1 billion people across 187 markets (vendor figure) | 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail |
| Default targeting | Not covered here; check the live help page | Optimized targeting is the default |
| Conversion bidding | Not covered here | Pay for conversions with Target CPA bidding |
| Free creative | Not covered here | Library of images at no cost for responsive display ads |
| Local location assets | Not covered here | Only through Local Campaigns or Performance Max with store goals |
| Cost claim | 19% lower CPCs than a leading platform (vendor claim) | No CPC figure on the Google help page |

Google's help page says optimized targeting is set as the default for Display campaigns, and advertisers can choose a different method. Google also says that advertisers can pay for conversions rather than clicks when they use Target CPA bidding. Those settings matter more for a small budget than the headline reach.

Secondary commentary on [display advertising versus programmatic buying](/blog/display-advertising-vs-programmatic-local-business) notes that Google's display network is limited to Google's ecosystem, while programmatic can reach inventory across several exchanges. That difference matters if you want reach beyond Google properties.

## Does Microsoft's DSP Change Affect Local Advertisers?

A secondary analysis from Strategus reports that Microsoft is sunsetting its Invest DSP, the buying platform, by early 2026. It says the Microsoft Monetize SSP, the seller side, remains operational, and that inventory stays available through Xandr as an exchange.

For most local businesses, this changes nothing directly. You buy ads through the Microsoft Advertising account, not through a DSP. It matters if an agency, a freelancer or a third-party tool buys media for you through Microsoft Invest. Ask them which buying path they use and whether it still works after the sunset.

Ask for written confirmation of the platform and the account that will hold your budget. A change in a vendor's tools can move spend without any change to your own campaign settings.

## How Should You Test Microsoft Ads on a Small Budget?

The worked example below uses assumptions throughout. The only sourced figure is the vendor's claim of 19% lower CPCs, which is not verified and is used only for the cross-check at the end.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget (assumption) | $300 over 30 days |
| Share going to Microsoft (assumption) | 30%, so $90 |
| Cost per click you must beat (assumption) | $1.50 |
| Clicks at that price | 60 ($90 divided by $1.50) |
| Landing page conversion rate (assumption) | 3% |
| Enquiries | 1.8, about 2 |
| Cost per enquiry | $50 ($90 divided by 1.8) |

If one new customer is worth more than $50 to you after your close rate, keep testing. If not, move the money back to your best channel. Replace the $1.50 with your own first-week cost per click before you decide.

For the cross-check, assume your Google cost per click is $1.85 (assumption). A 19% lower price would be about $1.50, because $1.85 times 0.81 is about $1.50. Whether Microsoft's price is actually that low is not something this test can confirm. Only your own account data can.

For budget framing across channels, see the [display advertising guide for small businesses](/blog/display-advertising-for-small-business).

## What Should You Check Before Spending?

Work through these steps in order before you spend the first dollar.

1. Confirm you qualify for the new-customer credit and note the 90-day expiry on unused credit.
2. Pick one conversion goal, such as a call, a booking or a form enquiry, and make sure it is tracked.
3. Set location targeting to your real service area, and check the live help page for current targeting options.
4. Write down a cost-per-enquiry ceiling before launch, using a figure like the $50 in the example.
5. Run a search campaign in the same period as a baseline, so you can compare results.
6. If you want to connect ads to physical locations, read the [geofencing advertising guide](/blog/geofencing-advertising-local-business-guide) before adding location-based audiences.
7. Review the results after 30 days (assumption) and keep, change or stop the test.

## FAQ

### Is Microsoft Audience Network still a product name?

The pages cited here describe Microsoft Advertising's network and do not use the older name. Check Microsoft's live help pages for current product names before you search for them.

### Does the $500 credit apply to every advertiser?

Microsoft states the offer for new US customers who spend $250 on advertising charges, excluding taxes. It expires June 30, 2027. Unused credit older than 90 days expires and cannot be carried over.

### Is Microsoft cheaper than Google for display ads?

Microsoft claims 19% lower CPCs than a leading platform, citing EMARKETER data, but this is a vendor claim. Google's help page gives no cost per click figure. Only a test on your own account can show which is cheaper for your niche.

### Should a local business start with Microsoft or Google?

If you have no Bing search history, start with Google. Google says Google Ads reaches audiences across Search, Display, YouTube and Gmail, which covers more of the places local customers already look.
