A loyalty program for delivery customers works when it gives people a reason to order from you directly instead of through a marketplace. Delivery platform commissions run 15% to 30% per order, according to [AdWave's restaurant advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/restaurant-advertising-complete-marketing-guide), so each direct order keeps money that would otherwise go to the platform. Put a sign-up offer in every bag, link your menu page to your own ordering channel, and let points work across delivery, pickup and dine-in. Start with a 60-day pilot and track four numbers each week.

## What Does a Delivery Order Cost You?

Commission is the cost that a direct order avoids. Delivery platform commissions run 15% to 30% per order, and [AdWave's guide to increasing restaurant sales](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-increase-restaurant-sales) says fees often hit 30% of an order. Both are secondary write-ups, so check your platform's current fee schedule before you build a budget on either figure.

The rate you pay depends on your plan and platform, so treat 15% to 30% as a planning range, not a quote. Each order moved to your own ordering channel keeps the gap between that rate and your payment costs. Payment processing on your own channel is not modelled in this guide, so add it to your own calculation.

## How Do You Move Delivery Customers Into Your Program?

Ask for the customer's details at the moment of the order, when they are already engaged and the offer is easy to understand. A practical setup has four parts:

- A card or sticker in the bag with a QR code to your rewards sign-up.
- A link on your menu page and Google Business Profile pointing to your own ordering channel, as covered in [menu optimisation for delivery platforms](/blog/restaurant-menu-optimisation-delivery-platforms).
- A reward for the first direct order, funded from the commission you save.
- A short follow-up email sequence after that first order.

Post-purchase email works best as a short sequence. [AdWave's loyalty email guide](https://adwave.com/resources/loyalty-email-sequences-keeping-customers-engaged-post-purchase) recommends five emails: confirmation, delivery or service follow-up, onboarding or education, a satisfaction check-in, and one loyalty or referral message. The loyalty message comes last, after the customer has received value.

Timing matters too. [Propello's win-back guide](https://propellocloud.com/blog/win-back-campaigns/) says loyalty programme messaging is triggered 7 to 30 days after last activity, with weekly value-add communication.

Square states there are no hidden fees or locked-in contracts, and plans can be cancelled or switched at any time. Its VIP tier example gives Bronze customers 1.5x points, Silver 2x and Gold 3x.

## What Does One Direct Order Save?

The worked example below uses assumed inputs, labelled in the table. Replace them with your own subtotal, commission and reward before relying on the result.

| | Marketplace order | Direct order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order subtotal (assumption) | $40.00 | $40.00 |
| Commission (assumption, within 15% to 30%) | 25% = $10.00 | 0% = $0.00 |
| Reward given (assumption: 10% of subtotal) | $0.00 | $4.00 |
| Left after commission and reward | $30.00 | $36.00 |
| Payment processing | not modelled | not modelled |

On this order you keep $6 more after paying a $4 reward, using the assumed 25% commission. At an assumed 15% commission the gap falls to $2. At an assumed 30% it widens to $8.

Across an assumed 100 direct delivery orders a month, the 25% case gives about $600 more before processing fees. That figure is the one to test against your reward cost.

## How Do You Know the Program Is Working?

Run the program as a 60-day pilot before changing its rules. [AdWave's customer retention guide](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-retention-strategies-that-cost-less-than-new-acquisition) recommends this, and suggests tracking four numbers weekly: enrolment rate, reward redemption rate, repeat purchase rate, and average spend of members versus non-members.

Track redemptions for 30 days before you adjust the reward threshold or perk value. The same [customer appreciation guide](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-appreciation-ideas-that-cost-under-50) estimates admin time at 15 to 30 minutes a week to track redemptions and spot abuse.

For launch, [AdWave's loyalty budget guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-build-a-customer-loyalty-program-on-any-budget) says to treat the rollout in three stages: prep, announcement and reinforcement. It also warns against programs with five reward paths when one would do.

Vendor survey data points the same way. Square's Future of Restaurants 2025 report, cited on [its marketing plan page](https://squareup.com/ca/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/marketing-plan-for-a-coffee-shop), says 82% of restaurant leaders report loyalty drives repeat visits and 83% say it boosts order size. These are a vendor's survey results, not independent measurement.

## FAQ

### Can I email customers who order through a delivery app?

Check your platform's merchant terms first. This guide does not verify what customer data platforms share, so build your list from customers who sign up directly with you.

### What reward should I give for a first direct order?

This guide uses 10% of the subtotal as an assumed test value. Set your own reward after running the worked example with your margins and reward cost.

### Does a loyalty program pay off for a small restaurant?

It depends on your repeat rate, margins and reward cost. Run the worked example with your own numbers, then use a 60-day pilot to test the result.

### Do I need software to start?

No. [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-appreciation-ideas-that-cost-under-50) puts printed punch cards or simple loyalty cards at $0 to $20.
