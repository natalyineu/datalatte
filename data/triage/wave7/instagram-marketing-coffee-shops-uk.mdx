An independent UK coffee shop gets the most from Instagram by posting a steady rhythm of real photos and short videos, tagging the location, replying to every message, and asking customers how they heard about the shop. A coffee shop guide from Bellwether Coffee suggests three to five feed posts a week, plus daily Stories, and about 30 minutes a day of effort. Paid ads are optional and should be tested on a small budget before any scaling.

No published UK figure for coffee shop footfall from Instagram is quoted here, so the budget examples below use labelled assumptions. Treat them as a planning frame, then replace them with your own numbers after the first month.

## How Often Should a Coffee Shop Post on Instagram?

The two guides give different rhythms. The Restaurant HQ article recommends posting on social media at least once a week, ideally one to two times a day ([The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/)). Bellwether Coffee's Instagram FAQ is narrower and more practical: three to five feed posts a week, plus daily Stories ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)).

For a single café with a small team, the Bellwether pattern is the realistic starting point. Stories can carry the daily specials and the queue at 8am, while feed posts hold the better photos. Three feed posts a week for twelve weeks teaches more than thirty posts in one month followed by silence. The [coffee shop social media strategy](/blog/coffee-shop-social-media-strategy) guide sets out a weekly plan you can adapt.

## What Should a Café Post on Instagram?

Post what a passer-by needs to know before stepping in: the drinks, the room, the people behind the bar, and opening hours. Behind-the-scenes clips such as the morning bake, a seasonal drink being made, or a regular's usual order give people a reason to follow. The [coffee shop Reels ideas](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-reels-ideas-that-get-views) list formats worth testing.

If you roast your own beans, The Restaurant HQ article notes that a coffee subscription suits shops in that position, which gives you a recurring topic for posts. The Instagram bio should carry the area, opening hours and a link to the menu or booking page.

Your Google listing matters as much as the feed. Per Diem advises answering common questions in the profile's Q&A section before customers ask, such as whether you offer free Wi-Fi, dairy-free options, or dogs on the patio ([Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). Use the same address on Instagram and Google so the two profiles reinforce each other.

## How Much Time Does Instagram Take Each Week?

Bellwether Coffee's time estimates give a simple starting budget. It lists Instagram at about 30 minutes a day and Google Business at about 5 minutes a day ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)). The table converts those figures into weekly time and cost, using an assumed hourly rate.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram | 30 min | 3 h 30 min | £42.00 |
| Google Business | 5 min | 35 min | £7.00 |

The £12 rate is an assumption. Replace it with your real labour cost, including the time of whoever runs the till and takes the photos. Bellwether also lists Facebook at about 15 minutes a day for local community and events, and TikTok at about one hour a day. A café with one person on the till may not have the hour for TikTok, so it is reasonable to start with Instagram and Google alone.

## How Do You Track Whether Instagram Brings People In?

Instagram Insights shows reach and profile visits, but not footfall. For a café, low-tech tracking works better. Ask at the till how each customer found you, use a code word or a small offer tied to one specific post, and log the answers on a tally sheet reviewed weekly.

Watch direct messages and tags as well. When a customer tags the café in their own story, reply to it and reshare it where the customer agrees. Ask for Google reviews at the same moment, because a visitor who has just enjoyed the coffee is the easiest person to ask.

Be honest about attribution. A regular who passes the door every morning does not become a new visit because of a post. Treat the tally as a direction, not a precise count, and compare the number of Instagram mentions each month rather than chasing a single figure.

## Do Paid Instagram Ads Make Sense for One Café?

Possibly, for a defined event such as a launch, a new menu or a [grand opening](/blog/coffee-shop-grand-opening-marketing). Keep the radius small, set a short window, and judge the ad by redemptions, not clicks.

Here is a worked test. Every input is an assumption for planning. Spend £70 across two launch weeks. At £0.40 per click, that buys about 175 clicks. If 3% of those clicks visit and mention the ad, that is about 5 visits. At £6 average spend per visit, first-visit revenue is about £30, which is less than the £70 spent. On these numbers the ad does not repay on the first visit, which is common for a low-ticket product.

It only pays if those people come back. Track repeat visits from anyone who mentions Instagram before deciding to run the ad again. If repeat visits are rare, the same money may do more for photography or for review requests. Check the live cost per click in Meta Ads Manager for your own postcode before committing, because the £0.40 figure is not a UK benchmark.

If you do run video, one pet-shop guide advises keeping video ads to 15 seconds or less to hold attention, and suggests starting with micro-influencers of 5,000 to 50,000 followers ([Blackstone Infomatics](https://blackstoneinfomatics.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-pet-shops/)). That advice is written for pet shops, so test it with your own audience before relying on it.

## What Are the Rules for Paid and Gifted Posts?

If you pay a creator, or give free products in return for a post, that content is advertising and must be disclosed clearly. The Advertising Standards Authority publishes the current UK rules for influencer and social media ads. Check that guidance before agreeing a deal with a creator or accepting a free product in exchange for a post, not after the post is live.

Keeping the disclosure visible on every post is the simplest way to avoid a problem, and it costs nothing. A disclosure buried among hashtags or in the bio is weaker than one placed at the top of the caption.

## FAQ

### How many times a week should a coffee shop post on Instagram?
Bellwether Coffee recommends three to five feed posts a week, plus daily Stories. The Restaurant HQ article sets a minimum of once a week. Three feed posts a week is a workable rhythm to keep for months.

### How much time does Instagram take each week?
Bellwether Coffee estimates about 30 minutes a day for Instagram. At an assumed £12 an hour, that is about £42 of time a week, as set out in the table above.

### Should a coffee shop pay for Instagram ads?
Only for a defined goal such as a launch, with a small radius and a way to count redemptions. The worked example above shows how a small budget may not repay on the first visit.

### How do I track footfall from Instagram?
Ask customers how they heard about you, use a code word tied to a specific post, and keep a weekly tally. Treat the results as a direction rather than an exact count.
