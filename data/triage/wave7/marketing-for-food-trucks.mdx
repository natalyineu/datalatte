## How Do Customers Find a Food Truck That Moves?

Customers need one place to check where the truck is today. Choose one link, such as your Google Business Profile, and repeat it on the truck, in your social bios and in every post. Google's posts feature is available in English for single-location food and drink businesses in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, so check whether your truck qualifies. For readers in New Zealand, the [New Zealand small business guide](/blog/local-marketing-new-zealand-small-business-guide) covers the wider local picture.

Published figures on how truck customers discover them are thin. An [article from Keepers Digital](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/), secondary, says Google is where most customers start for businesses outside food and personal services. That wording suggests food businesses should not assume Google alone covers them. Ask customers at the window which stop, app or account brought them in, and log the answers for a month.

## What Should a Food Truck Post Each Service Day?

Post the same three things on every service day: where you are, when you open and the one item to order. Put the address or landmark in the first line, because people read the start of a post first. Keep the wording identical from week to week so followers learn the pattern.

Organic posting is free. [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-food-trucks-mobile-vendors) says posting on Instagram and TikTok costs nothing, so start there before you pay for anything. If customers can pre-order by message, the [Brazil guide on WhatsApp](/blog/local-marketing-brazil-small-business-whatsapp-meta) explains how messaging channels work for small businesses.

## Is a Food Truck Profile Free, and Are Ads Worth It?

The profile is free. Google's Business Profile is free to set up and maintain, and the same AdWave page lists it at no starting cost for food trucks. Organic social posts are also free. Paid activity is where budgets start, so keep it small and test one stop at a time.

AdWave suggests food trucks add $5 to $15 a day on paid social boosts or Google Ads after free organic use. It says $5 to $10 a day targeted to a metro can meaningfully grow a local following, and $10 to $15 a day can capture meaningful local search traffic. These are vendor claims, not guarantees. A [LocaliQ benchmark](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/), secondary, puts restaurants and food at an average $2.05 per click in 2026. For Google Ads setup, the [Germany guide on Google Ads](/blog/local-marketing-germany-small-business-google-ads) walks through the basics.

## Which Profile Rules Apply to a Truck?

Google's guidelines say delivery-only brands must add their service areas and hide their address on the profile. If your truck runs with no public counter and takes only delivery-style orders, check this rule before you publish. Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) are the primary reference.

If you link your Business Profile to Google Ads, the [Google Ads Help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en) says location updates can take up to a day to reflect in the linked account. Plan for that delay when a stop changes. Keep the owner role for one or two key people. [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/managing-google-business-profiles-for-multiple-locations), secondary, says a manager role can reply to reviews and create posts but cannot add users or delete the profile, so a crew member can post without control of the listing.

## Worked Example: Is a $10 Daily Ad Worth It?

Every input marked assumption is your own estimate. The $2.05 benchmark is a restaurant average, not a truck figure. Card fees and labour are left out to keep the math simple.

| Item | Assumption or calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily ad budget | Assumption, within AdWave's $5 to $15 range | $10 |
| Clicks per day | $10 / $2.05 (benchmark CPC, assumption it applies) | about 4.9 |
| Service days per week | Assumption | 4 |
| Spend per week | $10 x 4 | $40 |
| Clicks per week | 4.9 x 4 | about 19.5 |
| Average order | Assumption | $12 |
| Food cost | Assumption: 35% of sale | $4.20 |
| Gross profit per order | $12 - $4.20 | $7.80 |
| Orders needed to cover spend | $40 / $7.80 | about 5.1 |
| Conversion needed from clicks | 5.1 / 19.5 | about 26% |

On these inputs, the ad needs about one in four clicks to become an order just to cover its spend. If your clicks convert at a lower rate, the ad loses money before fees and labour. Track orders by source with a short code, such as "STOP10", and compare results over eight weeks before raising the budget.

## FAQ

### Do food trucks need a Google Business Profile?

AdWave says the profile is free to set up and maintain. Check Google's guidelines for how mobile businesses should list their service area, and whether a delivery-only setup changes what you show publicly.

### How much should a food truck spend on ads?

AdWave suggests $5 to $15 a day as a common approach, and it is a vendor view rather than a rule. Start at the low end with one stop and a fixed cap, then compare sales on that day with a normal day.

### Is Google Ads worth it for a food truck?

It depends on your conversion rate. A LocaliQ benchmark puts restaurants and food at $2.05 per click, so use the worked example to find how many clicks must turn into orders to break even.

### Should a food truck also use Yelp?

Keepers Digital says Google is where most customers start for businesses outside food and personal services, which implies food businesses should check other platforms too. Check where your own customers look, since the article gives no food-specific share.

### Can staff post for the truck without controlling the profile?

Yes. AdWave says the manager role can reply to reviews and create posts, while the owner role should stay with one or two key people. Limit owner access to people who must change the listing itself.
