## Is Klaviyo Worth It for a Small Grooming Business?

It can be, while the list stays small. The free plan caps at 250 contacts and 500 sends per month, according to [a 2026 pricing review](https://thestackreviewer.com/posts/klaviyo-pricing-review/). A list of 200 clients mailed twice uses 400 of those sends, which leaves little room for anything beyond the two core flows.

Costs rise with the list. The same review lists the Email plan at about $100 a month for 5,000 contacts and about $400 a month for 25,000. [AdWave puts email platforms like Klaviyo at $20 to $500+ per month depending on list size](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-ecommerce-stores). For a groomer with a few hundred clients, the first paid tier is the realistic budget line.

If you want automation more than a newsletter, Klaviyo is a reasonable fit. The [Klaviyo guide for local businesses](/blog/using-klaviyo-for-email-and-sms-marketing-in-local-business) covers the wider setup, and the [Mailchimp version for pet groomers](/blog/pet-groomer-email-marketing-with-mailchimp) walks through similar flows on a different tool.

## What Client Data Do You Need Before Building Flows?

Keep it short. For each client, store an email address, the pet's name, breed or size, and the date of the last groom. Record marketing consent at the time of booking, along with where that consent came from.

The last-groom date does most of the work. It drives the rebooking reminder, so it must be updated after every appointment, not once at signup. Store it on the client record, whichever booking tool you use.

Do not collect more than you will use. A birthday field can support a yearly message, but only if you plan to send one.

## Which Two Flows Should a Groomer Build First?

Build these two before any newsletter:

1. Welcome flow. Start it when a new client joins your list. Send a first email covering parking, what to bring, vaccination rules and how to rebook. Send one follow-up a few days later to anyone who has not opened the first.
2. Rebooking reminder. Start it from the last-groom date, timed around how often that pet usually needs a groom. Send one short email with one booking button.

Keep each flow to one send, one delay and one follow-up. [A pet groomer email marketing guide](/blog/pet-groomer-email-marketing) has copy ideas for both.

For longer sequences, AdWave, citing Klaviyo's guidance, suggests 3 to 5 emails across 30 to 90 days for post-purchase flows. Grooming is a service rather than a product sale, so treat that as a guide, not a target.

## How Do You Measure Whether the Flows Work?

Open rate is the easiest number to read, but Klaviyo recommends tracking business-impact metrics such as repeat purchase rate, customer lifetime value increase and email engagement rather than relying on open rates alone, according to [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/loyalty-email-sequences-keeping-customers-engaged-post-purchase). For a groomer, the closest measure of repeat purchase is a rebooked appointment.

Count clients who rebook in the month after each reminder. Ask new bookings how they heard about you, or give the reminder its own booking link so you can trace the source. Compare this count with the same month before the flow went live.

## Should a Groomer Add SMS to the Mix?

Start with email and add texts later. [Klaviyo data cited by AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/sms-marketing-vs-email-when-to-use-each-channel) shows SMS flows account for just 7.6% of total SMS sends but generate 45.2% of total SMS revenue. Automated messages do the heavy lifting, not one-off blasts.

The same source says behavior-triggered messages achieve click rates nearing 10% on average, with top performers exceeding 16%. Keep text consent recorded separately from email consent so you can show where each came from. The [Klaviyo email and SMS guide](/blog/using-klaviyo-for-email-and-sms-marketing-in-local-business) covers setup once email is running.

## What Are the Legal Basics for Email Consent?

Rules for marketing email differ by country and by state or province, and this article does not cover them in full. Consent is the foundation: record when each client opted in and what they agreed to. Every email should offer a clear way to unsubscribe, and opted-out clients should be removed from your marketing list.

Check your local regulator's guidance before your first campaign. If you serve clients in more than one country, ask an adviser to review your sign-up forms. This is general information, not legal advice.

## FAQ

### Is Klaviyo's free plan enough for a pet groomer?
A 2026 pricing review lists the free plan at 250 contacts and 500 email sends per month. A list above 250 contacts needs a paid plan, so check current limits before you sign up.

### How much does Klaviyo cost as the list grows?
The same review lists the Email plan at about $20 a month for 500 contacts, about $100 for 5,000 and about $400 for 25,000, as of 2026. Confirm prices on Klaviyo's live pricing page.

### Which booking tools connect to Klaviyo?
This depends on the tool and on Klaviyo's current integrations. Check Klaviyo's help center for the current list before you choose a booking system.

### Can I text clients with the same consent I use for email?
Keep text consent recorded separately from email consent. Check the rules for your country before you send any texts.

### How many emails should a groomer send?
No single benchmark applies to every shop. Start with the welcome and rebooking flows, then adjust based on unsubscribes and rebookings.
