A real estate agent's website should prove you are credible and make it easy to call, not copy the listing portals. Referrals are a major route to clients.

The National Association of Realtors reports, as cited by Adwave, that 41% of sellers found their agent through a referral from a friend or family member. Those people search your name before they call, so the site needs reviews, shareable results, the areas you cover and a short contact form.

The video and virtual tour figures come from a vendor and a secondary citation, so treat them as directional and test them on your own listings.

## Which pages does an agent site need?

Each page should have one job. A visitor who has been referred wants to confirm you are real, see that you work in their area, and know how to reach you. The table below sets out a practical structure for a solo or small-team agent site.

| Page | Job on the site |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Who you help, the areas you cover, one clear contact button |
| About | Your background and licence details, as your jurisdiction requires |
| Neighbourhoods | One page per area you serve, with specific local detail you can stand behind |
| Buyers and sellers | Your process, what happens first, and fees only if you can state them |
| Results | Recent sales you have permission to publish |
| Contact | Phone, a short form and your typical reply time |

Put your strongest proof above the fold, so a visitor sees it without scrolling. Our [above-the-fold design guide](/blog/above-the-fold-design-local-business) explains how to order that first screen. If you display listings, check your broker's and MLS's rules before you publish them.

## What should a lead form ask?

Ask for as little as you can: name, phone or email, and what the person wants to do (buy, sell or get a valuation). Each extra field usually lowers the number of people who finish the form, so test the length with your own traffic.

Tag each lead with its source. Adwave's hypothetical example tags every lead in the CRM so the agent can see which channel produced listings, not just forms. That only works if the website passes the source through. Our [Google Tag Manager guide](/blog/google-tag-manager-for-local-website-analytics) covers how to set this up without developer help.

Adwave's example also assumes a 2% conversion rate, which means one closing would take about 50 internet leads. That is the vendor's illustration, not a benchmark, so use your own numbers.

## How do you check the site before it goes live?

Run three checks before launch. Adwave's guide on deceptive design lists them as the minimum, and they apply to any agent site with an offer or form:

- The ad and the landing page show the same price or offer.
- Decline or opt-out buttons use neutral wording, not guilt-based labels.
- Analytics separate real form submissions and calls from accidental clicks.

Then have a second person click through the full path on a phone, from the ad or search result to the form confirmation. If the path breaks anywhere, fix it before you spend on traffic.

## Which claims can the site make?

Adwave advises that in real estate, a human should review the final copy, and every personal or outcome claim should be checked. A claim about results is the one most likely to cause trouble if it is not backed by your own records.

Be careful with sample copy from marketing pages. Adwave's sample value line, "homes sell for an average of 3% over asking", is presented as a template, not measured data. Likewise, its sample prospecting message about a house that sold for $50,000 over asking is an example. Do not publish either as your own result.

## How does Google describe an agent's listing?

Google's guidelines for representing your business list real estate agents among individual practitioners, alongside doctors, dentists, lawyers and financial planners. That means the business you list should match how you actually operate, whether as a solo practitioner or a brokerage.

Keep your name, address, phone and hours the same on the site and on your Business Profile, and read Google's current guidelines before you change the listing. ## How much should you spend, and where does the website fit?

Adwave's secondary benchmarks say most successful agents spend 10-15% of gross commission income on marketing and lead generation. Another Adwave page puts the range at 5-10%. Pick the range that matches your own history.

The worked example below uses one assumption: annual gross commission income of $150,000. That gives $15,000 at 10%. Adwave's advertising split for newer agents is applied to that figure. The split does not include a website line, so reserve a separate amount for the site.

| Channel (Adwave split for newer agents) | Share | Annual amount on $15,000 (assumption) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Referral nurturing | 30% | $4,500 |
| Streaming TV | 25% | $3,750 |
| Social media | 20% | $3,000 |
| Direct mail | 15% | $2,250 |
| Google Ads | 10% | $1,500 |

Adwave also claims that TV and referral leads often convert better than internet leads, even when internet leads are cheaper to buy. That is the vendor's view, so test it with your own CRM data.

For cost per lead, Adwave's illustration compares $25 per lead on Facebook with $75 per lead on Google. Using its 2% and 50-lead example (both assumptions), a closing costs about $1,250 in Facebook leads and about $3,750 in Google leads. Your own conversion rate will change those totals. ## FAQ

### Do agents still need a website?

Referrals drive much of agent choice, and referred clients usually search your name first. The site gives them something to check before they call.

### How many neighbourhood pages should I build?

Only for areas you serve and can describe with real local information. Pages that repeat the same text add little and can weaken the rest of the site.

### Should I send paid traffic to the site?

Possibly. Adwave's split allocates 10% to Google Ads for newer agents, which is a starting point rather than a rule. Use the [keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-real-estate-agents-in-2026) and the [email guide](/blog/email-marketing-for-real-estate) to decide where the site fits in your funnel.

### Are the video and virtual tour figures reliable?

Treat them as directional. The 403% and 40% figures come from a vendor page citing other sources, so run your own test on listings with and without video.
