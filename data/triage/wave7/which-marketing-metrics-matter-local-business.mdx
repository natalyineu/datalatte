Local businesses should track the actions that lead to a sale: calls, bookings, orders, direction requests and repeat visits, each with what it cost to earn. Likes, followers and impressions matter only when they lead to one of those actions. Cost per lead differs by industry, so the most useful benchmark is your own cost per new customer, measured the same way every month. This guide covers the metrics worth checking weekly, how to turn spend into cost per customer, and which numbers to ignore.

## Which Metrics Should You Check Each Week?

Adwave's metrics guide sorts measures into four funnel stages: awareness, consideration, conversion and retention. That structure works for local businesses too. Awareness covers how many people see your business. Consideration covers profile visits and calls. Conversion covers bookings, orders and sign-ups. Retention covers repeat visits. Pick one or two metrics from each stage and review them on the same day every week.

Google Business Profile is a free tool that shows hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos directly in search results, according to [the Association of Fitness Studios](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google) (secondary). Check the platform's live help page for which actions its reports track, because reporting options change.

## How Do You Define a Conversion?

A conversion is an action that matters to your business. Decide the list before you spend anything. A salon counts a booking. A café may count an order or a loyalty sign-up. A groomer counts a booking request or a call. A studio counts an intro-class sign-up, which [Adwave reports](https://adwave.com/resources/ctv-advertising-drives-foot-traffic-physical-stores) typically lifts 25-45% during well-targeted campaigns. Everything else is secondary.

Coffee shops can also remove friction. [Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide) recommends answering common questions in the profile's Q&A section, such as free Wi-Fi, dairy-free options or dogs on the patio. Track whether those visitors book or order more often. For follow-ups, compare [SMS and email](/blog/sms-vs-email-marketing-local-business-which-is-better) on the same conversion metric rather than on opens.

## What Does Repeat Business Look Like in Numbers?

EnvisionNow, citing Marketing Metrics, gives a 60-70% probability of selling to an existing client against 5-20% for a new one. That gap is why repeat visits belong on your weekly list. [Winback Engine](https://winbackengine.com/blog/customer-reactivation-guide/) treats gyms and fitness studios that normally visit 2-4 times per week as lapsed after 30-60 days of inactivity, so a studio can flag members who have gone quiet.

Adwave's café example shows the same logic in dollars. A customer who orders a $5 latte once generates $5. A customer who drops in 250 days a year generates $1,250. Adwave also says most coffee buyers settle into a default café within 30 days of a habit forming, and that most cafe customers live or work within a 5-minute walking or driving radius. Track repeat visitors by postcode or ZIP code, and [automation can help](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-coffee-shops) keep them returning.

## How Do You Turn Spend Into Cost per Customer?

Divide spend by new paying customers, not by clicks or leads. The worked example below uses a coffee shop. Inputs marked as assumptions are illustrative; replace them with your own figures.

| Input or result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly Meta budget (assumption, inside Adwave's $300-$1,500 range) | $600 |
| Cost per first visit (assumption, inside the $10-$25 suburban range) | $12 |
| First-time visitors from the budget ($600 divided by $12) | 50 |
| Share who become habitual visitors (assumption) | 10% |
| Habitual customers (50 times 10%) | 5 |
| Cost per habitual customer ($600 divided by 5) | $120 |
| Annual revenue per habitual customer (Adwave's $5 latte, 250 days) | $1,250 |
| Annual revenue from 5 habitual customers | $6,250 |

This is revenue, not profit. Subtract cost of goods and labour before comparing the $6,250 with the $600 spend. The 250-day figure is Adwave's example, so swap in your own visit count if you have it.

## Which Metrics Can You Mostly Ignore?

Likes, follower counts and raw impressions do not show whether anyone bought. They can show that content is reaching people, but they should not drive budget decisions. Posting count is an input rather than a result. Restaurant HQ suggests posting at least once a week and ideally one to two times a day, but a busy feed does not prove sales.

Engagement rate is slightly more useful. Adwave defines it as total engagements divided by total followers, multiplied by 100. Its benchmark source, Sprout Social, reports that average inbound engagements on brand content climbed 20% year over year in 2024. Treat that as context, not a target, and check whether engagement actually leads to bookings.

## How Should Studios and Groomers Track Their Numbers?

Studios can measure response speed. [Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) advises following up with pop-up contacts within 24 hours using an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades fast after a pop-up ends. Track the share of new contacts who receive that follow-up inside the window. For budget, Mariana Tek notes that many boutique studios spend 5-10% of monthly revenue on marketing. Adwave suggests 45-55% of spend on Meta for fitness and wellness businesses. [Automation](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-fitness-studios) can handle the follow-up timing.

Groomers should size the audience before tracking conversions. A [UPrinting summary](https://www.uprinting.com/blog/10-proven-ways-to-attract-more-pet-owners-to-your-grooming-business/) citing the National Library of Medicine reports that 30% of dog owners use full-service grooming salons and 41% groom at home. That is a market share, not your conversion rate. Track bookings against it. When you store client records, [BuilderGrowthEngine](https://buildergrowthengine.com/top-client-communication-tools-for-pet-grooming-businesses/) advises choosing a platform that complies with data privacy regulations and securely handles client information. If one person runs all of this, see [AI tools and the marketing assistant role](/blog/ai-tools-replace-marketing-assistant-local-business).

## FAQ

### How many metrics should a local business track?
Start with one or two per funnel stage, which means about four to eight in total. Review them on the same day each week.

### Is cost per lead enough to judge a campaign?
No. A lead is not a customer. Divide spend by new paying customers, as the worked example shows.

### How often should I check these numbers?
Check bookings, orders and spend weekly. Check repeat visits monthly, since Adwave says habits usually form within 30 days.

### Do I need a paid tool to track this?
Not to start. A free Business Profile and your booking or POS records cover the basics. Check each platform's live help page for what its reports include.
