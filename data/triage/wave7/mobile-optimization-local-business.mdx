A local business website is mobile-optimized when its phone version carries the same content as the desktop site, loads the main content quickly, makes the phone number tappable, and keeps the layout from jumping while it loads. Google indexes the mobile version of sites, so gaps there affect search visibility too. Most sites still have room to improve: [Adwave's summary of the HTTP Archive Web Almanac](https://adwave.com/resources/mobile-first-website-design) (secondary) reports that only 48% of mobile websites passed all Core Web Vitals in 2025.

## What Does Google Check on a Mobile Site?

Google switched to mobile-first indexing in late 2020, which means it evaluates the mobile version of a website when it decides rankings, as [Grubhub's restaurant SEO guide](https://get.grubhub.com/blog/restaurant-seo/) explains (secondary). The change followed the April 2015 "Mobilegeddon" update, which prioritised mobile-friendly sites, according to [an overview of digital marketing changes](https://www.impactbc.com.sg/ultimate-guide-digital-marketing-success/) (secondary).

For a local business, the practical test is simple. If a service, price or opening-hours line appears on desktop but is hidden or trimmed on phones, it may be missing from what Google sees. Compare the two versions side by side, paying attention to headings, page titles, image alt text and the main text blocks.

Check Google's live mobile-first indexing help page for the current guidance on separate mobile URLs and responsive pages, since the rules are updated over time.

## How Fast Should a Local Business Page Load on Mobile?

Aim for under 3 seconds. [Adwave's spring marketing guide for local businesses](https://adwave.com/resources/spring-marketing-ideas-local-business) (secondary) recommends that mobile websites load in under 3 seconds, and the speed case is strong. Google-cited analysis, summarised in [Adwave's form optimization guide](https://adwave.com/resources/form-optimization-conversions) (secondary), found that bounce probability rose 123% as load time increased from one second to ten.

Core Web Vitals are the measurements Google uses to judge page experience. Their exact thresholds change, so check the current values on web.dev before setting a target. Run your home page through PageSpeed Insights on a phone profile, and note the loading, responsiveness and layout-stability scores.

Speed fixes that usually help small sites are compressing images, resizing them to the size they display at, and avoiding heavy sliders or video on the first screen. Retest after each change, because a new plugin or chat widget can undo a gain.

## What Should Sit on the First Screen of a Phone?

Phone visitors usually want one of three things: to call, to get directions, or to check hours. Put those within the first screen, and keep one main action on the page.

A tap-to-call link is the most direct option. Constellate's 2023 review found that 76.6% of 303 law-firm websites included at least one click-to-call link, as [Adwave's microcopy guide](https://adwave.com/resources/website-microcopy-that-converts) reports (secondary). Local businesses in other trades can use the same pattern.

PatientMoon's guide to Pilates studio websites describes calls to action as the lines that lead potential clients to take action on a site, and it recommends clear, concise wording ([patientMoon](https://www.patientmoon.com/marketing-business-strategy/pilates-studios), vendor). For wording examples, see our [guide to local business website copy](/blog/how-to-write-website-copy-local-business-examples).

Keep address and hours as plain text rather than inside images. Text is readable by both visitors and search engines, and it stays consistent with your listing.

## How Do You Build a Mobile Checklist?

Work through these steps on a real phone, on mobile data rather than office wifi:

1. Open the home page and time how long the headline and main image take to appear.
2. Tap the phone number and confirm the dialer opens with the right number.
3. Scroll slowly and watch for buttons or text that shift as images arrive.
4. Complete the booking or contact form, and check that errors are clear.
5. Compare services, prices and hours with the desktop version.
6. Confirm the site loads over HTTPS. Adwave's security guide cites a third-party figure that 87.6% of websites used a valid SSL certificate by 2024, up from 18.5% six years earlier (secondary).
7. Check contrast and tap-target sizes. Adwave describes WCAG 2.2 Level AA as a sensible target for most small business websites today ([Adwave accessibility guide](https://adwave.com/resources/website-accessibility-ada-compliance-for-small-business-sites), secondary).

If your site has a map, check that it scales on a phone. Our [Google Maps embed guide](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) covers sizing. For the wider search picture, see the [15-step local SEO guide](/blog/how-to-improve-local-seo-for-your-small-business-15-steps-that-work-in-2026).

## FAQ

### What are the Core Web Vitals thresholds?
Google publishes exact thresholds for loading, responsiveness and visual stability on web.dev. Check the live page for current values, then test your mobile pages against them.

### Does Google use my mobile site for rankings?
Yes. Google switched to mobile-first indexing in late 2020, so it evaluates the mobile version of a site when deciding rankings.

### Do I need a separate mobile site?
Not necessarily. A single responsive site is the simplest setup to maintain, provided the mobile version carries the same content as desktop.

### How much can a faster page help?
Google-cited analysis found bounce probability rose 123% as load time went from one second to ten. Results vary by business, so test your own pages.

### How do I add a tap-to-call button?
Use a link that starts with tel: followed by your number. On most phones, tapping it opens the dialer.
