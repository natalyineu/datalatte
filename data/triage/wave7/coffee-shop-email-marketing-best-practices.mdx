Coffee shop email marketing works best when you send a few relevant messages to people who already know your shop. Split your list into regulars, occasional visitors and lapsed customers, make one clear offer per email, and track redemptions with a unique promo code. Many POS systems include email tools, but [most charge an additional monthly fee of $25 or more](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/), so check that cost before you commit. This guide covers list building, segmentation, sending schedules, a worked budget example and the metrics to track.

## Is Email Still Worth It for a Coffee Shop?

Email is worth testing because customers already say they want it. In the [Square Future of Commerce 2025 report](https://squareup.com/ca/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/marketing-plan-for-a-coffee-shop), 65% of Canadians said they like businesses to communicate with them by email, while 39% appreciate text message updates. Those figures come from a Canadian sample, so US, UK and Australian owners should treat them as a signal to test rather than a local benchmark.

Phone habits also shape how your emails get read. Statista data cited by Square says seven in 10 Canadians use smartphones to access the internet. Write subject lines that work on a small screen, keep the offer near the top and make the call to action easy to tap.

Email also works alongside your public listing. Google lets people message businesses through their profiles, and [Per Diem notes that Google verifies a listing before it goes live](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide), usually with a postcard code. Without verification, updates will not show publicly, so confirm your profile before you promote anything.

## How Do You Build a List Customers Actually Want to Join?

Start with the people already in your shop. Put a sign-up option at the counter, on table cards and on your receipts or app screens, and give a specific reason to join, such as a first-visit pastry offer or early access to a seasonal drink. A vague "subscribe to our newsletter" rarely explains what someone gets.

Keep the sign-up short. Ask for an email address and, if you need it, a first name for a greeting. Add one optional question, such as "What is your go-to order?", because the answer helps you segment later without slowing people down.

Link to your sign-up from your Google profile too. [Per Diem reports that Think Coffee, an independent brand, has thousands of app downloads after placing its app link in its Google profile](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide). That is an app example, not an email one, but the principle of sending profile visitors to a capture point applies to email sign-ups.

## Should You Segment Your Coffee Shop Email List?

Segmentation means sending different messages to different groups, and it is the easiest way to avoid irrelevant emails. Start with one split: regulars and everyone else. Regulars need perks, such as a secret menu item or a skip-the-line pre-order. Occasional visitors need reminders and a small incentive. Lapsed customers need a reason to come back.

Define the groups with a simple rule. For example, you might call someone a regular after eight visits in 60 days (assumption: 60-day window, adjust to your traffic). If you use a POS or loyalty app, export that visit count. If you do not, a tagged spreadsheet is enough to start.

Multi-shop owners can segment by location. A subscriber who visits your downtown shop should not receive a note about a new pastry case at the uptown shop unless they also visit there. Each location can then send its own updates. ## How Often Should a Coffee Shop Send Email?

Pick a fixed schedule and keep it. Customers learn when to expect your emails, and staff can plan the content. Two sends a month is a reasonable starting point for most shops (assumption), with an extra note only when there is a real event, such as a new seasonal menu.

Plan the time before you commit. Bellwether Coffee lists Google Business at about 5 minutes a day, Facebook at 15 minutes, Instagram at 30 and TikTok at one hour, and recommends 2 to 3 hours a week for shooting and writing content. Email writing and list maintenance fit into the same weekly time budget, so decide which channels you can actually sustain before adding more.

Keep each email to one message and one offer. If a campaign has three promotions, customers tend to remember none of them. Put the single offer in the subject line and first paragraph, and use one button.

## What Does a Coffee Shop Email Campaign Cost and Return?

A simple budget shows whether email is paying off. The inputs below are assumptions for an illustrative shop; replace them with your own numbers.

Assumptions: a list of 1,500 subscribers, an average ticket of $6.50, four campaigns a month, and 15 promo-code redemptions per campaign (about 1% of the list). The email tool costs $25 a month, matching the typical POS add-on fee cited earlier.

Revenue per campaign: 15 redemptions × $6.50 = $97.50. Monthly revenue across four campaigns: 4 × $97.50 = $390. Subtract the $25 tool fee and the monthly figure becomes $365 in revenue, before cost of goods. Apply your own margin to find the profit.

Now test a weaker month. If redemptions fall to 5 per campaign (assumption), monthly revenue drops to $130, and after the $25 fee, $105 remains. The gap between those two outcomes is the reason to track redemptions each month rather than assume the list is working.

## Which Email Tools Fit a Small Coffee Shop?

Start with the tool you already pay for. If your POS includes email marketing, check whether the feature is free or charges a monthly add-on. Many do not include it, so compare the monthly cost against the revenue your redemptions produce.

A dedicated email platform may fit better once your list grows or you want automation. [AdWave's guide plans $50 to $300 per month for a loyalty platform, plus $200 to $1,000 in incentive payouts](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes). Loyalty and email are separate budgets, but both should be justified with the same numbers. ## How Do You Track Whether Email Brings Customers In?

Use a unique promo code for each campaign, such as COFFEE1 for the first send and COFFEE2 for the next. Record how many times each code is scanned at the register, because offline visits are the hardest part of email attribution to see.

Track these numbers each month:

1. Emails delivered for each campaign.
2. Redemptions for each code.
3. Revenue from redemptions, using your average ticket if you do not track it directly.
4. Revenue per email, calculated as total email-attributed revenue divided by emails sent.
5. Unsubscribes, to spot content that is not landing.

Compare month to month rather than against outside benchmarks. Check your email platform's help page for current definitions of open and click metrics, because providers change how they count them. For inspiration on campaign formats that you can test against your own numbers, see [coffee shop email marketing examples](/blog/coffee-shop-email-marketing-examples).

If your list is small, paid search can help you reach nearby people who are not subscribers yet. [Google Ads management](/services/google-ads) can target searches near your shop, and seoplus notes that localized phrases such as "coffee shop downtown Phoenix" are likely to face less competition than a generic term, which can mean a lower cost per click.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do customers want emails from coffee shops?
Some do. In the Square Future of Commerce 2025 report, 65% of Canadians said they like businesses to communicate with them by email. Ask for permission at sign-up, keep the frequency predictable and make it easy to unsubscribe.

### Is text messaging better than email for a coffee shop?
Not for everyone. The same Square report found 39% of Canadians appreciate text message updates. Test both channels with consent, and use text for time-sensitive notices while keeping offers in email.

### How much should I budget for email marketing?
Start with your tool's actual price. Many POS email add-ons charge $25 or more a month. Then compare that cost with the revenue your promo codes produce, using the worked example above with your own ticket size and redemption numbers.

### What should I track first?
Start with promo-code redemptions per campaign and revenue per email. Unsubscribes tell you whether people still want your messages, but redemptions show whether the email brings people through the door.
