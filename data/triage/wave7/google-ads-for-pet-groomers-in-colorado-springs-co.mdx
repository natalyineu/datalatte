## What Does a Dog Groomer Click Cost on Google Ads?

AdTurbo's benchmark puts dog groomer clicks at about $2–$8, and warns that a few wasted clicks add up fast at that range ([AdTurbo](https://adturbo.ai/google-ads-for-dog-groomers)). The same page says most dog groomer advertisers spend around $800–$5,000 per month. A wide range like that means your first month is a test, not a forecast.

No source in this guide gives a Colorado Springs-only cost per click. What the local sources do show is competition and price. Petco's Northeast Colorado Springs grooming page lists a Bath Only service from $22 and a Bath & haircut service from $38, with add-ons such as Essentials and Flea Cleanse at an extra $25 each ([Petco](https://stores.petco.com/co/colorado-springs/full-service-grooming-coloradosprings-co-2422.html)). Those prices set the range your ads will be compared against.

Seasonality also changes demand. AdWave, summarising Groomer to Groomer, notes that November and December are traditionally a very busy time for groomers, sometimes too busy ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/pet-grooming-advertising), secondary). Check your own booking calendar before you judge a month's results.

## How Do You Keep Ads Inside Your Service Area?

Barklead suggests targeting users within a 10–15 mile radius of a groomer's location, in its guide to Facebook ads for groomers ([Barklead](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers)). That is a Facebook recommendation, not a Google Ads rule, so treat it as a starting radius only. Set your own distance from the shop based on how far customers realistically drive.

Radius settings are the main cost control for a single-location shop. Check the current location targeting options on Google's live Google Ads help page before you save changes, because those settings and their minimums can change.

Marketing 360 reports that its mobile pet groomer case study advertised on Facebook and Google, which helped it choose the right channels ([Marketing 360](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/)). A mobile groomer has a different radius from a fixed salon, so match the geography to your model. ## What Should You Set Up Before You Spend?

AdTurbo names booking a grooming appointment as the primary conversion to track ([AdTurbo](https://adturbo.ai/google-ads-for-dog-groomers)). Tracking a phone call or a form fill alone can overstate results if those contacts never book. Set the booking as your main goal, then track calls and direction requests as secondary actions.

Work through this order before launch:

1. Set your radius around the shop or service area, using a distance you can drive in your own schedule.
2. Build an ad group for each service: dog grooming, puppy grooming and any cat or mobile services.
3. Add negative keywords for job seekers and training searches, such as "jobs", "salary", "school" and "course".
4. Confirm the booking confirmation page fires as a conversion, and test it with a real booking.
5. Set a monthly budget you can hold for at least four weeks before judging results.

GroomerGenie says its proprietary negative keyword list contains more than 500 groomer negatives ([GroomerGenie](https://www.groomergenie.com/services/google-ads)). That is a vendor claim, so check the terms in your own search terms report rather than trusting a generic list. ## FAQ

### How much should a dog groomer spend on Google Ads in Colorado Springs?

No source gives a Colorado Springs figure. AdTurbo reports that most dog groomer advertisers spend $800–$5,000 a month nationally. Start at the low end, measure cost per booking for at least four weeks, then decide whether to raise it.

### Is Google Ads worth it for a pet groomer?

It depends on what a booked client is worth to you over time. Using the $60 visit value in the worked example, you need about 14 bookings a month to cover an $800 budget from first visits. Run the same calculation with your own prices.

### How long before results show up?

The sources in this guide do not give a learning period. Review the account weekly and judge it on at least a full month of data, longer if your booking cycle is long. Check Google's live help pages for current guidance on campaign learning.

### Should I target the whole Colorado Springs area?

Usually not. A radius around your shop or service area keeps clicks closer to people who can actually book. Barklead's 10–15 mile suggestion is a Facebook guide, so set the distance that matches your driving range and your mobile service area, if you offer one.
