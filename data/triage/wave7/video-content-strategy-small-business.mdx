A video content strategy for a small business comes down to four decisions: one main platform, one repeatable format, a fixed weekly time budget, and one metric tied to bookings or orders. Video marketing is a strategy that uses video content to promote, educate, or engage audiences, often focusing on long-term brand building, according to [StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/programmatic-video-advertising). For most local businesses, that means one short clip a week, filmed in batches and reviewed over a trailing 90-day window.

## Which Video Platform Should a Small Business Start With?

Start where your customers already spend time, then add a second platform only after a steady run on the first. Bellwether Coffee's guide says TikTok is worth using if your customers are under 35, citing significant organic reach for authentic content ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)).

| Your customers are | Start with | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Searching for a service near them | Google Business Profile video | It sits next to the searches that already bring people to you |
| Under 35 and trend-led | TikTok | Bellwether Coffee points to organic reach for this audience |
| Browsing for food, style or fitness | Instagram Reels | Adwave's restaurant guide recommends short videos such as Reels |

Decision rule: do not run all three at once. One channel done every week beats three done now and then.

## Which Video Formats Work for Local Businesses?

Pick one format you can repeat in a short, fixed session. Five formats fit most local niches:

- Before and after shots, with written permission from the customer.
- A process clip showing one drink, dish, cut or treatment from start to finish.
- A single question answered on camera, such as what a first visit includes.
- A short introduction from the owner or a staff member.
- A customer story read aloud, with consent.

Adwave's restaurant guide recommends mixing photos, short videos and customer-generated stories ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/restaurant-marketing-tips)). Its calendar article suggests an 80-20 split for small businesses, with 80% educational or entertaining posts and 20% promotional ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-create-a-social-media-content-calendar-in-30-minutes)). Apply that to video: most clips should teach or entertain, not sell.

Trends fade. Infegy reports that GRWM (Get Ready With Me) video content took off in early 2023 on TikTok and Instagram, after which engagement and post volume declined as content moved toward lifestyle storytelling ([Infegy](https://www.infegy.com/blog/three-ways-to-find-beauty-insights)). Anchor formats in your real work rather than in a passing trend. A fitness studio can apply the same logic, as set out in the [fitness studio social media strategy](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-marketing-strategy).

## How Much Time Does a Weekly Video Plan Take?

Plan time before buying equipment. Bellwether Coffee advises 2-3 hours a week for shooting and writing, and 3-4 hours a week for content creation and scheduling ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)). The worked example below uses two clips a week and the low end of both ranges. Every input is an assumption to replace with your own.

| Item | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Shooting and writing (low end of 2-3 hours) | 2 hours a week |
| Planning and scheduling (low end of 3-4 hours) | 3 hours a week |
| Total time | 5 hours a week |
| Monthly time, four weeks | 20 hours |
| Owner time at $30 an hour | $600 a month |
| Calendar template, free Hootsuite file for Excel or Google Sheets | $0 |

Adwave lists Hootsuite's calendar template as free to use in Excel or Google Sheets, while Later's Notion template needs a Notion account ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/planning-templates-for-every-season)). For the planning side, see [how to create a social media content calendar](/blog/how-to-create-social-media-content-calendar-small-business).

Decision rule: if five hours is too much, cut to one clip a week but keep the planning block. Planning is what keeps the schedule consistent.

## What Does a Simple Weekly Video Workflow Look Like?

Write the plan down. Floodlight New Media research cited by Adwave finds companies with a documented content strategy are 3x more likely to report success than those without one ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/content-distribution-getting-eyeballs-on-what-you-create)). Then follow this checklist each week:

1. List the week's content pillars in the first three minutes. Adwave's 30-minute calendar method starts with a five-minute prep step ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-create-a-social-media-content-calendar-in-30-minutes)).
2. Assign one format to each video idea, and match the platform to your customers using the table above.
3. Film both clips in one session, in daylight if possible.
4. Add captions and put the offer or the action in the first sentence.
5. Schedule the posts, then reply to comments on the day they go live.
6. Upload the same clip to your Google Business Profile.

The same workflow suits restaurants, salons and studios. For more on producing content for local niches, see [local business social media content creation](/blog/local-business-social-media-content-creation).

## How Do You Know If Video Is Working?

Views are a weak signal on their own. Crowbert's social media audit framework defines engagement rate as total interactions divided by reach, and Adwave recommends a trailing 90-day window applied to every active channel when auditing ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/social-media-content-audit)). Judge video over that window, not over a single week.

Track actions that lead to bookings. Ask new customers how they heard about you, watch calls and direction requests from your Google Business Profile, and count clicks from your bio link. Keep the formats that produce those actions and drop the ones that only produce views.

## FAQ

### Which video platform should a small business start with?

Start with the platform where your customers already look for you. For local searches, that is Google Business Profile. For under-35 audiences, TikTok is the platform Bellwether Coffee suggests. Add a second platform only after a steady run on the first.

### How much time should I plan for video each week?

The worked example uses 5 hours a week, based on the low end of the ranges in the coffee-shop guide. These are assumptions. If that is not realistic, cut to one clip a week and keep the planning block.

### Do I need expensive equipment?

Not to start. Daylight, a phone and captions are enough for a test. Spend money on equipment or ads once a video is clearly bringing in bookings. Check each platform's current help page for file limits, because they change.

### Should I outsource video production?

Outsourcing can cover parts of the work. Code Kraft Solutions says its Canadian packages cover social media posts and graphic design, content writing and social media optimisation ([Code Kraft Solutions](https://codekraftsolutions.com/blog/digital-marketing-small-business-canada)). Outsource only if the person films your real staff, premises and work, since generic footage does not show customers what to expect.
