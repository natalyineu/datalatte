The coffee shop marketing ideas worth your time are free and local first: a verified Google Business Profile, a reason to come back, a list of customers you can message and a steady review habit. Paid ads come after those. A [Square guide for coffee shops](https://squareup.com/ca/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/marketing-plan-for-a-coffee-shop) cites over 8,000 coffee shops across Canada as of 2025, and Square's Future of Commerce 2025 report finds 65% of Canadians like businesses to communicate with them by email. This guide covers eight ideas. Each one rests on a published source or is labelled as an assumption. None promises a specific sales lift.

## How Do You Get Found When People Search Nearby?

Start with your Google Business Profile. [The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/) cites Statista data saying 98% of consumers use the internet to find new businesses, so a listing that is incomplete or unverified is a weak first impression. Per Diem's [Google Business Profile guide for coffee shops](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide) explains that Google usually verifies a listing by mailing a postcard with a code to the business address. Without verification, your updates will not show publicly.

1. Verify the profile and pick the right category. Per Diem recommends "Coffee shop" or a related term rather than something broad like "Restaurant." Follow the steps in our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization).

2. Put an order or app link in the profile. Per Diem reports that Think Coffee, an independent brand, has thousands of app downloads after placing its app link in its Google profile. That is one brand's result, not a benchmark. Google also lets users message businesses directly from a listing, so check that messaging is set up and that someone answers it.

## How Do You Build a List Customers Want to Hear From?

Square's 2025 report also finds 39% of Canadians appreciate text message updates from businesses. Collect email and phone numbers at the counter, with a clear promise such as news of new drinks and events. Make the opt-in visible and keep the list to messages you would send anyway.

Most coffee shop POS systems include email marketing tools, but most charge an extra monthly fee of $25 or more, according to The Restaurant HQ. Check that cost before you choose a tool. A third-party benchmark cited by [Brenton Way](https://brentonway.com/blog/beauty-salon-marketing-ideas) puts email returns at $36 to $42 for every $1 spent, based on Litmus and Constant Contact figures. That is general email marketing, not coffee-specific, so treat it as direction only.

3. Collect contacts at the counter. Ask for an email or number at payment, offer one reason to join, and count sign-ups each week.

4. Automate a welcome message. Set one welcome email that runs at sign-up. Mariana Tek's table lists automated email and SMS as low effort once set up, with an immediate payoff, though that advice is written for fitness studios. Our guide to [marketing automation for local coffee shops](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-coffee-shops) covers the setup, and [coffee shop email examples](/blog/coffee-shop-email-marketing-examples) show the copy.

## Which Ideas Bring People Back in Slow Hours?

5. Run a monthly anchor event. First Branch Coffee in South Royalton, Vermont hosts an "Old-time Music" day on the second Sunday of every month, per The Restaurant HQ. A repeating date is easy to promote. For a one-off event such as a grand opening, Livestorm's 2026 Webinar Benchmark timeline sends the first invitation to segmented lists three to four weeks ahead and a reminder 24 to 48 hours before. Those are webinar figures, so use them as a schedule rather than a forecast. The [grand opening marketing guide](/blog/coffee-shop-grand-opening-marketing) applies the same timing to a launch.

6. Text a short list when a slow window opens. Booksy's salon guide suggests an automated SMS blast to a "Top 50" client list can fill a last-minute opening in minutes. It also recommends rebooking reminders to clients who have not rebooked, sent 48 hours later. A café can copy the pattern: text regulars one offer for a quiet window (assumption: 2pm to 4pm). The [salon marketing ideas guide](/blog/salon-marketing-ideas-25-strategies-that-fill-your-chair-every-week) has the salon version.

## How Do You Turn Customers Into Reviewers?

7. Send a tracked review link after payment. DMLY's [Google Business Profile automation guide](https://dmly.io/google-business-profile-automation-guide/) describes review requests sent as a tracked link by WhatsApp, SMS or email, either by hand or automatically after a payment or appointment. Use whichever flow fits your point-of-sale. Ask everyone, not only happy customers, and check Google's current review policy before offering anything in return.

Reviews take time. Mariana Tek's table lists 6 to 12 months for local SEO and Google reviews to pay off, while noting review volume can start to influence visibility within a few weeks. Plan for months rather than days.

## Is Short Video Worth the Time?

8. Film short clips on a steady schedule. Mariana Tek advises posting short-form video three to five times a week, not in bursts, and using highlight clips of 15 to 30 seconds. The advice is for studios, but the cadence transfers. Film a pour, a bake or the morning queue.

Square reports, citing Nordicity, that 83% of Canadian SMBs use TikTok for brand awareness and marketing, and that TikTok boosted their sales by $950 million in 2024. Those are all-SMB totals, not coffee-shop figures. Bellwether Coffee's [social media guide](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops) lists Google Business at about 5 minutes per day for a coffee shop, which is a useful time budget for the profile tasks above.

## Do Paid Ads Make Sense Yet?

Run paid ads after the free steps are in place. Seoplus's [multi-location PPC guide](https://seoplus.com/paid-ads/the-ultimate-guide-to-multi-location-ppc-advertising-on-google-ads/) says a localized phrase such as "coffee shop downtown Phoenix" is likely to have less competition than a generic term like "coffee shop," which can mean a lower cost per click. It is vendor advice, but it suggests a practical test: bid on neighborhood phrases before city-wide ones, and cap the budget for a month.

## What Does an Email Month Return?

This worked example uses one sourced cost and several assumptions. Replace every assumption with your own numbers before you decide.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email subscribers | 350 | Assumption |
| Share who visit in a month after an email | 12% | Assumption |
| Visits from email | 42 | Calculated (350 × 12%) |
| Average spend per visit | $6 | Assumption |
| Email revenue per month | $252 | Calculated (42 × $6) |
| Email tool cost | $25 | Sourced, The Restaurant HQ (typical POS add-on) |
| Revenue after tool cost | $227 | Calculated, before cost of goods and labour |
| Break-even visits | 5 | Calculated ($25 ÷ $6 = 4.2, rounded up) |

Break-even is five visits a month. If your list does not produce that, the tool is not yet earning its place. Track each campaign with a code on a sign or a question at the till.

## FAQ

### Which idea should a new coffee shop start with?

Verify the Google Business Profile and set the category to "Coffee shop." Those steps are free and sit under every other local idea. The [grand opening guide](/blog/coffee-shop-grand-opening-marketing) covers the launch sequence.

### Is email still worth it for a coffee shop?

It can be. Square's 2025 report finds 65% of Canadians like businesses to communicate by email, and 39% appreciate text updates. Check any tool fee first, since most POS email add-ons cost $25 or more a month.

### Can I offer a free drink in return for a review?

Check Google's current review policy before offering anything in return. Do not make a reward depend on a positive review.

### How many ideas should I run at once?

Start with one or two. Track each for at least a month so you can tell what worked.
