Fitness studios in Boston, MA appear in Google's Map Pack when their Google Business Profile is complete, their reviews are recent, their business details match across the web, and their photos and posts stay current. The Map Pack is the block of local listings Google shows for searches such as "personal training studios near me" ([S1](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google)). Google offers the Business Profile free of charge, so the core work is time and consistency rather than ad spend. This guide covers the checklist, the figures that have a verifiable source, and a worked example you can adapt.

## How much does the Google Business Profile matter for Map Pack rankings?

Local search agencies disagree on the exact weighting of ranking signals, and Google does not publish one. A survey cited by Ahrefs found that 36% of SEOs in 2021 considered the Google Business Profile the most important ranking factor for the map pack, up from 23% in 2013 ([Ahrefs, secondary](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo)). That is a measure of professional opinion, not a Google disclosure, so treat it as a reason to prioritise the profile, not as a formula.

For a Boston studio, the profile is the asset you control most directly. The other signals, such as reviews and citations, feed into the same local picture. Fixing the profile first costs nothing but time and gives you a clean baseline to measure against.

## What should a Boston studio fix on its Google Business Profile first?

Start by confirming that the profile is claimed, verified and shows the correct name, address, phone number and hours. Then work through the sections that customers see first. Choose a primary category that matches your core service, and add secondary categories for each class type you offer, such as yoga, Pilates or personal training. Google's own description of the profile is that it shows business hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos directly in search results ([S1](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google)).

Photos are the next priority. One vendor guide recommends uploading at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes in action and staff ([Vertical Impression, secondary](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026)). Use real images from your own studio rather than stock photos. For a wider walk-through of profile settings, see the [fitness studio Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization).

## Do posts and reviews on the profile affect visibility?

Posts can help, but only when they are visible. Google's help page states that a post is public on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, and that Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). After publishing an offer or class schedule, check the status in your dashboard rather than assuming it is live.

Reviews are displayed next to your profile in Maps and Search, according to Google's review guidance ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Google does not publish a minimum review count. A landscaping vendor's page claims that businesses with over 50 reviews and a 4.7+ average dominate top map pack spots, but it cites no source ([AccountabilityNow, secondary](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/)). Treat that as a target to test in your own market, not a rule.

## How should a Boston studio manage citations and NAP consistency?

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address and phone number. The goal is that every mention matches exactly. "St." and "Street" or two different phone formats can create duplicates that confuse search engines and customers. Make a single master version of your details and copy it everywhere.

If you run Google Ads alongside your profile, the link matters too. Google states that location asset data comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and that successful corrections made there flow into linked Google Ads accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). Fix the profile first, then check the ads account. If a multi-location operator cannot link locations through the profile, Google allows selecting up to 10 locations from Google Maps, provided they are yours ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)).

Once the profile is accurate, build outward carefully. Do not create new listings on directories you cannot maintain, because an outdated listing is worse than none. For paid search alignment, see the [Google Ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/google-ads-for-fitness-studios-complete-2026-guide).

## Worked example: what can a Boston studio's profile be worth?

This example uses assumed inputs. Replace them with your own analytics from Google Business Profile Insights and your booking system.

| Step | Input or calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly profile views | Assumption | 500 |
| Share who call, request directions or visit the website | Assumption: 8% | 40 actions |
| Share of actions that book a trial | Assumption: 1 in 4 | 10 trials |
| Share of trials that join | Assumption: 1 in 3 | about 3 members |
| Monthly fee per member | Assumption: $150 | $150 each |
| Estimated recurring revenue from profile leads | 3 members × $150 | about $450 per month |

The point of the table is the chain, not the final number. If your profile views rise by 100 a month with the same conversion rates, the estimate rises by roughly $90. Track each step so you can see which one is weak. If views are high but actions are low, the photos, hours and category list are the likely problem. If actions are high but trials are low, the issue is probably the booking path after the click.

## How do you track whether local SEO is working?

Set up three measurements before you change anything. In Google Business Profile Insights, record monthly searches, views and actions. In Google Search Console, track organic clicks and impressions for your service pages. For rankings, check your map pack position for your main keywords on a fixed schedule, from the same location and in private browsing, so results are comparable.

Record your starting numbers and the date of each change. Local results shift with seasons and with competitors, so a single week's rank is less useful than a trend across several months. ## FAQ

### Is the Google Business Profile free to use?

Yes. Google describes the Business Profile as a free tool for managing a business's presence across Google Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile, CA](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). Paid options such as Google Ads are separate.

### How many photos should a studio upload?

A vendor guide recommends at least 15 authentic photos of your facility, classes and staff ([Vertical Impression, secondary](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026)). Upload real images from your own space and refresh them as your classes change.

### Why does my post not appear on Maps?

Google shows a post publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Posts marked Pending or Not approved do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after publishing.

### Do I need a set number of reviews to rank in Boston?

Google does not publish a threshold. Reviews appear next to your profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Steady, genuine review requests are a safer approach than chasing a number that a third party has not sourced.
