Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up, and its help pages say reviews appear beside your profile in Maps and Search. This guide turns those points into a checklist. ## Is a Google Business Profile Still Free to Set Up?

Google's Business Profile page leads with the line "Stand out on Google with a free Business Profile" ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). BrewRound's 2020 guide adds that a listing requires a Google account, which can also be registered for free ([BrewRound, secondary](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/)).

Verification is the step most owners skip. BrewRound says Google mails a letter containing a code, and the profile does not appear in results until it is verified (as of 2020). Per Diem warns that without [verification](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en), updates will not show publicly and customers may not find the business ([Per Diem, secondary](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). Check verification status before anything else.

For a step-by-step setup walkthrough, see [coffee shop Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization).

## Which Category Should a Louisville Coffee Shop Choose?

Per Diem recommends choosing "Coffee shop" or a related term rather than something broad like "Restaurant" ([Per Diem, secondary](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). A broad category tells Google less about what you sell, which weakens matches for coffee searches.

BrewRound notes that you can select only one category when you create a listing, and more can be added later ([BrewRound, secondary](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/)). Pick "Coffee shop" as the primary category, then add secondary terms such as "Cafe" or "Espresso bar" if they describe your menu accurately.

## Which Questions Should Your Profile Answer First?

Per Diem recommends answering common questions in the profile's Q&A section before customers ask them, such as whether you offer free Wi-Fi, dairy-free options, or allow dogs on the patio ([Per Diem, secondary](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). Write these answers yourself so the first thing a searcher sees is accurate.

Google also allows users to message businesses directly through their listings ([Per Diem, secondary](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). Check that messages reach someone who replies quickly. Per Diem also describes Think Coffee, an independent coffee brand, which placed its app download link in its Google profile and now has thousands of downloads and regular app-based orders ([Per Diem, secondary](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). Any link that moves a searcher to the next action is worth testing.

## Do Google Posts Need to Stay Live?

Google reviews each Business Profile post after publishing. A post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live; Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Help: Create and manage posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after you publish, especially for event or offer posts.

For social media, The Restaurant HQ recommends posting at least once per week, ideally one to two times per day ([The Restaurant HQ, secondary](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/)). That guidance covers social channels, not the profile itself, so treat it as a separate calendar and keep your Google posts on a steady weekly rhythm.

## How Much Do Reviews Matter for Louisville Map Results?

Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google Help: Tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). They are the most visible social proof a searcher sees before tapping through.

On ranking weight, Ahrefs cites a BrightLocal survey in which 36% of SEOs think the Business Profile is the most important ranking factor for the map pack, up from 23% in 2013 ([Ahrefs, secondary](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo)). That is a survey of practitioners, not a ranking formula, so treat it as direction rather than proof.

**Worked example (all inputs are assumptions):**
- Assumption: 150 paid transactions per week.
- Assumption: 2% of customers leave a review after a text request.
- Calculation: 150 × 2% = 3 reviews per week, or about 12 per month.

Steady review flow beats a burst, so the goal is a repeatable ask after each visit, not a one-off campaign. Reply to each review in plain language that names your drinks and neighbourhood.

## How Consistent Should Your Citations Be?

Citations are listings of your name, address and phone number (NAP) on other websites. The rule is simple: match every detail exactly. "St." and "Street" or two different phone formats can read as different businesses to a directory. Start with the profile itself, then check major directories and any Louisville chamber or neighbourhood listings you already appear in. Fix existing mismatches before adding new listings.

## How Does Google Ads Connect to Your Profile?

Google states that data for location assets in Google Ads is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and that successful corrections made there flow into linked Google Ads accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). Fixing your profile therefore also cleans up paid location data.

If the Business Profile and chain store options do not work, you can select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link to your Ads account, provided they belong to you ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). Work through the table in order. Verification comes first because nothing else shows publicly until it is complete.

## How Do You Track Whether Local SEO Is Working?

Use the profile's insights for views and actions, and Google Search Console for organic search pages. Check your Maps position for your main coffee searches from time to time, using private browsing to reduce personalization. Record the date and result each time so changes can be compared against your own history rather than a general benchmark.
