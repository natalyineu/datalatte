## How much does your Google Business Profile matter in the Columbus map pack?

Adwave's multi-location benchmark reports that Google Business Profile signals represented 32% of local pack ranking weight in 2025 (secondary). A separate Adwave beginner's guide puts it differently, claiming the profile is responsible for 32% of Map Pack ranking potential, without naming a source (secondary). Treat both figures as directional rather than exact.

Survey data points the same way. In BrightLocal's survey cited by [Ahrefs' local SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo) (secondary), the share of SEOs rating Google Business Profile signals as most important for the local pack rose from 23% in 2013 to 36% in 2021.

Adwave also claims that the primary category is the number one ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, based on an analysis it does not name ([Adwave categories guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones), secondary). Search the service you offer in Columbus, then review the top three to five businesses in the map results to see which categories they use.

## Which Google Business Profile fields should pet groomers complete first?

Work through the profile in the order below. Each row lists what the source says and the action to take.

| Field | What the sources say | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Primary category | Adwave calls the primary category the top ranking factor for the Local Pack (secondary) | Choose the category your top competitors use, then add secondary services |
| Business description | Allows 750 characters (secondary) | Name the service, the city, and one clear differentiator in plain language |
| Hours, phone, address | Should be 100% accurate (secondary) | Check against your website each month |
| Photos | Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos; another Adwave guide says at least 10 (secondary) | Upload at least 10 at launch, then add 15 over time |
| Posts | Adwave advises at least one new post a week (secondary) | Schedule one post weekly; only posts with Live status show publicly ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)) |
| Street address | Home-based or travelling businesses can hide the street address and become a Service Area Business (secondary) | Hide the address if you groom at customers' homes, then set a service area |

Google's own help pages confirm that posts go through review before they appear, so a post stuck in Pending will not help your profile. Check status after each post.

## Why must your name, address, and phone match across citations?

Citations are mentions of your business name, address, and phone number (NAP) on other websites. Adwave says the NAP must be exactly the same on your website, your Google Business Profile, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places (secondary). Small differences count. Adwave's beginner's guide specifically warns about "St." versus "Street" and punctuation (secondary).

Adwave also cites an example landscaping firm that cleaned up its NAP data and saw an 18% jump in local pack appearances. The page gives no independent verification, so treat it as an anecdote (secondary).

Apple matters too. Adwave says Apple Business Connect has captured a 7% share in a maps-only comparison that excludes web search, and is growing (secondary). Its Apple Maps Actions let a business connect services such as booking or ordering, and its Profile Views metric counts how many people saw the business card in Apple Maps (secondary).

Fix mismatches in existing listings before you build new ones. Adding more inconsistent listings makes the problem worse.

## How do reviews affect map pack position?

Google states that reviews appear next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). They are visible to searchers before they click, which makes review volume and rating a direct conversion factor as well as a ranking signal.

The 200+ review benchmark comes from a third-party analysis by Blogging Wizard of 2 million Google Business Profile pages, which found that businesses with over 200 reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack, as cited by Adwave (secondary). A separate Adwave claim suggests that businesses with more than 50 reviews and a 4.7+ rating dominate top map pack spots, without citing a source ([AI Google Business Profile ranking for landscapers](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), secondary). These numbers conflict, so set a realistic first milestone and track your own position.

Ask for reviews soon after each appointment, and reply to every review. Mention your service in replies where it fits naturally.

## Which photos, posts, and Q&A content help most?

Adwave describes Google Posts as free mini-billboards that appear on your profile in Search and Maps (secondary). For grooming, the most useful posts show seasonal availability, new services, or mobile visit openings, written in plain language.

Adwave also says a robust photo gallery gets far more clicks and calls (secondary). Use real photos of your space, your van if you have one, and finished grooms where customers have given permission.

Google Business Profile lets you add questions and answers. Seed the questions customers ask most, such as whether you handle anxious dogs or offer mobile visits, and answer them accurately.

Google's Ask Maps answers draw on business profile data, website content, and reviews, according to Accrisoft's transition summary (secondary, [Adwave Q&A guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a)). Keep your website and profile saying the same thing.

## Worked example: what might a map pack call be worth?

The inputs below are assumptions for illustration. They are not sourced figures, so replace them with your own numbers.

- Calls, direction requests, and website visits from your profile per month: 60 (assumption)
- Share of those contacts that book: 25% (assumption)
- Average booking value: $70 (assumption)
- Staff time spent on profile and review upkeep: 2 hours a week at $25 an hour, or $200 a month (assumption)

Bookings: 60 × 25% = 15 per month.
Revenue: 15 × $70 = $1,050 per month.
Net after upkeep: $1,050 − $200 = $850 per month.

Run the same calculation with your own counts. Unless the calls and bookings are tracked, the figure is a guess.

## FAQ

### Is the Google Business Profile free for pet groomers?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as a free listing on Google Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)).

### How many reviews does a pet groomer need to rank in the map pack?

No source gives a guaranteed threshold. One third-party analysis, cited by Adwave, links 200+ reviews with higher local pack odds (secondary), while another Adwave claim cites 50+ reviews and a 4.7+ rating (secondary). Track your own map position as your count grows.

### Should a mobile pet groomer show a street address?

Google allows businesses that serve customers at their location to hide the street address and be designated a Service Area Business, according to Adwave (secondary). Set a service area that matches the distance you travel.

### How long until a new profile starts ranking?

Adwave says a business may start ranking for its own name a few weeks after verification (secondary). Ranking for broader grooming searches takes longer and depends on competition, so judge progress over several months.

### What should I do first with a new Google Business Profile?

Confirm the category, hours, phone, and address, then add 10 to 15 photos and post weekly. Check the citation listings on Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places for mismatches before building new ones.
