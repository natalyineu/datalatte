## How much should a Chicago salon spend on Meta ads?

Adwave estimates Meta (Facebook and Instagram) advertising at $500 to $2,000 per month for most single-location businesses ([Adwave restaurant guide](https://adwave.com/resources/restaurant-advertising-complete-marketing-guide), secondary). A separate Adwave channel table puts a standalone Meta buy at $300 to $1,000 per month ([Adwave channel guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels), secondary). For beauty salons, Bookeo suggests starting Facebook and Instagram ad spend at $750 to $1,500 per month ([Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/), secondary).

These figures come from several industries, so treat them as starting points. Adwave also notes that Meta takes weeks to optimize, so a budget held steady for several weeks will tell you more than a larger budget run for a few days ([Adwave best advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-small-business-2026), secondary).

## Which Meta platforms and placements should a salon use?

Instagram ads run through the same system as Facebook ads, Meta Ads Manager, because Instagram is owned by Meta. One campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook or both ([Adwave Instagram guide](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business), secondary). Meta Ads Manager covers Facebook, Instagram and Messenger ([Turrboo](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media), secondary), and Adobe describes it as a central point for running ads across both platforms ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising), secondary).

Running one campaign on both platforms and comparing results by placement in Ads Manager is a simple way to see where bookings come from. Paid ads also work best when they send people to a feed that already shows your work. Bookeo says most salon owners find three to four Instagram posts and one to two Facebook posts a week sustainable ([Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/), secondary). The steps for that organic base are covered in [Instagram marketing for hair salons](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-hair-salons).

## Which ad formats work best for salon creative?

Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, as cited by Adwave, says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram ([Adwave electrician guide](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners), secondary). That is an engagement figure, not a booking figure, and it comes from a vendor citing Meta, so test it on your own account.

For a salon, video usually means short clips of a colour process, a finished cut or a before-and-after, shot in good light. Run a video ad and a static photo of the same service side by side for two weeks, then compare cost per click and booking rate. Use the same booking link on both so the comparison is fair.

## How should you target clients around Chicago?

Bookeo advises setting a 5 to 10 mile radius around the salon for Facebook and Instagram ads ([Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/), secondary). For a single location in a dense neighborhood, start near 5 miles and widen only if reach is too thin to learn from.

Bookeo also points to the Insights tab for peak follower times, which requires a business account ([Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/), secondary). Use those hours to schedule posts and to check when your audience is online before you set ad delivery windows. Custom audiences built from past clients are also worth building once you have a list, since they let you reach people who already know the salon.

## What does a worked budget look like for a salon?

The inputs below are assumptions for illustration, not benchmarks for Chicago. The CPM assumption sits inside Adwave's $5 to $15 range, and the resulting cost per click should sit inside its $1 to $3 range.

- Monthly budget: $900 (assumption)
- CPM: $10 (assumption, within $5–15)
- Click-through rate: 0.5% (assumption)
- Booking rate from click: 10% (assumption)
- Average ticket: $60 (assumption)

At $900, impressions equal $900 ÷ $10 × 1,000 = 90,000. Clicks equal 90,000 × 0.5% = 450. Cost per click equals $900 ÷ 450 = $2.00. Bookings equal 450 × 10% = 45, and booked revenue equals 45 × $60 = $2,700. That is a 3.0x ratio to spend, measured as gross revenue before product and staff costs.

| Budget (assumption) | Impressions | Clicks | Bookings | Booked revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $750 | 75,000 | 375 | 38 (rounded) | $2,250 |
| $900 | 90,000 | 450 | 45 | $2,700 |
| $1,500 | 150,000 | 750 | 75 | $4,500 |

Because every input is linear, the ratio stays at 3.0x across budgets. Real campaigns do not behave this neatly, so review actual clicks and bookings weekly and replace the assumptions with your own numbers.

## Which tracking steps should be set up before launch?

Work through this checklist before any ad goes live:

1. Confirm you have a business account, which Insights needs for peak-time data.
2. Install the Meta Pixel on your booking confirmation page and any contact form.
3. Set a Lead event that fires when someone submits a booking form.
4. Add UTM parameters to every ad URL so Google Analytics can track Meta traffic separately.
5. Compare Ads Manager bookings against your booking system once a week.

Without these steps, you can only judge a campaign by impressions and reach, which do not show whether anyone booked.

## How do you turn ad views into repeat bookings?

Bookeo claims that a client who has seen a salon's brand on Facebook 5 to 10 times before searching for a salon converts at 2 to 3 times the rate of a cold searcher. The page does not back this with data, so treat it as a vendor claim ([Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/), secondary).

Retargeting puts that idea into practice. Adwave lists a typical retargeting CPM of $5 to $15 on Meta ([Adwave retargeting guide](https://adwave.com/resources/retargeting-ads-small-business), secondary). Once a client books, follow-up matters as much as acquisition, so pair ads with [marketing automation for hair salons](/blog/marketing-automation-for-hair-salons) to send reminders and rebooking prompts.

## FAQ

### Is $500 a month enough for a Chicago salon?

It can be, for a single location. Adwave estimates $500 to $2,000 per month for most single-location businesses, and its channel table lists a standalone Meta buy from $300 to $1,000. Start within your comfort range and judge results over several weeks.

### Do I need a business account to see when my followers are online?

Yes. Bookeo says peak follower times appear in the Insights tab, which requires a business account on Instagram or Facebook.

### Does Meta offer free training on Facebook and Instagram ads?

Meta Blueprint offers free Facebook and Instagram advertising courses with certification on completion, according to SASCOM ([SASCOM](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/), secondary). Check Meta's current course list for availability.

### Can one campaign run on both Instagram and Facebook?

Yes. Instagram ads run through Meta Ads Manager, and one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook or both ([Adwave Instagram guide](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business), secondary).
