## What Results Did a Mobile Pet Groomer See From Google Ads?

Marketing 360 published a [case study of a mobile pet groomer](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/) who advertised on Facebook and Google. Compared with the previous six-month period, the groomer had a 134% increase in impressions and a 696% increase in clicks. The page is dated June 13, 2023, so the figures describe that period.

The study does not describe a New York City business, and a mobile groomer serves a different area than a salon does. Marketing 360 is also an agency, so the result is a vendor's account. Treat it as evidence that search and social ads can move clicks, not as a forecast for your shop.

The channel mix matters. Search ads reach people already typing a request for a groomer, which is a different job from social ads that reach people while they browse. A salon owner can use both, but the search side is where a "near me" booking intent shows up most directly.

## How Wide Should a New York City Groomer's Radius Be?

Barklead's [guide to Facebook ads for groomers](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers) suggests targeting users within a 10-15 mile radius of the groomer's location. That advice is for Facebook, not Google, but it gives a workable starting size for a service that clients travel to.

New York City is large, and a radius around one salon can reach into more than one borough, depending on where the shop sits. Start with the radius, then narrow it by ZIP code once bookings show where clients actually live. A groomer in another market can compare approaches in the [Oklahoma City pet groomer Google Ads guide](/blog/google-ads-for-pet-groomers-in-oklahoma-city-ok).

Flyers follow the same logic. [uprinting advises](https://www.uprinting.com/blog/10-proven-ways-to-attract-more-pet-owners-to-your-grooming-business/) distributing 500 to 1,000 flyers in the right neighborhoods, which it says usually works better than handing them out all over the city.

## Which Keywords and Settings Should a Groomer Start With?

Start with searches that show a booking need, such as dog grooming near me, or grooming paired with a neighborhood name. Use the service names clients actually type, such as bath, nail trim or de-shedding, rather than broad terms like pet care.

Add a short list of negative terms for people who are not buying, such as jobs, training or DIY. Check Google's live Ads Help pages for current location and match-type settings before launch, since these settings change. Send ads to the same booking page the groomer uses for walk-in customers so the counts line up.

If you want a pay-per-lead option alongside search, compare it with the [Local Services Ads guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-local-services-ads-for-pet-groomers) before you commit budget.

## How Do You Count Bookings as Conversions?

Count phone calls, online booking form submissions and booking-message clicks as conversions. Then compare cost per booking rather than cost per click. Cost per click shows what a visit costs. Cost per booking shows whether the spend pays.

Build the reporting once so the numbers do not depend on someone exporting spreadsheets each week. A [guide to automated local SEO reports](/blog/ai-driven-local-seo-reports-with-google-data-studio) shows one way to connect ad and listing data in a single view, and the same dashboard can track both paid and organic bookings.

## Do Holiday Months Change the Budget?

Adwave cites Groomer to Groomer as saying November and December are traditionally a very busy time for groomers, sometimes too busy. When the book is already full, extra ad spend only adds value if the salon can take the extra bookings.

Test this rather than assume it. Compare cost per booking in a busy month against a quiet one, and keep spend steady if your schedule has room. If the calendar is full, pause or reduce campaigns for those weeks and keep the listing active.

## FAQ

### How much does Google Ads cost for a New York City pet groomer?

No verified New York City cost-per-click figure is cited here. The worked example uses an assumed $4.00 per click, and your account's live data will show the real number.

### Should a groomer target the whole city or a radius?

A radius around the salon is the safer start. Barklead suggests 10-15 miles for Facebook ads, and a similar size can be tested on Google. Narrow it by ZIP code once bookings show where clients live.

### What should count as a conversion?

Calls, online booking form submissions and booking-message clicks. Measure them as cost per booking, not just cost per click.

### Is a Google Ads budget worth it for a small groomer?

Only if cost per booking stays below the profit from one groom. The worked example compares a $40 cost per conversion with a $75 first visit, and both figures are assumptions to replace with your own numbers.
