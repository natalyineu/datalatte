## What do Meta ads cost for a Portland fitness studio?

[Adwave's guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-small-business-guide) lists typical Meta costs of $5 to $15 per thousand impressions and $1 to $3 per click. Its [retargeting table](https://adwave.com/resources/retargeting-ads-small-business) gives a similar $5 to $15 CPM range for Meta retargeting. A [separate vendor table](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels) puts a standalone Meta buy at $300 to $1,000 per month.

No reliable Portland-specific CPM figure is published, so treat these as national planning ranges. Run a campaign for at least two weeks, then replace the estimates with your own Ads Manager numbers. Cost per click and cost per booking matter more than impressions, because a cheap impression that never books a trial is wasted spend.

Meta Ads Manager covers Facebook, Instagram and Messenger, according to [Turrboo's overview](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media). One campaign can run on both Instagram and Facebook, so you do not need separate accounts for each app.

## Which ad formats work best for fitness studios?

Short-form video is the strongest format to test first. [Adwave, citing Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners), says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram. Film real classes, coaches and member moments rather than stock footage. Most studios can do this with a phone and a simple script.

Posting pace matters as much as the ad itself. [Mariana Tek advises studios](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) to post at a steady cadence of three to five short-form videos per week, not in bursts. For the organic side of that schedule, see [how to build a Reels habit for a studio](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-instagram-reels) and the [studio Instagram marketing strategy](/blog/fitness-studio-instagram-marketing-strategy).

Stories are a separate placement with their own rules and creative sizes. If you want to test them alongside feed ads, read the [Stories ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/instagram-stories-ads-fitness-studios) before building the campaign.

## Who should you target and how much should you spend?

Start with people who live or work near the studio, then add retargeting for website visitors and people who attended a trial class. Retargeting usually costs less per impression than cold prospecting, because the audience already knows the brand. Keep the trial offer simple so a cold viewer can say yes without a long commitment.

Budget split matters too. For fitness studios and wellness businesses, [Adwave recommends a 45 to 55% Meta share](https://adwave.com/resources/connected-tv-vs-social-media-ads-comparison), with 30 to 40% on connected TV and the balance on local platforms. That is a vendor recommendation, so test it against your own results rather than treating it as a rule.

Overall marketing spend is a useful ceiling check. [Mariana Tek notes](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) that many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10% of monthly revenue for marketing. Check your total budget against that band before you raise the Meta line.

## How should you set up tracking and booking?

Instagram and Facebook let you add a Book Now button to your profile, as [Appointy notes](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/). Link that button to the same booking page your ads send traffic to, so a profile visitor and an ad clicker reach the same offer.

Install the Meta Pixel on your booking confirmation page and add UTM tags to every ad URL so you can see which ad produced each booking. Without these, you are optimising for clicks rather than members. The [marketing automation guide for fitness studios](/blog/marketing-automation-for-fitness-studios) covers how to pass those booking leads into your follow-up sequence.

## Why does follow-up speed matter for trial leads?

Interest fades quickly after a pop-up or trial ends. [Mariana Tek advises](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) following up within 24 hours using an automated welcome sequence. A paid click that books a trial is only half the work; the sequence that follows decides whether that person becomes a member.

Set up the sequence before you launch the ad. A basic version sends a confirmation, a reminder before the class, and a message within 24 hours of the trial. For more on building that flow, read the [marketing automation guide for local fitness studios](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-fitness-studios).

## FAQ

### What is a realistic starting budget for Meta ads?

A standalone Meta buy often costs [$300 to $1,000 per month](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels), according to Adwave's channel table. Start at the low end, measure cost per booking for four to six weeks, then increase the budget if bookings hold.

### Should I use video or static images?

Start with video. Adwave, citing Meta's 2025 report, says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images. Test one static image against one video before you commit, because results vary by studio and audience.

### Where can my staff learn Meta ads for free?

[A secondary guide](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/) says Meta Blueprint offers free Facebook and Instagram advertising courses with certification on completion. Check the current course list on Meta's own help pages before enrolling.

### How do I know if my Meta ads are working?

Compare cost per booking and bookings per month against the membership revenue each booking produces. Impressions and reach show awareness, but only booked trials and paid memberships show whether the campaign is paying off.
