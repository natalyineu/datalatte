Year-round HVAC marketing works best when it treats repair calls and maintenance as two separate jobs. Repairs come from urgent searches, so the Google Business Profile, phone answering and reviews need to be ready in every month. Maintenance plans fill the quieter seasons. A single HVAC customer can be worth far more than one call: AdWave's benchmark table puts HVAC and plumbing lifetime value at $1,500-10,000 ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-lifetime-value-small-business)). This guide covers the plan, the numbers and a budget example with every input labelled.

## What Is an HVAC Customer Worth Over Time?

Lifetime value changes the budget question. AdWave's illustrative HVAC example uses a $350 average service call, 1.5 calls per year and an 8-year lifespan, giving a customer lifetime value of $4,200 ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-lifetime-value-small-business)). Its benchmark range for HVAC and plumbing is based on $250-500 transactions, 1-2 calls a year and 6-10 years of lifespan.

Get-Ryze's vendor blog says HVAC and landscaping businesses often see 2–3x higher lifetime value than their initial project value ([Get-Ryze](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/google-local-services-ads-reporting-tool)). That supports paying attention to the first repair as the start of a relationship, not the end of a sale.

AdWave recommends recalculating customer lifetime value at least once a year, and every quarter if the business is growing quickly or has changed pricing, services or retention work. Those figures are illustrations, so use your own invoices as the input.

## How Do You Get Found When a System Fails?

When a system fails, people search and call the first credible result. Findability starts with a complete profile. Citing Yelp's 2023 report, AdWave says home services businesses with complete profiles and 10+ photos received 5 times more customer leads than those without ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/hvac-advertising-getting-more-service-calls)). That figure is secondary, so treat it as a direction, not a guarantee.

Google's structured data guidance also covers hours. Leaving out validFrom and validThrough in openingHoursSpecification signifies that the hours are valid year-round ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)). If your hours do not change by season, say so clearly in the data and on the profile.

Phone-only operation is common. Origami Chat reports that 45% of HVAC, plumbing and electrical companies operate phone-only ([Origami Chat](https://origami.chat/blog/find-businesses-without-websites)). A complete profile and a working website can set you apart from those competitors.

## Does Always-On Marketing Fit a Seasonal Trade?

Always-on marketing means activity runs every day, year-round, rather than being tied to seasons or campaigns ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/what-is-an-always-on-marketing-strategy)). For HVAC, the always-on part is the basics: the profile, review replies, call answering and a steady stream of customer contact. The seasonal part is the ad spend and the maintenance offer.

Campaign budgets need a buffer. AdWave's HVAC page notes that its campaigns pause if the wallet cannot cover the day's budget ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-hvac-companies)). Check your own account for the same rule before you set a winter or summer push.

Answering calls matters as much as generating them. Centro reports that improving answer rate, lead rate and conversion rate together produces roughly 38% more conversions from the same call volume. That is a third-party claim from a benchmark report, so test it with your own call log. [AI agents for customer service](/blog/ai-agent-for-customer-service-local-business) can handle after-hours calls, but a human still needs to book the job.

## How Do You Build Maintenance Plans in the Off-Season?

Maintenance plans turn one call into a repeating relationship. AdWave's pest control example says a customer acquired for a $250 one-time treatment can be worth $1,200-$2,000 per year if converted to a recurring plan ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/pest-control-advertising)). That is a different trade, so use it as a model, not a forecast for HVAC.

AdWave's retention guidance describes a five-step loyalty plan: set the goal, set the reward threshold, pick margin-protecting rewards, train staff and add prompts, then track four numbers weekly ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-retention-strategies-that-cost-less-than-new-acquisition)). The same page says 44% of companies admit they focus more on acquisition than retention ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-acquisition-cost-calculation)).

Offer the plan when the customer is already on the phone or in the home. Keep the offer simple, and make the reminder timing match your own service intervals. For retention analysis, see [AI-driven customer retention strategies](/blog/ai-driven-customer-retention-strategies).

## The Math: What Does a $1,500 Budget Test Return?

Every input below is an **assumption** except the formula, which comes from AdWave.

| Step | Input or formula | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $1,500 (assumption) | $1,500 |
| Cost per new customer | $300 (assumption) | 5 new customers |
| Average service call | $350 (assumption) | $350 per call |
| Calls per year and lifespan | 1.5 calls, 8 years (assumption) | 12 calls |
| Customer lifetime value | $350 × 1.5 × 8 (AdWave formula) | $4,200 |
| Lifetime value of 5 customers | 5 × $4,200 | $21,000 |
| Revenue per $1 spent | $21,000 ÷ $1,500 | about $14 |

This is lifetime revenue, not profit. Subtract parts, labour and any maintenance discounts before you judge the test. If only 2 of the 5 customers stay for the full lifespan, the lifetime value falls sharply, so track retention as closely as cost per lead.

## What Should You Do in Week One?

Start with an audit. AdWave's service plan suggests auditing the last 20 customer issues in week one and looking for delays, repeat complaints and handoff failures ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-retention-strategies-that-cost-less-than-new-acquisition)). Those problems usually cost more than the ads do.

1. Audit the last 20 customer issues for delays, repeat complaints and handoff failures.
2. List 10 to 20 recurring customer questions to use as content ideas.
3. Check profile hours, service areas and photos against what your team actually does.
4. Set a review request after every completed job, and reply to every review.
5. Write one maintenance-plan offer and one reminder schedule.
6. Recalculate customer lifetime value at least once a year.

Reviews need care. Guardian reporting says customer-review fraud costs consumers billions of pounds a year in misdirected spending ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/mar/27/paid-write-fake-google-reviews-scammer-cryptocurrency)). Ask for genuine feedback only, and never pay for positive reviews.

## FAQ

### How much should an HVAC company spend on marketing?
No single figure fits every firm. Start with a test budget, such as the $1,500 example above, and measure cost per new customer against lifetime value.

### Why does lifetime value matter for HVAC?
A repair is often the first job in a longer relationship. AdWave's HVAC and plumbing benchmark is $1,500-10,000, so one customer can justify more spend than one call suggests.

### Should I list my address on my Google Business Profile?
Google's structured data guidance covers hours, not addresses. Check the platform's live help page for service-area settings before you change your profile.

### How do I handle seasonal swings?
Use off-season maintenance plans, keep the profile and reviews current all year, and track calls by month in your own records.

### What should I do first?
Audit your last 20 customer issues. Fix the delays and handoff failures before you increase ad spend.
