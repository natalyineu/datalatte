Display ads are banner and image placements on websites, apps and social feeds. Programmatic advertising is a way of buying that inventory, along with video and other formats, through automated auctions where software decides in real time which ad reaches which user. For a local business, the choice depends mostly on goal and budget rather than on which option is better. Programmatic now handles most display buying, so the practical question is which setup fits your audience, your offer and your monthly spend.

## What is the difference between display ads and programmatic advertising?

Display is the broad category. StackAdapt's explainer places display alongside social media and search engine marketing as forms of digital advertising. Programmatic is the buying method that increasingly sits behind display. [StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/what-is-programmatic-advertising) notes that ad selection in programmatic happens within a fraction of a second, faster than the page can fully load. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/tv-media-buying) adds that the same auction dynamics have operated in display and video on websites for over a decade.

The shift in share is large. EMARKETER, as cited by [StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/what-is-display-advertising) (secondary), says programmatic's share of display ad budgets grew from 51.7% in 2014 to nearly 97% in 2025. A display placement bought today is therefore very likely to be bought programmatically, even when the platform does not use that word. For how the auction itself works, read [how programmatic buying works for local businesses](/blog/how-programmatic-buying-works-local-business).

These figures are cost per thousand impressions, or CPM. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/programmatic-political-advertising) lists standard display at $3-8 CPM, native display at $5-12 and high-impact formats at $8-15. They are vendor-listed programmatic ranges rather than guarantees, and actual prices vary by market, placement and audience. The 96% figure is EMARKETER's forecast for programmatic's share of new display spend in 2026, held steady from the prior year.

Impressions only matter if people can see them. The IAB standard, as summarised by [StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/programmatic-advertising-glossary) (secondary), counts a display ad as viewed when at least 50% of it is in view for one second. Video needs two seconds. A low CPM with poor viewability can cost more per seen ad than a higher CPM with good placements, so ask any platform for its viewable-impression rate before committing.

## Which targeting options separate display from programmatic?

[Awarity](https://www.awarity.com/blog/google-display-network-vs-programmatic-display-ads) describes Google Display Network targeting as mainly based on interests and keywords, while programmatic can include behavioral and contextual targeting. It also states that Google Display Network is limited to Google's ecosystem, whereas programmatic can access broader inventory across multiple ad exchanges. Awarity is a vendor, so treat this as one view of the trade-off rather than a neutral test.

Location is a separate tool that works alongside either channel. The [geofencing guide for local businesses](/blog/geofencing-advertising-local-business-guide) explains how radius-based audiences work and is worth reading before you set up a campaign. A useful test is to ask whether your ideal customer is defined by where they are, what they search for, or what they already did on your website. The answer usually points to one channel more clearly than the other.

## What does a small monthly test need to return?

[ClicksGeek](https://clicksgeek.com/best-advertising-platforms/) (secondary) states that programmatic makes sense when a business spends $10,000 or more monthly and needs scale beyond individual platforms. That is a vendor-style threshold, not a rule, but it shows programmatic is not a small-budget default. A smaller test can still run, provided you know the break-even point before you start.

Worked example. Inputs labelled as assumptions: a $600 monthly test, a $45 average sale, and a range of customer outcomes from the campaign. Using Adwave's standard display range of $3-8 CPM, $600 buys about 200,000 impressions at $3 CPM or about 75,000 at $8 CPM. Revenue equals customers multiplied by $45, and ROI equals (revenue minus $600) divided by $600, times 100.

| Customers from the test (assumption) | Revenue | Net after $600 | ROI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | $540 | -$60 | -10% |
| 14 | $630 | +$30 | 5% |
| 15 | $675 | +$75 | 12.5% |

Break-even is $600 divided by $45, which is 13.3, so the test needs 14 customers to break even. A 12.5% return is modest, which is why a test should be judged on cost per customer against your margin, not on impressions.

## When should a local business choose display ads?

Display suits the goal of a visible, recognisable presence on specific sites rather than fine-grained audience selection. If a local news site or community blog reaches the people you need, a direct display placement there deserves a comparison with programmatic. The [display advertising guide for small businesses](/blog/display-advertising-for-small-business) covers formats and setup in more detail.

Display also suits visual offers, because a placement can carry strong product photos. Awarity's point about Google Display Network staying inside Google's ecosystem matters here. A Google-only display plan reaches fewer sites than a multi-exchange plan, so keep display when the placement itself is the point, and check whether your audience is concentrated on the sites you can name.

## When does programmatic advertising make more sense?

Programmatic makes more sense when you need precise targeting, retargeting, or a wider pool of inventory. [StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/outdoor-advertising-las-vegas) describes using location data and its identity graph to match people exposed to out-of-home ads and retarget them on native, display and video. That is a vendor example, but it shows a retargeting logic that a standalone display placement rarely offers.

Programmatic also fits a mixed-channel plan. The [audio versus display guide](/blog/audio-ads-vs-display-ads-local-business) compares the two directly. StackAdapt cites Nielsen Media Lab for audio ads having a 24% higher recall rate than traditional display ads, a secondary figure worth checking against the original report before relying on it.

## How do you tell whether either channel is working?

Judge both channels on cost per customer, not on clicks or impressions. Set up conversion tracking for phone calls, bookings or store visits before spending, and compare each channel's cost per customer against your average sale. The viewability rate from the platform works as a quality check on placements you are paying for.

Review results after a full month where possible, because a single week rarely shows a stable cost per customer. If you run search as well, the [Microsoft Advertising guide for local businesses](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business) explains how a second platform fits alongside display. ## FAQ

### Is programmatic advertising the same as display advertising?

No. Display describes the ad format and placement type, while programmatic describes how the ad is bought, through automated auctions. Most display is now bought programmatically, according to EMARKETER figures cited by StackAdapt, so the two overlap heavily without being identical.

### Is display cheaper than programmatic?

Not in a simple way. Adwave's listed programmatic CPM ranges run from $3 to $15 depending on format, and the ranges overlap across standard, native and high-impact units. Compare cost per customer from a live test rather than CPM alone.

### How big should a first programmatic budget be?

ClicksGeek describes $10,000 a month as the point where programmatic makes sense, which is a secondary threshold. A smaller test is possible, but set break-even first. At a $45 average sale, a $600 test needs 14 customers to break even, as the worked example shows.

### Does programmatic reach people beyond my town?

It can. Awarity states that programmatic can access broader inventory across multiple ad exchanges. Radius-based location targeting is what keeps that reach local, so set it in every campaign you run.
