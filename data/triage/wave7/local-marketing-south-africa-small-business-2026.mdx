South African small businesses get the most from digital marketing by giving each platform one job. Google Search ads reach people already looking for a service, Facebook and Instagram build awareness among people who are not searching yet, and WhatsApp handles the enquiries that follow. Start with a small monthly test, track enquiries rather than clicks, and scale only what produces paying customers. The worked example below uses an assumed ZAR 6,000 monthly budget so you can test the arithmetic against your own figures.

## How big is the digital opportunity for South African small businesses?

Connectivity and cost set the limits of what online marketing can reach. Research ICT Africa ranks Mozambique third on 1GB data pricing among 49 African countries, ahead of Ghana, Kenya and South Africa. The same research notes that only six percent of South Africans lack access to electricity, compared with half of the Mozambican population. That figure describes electricity access, not power cuts, but it shows how uneven conditions are across the region.

Planning for mobile screens is a reasonable default for local audiences in this kind of market. The research covers 20 countries in the Global South, 10 of them in Africa, so treat these numbers as regional context rather than a measure for your own suburb. Before choosing a channel, check what your customers actually search and where they already spend time.

## Which platforms should a South African small business start with?

Google Search ads suit services people look up before they buy, such as a plumber, a salon or a gym. Facebook and Instagram suit businesses that need to be seen before anyone searches for them. Choosing between them is easier when you compare cost and intent side by side, as the [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads comparison](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins) explains.

Cost is the first thing to check. Adwave reports that WordStream/LocaliQ's 2026 US search medians were $5.42 per click, an 8.18% conversion rate and $66.69 per lead. These are US figures, so use them only for scale. Meta also reported its average price per ad up 12% in Q2 2026, but that is a company-wide measure rather than a CPC or CPM, so it does not predict what your campaign will cost.

## What does a worked monthly budget look like in ZAR?

Every input in this example is an assumption, not a benchmark. Replace each one with figures from your own account or your platform's live keyword tools before making any decision.

| Line | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Total monthly budget | ZAR 6,000 | Assumption |
| Google Search spend | ZAR 3,000 | Assumption |
| Meta spend (not modelled below) | ZAR 2,000 | Assumption |
| Test reserve | ZAR 1,000 | Assumption |
| Average cost per click, Google | ZAR 5 | Assumption |
| Clicks from Google | 600 | ZAR 3,000 ÷ ZAR 5 |
| Enquiry rate from clicks | 3% | Assumption |
| Enquiries | 18 | 600 × 3% |
| Close rate | 25% | Assumption |
| New customers | 4.5 | 18 × 25% |
| Average sale | ZAR 1,200 | Assumption |
| Revenue from Google | ZAR 5,400 | 4.5 × ZAR 1,200 |
| Return on Google spend | 1.8× | ZAR 5,400 ÷ ZAR 3,000 |

A return of 1.8× is profitable only if your margin on a ZAR 1,200 sale covers the cost of the sale, and this example does not assume a margin. If an agency managed the ZAR 3,000 Google spend at the 15% to 20% fee range, the fee would be ZAR 450 to ZAR 600 a month, which comes out of the return before any profit.

## Should you manage ads yourself or hire an agency?

Agency fees are commonly set as a share of spend. A secondary source reports that Facebook or Google Ads management typically earns 15% to 20% of the managed ad spend. On a ZAR 3,000 Google budget, that is a meaningful monthly cost for a small business, so compare it with the time you would spend doing the work yourself.

Free training exists for owners who want to run the account in-house. The same secondary source describes Google's Digital Skills for Africa programme as free digital marketing training, and Meta's Blueprint courses on Facebook and Instagram advertising as free, with certification on completion. Check each provider's live page for current course terms before you rely on them.

## How do you measure enquiries, not just clicks?

Clicks do not show whether a campaign pays. A practical measurement setup, described in a PinkLime checklist for small businesses, has four parts:

- Google Analytics configured with conversion tracking
- Google Ads conversion tracking
- A Facebook Pixel on the website
- Call tracking for phone leads

Add WhatsApp enquiries to that list as a conversion you record manually, because many South African customers start a conversation there rather than by phone or form. Adwave advises checking return on ad spend daily or at least weekly for platforms such as Google and Facebook, which gives you time to act before a weak week becomes a weak month.

## How should you test before committing a larger budget?

Keep the first test small. Adwave's 30-day test plan prices its Week 3 paid digital test, a Google Local Services or Facebook campaign, at $50 to $100. That amount is enough to check whether enquiries come in before you commit the ZAR 6,000 in the worked example.

If results are modest rather than clearly positive, Adwave suggests testing different creative for another 60 days before deciding. Compare each test against the enquiry and customer figures in your worked budget, not against impressions. For a broader comparison of how to split a small budget, see the [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads guide for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business).

## FAQ

### Which platform should a South African small business test first?

Start with Google Search if customers look up your service before buying, and Facebook or Instagram if people discover you through content. Run the smallest test you can measure, then compare enquiries against the worked budget above.

### Is the $5.42 cost per click a South African benchmark?

No. It is a US search median for 2026 reported by Adwave. Use it only to understand scale, and check rand costs in your own platform account before setting a budget.

### Do I need an agency to run South African ads?

Not necessarily. Agencies often charge 15% to 20% of managed spend, so compare that cost with the time you would spend. Free training from Google and Meta is available for owners who want to manage the account themselves.

### How long should a test run before I decide?

Adwave's 30-day test plan is a practical starting point. If results are modest, its guidance is to test different creative for another 60 days before deciding.
