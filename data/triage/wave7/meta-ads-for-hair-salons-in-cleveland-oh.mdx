## How much should a Cleveland hair salon spend on Meta ads?

Adwave's channel comparison puts a standalone Meta (Facebook and Instagram) buy at $300 to $1,000 per month, [per its channel guidance](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels). Bookeo suggests starting Facebook and Instagram ad spend at USD 750 to USD 1,500 a month, [in its salon guide](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/). The gap is wide because a single-chair salon and a multi-stylist salon need very different booking volumes.

Start at the lower end if your calendar has open slots you need to fill and you have not yet tested your booking flow. Start higher only if you already know which offer converts. Adwave notes that Meta campaigns [take weeks to optimize](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-small-business-2026), so judge results after at least four weeks at a steady spend, not after the first few days.

## Which Meta ad formats should a hair salon test first?

Short-form video is the format most worth testing. Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report as showing that [short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners) on Facebook and Instagram. That figure comes from a vendor summary of Meta's report, so verify it against Meta's own material before relying on it.

For a salon, useful video is simple: a colour process, a finished cut, or a short before-and-after. Stories can carry time-limited offers, since [Stories are temporary by design](https://www.associatedhairprofessionals.com/updates/blog-posts/power-social-media-your-hairstyling-brand). Ads need organic content behind them, too. Bookeo says most salon owners find [three to four Instagram posts and one to two Facebook posts weekly](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) sustainable. For a fuller organic plan, see [Instagram marketing for hair salons](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-hair-salons).

## How do you target a 5 to 10 mile radius around a salon?

Bookeo advises setting [a 5 to 10 mile radius around your salon](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) for Facebook and Instagram ads. Begin inside that band. A salon in a dense part of Cleveland may want the tighter end, while a salon near a suburb with fewer competing options may need the wider one. Let the placement and location breakdown in Ads Manager show where bookings actually come from, then narrow or widen the radius from there.

Meta Ads Manager is the central tool for this. It can [run ads across Instagram and Facebook](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising) from one place, and it covers [Facebook, Instagram, and Messenger](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media). Detailed targeting options change over time, so check the current list in Ads Manager instead of relying on a saved template. For Facebook-specific setup, read [Facebook ads for hair salons](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons).

## How should the ad copy and offer be written?

Lead with the service and a clear booking action rather than a general brand line. One offer per ad set is easier to judge than several offers mixed together. Test two versions of the creative at a time so you can see which message drives bookings, then retire the weaker one. Ads that run unchanged for months tend to lose effectiveness, so refresh the creative on a regular schedule.

Not every enquiry books on the first click. Routing new contacts into a follow-up sequence can recover some of them. The approach is covered in [AI-powered email marketing for hair salons](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-for-hair-salons). Search also matters: people who already type a service into Google are often closer to booking, so compare the two channels in [Google Ads for hair salons in Cleveland](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-in-cleveland-oh).

## What tracking should be in place before you spend?

Before the first dollar goes out, confirm four things:

1. The Meta Pixel is installed on the website and fires on the booking confirmation page.
2. A conversion event, such as a completed booking or form submission, is set up in Ads Manager.
3. Every ad URL carries UTM tags so Google Analytics can record Meta-sourced visits.
4. The booking system can report how many bookings came from each source.

Without these steps, the only numbers you can see are impressions and reach. Those do not show whether a chair got filled.

## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Meta ads per month?

Adwave lists a standalone Meta buy at $300 to $1,000 per month, and Bookeo suggests starting at USD 750 to USD 1,500. Begin where your booking capacity and tracking can support the spend, then adjust after several weeks of data.

### Do Facebook and Instagram ads need a business account?

Ads are run through Meta Ads Manager, but Bookeo notes that viewing [peak follower times in Insights requires a business account](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/). Set up the business account before you start, so the audience data is available.

### How long before Meta ads show results?

Adwave says Meta campaigns take weeks to optimize. Plan for at least four weeks at a steady spend before judging whether the budget is working.

### Should a salon run Meta ads or Google ads first?

It depends on how people find you. Meta reaches people who are not yet searching, while Google captures people already looking for a service. Many salons run both, starting with the channel whose results are easier to track in your booking system.
