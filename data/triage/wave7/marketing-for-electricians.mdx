Electricians should start with three channels: a service-area Google Business Profile, a review request after every completed job, and a call-focused paid campaign. Adwave's electrician guide suggests a starting budget of $500 to $1,500 a month, with half going to Google Local Services Ads and 30% to Business Profile and reviews. Measure booked jobs rather than clicks, because a cheap click that never becomes a call is not a lead.

This guide is for residential and light commercial electricians in the US. Any example built on invented inputs is labelled as an assumption.

## How Much Should an Electrician Spend on Marketing Each Month?

Adwave sets three budget tiers for electricians. The starting tier is $500 to $1,500 a month, split 50% to Google Local Services Ads, 30% to Google Business Profile and reviews, and 20% to truck wraps and yard signs. Those physical items are a useful reminder that a van is itself an advert in a local trade.

The growing tier of $1,500 to $4,000 shifts weight toward search: 35% to Google Ads and Local Services, 25% to social media, 20% to local SEO and 20% to connected TV. The established tier of $4,000 to $8,000-plus splits 25% to Google Ads and Local Services, 20% to connected TV, 20% to social media, 15% to local SEO, 10% to direct mail and 10% to reviews.

These tiers come from one vendor, so treat them as a starting structure rather than a benchmark. Adjust the split once you have several months of call and job data for your own area.

## What Does a Starting Budget Return for an Electrician?

The table below is a worked example. Every input marked assumption is an invented figure for illustration, and only the 50% split comes from Adwave's starting tier.

| Monthly budget (assumption, inside the $500 to $1,500 starting tier) | $1,000 |
| Share to Local Services Ads (Adwave starting tier) | 50%, or $500 |
| Booked jobs from that $500 (assumption) | 4 |
| Ad cost per booked job ($500 divided by 4) | $125 |
| Average gross profit per job (assumption) | $350 |
| Gross profit from those jobs (4 times $350) | $1,400 |
| Gross profit left after the $500 ad spend | $900 |

If only 2 of those jobs booked, the ad cost per job would double to $250, which is still below the assumed $350 gross profit. If your average job earns less than your ad cost per job, cut the spend or fix call handling before adding budget. Log which channel each call came from so the sums use real numbers.

## Are Local Services Ads Worth Testing for Electricians?

Adwave places half of the starting budget into Google Local Services Ads, which makes them the main paid channel at that level. That allocation is a vendor's recommendation, not a measured result, so run your own test long enough to log every call and job outcome.

Requirements for electricians can change, so check Google's live Local Services Ads help page for category eligibility and verification steps before you set up billing. For a comparison with a conventional search setup for a small local firm, the [Germany Google Ads guide for small businesses](/blog/local-marketing-germany-small-business-google-ads) shows how campaign structure can differ by market.

## Do Reviews Change Whether Customers Call an Electrician?

BrightLocal's consumer review survey, as reported by May-Fly, found that 89% of consumers are highly or fairly likely to use a business that responds to all of its online reviews. That makes replying to every review a cheap habit with a measurable effect on trust.

The same reporting cites a 2022 BrightLocal comparison in which the share of people who only paid attention to reviews from the previous two weeks dropped from 50% to 22%. Older reviews therefore still count. A third-party study cited in the same article says 72% of customers use Google reviews to find businesses. Ask for a review after every finished job and reply to each one, including the unhappy ones.

## Is Google Ads Fast Enough to Fill an Electrician's Diary?

99 Calls states that Google Ads often delivers visibility within hours of launching a campaign, compared with SEO. That speed suits emergency searches such as a power outage, where a phone number needs to be visible straight away. Planned work such as a panel upgrade or EV charger usually comes from searches that take longer to build through SEO and reviews.

The same vendor page tells client stories about electricians whose bookings rose. These are illustrative anecdotes rather than measured studies, so do not budget against them. Weave's guide to electrical marketing lists 17 practical techniques electricians can implement, which is a useful checklist for choosing what to try next.

## Does an Electrician Need a Website for Local Leads?

A website gives paid search and local listings a destination with service pages, license details and a contact form you can track. Keep one page per main service, and make the phone number a tap-to-call link, since many people will call from a mobile phone. For a mobile-first approach to small-business sites, the [Kenya mobile-first guide](/blog/local-marketing-kenya-small-business-mobile-first) is a useful reference.

Past customers, property managers and builders are common sources of repeat work. Note the source of every new job in a simple sheet, so you can see which channel and which referrer produce the bookings that pay the most.

## FAQ

### How much should an electrician spend on marketing?

Adwave's starting tier for electricians is $500 to $1,500 a month, and its growing tier is $1,500 to $4,000 a month. Start at the lower end, measure booked jobs, and move up only when the gross profit per job covers the cost per job.

### Are Local Services Ads a good first channel?

Adwave allocates 50% of its starting electrician budget to Google Local Services Ads. Check Google's live help page for eligibility and verification before you start, and test long enough to compare cost per booked job with other channels.

### Does responding to reviews really matter?

BrightLocal's survey, as reported by May-Fly, says 89% of consumers are highly or fairly likely to use a business that responds to all of its online reviews. Replying to every review is free and covers the reviews that people read first.

### How fast will Google Ads bring in calls?

99 Calls says Google Ads often delivers visibility within hours of launching a campaign, compared with SEO. Visibility is not the same as booked work, so judge the campaign on calls and booked jobs over a longer period.
