Five emails cover most of what a coffee shop needs: a welcome email, a new-drink announcement, a loyalty nudge, a win-back message and an event invite. The welcome email runs automatically for each new subscriber. The other four go out when there is news or when a regular has gone quiet. Email suits this job: according to Square's Future of Commerce 2025 report, [65% of Canadians like businesses to communicate with them by email](https://squareup.com/ca/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/marketing-plan-for-a-coffee-shop). The drafts below were written for this guide, not taken from real shops, so treat the subject lines as starting points to test.

## Which Coffee Shop Email Should You Send First?

Start with the welcome email. It goes to each new subscriber automatically and does one job: tell the reader what to do next. Use the subject line "Welcome. Your first coffee is on us" if you offer a first-visit treat, or "Here is what we pour" if you do not.

Keep the body short. Include one line on who you are, one offer or fact, your address and opening hours, and one button to order ahead if you offer that. Check your platform's help page to confirm which automation features your plan includes before you build a series.

Many shops already pay for a point-of-sale system that includes email tools. [The Restaurant HQ notes](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/) that most coffee shop POS systems with email marketing charge an additional monthly fee of $25 or more. Check whether your current system already includes this before you add another subscription.

## What Does a New-Drink Email Look Like?

The goal is to bring regulars in for a limited release. A sample subject line is "New on the menu: the cinnamon oat latte", where the drink name is invented for illustration. The body needs one photo, two sentences on what is in the drink, the dates it is available and a single button.

Measure clicks first. If you attach a discount code, count redemptions as well, because a click does not always become a sale. A new-drink email is a one-off campaign, so it reaches people who are already interested. Keep the send list to people who opted in, and do not add a second send unless the first one shows the drink is popular.

If you draft these with an AI assistant, [AI-assisted email marketing for coffee shops](/blog/ai-assisted-email-marketing-for-coffee-shops) covers related drafting steps. Always check any AI-written offer against what your staff can actually honour.

## How Do You Write a Loyalty Nudge?

The goal is to remind a customer how close they are to a reward. A sample subject line is "You are two stamps from a free coffee". This email needs visit data from your loyalty or POS system. Without that data, send a general reminder that explains the reward rules instead of a personal count.

Keep the message factual. State the number of visits still needed and the expiry date if there is one. Avoid urgency language that the offer cannot support.

Budget the reward before you launch. [Adwave's guide](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes) suggests planning $50-$300 per month for a loyalty platform, plus $200-$1,000 in incentive payouts such as free drinks or credit. For a platform that sends these emails automatically, [Using Klaviyo for coffee shop email marketing](/blog/using-klaviyo-for-coffee-shop-email-marketing) shows how to set up segments.

## What Should a Win-Back Email Say?

The goal is to bring back someone who has not visited for a while. A sample subject line is "We saved your usual". The body needs one friendly line, one reason to return and one small offer you can afford.

Decide what counts as lapsed using your own visit data. A common approach is to flag a customer once they are overdue by a set multiple of their normal gap between visits. There is no industry number for this in the sources, so base it on your own counter records.

Send the win-back once or twice, then stop. A customer who ignores two messages should move off the regular sends. ## Which Coffee Shop Emails Are Worth Measuring, and How?

The table below sets out what triggers each email and what to track. It is a planning tool, so adapt the measures to what your system records.

| Email | Trigger | Main measure | Sample subject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome | New subscriber joins | Clicks and first-visit redemptions | Welcome. Your first coffee is on us |
| New drink | Scheduled release date | Clicks and code redemptions | New on the menu: the cinnamon oat latte |
| Loyalty nudge | Visit count reaches threshold | Reward redemptions | You are two stamps from a free coffee |
| Win-back | Customer overdue by your set gap | Returning visits within two weeks | We saved your usual |
| Event invite | Date set for the event | Clicks and RSVPs | Saturday cupping: seats are limited |

Set a single measure for each email before you send it. If you track five metrics on every campaign, the results are hard to compare across sends.

## What Does a Win-Back Campaign Need to Return?

The break-even check below uses assumptions, not benchmarks. A single regular can be worth far more than a tool fee. [Adwave's illustrative example](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-lifetime-value-small-business) shows a customer spending $5 per visit who comes in four times a week for three years, generating $3,120 in total revenue. Adwave's benchmark table puts coffee shop customer lifetime value at $1,200-7,000.

The calculation below sizes a single monthly win-back send. All inputs are assumptions except the $25 fee, which comes from the POS reference above.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly email tool fee (from POS reference) | $25 |
| Average visit spend (assumption) | $5 |
| Lapsed customers emailed in the month (assumption) | 150 |
| Share who return within two weeks (assumption) | 4% |
| Returning visits (assumption) | 6 |
| Revenue from returning visits | $30 |
| Visits needed to cover the $25 fee | 5 |

At these assumptions, the send only just covers the fee. If your returning rate is higher, or a returning customer comes back more than once, the result improves quickly. Track the real return rate for your own list for two or three months before you judge the email.

## FAQ

### How many emails should a coffee shop send a month?
No source in this guide sets a number. Start with the welcome automation, add one news email at a time, and watch unsubscribes and redemptions before you increase volume.

### Do I need an app to send coffee shop email?
No. You need an email tool and a way to collect addresses, such as a sign-up prompt at the counter. A POS system with email features can cover both, but check its monthly fee first.

### Can a Google listing help grow the email list?
Yes, with conditions. [Per Diem's guide](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide) explains that Google asks you to verify your listing before it goes live, and that without verification your updates will not show publicly. Once verified, link your sign-up page in the profile.

### Are open rates reliable?
Not always. Privacy features in some mail apps can distort open counts, so measure clicks, redemptions and returning visits instead.
