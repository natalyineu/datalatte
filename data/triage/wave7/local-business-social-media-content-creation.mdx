A local business needs a small, repeatable content routine rather than a large strategy. A coffee shop guide from Bellwether Coffee suggests planning 3-4 hours a week for content creation and scheduling, which is a reasonable budget to test. Batching, which means planning and shooting several posts in one block, is the main way to keep that time under control. Each post should show something a nearby customer can act on: what you sell, proof that others trust you, or when to visit.

This guide covers shops, cafes, salons, studios and trades. The weekly and monthly figures are worked examples with inputs labelled as assumptions.

## How Many Hours a Week Should Content Take?

Bellwether Coffee advises 3-4 hours weekly for content creation and scheduling, and separately suggests 2-3 hours weekly for shooting and writing. Those ranges overlap, so treat them as one budget: about three to four hours including the photos, captions and scheduling. The MindStudio article reports that the average marketer spends 5 hours per week on content creation and approvals, which is a useful ceiling if you have to share sign-off with someone else.

Time is the main cost. Marketing 360 notes that the platforms are free to use, so the real expense is the time to create and post content. Track your hours for two weeks before you decide whether to add any paid tools.

## What Should a Local Business Post?

Pick from a small set of content types rather than inventing something new each week. Blackstone Infomatics lists six types that work for pet stores: user-generated content, behind-the-scenes moments, tips and advice, seasonal content, interactive features and product highlights in context. These translate well to most local shops, cafes and studios.

Customer content is worth prioritising. Bookeo reports that engagement on transformation posts is 28% higher when user-generated content is included. Ask customers for permission to share photos and reviews, and keep the caption specific: what was done, what it cost in time and what the next step is. Avoid generic stock imagery, because a real phone photo of today's product usually does more work than a polished graphic.

## Should You Batch Content in Advance?

Yes, where the content is predictable. Bookeo says batch-creating content proves 3 to 4 times more efficient than creating one post at a time. The Appointy guide recommends preparing at least 5-10 posts in each content category in advance, for days when you are too busy to create anything new. Reserve one block a month for shooting and a weekly slot for scheduling.

A content calendar is the simplest way to batch. Endless Media Solutions describes a 30/60/90-day content plan as a defined deliverable, which shows how far ahead a plan can run. If you later use software to draft posts, read [how AI agents for social media posting work for local businesses](/blog/ai-agent-for-social-media-posting-local-business) first, and keep a human review step before anything publishes.

## Which Platform Should You Start With?

Start with the platform where your customers already spend time, then do it well before adding another. Bellwether Coffee suggests TikTok is worth using if your target customers are under 35, citing significant organic reach for authentic content. Bookeo reports that 83% of Pinterest users say they have bought something after seeing a brand's content there, which can suit home, beauty and food businesses.

Short-lived formats need regular posting. An Associated Hair Professionals article notes that Instagram and Facebook stories are temporary, so they suit quick updates such as same-day openings or new stock rather than evergreen information. Check your own booking sources before choosing.

## What Tools Do You Need?

Few, at first. A notes app, a phone camera and the native scheduler in each platform are enough for a month of posting. When posting on time becomes the bottleneck, compare scheduling tools on price and channel limits. ## Worked Example: The Monthly Cost of Content

All inputs below are assumptions for a single owner running a small salon or cafe.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly content time | 3.5 hours | Assumption, midpoint of Bellwether's 3-4 hour range |
| Owner time value | $25 an hour | Assumption |
| Weeks per month | 4.33 | Calendar average |
| Average value of one new booking | $40 | Assumption |

Weekly cost is 3.5 × $25 = $87.50. Monthly cost is about $379 before any ad spend. At $40 a booking, the routine needs about 10 new bookings a month to break even (379 ÷ 40 = 9.5, rounded up).

If batching cuts the time by a third (assumption), the monthly cost falls to about $253, and break-even drops to about 7 bookings. Track how many new customers say they found you through social media for eight weeks, then compare that number with the break-even figure. If it stays well below break-even, change the content type before adding more posts.

## FAQ

### How often should a local business post?

The sources reviewed do not set one standard. Choose a frequency you can sustain for two months, using the 3-4 hour weekly budget as a guide, then compare enquiries and bookings.

### Do I need a paid scheduling tool?

Not at first. Platform schedulers are free to use, so the main cost is your time. Add a paid tool only when posting on time becomes the bottleneck.

### Which content should I prioritise?

Customer content and behind-the-scenes posts are good starting points. Bookeo reports 28% higher engagement on transformation posts when user-generated content is included.

### What if I run out of ideas?

Use the six content types from Blackstone Infomatics as a checklist, and turn each frequent customer question into a post.
