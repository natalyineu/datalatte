The best Google Ads keywords for a pet groomer are local and service-specific: "dog grooming near me", "[city] dog groomer" and "mobile dog grooming [city]". These phrases show a searcher who is close to booking. Group keywords by how ready the buyer is, bid on the ready-to-book terms first, and block searches for jobs, courses and do-it-yourself grooming. A worked example below uses assumed inputs to show how a $450 monthly budget could break even at three new clients.

Search volumes differ by city, so check your own numbers rather than copying a generic list. Google's [Keyword Planner](https://business.google.com/en-all/ad-tools/keyword-planner/) lets you start from your services or from your website to find related keyword ideas.

## Which Keywords Should a Pet Groomer Bid On?

Group keywords by how close the searcher is to booking, and put each group in its own ad group so the ad copy can match the search. The table below sets out three groups that suit most grooming businesses.

| Group | Example keywords | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready to book | dog groomer near me, [city] dog grooming, cat grooming [city] | Location plus service points to a nearby buyer |
| Specific service | mobile dog grooming, de-shedding treatment, puppy first groom, nail trim near me | The searcher already knows what they want |
| Problem or timing | dog groomer open saturday, same day dog grooming, groomer for matted dog | Urgency signals high intent |

Keep broad research terms such as "dog grooming tips" out of the paid plan. They attract clicks from people who are learning, not people who are ready to book. If you want a wider list for a different service type, the [dog groomer keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-dog-groomers-in-2026) goes deeper into phrasing.

Start with 10 to 15 keywords in one campaign. A small list is easier to review and easier to match to ad text.

## Which Match Types Should You Use?

Match types control how closely a search has to match your keyword before your ad can show. Exact match is the most controlled option: it covers searches with the same meaning or intent, and it produces the fewest unexpected searches. Phrase match covers searches that include the meaning of your phrase, so it reaches more buyers while still filtering some noise.

Broad match is the widest setting. It can show ads on many related searches, which is useful for finding new terms but harder to control on a small budget. Use it only after conversion tracking is working and you can see which searches actually book.

A practical order for a small groomer is to run exact and phrase match on core terms, review the search terms report for a few weeks, and test broad match later. The full definitions are in Google's [match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836).

## What Negative Keywords Should Pet Groomers Add?

Negative keywords stop your ads from showing on searches that will not book. Add them at the start, then keep adding to the list as real searches come in. Google's [negative keyword help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972) explains how to add them at campaign or account level.

A starter list for groomers:

- jobs, career, hiring, salary
- school, course, training, certification
- diy, at home, how to, tutorial
- supplies, clippers, shampoo, brush (unless you sell them)
- free, cheap (only if your pricing makes these irrelevant)
- vet, veterinarian, emergency (unless you offer those services)

Check the [search terms report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708) every week for the first month. Add any irrelevant term as a negative, and add plural or synonym versions yourself, because a negative does not automatically cover close variants.

## How Do You Target Only Nearby Pet Owners?

Set your location targeting around your shop or your service-area postcodes, and name the area in ad text. Google's [location targeting options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038) let you choose between people physically in your area and people who show interest in it. Read the current options on that page before you choose, since the settings can change.

Mobile groomers should draw their service area honestly. If a van cannot reach a postcode in a reasonable time, that postcode should not be in the targeting, even if it sounds like demand. The same radius problem affects mobile trades, which the [cleaning services keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-cleaning-services-in-2026) covers from a different angle.

On Facebook, one guide suggests targeting users within a 10 to 15 mile radius and pet owners aged 25 to 60 (secondary). Treat that as a starting test, not a rule, and adjust using booking data from your own area.

## What Does a Starter Budget Look Like?

The worked example below uses assumed inputs, not benchmarks. Replace each assumption with your own numbers before you decide anything.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $450 |
| Cost per click | $4.00 |
| Clicks per month | about 112 |
| Click-to-booking rate | 8% |
| New bookings | about 9 |
| Visits in a client's first year | 4 |
| Price per visit | $75 |
| First-year revenue per new client | $300 |
| Margin after groomer pay and supplies | 50% |
| Profit per new client | $150 |

At 9 bookings, profit is 9 × $150 = $1,350 against $450 of spend, a 3x return on paper. Break-even is $450 ÷ $150 = 3 clients, which is 3 out of 112 clicks, or about 2.7%. If your real booking rate sits below that, fix the landing page, phone answering or offer before adding budget.

Only first-year value is counted here, so repeat grooming can improve the return over time. Keep the daily cap low until a few weeks of data show which keywords book. ## How Should Groomers Handle Client Data From Ads?

Ads often send people to a booking form, a text line or a messaging tool, so the tools behind those routes matter. One [guide to client communication tools](https://buildergrowthengine.com/top-client-communication-tools-for-pet-grooming-businesses/) recommends choosing a platform that complies with data privacy regulations and securely handles client information (secondary).

Before you launch, check where form submissions go, who can see them and how long they are kept. Pick one booking route and make sure every ad points to it. A mixed set of contact methods makes it harder to tell which keywords produced a booking.

## FAQ

### What is the best Google Ads keyword for a pet groomer?

A local, service-specific phrase such as "dog grooming near me" or "[city] dog groomer" is usually the strongest starting point. Check Keyword Planner for your own area, because search volume varies by city.

### Should a small pet groomer use broad match?

Only after conversion tracking is working and you can see which searches book. Many small accounts get better control by starting with exact and phrase match, then reviewing the search terms report.

### What negative keywords should I add first?

Start with job, course, DIY and free-or-cheap terms, plus any competitor or service you do not offer. Review the search terms report weekly during the first month and keep adding negatives.

### How much should I start with?

A small daily cap that you can afford to test is safer than a large launch. The budget example in this guide uses a $450 monthly assumption to show the break-even math, not a recommended spend.

### Can I use Keyword Planner without a large budget?

Yes. Keyword Planner can help you find related keyword ideas from your services or your website before you commit to a campaign.
