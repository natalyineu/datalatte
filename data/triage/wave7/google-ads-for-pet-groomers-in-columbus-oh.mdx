## When Do Groomers Get the Most Bookings?

Adwave, citing Groomer to Groomer, notes that November and December are traditionally a very busy time for groomers, sometimes too busy ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/pet-grooming-advertising)). That matters for budget timing. A salon that already fills its calendar in late autumn may not need extra clicks then, while a salon with open slots in the slower months may want to spend more before the holidays.

No source here measures Columbus demand by month. Check your own booking history for the last two or three years and mark which weeks filled first. Use that calendar to set higher spend during the weeks you have openings, and lower spend when you are already booked. Pausing ads during full weeks avoids paying for clicks you cannot serve.

## Which Keywords Should a Columbus Groomer Bid On?

Start with searches that name the service and the place. AdTurbo lists "dog groomer near me", "dog grooming near me" and "puppy grooming near me" as keywords worth bidding on ([AdTurbo](https://adturbo.ai/google-ads-for-dog-groomers)). For Columbus, pair those with city and neighborhood terms you can verify in Google Keyword Planner, since no published source gives Columbus search volumes.

| Theme | Example | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service plus city | "dog grooming Columbus" | Clear local intent |
| Near me | "dog groomer near me" | Searchers ready to book |
| Neighborhood | "dog groomer [your neighborhood]" | Narrower audience, less waste |
| Specific service | "puppy grooming near me" | Fewer direct rivals |
| Mobile | "mobile dog grooming Columbus" | Only if you actually travel to clients |

Negative keywords matter as much as the positive list. GroomerGenie says its proprietary list holds more than 500 groomer negatives ([GroomerGenie](https://www.groomergenie.com/services/google-ads)). You do not need a vendor list to start. Add terms such as "jobs", "course", "DIY", "supplies" and "how to" based on your own Search terms report, and check the platform's live help page for how negative keywords match singular and plural forms.

## How Should a Columbus Groomer Set the Service Area?

Radius is the main lever for keeping spend near your salon. Barklead suggests targeting users within a 10 to 15 mile radius, though that advice is written for Facebook ads, not Google Search ([Barklead](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers)). Treat it as a starting test, not a rule. Google's location settings and minimum radius rules change, so confirm them on the platform's live help page before you set a campaign.

Use your own booking records to pick the radius. Map where your last 50 bookings came from, then set the radius to cover most of them. Review the Search terms and location reports after two weeks. If clicks come from areas you cannot serve, tighten the radius. If you are comparing paid formats rather than only Search, the [Local Services Ads guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-local-services-ads-for-pet-groomers) covers a different model with its own setup.

## What Do Published Groomer Case Results Show?

Marketing 360 published a case study on a mobile pet groomer who advertised on Facebook and Google. The study reports a 134% increase in impressions and a 696% increase in clicks compared with the previous six months, with conversions also rising ([Marketing 360](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/)). The page was published on June 13, 2023.

Those percentages are useful as proof that paid search can grow traffic from a small base, but they do not give starting spend, cost per click or the number of bookings. Percentage growth from a low baseline can look large. Measure your own before-and-after numbers using bookings, not clicks alone.

## What Should You Track Before Spending More?

AdTurbo names booking a grooming appointment as the primary conversion to track for dog groomer ads ([AdTurbo](https://adturbo.ai/google-ads-for-dog-groomers)). Set that up first as a conversion action, then add calls and contact-form requests as secondary actions. Count only calls long enough to be real inquiries, and keep the threshold consistent across weeks.

Review the Search terms report weekly. Cut terms that bring clicks but no calls or bookings, and add those terms to your negative list. For a single view of spend, calls and bookings across several weeks, the [AI-driven local SEO reports built in Google Data Studio](/blog/ai-driven-local-seo-reports-with-google-data-studio) walkthrough shows a reporting setup you can adapt.

On these assumptions, first-visit revenue slightly covers the ad spend. Now test the high end of the range. At $8 per click, the same $600 buys 75 clicks, about 9 requests and roughly 4.5 new clients (assumption: same rates), which is about $133 per client. That is more than double the cost, so the click price matters as much as the budget. Check your own conversion rate before you scale a campaign. ## FAQ

### How much does Google Ads cost for a Columbus pet groomer?

No published source gives a Columbus-specific groomer click price. AdTurbo places dog groomer clicks at about $2 to $8, and most advertisers spend $800 to $5,000 a month. Your real cost depends on your radius, keywords and conversion rate.

### How much should I spend to start?

Start with a budget you can test for several weeks. Work backward from how many new clients you can take each week and your cost per booking request, using the worked example above with your own inputs.

### Should I target all of Columbus?

Not at first. Start near your salon, using your own booking records to choose the radius. Widen only if the location report shows strong calls and bookings from farther away.

### How long until I know if it works?

There is no standard timeline in the sources reviewed. Compare booked appointments against cost over several weeks, and judge the campaign by bookings rather than clicks.
