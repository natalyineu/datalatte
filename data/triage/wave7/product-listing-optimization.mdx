Google can reject a product listing when its title carries promotional wording or when a service is submitted as a product. To optimize a listing, keep discounts and store names out of the title, lead with the product type and the detail that sets it apart, and submit only physical goods in the Products tab. Pet boutiques, coffee roasters and salon retail lines all face the same rules. The steps below turn them into a checklist, with a worked example and sources marked by type.

## Which title wording can get a Google product listing rejected?

Adwave's guide to Google Business Profile products says Google may reject a product listing whose title contains promotional text. Its examples are a discount such as "50% OFF" and a phrase such as "Best Deal in Town," which the guide calls no-go wording (secondary).

Treat sale dates, shipping offers and store names the same way. They are promotional or store-level detail, not product detail. This is a cautious reading of that rule, not a quoted Google requirement.

A stronger title leads with the product type, then adds the details a shopper would search for, such as material, size, scent or colour. Those words belong in the title. A discount does not.

## Can you list a service in the Products tab?

No. The same Adwave guide states that you cannot put "Free Consultation" in the Products tab, and that Google may reject a listing that presents a service as a product (secondary).

For a salon, a cut or colour appointment is not a product listing, but a retail shampoo is. Keep the two apart. Link bookings to your service pages rather than the product grid, so the listing describes something a shopper can buy as a physical item.

## What does a before and after listing look like?

The example below is an **assumption**. The shampoo, its claims and the brand "Paws & Co" are invented for illustration. Write only what is true of your own product.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Title | SALE!!! Best Dog Shampoo - Free Shipping - Paws & Co | Oatmeal Dog Shampoo for Sensitive Skin, Fragrance Free, 16 oz Bottle |
| Promotional text | "SALE!!!" and "Free Shipping" | None |
| Store name | "Paws & Co" in the title | None |
| Product detail | Only "Best Dog Shampoo" | Product type, skin type, scent and size |

Description before: "Our groomers love it! See also our conditioner. Buy now, free shipping over $50." It mixes testimonials, a link to another product and a shipping offer, none of which describe the shampoo.

Description after: "Gentle oatmeal shampoo for dogs with sensitive or dry skin. Fragrance free. 16 oz bottle with pump. Ingredients: oatmeal, aloe, glycerin." The ingredient list is an assumption for this example. Every claim in your own description needs to appear on the product page too.

## What is a realistic conversion gain for a listing?

Adwave's summary of benchmark data puts e-commerce product pages at around 2 to 5% conversion (secondary). Use that range for planning, not as a promise. Adwave's guidance on review signals makes a similar point: its ranges are decision rules, not universal performance thresholds (secondary).

The arithmetic below uses two inputs that are **assumptions**: 300 visits a month to the shampoo page, and an average order value of $45.

At 2% conversion, 300 × 0.02 = 6 orders, or $270 a month. At 5%, 300 × 0.05 = 15 orders, or $675 a month. The gap is $405 a month. That is the size of the range a listing change is working within, not a forecast of what a new title will deliver.

## What is the step checklist for a new listing?

1. Remove promotional wording and store names from the title (Adwave, secondary).
2. Check that no service appears in the Products tab.
3. Shoot five images: hero, angled view, side or profile, back or secondary face, and close-up detail (Adwave photo guide, secondary).
4. Make the landing page match the listing exactly, including bundle contents. Check it on a phone with the [mobile optimization guide](/blog/mobile-optimization-local-business) and confirm the key facts show above the fold using the [above-the-fold design guide](/blog/above-the-fold-design-local-business).
5. Give the listing about a week at full price before adding any offer (Tracknlist, secondary).
6. If a listing has steady views and no sales after about 90 days, change the price or photos rather than relisting (Tracknlist, secondary).
7. Test title variants one at a time, following the [A/B testing guide](/blog/a-b-testing-for-local-business).

## How should you check AI-written listing copy?

AI drafting tools can produce titles and descriptions quickly, but each claim still needs checking against the product: materials, ingredients, age suitability and size. Any claim you cannot prove from your packaging or supplier sheet should be removed before the listing goes live.

For broader context on how AI-driven search reads business pages, see the [generative engine optimization guide](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-local-business). It does not replace checking your own copy.

## Where do offers and local listings fit?

Your Google Business Profile is a free listing. Local Search Ads are a separate paid product that runs through a Google Ads account (Keepers Digital, secondary). Keep these two budgets and workflows apart when you plan.

An Offer post on the profile needs a title plus a start and end date, and Google will not publish it without them (dmly, secondary). A 2020 BrewRound guide also lists a voucher code and a redemption link among the accepted fields (secondary, dated 2020), so check the current form before you rely on that list.

## FAQ

### Can I put the sale price or "free shipping" in a product title?
Google may reject a listing whose title contains promotional text, and Adwave gives "50% OFF" as an example of wording to avoid (secondary). Leave sale prices and shipping offers out of the title.

### Can a service such as a consultation go in the Products tab?
No. Adwave's guide says a service such as "Free Consultation" cannot go in the Products tab, and Google may reject that listing (secondary).

### How long should a new listing run at full price before I send offers?
Tracknlist's guide suggests about a week at full price so the listing can find its audience (secondary). Check your own sales data before adding discounts.

### Does a better listing guarantee more sales?
No. The 2 to 5% conversion range is a benchmark summary, and Adwave's own guidance treats its ranges as decision rules rather than guaranteed results (secondary). Measure your own pages before and after a change.
