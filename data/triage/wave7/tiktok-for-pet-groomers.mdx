TikTok can pay for a pet groomer when the viewers who book live close enough to drive to your salon. Posting organically costs nothing in fees, but it costs time. In the worked example below, an assumed 6 hours a month at an assumed $30 an hour comes to $180. With an assumed $63 gross profit per new client, you need about 3 new clients a month to break even. Every input there is an assumption, so replace each one with your own figures. This guide is for salon and mobile groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada.

## Is TikTok worth a groomer's time?

Short video suits grooming because a groom already has a clear before and after. Booksy's salon marketing guide says high-impact "Before and After" videos capture attention in the first two seconds ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-marketing-ideas), secondary), so the reveal belongs near the start of the clip.

## Which before-and-after videos should you film?

Five formats cover most of what a local groomer needs. Each can be filmed during a normal working day, so you do not need a studio or a script.

### 1. The before-and-after reveal

Film the dog on arrival and the finished dog from the same angle, in the same light. Use a plain towel or backdrop so the coat is the focus. An admanage guide for construction firms recommends building 5 to 10 before-and-after assets in the first week, photos and short videos together, and it suggests the 4:5 format for feed placements and before-and-after content ([Admanage](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-for-construction-companies), secondary).

### 2. The process clip

Clippers through a coat, a brush finishing a doodle, scissoring a face. Keep real sound where the dog is calm. These are easy to batch because you film them while you work.

### 3. Coat-care explainers

Answer the questions your regulars already ask, such as how often a doubled coat needs attention. These usually get fewer views than a reveal, but viewers who ask those questions tend to be serious about booking. Avoid chasing dance formats. Infegy reports that get-ready-with-me content surged on TikTok and Instagram in early 2023, then declined as creators shifted toward lifestyle storytelling ([Infegy](https://www.infegy.com/blog/three-ways-to-find-beauty-insights), secondary).

### 4. First-groom and nervous-dog stories

A puppy's first visit, filmed gently with the owner's permission, shows the calm approach that most owners are looking for. For more ideas that suit this format, see [pet groomer Instagram content ideas](/blog/pet-groomer-instagram-content-ideas-that-go-viral).

### 5. Local hooks

Name the neighbourhood, the street outside your salon, or a seasonal coat tip for your town. These bring nearby viewers, which matters more than total reach. A [coffee shop TikTok guide](/blog/coffee-shop-tiktok-marketing-guide) uses the same local logic for a different trade.

## What rules keep grooming videos safe and kind?

Platform rules on animal content change, so check the current version before you post. The rules below are good practice for the dogs and owners in your videos.

Never film a dog in distress for a more dramatic clip. A frightened dog on camera costs trust faster than a dull video costs views. Get the owner's permission before posting any client's pet, and keep a note of it. The consent logic in [salon before-and-after photo guidance](/blog/hair-salon-before-after-photos-legal-and-converting) applies here too: a line on your booking form covering photos and video is a sensible start.

Be honest about matting. A badly matted dog may need a shave-down for its comfort, so explain that kindly rather than as a transformation failure. Do not post health or behaviour details about a pet or owner. Finally, keep the dog safe first. If you are filming, you are not watching the dog, so use a tripod or a second pair of hands near clippers and dryers.

## How do you make a video send viewers to your door?

Shoot vertical. Adwave's guide says to export 9:16 for Reels, TikTok and Stories so the video fills the screen, and to use 16:9 widescreen for your website, Google Business Profile or a TV spot ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/restaurant-promo-videos-with-ai), secondary). If you use an AI video tool, set the aspect ratio before generating so framing and captions fit a phone.

Before uploading, check the current length limits and music permissions for each platform, as Adwave advises ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-make-tiktok-videos-with-ai), secondary). A trending sound you do not have a licence for can be a problem on a business account.

Put your neighbourhood in the first seconds, on screen or spoken, and repeat it in the caption. Name the place in your bio too, and add a booking link if your account allows one. Cross-post the best clips to Instagram using the approach in [the pet groomers' Instagram marketing guide](/blog/pet-groomers-guide-to-instagram-marketing).

Break-even is $180 divided by $63, about 2.9 new clients a month. The Strong column is a rare outcome, so do not plan around it. Two levers matter more than views. The first is repeat visits: a dog groomed every 6 to 8 weeks (assumption) makes a client worth far more than the first booking. The second is local share, because a clip with half the views but twice the local viewers can deliver the same bookings.

## Should you pay for TikTok ads yet?

Not before you know which organic formats get booked. Paid testing only makes sense once you have a format that already draws local comments and enquiries. TikTok's ad budget rules are on its help centre, and minimums can change, so check the current figures before you plan a test. As a rough planning figure, a 14-day test at an assumed $280 would need about 4.4 new clients at $63 gross profit each just to cover its cost.

## What should you do this week?

1. Look at your last 30 bookings and note rough client ages, so you know whether TikTok fits your regulars.
2. Add a photo and video permission line to your booking form, plus a "How did you find us?" question with TikTok as an option.
3. Film one batch during normal work: three reveals, two process clips, and one clip that names your neighbourhood out loud.
4. Set every clip to vertical, check current music permissions, and add your area to the first seconds and the caption.
5. Each Sunday, log views, local comments and bookings that mention a video. Judge the account after 8 to 12 weeks (a planning assumption), not after one viral clip.

## FAQ

### Do I need to be on camera?

No. Hands, tools and the dog can carry the video. A voiceover or on-screen text can tell the story. Show your face if you want to, but it is not required.

### How often should I post?

The sources here do not set a minimum. Pick a schedule you can sustain for months, and review results by bookings rather than views.

### Can I use trending sounds?

Only after checking the current music permissions for the platform, as Adwave advises before uploading ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-make-tiktok-videos-with-ai), secondary).

### What if my videos get views but no bookings?

Usually the viewers are not local, or the clip gives no reason or route to book. Add the neighbourhood, a booking link and a clear next step, then track enquiries with your form.
