An AI chatbot for a local business is worth buying when it answers the same handful of questions (opening hours, prices, location, availability and how to book) and passes everything else to a person. The monthly fee is only part of the cost. Setup time, usage caps, and whether the bot can write a booking into your calendar decide whether it pays for itself. This guide covers what to check on each pricing page, a worked break-even calculation, and a four-step setup.

## What can a booking and FAQ chatbot actually do?

A chatbot for a small business does three jobs well. It answers fixed questions, collects the details for a booking, and routes anything unusual to a person with the conversation attached.

Fixed answers cover opening hours, parking, price ranges, cancellation rules and the services list. Booking is the valuable part, because the bot either shows a link or collects a name, service and preferred time. Handoff matters because a bot that guesses at a complaint or a custom quote loses the customer quickly.

It should not negotiate prices, give medical or legal advice, or promise anything missing from its knowledge base. If your enquiries involve quotes or multi-step jobs, you need more than a scripted bot. The comparison in [AI-powered chatbot vs live chat for local business](/blog/ai-powered-chatbot-vs-live-chat-local-business) explains where that line sits.

## How much does an AI chatbot cost for a small business?

Plan prices change often, and pricing pages do not always agree with review sites. A [secondary review of Grok](https://toolschool.ai/tools/grok) lists the X Premium+ price as $16 a month in one section and $40 a month in its FAQ. Treat any review as a starting point and use the vendor's own pricing page on the day you buy.

Check four things before comparing plans. First, setup fees. Some vendors state there are none on monthly pay-as-you-go plans, as [Stamp Me's pricing page](https://www.stampme.com/pricing) does, so ask every vendor the same question directly.

Second, usage caps. Free tiers are useful for testing but limit volume. The same Grok review says its free tier allows about 10 requests every two hours for Grok 3, which is far too few for a busy inbox. Third, bundling. Grok is included with X Premium+ or sold as a standalone SuperGrok subscription, and that choice changes the real monthly bill. Fourth, add-ons. AI answers are often billed separately from the base chat plan, so read exactly what each price includes.

## Worked example: when does a chatbot pay for itself?

Every input below is an assumption. Replace them with your own numbers before deciding. The example uses a nail studio that receives 120 chat enquiries a month.

1. Recovered revenue: 10 extra confirmed bookings × $60 (assumption) = $600 a month.
2. Setup cost: 4 hours × $25 an hour (both assumptions) = $100, spread over 12 months = $8.33 a month.
3. Subscription: $50 a month (assumption).
4. Total monthly cost: $50 + $8.33 = $58.33.
5. Cost per booking: $58.33 ÷ 10 = $5.83. Bookeo defines cost per booking as total spend divided by confirmed bookings from that channel, and this uses the same method.
6. Break-even: $58.33 ÷ $60 = 0.97, so one confirmed booking covers the monthly cost.

The check that matters is the recovery rate. Ten recovered bookings from 120 enquiries is about 8%. If you cannot see the bot producing bookings at that rate, the plan is not paying back, whatever the break-even says.

Break-even also assumes each booking is completed. A form submission is an inquiry until the booking process confirms the appointment, a point [TailWerks makes](https://tailwerks.com/industries/pet-grooming-marketing-agency/) directly. Count only confirmed appointments in the calculation.

## Does the bot book appointments or only send a link?

This is the question most buyers skip. A booking link is different from an integration that can read or change appointments, and TailWerks does not assume a two-way connection from a software name alone. Ask the vendor to show a test booking appearing in your calendar, not just a demo of the chat window.

Setup effort varies by calendar tool. GoHighLevel's calendar asks you to define the class name, duration, seat capacity and payment amount in a visual interface, and a [SMMcalc review](https://smmcalc.com/blog/best-class-booking-system/) says the initial setup takes a few hours. Costs for that platform are covered in [GoHighLevel pricing for local business](/blog/gohighlevel-pricing-for-local-business).

## Which questions should the bot answer first?

Start with booking intent, because most online contact is about booking. In a 2024 medical practice study summarised by [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/appointment-booking-page-design), 76.8% of online interactions were new bookings, 13.1% were rescheduling requests and 10.1% were cancellations. A medical practice is not a salon, so treat the split as a hint, not a benchmark for your shop.

Social media booking deserves the same caution. Bookeo states that brands adopting social media booking tools see a 14% lift in total bookings. That is a vendor claim, so test it on your own enquiries. A simple way is to run two greeting messages for two weeks and compare booking rates, as set out in [A/B testing for local business](/blog/a-b-testing-for-local-business).

## How do you set up a chatbot in four steps?

1. Write the ten questions customers ask most, with exact answers. Pull them from direct messages, phone calls and reviews, and keep answers short.
2. Pick one channel where customers already message you, such as website chat, Instagram or Facebook Messenger. Adding every channel at once makes transcripts hard to review.
3. Connect the booking tool and test with a real appointment. Confirm whether the bot writes to your calendar or only sends a link.
4. Set up tracking. [Adwave's pet grooming guide](https://adwave.com/resources/pet-grooming-advertising) advises installing the tracking snippet on the booking confirmation page so bookings count as conversions. Then review transcripts weekly for the first month and fix wrong answers about holiday hours and prices.

## Which type of chatbot fits your business?

If you get only a handful of chats a month, a free plan or a plain FAQ page with a booking link may be enough. If most contact comes through Instagram or Facebook messages, pick a tool built for social messaging. If most visitors arrive on your website, pick a website chat tool.

Complex jobs such as quotes, custom packages or multi-step bookings need more than a scripted bot. In that case, look at an AI agent that can check availability and confirm slots, which is covered in [AI agent for appointment booking](/blog/ai-agent-for-appointment-booking-local-business).

## FAQ

### Will a chatbot make my business feel impersonal?

Only if it replaces people instead of supporting them. Use your own wording, keep answers short, and always offer a visible option to talk to a person during opening hours.

### What if the chatbot gives wrong information?

It can. Keep the knowledge base short, update it for holidays and price changes, and read transcripts weekly. Limit the bot to facts you have written down.

### Can it connect to my booking software?

Check each vendor's integration list and ask for a test booking. A booking link and an integration that reads and changes appointments are different things, so confirm which one you are buying.

### How do I know it is working?

Compare two weeks before launch with two weeks after. Track confirmed bookings per week, average first-response time and revenue from online bookings.
