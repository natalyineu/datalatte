An Amsterdam café needs to win two groups: visitors who search for a coffee shop on their phone, and locals who return because the place fits their routine. Start with a verified Google Business Profile in the right category, answer every review, post on Instagram in Dutch and English, and offer a simple loyalty card. Most of these steps cost little cash but need steady weekly time. Here "coffee shop" means a café that sells coffee, not a cannabis coffeeshop.

## How Do You Set Up a Google Business Profile in Amsterdam?

Visitors and locals both search before they walk in. Statista data cited by [The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/) says 98% of consumers use the internet to find new businesses (secondary). Your profile is the first thing they see, so it has to be verified and complete.

Google asks you to verify a coffee shop before the listing goes live. [Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide) describes this as usually a postcard with a code sent to the business address, with some businesses able to verify by phone or email (secondary). Without verification, updates do not show publicly and customers may not find you.

Choose "Coffee shop" or a related term as your category, not something broad such as "Restaurant". Google also lets people message businesses through the listing, so assign one person to check those messages every day.

Searchers often use phrases like "best coffee shop in [city]", which [Salesforce UK](https://www.salesforce.com/uk/marketing/local-marketing/?bc=OTH) gives as a long-tail example (secondary). For Amsterdam, test "best coffee shop in Amsterdam" alongside neighbourhood phrases such as "coffee shop De Pijp". [seoplus](https://seoplus.com/paid-ads/the-ultimate-guide-to-multi-location-ppc-advertising-on-google-ads/) notes that localised phrases are likely to face less competition than generic terms (secondary), so check real search volumes in your own keyword tool before building pages around them.

## What Should a Café Do in Its First 30 Days?

Work through these steps in order, because each one depends on the one before it.

1. Confirm that verification is complete, since unverified updates do not show publicly.
2. Set the category to "Coffee shop" and choose two or three location phrases to test.
3. Reply to every review, in the reviewer's language where you can write it well.
4. Post on one social channel every day, and add a second only once the first has a routine.
5. Run a loyalty card for two months with tracked visits before deciding whether to keep it.

## How Should You Reply to Reviews in Two Languages?

[Digital Resource](https://www.yourdigitalresource.com/post/seo-for-restaurants-coffee) says featuring reviews or testimonials from real customers can help build trust and increase conversion rates by 270% (secondary). The article was published on 15 August 2019, so treat that figure as a vendor claim. Still, real quotes from real customers are cheap to collect and easy to check.

Reply in the reviewer's language when your writing is good enough, and in English otherwise. Keep replies short: thank the person and mention the drink or the staff member they named. For complaints, acknowledge the problem, offer a direct contact, and avoid arguing in public.

Collect email addresses at the counter so regulars can hear about new drinks. For ideas, see [coffee shop email marketing examples](/blog/coffee-shop-email-marketing-examples).

## Which Social Channels Fit an Amsterdam Café?

[Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops) gives a daily time estimate and audience for each channel. Use it to match effort to return, especially if one person runs everything.

| Channel | Time per day (guide) | Audience it suits (guide) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram | 30 min | Visual brand, local discovery, all ages |
| TikTok | 1 hour | Growth, mainly 18 to 34 |
| Facebook | 15 min | Local community, events, skews 30 and older |
| Google Business | 5 min | Local search, reviews, anyone searching |

The same guide recommends 2 to 3 hours a week for shooting and writing content, and sets a goal engagement rate of 3 to 6% on Instagram. For a bilingual café, a bilingual caption lets one post serve both groups. See [Instagram marketing for coffee shops](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-marketing-strategy) for post formats.

Digital Resource also says a giveaway or contest can boost brand awareness by 30% and grow followers 70% faster (secondary). Run one small contest at a time and measure new followers who visit.

## How Do You Build a Loyalty Offer Locals Will Use?

[Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes) recommends planning $50 to $300 per month for a loyalty platform, plus $200 to $1,000 in incentive payouts such as free drinks or credit (secondary). Its 2026 comparison lists in-shop loyalty programs at $50 to $1,300 per month (secondary). These are US-dollar planning figures, so convert them to euros and check real vendor quotes.

A regular comes back for the habit, the staff and a small reward. The aim is visit frequency, not deep discounts. Tools that handle stamps and customer messages are covered in [AI tools for coffee shop marketing automation](/blog/ai-tools-for-coffee-shop-marketing-automation).

## What Does a Loyalty Card Cost Each Month?

The worked example below uses assumptions for every input. Replace them with your own sales data before deciding.

| Item | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active card holders | 120 | Assumption |
| Visits per holder per month | 8 | Assumption |
| Drinks bought per month | 960 | 120 times 8 |
| Card rule: every 10th drink free | 96 free drinks | 960 divided by 10 |
| Menu price per drink | EUR 3.80 | Assumption |
| Ingredient and cup cost per drink | EUR 0.90 | Assumption |
| Revenue given up on free drinks | about EUR 365 | 96 times 3.80 |
| Direct cost of free drinks | about EUR 86 | 96 times 0.90 |
| Extra visits caused by the card | 120 | Assumption: one per holder per month |
| Revenue from extra visits | about EUR 456 | 120 times 3.80, assumption |

On these assumptions, the card gives up about EUR 365 of menu revenue, but extra visits bring about EUR 456. Whether that covers labour and rent depends on your own cost base, so compare the result with the platform fee converted from the $50 to $300 range. Track visits per card for two months before you decide.

## Should You Advertise Around the Café?

Adwave recommends a 1 to 3 mile geo-targeting radius around the shop for Reels promotions (secondary). Its multi-channel example runs ads for a new fall latte to everyone within a five-mile radius of the store (secondary). Those are US examples, so for Amsterdam, a radius matching a walk or short cycle from your door is an assumption to test.

Start small, measure visits or redeemed offers, then widen the area if the results hold. ## FAQ

### Do I need a verified Google Business Profile?

Yes. [Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide) states that without verification, updates do not show publicly and customers may not find the café. Verification usually arrives by postcard with a code.

### Should my café use Dutch or English?

Use both. A Dutch lead line speaks to neighbours, and an English one serves visitors. Check which language gets more replies, messages and review responses over two months, then adjust.

### How much time should I spend on content each week?

The [Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops) guide recommends 2 to 3 hours a week for shooting and writing content (secondary).

### Are giveaways worth running?

[Digital Resource](https://www.yourdigitalresource.com/post/seo-for-restaurants-coffee) reports a 30% awareness boost and 70% faster follower growth from contests (secondary). These are vendor claims from a 2019 article, so measure new followers and visits from your own contest before repeating it.
