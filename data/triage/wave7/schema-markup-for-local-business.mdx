To add LocalBusiness schema, write one block of JSON-LD that matches your Google Business Profile, place it on the page that describes your business, and check it with Google's free Rich Results Test. Google says businesses must follow its guidelines to be eligible for Local Business rich results, so accuracy matters more than volume. This walkthrough assumes one location and about one hour of work. For the broader picture, read our [LocalBusiness schema and rich snippets guide](/blog/local-business-schema-markup-rich-snippets-google).

## Why Start With the LocalBusiness Type?

The starting point for pretty much all local schema is the LocalBusiness type, according to [Adwave's local schema guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-schema-markup-helping-google-understand-your-business) (secondary). Structured data describes location and services so search engines can display rich snippets, as [Efficient Media explains](https://www.efficientmedia.com.au/top-local-seo-sydney-services/) (secondary).

Another vendor guide calls LocalBusiness schema the gold standard for any physical business. It lists business name, address, phone number, opening hours, aggregate rating and price range as the core details ([Adwave on voice search](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses), secondary).

A more specific subtype of LocalBusiness may fit your business better. Start from the general type, then choose a subtype only if it describes you accurately.

## Which Details Belong in the Markup?

Only use details that your customers can also see on your page and in your listing. If the website and the Business Profile disagree, the markup repeats the mismatch.

Restaurant guidance from Grubhub lists address, hours of operation, menu, cuisine type, star rating, price range and service area as details schema can identify ([Grubhub](https://get.grubhub.com/blog/restaurant-seo/), secondary). Price range is often shown with the notation $, $$, $$$.

Gather these details before writing any code:

1. Copy your exact business name, address and phone number from your Google Business Profile.
2. Write down opening hours, including any closed days.
3. List only the social or listing URLs you own.
4. Decide your price range notation.
5. Delete any field you cannot fill truthfully.

## What Does a Minimal Template Look Like?

The example below uses placeholder values for a fictional coffee shop. Replace every value with your own details.

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "LocalBusiness",
  "name": "Example Corner Coffee",
  "url": "https://www.example-cafe.com/",
  "telephone": "+1-555-010-0199",
  "priceRange": "$$",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "45 Oak Avenue",
    "addressLocality": "Springfield",
    "addressRegion": "IL",
    "postalCode": "62701",
    "addressCountry": "US"
  },
  "openingHours": "Mo-Fr 07:00-17:00, Sa-Su 08:00-15:00"
}
```

Keep the JSON valid, including quotation marks and commas. Paste the block exactly as it is into your page's script tag. The `priceRange` value should match the notation you chose, such as $$.

## Where Should the Block Go, and Will It Show?

Put the block on the page that describes the business, usually the home page or contact page. Make sure the same facts appear visibly on that page. Our guide to [above-the-fold design for local businesses](/blog/above-the-fold-design-local-business) covers how to place those details where visitors see them first.

Eligibility and display are not the same thing. Google's guidance says businesses must follow its guidelines to be eligible for Local Business rich results. It also says non-critical issues flagged by the testing tool are not required for eligibility, though fixing them can improve the quality of your structured data.

Treat valid markup as a requirement to meet, not a promise that a rich result will appear.

## How Do You Test It After Publishing?

Paste your code or live URL into Google's [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results), which Adwave describes as a free tool from Google ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-schema-markup-helping-google-understand-your-business), secondary). Fix critical errors first. Non-critical warnings can wait, as Google's guidance allows.

After the page is live, check Search Console's Enhancements report. According to Adwave, it sorts a site's pages into three buckets: Valid, Valid with warnings, and Error (secondary). Recheck after any change to hours, address, phone number or website theme, since changes can break markup without warning.

## What Should You Do This Week?

Work through these steps in order:

1. Copy your name, address, phone, hours and listing URLs from your Business Profile into a note.
2. Fill the template with those exact values and remove any field you cannot verify.
3. Add the block to your home or contact page, or send the code to whoever manages your site.
4. Run the Rich Results Test on the live page and fix critical errors.
5. Check the Search Console Enhancements report after a few weeks, then set a quarterly reminder to recheck.

If you want help finding the questions your customers ask most, Adwave suggests identifying 8-12 of the most common ones to build FAQ content (secondary). Use those answers in visible page text, then keep the wording consistent with your listing.
