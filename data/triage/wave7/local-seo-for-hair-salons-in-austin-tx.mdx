## What decides who appears in the Austin map pack?

Google pulls the top three map pack listings [directly from their GBP listings](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business), so the profile is the starting point for any map pack work. Adwave's 2025 multi-location benchmark, as the page reports it, puts [GBP signals at 32% of local pack ranking weight](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit). That is a secondary figure, so use it as direction rather than a precise weighting.

Category choice matters too. Adwave says [primary category is the top ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones), though the page does not name the analysis behind that claim.

Your real competitors are the businesses that already show up. Search the service you sell from a spot near your salon and review the [top three to five businesses in the map results](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones). Note the categories they use and the services they list. That check tells you more about Austin than any city-wide average.

## How should a salon set up its Google Business Profile?

Setting up a profile costs nothing. Google describes its [Business Profile as free to set up](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/), so the work is time, not spending.

Start with the description. Adwave's guide notes that [a GBP description allows 750 characters](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Lead with the service, the Austin neighborhood, and one detail that sets the salon apart. Write it for a person reading it, and avoid stuffing keywords.

Accuracy comes next. Adwave says to make [hours, phone number, and address 100% accurate](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Match punctuation too: the same guide warns that the address must match the listing [right down to the comma and whether you use "St." or "Street."](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)

If you work from home or travel to clients, you can [hide your physical street address](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) and be designated a Service Area Business. A storefront salon should show its address.

Reviews also appear on the profile. Google states that reviews [show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), so the review count is visible to every searcher who sees your listing.

## Which photos, posts, and Q&A keep the profile active?

Photos are the easiest signal to add. Adwave recommends [at least 15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), while a separate Adwave guide suggests [at least 10](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Vendor advice varies, so aim for the higher number and use real images from your salon: finished colour work, the reception area, and the stylists at work.

Posts need upkeep. Adwave advises [at least one new Google Post a week](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Google reviews each post after publishing, and a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its [status is Live](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en). Pending and Not approved posts stay hidden, so check the status after each one.

Q&A is worth seeding. Adwave notes that Google [launched the feature in Maps in 2017](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a). Its summary of Google's AI answers adds that responses are [sourced from business profile data, website content, and reviews](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a). Keep your profile, website, and reviews telling the same story. For more on that, see [AI for Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization).

## Do reviews and citations change map pack placement?

Reviews are the clearest lever you can measure. A third-party analysis of [2 million Google Business Profile pages](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business) found that businesses with over 200 reviews were significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. That is an association, not proof that reviews cause the ranking, but it gives a useful target. A survey cited by Ahrefs found [36% of SEOs](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo) rated the GBP as the most important map pack ranking factor in 2021. For category-level review counts, see the [Denver salon review benchmarks](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026).

Citations are the consistency check. Adwave says your NAP [must be exactly the same](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses) on your website, your GBP, and directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places. Audit those listings before building new ones, because an inconsistent listing creates the same confusion as a wrong one.

Apple's directory is worth claiming. Adwave describes Apple Business Connect as a [Google alternative you are ignoring](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), and it reports Profile Views, which count [how many people saw your business card in Apple Maps](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring).

## How do you check whether the profile is working?

Measure before you change anything. Adwave lists six useful local search metrics: [GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent). Record a baseline for each one, then compare monthly.

Rank checks can be simple. Search your main service terms in a private browser window from a location near the salon and note where you appear. Pair that with the [keyword list for hair salons](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026) so the searches you track match what clients type.

If you also run paid search, check how location data flows. Google says data for [location assets is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), and corrections made there flow into linked Google Ads accounts. The [complete guide to Google Ads for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide) covers the paid side.

## What does a monthly profile-to-booking estimate look like?

The example below uses assumed inputs, so swap in your own numbers from GBP Insights.

Assumed inputs (all assumption, not sourced):
- Monthly profile views: 400
- Share of viewers who tap call: 10%
- Share of calls that become a booking: one in three
- Average ticket: $85

Steps:
1. Calls: 400 × 0.10 = 40 calls per month.
2. Bookings: 40 ÷ 3 ≈ 13 bookings per month.
3. Revenue: 13 × $85 ≈ $1,105 per month.

The profile itself is free, so this estimate shows what a listing can return on time alone. Booking can also happen online: Booksy says [Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), which may convert some of the views that do not call. ## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does an Austin salon need?

Sourced research gives a benchmark rather than a minimum. A third-party analysis of 2 million profiles found that businesses with [over 200 Google reviews were significantly more likely to appear in the local pack](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business). Build toward that number steadily, and reply to each review.

### Do I need to hide my address if I run a salon?

Only if you work from home or serve clients at their location. Adwave says you can hide your street address and [be designated a Service Area Business](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). A storefront salon should keep its address visible and accurate.

### How often should a salon post on its Google profile?

Adwave advises [at least one new post a week](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Check that each post shows a Live status, since Google only [displays Live posts publicly on Search and Maps](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en).

### Does a Google profile cost anything to set up?

No. Google describes its [Business Profile as free to set up](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/). Your costs are time for photos, posts, and reviews, plus any paid advertising you choose to run.
