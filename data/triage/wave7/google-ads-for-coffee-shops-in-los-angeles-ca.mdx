## Why target local searches in Los Angeles?

People who type "coffee shop near me" are usually deciding where to go now. BrewRound states that 46% of all Google searches are for local businesses, but the page is dated August 29, 2020 and cites no source for the figure. Treat it as a rough indicator, not a current benchmark ([BrewRound](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/), secondary).

Google Ads reaches that moment, but only when the click leads to a profile and landing page that match what the searcher wants. For a Los Angeles shop, that means one clearly defined service area, accurate hours, and a listing that makes the location easy to find on the map.

## How much should a coffee shop budget for Google Ads?

No single budget fits every shop. The table uses one assumed average cost per click of $6.40 to show how clicks scale with spend. Replace that figure with the estimate from your own keyword research before you rely on it.

| Monthly budget | Estimated clicks (CPC assumed at $6.40) |
|---|---|
| $500 | about 78 |
| $1,000 | about 156 |
| $2,000 | about 313 |
| $5,000 | about 781 |

Worked example, with every input labelled as an assumption:

- Monthly budget: $1,000 (assumption)
- Cost per click: $6.40 (assumption)
- Click-to-visit rate: 5% (assumption)
- Average spend per visit: $10 (assumption)
- Gross margin: 40% (assumption)

$1,000 ÷ $6.40 gives 156 clicks. 156 × 5% gives 7.8 visits. 7.8 × $10 gives $78 in revenue, or about $31 in gross profit. To break even on the $1,000 at $4 gross profit per visit, the shop needs 250 visits. At a 5% click-to-visit rate, that means 5,000 clicks, which costs $32,000 at $6.40 each. Single-visit math rarely pays back on its own, so the value usually comes from repeat visits. Track how many visits a new customer makes, and use follow-up such as [coffee shop email marketing examples](/blog/coffee-shop-email-marketing-examples) to bring them back.

## What should you finish in your Google Business Profile before spending?

Google verifies a listing before it goes live. Per Diem states this usually involves a postcard with a code mailed to the business address, though some businesses can verify by phone or email ([Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). Start verification early so ads are not waiting on a postcard.

Choose "Coffee shop" or a related term rather than a broad category such as "Restaurant", as Per Diem recommends. BrewRound notes that one primary category can be selected at setup, with more added later. Answer common questions in the Q&A section before customers ask them, such as free Wi-Fi, dairy-free options, or dogs on the patio. Google also allows users to message businesses directly through the listing.

The Restaurant HQ recommends posting on social media at least once per week, ideally one to two times per day ([The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/), secondary). For profile upkeep, see [Coffee Shop Google Business Profile Optimization](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization).

## How should match types and negative keywords protect the budget?

Start with exact and phrase match on your strongest terms. Broad match on generic terms such as "coffee shop" can pull in searches you do not serve, so hold it back until you have several weeks of conversion data.

Review search term reports and add negatives for searches that rarely turn into visits. Common examples for a coffee shop include:

- Job-related terms such as "jobs", "hiring", or "career"
- Training terms such as "course" or "certification"
- Wholesale or supplier terms such as "wholesale" or "supplier"
- Nearby cities outside your service area

Radius targeting around your address helps keep spend close to the shop. Set the radius from your own visit data, not from a generic rule.

## What should you track, and how long should you test?

Set up conversion tracking before the first click. Useful signals for a coffee shop include:

- Phone calls from the ads, using a tracked number
- Booking or contact form completions on your site
- Direction requests from your Google Business Profile
- "Book now" or contact page clicks as a secondary signal

Set a fixed test window before launch. This guide uses 60 days as an assumed planning window. The right length depends on how many conversions you get each week. ## FAQ

### How much should a coffee shop in Los Angeles spend on Google Ads?

There is no sourced benchmark for this. Use the worked example in this guide: test a budget you can run for your full test window, then judge it against measured visits and repeat customers.

### Is Google Ads worth it for a small coffee shop?

It depends on tracking and margin. The worked example shows that single-visit returns can be small, so measure repeat visits before deciding.

### How long before Google Ads results appear?

Set your own test window and review conversion data across it. A 60-day window is used here as an assumption, and the right length depends on conversion volume.

### Should a Los Angeles coffee shop target the whole metro area?

Usually a tighter radius around the shop is easier to measure and control. Use your visit data to decide how far customers travel before widening the area.
