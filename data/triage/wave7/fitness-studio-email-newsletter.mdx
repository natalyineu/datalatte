To create a fitness studio newsletter, pick one goal, choose a fixed schedule, build one reusable template with a single call to action, collect addresses only from people who opted in, and measure clicks and bookings. [Sequenzy's guidance for local businesses](https://adwave.com/resources/newsletter-for-service-business), as summarised by Adwave, identifies 2-3 sends per month as an optimal cadence. Treat that as a starting point and compare it with your own results after a few issues.

## What Should a Studio Newsletter Do?

Choose one main job. Filling next week's classes needs the schedule near the top and a booking link. Retention needs member stories and instructor introductions. Referrals need a clear invitation to bring a friend. A newsletter that tries all three tends to lack a clear action, so rotate the secondary content and keep one primary link in each issue.

Keep the newsletter separate from your other email types. The [email marketing overview for fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-fitness-studio) covers welcome, win-back and promotional emails that can run alongside it.

## What Goes in Each Issue?

Use the same layout every time so writing an issue stays quick. This order is a suggestion rather than a tested formula:

1. Headline and one line on what is inside
2. This week's class highlights, with a booking link
3. One instructor or member story, with their permission
4. One short practical tip
5. A single call-to-action button

Copy-ready layouts for your email tool are covered in the [fitness studio email template guide](/blog/fitness-studio-email-marketing-template). Keep each paragraph short so the issue reads quickly on a phone.

## How Often Should You Send?

The Sequenzy guidance cited by Adwave puts the optimal newsletter cadence at 2-3 sends per month for local businesses. GetResponse data cited by Snov.io shows the first newsletter sent in a week with a 4.88% average click-through rate, and the second with 3.53%. Those figures cover newsletters in general, not fitness studios, so they show a pattern rather than a target for your list.

Sending more often did not raise the per-email click rate in that dataset, so each extra issue needs to earn its place. A fixed schedule also tells members when to expect the newsletter. For automated messages that run after sign-up, see the [fitness studio email campaigns guide](/blog/fitness-studio-email-marketing-campaigns).

## How Do You Grow the List Without Buying It?

Collect addresses at sign-up, at trial bookings and through a front-desk QR code that opens a sign-up form. Tell people what they will receive and how often. Do not buy lists. Consent rules differ by country and by state, so check the guidance from your local regulator before adding anyone to the list.

New subscribers are the easiest group to reach. Campaign Monitor's small-business guide, cited by Adwave, gives welcome emails a 91.43% open rate. Vertical Impression's retention sequence sends automated emails at Day 7 (welcome and tips), Day 30 (milestone celebration) and Day 60 (habit encouragement with a referral ask).

Mariana Tek advises following up with pop-up contacts within 24 hours through an automated welcome sequence, since interest fades quickly after a pop-up ends. Its comparison table lists automated email and SMS as low effort once set up, with an immediate payoff.

## Which Email Tool Fits a Studio Budget?

Vertical Impression's benchmark for a small independent studio with under 200 members puts email and SMS tools at $100-200 a month. GetPricePulse's 2026 estimate for a 50,000-subscriber list sending one monthly newsletter is $65 a month on Brevo, against $350-700 on Mailchimp or Klaviyo. That list is far larger than a studio's, so the gap shows how pricing scales with list size rather than what a studio should expect to pay.

SMS has a distinct job. Mariana Tek advises an SMS reminder a few hours before class to reduce no-shows, which is a booking-reminder task more than a newsletter one. Check whether your booking software already includes email and SMS before paying for a separate tool. ## How Do You Know the Newsletter Works?

Track four things: clicks on the booking link, bookings made after each send, unsubscribes per send, and visits from subscribers compared with non-subscribers. Clicks and bookings show what subscribers do, which makes them more reliable than opens.

Sequenzy's directional range for local business campaigns is 25-35% for opens and 2-5% for clicks. The source presents these as context rather than a promise, so use them only to sense whether your numbers are in a plausible range.

## FAQ

### How often should I send a fitness studio newsletter?

Sequenzy's guidance for local businesses, cited by Adwave, identifies 2-3 sends per month as an optimal cadence. Pick a schedule you can keep, then compare unsubscribes and clicks after each send.

### What should the subject line say?

Say what is inside the email. Match the subject line to the content so readers are not misled about what they will open.

### Do I need permission to email members?

Collect addresses with clear notice and an easy opt-out. Consent rules differ by country and state, so check your local regulator's guidance before sending.

### Is the open rate reliable?

Treat it as a rough signal. Clicks and bookings show what subscribers actually do, so use them for decisions about content and frequency.
