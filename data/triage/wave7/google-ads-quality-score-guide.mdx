Quality Score is a diagnostic rating from 1 to 10 that Google assigns to each keyword. It is built from expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Google states that Quality Score "is not an input in the ad auction", so you cannot raise it directly to buy cheaper clicks. Instead, use it to find which part of your ad or landing page is weak, then fix that part. Google also warns against treating the score as a key performance indicator, so judge results by leads and cost per lead.

## What Is Quality Score in Google Ads?

Google measures Quality Score on a scale of 1 to 10 and makes it available at the keyword level, so each keyword in your account has its own rating. Google describes it as a way to show how your ad quality compares with other advertisers. The rating is calculated from three components, which are covered below.

The comparison uses advertisers whose ads showed for the exact same search over the last 90 days. The score is based on historical impressions for exact searches of your keyword. Google notes that changing keyword match types therefore does not affect the score.

A keyword with few exact-match impressions may have little or no data behind its rating. Treat a missing score as a volume problem first, not a quality problem.

## Does Quality Score Change What You Pay?

Not directly. Google says Quality Score is not an input in the ad auction and is a diagnostic tool for how ads shown on certain keywords affect the user experience. Its own guidance is that the score should not be optimized or aggregated with the rest of your data as if it were a performance metric.

Some third parties make stronger claims. Home Service Direct, a vendor writing for window companies, says a Quality Score of 7+ reduces actual cost per click by 30-50% compared with a score of 4-5, and estimates overpayment of $600-$1,500 per month on a typical campaign. Those are vendor figures, not Google figures, so test them in your own account before planning around them.

Another guide makes a similar point: relevance boosts Quality Score, which can lower CPC and improve ad position. Read the link between score and cost as a likely direction, not a formula.

## What Are the Three Components?

Google's score combines three components, and each is rated Above average, Average or Below average. The rating compares you with other advertisers for the same search over the last 90 days. The table below maps each component to where you can act on it. The fixes are practical readings of the definitions, not rules Google publishes.

| Component | What it measures | Where to act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expected CTR | How likely people are to click your ad when it shows | Ad text, extensions, keyword choice |
| Ad relevance | How closely the ad matches the intent behind the search | Ad group structure, headlines |
| Landing page experience | How relevant and useful the page is to people who click | The page itself |

Work out which component is rated lowest on your highest-spend keywords. That component is where to start, because fixing a strong component rarely changes the score much.

## How Can a Local Business Improve Each Component?

Start with ad relevance. Split broad ad groups so each holds closely related searches, and put the service and the town in the headline. A coffee shop, for example, should keep "espresso near me" and "coffee shop wifi" in separate groups with matching ads. Our [coffee shop Google Ads guide](/blog/google-ads-for-coffee-shops-complete-2026-guide) walks through that split.

For expected CTR, write ads around what a nearby customer wants to know, such as opening hours, price or same-day booking. Add call and location assets as well. The [Google Ads extensions guide](/blog/google-ads-extensions-guide) explains how to set those up.

For landing page experience, send each ad group to a page that matches it. A barbershop running "fade haircut near me" should land on a fade-specific page with the address, hours and booking button, not the homepage. Our [barbershop guide](/blog/google-ads-for-barbershops-complete-2026-guide) covers page-to-ad matching in more detail.

## How Do You Check Your Quality Score?

Use this checklist once a month, or after any major change to ads or pages:

1. Open the Keywords view in Google Ads and add the Quality Score, expected CTR, ad relevance and landing page columns. Menu paths change, so confirm them on the platform's live help page.
2. Sort keywords by spend or impressions, not by score alone. A rating on a keyword with 20 impressions tells you little.
3. Flag any top-spend keyword with a Below average rating on any component.
4. Fix the lowest component on that keyword's ad group, then wait for new impressions before judging.
5. Compare cost per lead before and after, not the score on its own.

Keywords with too few exact-match impressions will not show a useful rating, so Keyword Planner is the place to find higher-volume terms in the first place.

## FAQ

### Is a 10/10 Quality Score worth chasing?

Not as a goal. Google describes the score as a diagnostic and says it should not be optimized as a key performance indicator. Use the component ratings to find weak ads and pages, and judge the account on leads and cost per lead.

### Does a low Quality Score stop my ads from showing?

Google says Quality Score is not an input in the ad auction. Ad quality still matters through the auction, so weak ads and landing pages can lose position, but the score itself is not what decides whether you show.

### Why does a keyword show no Quality Score?

The score is based on historical impressions for exact searches of the keyword. If there are too few, there is nothing to rate. Check the keyword's volume before changing its ad copy.

### Does changing match type fix my score?

No. Google says changing keyword match types does not impact Quality Score, because the score is based on exact-search history. Changing match type changes which searches you enter, not the rating.

### Where should a local business start?

Start with the lowest-rated component on the keywords that spend the most. For many local accounts that means splitting ad groups, matching ads to a dedicated page, and checking the location and call assets. Our [free audit](/free-audit) can show where your spend is going.
