Local marketing in Sweden starts with Google search. A Swedish agency page cites Consumer Barometer survey data stating that 77% of Swedish consumers use Google to find information about local businesses ([Webbyrasigtuna](https://webbyrasigtuna.se/digital-marketing-strategy-for-local-business-2/)). Those surveys date from 2014 to 2017, so treat the figure as directional. A practical plan puts a complete Google Business Profile and a capped local search campaign first, then adds social channels that support them. Set every budget against a cost-per-lead target, because published industry figures say many small businesses overspend without tracking it.

## How do Swedish consumers find local businesses?

A Swedish digital marketing page reports that 95% of consumers use the internet, 44% use mobile devices, and 77% use Google to find information about local businesses, all drawn from Consumer Barometer survey data ([Webbyrasigtuna, secondary](https://webbyrasigtuna.se/digital-marketing-strategy-for-local-business-2/)). The page is secondary and its underlying figures come from 2014/15 and 2017 surveys, so they show a pattern rather than a current measurement.

The useful takeaway is about intent. People searching for a local service are close to a purchase decision, which is why search campaigns and a complete Google Business Profile usually come before any paid social test. Check the profile's categories, opening hours and service list first, because those are the details a searcher compares.

Mobile use is high as well, so the pages you send paid and map traffic to should load quickly on a phone and show a clear way to book or call.

## Should a small business in Sweden start with Google Ads?

Search ads are a reasonable first test when a business can name the exact local searches it wants to win, such as a service plus a neighbourhood. The paid click and the map listing should show the same phone number, opening hours and booking page, so the two do not contradict each other.

A third-party claim, cited by Adwave from Power Digital, says a 1% uplift in click-through rate can drive 15% to 30% more leads for e-commerce SMBs ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/social-media-analytics-what-metrics-actually-matter)). That figure covers e-commerce, not service businesses, so use it as a reason to test ad copy rather than as a forecast for a salon or café.

Start with one campaign, a daily cap you can afford to lose, and a conversion action such as a booking or a call. Review it after a fixed period, then decide whether to widen it. ## Does Google allow every category of ad in Sweden?

No. Adwave reports that Google does not allow locksmith ads to run at all in Spain, the Netherlands, Germany, Sweden and Belgium, as of 27 October 2026 ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/locksmith-advertising)). A locksmith business in Sweden cannot rely on paid search for that service, so its budget belongs in a Google Business Profile, organic local search and its own website.

The sources available for this guide do not cover other categories. Check Google's current advertising policy pages before building a campaign for a regulated or sensitive service. Policies change, so confirm the rules before spending rather than after a campaign is rejected.

## How much should a small business budget for ads in Sweden?

Published figures suggest the problem is usually tracking rather than the size of the budget. Adwave cites American Eagle saying over 60% of SMBs overspend without tracking Customer Acquisition Cost, which leads to 20% to 30% of budget being wasted ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/how-much-should-a-small-business-spend-on-social-media-ads)). This is a third-party claim, so use it to justify measurement, not as a benchmark you must hit.

Worked example (every input below is an assumption, not a sourced rate):

Assumed monthly ad budget: SEK 10,000 (assumption).
Assumed target cost per lead: SEK 400 (assumption).
At 20% waste, SEK 2,000 is lost and SEK 8,000 remains, which buys about 20 leads at SEK 400.
At 30% waste, SEK 3,000 is lost and SEK 7,000 remains, which buys about 17 leads.
If tracking removed the waste entirely (assumption), the full SEK 10,000 would buy 25 leads.

A simple routine keeps the budget honest:

1. Write down the cost per lead you can afford before launch, for example the assumed SEK 400.
2. Set up a conversion for each booking, call or form submission.
3. Compare spend with leads every week, and pause any ad group that runs above your target.
4. Move budget only after two review periods show the same pattern.

## Which social channels should support search?

Social works best as a support to search, not a replacement. Citing Sprocket Digital, Adwave reports that 68% of SMBs use social to complement TV ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/social-media-analytics-what-metrics-actually-matter)). A separate Adwave Q1 2026 review says social media ad engagement rates saw a 10 to 15% lift when connected TV ran alongside them ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/q1-2026-tv-advertising-review-smbs)). Connected TV is rarely a realistic step for a single local shop, so treat that figure as context only.

Capacity is the constraint. Adwave cites Verizon's State of SMB report, which found 54% of SMBs struggle to produce enough content across multiple platforms, as of 2025 ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/social-media-content-audit)). Pick one or two platforms where your customers already look, and make content that can be reused. Messaging-led local marketing works differently in other markets, so for comparison see [the Brazil WhatsApp and Meta guide](/blog/local-marketing-brazil-small-business-whatsapp-meta) or [the Japan LINE and Google guide](/blog/local-marketing-japan-small-business-line-google).

## How do you measure whether local marketing works?

Measure cost per lead and cost per booked customer, not clicks or impressions. Untracked spend is the main risk identified in the sources above. Set calls from ads, booking form submissions and direction requests from the Google Business Profile as conversions, then compare them with spend each month.

Keep one simple report per channel: spend, leads, cost per lead and bookings. Local volumes can be small, so one slow month is not a verdict. For a different smaller market's approach to reporting, see [the New Zealand guide](/blog/local-marketing-new-zealand-small-business-guide).

## FAQ

### Is Google the main search channel for local businesses in Sweden?

Survey data cited by a Swedish agency suggests so: 77% of Swedish consumers use Google to find local business information. The surveys run from 2014 to 2017, so check current search data in your own account before committing a budget.

### Can a locksmith run Google Ads in Sweden?

No. Adwave reports that Google does not allow locksmith ads to run in Sweden, as of 27 October 2026. Check Google's policy pages for any other category before you build a campaign.

### Is a 1% click-through improvement worth testing?

Yes, as a test. The claim that a 1% uplift can drive 15% to 30% more leads is a third-party figure for e-commerce SMBs, so measure your own result instead of relying on it.

### How common is overspending among small businesses?

Adwave cites American Eagle saying over 60% of SMBs overspend without tracking Customer Acquisition Cost. That is a third-party claim, not a national survey of Swedish businesses.
