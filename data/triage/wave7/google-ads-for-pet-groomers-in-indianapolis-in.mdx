## What Does a Click Cost for a Dog Groomer?

AdTurbo's benchmark puts dog groomer clicks at about $2 to $8 each, and the page warns that a few wasted clicks add up fast. That spread is wide. Where your account lands depends on which keywords you bid on, how tight your location targeting is and how much competition you face in your part of the city.

No source publishes an Indianapolis figure, so the general dog groomer range is the only starting point available. Replace it with your own cost per click once you have enough clicks to compare against bookings. Until then, treat $2 and $8 as the two ends of your planning range, not as a forecast.

## How Much Do Groomers Usually Spend Each Month?

AdTurbo states that most dog groomer advertisers spend around $800 to $5,000 per month on Google Ads. That is a wide band, and the page does not explain what separates the bottom from the top. Shop size, service area and local competition are all plausible reasons, but the page does not test them.

The more useful question is capacity. Match your monthly budget to the number of grooming slots you can actually fill each week. Spend that produces bookings you cannot groom will cost money without adding revenue, so raise the budget only after your calendar shows room for the extra appointments.

## What Did a Mobile Groomer Case Study Show?

Marketing 360 published a case study on June 13, 2023, about a mobile pet groomer that advertised on Facebook and Google. Compared with the previous six months, the case reports a 134% increase in impressions and a 696% increase in clicks. It also reports a 682% increase in conversions, which is the figure a groomer should weigh most.

This is a 2023 vendor case, not a benchmark. The study does not publish spend or budget, and a mobile operation has different costs from a salon. Read the percentages as a sign of direction, not as a forecast for your account.

## Which Keywords Should You Start With?

AdTurbo lists three terms as worth bidding on: "dog groomer near me", "dog grooming near me" and "puppy grooming near me". These searches come from people looking for a nearby groomer, which is the intent a shop needs. Keep the city name in your ad copy and landing page where it reads naturally, rather than forcing it into every keyword.

Negative keywords matter as much as the terms you bid on. GroomerGenie, a vendor, says its proprietary list contains more than 500 groomer negatives, which shows how long a negative list can grow. Start with the searches that never lead to a booking, and add them to your negatives as your search terms report shows them.

## How Do You Keep Ads Close to Your Shop?

Barklead, a vendor blog, suggests targeting users within a 10 to 15 mile radius of a groomer's location. That advice is written for Facebook ads, not Google Ads, but it gives a reasonable starting range for a shop that customers drive to. Begin close to that range and cut any area that never produces a booking.

A mobile groomer works differently. Its service area follows drive time rather than a fixed shop, so draw the radius around the neighbourhoods you already visit. Whichever model you run, check where your bookings come from every two weeks and tighten targeting where clicks come from outside your service area.

## Which Conversion Should You Track?

AdTurbo names booking a grooming appointment as the primary conversion to track for dog groomer Google Ads. Set that up first. A click that never becomes a booking tells you little about whether the campaign is working, so tie each conversion to a real appointment in your booking system.

Track calls and form submissions as supporting signals if your booking system does not already record them. If you are weighing other paid channels for leads, compare them on booked grooms, not on lead volume, using the same method. The inputs below are labelled assumptions. The CPC range comes from AdTurbo's benchmark, and the $800 budget sits inside its typical spend band.

The gap between $25 and $100 per booking is the reason to measure before scaling. At the high CPC, each booking must bring in more than $100 of service revenue to cover its ad cost. Your own price list sets that number, so calculate it before you commit a budget.

## When Does Demand Peak?

Groomer to Groomer, quoted on the AdWave resource page, notes that November and December are traditionally a very busy time for groomers, sometimes too busy. That makes those months a useful checkpoint. Plan staffing and budget before they arrive, since a campaign that books more appointments than you can groom wastes money.

Set seasonal spend from your own calendar rather than a generic rule. If your diary fills early in those months, lower the budget and let the existing client base carry demand. If you have open slots, the same months may justify more spend.
