Organic social media costs no cash but takes time, and it mostly reaches people who already follow you. Paid social costs money per click or per 1,000 impressions, can reach people who do not follow you yet, and stops when the budget stops. For most local businesses the practical answer is both: keep a steady organic presence for credibility, then test a small paid budget against one clear offer. The benchmarks, a worked budget and a 30-day plan below show how to measure which channel brings in customers at a lower cost.

## How far does an organic post actually reach?

Adwave's vendor page says organic social posts now reach roughly 2-5% of a business's followers ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-advertising-ideas-beat-social-media)). Applied to a page with 2,000 followers (assumption), that works out to 40 to 100 people per post. Treat this as a vendor estimate rather than a platform rule, and check the reach figures in your own page insights.

The same page says organic growth typically requires 6-12 months of consistent effort before it generates meaningful traffic. A low reach figure in the first month tells you mostly how many existing followers saw the post. It does not tell you whether the channel can bring in new customers over time.

## What do CPM, CPC, CTR and engagement rate mean?

Paid and organic results are reported with different metrics, so check the definitions before comparing them. Turrboo's guide sets out the four terms most local advertisers will see.

| Metric | Definition ([Turrboo](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media)) | What to do with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPM | Platform billing rate, bills every 1,000 impressions | Compare the price of reach across placements |
| CPC | Pays for each click on an ad | Your main cost check for a link-based offer |
| CTR | Clicks divided by impressions | Shows whether the ad creative earns attention |
| Engagement rate | Interactions divided by reach | Measures how organic posts perform against their audience |

WARC forecasts global social media ad spend will grow 21.3% to $394.6bn in 2026, the strongest growth of any channel it tracks ([Turrboo](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media)). That is a global figure. It does not tell a salon what a click costs in its own postcode, so track your own CPC from the first week.

## How much can a small paid budget buy?

Adwave's food truck guide suggests adding $5 to $15 a day on paid social boosts or Google Ads, after free organic Instagram and Google Business Profile use ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-food-trucks-mobile-vendors)). Over 30 days (assumption) that is $150 to $450 a month. Adwave's personal-brand article adds that a modest budget of a few hundred dollars a month can boost top-performing content ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/promote-my-personal-brand)).

## Does organic social still bring in customers?

Yes, mostly as a destination that people check before booking. One playbook summarises that organic search, local listings and paid search generate 86% of local business leads, with organic search at 31%, local listings and maps at 28% and paid search at 27% ([Local SEO AI Tools Directory](https://ai-tools-for-local-seo.com/blog/local-business-lead-generation)). Social channels are not in that breakdown, so treat social as a supporting lead source for most local businesses.

A profile with recent work, current hours and a clear booking link turns a curious visit into an appointment. For the content side, see [local business social media content creation](/blog/local-business-social-media-content-creation) and [AI-driven social media content for local businesses](/blog/ai-driven-social-media-content-for-local-businesses).

Platform choice should follow your customers. Bellwether Coffee suggests TikTok is worth using if your target customers are under 35, citing significant organic reach for authentic content ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)). Check the age mix of your own customers before picking a platform.

## How do you run a 30-day split test?

Follow these steps in order, and note the output of each one.

1. Week 1, fix the destination. Update the bio, hours, photos and one booking link. Tag each link with lowercase UTM parameters so organic and paid visits can be told apart.
2. Week 2, post to learn. Publish three to four posts and note which ones earn saves, shares and messages. Scheduling can be automated: RingCentral lists social media publishing among repetitive, rule-based tasks that can be automated ([RingCentral](https://www.ringcentral.com/us/en/blog/10-marketing-automation-tools-every-smb-needs/)). Sintra's review lists scheduling tools with 7-day, 14-day and 30-day free trials ([Sintra](https://sintra.ai/blog/ai-social-media/best-social-media-ai-agents)).
3. Week 3, spend small. Choose one offer with a clear next step, such as a first appointment or trial class. Boost your best organic post or run a simple lead form at $5 to $15 a day.
4. Week 4, count customers by source. Use UTM data, booking codes and "how did you find us" answers. Calculate cost per customer for each channel. [AI-powered social media analytics](/blog/ai-powered-social-media-analytics-for-local-businesses) can help with the reporting.
5. Keep the channel with the lower cost per customer and enough volume to trust, then repeat the test for another 30 days.

## Which should a local business lean toward?

Lean organic when the budget is close to zero. Adwave's personal-brand article says you can start with a $0 budget using organic content, SEO and social engagement ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/promote-my-personal-brand)). Use that time to build the profile, then run one small paid test once the offer is clear.

Lean paid for launches, events and seasonal offers, where you need people who do not follow you yet. Salon marketing budgets vary widely: Boulevard lists $150 to $750 a month for a smaller operation and $500 to $1,500 for a larger one, covering social media, email, print and paid ads ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/salon-advertising-growing-your-beauty-business)). Your split inside that range matters more than the total. ## FAQ

### Is organic social media still worth it for local businesses?

Yes, mainly as a profile customers check and as a source of content for ads. Adwave's vendor page estimates that posts reach roughly 2-5% of followers and says organic growth typically takes 6-12 months of consistent effort.

### How much should a local business spend on paid social?

No official figure exists. The food truck guide suggests $5 to $15 a day, and the personal-brand article mentions a few hundred dollars a month for boosting top content. Start with an amount you can afford to lose for 30 days, then set the ceiling from your own cost per customer.

### How long before paid social shows results?

Adwave says a well-run social campaign can start delivering measurable results within a few weeks. Judge the test on customers rather than clicks, and give it the full 30 days.

### Which conversion rate should I use to estimate bookings?

Ruler Analytics reports paid social converting at 2.11% and organic social at 2.23% across all industries. Treat these as starting points, and replace them with your own booking log after a month of data.
