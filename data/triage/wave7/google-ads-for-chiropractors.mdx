## What Does a Chiropractor's Google Ads Budget Look Like?

AdWave describes a monthly budget of $500 to $1,500 as a common starting band for chiropractors. REVERB says Google Ads for chiropractors has no minimum spend, but a single-location practice needs enough daily budget to collect meaningful click and conversion data in its own market. The practical test is whether the budget produces a steady flow of calls and form fills you can count. A budget so small that only a handful of clicks arrive each month will not tell you much.

Chirotouch's illustrative example uses a $1,800 monthly budget, a $6.50 click price, and an assumed $1,000 value per new patient. It projects about nine new patients, or $9,000. That is a 2018 vendor example, so use it to understand the funnel, not to forecast your clinic. If you are still choosing between platforms, [Amazon Ads vs Google Ads for local businesses](/blog/amazon-ads-vs-google-ads-local-business) compares the two. A short seasonal push can justify a temporary budget change, as covered in this [holiday marketing strategy guide](/blog/holiday-marketing-strategy-local-business).

## Which Campaign Type and Keywords Should a Clinic Use?

Mike Ncube's guide recommends the Search campaign type for chiropractors. Search ads appear when someone types a query, which means the clinic reaches people who already want a chiropractor rather than people who might later. Start with intent phrases such as "chiropractor near me", "chiropractor" plus your town, and the specific treatment you offer.

Build one ad group per service, and send each ad group to a landing page about that service. Keep broad symptom searches in a separate, smaller test. Mixing them into the main campaign makes it harder to tell which keywords produce booked visits and which only produce clicks.

## What Does a Click Cost and How Many Leads Follow?

In the T3 Chiropractic case study, The Mina Company reports click-through rates that consistently held between 3% and 5%, and 100 to 150 clicks per month. Cost per click ranged from $1.36 to $2.79, and the campaign generated up to 39 appointment requests in a single month. In March, cost per click fell 24% compared with February.

Chirotouch's funnel assumes about 15 clicks produce one lead and three leads produce one new patient. That means roughly 45 clicks per patient. Multiply that ratio by your real click price and you get a first estimate of what each patient costs. Replace the ratio with your own numbers after a month of tracking.

## Should a Chiropractor Use Local Services Ads?

REVERB describes Local Services Ads as paid per lead rather than per click. The advertiser pays when a qualifying lead contacts the business. Standard ads work the other way: the advertiser pays when someone clicks, whether or not that person calls. REVERB also lists "Chiropractor" as a supported Local Services category in the United States.

Check the limits before planning around this format. REVERB notes, citing Google's help pages, that booking leads and pre-badge ads are not available for health care verticals. Local Services Ads also require a verification process. Confirm which formats appear in your account before you build a budget around them.

## What Should the Landing Page Show?

Perfect Patients cites a study stating that 91% of consumers check online reviews when looking for a local business online. The landing page should therefore show reviews or a review summary near the top, not buried at the bottom. Zarla's description of Melbourne City Chiropractic shows one approach: it front-loads four stats on the homepage, including 16+ years, 150+ five-star reviews, and 5,000+ patients.

Since 98% of chiropractors have a website, according to Perfect Patients' survey, a basic site will not set you apart. The ad's promise and the landing page headline should match. If the ad says "back pain chiropractor in [town]", the page should say the same thing and show the address, opening hours, and how to book.

AdWave's physical therapy guide says most PT patients travel 10 to 15 minutes to their clinic. For a chiropractor, that suggests a starting radius around a 10 to 15 minute drive. This is an assumption for chiropractic, not a sourced figure for the profession.

## How Should a Clinic Track Results?

Track three things: calls and form submissions, booked first visits, and cost per new patient. The worked example above turns those counts into a cost per patient. Count bookings through your scheduling system or front desk log, not only the ad platform's conversion number, because the platform cannot see whether the call became a visit.

Patients who return also matter. AdWave's dental guide recommends sending a targeted email series to patients who have not visited in over a year. That is a reactivation step outside the ads, but it affects the value of each patient you already paid to acquire.

## FAQ

### How much should a chiropractor spend on Google Ads?
AdWave describes $500 to $1,500 a month as a common starting band. REVERB says there is no minimum spend, but a single-location practice needs enough daily budget to gather meaningful data. Start within that band and adjust once cost per new patient is clear.

### Are Local Services Ads available to chiropractors?
REVERB lists "Chiropractor" as a supported Local Services category in the United States. It also notes that booking leads and pre-badge ads are not available for health care verticals, so check which formats appear in your account.

### Should a chiropractor manage Google Ads in-house or hire help?
That depends on time and the size of the budget. This [comparison of AI tools, humans and marketing for local businesses](/blog/ai-vs-human-marketing-local-business-honest-comparison) sets out the trade-offs. Whichever route you choose, someone should own cost per new patient.

### How do I know if the ads are working?
Count calls, form submissions and booked first visits. Divide the monthly budget by new patients to get cost per new patient, then compare that with the value of a patient to your practice.
