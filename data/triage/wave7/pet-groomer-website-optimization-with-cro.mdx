Start with mobile speed, a single main button, visible proof and tracking that counts calls and booking clicks, not only visits. Website add-ons for grooming businesses are listed at $79 to $99 a month, so the work pays off only if a few extra bookings arrive each month.

## What Should a Pet Groomer Website Do Before Anything Else?

Visitors arrive from Google Maps, a profile link, social media or a search ad. Most want three answers quickly: what you groom, where you are, and how to book. Mobile grooming is growing, too. SchedulingKit data, reported by Scrap.io, puts mobile grooming growth at 12-15% a year ([Scrap.io](https://scrap.io/pet-groomer-email-list), secondary). Faster growth means more groomers competing for the same local searches.

A site that answers those three questions on the first screen gives a visitor a reason to stay. A site that hides them behind menus gives a reason to leave. Fixing the page costs less than buying more clicks, and it improves every visit, including visits from people who found you through your profile.

## Is Your Site Fast Enough on a Phone?

Speed decides whether a phone visitor waits or leaves. Test the page on a mobile connection and find the largest element, which on most groomer sites is a hero photo. Google publishes current speed guidance in its web performance documentation, so check the live page before setting a target rather than copying a number from a forum.

Compress the hero image and size it to the screen. Avoid autoplay video or large sliders above the fold. Remove plugins and widgets you no longer use, since each one adds weight. If your builder is slow, compare hosting options in the [website hosting guide for small businesses](/blog/website-hosting-for-small-business) before paying for more features.

## Does the Top of the Page Show Services, Area and One Action?

Three things belong above the fold: the services (dog grooming, cat grooming, mobile or salon), the area, and one main button. Use a headline that names the service and the town. Show opening hours and the address as plain text, not as an image.

Keep one action. Several competing buttons (book, shop, subscribe) split attention. If you groom at the client's home, say so on the first screen, because that changes whether a visitor can use you at all. The name, address and phone number on the site should match your Google Business Profile exactly. For profile tuning, read the guide to [AI for Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization).

## Can a Customer Call With One Tap?

Make the phone number a tel: link so a tap starts the call. Many owners have a dog on a lead or in a crate and will not type a number from a web page. Place the number in the header of every page, not only on a contact page.

Test it on your own phone. Tap the number, confirm the dialer opens with the right number, and try it on both Wi-Fi and mobile data. If you run search ads, the same tap-to-call approach suits the landing page; the guide on [setting up Google Ads for a small business](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-ads-for-your-small-business-in-2026-step-by-step-with-screenshots) covers the ad side.

## Should You Publish Prices and Booking Rules?

Grooming prices vary by breed, size and coat. Publish a "from" price for each size band and list what changes the price, such as matting or a double coat. A visitor who sees a starting price is more likely to call than one who sees nothing at all.

Add the rules a new client needs: vaccination requirements, how long a typical groom takes, and your cancellation terms. Each unanswered question is a reason to call a competitor instead. Date the page when you update prices, so visitors know it is current.

## Worked Example: Does a $79 Website Pay for Itself?

All inputs are assumptions for one mobile groomer:

- Monthly site visits: 400
- Share of visits that tap call, book or submit a form: 4%, giving 16 enquiries
- Enquiries that become a booked groom: 50%, giving 8 bookings
- Average groom price: $70

Suppose a better page lifts the enquiry share from 4% to 5%. That gives 20 enquiries and 10 bookings, which is 2 extra bookings. Two extra grooms at $70 bring in $140 a month. After the $79 add-on, the net gain is $61. At $99, the net gain is $41.

This assumes the page change, not chance, caused the lift. Test one change at a time and compare several weeks of data before deciding.

## Does Google Understand the Page?

Structured data gives search engines a clear description of your business. Check Google's LocalBusiness structured data documentation for the required and recommended fields, and use the most specific business type that fits your work. Add only details that are true and that appear on the page.

Also check that mobile pages show the same booking details as desktop pages. A price list or service area that appears only on desktop is easy to miss on the version most visitors use.

## Are You Tracking Bookings, Not Just Visits?

Record calls, form submissions and booking clicks as events, and mark the ones that matter as key events in Google Analytics. Without this, a visit count tells you little about which change worked. Compare calls from your website with calls from your Google Business Profile, because they reach you through different routes.

Keep a simple log of each change, its date and the result over the next few weeks. The testing method in the [A/B testing guide for small business emails](/blog/ab-testing-emails-small-business-guide) applies here too: change one thing, wait, and read the result before the next change.

## FAQ

### What is a good conversion rate for a pet groomer website?
Use your own history. Count calls and bookings from the website for several weeks, then compare each change against that baseline.

### What should I fix first?
Mobile speed and the call button. Both are inexpensive to check and affect every visit.

### Do I need online booking?
Not always. A tap-to-call button and a short enquiry form work for many small shops. Online booking helps when calls come in while you are busy grooming.

### Should I show prices?
Yes, as a "from" price for each size band, plus what changes the price.

### Can I use stock photos?
Use photos of your own salon and your own work. Stock "before and after" images undermine the trust you are trying to build.
