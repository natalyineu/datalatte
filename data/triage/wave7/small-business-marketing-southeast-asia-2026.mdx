## Which Platforms Lead in Thailand?

LINE holds a unique position in Thailand with over 47 million users, according to CloudTech. Facebook remains the dominant social platform there, with over 51 million active users, and TikTok has over 24 million users, especially among Gen Z and Millennial consumers. The same guide puts Thailand at over 54 million internet users, a large online audience for any local offer.

These totals describe the whole country, not your city. A salon in Bangkok and a cafe in Chiang Mai draw on very different catchments, so use the national figures only to decide which platforms to try first. Then check the audience size each ad tool shows for your own city or postcode before setting a budget.

For LINE tactics in another Asian market, see [the Japan LINE and Google guide](/blog/local-marketing-japan-small-business-line-google).

## Which Channel Fits Each Job?

Each platform tends to do a different job for a local business. The table pairs the Thailand figure from the source with the role the channel usually plays, and the check to run before spending.

| Channel | Thailand figure | Usual job for a local business | Check before spending |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LINE | Over 47M users | Bookings, offers and repeat messages | LINE's own business plan pricing |
| Facebook | Over 51M active users | Local discovery and event posts | Cost per enquiry in your ad account |
| TikTok | Over 24M users | Short video reach among Gen Z and Millennials | Whether your product suits video |
| YouTube | Shorter videos (2-5 min) show higher completion | Demonstrations and how-to clips | Length of your existing videos |

The "usual job" column is a practical assumption about how each channel is used, not a sourced result. None of the user counts shows which channel converts best for your business. Treat the table as a starting checklist, then let your own enquiry records decide.

## When Should a Thai Business Post?

CloudTech reports that peak Facebook engagement in Thailand occurs between 7-9 PM and 12-1 PM. Put the main offer in the evening window first, and use the lunchtime slot for a shorter update. Compare results across both slots over several weeks before choosing one.

For video, the same guide says shorter YouTube videos of 2-5 minutes have higher completion rates in Thailand. Cut demonstrations to that length and state the key message at the start, so viewers who stop early still see it.

Slideshow ads suit cases where data use matters. According to [AdEspresso](https://adespresso.com/guides/facebook-ads-beginner/facebook-ads-types/) (secondary), Facebook slideshow ads use five times less data than video ads, which is why they are intended for markets with limited internet connections. Check whether a slideshow can carry your offer before you pay for video production.

## What Does a Test Budget Look Like?

The example below uses assumed inputs throughout. Assume a single-location cafe in Bangkok with a test budget of US$300 a month (assumption). Split it as US$150 for Facebook ads, US$100 for short video and US$50 for content and design (all assumption).

Assume the Facebook ads bring 15 enquiries in the month (assumption). That gives a cost of US$10 per enquiry, since US$150 divided by 15 is US$10. Assume the video brings 5 enquiries (assumption), which is US$20 each, since US$100 divided by 5 is US$20. Facebook is cheaper per enquiry in this example, so the next month's video money moves to Facebook.

Run the test for four weeks before deciding. If a channel's cost per enquiry is higher than the margin on a first order, change the offer before adding budget. The [marketing dashboard guide](/blog/how-to-create-marketing-dashboard-small-business) shows how to log enquiries by source, so the decision rests on real numbers rather than assumed ones.

## Does Tourism Change the Channel Choice?

If your customers are visitors rather than residents, the origin market matters. [Shanghai Jungle](https://www.shanghaijungle.com/blog/tourism-marketing-china-guide) (secondary) reports that Southeast Asia accounts for 37% of Chinese outbound overnight visitors, followed by Northeast Asia at 29% and Europe at 21%.

An older figure points the same way. [Hicom Asia](https://www.hicom-asia.com/marketing-to-chinese-tourists-in-2023-where-are-they-going/) (secondary, citing a Fitch Ratings analysis) says Chinese tourists contributed roughly a third of all international tourists to Vietnam, Thailand and Korea in 2019. That is a 2019 figure, so treat it as background rather than a current forecast.

This guide does not cover the Chinese platforms those visitors use. A tourist-facing business should research them separately before buying media aimed at that audience.
