Ahrefs can help a local business with keyword research, competitor research and rank tracking. Its local SEO guide says its Rank Tracker can follow rankings by country, state, city and ZIP or postal code. The sources cited here do not show it replacing a local-specific tool for map-grid or citation work. Before paying for any keyword platform, try the free tools first, check the current pricing page, and work out what one new customer is worth to your business. The worked example below lets you test that with your own figures.

## Can Ahrefs track local rankings by city or ZIP code?

Ahrefs' [local SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo) states that its Rank Tracker lets you track up to 10,000 keyword rankings for regular organic search by country, state, city, and even ZIP or postal code. For a local business, location is the setting that matters. A plumber in one suburb and a plumber in the next can rank differently for the same phrase, so a tool that checks by place is useful.

Tracking is only one step. You still need to pick the keywords, the places and the pages that should rank. The guide does not say how many keywords a small business needs, so start with the handful of service and town phrases your customers actually type. The [local keyword research guide](/blog/local-keyword-research-guide) explains how to build that list before you add any tracker.

## Which free tools can you use before paying for Ahrefs?

Google's own tools are the cheapest start. Google describes Keyword Planner as a [free tool integrated within the Google Ads platform](https://business.google.com/en-all/ad-tools/keyword-planner/). You reach it by clicking the Tools icon, selecting Planning from the section menu, then choosing Keyword Planner. Google Trends is also a free tool from Google, useful for checking whether interest in a service rises at certain times of year.

Wildheart's [keyword research guide for yoga retreats](https://wildheartmedia.com/keyword-research-yoga-retreats/) makes the same point: Google Search, Google Trends and Google Keyword Planner can show what people search for without paid SEO software. Ubersuggest is another option, described in the [Globalgenie roundup](https://www.globalgenie.com.au/blog/best-local-keyword-research-tools-for-local-seo) as a free SEO tool for finding new keywords and understanding SEO difficulty. The [local SEO keyword research tools guide](/blog/local-seo-keyword-research-tools) compares more options.

Free tools give rough numbers rather than exact ones. Treat them as a ranking of options, not a forecast.

## How do the free plans of paid keyword tools compare?

Free plans of paid tools are usually capped. Zapier's [roundup of free keyword research tools](https://zapier.com/blog/best-keyword-research-tool/) lists Semrush's free plan at 10 Analytics reports per day and 10 tracked keywords. It lists KWFinder at 5 searches per day and AnswerThePublic at 3 searches per day. That roundup is dated June 2, 2021, so confirm current limits on each vendor's pricing page before you plan around them.

The same roundup says its author considered more than 90 tools with a free option for keyword research and spent over a dozen hours testing the shortlist. No single free plan covers a full local keyword program, so expect to combine a few.

## What do local search volumes look like in practice?

Volumes for local phrases vary a lot. A [batlinks guide to catering keywords](https://batlinks.io/catering-keywords/) reports that "catering for 50 people" has an average of 600 searches per month and a keyword difficulty of 36, based on its Ahrefs check. The author calls Ahrefs a well-known SEO platform. That is one secondary check, so treat it as a snapshot rather than a benchmark for your city.

A [paid-ads guide for moving companies](https://www.ce-paid-ads.com/google-ads-for-moving-companies) cites Ahrefs data showing "moving company" at 1,700 monthly Google searches in the UK and "moving company near me" at 900. Those are UK figures for national-style phrases. They show the scale of a phrase, not what a suburb in the US, Canada or Australia sees. For town-level work, check the numbers against your own Search Console queries.

## Where does Ahrefs fall short for local SEO?

Ahrefs is a general SEO platform, and its local SEO guide focuses on location-based rank tracking. Nothing in the Ahrefs material cited here establishes grid-based Maps tracking, Google Business Profile management or citation building. If you need those, check the current feature list before you buy.

Some local businesses use a general platform alongside a local-focused tool. A Sydney local SEO services page says [local SEO agencies use SEMrush tools](https://www.efficientmedia.com.au/top-local-seo-sydney-services/) to conduct keyword research, analyze competitors and track performance metrics. That describes how agencies work, which is not the same as what one shop needs. The [AI-driven local SEO tools guide](/blog/ai-driven-local-seo-tools) covers options for local-focused work.

If your goal is calls and bookings rather than rankings, a paid lead route can be a different decision entirely. The [Google Local Service Ads guide](/blog/google-local-service-ads-guide) covers that path.

## What would a paid keyword tool need to earn back?

Every number in this example except the Ahrefs rank tracking limit is an assumption. Replace them with your own figures. Assume a tool costs $100 a month (assumption), and a new customer brings $200 of gross profit (assumption).

| Option | Tool cost (assumption) | Gross profit per new customer (assumption) | New customers to break even |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free tools only | $0 | $200 | 0 |
| One three-month paid sprint | $300 | $200 | 1.5, so 2 in practice |
| Twelve months of paid access | $1,200 | $200 | 6 |

A three-month sprint needs two extra customers to cover its cost. A full year needs six. The free tools cost nothing, so the real question is whether paid data leads to customers you would not otherwise win.

For a low-value business, the sums change. If a customer is worth $20 (assumption), a $1,200 year needs 60 customers to break even. In that case, free tools are the sensible start.

## What should you do this week?

1. Write down the gross profit from one typical new customer. Use that figure in the table above.
2. Find your core service and town phrases in Google Keyword Planner, reached from the Tools icon, then Planning, then Keyword Planner.
3. Check Google Trends for seasonal patterns in your top three phrases.
4. Compare those phrases with the queries in Google Search Console and note which ones already bring impressions.
5. If you still want paid rank tracking, check the current pricing page, set a short trial, and write down the three actions you will take from the data before you start.

## FAQ

### Does Ahrefs track rankings by ZIP code?

Ahrefs says its Rank Tracker can track rankings by country, state, city and ZIP or postal code, for up to 10,000 keyword rankings. Check the current plan limits before you sign up.

### Is Google Keyword Planner free?

Google describes Keyword Planner as a free tool integrated within the Google Ads platform. You can use it to research keywords without buying a separate SEO platform.

### Are the free plan limits still current?

Zapier's roundup is dated June 2, 2021, so its free-plan limits may have changed. Confirm each limit on the vendor's pricing page.

### Is a paid tool worth it for one small shop?

It is worth it only if the data leads to customers you would not otherwise win. Run the break-even table with your own gross profit per customer before you pay.
