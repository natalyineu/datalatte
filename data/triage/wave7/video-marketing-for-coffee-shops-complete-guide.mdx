A coffee shop should film short vertical videos of drinks being made, the space and regulars, then post them where its customers already watch. Keep each clip between 15 and 30 seconds, hook the viewer in the first 3 seconds, and post at a steady pace of three to five times a week, following [Mariana Tek's guidance for studios](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/). That advice comes from a fitness-studio vendor, so treat it as a starting point.

This guide covers which platforms fit, what to film, what a paid boost costs, and a worked break-even example where every input is marked as an assumption.

## Which Platforms Should a Coffee Shop Use?

Start with the platform your regulars already use. Salesforce's local marketing guide notes that [TikTok has gained immense popularity among Gen Z users, particularly for its short-form video content](https://www.salesforce.com/marketing/local-marketing/?bc=OTH). For a café near a college or office, TikTok and Instagram Reels are the first places to test.

Taste matters as well. Attest research, cited by AdWave, found that 67% of Gen Z prefers short-form comedy and memes as content. A light, funny clip often suits the audience better than a polished ad.

Adults spend real time on this format. StackAdapt reports that more than 75% of US adults spend up to 2 hours a day watching short-form digital video, and it cites a HubSpot figure that 75% of viewers prefer it on mobile devices. A phone is the right camera. Export vertical video at 9:16 and 1080x1920, the size AdWave recommends for Shorts, Reels, TikTok and Stories together.

For how video fits with blog and email, see [content marketing for coffee shops](/blog/content-marketing-for-coffee-shops-blog-social-email).

## What Should a Coffee Shop Film?

Film what a customer can see in the room. AdWave's coffee shop guide suggests latte art, a morning vibe and customer photos, paired with a "first drink on us" offer for a paid test.

A simple request works for customer clips. AdWave's example asks a customer to record a 15-second clip about what stood out most. Ask regulars the same way, and get their permission before you post.

- A drink being made from start to finish
- The seasonal item, with the price on screen
- The morning opening, or the quiet minutes before doors open
- A staff member making one signature drink
- A customer comment, shared with permission

AdWave suggests a 30 to 60 second clip follows a hook, explanation, local angle and call to action. A 15-second cut is tighter: AdWave gives its first 0 to 2 seconds to a pattern interrupt and a promise, so the viewer has a reason to keep watching. AdWave's 2026 trends piece also argues that small businesses should test about five versions of a concept rather than polish one. For a café, that could mean five openings of the same drink.

## How Often Should You Post, and How Long Should Clips Be?

Mariana Tek advises posting at a steady cadence, three to five times a week, rather than in bursts. It also recommends highlight clips of 15 to 30 seconds. Its table lists a typical payoff timeline of 3 to 6 months for short-form video, so judge the channel over a quarter rather than a week.

AdWave gives a measurable goal as an example: three short-form videos a week to lift Instagram engagement by 25% over three months. That figure is an example from one planning guide, not a result to promise.

Plan the month in one sitting. AdWave says a planning sprint of about 30 minutes is enough to decide the quarter's goal, weekly topics, channel mix and paid amplification. ## Does Short-Form Video Outperform Static Posts?

Several figures point the same way, though they come from surveys and vendors rather than coffee shops. AdWave cites Sprout Social's 2024 benchmark that short-form video gets 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Meta platforms. AdWave's electrician guide cites a similar 2.5 times figure from Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report.

HubSpot's 2026 State of Marketing report, as summarised by AdWave, found 49% of marketers rank short-form video as their top-performing format. AdWave's 2026 statistics page also says 85% of marketers consider short-form the most effective social format.

Engagement is not the same as visits. Measure visits, as the worked example below does.

## What Does a Paid Boost Cost?

AdWave's coffee shop guide recommends planning $100 to $1,000 per month for creator partnerships and content tooling. Its week-2 plan suggests Instagram Reels ads at $30 to $50 per day, targeting a 1 to 3 mile radius around the shop.

The radius controls who sees the ad, so match it to how far your regulars actually travel. ## Is It Worth It? A Worked Example

Every input below is an **assumption** for one café. None is a benchmark.

| Input | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Videos per month (3 per week) | 12 (assumption) |
| Hours per video (film, caption, post) | 1.5 (assumption) |
| Total hours | 18 |
| Value of staff time | $20 per hour (assumption) |
| Monthly cost of time | $360 |
| Margin per extra visit | $4 (assumption) |
| Extra visits to break even | 90 ($360 / $4) |
| Extra visits per day over 30 days | 3 |

On these assumptions, organic video pays if it brings three extra visits a day. That is plausible on a busy street and harder on a quiet one.

If you cut editing to 45 minutes per video, the time cost falls to 9 hours and $180, so break-even drops to 45 visits, or 1.5 a day.

A paid Reels test at $30 per day, the low end of AdWave's range and an assumption for this café, costs $900 a month. At $4 margin, that needs 225 extra visits, or 7.5 a day. Test organic posts first.

## How Do You Measure Whether Video Pays?

Use simple counts. Track views and saves in each platform's insights, and ask customers at the till where they heard about you. A reel-only discount code turns views into a number you can count.

Compare weeks with video against weeks without, using the same staffing. Set one goal each quarter so the test has a clear end. For a full method, see [how to track marketing ROI for a local business](/blog/how-to-track-marketing-roi-local-business-guide).

Before you upload, check Google's current Business Profile help page for video length and file requirements, since those rules change.

## FAQ

### How often should a coffee shop post video?

Mariana Tek advises a steady cadence of three to five times a week rather than bursts. If time is tight, start at the low end and keep it consistent.

### How long should a coffee shop video be?

Mariana Tek recommends highlight clips of 15 to 30 seconds. AdWave's 30 to 60 second structure of hook, explanation, local angle and call to action suits a longer explainer.

### Do I need professional equipment?

A phone, vertical framing at 9:16 and natural light cover most café clips. PosterMyWall offers basic motion and video templates if you want quick edits without much editing work.

### Should I pay to boost videos?

Test organic posts first. AdWave's plan suggests $30 to $50 per day for Reels ads, and the worked example shows how many extra visits that spend needs to cover.
