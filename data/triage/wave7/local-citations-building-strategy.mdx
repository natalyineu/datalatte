A local citation is an online mention of your business name, address and phone number (NAP), usually in a directory, map service or listing site. For most small businesses, citation work follows a short order: make the core platforms accurate first, add a limited set of secondary directories, and keep every detail identical. Accuracy matters more than volume. One guide places the core tier at the first 5 to 10 platforms and the secondary tier at roughly 20 to 30 directories, while describing those tiers as informed guidance rather than an official standard.

This guide covers the order of work, what the sources say about rankings and AI search, and a time budget. Every time figure is labelled as an assumption.

## Which Directories Should You Build First?

Work from the most important platforms outward. Adwave recommends starting with Google, Apple Maps and Facebook, cleaning those up before moving to major secondary sites such as Yelp, then smaller niche directories. Google Business Profile comes first in that order, and Google's own documentation, as quoted by SocialPilot, says a complete profile makes customers 70% more likely to visit the business.

Apple Maps is the default map service for hundreds of millions of iPhone users, according to Adwave. That is a vendor claim, so treat it as a reason to check Apple Maps, not as a measured share. The table below is a practical build order for a small business.

| Step | Listing | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Google Business Profile | Complete every field and match the master sheet |
| 2 | Apple Maps | Claim or create the listing, then match the details exactly |
| 3 | Facebook page | Match name, address and phone |
| 4 | Major secondary site, such as Yelp | Claim and correct the existing listing |
| 5 | Trade or niche directories | Add only the ones your customers use |
| 6 | Data aggregators | Check what they pass on to downstream sites |

Aggregators deserve a check of their own. Firestarter SEO names Data Axle, Localeze and Foursquare as three major aggregators that distribute business information to hundreds of downstream directories, so one wrong detail at that level can repeat across many listings.

## Does Accuracy Matter More Than the Number of Citations?

Yes, according to the sources here. SocialPilot says what matters is not just how many citations you have, but how accurate they are and where they appear. Adding citations over inconsistent NAP data multiplies the mess. Adwave makes the same point more briefly: when it comes to citations, quality beats quantity every time.

Adwave also reports its own data showing that businesses with NAP consistency across 50+ quality directories often see a 25-35% lift in local pack visibility over time. That is a vendor figure with no published method, so use it as a direction rather than a forecast for your own results.

## How Many Citations Does a Small Business Need?

No official standard sets a number. SocialPilot describes a core tier of the first 5 to 10 platforms and a secondary tier of roughly 20 to 30 directories, and says its tiers are informed guidance synthesized from across the field. Adwave's 50+ figure is a benchmark from its own data, not a rule.

The practical answer is to stop at the point where every listing you have can be kept accurate. A business that cannot keep 40 listings correct is in a worse position than one with 15 correct listings. Build the core tier first, then add only the secondary directories your customers actually use.

## Do Citations Still Matter for Rankings and AI Search?

Adwave says local citations have become a top-5 local SEO ranking factor in 2026. That is vendor opinion. Firestarter SEO cites BrightLocal's annual Local Search Ranking Factors survey as consistently identifying citation signals as one of the top factors influencing local pack rankings. The local pack is the top three map results shown for searches such as "dental clinic near me," according to Adwave. Neither source gives a numeric weight for a single listing.

For AI answers, the Cheers Market Baseline counted 356,019 citations across 49,677 local-intent runs in 100 metros and four engines over 28 days ending September 2, 2026. That summary is aggregate only, so it does not show which directories were cited. Read it as evidence that citations appear in AI answers, not as a directory checklist.

## How Do You Keep Name, Address and Phone Consistent?

Pick one exact version of your name, address and phone number and copy it everywhere. Write a master sheet with the name, address, phone, website, hours and categories. When a number or address changes, update the sheet first, then each listing, so no copy drifts behind the others.

Keep the name exactly as it appears in real life. Avoid adding keywords or taglines to the business name, because that is the most common way a listing starts to differ from the rest. ## How Often Should You Audit and Fix Listings?

Adwave recommends a mini-audit of your most important listings every three months. Search your phone number and any old addresses to see what comes back. Claim listings where the directory allows it, correct the details, and log the date in your master sheet.

Duplicates on the same directory should be merged or removed through that directory's own support process, since the steps differ by site. ## FAQ

### What is a local citation?

A local citation is an online mention of your business name, address and phone number, usually in a directory, map service or listing site. Sources define it as NAP mentions, and a link is not required.

### How many citations do I need?

No official standard sets a number. SocialPilot describes a core tier of the first 5 to 10 platforms and a secondary tier of roughly 20 to 30 directories, as informed guidance rather than a rule.

### Do citations help with AI search?

Adwave calls citations a top-5 ranking factor in 2026, which is vendor opinion. The Cheers baseline counted citations across AI engines in aggregate, but it does not show how much a single listing contributes.

### Should I pay for mass submission to hundreds of directories?

No source here shows that volume beats a handful of accurate listings. Adwave and SocialPilot both favour quality over quantity, so inconsistent details spread further if you submit them everywhere.

### Which listings should I fix first?

Start with Google Business Profile, Apple Maps and Facebook, then major secondary sites such as Yelp, then smaller niche directories, following Adwave's order. Check each listing against your master sheet before moving on.
