## How much should a fitness studio spend on Meta ads?

Adwave's fitness and wellness guidance recommends a [45 to 55 percent Meta budget split](https://adwave.com/resources/connected-tv-vs-social-media-ads-comparison), with 30 to 40 percent going to connected TV and the balance to local platforms. That split applies to the total advertising budget, not to revenue. For the total, [Mariana Tek notes that many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/).

These are planning ranges, not guarantees. A studio with steady bookings can test a smaller Meta share, while a new location may need more time before results settle.

Worked example (all inputs are assumptions): a studio with $30,000 monthly revenue and a 5 to 10 percent marketing budget has $1,500 to $3,000 to spend. At 45 to 55 percent for Meta, the Meta share is $675 to $1,650. Assume a $2 cost per click, inside Adwave's $1 to $3 range, and a 5 percent click-to-trial rate.

| Monthly Meta budget (assumption) | Clicks at $2 CPC (assumption) | Trial bookings at 5% (assumption) |
|---|---|---|
| $300 | 150 | about 8 |
| $675 | 338 | 17 |
| $1,000 | 500 | 25 |
| $1,650 | 825 | 41 |

Booking rates depend heavily on the offer and the landing page, so replace the 5 percent with your own figure once you have data.

## Which Meta ad formats work best for fitness studios?

Short-form video is the format to test first. Adwave, citing Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, says [short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners) on Facebook and Instagram. That figure comes from a vendor retelling of Meta's report, so check Meta's own creative guidance before you commit a large budget.

Video also supports organic posting. Mariana Tek advises posting [three to five short-form videos per week](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) at a steady pace rather than in bursts. The same clips can rotate through ads, which keeps creative fresh. For format ideas, see the [Instagram Reels guide for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-instagram-reels).

Stories placements suit short, time-bound offers such as a trial week. The [Stories ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/instagram-stories-ads-fitness-studios) covers placement details.

## How should a Lexington studio target local clients?

Set the radius and age band from your own member data rather than a generic template. Start with a radius around your studio and a test group based on the age and location of current members. Test one audience per campaign so the results show which group responds.

Meta Ads Manager is the central place to run ads across Instagram and Facebook, as [Adobe's social media advertising guide](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising) explains. Keep each campaign's audience separate so you can compare cost per click and bookings directly.

Custom audiences built from past members are a useful starting point once you have that data. For a broader view of setup, read the [complete guide to Facebook ads for gyms and fitness studios](/blog/facebook-ads-for-gyms-fitness-studios-complete-guide).

## How do you turn ad clicks into new members?

A click is not a member. Mariana Tek advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, [because interest fades fast after a pop-up ends](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/). The same logic applies to Meta leads, so the first message should go out the same day a lead arrives.

Send each ad to one booking page that carries the trial offer. Instagram and Facebook also let you add a [Book Now button to your profile](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/), so a booking action sits on the page people reach from your ads. Build the follow-up flow with the [marketing automation guide for local fitness studios](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-fitness-studios).

## What should you check before a Meta campaign goes live?

Work through this checklist before spending:

1. Confirm in Meta Ads Manager whether the campaign runs on Facebook, Instagram, or both.
2. Record your baseline cost per click from past campaigns, or start with the $1 to $3 vendor range.
3. Set one offer per ad, such as a free trial class, and keep the commitment low for cold traffic.
4. Film at least three short videos to rotate, then plan a pace of three to five per week.
5. Build the booking page and Book Now button before the ad launches.
6. Schedule follow-up messages to fire within 24 hours of each lead.
7. Check that bookings, not clicks, are the conversion event you track.

## What mistakes slow down Meta campaigns for studios?

The most common mistake is judging a campaign too early. [Adwave notes that Meta advertising takes weeks to optimize](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-small-business-2026), so a campaign reviewed after a few days may show noise rather than a trend. Give each test a fixed window and a fixed budget before changing it.

A second mistake is sending paid traffic to a generic homepage. An ad with a specific offer and a clear booking step gives you a result you can measure. A third is tracking impressions and reach, which show exposure but not members. Count trial bookings and paid sign-ups instead.

## FAQ

### How much should a fitness studio spend on Meta ads per month?

Adwave's channel table lists a standalone Meta buy at often $300 to $1,000 a month. Many boutique studios budget 5 to 10 percent of monthly revenue for all marketing, so a Meta share of that total is the practical limit. Start at the low end, measure bookings, and increase spend only when cost per booking holds steady.

### Should a studio run ads on Facebook, Instagram, or both?

Both platforms run through Meta Ads Manager, and one campaign can run on either or both. Test Instagram-only and Facebook-only versions first if you want clear comparisons, then combine the placements that produce the lowest cost per booking.

### Is video really better than static images for gym ads?

Adwave, citing Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images. Engagement is not the same as bookings, so test video against a static version with the same offer and compare trial sign-ups.
