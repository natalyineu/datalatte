## Which searches should a St. Louis hair salon bid on?

Intent comes first. A salon guide lists "spa near me", "hair salon near me" and "nail salon in [city]" as examples of the terms people type into Google ([Trafft](https://trafft.com/salon-ads/), secondary). The pattern is a service plus a location, which is why location modifiers matter in every keyword group.

Build the list from your actual service menu. Start with exact-match terms for services you can deliver this month, then add phrase-match variants that pair each service with a neighborhood or city. Neighborhood names such as Cherokee Street or The Loop can work as modifiers where they fall inside your service area.

Check live search volume and cost per click in Google Ads Keyword Planner for your own ZIP codes. National estimates cannot tell you what a St. Louis click will cost. The [best Google Ad keywords for hair salons in 2026](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026) guide covers how to shortlist terms once you have that data.

## How much should a St. Louis hair salon spend on Google Ads?

No St. Louis spend benchmark is published in the sources used for this guide, so the budget should come from your own numbers. Salon Guru, which says it has run hair salon campaigns in the UK, Ireland and the USA, charges a one-time set-up cost per campaign covering its first four steps, then monthly management and analysis for three months ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/), secondary). It does not publish a fixed price and offers a bespoke quotation on request.

Ask any agency for the set-up fee and monthly management fee in writing, then compare both with the ad spend you plan to run. Spend bands published for salons in other countries use different currencies and market conditions, so they do not carry over to St. Louis. Set the budget from booking value and target cost per booking instead.

## What does a $1,000 monthly budget return? (worked example)

All inputs below are assumptions to replace with your own data. Monthly budget: $1,000. Average cost per click: $4.00. Share of clicks that become a booking: 6%. Average booking value: $80.

Clicks come first: $1,000 divided by $4.00 gives 250 clicks. At 6%, that is 15 bookings. Cost per booking is $1,000 divided by 15, or $66.67. Booking revenue is 15 multiplied by $80, or $1,200. Break-even on revenue alone needs 12.5 bookings, so 13 bookings per month is the threshold.

| Booking rate (assumption) | Bookings per month | Cost per booking | Booking revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3% | 7.5 | $133.33 | $600 |
| 6% | 15 | $66.67 | $1,200 |
| 9% | 22.5 | $44.44 | $1,800 |

Booking value should count repeat visits, not just the first appointment. Booksy data says engaged loyalty members visit salons 2.4x more often ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), secondary). Revenue is not profit, so subtract product and staff costs before judging the result. The 3% row returns less than the $1,000 spend, which is a signal to fix the landing page before raising the budget.

## Does the Google Business Profile affect Google Ads results?

It does, because the profile sits next to the ad in local results. The RŌZ comparison table lists a Google Business Profile and Google reviews as free to set up, and it cites Google as saying local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence ([RŌZ](https://rozhair.com/blogs/education/hair-salon-advertising-ideas?srsltid=AYCX9vIdNXXuxGV-rKdglolA2QOQJbEGs8irFigzpePpcaBIjnjcFKs6), secondary).

Booking friction matters as much as ranking. Booksy says Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds once it is connected ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), secondary). Booksy's 30-day plan starts with claiming the Google Business Profile, syncing it with your booking system, and updating the service menu in days 1 to 7. Fixing those basics before the ad launches keeps paid clicks from landing on an outdated listing. ## Why do ad clicks fail to become bookings?

Usually the landing page. One agency's guide says that if a salon gets 100 visitors and no bookings, there is probably a problem with the landing page ([Sunnystorm](https://sunnystorm.marketing/blog/google-adwords-salons), secondary). Salon Guru's campaign process also starts with optimising the landing page before any ads run.

Check that the page names the service, shows a price or price range, and puts a booking button above the fold on phones. Confirm that the phone number and booking link work on mobile. Track whether visitors reach the booking step, not just whether they land. ## What offers and targeting should a salon test first?

Offers that name a specific service convert more clearly than generic discounts. One salon guide gives "$20 off first color service" as a new-client offer to promote monthly ([Zeely](https://zeely.ai/blog/hair-salon-advertising-ideas/), secondary). Another lists "First Haircut 20% Off" as an example promotional offer ([CIIM](https://www.ciim.in/google-ads-for-hair-salons/), secondary). Test one offer at a time so you can attribute bookings.

For radius, CIIM recommends targeting roughly 5 to 10 km around the salon, which is about 3 to 6 miles ([CIIM](https://www.ciim.in/google-ads-for-hair-salons/), secondary). Treat that as a starting point and narrow it if your booking data shows that distant clicks rarely convert.

Add negative keywords for job seekers and DIY searchers. Terms such as "jobs", "hiring", "salary", "course" and "training" attract clicks that rarely turn into appointments. Review the search terms report weekly during the first month.

## How long before Google Ads results show?

Set a review date and judge the campaign against break-even, not against someone else's results. Salon Guru says an SEO-optimised salon site usually takes a few weeks to reach the top of organic results for multiple phrases ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/), secondary). That timeline applies to organic search, not paid ads, so do not expect the same pace from a campaign.

Case studies from one market do not predict another. ARC notes its NYC salon figures come from one salon in one market and that results will differ for other salons ([ARC](https://arccreativeco.com/what-a-salon-booking-costs-on-google-ads/), secondary). ## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon in St. Louis spend on Google Ads?

No sourced St. Louis figure is available here. Use your own cost per click and booking value. The worked example uses a $1,000 budget, a $4.00 assumed click cost and a $80 assumed booking value, which needs about 13 bookings a month to cover its revenue. Ask agencies for a written quote before committing.

### Is Google Ads worth it for a small hair salon?

It is worth testing when cost per booking stays below the value of a booking. Run a small test with a clear offer and a working booking page, then compare results against break-even. Treat agency return claims as untested until you see your own booking data.

### How long does it take to see results from Google Ads?

Results depend on your budget, landing page and market. Set a review date and track cost per booking each month. A single salon's results from another city will not predict yours.

### What is the most common reason clicks do not turn into bookings?

The landing page is the usual cause. If visitors reach the site but do not book, check that the service, price range and booking button are clear on a phone, and that the booking link works.
