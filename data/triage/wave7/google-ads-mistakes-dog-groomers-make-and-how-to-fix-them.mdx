## Are You Paying for Searches That Cannot Book?

Without negative keywords, your budget goes to people looking for a job, a course, a product or a free tip. Dental Rank, which writes about Google Ads for dental practices, lists "free," "cheap," "jobs" and "DIY" as common negative keywords. The same words fit dog grooming, and you should add "school", "training", "supplies" and "diy" alongside them.

Add these before launch, then check the search terms report in Google Ads once a week for the first two months. Any term that brought a click but could never become an appointment goes on the negative list. ## Is Your Search Radius Wider Than Your Drive Time?

A groomer serves a drive-time area, not a whole county. Barklead's guide to Facebook ads for groomers suggests targeting users within a 10-15 mile radius of the groomer's location. Dental Rank gives a 5-10 km radius as its typical starting point for dental practices. Both are starting points, not rules.

Pick the radius that matches how far clients will actually drive, then check which towns your clicks and bookings come from. Remove towns where you never book. Mobile groomers should draw the service area around their real van routes and name the main towns in the ad text.

## Are You Counting Clicks Instead of Bookings?

A click tells you very little. A booking, a call or a form submission tells you that an ad is working. If you only watch clicks, you can spend a month paying for traffic that never books.

Set up conversion tracking for calls, the booking form and any online scheduler link. Then ask the front desk to note how each new client found you. Once conversions record, you can compare cost per booking across keywords, which is the number that decides where the budget goes.

## Do Ads Run When Nobody Can Answer the Phone?

If your ads send callers to voicemail, you pay for clicks that never turn into appointments. Run call-focused ads only during the hours someone can answer. Keep the booking-link ads running outside those hours, because many pet owners look for a slot in the evening.

Check the schedule against your real opening hours each month. A groomer who closes at 5 pm on Saturday should not be paying for calls at 7 pm.

## Where Do Your Clicks Land?

A home page makes the visitor search for what they came for. Send each ad group to a page that matches it. A "dog grooming near me" ad should land on a page that shows services, starting prices, your area, opening hours and a booking button. Put the phone number near the top on mobile.

Build one page for each main service, such as full groom, puppy first groom, de-shedding and nail trim. Only build pages for services you actually offer. The [Google Ads guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-ads-for-pet-groomers-guide) covers how the pages and ad groups fit together.

## Is Your Business Profile Undercutting Your Ads?

Paid clicks are only part of the decision. BrightLocal's 2026 survey finds that 85% of consumers say positive reviews make them more likely to use a business, while 77% say negative reviews make them less likely to choose one. A groomer with few recent reviews and missing photos loses the client after the click.

Ask for a review after each appointment, reply to every review, and keep photos, hours and services current. Check the live Business Profile help pages for the current steps, as the interface changes.

## How Much Should You Plan to Spend, and How Often Should You Check?

Many groomers set a monthly budget by feel. Start instead from what one new client is worth to you, then work out how many bookings the budget needs to produce. Dental Rank recommends setting aside time each month to analyse ad performance, test new ad creatives, adjust targeting and refine bidding. The same routine works for groomers.

A mobile pet groomer in a 2023 Marketing 360 case study reported a 134% increase in impressions and a 696% increase in clicks compared with the previous six months, running on Facebook and Google. Those results reflect one business and one period, so use them as a sign of what testing can do, not as a forecast.

## FAQ

### What is the biggest Google Ads mistake for dog groomers?
Paying for irrelevant searches. Without negative keywords, searches for jobs, courses and supplies use the same budget as searches for grooming.

### Which words should dog groomers add as negative keywords?
Dental Rank lists "free," "cheap," "jobs" and "DIY" as common negatives for dental practices. Dog groomers can add those plus terms such as "school," "training" and "supplies," then check the search terms report for more.

### How do I know if my radius is too wide?
Check where your bookings come from, not just where your clicks come from. Remove towns that send clicks but no appointments. Barklead suggests a 10-15 mile starting radius for groomers.

### Should I track calls or only bookings?
Track both. Calls and booking-form submissions are the two most direct signs that an ad produces a client, so set up both as conversions.

### How often should I review a Google Ads campaign for a grooming business?
Review the search terms report weekly during the first two months. After that, Dental Rank's monthly routine of analysing performance, testing creatives and adjusting targeting is a sensible cadence.
