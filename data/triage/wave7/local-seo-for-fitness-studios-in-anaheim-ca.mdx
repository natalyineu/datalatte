## What does the Map Pack show a searcher?

The Association of Fitness Studios describes the Map Pack as the section of Google's results that lists relevant local businesses for queries such as "personal training studios near me" ([Association of Fitness Studios](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google), secondary). The same article describes the Business Profile as a free tool that puts business hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos straight into search results.

Google's own help material confirms that reviews appear next to your profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)).

This means many searchers decide from the listing alone. Accurate hours, current photos and recent posts answer their questions before they click through to your site. Check your listing the way a customer would: search from your neighbourhood in a private window and note what appears, then compare it with the studios above and below you.

## How much weight do Google Business Profile signals carry?

A BrightLocal survey, cited by Ahrefs, found that 36% of SEOs in 2021 considered the Google Business Profile the most important ranking factor for the map pack, up from 23% in 2013 ([Ahrefs, Local SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), secondary). That is an opinion survey of SEO practitioners, not a Google ranking rule. Read it as a sign of where specialists focus their effort, not as a formula.

The practical takeaway is that profile accuracy and completeness are the parts you control directly. Competitor density, distance from the searcher and other factors also affect results, and the sources used for this guide do not give a full weighting. Confirm current local ranking factors against Google's live documentation before you change strategy.

## Which Google Business Profile fields should a studio fill in first?

Work through these in order. Each step is a field you control.

1. Confirm the primary category. Pick the category that matches your main service, then add secondary categories for each class type you run, such as yoga, Pilates or personal training.
2. Write a factual description. Lead with the service, the Anaheim location and your main differentiator. Avoid repeating keywords. The [AI for Google Business Profile guide](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization) can help with drafting, but check every claim against your real offer.
3. Upload authentic photos. Vertical Impression recommends at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes in session and staff ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), secondary).
4. Publish posts that will actually show. Google's help page says a post is public on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and not-approved posts do not appear ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after each post.
5. Seed the Q&A section with questions prospects really ask, such as whether a trial class is available, and answer them plainly.
6. Confirm hours, holiday closures and the service area. The [fitness studio profile optimisation guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization) walks through each field in more detail.
7. Embed your map on the contact page. The steps are covered in the [Google Maps embed guide](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

## How should a studio ask for reviews?

Ask after a member has had a result to talk about, such as a first class, a completed trial or a milestone, rather than at sign-up. Google's review tips page confirms that reviews display next to your profile in Maps and Search, so every review is visible to people comparing studios ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)).

Respond to every review, good or bad. Responses are public, so keep them factual and name the service in plain language. Do not stuff the city name into every reply.

Be cautious with review targets. A fixed review count or star rating is a local goal, not a sourced threshold. Set it by checking the listings of studios that already rank for your main searches, and check Google's review policy before running any incentive scheme.

## How do citations and NAP details affect the listing?

Citations are mentions of your business name, address and phone number on other websites. Consistency matters more than volume. "St." and "Street", or "(555) 123-4567" and "555-123-4567", can look like two different businesses to a directory. Pick one format and use it everywhere.

Start by auditing the directories where your studio already appears, including Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps and Facebook, then correct mismatches before you add new listings. Include class-booking platforms you use, because their details feed into other directories.

Paid search draws on the same data. Location assets in Google Ads are sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, so corrections made there flow into linked accounts. You can also select up to 10 locations from Google Maps when profile-based linking does not work, and those locations must belong to you ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). If you plan to run search ads alongside your listing, the [Google Ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/google-ads-for-fitness-studios-complete-2026-guide) covers setup.

## What does a budget look like for a studio in Anaheim?

The figures below are illustrative assumptions, not sourced benchmarks. Replace them with your own CRM and Insights data before making decisions.

Assumptions:
- 120 calls, direction requests and website visits from your profile each month (assumption)
- 15% of those contacts book a trial (assumption)
- 40% of trials join as paying members (assumption)
- $149 monthly membership (assumption)
- 4 staff hours a month on the profile, at $30 an hour (assumption)

Calculation:
- 120 × 15% = 18 trials
- 18 × 40% = 7.2 new members on average
- 7.2 × $149 = $1,072.80 in monthly recurring revenue
- 4 × $30 = $120 in staff time
- $1,072.80 ÷ $120 ≈ 8.9 times the labour cost, before software, photography or ads

The ratio shows why the profile is worth maintaining even on a small budget. The result depends entirely on your conversion rates, so test them. ## How long until the listing changes?

The sources used here do not set a timeline for ranking changes, so avoid promising one to yourself or clients. Instead, set a checkpoint. Track GBP Insights monthly for searches, views and actions such as calls and direction requests. Check your position for your top five searches weekly in a private window, and compare each month with the one before. Changes that persist across several checkpoints are the ones to act on.

## FAQ

### Does a Google Business Profile cost anything?

Google states that its Business Profile is free to set up and shows your business on Google Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)).

### Do all of my posts show publicly?

No. Only posts with the status Live appear publicly on Search and Maps. Pending and not-approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).

### Can I link more than one location to Google Ads?

Yes. You can select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link with a Google Ads account, and the locations must belong to your business ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)).
