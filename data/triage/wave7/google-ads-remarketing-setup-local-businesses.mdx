## What is remarketing, and is it worth it for a small shop?

Remarketing shows ads to people who already visited your website. It suits businesses where visitors look at prices or a booking page and leave without acting, such as salons, groomers and studios. Those customers often take longer to decide, so a second ad can keep you in mind.

It is a weaker fit if your traffic is thin. A small audience gives noisy results, and you learn little from a handful of visitors. Build visits first through search and your Google Business Profile, and read about [how AI Overviews are changing local search](/blog/google-ai-overviews-local-business-seo) before you rely on organic traffic.

If you are still choosing between channels, compare [Google Ads and Facebook Ads for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins) before you spend.

## How many website visitors do you need before you start?

Defined Digital recommends not launching a display remarketing campaign until the website audience exceeds 1,000 visitors ([secondary](https://www.definedigitalacademy.com/blog/google-ads-for-local-service-based-businesses-5-step-strategy-for-2025)). Below that size, each segment holds too few people to show you what works. The source does not state the time window, so check your analytics for the period you prefer.

Measurement comes first. If your site does not record bookings, calls or form submissions, set that up before building any audience. The [GA4 setup guide for local businesses](/blog/google-analytics-4-setup-local-business) walks through the counting side.

Waiting is not a loss. A remarketing campaign with a small audience usually costs the same per click as a large one but reaches fewer people, so the test tells you less.

## How do you set up remarketing step by step?

Follow these six steps in order.

1. Install the Google tag on every page of your site. Confirm it fires on your own pages before you build any audience. Many site builders include a Google Ads integration that does this for you.
2. Create audiences by page. Make one for your pricing or booking pages and one for the whole site, so you can see which visitors are closest to buying.
3. Add the audience to a remarketing campaign. Keep it separate from your main Search campaign so you can read the results on their own.
4. Set the audience to "Target" rather than "Observation", as Defined Digital advises ([secondary](https://www.definedigitalacademy.com/blog/google-ads-for-local-service-based-businesses-5-step-strategy-for-2025)).
5. Add a conversion action for bookings, calls or form submissions, so you can judge the campaign by results.
6. Note a launch date and a review date. Leave the campaign running for the full test window before changing it.

If you are tempted to add Performance Max alongside, a Spain-focused guide says it works well only when you already have analytics history and high-quality conversion data ([secondary](https://adwservice.com.ua/en/google-contextual-advertising-in-spain)). Check the platform's live help page for current setup screens, since menu names change.

## How much of your budget should go to remarketing?

Two published rules give different answers. Defined Digital suggests 5 to 10% of your total Search budget for remarketing ([secondary](https://www.definedigitalacademy.com/blog/google-ads-for-local-service-based-businesses-5-step-strategy-for-2025)). For a shop spending $1,000 a month on Search, that means $50 to $100.

An Irish framework works from total monthly budget instead. It allocates 20% to remarketing at the starting stage, 20% when scaling and 15% when mature, with the rest moving to prospecting and video as the account grows ([secondary](https://orbis.agency/ie/blog/paid-media-google-ads-ireland-guide/)). Pick the rule that matches your stage, and treat either as a starting point rather than a target.

## What does a remarketing test cost, and when does it break even?

Google publishes no standard cost, so test with your own numbers. The table below uses a hypothetical hair salon. Every input marked assumption is an example to replace with your figures.

| Item | Figure | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search budget | $1,000 a month | Assumption |
| Remarketing share | $75 (7.5%) | Assumption, within the 5-10% range |
| Cost per click | $1.50 | Assumption, within the $0.50-$3.00 range |
| Clicks | 50 | Calculated ($75 divided by $1.50) |
| Booking rate | 4%, so 2 bookings | Assumption |
| Average ticket | $85 | Assumption |
| Gross margin | 60%, so $51 per booking | Assumption |
| Margin from 2 bookings | $102 | Calculated |
| Result after $75 spend | +$27 | Calculated |

The campaign breaks even at about 1.5 bookings, so you need 2. At a 2% booking rate you get 1 booking, which means $51 of margin against $75 of spend, a loss of about $24. The cost-per-click range comes from a window company's guide, which also says remarketing clicks can reach visitors for 30 to 90 days ([secondary](https://www.homeservicedirect.net/google-ads-ppc-guide-window-replacement/)).

## How long should a campaign run, and how often should you review it?

Defined Digital suggests letting a remarketing campaign run for 60 to 90 days before you measure performance ([secondary](https://www.definedigitalacademy.com/blog/google-ads-for-local-service-based-businesses-5-step-strategy-for-2025)). Expect the early weeks to be for gathering data and fine-tuning, as the Spain-focused guide describes ([secondary](https://adwservice.com.ua/en/google-contextual-advertising-in-spain)). Judge the campaign on bookings, not clicks.

Once it is running, review your audience segments. AdWave's guidance is that checking every quarter is a solid practice for most businesses ([secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/what-is-audience-segmentation)). Use that check to remove pages that no longer match what you sell and to adjust budgets.

## Do privacy rules change how you set up remarketing?

Yes, they can. Seoplus notes that regions can have unique advertising rules, such as data privacy laws, that affect campaign setup and how data is handled ([secondary](https://seoplus.com/paid-ads/the-ultimate-guide-to-multi-location-ppc-advertising-on-google-ads/)). Check the rules that apply to the places your visitors come from.

If visitors from the EU are in your audience, GDPR can apply. AdWave notes that a lead form, remarketing audience, CRM entry or follow-up campaign can all fall in scope when they involve data from EU residents ([secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/can-spam-and-gdpr-compliance-what-small-business-owners-must-know)). Update your privacy notice to describe how visitor data feeds your audiences.

## FAQ

### How many visitors do I need before remarketing?

Defined Digital recommends waiting until the website audience exceeds 1,000 visitors before launching a display remarketing campaign ([secondary](https://www.definedigitalacademy.com/blog/google-ads-for-local-service-based-businesses-5-step-strategy-for-2025)). Start with measurement, then build traffic.

### How long should a remarketing test run?

Let it run for 60 to 90 days before you measure performance, per the same guidance ([secondary](https://www.definedigitalacademy.com/blog/google-ads-for-local-service-based-businesses-5-step-strategy-for-2025)). Use bookings or calls as the measure, not clicks.

### Should I use Target or Observation for remarketing audiences?

Defined Digital advises using "Target" for audience targeting rather than "Observation" ([secondary](https://www.definedigitalacademy.com/blog/google-ads-for-local-service-based-businesses-5-step-strategy-for-2025)). Check the current options in your account, since labels can change.

### Is remarketing expensive for a small business?

It depends on your costs per click and booking rate. A window company's guide puts remarketing clicks at $0.50 to $3.00 each ([secondary](https://www.homeservicedirect.net/google-ads-ppc-guide-window-replacement/)). Run the break-even table above with your own figures before you commit.
