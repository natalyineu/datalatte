Spotify audio ads let a gym reach local listeners with a 15-second or 30-second slot that plays between songs and podcast episodes on the free tier. Spotify's ads page confirms the 15 or 30 second options, and a secondary source reports a typical audio CPM of $15 to $25. Spotify does not publish gym sign-up results, so a gym needs its own small test to learn whether listeners become members. This guide covers formats, cost, targeting, scripts, a worked budget and how to measure the outcome.

## Which Spotify Ad Formats Can a Gym Buy?

Spotify's ads page says advertisers can choose a 15-second or 30-second ad slot, and that audio ads cannot be closed or swiped past by listeners. A second source, Your Digital Resource, says users can skip audio ads after a few seconds. The two statements conflict, so check Spotify's current help pages before writing a brief that depends on listeners hearing the full message.

Other formats exist. The same article lists banner ads shown in the app and interactive video spots. Tinuiti states that audio is not supported in the Spotify Homepage Takeover format on desktop, though video can be included, and that sponsored session video ads are generally more expensive than audio ads.

Power Digital Marketing says Spotify plays a non-skippable audio ad approximately every fifteen minutes. That claim also conflicts with the skip statement above. Spotify describes Ad Studio as its self-serve advertising platform for building audio or video campaigns, which is where a first test would be set up.

## What Does a Spotify Audio Ad Cost?

ClicksGeek, a secondary source, puts typical Spotify audio CPM at $15 to $25, which it says is higher than visual platforms because audio gets forced attention. CPM means cost per 1,000 ad plays. Power Digital Marketing gives Pandora's average audio CPM as $8 to $12, which is a useful comparison if a gym is weighing both services.

These are third-party figures, not Spotify's own price list. Soda Spoon Marketing says sponsored playlists cost more than simple audio ads but gives no dollar figures. Spotify does not publish a cost per listener or per sign-up on the pages reviewed, so the quote in Ad Studio is the number to trust.

Using the midpoint of the CPM range, $250 buys about 12,500 plays. The worked example below uses that calculation.

## Who Can You Target With Spotify Ads?

Soda Spoon Marketing says advertisers can target Spotify audio ads by age, gender and location. That lets a gym set an age range close to its typical member and a local area around its address. The source does not describe radius or postal-code options, so check what Ad Studio offers in your market before planning the audience.

Tinuiti reports that Spotify says audio-only campaigns tend to drive more foot traffic at a more efficient cost per visit than multi-format campaigns. For a studio with a physical front desk, that points toward testing audio alone before adding video. It is still a platform claim, so the result has to be measured in your own data.

For more on audience choices, read the [Spotify targeting guide for fitness studios](/blog/spotify-ads-for-fitness-studios-target-active-listeners-in-your-city).

## When Should a Gym Run the Test?

Adwave, a vendor, reports that January, March to April and September together drive 60 to 70% of the year's new sign-ups for most gyms. Sprites, which tracks gym ads in Meta's Ad Library, says the category crowds into the same six weeks of January auction.

A test in January meets more prospects but also more competing advertisers. A test in the quieter months gives a cleaner read on cost, though it may reach fewer people who are ready to join. Neither source measures Spotify pricing by month, so run the test long enough to collect a usable number of visitors.

## How Do You Write a 30-Second Script?

StackAdapt's interview notes that a typical Spotify ad is 30 seconds long, and it advises focusing on key messaging and value points. Since Spotify offers both slot lengths, write one 30-second version and one 15-second version from the same core message.

Keep one offer, one location and one action. Open with the listener's situation, state the offer and any limits, then give one web address that is easy to remember. Do not promise results such as weight loss. Read the script aloud with a timer, and cut anything that runs past the slot length.

## A Worked Budget Example

The inputs below are assumptions unless marked otherwise. The CPM range comes from ClicksGeek, a secondary source, and the membership fee and lifetime figure come from Adwave's vendor example.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test budget | $250 | assumption |
| CPM used | $20 | midpoint of $15 to $25 (ClicksGeek, secondary) |
| Ad plays bought | 12,500 | $250 ÷ $20 × 1,000 |
| Click rate to landing page | 0.5% | assumption |
| Landing page visitors | 62 | 12,500 × 0.5% |
| Visitors who book a free trial | 10% (about 6) | assumption |
| Trial-takers who join | 30% (about 2) | low end of 30-60% range (Adwave, vendor claim) |
| Monthly fee | $80 | vendor example (Adwave) |
| Monthly revenue from new members | $160 | 2 × $80 |
| Cost per new member | $125 | $250 ÷ 2 |
| Vendor lifetime value | $3,800 | 2 × $1,900 (Adwave example, 24-month tenure) |

Adwave says most gym owners cap their cost per acquisition at $40. At $125 per member, this test is above that cap. Over a 24-month tenure, the vendor's lifetime figure would cover the cost, but that is revenue, not profit, and it rests on the vendor's own example. Change any assumption and the result moves, so replace the guesses with real counts after the test.

## How Do You Measure Whether It Worked?

Send listeners to one dedicated landing page or a tracked offer code, so each sign-up can be traced to the audio test. Ask each new member how they heard about the gym during the first month, as a check on the page data. Confirm which metrics your Ad Studio account shows, since the sources reviewed do not list them.

If the test collects names at an event, Mariana Tek advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, since interest fades fast after a pop-up ends. Listeners who search for the gym afterwards will find it through Google Business Profile, a free tool that shows hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos in search results, according to the Association of Fitness Studios. Keeping that profile current supports the test, and the [local SEO strategies guide](/blog/ai-driven-local-seo-strategies-for-fitness-studios) covers the details.

Once the audio test has numbers, compare the same sign-up path with other channels such as [Facebook ads for gyms](/blog/facebook-ads-for-gyms-fitness-studios-complete-guide) or [CTV ads](/blog/ctv-ads-for-fitness-studios). Adwave reports intro-class signups typically lift 25 to 45% during well-targeted CTV campaigns, a vendor figure to test against your own results.
