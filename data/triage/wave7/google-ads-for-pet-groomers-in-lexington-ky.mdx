## What Does a Click Cost for Dog Groomers?

The benchmark most relevant to dog groomers comes from AdTurbo, which states that dog groomer clicks run about $2–$8 and that a few wasted clicks add up fast ([AdTurbo](https://adturbo.ai/google-ads-for-dog-groomers)). That range is a vendor's estimate, not a measured average across all groomers.

The same page says most dog groomer advertisers spend around $800–$5,000 a month on Google Ads ([AdTurbo](https://adturbo.ai/google-ads-for-dog-groomers)). Treat that as a range for planning, not a target. A new shop in a smaller market may reasonably start at the bottom of it.

## Is There Any Lexington-Specific Data?

No source reviewed for this article publishes cost per click, conversion rates or search volume for Lexington pet groomers. Any city-level figure you see online without a named method should be treated with caution.

Your own account is the only reliable local data. Check the Keyword Planner and the auction insights in Google Ads after a few weeks, and compare the figures against the national ranges above.

## Which Keywords Should a Lexington Groomer Bid On?

AdTurbo lists "dog groomer near me", "dog grooming near me" and "puppy grooming near me" as keywords worth bidding on ([AdTurbo](https://adturbo.ai/google-ads-for-dog-groomers)). These match people ready to book, which is why they are a sensible starting set.

Groomer-specific negative keyword lists exist. GroomerGenie says its proprietary list holds more than 500 groomer negatives ([GroomerGenie](https://www.groomergenie.com/services/google-ads)). That is a vendor claim, so review your own Search terms report and add exclusions for searches like job or school queries that do not lead to bookings.

## How Wide Should Your Targeting Radius Be?

Barklead's guide to Facebook ads for groomers suggests targeting users within a 10–15 mile radius of the groomer's location ([Barklead](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers)). That advice is written for Facebook, not Google, so treat it as a starting test rather than a rule for Search.

A mobile or home-visit groomer may need a wider area than a fixed salon. Narrow the radius if you see clicks from places you do not serve, and widen it only if bookings from the edge zones justify the cost. Check the platform's live help page for current location targeting options.

## What Does $800 a Month Buy?

The worked example below uses assumption-labelled inputs. The $800 budget is the low end of the typical range cited above. The 5% booking rate is an assumption, not a benchmark, and you should replace it with your own figure.

| Input or result | Low CPC end of range | High CPC end of range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $800 | $800 |
| Cost per click (benchmark) | $2 | $8 |
| Clicks ($800 ÷ CPC) | 400 | 100 |
| Booking rate (assumption) | 5% | 5% |
| Bookings | 20 | 5 |

The spread is wide, which is the point. A groom booked at $40 in ad cost and a groom booked at $160 can both be profitable, depending on what a client spends over repeat visits. Work out your own average ticket and return rate before deciding on a budget.

Seasonality also matters. Groomer to Groomer notes that November and December are traditionally a very busy time for groomers, sometimes too busy ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/pet-grooming-advertising), secondary). Consider whether the slower months need extra spend or whether your shop is already full.

## Which Conversion Should You Track?

AdTurbo names booking a grooming appointment as the primary conversion to track for dog groomer Google Ads ([AdTurbo](https://adturbo.ai/google-ads-for-dog-groomers)). Track that first, then add secondary actions such as calls and form submissions. Tracking only clicks tells you very little about whether the campaign pays.

Once conversions flow in, a simple report that compares spend, bookings and cost per booking each month will show what is working. Our guide on [AI-driven local SEO reports with Google data](/blog/ai-driven-local-seo-reports-with-google-data-studio) covers how to build that kind of dashboard.

## Should Google Ads Be Your Only Channel?

Google Ads is not the only option. One Marketing 360 case study describes a mobile pet groomer that advertised on Facebook and Google, and reports a 134% increase in impressions and a 696% increase in clicks compared with the previous six months, as of 2023 ([Marketing 360](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/)). That is one business, so it does not predict your results.

For a shop choosing between paid channels, the [Amazon Ads vs Google Ads comparison for local businesses](/blog/amazon-ads-vs-google-ads-local-business) explains how the two differ for local service work. Google's own local listings are a separate question, which is covered in [Google Local Services Ads for pet groomers](/blog/google-local-services-ads-for-pet-groomers).

## FAQ

### How much should a pet groomer in Lexington spend on Google Ads?

There is no Lexington-specific figure. Vendors report that most dog groomer advertisers spend $800–$5,000 a month, so start near the low end and scale only when cost per booking is acceptable.

### What is the average cost per click for dog grooming?

AdTurbo puts dog groomer clicks at about $2–$8 ([AdTurbo](https://adturbo.ai/google-ads-for-dog-groomers)). Your actual cost depends on competition, keywords and location, so check your own account data.
