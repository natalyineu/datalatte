The 5 C's of a marketing plan are Company, Competitors, Customers, Collaborators and Climate. Together they form a situation analysis: you examine your own business, the rivals, the buyers, the partners and the wider environment before you decide what to promote and where. This guide applies the five headings to a coffee shop. Sourced figures are cited inline. The worked examples use inputs labelled as assumptions, so you can replace them with your own numbers.

## How Does a Coffee Shop Analyse Its Company?

Start with facts a customer can check in seconds: opening hours, price range, specialities, review score and review count. Write them down, then list your constraints, including staff hours, equipment and how many hours the owner can spare each week for marketing.

Your Google Business Profile belongs in this picture because it is often the first thing a customer sees. Google verifies a listing before it goes live, usually by mailing a postcard with a code to the business address. The [Per Diem guide to Google Business Profiles for coffee shops](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide) states that without verification, updates will not show publicly and customers may not find you.

Per Diem also recommends the "Coffee shop" category or a related term, rather than a broad category such as "Restaurant". Set one goal with a number and a date, such as new customers per month. If you later test paid search, the [guide to setting up Google Ads for a small business](/blog/how-do-i-set-up-google-ads-for-my-small-business-complete-guide-for-2026) covers the account basics.

## Who Are the Competitors, and Where Is the Gap?

Start with the businesses that appear beside yours when someone searches for your main product nearby. Note each one's category, hours, review count and score, and what its listing offers. Their revenue is rarely visible, so focus on gaps you can observe: a service they lack, a time they are closed, or a complaint that recurs in their reviews.

Competitor pressure also shows up in keyword choice. The vendor seoplus states that a localised phrase such as "coffee shop downtown Phoenix" is likely to have less competition than a generic term like "coffee shop", which can lead to a lower cost per click ([seoplus](https://seoplus.com/paid-ads/the-ultimate-guide-to-multi-location-ppc-advertising-on-google-ads/)). Treat that as a vendor claim, and check the bid estimates in your own account before relying on it.

For a market outside North America, compare how local rivals bid and advertise with the [Germany local marketing guide](/blog/local-marketing-germany-small-business-google-ads). Write a single sentence for each rival that names the gap you will target.

## What Do Customers Want, and Where Do They Look?

Customers look online first. The Restaurant HQ article on coffee shop marketing cites Statista data stating that 98% of consumers use the internet to find new businesses ([The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/), secondary). The same article cites a Drive Research survey finding that three out of four Americans drink at least one coffee a day, which gives a broad baseline for demand.

Preferred channels vary by country. Square's Canadian page reports that 65% of Canadians like businesses to communicate with them by email, 39% appreciate text message updates, and seven in 10 Canadians use smartphones to access the internet ([Square](https://squareup.com/ca/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/marketing-plan-for-a-coffee-shop)). Those figures support a simple follow-up plan: one email and one text option at the till.

Location shapes the offer. AdWave recommends a 1-3 mile geo-targeting radius around a shop for Reels promotions ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes)). Its multi-channel example runs fall latte ads to everyone within a five-mile radius ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-channel-marketing-approach)). Choose a radius based on where your customers actually come from, which your survey answers will show.

## Which Collaborators and Climate Factors Matter?

Collaborators are outside partners: a bakery that supplies pastries, a neighbouring business, a delivery app, or an ad platform. For each, write down what it costs and what it gives you. Loyalty schemes are a common example. AdWave says a coffee shop loyalty platform should be planned at $50-$300 per month, plus $200-$1,000 in incentive payouts such as free drinks or credit ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes)). Its 2026 comparison lists in-shop loyalty programs at $50-$1,300 monthly for coffee shops ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes)).

If a partner controls your reach, such as an automated ad platform, record how much control you keep. The [Performance Max guide for local businesses](/blog/google-performance-max-for-local-business-complete-guide-2026) covers that trade-off in detail.

Climate is the wider environment: local rent, rules that affect hours or signage, changing buying habits and new technology. Competitive density is part of the climate. Square's page cites IBISWorld for over 8,000 coffee shops across Canada as of 2025 ([Square](https://squareup.com/ca/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/marketing-plan-for-a-coffee-shop)). In a market that crowded, a clear point of difference matters more than general awareness.

## How Much Daily Time Should a Coffee Shop Put Into Each Channel?

Time is a real constraint in the Company analysis. The Bellwether Coffee guide lists the daily time each channel requires for a coffee shop: Google Business about 5 minutes, Facebook about 15 minutes, Instagram about 30 minutes and TikTok about 1 hour ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)).

The worked example below uses one assumption: the owner can spare 4 hours a week for marketing, and the shop opens six days a week. Each channel's time is multiplied by the days it is used.

| Channel | Source time | Days per week (assumption) | Weekly time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Business | 5 min/day | 6 | 30 min |
| Facebook | 15 min/day | 6 | 90 min |
| Instagram | 30 min/day | 3 | 90 min |
| TikTok | 60 min/day | 0 | 0 min |
| Total | | | 210 min (3.5 h) |

Adding TikTok on three days would add 180 minutes and bring the total to 6.5 hours, which is above the 4-hour budget. The Bellwether guide also recommends dedicating 2-3 hours weekly to shooting and writing content ([Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops)), so Instagram's 90 minutes may need to share time with the shoot schedule. The result is a plan that fits the hours available, not one that chases every platform.

## How Do You Turn the 5 C's Into a One-Page Plan?

Put each C on one row with a finding and one action you can complete within a month. All figures in this example are assumptions for illustration, not data from a real business.

| C | Finding (assumption) | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company | Strong espresso, 4.7 stars from 120 reviews, closes at 3 pm | Promote morning offers; test later hours |
| Competitors | Two nearby cafes open until 6 pm, one has no food | Highlight pastries baked on site |
| Customers | Survey: most visit before work, few on weekends | Send a weekend brunch offer by email |
| Collaborators | Local bakery supplies pastries | Co-branded loyalty card, planned at the low end of the $50-$300 monthly range |
| Climate | Office footfall changing, rent review in spring | Build a pre-order option |

Review the sheet each quarter. If a row has no action you can finish, it is not yet a plan. Once you have a short list of actions, price the paid ones before committing. ## FAQ

### What are the 5 C's of a marketing plan?

They are Company, Competitors, Customers, Collaborators and Climate. Together they make up a situation analysis that comes before you choose channels or budgets. Some teams use a different fifth heading, so use whichever version your plan template follows.

### Is the 5 C's framework the same as a marketing plan?

No. It is the analysis that feeds the plan. A marketing plan also needs goals, channels, a budget and a schedule. Use the 5 C's to decide what to promote and where, then write those decisions down.

### How long should a small business spend on it?

A first version can be done in an afternoon if you use your own data, such as Google Business insights and a short customer survey. Update it every quarter, and any time a major competitor or landlord change affects your market.

### Which C matters most?

None is fixed. Customers usually decide the channel, while Competitors shows where a gap exists. A plan that skips Competitors can spend money competing in a market where your offer does not stand out.
