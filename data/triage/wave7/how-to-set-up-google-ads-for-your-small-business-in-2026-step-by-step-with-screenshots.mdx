To set up Google Ads for a small business, pick one goal, configure the campaign settings, build a tight ad group, write ads that match those keywords and set a daily budget you can test for several weeks. Google Ads is pay-per-click, so you pay when someone [clicks your ad](https://www.ce-paid-ads.com/google-ads-for-moving-companies). Start with one campaign, one service area and a modest budget, then review what people search for before you expand. Menu names change over time, so check Google's live help pages while you build.

## What Is the Difference Between a Smart and a Search Campaign?

A Smart campaign asks for less information and automates more of the targeting. It suits an owner with little time and one simple offer, because the setup covers the basics without asking you to build a keyword list.

A Search campaign gives direct control over keywords, match types and locations, and it shows which searches trigger your ads. That visibility helps you block wasted clicks, but it needs more attention each week. Most owners with several services start with Search, since the search terms report shows exactly which phrases are costing money.

## What Do You Need Before You Create the Account?

Gather these first:

- A website or landing page that works well on a phone
- One clear goal, such as phone calls, bookings or form fills
- A payment method
- A Google Business Profile if customers visit you or you serve them locally

Setting up the profile costs nothing. One vendor guide for mobile mechanics describes setting up a [Google Business Profile as a service-area business and collecting reviews as both free](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-mobile-mechanics). Another vendor's insurance guide advises [uploading 20+ photos and sending review requests to your last 20 clients](https://adwave.com/resources/insurance-agent-advertising). Photos and reviews are parts of the profile you control directly, so fill them in before you send paid traffic to it.

## How Do You Set the Goal, Settings and Locations?

Open the campaigns area and create a new campaign. Choose a goal that matches the result you care about, select the campaign type and add your conversion goals. Then set the bidding approach, choose locations and languages, and decide whether search partner sites should be included. A first campaign is easier to judge if it runs on Search only.

Leave display placements off on the first campaign unless you have a specific reason to test them. Our [display advertising guide for small business](/blog/display-advertising-for-small-business) explains when they make sense and what they tend to cost in clicks and attention.

If you serve several towns or sites, one vendor guide recommends creating [an individual campaign for each physical location or major service area](https://seoplus.com/paid-ads/the-ultimate-guide-to-multi-location-ppc-advertising-on-google-ads/) within a single account. The same guide warns that different regions may have their own rules, such as data privacy laws, which affect how you set up campaigns and handle data.

## How Do You Build Ad Groups, Keywords and Ads?

Group keywords by the service they describe, so each ad group sends people to a matching page. Enter each keyword with the match type you choose: broad, phrase or exact. Add negative keywords for unrelated searches, then check the search terms report regularly, because plurals, synonyms and mismatched phrases appear as the campaign runs. Negatives need upkeep for that reason.

Ads should mirror the keywords in their ad group. Put the service and the town in the headline where that is accurate, name the offer in the description, and point the final URL to the page that matches the ad. Write several ads per ad group so you can compare them, and retire the weakest one once you have enough clicks to judge.

## What Daily Budget Should a Small Business Set?

Google lets you set an average daily budget or a total campaign budget with start and end dates. Daily spend can vary, so judge the budget over a full month rather than a single day.

| Item | Value |

At these assumed figures, a small budget produces only a handful of enquiries a month. That is enough to start comparing ads, but not enough to judge small changes. After the first full month, adjust the budget only if the cost per enquiry fits what a new customer is worth to you. ## How Do You Track Calls and Form Fills?

Set up conversion tracking before you judge any results. A website action needs a tag or code that fires when someone completes the goal, such as a form submission or a booking confirmation. A phone call can be counted through a call asset or a number on your site, depending on how customers reach you.

Turn on call reporting in the account settings, and check the help page for the minimum call length you can set, since very short calls may not count as conversions. Confirm each conversion once with a test before you rely on the numbers.

Google Analytics records the same visits from a second angle, which helps when ad numbers and website numbers disagree. Our [Google Analytics 4 guide for small business](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business) explains how to connect the two.

## How Should You Review the First 30 Days?

A vendor's salon plan splits its first month into blocks: [days 1 to 7 for the digital audit, days 8 to 14 for the visual update, days 15 to 21 for the loyalty launch and days 22 to 30 for automation setup](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-marketing-ideas). That schedule is written for a salon rather than for ads, but the logic carries over. Audit before you spend, then fix what the audit finds before you expand.

For ads, the same order works. In the first week, check the search terms and add negatives for anything unrelated. Keep the budget steady while you collect data, and avoid changing several settings at once, because you will not know which change made the difference. Judge the campaign against the cost per enquiry you can afford at the end of the month.

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to start Google Ads?

You choose the budget, and you pay when someone clicks your ad. The cost of each click depends on your market and competition, so start with a budget you can test for a full month and use the worked example above to size it.

### How long does setup take?

The setup itself can be done in one sitting when your website, phone number and conversion goal are ready. Allow extra time for Google to review your ads and for you to check the settings you chose.

### Is a Smart campaign enough for a small business?

It can be for one simple local service with a linked Business Profile. The trade-off is less control over keywords and search terms, which matters most when the budget is tight.

### Can I set up Google Ads without a website?

A Search campaign needs a destination page for its ads. A single mobile-friendly page with your phone number, service and offer is enough to begin, and you can expand later.
