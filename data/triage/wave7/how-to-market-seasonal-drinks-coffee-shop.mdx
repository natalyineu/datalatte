## Why Run a Seasonal Drink at All?

A limited drink gives regulars a reason to come back and gives you something new to announce. Adwave's guide describes a coffee shop that built its videos around regulars and their go-to drinks, and reports the 40% engagement lift within two weeks. The useful lesson is the content angle: a seasonal drink gives you a reason to film people who already order.

Discovery matters too. The Restaurant HQ cites Statista for the figure that 98% of consumers use the internet to find new businesses. A drink that appears in your Google profile and social feeds reaches people who have not visited yet. No published figure in this plan ties seasonal drinks directly to higher traffic at independent cafés, so measure your own results before you repeat the launch.

For other ways to bring regulars back, read the [coffee shop marketing ideas guide](/blog/coffee-shop-marketing-ideas-30-strategies-to-fill-every-seat-in-2026).

## How Do You Price a Seasonal Drink?

Pick a drink your team can make quickly at your busiest hour. Then work out the cost of each component per serving and set a price that protects your margin. Price against your costs and your local market, not against a chain's menu.

Check these before you print signs:

- Ingredient cost per cup, including syrup, milk and toppings
- Preparation time at peak hour, converted into labour cost per drink
- Waste from unused syrup, sauce or cups at the end of the run
- Allergens in every component

Underestimating staffing costs is a common mistake when starting a coffee shop, according to [TGP International's café opening checklist](https://www.tgpinternational.com/blog/the-ultimate-caf%C3%A9-opening-checklist-a-complete-pre-launch-guide). A drink that looks profitable on ingredients alone can lose money if it slows the bar. The worked example below shows the arithmetic.

## What Allergen Information Should You Show?

Show the allergens in the seasonal drink on the menu board or a printed card at the till, and train staff to answer questions about every syrup, sauce and topping. Seasonal add-ins are easy to overlook because they often arrive as a new product rather than a recipe change.

Local rules for food labelling vary, so check with your local health department before launch. Whatever the rule, a clear allergen card protects customers and reduces the number of questions your team fields during a rush.

## Where Should You Announce It?

Start with channels you control, then add paid reach if the numbers justify it. Each message should carry one fact and one action: what the drink is and when it ends.

Google Business Profile comes first. Per Diem's guide says Google asks you to verify your listing before it goes live, usually by postcard, and that without verification your updates will not show publicly. Per Diem also recommends the "Coffee shop" category rather than a broad term like "Restaurant" ([Per Diem guide](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)).

Email is next. Square's 2025 Future of Commerce report, as summarised by Square, found 65% of Canadians like businesses to communicate with them by email, and 39% appreciate text message updates. Use the [coffee shop email examples](/blog/coffee-shop-email-marketing-examples) as a starting layout.

Social comes third. Bellwether Coffee lists Instagram at about 30 minutes a day and Google Business at about 5 minutes a day, and recommends 2 to 3 hours a week for shooting and writing content. For a seasonal drink, plan one short video of the drink being made, then use the [Instagram strategy guide](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-marketing-strategy) to schedule it.

## Should You Pay for Ads?

Test small and local. Adwave recommends a 1 to 3 mile geo-targeting radius around the shop for Reels promotions. In its multi-channel example, a local coffee shop runs Facebook and Instagram ads for a new fall latte to everyone within a five-mile radius of the store. A tighter radius keeps spend near the people most likely to visit.

If you run search ads, seoplus states that a localised phrase such as "coffee shop downtown Phoenix" is likely to have less competition than a generic term like "coffee shop", which can lead to a lower cost per click. Choose the phrase that matches your neighbourhood.

Clicks are not sales. Assumption: at a $0.50 cost per click, a $100 test buys about 200 clicks. Whether those clicks become drinks depends on the offer and how close people live. Ask customers how they heard about the drink.

## Does One Seasonal Drink Pay?

All inputs below are **assumptions**. Replace them with your own figures.

- Price: $6.25
- Ingredient and cup cost: $1.75
- Contribution per drink: $4.50
- Drinks sold: 30 per day for 42 days, so 1,260 drinks
- Total contribution: 1,260 x $4.50 = $5,670
- Launch costs: $100 ads plus $60 printing = $160
- Share of buyers who would have bought another drink anyway: 60%, so 40% are extra: 504 drinks
- Extra contribution: 504 x $4.50 = $2,268
- Net after launch costs: $2,268 - $160 = $2,108, before labour and waste

The gain is the 504 extra drinks, not all 1,260. If nearly every buyer would have ordered a latte anyway, the seasonal drink adds only the price difference. Track total drinks sold before and during the launch, not only the seasonal count.

## How Do You Know If It Worked?

Compare weekly sales and transaction counts against the same weeks last year or the weeks before launch. Count email clicks, views of your Google Business Profile post and any code redemptions. Set your target before launch, so you know what result justifies a repeat.

If you reward repeat buyers, Adwave's guide plans $50 to $300 per month for a loyalty platform, plus $200 to $1,000 in incentive payouts such as free drinks or credit. Count those costs in the same calculation as the drink's contribution. ## FAQ

### How far ahead should I announce a seasonal drink?
Announce it a few days ahead, and tie the date to a moment your regulars already expect, such as a local event. Give the drink a fixed end date in every message.

### Do I need a verified Google Business Profile?
Yes, if you want updates to appear publicly. Per Diem's guide says unverified updates will not show up publicly and customers may not find the listing.

### Is paying for ads worth it for a seasonal drink?
A small test within a 1 to 3 mile radius is a reasonable start. Judge it by sales and redemptions, not by clicks.

### How should I price a seasonal drink?
Add up ingredient, cup and waste costs, then add labour time at peak hour. Make sure the contribution after those costs covers the effort, not only the ingredients.
