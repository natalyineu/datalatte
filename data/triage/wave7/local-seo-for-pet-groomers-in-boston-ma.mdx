Local SEO for a pet groomer in Boston, MA means one thing: getting into Google's local pack, which [shows the top three business listings in local search results](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today). Groomers get there by claiming and completing a Google Business Profile (GBP), a [free tool for managing a presence across Google Search and Maps](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/). Then they keep name, address and phone identical everywhere, collect reviews and photos steadily, and track calls and direction requests. The sections below cover each step in that order.

## What does the Google Maps local pack show a pet groomer?

The local pack is the map block that appears above ordinary search results. A groomer needs to be in that block of three to get most of the map-driven calls. [Adwave states that the listings in the top three are pulled directly from their GBP listings](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business), which makes the profile the main thing you control.

Your profile also feeds other places. Google says reviews [show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122). Posts appear publicly only when their status is Live, and Pending or Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Google's Q&A feature launched in Maps in 2017, and a [secondary summary of Google's AI answers says responses draw on business profile data, website content, and reviews](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a).

Vendor estimates of how much the profile matters vary widely. Adwave cites a figure of 32% for GBP signals in local pack weight, and its categories article says the primary category is the top ranking factor, [without naming the underlying analysis](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones). Treat these as directional. The practical takeaway is the same either way: a complete, accurate profile is the foundation. For a full walkthrough, see [Google Business Profile for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-for-pet-groomers).

## Which Google Business Profile fields should a Boston groomer fill in first?

Start with the fields that are easiest to get wrong. [Adwave advises making hours, phone number, and address 100% accurate](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). A wrong hours field sends a customer to a closed door, and that loss is hard to see in your reports.

Choose your primary category carefully. Adwave recommends [checking the top three to five businesses in local Maps results for your main service search and noting which category they use](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones). Then use the business description. Adwave's beginner guide says [you get 750 characters for your business description](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), so name your services, your neighborhood, and what sets your grooming apart.

If you travel to customers, your street address can be hidden. The same beginner guide notes that [a business that works from home or serves customers at their location can hide its street address and is then designated a Service Area Business](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Finally, post regularly. Adwave advises [at least one new Google Post a week](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). For a deeper field-by-field list, read [Google Business Profile optimization for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers).

## How many photos and reviews does a pet groomer need?

Photos are the cheapest signal to improve. Adwave recommends [at least 15 high-quality photos, which it says gets far more clicks and calls](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Its own checklist elsewhere suggests [at least 10](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), so use 15 as the target. Show grooming stations, finished dogs (with owner consent), and your vehicle if you run a mobile service.

Reviews are harder to pace. A third-party analysis reported by Adwave found that [businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business). Another vendor article claims that [businesses with over 50 reviews and a 4.7+ average rating dominate top map pack spots](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), but it cites no source, so do not treat that threshold as fixed.

Ask for reviews after each appointment, and mention the service type in your request. Google's own review guidance is a useful reference for how reviews display, and it is worth reading before you build a request template. Reviews are a slow channel. Track the count each month instead of expecting quick jumps.

## Which citations should a Boston groomer check first?

Citations are copies of your name, address, and phone number on other sites. Consistency matters more than volume. Adwave's voice search guide says [your NAP must be exactly the same on your website, your GBP, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses).

Small differences count. The beginner guide says the NAP must match [right down to the comma and whether you use "St." or "Street."](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) Fix existing listings before adding new ones. Apple's listing platform deserves attention too. Apple Business Connect reports Profile Views, defined as [the total count of how many people saw your business card in Apple Maps](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring).

## Where do paid ads fit next to the map pack?

Paid search and local SEO use the same location data. Google states that [data for location assets used in Google Ads is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), and corrections made in your profile flow into linked accounts. A wrong address on your profile can therefore hurt both channels at once.

If your business has several sites or a mobile route, Google says you can [select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link with your Ads account](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), as long as they are yours. For ad platform choice, see [Google Ads vs Facebook ads for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins). A [vendor case study](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/) describes a mobile pet groomer that chose Facebook and Google as its channels, which shows a common split.

**Worked example (all inputs are assumptions):** Assume 40 calls per month come from your profile, 1 in 4 callers books, and the average groom is $60. That is 10 bookings and $600 a month. Now assume wrong hours cost you 10% of those calls. You lose 4 calls, which is 1 booking, or about $60 a month. Fixing hours takes minutes, so the return on that task is high.

## How do you measure whether local SEO is working?

Track the same few numbers every month. Adwave lists six useful local search metrics: [GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent). Pick a baseline before you change anything, so you can see what moved.

Check rankings privately, not on your own logged-in browser, because results vary by user. Compare your results to the top three you saw in the same search. If calls rise and map views hold, your profile is working even if your position shifts day to day. ## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a pet groomer?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as [a free Business Profile on Google Search and Maps](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/). Paid options such as ads are separate.

### Why does my Google Post not show on my profile?

Google reviews each post. A post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, so Pending and Not approved posts stay hidden ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).

### Can I manage customer messages from a desktop?

According to a [secondary guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on), yes. You can manage chats from the Google Business Profile dashboard on a desktop, not only in the Maps mobile app.

### Should a mobile groomer show a street address?

If you work from home or travel to clients, you can hide the street address and register as a Service Area Business ([Adwave beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). Set a service area that matches where you actually drive.
