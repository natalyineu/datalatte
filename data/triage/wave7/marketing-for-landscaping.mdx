A single-location landscaping company should plan Google Ads at $1,200-$4,000 a month during peak season (March-July) and $500-$1,500 a month off-season, according to [AdWave's landscaping advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/landscaping-advertising). Treat that range as a starting point. Your real spend should follow your enquiries, your crew capacity and what each job earns. This guide covers residential and light commercial landscapers in the US and shows how to build a seasonal budget you can test before committing to it.

## How Much Should a Landscaper Spend Each Season?

The range above covers Google Ads only. AdWave also states that March through June accounts for 70% of annual new customer acquisition for most landscaping companies. Spend should therefore rise before that window opens, not after the phone starts ringing. The same guide recommends announcing seasonal offerings on Meta three to four weeks before the peak.

Competition is heavy. AdWave counts more than 600,000 companies competing for residential, commercial and municipal work in the US. A single-location firm rarely wins on budget size alone. Timing, a clear local offer and fast quote follow-up usually decide who gets the job.

## When Does Landscaping Demand Peak?

For most landscaping companies, March through June carries 70% of annual new customer acquisition, according to AdWave. Your own peak may differ by region and service mix, so check enquiries by month in your call log before you lock in dates. Enquiry timestamps and the service requested are usually enough to see the pattern.

Use the three-to-four-week lead time for announcements. If your spring peak starts in March, publish seasonal offers in late February. Ask your crew lead which weeks they can staff, and raise spend only for weeks you can deliver. Ads that bring in more jobs than you can schedule waste budget and damage reviews.

## How Do You Build a Seasonal Budget?

AdWave's illustrative example uses a landscaping company with a $24,000 annual ad budget running April to October. Its February-March ramp phase has an $8,400 budget, with CTV at $2,800 a month and always-on search at $1,400 a month. The April-July peak phase also has $8,400, with search at $1,600 a month and CTV at maintenance levels of $500 a month.

Check the off-season line against AdWave's $500-$1,500 Google Ads range. At $2,400 a month, the example spends above that range. It also includes CTV, which the Google Ads range does not cover, so the comparison is not like for like. Test whether August to October enquiries justify the extra spend before you keep it.

## What Does a Month of Ads Return?

The worked example below uses assumptions for every input except the budget pattern above. Replace each one with your own figures before you use the result.

| Input or result | Value | Label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend | $2,000 | Assumption |
| Cost per lead | $75 | Assumption |
| Close rate | 30% | Assumption |
| First-season job value | $1,500 | Assumption |
| Leads | about 27 | $2,000 divided by $75 |
| Signed jobs | about 8 | 27 multiplied by 30% |
| First-season revenue | about $12,000 | 8 multiplied by $1,500 |
| Cost per signed job | $250 | $2,000 divided by 8 |

The result is sensitive to close rate. If only 15% of leads sign, cost per job rises to about $490. Measure close rate from quotes sent, not from enquiries received, because a lead that never gets a quote cannot be won. A [dashboard that tracks spend, leads and signed jobs](/blog/how-to-create-marketing-dashboard-small-business) makes this calculation a monthly habit.

## How Many Ads and Tests Should You Run?

AdManage notes that covering a mid-size remodeling company across service lines, cities, seasonal offers and form variations can require 60-100+ active test variants. That is a remodeling figure, not a landscaping one, but it shows how quickly variants multiply. A landscaper with one service area and three core services needs far fewer.

Start with one offer per service and one seasonal message per peak. Compare cost per signed job after the first full month before adding variants. Do not split a small budget across so many ads that no single one gets enough enquiries to judge.

## How Do You Keep Enquiries Flowing Between Seasons?

Off-season months are the cheapest time to rebuild demand for next spring. Use them to ask past clients for reviews, refresh your service photos and check which neighbourhoods respond to your posts. A [hyperlocal campaign on Nextdoor](/blog/hyperlocal-marketing-nextdoor-small-business) can reach households near recent jobs at a lower cost than a broad search campaign.

If you run campaigns across several channels, keep the tools simple. A small team can often manage with the [affordable marketing help options for small businesses](/blog/affordable-marketing-help-small-business-2026) and a basic [marketing automation setup](/blog/best-marketing-automation-tools-small-business) for reminders and follow-up.

## FAQ

### How much should a landscaping company spend on Google Ads?
AdWave suggests single-location landscaping companies budget $1,200-$4,000 a month during peak season (March-July) and $500-$1,500 a month off-season. Adjust the figure to your close rate and crew capacity.

### When should I start seasonal promotions?
AdWave recommends announcing seasonal offerings on Meta three to four weeks before the peak. For a March start, that means publishing in late February.

### How much of the year's new business comes in spring?
AdWave states that March through June accounts for 70% of annual new customer acquisition for most landscaping companies. Check your own enquiry history, because your region may differ.

### How many ad variants does a small landscaper need?
Fewer than a large remodeler. AdManage's 60-100+ figure is for a mid-size remodeling company. Start with one offer per core service and expand only after you have a clear cost per signed job.
