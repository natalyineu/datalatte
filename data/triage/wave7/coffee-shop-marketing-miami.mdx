Marketing a coffee shop in Miami in 2026 comes down to three basics: a verified Google Business Profile in the right category, a short daily social routine, and a paid campaign limited to the neighborhood you serve. Miami adds local layers on top. Wynwood, Brickell, and Little Havana draw different customers, and seasonal visitors change who is nearby. The time commitment for the basics is modest. Bellwether Coffee's guide lists about 30 minutes a day for Instagram and 5 minutes a day for Google Business.

This guide covers four decisions: how to set up your Google listing, which social channels justify daily time, how to set an ad radius and budget, and how to adjust for seasons. Where a figure is illustrative rather than sourced, it is labelled as an assumption.

## How should a Miami coffee shop set up its Google Business Profile?

Choose the "Coffee shop" category or a related term rather than something broad like "Restaurant." [Per Diem's coffee shop guide](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide) recommends this for coffee businesses. Google will ask you to verify the listing before it goes live, usually by mailing a postcard with a code to the business address, though some businesses can verify by phone or email. Without verification, your updates will not show publicly and customers may not find you.

Turn on direct messaging. Google allows users to message businesses through their listings, so check the inbox and answer quickly. The same guide cites Think Coffee, which placed its app download link in its Google profile and now has thousands of downloads.

Localized phrases are the practical SEO lever for a neighborhood café. [Salesforce's local marketing guide](https://www.salesforce.com/uk/marketing/local-marketing/?bc=OTH) gives phrases such as "best coffee shop in" a city as long-tail keywords. [seoplus](https://seoplus.com/paid-ads/the-ultimate-guide-to-multi-location-ppc-advertising-on-google-ads/) notes that a localized phrase like "coffee shop downtown Phoenix" is likely to face less competition than "coffee shop," which can mean a lower cost per click. In Miami, the same logic points toward phrases that name Wynwood, Brickell, Little Havana, or Coral Gables.

Many Miami neighborhoods have Spanish-speaking customers, so a profile that mixes English and Spanish is a sensible default. Include terms like cafecito or colada if they appear on your menu. Reply to reviews in the language they were written in, and keep hours current, since visitor-season and summer hours often differ.

## Which social channels are worth the daily time?

The table below uses the time-per-day figures from [Bellwether Coffee's social media guide](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops). These are planning estimates for a coffee shop, not measured results.

| Channel | Time per day (guide) | Audience listed in guide | Best use for a café |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 30 min | All ages, broad reach | Drink visuals, local identity |
| TikTok | 1 hour | 18–34 primarily | Process videos, younger reach |
| Facebook | 15 min | 30+ skewing older | Events, community updates |
| Google Business | 5 min | Anyone searching | Hours, photos, review replies |

Instagram suits a café with visual drinks and a clear local identity. The guide sets a goal engagement rate of 3–6% on Instagram and recommends dedicating 2–3 hours weekly to shooting and writing content. [The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/) cites Pew Research, as relayed on that page, that nearly 70% of consumers aged 18 to 49 use Instagram (secondary). A deeper plan is in the [coffee shop Instagram marketing strategy](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-marketing-strategy).

Facebook takes about 15 minutes a day and can suit event posts for older local regulars. TikTok takes about an hour a day for a younger audience. For most independent cafés, starting with Instagram and Google Business and adding other channels only when time allows is the practical order.

## How do you set an ad radius and budget for a Miami café?

[AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes) recommends a 1-3 mile geo-targeting radius around a coffee shop for Reels promotions. Its [multi-channel guide](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-channel-marketing-approach) uses a five-mile radius for a fall latte campaign on Facebook and Instagram. In dense neighborhoods such as Wynwood or Brickell, start with the tighter radius and widen it only when a campaign targets visitors.

Worked example, where every input is an assumption: a Brickell café spends $300 per month on Instagram ads inside a 3-mile radius. The average visit is $6, and the campaign drives 150 visits per month. Ad revenue is 150 × $6 = $900, so the return on ad spend is $900 ÷ $300 = 3:1. AdWave's [CTV measurement guide](https://adwave.com/resources/ctv-measurement-attribution-guide) defines ROAS the same way, using a hypothetical 4:1 result from $500 spend and $2,000 attributed revenue.

Now add a loyalty program. AdWave's guide plans $50–$300 per month for a loyalty platform, so assume the low end at $50 (assumption). Total monthly marketing cost becomes $350. At $6 per visit, the café needs 350 ÷ 6 ≈ 59 incremental visits per month just to cover that spend in revenue terms, before cost of goods. Against 150 assumed visits, the plan clears the bar. If the campaign produced only 40 visits, the same budget would lose money, so cut or retarget the ads before cutting the loyalty program.

## How should Miami cafés adjust for seasons and neighborhoods?

Visitor flow and local demand peak at different times, so plan two light campaigns instead of one. During the visitor season, emphasize discovery: current Google Business Profile photos, English-forward descriptions, and a wider radius. In the hotter months, shift toward local loyalty, SMS or email offers, and comfort messaging. The [coffee shop email marketing examples](/blog/coffee-shop-email-marketing-examples) show offer formats that suit repeat customers.

Event weeks such as Art Basel or Miami Music Week can justify extra posts and temporary hours for cafés nearby. Check the live event calendar for dates, since they change each year. For cafés far from event zones, steady local SEO usually matters more than event-driven spikes.

Automation can free up the daily time these channels require. ## What should a Miami café track each month?

Track three numbers: Google Business Profile activity, Instagram engagement against the 3–6% goal, and paid-campaign visits against spend. Compare the monthly visit count with the break-even figure from the worked example, using your own ticket size in place of the assumed $6.

If you run a loyalty program, track how many visits come from members. Review the numbers monthly rather than daily, and change one variable at a time, such as the radius or the offer, so you can see what caused the change.

## FAQ

### How much time should a Miami coffee shop spend on marketing each day?

Bellwether Coffee's guide lists about 30 minutes a day for Instagram, 5 minutes for Google Business, 15 minutes for Facebook, and about 1 hour for TikTok. Most independent cafés can start with Instagram and Google Business and add other channels later.

### Should a café's marketing be in English or Spanish?

Many Miami neighborhoods have Spanish-speaking customers, so a mix of both languages is a sensible default. Reply to reviews in the language they were written in, and include menu terms like cafecito or colada where relevant. Running fully separate accounts is usually more work than a single bilingual presence.

### Do I need a Google Business Profile if I already have Instagram?

Yes. Per Diem notes that without verification, updates will not show publicly and customers may not find the business. Google Business is also listed in Bellwether's guide as a 5-minute daily task with a search-focused audience.

### What radius should Reels promotions use?

AdWave recommends a 1-3 mile geo-targeting radius around a coffee shop for Reels promotions. Start tighter in dense neighborhoods, and widen the radius only for visitor-focused campaigns.
