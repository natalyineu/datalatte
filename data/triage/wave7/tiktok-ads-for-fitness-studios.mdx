## How Much Do TikTok Ads Cost for a Fitness Studio?

Adwave's TikTok advertising page says TikTok ads start at $91 a day and run only when the user turns them on in Customize and connects a TikTok account. Adwave's fitness app guide also gives TikTok a 10% share in one budget stage with a $1,000-$10,000 allocation. That guide is written for fitness apps, not studios, so treat it as context rather than a studio benchmark.

Cost per click and cost per thousand views for fitness studios are not given in these sources. Your first test sets that number. Check TikTok's current help center for minimum daily budgets, because platform minimums change and should be confirmed before you set a budget in Ads Manager.

For a studio, the question is not whether TikTok is cheap. It is whether a fixed monthly test can produce enough trial sign-ups to justify the spend. Set the test length and the budget in advance, so the result is judged on numbers rather than on how the ads feel.

## What Share of the Marketing Budget Should TikTok Get?

Mariana Tek says many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing. Applying that range to a studio's revenue gives the total pot, and the TikTok share comes out of that pot. The table below uses an assumed revenue figure to show the arithmetic.

| Line | Amount | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly revenue | $25,000 | assumption |
| Marketing budget at 5% to 10% | $1,250 to $2,500 | Mariana Tek range applied to assumed revenue |
| TikTok share at 10% | $125 to $250 | assumption, borrowed from Adwave fitness app stage |
| TikTok starting spend, 30 days at $91 | $2,730 | Adwave start rate; 30 days is an assumption |
| Shortfall against TikTok start | $2,480 to $2,605 | calculated |

To fund the $2,730 starting spend with a 10% share, the marketing budget would need to reach $27,300. At a 10% marketing share, that means monthly revenue of $273,000. At 5%, it means $546,000. For a typical small studio, paid TikTok does not fit at these numbers, which is why the first budget dollars usually go elsewhere.

## How Often Should a Studio Post Short Video?

Mariana Tek advises studios to post at a steady cadence rather than in bursts, with three to five short videos a week. The same article describes that pace as realistic and sustainable for most studios. Organic posting costs staff time rather than ad spend, so it suits a small studio that has not yet proven TikTok paid results.

Consistent posts also give you something to test. Pick the clips that bring people to the front desk or into a trial class, then decide whether any of them deserve paid reach. Our [TikTok guide for fitness studios](/blog/using-tiktok-for-fitness-studio-marketing) covers content ideas that work without ads.

Mariana Tek also advises following up with pop-up contacts within 24 hours using an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades fast after a pop-up ends. That follow-up matters more than ad reach for most studios. ## What Rules Shape Fitness Ads on TikTok?

TikTok's ad policies restrict how weight and body claims can be made, so read the current weight management policy before publishing any transformation content. Do not promise results, and do not show body shaming or unrealistic outcomes. Stick to what the studio actually offers: classes, coaches, the schedule and a trial.

Keep the offer simple. A first class or a trial week is easier for a stranger to accept than a promise about how someone will look. Send clicks to a booking page that works on a phone and shows the class schedule, address and price.

Review the policy again before every new campaign, since rules and wording change. If a question is not answered on the policy page, do not assume it is allowed. Ask the platform's support team or check its live documentation first.

## Where Should a Small Studio Spend Before TikTok Ads?

The Vertical Impression guide suggests $600-$1,200 a month on Google Ads for a small independent studio with under 200 members. Google captures people who are already searching for a class, which is why it usually comes before TikTok for a local studio. Our [local SEO guide for fitness studios](/blog/ai-driven-local-seo-strategies-for-fitness-studios) covers how to appear in those searches.

Reviews also matter. Adwave's yoga studio guide says studios with 50+ reviews and a 4.7+ rating consistently outrank studios with fewer reviews. That page is written for yoga studios, but the principle carries over to other studios competing in local search.

Referrals cost little. ClubRight's budget guide notes that even if only half of your members refer one friend, you'll still have doubled your membership base. That example is illustrative, but it shows why member referrals deserve a test before paid TikTok reach.

## FAQ

### Do I need a business account to run TikTok ads?

Adwave's guide says TikTok ads run only when you turn them on in Customize and connect your TikTok account. Set up the account and ad access before you plan the budget.

### Can a studio show weight-loss results in TikTok ads?

TikTok's policy restricts weight and muscle claims, so keep to classes, coaches and trial offers. Review the current policy page before publishing any body-related content.

### Is TikTok better than Google Ads for a small studio?

The sources here do not settle it. Google captures people already searching for a class, and the benchmark for a small studio is $600-$1,200 a month. Test either channel with a fixed budget and a fixed test length before deciding.
