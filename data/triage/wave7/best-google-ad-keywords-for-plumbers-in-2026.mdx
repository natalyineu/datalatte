The best Google Ads keywords for plumbers are urgent, local, job-specific searches such as "emergency plumber near me", "burst pipe repair", "clogged drain repair" and "water heater replacement". Group them by job value, bid harder on big-ticket work and keep cheap repair terms tight. Click prices vary by market. A WordStream 2025 figure cited by [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/performance-marketing-small-business) puts an emergency plumber click at $30-50 in some markets, so a small budget buys few clicks.

## Which Keyword Groups Should a Plumber Start With?

Separate keywords by urgency and job value, because each group needs its own budget and landing page. Emergency searches, such as "emergency plumber near me", "burst pipe repair" and "24 hour plumber", are call-ready, so they suit a prominent phone number. Common repairs like "leaking faucet repair", "clogged drain" and "toilet running" bring steady demand but smaller tickets.

Big-ticket terms such as "water heater replacement", "tankless water heater install" and "repipe house" carry larger invoices and justify higher bids. "Sewer line camera inspection" is a planned job with less price pressure. Brand terms using your company name protect it from competitors.

Add your city or service area in a second pass, such as "emergency plumber" plus the city. Keyword examples are starting points to check in Keyword Planner, not search-volume claims.

## How Do Local Phrases Shape Plumber Keywords?

Local intent drives most plumbing searches. [Salesforce UK's local marketing guide](https://www.salesforce.com/uk/marketing/local-marketing/?bc=OTH) gives "plumber near me" as an example of a location-specific long-tail phrase, alongside "best coffee shop in [city]".

Google Business Profile Insights defines a "Discovery" search as someone searching for a category, like "plumber near me", without a specific business in mind ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-insights-what-your-analytics-actually-mean)). Google's illustrative example notes that a plumber can only consider the few hundred or thousand customers within visiting distance, compared with the millions a global e-commerce brand reaches ([Google Business](https://business.google.com/us/resources/articles/lead-generation/)). Keep service areas tight rather than broad.

[Mailchimp's Australian guide](https://mailchimp.com/resources/australia/local-marketing-in-australia/) gives a hypothetical Melbourne plumber who creates service pages for Brunswick and Footscray to appear in "near me" searches. The same suburb-level logic applies to paid keywords. Build one ad group per core area rather than one citywide group.

## What Negative Keywords Should a Plumber Add?

Negative keywords stop ads from showing for searches that do not lead to paid work. Most plumbers block job seekers, training queries, do-it-yourself searches and parts shoppers from the start.

- Job seeker terms such as jobs, hiring, salary, apprentice and career
- Training terms such as course, school and certification
- Do-it-yourself and parts terms such as diy, how to, parts, supplies and wholesale
- Price terms such as free and cheap, but only if you do not compete on price
- Services you do not offer, such as pool, septic or commercial

Review the search terms report weekly and add anything that does not match a job you would accept. Add plural and singular variants yourself, because a negative does not automatically cover close variants.

## Should a Plumber Use Local Services Ads or Search Ads?

Local Services Ads (LSA) are a pay-per-lead format that lets a business use its existing Google Business Profile inside a Google Ads campaign ([Mammoth-style plumbing guide by Plumbing Webmasters](https://www.plumbingwebmasters.com/plumber-advertising/), secondary). AdWave advises plumbers to budget $500-2,000 a month to start with LSAs, depending on market size, and $1,000-3,000 a month on search ads for meaningful results ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/plumbing-advertising-local-lead-generation)).

Google's verified list of home services eligible for a Google Verified badge includes Plumber, which helps trust-based formats ([Google Ads](https://business.google.com/us/ad-solutions/local-service-ads/)). For a plumber with a $500 total monthly budget, AdWave's example runs LSAs at $400 and spends $100 optimizing the Business Profile, or does that work free ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-plumbers)). Neither range is a guarantee, so test both against cost per job.

## How Big Should a Plumber's Ad Budget Be?

AdWave's example for a solo plumber with $500,000 a year in revenue suggests $25,000-60,000 in annual ad spend, or $2,000-5,000 a month. Multi-truck operations typically spend $5,000-20,000 or more monthly on Google's ad ecosystem. If you hire help for Business Profile posts, photos, reviews and citations, AdWave suggests $200-700 a month ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-plumbers)).

These are vendor ranges, not benchmarks from independent research. Use them to frame a test, then base the real budget on your own cost per job.

## What Does a $1,000 Monthly Search Budget Buy?

The inputs below are assumptions except the click costs, which come from the WordStream figure cited above. The conversion steps are the point of the exercise.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,000 |
| Daily budget (about $1,000 / 30) | $33, inside AdWave's $20-40 emergency range |
| Cost per click, low case | $30 |
| Cost per click, high case | $50 |
| Clicks per month | 20 to 33 |
| Calls per click (assumption) | 10% |
| Calls per month | 2.0 to 3.3 |
| Share of calls that book a job (assumption) | 50% |
| Jobs per month | 1.0 to 1.7 |
| Cost per job | $600 to $1,000 |
| Average invoice (assumption) | $350 |

At a $350 invoice, a $600-1,000 cost per job loses money on the first visit. Emergency search only pays if the average invoice is higher, the customer returns, or the ad moves toward a larger job. Shift part of the budget toward big-ticket groups and test again. Change the assumed rates to match your own call and booking data before you trust the result.

## What Should Happen After the Click?

Send each keyword group to a page that matches it. Emergency terms need a phone number and service area above the fold. Big-ticket terms need pages that explain the job and the price range. Use call tracking so you can see which keyword produced each booked job, not just each call.

## FAQ

### What is the average cost per click for plumbers on Google Ads?

No single national plumber figure is published in the sources. One WordStream 2025 figure, cited by AdWave, puts an emergency plumber click at $30-50 in some markets. Check Keyword Planner for your own terms and locations.

### Should a plumber start with Local Services Ads or search ads?

Test both. AdWave suggests $500-2,000 a month to start with LSAs and $1,000-3,000 a month for search ads to see meaningful results. Compare cost per job, not cost per lead, before choosing.

### How much should a plumber budget for Google Ads?

Start from the vendor ranges and then work backwards from jobs wanted, booking rate and average invoice. The worked example above shows how a $1,000 monthly budget can produce a cost per job higher than the invoice.

### Which keywords should a plumber avoid?

Block job seeker, training, do-it-yourself and parts searches. Block price-only terms if you do not compete on price, and block services you do not offer. Review the search terms report weekly.
