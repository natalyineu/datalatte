A local business can automate customer follow-ups in five steps: collect permission, send a thank-you after each visit, ask for a review, remind customers when they are due to rebook, and honour every opt-out. Automation handles the timing, but a person still needs to check the wording and the permission records. Follow-ups pay off because they bring people back. The [Winback Engine guide](https://winbackengine.com/blog/customer-reactivation-guide/) states that permission-based follow-ups convert at 15–20% within 90 days. This guide walks through each step and gives an assumption-based example. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Which Follow-Up Tasks Can Automation Handle?

Marketing automation refers to software designed to automate repetitive marketing tasks, as the [Builderall list of marketing automation platforms](https://thebuild.builderall.com/ultimate-list-of-marketing-automation-platforms-for-2023/) describes it. The [RingCentral guide to marketing automation tools](https://www.ringcentral.com/us/en/blog/10-marketing-automation-tools-every-smb-needs/) names email follow-up sequences, SMS workflows, CRM data entry and customer segmentation as rule-based tasks that can be automated.

The main risk is over-automation. The [SOCi guide on AI agents for marketing](https://www.soci.ai/blog/ai-agents-for-multi-location-marketing-5-tasks-you-can-fully-automate-today/) lists lack of context in sensitive situations, inconsistent brand voice without training, and over-automation as the main risks. A thank-you email can run on a schedule. A complaint should reach a person. For the trade-off in more depth, see [AI vs human marketing for local business](/blog/ai-vs-human-marketing-local-business-honest-comparison).

## How Do You Get Permission to Follow Up?

Ask at booking or checkout, record the answer, and keep the date and channel. A customer who agreed to email has not agreed to texts, so keep separate records for each channel. Do not assume permission from a booking alone.

Rules for marketing email and text differ by country and by channel. Check the current requirements for the US, UK, Australia or Canada, whichever applies to you, and read your regulator's guidance before you send anything. Transactional messages, such as a booking confirmation, can follow different rules, so confirm how your messages are classified.

Keep one simple record per customer: name, contact details, consent given (yes or no), the date, and the channel. This record is what you will check when a customer later asks why they received a message.

## What Should the Thank-You Message Say?

Send it the same day or the next day. Thank the customer, name the service they had, and give one way to reply or book. Keep the message short and plain, with no discount attached.

Drafts can start from the prompts in [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing), but read every version before it goes out. A wrong service name or a wrong price in an automated message reaches every customer on that flow.

Set up the thank-you as one automated flow triggered by the booking or checkout, not as a manual task. Test it with your own phone and email address before you switch it on for real customers.

## How Do You Ask for a Review Without Breaking Platform Rules?

Send the review link or QR code a day or two after the visit. Ask every customer, not only the ones who seemed happy, because a request that goes only to happy customers gives a skewed picture of your business. Keep the wording neutral.

Do not link a reward to a review without first checking the review platform's current policy. Google Business Profile Help explains what is allowed when you ask for reviews, and its rules can change, so read the live page before you launch.

Reviews are also a reply job. The [SOCi guide](https://www.soci.ai/blog/ai-agents-for-multi-location-marketing-5-tasks-you-can-fully-automate-today/) describes review response automation that drafts personalised replies and adjusts tone to sentiment. A drafted reply still needs a person to check it, and an [AI agent for customer service](/blog/ai-agent-for-customer-service-local-business) can help with the first draft.

## When Should You Remind Customers to Rebook?

Set the reminder from your own service cycle. A hairdresser might use six weeks, and a dental clinic might use six months. Send the reminder a little before the customer is due, with one booking link and one clear call to action.

Give the reminder a set date. The Winback Engine guide says that an "I'll think about it" response converts at 20–30% when a scheduled follow-up date is set, but converts less than 5% of the time without a follow-up mechanism. The same idea applies to rebooking: a reminder with a date to act is easier to follow through than an open invitation.

Where customers already use WhatsApp, the [WhatsApp Business API guide for local marketing automation](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-local-marketing-automation) covers how that channel works. Its opt-in rules differ from email and SMS, so check them separately.

## How Do You Handle Opt-Outs and Complaints?

Build the opt-out into every message. When someone asks to stop, remove them from all marketing messages, not only from the campaign they replied to. Confirm the change in your system, and check your country's current deadline for honouring opt-outs.

Route complaints to a person. The Winback Engine guide says that adding a manager follow-up call to a specific acknowledgment and recovery offer yields a 35–45% re-engagement rate. That result comes from a human step, which an automated sequence cannot replace.

Review your opt-out handling once a month. Check that unsubscribed contacts are not still on a send list, since one missed record can undo the consent work you did in step one.

## Which Tool Fits a Small Shop?

The tool matters less than the flow. Start with the one that connects to your booking or point-of-sale system, because a manual copy-paste step is where errors creep in. The [RingCentral guide](https://www.ringcentral.com/us/en/blog/10-marketing-automation-tools-every-smb-needs/) notes that Zapier lets businesses automate work across more than 9,000 apps, so check whether your booking tool connects directly.

Compare costs by the number of contacts and by which automation features each plan includes, because a free plan may exclude flows. The [best marketing automation tools for small business](/blog/best-marketing-automation-tools-small-business) article compares options by use case.

## FAQ

### Can I text customers without permission?
No. Do not assume permission from a booking alone. Check your country's current rules for marketing texts before you send any, and ask a qualified lawyer about transactional messages.

### How fast must I honour an opt-out?
Stop marketing messages to that person straight away, across all lists. Check your country's current deadline for the exact time limit.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?
Check the review platform's current policy first. Do not attach a reward to a review until you have confirmed it is allowed.

### Which messages need the most care?
Complaints and sensitive situations. The SOCi guide lists lack of context in sensitive situations as a risk of automation, so route these to a person.

### Is this legal advice?
No. This article is general information. Ask a qualified lawyer in your country to confirm how the rules apply to your business.
