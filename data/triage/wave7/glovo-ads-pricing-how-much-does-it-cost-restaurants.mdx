Your total Glovo Ads cost is what you spend inside a campaign, so estimate it from three numbers: your daily budget, the cost per click you actually pay, and how many clicks turn into orders. Headline prices tell you little on their own. The most useful planning figure is break-even cost per click, which equals average order value multiplied by conversion rate. A $12 order with a 2.5% conversion rate supports a click cost of about $0.30 before your costs. ## Does Glovo Ads have a fixed price per click?

Glovo Ads costs move with your bids, your delivery area and the competition for each click. Treat any single cost-per-click figure as a starting point and replace it with your own data after the first test period. Deliverect, a vendor that publishes Glovo guidance, advises restaurants to dedicate one or two days per week to Glovo promotion campaigns ([Glovo 101 guide](https://www.deliverect.com/en-mx/blog/online-food-delivery/glovo-101-the-essential-guide-for-restaurants)). Scheduling campaigns this way makes spend easier to cap.

The same guide cites Grand View Research, which estimated the online food ordering and delivery market at USD 189.70 billion globally in 2021. That figure explains why placements compete for attention, but it says nothing about the price of a click in your postcode.

## How do you calculate your break-even cost per click?

Use this formula: break-even cost per click equals average order value multiplied by conversion rate. Enter the conversion rate as a decimal, so 2.5% becomes 0.025. A $12 average order at 2.5% gives $12 × 0.025 = $0.30 per click.

Any click that costs more than this value loses money on average, before food costs, packaging and fees are counted. The same coffee shop paying $0.80 per click would need a conversion rate of about 6.7% to break even ($0.80 ÷ $12). Run the calculation with your real order values before you set a budget, because a high click cost is only a problem when it sits above your break-even point.

## What does a 14-day Glovo Ads test cost in a worked example?

The inputs below are assumptions for illustration. Replace them with your own numbers.

| Input | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Daily budget | $25 | assumption |
| Test length | 14 days | assumption |
| Cost per click | $0.80 | assumption |
| Conversion rate | 2.5% | assumption |
| Average order value | $18 | assumption |

Total spend is $25 × 14 = $350. At $0.80 per click, that buys about 438 clicks ($350 ÷ $0.80). At 2.5%, roughly 11 clicks become orders (438 × 0.025 ≈ 10.95). Those orders bring in about $198 (11 × $18), which leaves a gap of about −$152 before costs.

Order value is revenue, not profit. At an assumed 40% gross margin, the $198 returns about $79 of gross profit, so the shortfall against $350 is larger. The break-even cost per click at $18 and 2.5% is $0.45. To justify $0.80 per click, the conversion rate would need to reach about 4.4% ($0.80 ÷ $18).

## Which checks protect a Glovo Ads budget?

Use this checklist before and during the test:

1. Set up order tracking so each click can be matched to a completed order before launch.
2. Place a small test spend and confirm the tracking records orders before you scale up.
3. Build a negative keyword list with words such as free, cheap, DIY, recipe, coupon and jobs.
4. Set your targeting radius to the area you can deliver to within your promised time.
5. Review the hour-of-day report and move budget toward your strongest-converting hours.
6. Compare actual cost per click with break-even every week, and pause any campaign that stays above it after the test.

Weekly checks take time, and a marketing assistant can handle much of the reporting. [AI tools that can replace a marketing assistant](/blog/ai-tools-replace-marketing-assistant-local-business) covers the options for small teams.

## Should a local business run Glovo Ads?

Glovo Ads suits businesses where the math works at the level of a single order. Ask these three questions:

- Does your average order value cover your break-even cost per click, with room left after food and packaging costs?
- Can you deliver within the time your customers expect?
- Are there enough customers inside your delivery radius that the same ad does not keep reaching the same people?

If the first answer is no, improve order value before buying clicks, for example by bundling items. Businesses with long sales cycles, such as renovation firms, usually need other channels. Glovo is built for quick, repeat purchases, so judge it against order value rather than general brand reach.

## FAQ

### Can I estimate Glovo Ads cost before my first campaign?

Yes. Calculate break-even cost per click from your average order value and expected conversion rate, then set a test budget you can afford to lose. Label any unknown input as an assumption and replace it with real numbers after the first test.

### How long should a Glovo Ads test run?

Fourteen days is a practical test window, and the worked example above uses it. Early results can swing widely from day to day, so judge the campaign after the full period rather than after a single strong or weak day.

### What if I have no conversion data yet?

Use a conversion rate you can justify, mark it as an assumption, and calculate break-even from it. Once the test produces orders, swap in your measured conversion rate and recalculate. Until then, treat the result as a plan, not a forecast.

### Is Glovo Ads better than social media ads for a small restaurant?

It depends on order value and margin. Social campaigns can start at about $100 per campaign according to The Restaurant HQ, while Glovo spend is controlled by your daily budget. Compare cost per order from both channels using the same break-even logic before choosing.
