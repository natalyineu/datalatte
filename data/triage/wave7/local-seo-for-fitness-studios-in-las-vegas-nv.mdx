## How does the Google Map Pack decide which Las Vegas studio appears?

The Map Pack is the block of three or so local listings that appears above regular results for local queries. The Association of Fitness Studios describes it as listing relevant local businesses for queries like "personal training studios near me" ([How to Make Your Fitness Business Stand Out on Google, Association of Fitness Studios, secondary](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google)). Your Google Business Profile is the record that feeds that listing, and it shows hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews, and photos directly in search results ([Keepers Digital, secondary](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/)).

Google describes the Business Profile as free to set up on Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile, Canada](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). Ranking weight is less clear. An Ahrefs summary of a BrightLocal survey reports that 36% of SEOs in 2021 considered the Business Profile the most important ranking factor for the map pack, up from 23% in 2013 ([Ahrefs Local SEO guide, secondary](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo)). That is a survey of SEO professionals, not a Google algorithm disclosure, so treat it as a signal of where practitioners focus.

For a studio that has never completed its profile, the [fitness studio Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization) covers the setup in more detail.

## Which Google Business Profile details should a Las Vegas studio fill in first?

Start with the fields that describe the service. Choose a primary category that matches your main offer, then add specific class types where the profile allows. Write a description that names your service and your Las Vegas neighborhood once, in plain language. Repeated city names read as keyword stuffing and do not help a reader decide to book.

Photos matter. One vertical marketing guide recommends uploading at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes in session, and staff ([Vertical Impression, secondary](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026)). Use real photos from your own floor, not stock images, because the guide specifically says authentic.

Posts need a live status to appear. Google states that a post is public on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, while Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help, primary](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after each post, and publish on a regular schedule, such as a class offer or a schedule change. If you draft profile copy with software, the [AI for Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization) explains where those tools help and where a person still needs to check the result.

## How many reviews and photos does a studio need to compete?

A landscaping guide claims that businesses with more than 50 reviews and a 4.7+ average rating dominate top map pack spots, but it cites no source ([Accountability Now, secondary](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/)). Treat 50 reviews as a working benchmark for a competitive local market, not a rule Google has published. Google does say that reviews appear next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help, primary](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)), so the review count is visible to every searcher, not just to the algorithm.

Worked example. Assumption: the studio has 10 Google reviews today and a target of 50. The gap is 40. Over 13 weeks, about one quarter, that means 40 ÷ 13 ≈ 3.1 new reviews per week. Assumption: one in four customers who receive a request leaves a review. Then the studio needs about 12 to 13 review requests per week. If the studio has 6 photos today against a 15-photo minimum, the photo gap is 9, which is one shoot session plus a weekly upload.

Ask for reviews after a class or session, and let customers describe what they liked in their own words. Reply to each review in a few sentences that name the service you offer. Replies are public and tell readers how you handle feedback.

## How should a studio handle citations and location data?

Citations are mentions of your business name, address, and phone number on other websites. Keep those three details identical everywhere. "Street" in one listing and "St." in another, or a different phone format, can make your records look inconsistent. Fix the listings you already have before adding new directories. Start with the major platforms your customers already use, such as Google, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and Facebook, and then add industry directories where your members sign up.

Location data also feeds paid ads. Google states that location asset data in Google Ads comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, so a correction you make there flows into linked ad accounts ([About location assets, Google Ads Help, primary](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). If the profile route does not work for a multi-location business, you can select up to 10 Google Maps locations to link, and those locations must be yours or they may be disapproved ([Google Ads Help, primary](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)).

One landscaping firm reported an 18% increase in local pack appearances after cleaning up its name, address, and phone data. That is a single anecdote from a secondary source, so use it as a reason to audit your own listings, not as an expected result.

## Which keywords and website signals support the profile?

Your website should repeat the same service and location language your profile uses. Give each major service its own page, and use a title format such as "[Service] in Las Vegas, NV | [Studio name]." Embedding a Google Map on your contact page is a small, easy addition, and the [Google Maps embed guide for local businesses](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) shows the steps.

For keywords, pair the service name with "near me" and with neighborhood names your customers actually use. ## What does a 90-day local SEO plan look like for a studio?

Use this step checklist to sequence the work over one quarter.

1. Weeks 1 and 2: audit your Business Profile, fill in categories, descriptions, hours, and service lists, and fix name, address, and phone mismatches across listings.
2. Weeks 3 and 4: bring your photo count up to 15 or more, and set a posting schedule with a live-status check after each post.
3. Weeks 5 to 8: start a review request routine after each class, and reply to every review within a few days.
4. Weeks 9 to 12: update service pages and the map embed on your website, and check that your website uses the same service and location wording as your profile.
5. Week 13: record your Business Profile views and actions, check your map pack position for your main keywords, and decide what to repeat.

Track progress with Google Business Profile Insights, which shows how customers find and interact with your listing, and with Google Search Console, which shows organic clicks by page. Check map position monthly, using a private browser window to see results without personalization. Google does not publish a timeline for map movement, so judge progress over several months rather than weeks.

## FAQ

### How long does local SEO take for a fitness studio in Las Vegas?

Google does not publish a timeline. Track Business Profile Insights and your map position for your main keywords each month, and expect movement to take several months of consistent work.

### Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Google describes the Business Profile as free to set up on Google Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile, Canada](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)).

### Do Google Business Profile posts show right away?

Not always. A post appears publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).

### Should a studio also run Google Ads alongside local SEO?

They work differently. Local SEO builds your organic presence in the Map Pack, while Google Ads places paid listings. The [Google Ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/google-ads-for-fitness-studios-complete-2026-guide) explains how the paid side works.
