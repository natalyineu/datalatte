To set up retargeting, install a tracking tag or pixel on your website, build an audience from visitors or engaged followers within a fixed time window, and launch ads aimed only at that audience with a capped share of your budget. Google uses a remarketing tag, while Meta uses its Pixel, and each platform has its own audience rules. Meta's pixel only reaches people who visited your properties in the last 180 days, so your window has to fit inside that limit. The steps below cover install order, audience choices, budget split, and frequency limits.

## What Can Retargeting Actually Reach?

Retargeting shows ads to people who have already visited your website or interacted with your business. How far back you can reach depends on the platform. On Meta, retargeting through the pixel is limited to users who visited your properties within the last 180 days, according to [WasteNot](https://www.wastenot.io/blog/retargeting-facebook-ads) (secondary). Meta audiences can also come from engagement. [Adstellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/meta-ads-for-local-businesses) (secondary) recommends building retargeting audiences from people who engaged with your Page or Instagram profile in the past 30 days.

Privacy rules apply too. WasteNot states that the Meta pixel must be included in your website cookie policy if it is used to track user activity, under the GDPR (secondary). If you serve visitors in the UK, Australia, or Canada, check the privacy rules that apply in your country before you launch, and confirm your cookie banner loads before the tags fire.

Two practical points follow from this. First, your audience window cannot be longer than the platform allows. Second, a small site has a small audience, so the window you choose decides how many people the ads can reach at all.

## How Do You Install the Tags for Google and Meta?

Install the tags before you build any audience, because audiences only fill from the moment a tag starts firing. Google's setup runs through Google Tag Manager, and Meta's runs through the Pixel. Menu names change often, so check each platform's live help page for the current path.

1. Create a Google Tag Manager container, add its code to your site, and confirm it loads using preview mode.
2. In Google Ads, open the audience tools and create a remarketing tag, then add that tag inside Tag Manager and publish it.
3. In Meta Events Manager, create a Pixel, copy the base code, and install it through Tag Manager or your site platform's plugin.
4. Make a test visit to your site and confirm each tag shows as firing before you build any audience.

If you can only install one tag this week, start with the one that matches the platform where most of your ad budget will go. [Facebook Pixel vs Google Tag: Which to Install First?](/blog/facebook-pixel-vs-google-tag-which-to-install-first) walks through that choice.

## Which Audiences Should You Build First?

Start with the audiences that match a clear next action. A visitor who reached your booking or pricing page is a stronger signal than someone who landed on your homepage and left. Build the broad audience first, then add narrower ones once you have enough people in each.

1. Create a site-visitor audience for each window you plan to use, keeping Meta audiences inside the 180-day limit.
2. Split audiences by action, such as visited a booking page versus visited any page.
3. Add an engagement audience built from people who engaged with your Page or Instagram profile in the past 30 days.
4. Exclude people who already booked or bought, so ads do not reach existing customers.
5. Record the audience size for each list before launch, so you can judge whether the budget fits the audience.

Step 5 matters most for local businesses. A list of a few hundred people cannot absorb a large budget without the same people seeing the ad many times.

## How Should You Split Your Budget?

Adstellar's ecommerce guide suggests 60-70% of Meta budget for prospecting, 20-30% for retargeting, and 10% for retention, according to [Adstellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/meta-advertising-for-ecommerce-brands) (secondary). Lead Nurture Close recommends allocating 20% of contractor ad budget, shared across both platforms, to conversion retargeting, per [Lead Nurture Close](https://leadnurtureclose.com/facebook-ads-vs-google-ads-for-contractors/) (secondary). Both are starting points, not rules for every business.

Worked example, with every input labelled as an assumption:

- Monthly Meta budget: $1,000 (assumption)
- Split: 65% prospecting, 25% retargeting, 10% retention (assumption, inside Adstellar's ranges)
- Retargeting audience size: 800 people (assumption)
- Cost per thousand impressions (CPM): $12 (assumption)

Retargeting spend is $1,000 × 25% = $250. At $12 CPM, that buys about 20,833 impressions. Spread across 800 people, average frequency is about 26, far above the 3-4 fatigue marker. A frequency cap of 4 allows 800 × 4 = 3,200 impressions, which costs about $38 at $12 CPM. In this case the audience is the limit, not the budget. The fix is to widen the window, grow the list, or move most of the retargeting money into prospecting.

Where the rest of the budget should go depends on your channel mix. [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for Local Business](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) compares the two for small businesses.

## How Long Should the Retargeting Window Be?

Adstellar's ecommerce guide suggests a 7-day window may suit impulse-buy products, while higher-consideration items may use 30 or even 60 days (secondary). The window should match how long your customers take to decide. A quick-purchase product and a service that needs a quote are different cases, so the window should follow your sales cycle.

Whatever you choose, keep it inside the platform limit. On Meta's pixel, that limit is 180 days. Test a shorter window first, then extend it only if you see conversions from people who visited near the end of the current window.

## How Do You Keep Retargeting From Wearing People Out?

Frequency is the number to watch. Adstellar states that frequency above 3-4 in retargeting, and above 2 in prospecting, signals audience fatigue (secondary). The same guide says most ecommerce ads experience fatigue after 2-4 weeks of consistent delivery, especially in retargeting campaigns.

Plan a creative refresh on that cadence. Rotate at least two ad versions, check frequency every week, and pause an ad that keeps climbing past the cap while results fall. Repeated ads that look the same are the most likely reason a retargeting audience stops responding.

## FAQ

### Can you retarget people who visited more than 180 days ago on Meta?

Not through the Meta pixel. WasteNot states that retargeting via the pixel is limited to users who visited your properties within the last 180 days (secondary). Visitors from older periods need a different audience source, such as a customer list you upload.

### Do you need a cookie policy for retargeting?

WasteNot states that the Meta pixel must be included in your website cookie policy if it tracks user activity, under the GDPR (secondary). Check the privacy rules in your own country, since the requirements differ across the US, UK, Australia, and Canada.

### How much of the ad budget should go to retargeting?

No single figure applies to every business. Adstellar's ecommerce guide suggests 20-30% for retargeting (secondary), and Lead Nurture Close suggests 20% shared across platforms for contractor conversion retargeting (secondary). Start inside that range and adjust based on audience size and frequency.

### Is Meta's advertising training free?

Sascom's guide states that Meta's Blueprint certification covers Facebook and Instagram advertising at no cost, with certification upon completion (secondary). Check Meta's live Blueprint page for current course details before you plan around it.
