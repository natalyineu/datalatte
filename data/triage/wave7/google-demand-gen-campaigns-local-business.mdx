Should a local business run Google Demand Gen campaigns? Usually not before Search is working. Demand Gen reaches people who are browsing rather than searching, so it only makes sense when you already track a booking or call and can fund a test properly. Adwave's published budget ladder places Demand Gen alongside YouTube and Google Search at $30 a day, which gives you a benchmark to test against your own monthly budget. The practical question is whether your budget clears that level and whether you have a conversion to measure.

## Where Does Demand Gen Sit in Your Budget?

Adwave's published budget ladder opens mobile web, mobile apps, TV, YouTube, Demand Gen, Google Search, Facebook and Instagram at $30 a day ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/ctv-advertising-market-size)). Treat this as one vendor's ladder, not a Google rule. Its useful point is that many channels start at the same entry level, so a small account has to choose where its money goes.

Other planning figures are higher. Focus Digital, a secondary source, recommends a monthly budget of $2,300 for YouTube campaigns as of 2025 ([Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/minimum-google-ads-budget-by-industry-2025-guidelines/)). Across a 30-day month that is about $77 a day, so the two sources frame the budget question differently. Use both as context, then check Google's live Demand Gen help page for the current minimums in your account.

Read the table as arithmetic. Option A gives $15 a day overall, so a 30% share leaves about $4.50 a day for Demand Gen, far below Adwave's entry point. Option B reaches $30 a day in total, but a 30% split still leaves $9 a day for the test. Option C allows a larger test, though it still has to be split against Search.

## When Does Reach Beat Intent?

Demand Gen is a reach tool, and it competes for budget with campaigns that capture ready buyers. Google's own Performance Max page says campaigns are AI-powered and can reach customers across Search, YouTube, Maps and other Google surfaces based on the goals you set ([Google Ads](https://business.google.com/en-all/google-ads/)). For a local firm whose main goal is bookings, that automation is often the first thing to test. Our [Performance Max guide for local business](/blog/google-performance-max-for-local-business-complete-guide-2026) covers that setup.

Google also says multiple campaigns can run in the same account without creating a new one ([Google Ads](https://business.google.com/en-all/google-ads/)). That makes a test cheap to start, but not cheap to run. Google's Display help page describes reach across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190?hl=en)). Broad reach is easy to buy. Booked customers are the harder part.

Premium video is a different scale. NETINT, a secondary source, states that traditional connected TV campaigns typically demand production budgets exceeding $50,000 ([NETINT](https://netint.com/research/blog/the-gen-ai-revolution-in-ctv-advertising/)). That gap is one reason local firms usually test YouTube-style video before anything bigger.

## What Should a Local Business Do First?

Work through these steps before you publish a Demand Gen campaign:

1. Confirm that Search is already producing a tracked booking or call.
2. Divide your monthly Google Ads budget by 30 and compare the result with $30 a day.
3. List video and image assets you already own, such as finished work, a menu, or a class timetable.
4. Pick one conversion goal and count only that.
5. Run the test for a fixed period, then compare its cost per booking with your Search results.

If you pay per lead rather than per click, compare the test with [Google Local Service Ads for local businesses](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) before deciding where the money goes.

## How Do You Measure a Demand Gen Test?

Do not judge a Demand Gen campaign by clicks or impressions alone. Compare cost per booking or call against your Search campaigns over the same weeks. Watch branded searches for your business name during the test as a rough sign that people remember you, but treat that as your own inference, not a metric Google publishes.

Track assisted bookings in your analytics too. A visitor may see a video ad, then book later through search or by phone. If your budget is split across platforms, our comparison of [Google Ads and Facebook Ads for local business](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins) explains how to read those results side by side.

## FAQ

### Is Demand Gen the same as Performance Max?

No. Google's Performance Max page describes an AI-powered campaign that reaches customers across Search, YouTube, Maps and other surfaces based on your goals. Demand Gen is a separate campaign type in the Google Ads list, so compare the two in your own account before choosing.

### Does Demand Gen show on Google Maps?

Check the placement options in your campaign setup before assuming Maps delivery. Do not plan around Maps reach until you can see it in your own account.

### What is the minimum daily budget?

Adwave's published budget ladder starts at $30 a day for Demand Gen and several other channels. That is a vendor figure, not an official minimum. Check Google's live Demand Gen help page for current requirements.

### Can a coffee shop or salon start with Demand Gen?

Most should start with Search ads and a complete Google Business Profile. Keeping that profile active is a steady task, and our guide on [automating Google Posts for local business](/blog/how-to-automate-google-posts-local-business) covers one way to handle it.
