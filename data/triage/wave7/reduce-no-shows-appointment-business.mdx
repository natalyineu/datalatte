Automated appointment reminders are the most common fix for no-shows, and vendor pages put the reduction between 20% and 50%. Those figures come from marketing material rather than independent trials, so treat them as a reason to test, not a forecast. The practical playbook: send an automated text close to each appointment, give clients an easy way to reschedule, offer same-day rebooking, and compare your no-show rate before and after the change. ## Do Automated Reminders Really Reduce No-Shows?

Vendor pages say they do, but the figures differ. Adwave's med spa guide puts the reduction at 20 to 30% as of 2026. Its dental advertising page claims 30% to 50%. A salon guide from BonusQR says up to 40%, but cites no study for that number. A scheduling guide from GoConverso gives 60 to 70% and also names no source.

None of these figures is attributed to a controlled study of salons or gyms. The consistent message is direction: every vendor expects reminders to lower no-shows. The size of the effect for your business is something only your own booking data can show.

## Which Channel Should Send the Reminder?

Mariana Tek, a booking vendor for fitness studios, advises sending an SMS reminder a few hours before class to reduce no-shows. That timing fits a class schedule, where a client needs to act on the same day.

The Central California Alliance for Health tip sheet recommends reminding patients before the visit by phone, email, and/or text. GoConverso groups email and SMS reminders together. The sources do not rank channels against each other, so the best channel is the one your clients reply to. Automating the send is covered in our guide to [AI agents for appointment booking](/blog/ai-agent-for-appointment-booking-local-business).

## What Should the Reminder Say?

The Alliance tip sheet offers a no-show script that stresses how important it is to attend a scheduled appointment. It asks patients to call ahead if they need to reschedule or cancel, before the appointment time. A reminder can carry the same two points: confirm the slot, and make changing it easy.

Adwave's customer service guide describes an illustrative dental practice that sends two proactive reminders and offers same-day rebooking. The page states that this is an example, not a verified performance study. Use it as a structure to test, not as proof. Clients who miss anyway can be followed up with the [abandoned appointment win-back guide](/blog/abandoned-appointment-email-win-back-no-shows), and wellness businesses can adapt the same logic from the [wellness marketing strategy guide](/blog/wellness-business-marketing-strategy).

## Should You Take a Deposit or Card Hold?

Prepaid deposits are a feature vendors promote. Salon Today reports that MyTime's prepaid deposits and automated confirmations help multi-unit enterprises and franchises reduce last-minute cancellations and no-shows. That is a vendor claim aimed at larger operators, and it does not say how much deposits cost in lost bookings.

For a single-chair business, deposits add friction at the moment of booking. Set them up through your booking platform's live help page, state the policy in plain words at checkout, and watch whether new-client bookings drop after you introduce one.

## Worked Example: What Is a Reduction Worth?

All inputs below are assumptions for a small salon, not data from the sources.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Appointments per month | 400 (assumption) |
| Current no-show rate | 12%, so 48 missed (assumption) |
| Average ticket | $70 (assumption) |
| Reduction from reminders | 20%, the low end of the 20% to 30% range cited above (assumption) |
| Share of freed slots refilled | 50% (assumption) |

A 20% cut removes 9.6 no-shows a month. If half of those slots refill, that is 4.8 extra paid visits, or about $336 a month at $70. The sources do not price reminder messages, so check your booking software's messaging terms before assuming the cost.

## How Do You Test It Without Guessing?

Change one thing at a time and compare the same length of weeks before and after. The table below sets each option against what the sources say and what to watch.

| Option | What the sources say | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automated SMS a few hours before | Mariana Tek advises this for classes | Whether clients reply to texts |
| Phone, email and text | Alliance tip sheet recommends all three | Staff time for phone calls |
| Two reminders | Adwave's illustrative dental example uses two | Opt-out rate |
| Prepaid deposit | MyTime claims fewer cancellations for multi-unit operators | Lost new-client bookings |
| Same-day rebooking offer | Offered in Adwave's illustrative example | Whether freed slots refill |

## FAQ

### What is a normal no-show rate?

None of the sources gives an average for salons or gyms. The figures in this guide are vendor claims, so your own booking history is the only reliable baseline.

### Are the reminder reduction figures reliable?

Treat them as a range of claims. The vendors differ widely, and the salon claim cites no study. Measure your own rate before and after a change.

### Should I charge a deposit?

A vendor claims deposits reduce cancellations for larger operators. For a single business, test whether deposits cut no-shows without costing you new clients.

### Which channel should I use for reminders?

The sources point to a text close to the appointment, with phone and email as options. No source ranks them, so test the channel your clients answer.
