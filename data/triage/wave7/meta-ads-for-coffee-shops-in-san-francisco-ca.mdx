## How much should a coffee shop budget for Meta ads?

AdWave recommends planning $300 to $1,500 per month on Meta for an active café campaign ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes)). Its restaurant guide puts Meta at $500 to $2,000 per month for most single-location restaurants ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/restaurant-advertising-complete-marketing-guide)). A coffee shop testing paid social can reasonably start near the lower end of that range and scale only after tracking shows results.

For budget shares, AdWave's coffee shop mix allocates 45% of the marketing budget to Instagram and Meta Ads in the pre-launch and first year, dropping to 30% in years two to four. Treat those shares as a starting allocation for your own plan, not a fixed rule.

## Which ad formats work best for coffee shops on Meta?

Video is the format to lead with. Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, as cited by AdWave, says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram ([AdWave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners)). For cafés, that means latte art, the morning rush and real customers rather than stock photos. The [Instagram Reels guide for coffee shops](/blog/instagram-reels-for-coffee-shops) covers how to film and edit that footage.

AdWave's week-two plan suggests Reels ads with a "first drink on us" offer at $30 to $50 per day, using authentic content such as latte art video, morning atmosphere and customer photos ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes)). At 30 days, that range works out to $900 to $1,500 per month (a calculation from the daily figures, not a separate quote).

Ad creative needs an organic base. The Restaurant HQ coffee shop guide recommends posting on social media at least once a week, ideally one to two times per day ([The Restaurant HQ, secondary](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/)). Ads pointing to an empty or inactive profile waste the click, so keep posting steady while the paid campaign runs. The [coffee shop Instagram strategy](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-marketing-strategy) explains how to plan that rhythm.

## How wide should the ad radius be around your café?

AdWave's example for a coffee shop runs Facebook and Instagram ads for a new fall latte to everyone within a five-mile radius of the store ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-channel-marketing-approach)). Its week-two Reels plan uses a tighter one-to-three-mile radius ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes)). San Francisco's neighbourhoods are dense and walkable, so starting at one to three miles and widening only if reach runs out is a sensible test. That is an assumption to check against your own results.

Instagram ads are bought through the same system as Facebook ads. Instagram is owned by Meta, so a single campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook or both ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business)). The [step-by-step Instagram ads playbook](/blog/instagram-ads-for-coffee-shops-step-by-step-playbook) walks through the setup screens.

## What does a $600 monthly budget return? A worked example

The table below applies AdWave's dense-urban cost-per-first-visit band to a budget. Every input except the band is an assumption you should replace with your own numbers.

Assumptions: monthly Meta budget of $600; average spend per visit of $7; each new customer makes three visits in the first three months. These are assumptions, not sourced figures.

| Cost per first visit | New customers from $600 | 3-month revenue per customer | Estimated 3-month revenue | Revenue per $1 spent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $5 (assumption: low band) | 120 | $21 | $2,520 | $4.20 |
| $10 (assumption: midpoint) | 60 | $21 | $1,260 | $2.10 |
| $15 (assumption: high band) | 40 | $21 | $840 | $1.40 |

Revenue is not profit. Once coffee cost of goods is subtracted, the margin on $840 or $1,260 will be lower. At $21 per customer, the budget breaks even on revenue only if the cost per first visit is near $21, so the result depends heavily on repeat visits. Track real repeat visits before deciding whether to scale.

## What tracking should be in place before spending?

Install the Meta Pixel on the website pages that matter, such as the contact form, booking confirmation or online order page. Set a conversion event, such as a Lead event on a form submission, so Ads Manager can optimise toward it. Add UTM parameters to every ad URL so Google Analytics can also see Meta-sourced visits. Without these, the only numbers available are impressions and reach, which say little about visits or sales.

Ads Manager is the central place to run campaigns across Instagram and Facebook ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising)). Once leads arrive, a follow-up sequence matters. The [marketing automation guide for local coffee shops](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-coffee-shops) covers how to respond to them without manual effort.

Also answer common questions in the Google Business Profile Q&A before the ads drive traffic. The Per Diem guide recommends addressing questions such as free Wi-Fi, dairy-free options and whether dogs are allowed on the patio ([Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)).

## FAQ

### How much does a Meta ad campaign cost per month for a coffee shop?

AdWave recommends planning $300 to $1,500 per month on Meta for an active café campaign, and lists Meta at $500 to $2,000 for most single-location restaurants ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-coffee-shops-cafes)). Start with the budget you can track properly rather than the top of the range.

### Should a coffee shop run ads on Facebook, Instagram or both?

One campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook or both, because both are owned by Meta and managed in Meta Ads Manager ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business)). Running both gives wider reach for the same audience, while Instagram alone suits a visual, Reels-led offer.

### What does a click cost on Meta?

AdWave gives typical Meta costs of $5 to $15 per thousand impressions and $1 to $3 per click ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-small-business-guide)). Check the live cost per click in your own Ads Manager results, since costs vary by audience and season.

### Is there free training for Meta ads?

A secondary source says Meta's Blueprint certification covers Facebook and Instagram advertising at no cost and includes a certificate on completion ([SASCOM247, secondary](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/)). Check Meta's own Blueprint page for current course availability before enrolling.
