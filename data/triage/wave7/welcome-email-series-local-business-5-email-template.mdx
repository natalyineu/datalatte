A welcome email series for a local business usually runs 3 to 5 emails over the first week. Adwave cites an average open rate of 83.63% for welcome emails, against 42.35% for typical marketing emails ([Adwave, email marketing metrics](https://adwave.com/resources/email-marketing-metrics-open-rates-and-click-rates-explained)). Send the first email as soon as someone signs up, space the rest over several days, and place any discount in the third or fourth email.

This article gives a timed template for cafes, salons, groomers and studios, with a worked example built on assumption-labelled inputs. Sources disagree on exact timing, so treat the schedule as a starting point and check it against your own numbers.

## How Many Emails Should a Welcome Series Have?

Most sources land on three to five emails. Adwave describes a welcome series as typically 3-5 emails sent over the first week ([Adwave, email marketing getting started](https://adwave.com/resources/email-marketing-for-small-business-getting-started-in-2026)). OptiMonk recommends 3-5 emails over 7-14 days for new subscribers ([OptiMonk, email frequency](https://www.optimonk.com/email-marketing-frequency)). Adwave's simpler version is a three-part series ([Adwave, email list segmentation](https://adwave.com/resources/email-list-segmentation-sending-the-right-message-to-the-right-people)).

A series also beats a single message. Stripo's welcome email statistics, as reported by Adwave, say an introductory series of two or more messages can increase revenue by up to 51% compared with one welcome email (secondary).

Start with three emails. Add the fourth and fifth only for subscribers who clicked earlier messages. Adwave's lead magnet guide makes the same case for a short sequence: deliver the resource, help the subscriber use it, then bridge to the next step ([Adwave, lead magnets](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-create-a-lead-magnet-that-builds-your-email-list)).

## What Timing Should Each Email Follow?

Sources agree on the early days but differ on the later ones. The table below combines the Adwave schedules and the OptiMonk window into one template. The Day 0 timing for email 1 is an assumption, since the sources describe the series as starting at signup.

| Email | Timing from signup | Job | Source basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Day 0 (assumption) | Deliver what the signup promised | Sent to every new subscriber ([Adwave, email list from zero](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-build-an-email-list-from-zero-without-buying-lists)) |
| 2 | Day 2 | Your story or one key piece of advice | Adwave, Day 2 example ([email automation](https://adwave.com/resources/email-automation-for-small-business-the-campaigns-to-set-up-first)) |
| 3 | Day 4 | A useful resource or best seller; discount can go here | Adwave Day 4 example; discount in email 3 or 4 ([Adwave, welcome sequences](https://adwave.com/resources/welcome-email-sequences-that-turn-subscribers-into-buyers)) |
| 4 | Day 6 or Day 7 | Customer proof, such as a real review | Adwave Day 6 and Day 7 examples |
| 5 | Day 7 to 14 | Invitation to book, for engaged subscribers only | Within the 7-14 day window ([OptiMonk](https://www.optimonk.com/email-marketing-frequency)) |

Adwave advises spreading welcome content over several days rather than overwhelming new subscribers with information ([Adwave, real estate marketing ideas](https://adwave.com/resources/real-estate-marketing-ideas)). Onboarding frameworks follow the same logic, moving through welcome, educate, engage and success phases ([Adwave, customer onboarding](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-onboarding-creating-a-great-first-experience-after-purchase)).

For a salon or café, email 4 is the natural place for a real customer review. Use only reviews customers have given permission to share. For drafting first versions of each email, see [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing).

## Which Metrics Should You Track?

Open rates are a weak guide. The sources do not agree on them. Adwave cites 83.63% for welcome emails, and reports a 91.43% welcome open rate from Campaign Monitor (secondary). Use open rates only to compare your own emails over time.

Track conversion instead. Adwave cites a 3.02% conversion rate for a well-tuned welcome series, citing a third-party analysis ([Adwave, welcome sequences](https://adwave.com/resources/welcome-email-sequences-that-turn-subscribers-into-buyers)). For a local business, a conversion is a booking, a visit with a code, or a reply. Wisepops data cited by Adwave found a welcome offer for new visitors converted at 8.30%, against 5.09% for returning visitors (secondary).

Lapsed customers need their own track. Adwave's loyalty guide recommends building three automated flows first: welcome, repeat-purchase and lapsed-customer ([Adwave, customer retention](https://adwave.com/resources/customer-retention-strategies-that-cost-less-than-new-acquisition)). A salon can send a win-back series to clients who have not booked in 60 or more days ([Brenton Way, salon marketing](https://brentonway.com/blog/beauty-salon-marketing-ideas)).

## What Does a Worked Example Look Like?

All inputs in this example are **assumptions**, except the conversion rate, which is a third-party benchmark from Adwave and not a guarantee for your business.

| Input | Value | Label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New subscribers per month | 80 | Assumption |
| Welcome series conversion rate | 3.02% | Benchmark (Adwave, third-party) |
| Average first order | $30 | Assumption |

At these inputs, 80 subscribers at 3.02% gives about 2.4 buyers a month. At $30 each, that is about $72 a month, or roughly $870 a year. If real conversion is half the benchmark, the figure falls to about $36 a month. The calculation is simple, and the result depends almost entirely on your own conversion, so measure it from the first month.

## Does a Welcome Series Replace Other Channels?

No. A welcome series handles the first contact well, but it does not cover every customer. Adwave's onboarding and loyalty guidance treat it as one stage in a longer relationship. Winback messages appear later in the journey: one service business guide starts a three-email nurture sequence on Day 20 or later ([Winback Engine, reactivation guide](https://winbackengine.com/blog/customer-reactivation-guide/)).

Confirm consent rules for your country before sending. The sources cited here do not cover them, so check your local rules or the platform's live help page.

## FAQ

### How long should a welcome email series be?

Most sources suggest 3 to 5 emails over the first week, or 7 to 14 days. Start with three and add later emails only for people who clicked.

### When should the discount appear?

Adwave recommends placing a discount or special deal in the third or fourth email of the series ([Adwave, welcome sequences](https://adwave.com/resources/welcome-email-sequences-that-turn-subscribers-into-buyers)).

### Does a welcome series beat one welcome email?

Stripo's statistics, as reported by Adwave, say a series of two or more messages can increase revenue by up to 51% compared with one welcome email (secondary).

### What open rate should I expect?

Sources range from 42.35% for typical marketing emails to 83.63% and 91.43% for welcome emails. Use them as benchmarks, and compare your own sends over time.

### Can the same series work for lapsed customers?

Use a separate series. A service business guide suggests a three-email nurture sequence from Day 20 onward, and a salon guide targets clients who have not booked in 60 or more days.
