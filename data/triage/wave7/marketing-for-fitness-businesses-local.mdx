A local fitness business gets most of its members from a few places: a complete Google Business Profile, a clear first-visit offer, referrals from current members, and follow-up that stops people quietly leaving. Distance matters more than many owners expect, since one industry guide ranks proximity as the top factor in gym selection. Wellhub's 2026 survey found 86% of gym and studio owners grew their membership base, but growth alone does not show profit. This guide covers the main channels, a referral cost example, and the numbers behind each choice.

## How Crowded Is the Market for a Studio?

In the Wellhub survey, 95% of owners said competition has intensified over the past three years, and 40% plan to open new locations in the next 12 months. The [Health & Fitness Association](https://www.healthandfitness.org/86-of-gym-and-studio-owners-grew-membership-this-year-even-as-costs-and-competition-rise/) reports these findings as a secondary source of the Wellhub report. The survey covered 662 owners across 11 countries, so it is not a US-only figure.

Treat these numbers as a sign of direction, not a count of rivals near your address. The same survey found 40% of owners say revenue is growing faster than costs, while 37% say costs are growing faster. A studio that tracks its own cost per new member can judge its position better than one relying on industry headlines.

## Why Does Distance Decide Which Gym Members Pick?

Vertical Impression, a marketing agency, states that proximity is the number one factor in gym selection, citing studies of gym membership behaviour. The same [guide](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026) says people leave gyms that require more than a 15 to 20 minute commute. This is a secondary source, so check the underlying studies before relying on the exact threshold for planning.

For a studio, the practical step is to set your paid reach from your real member addresses. Pull the postcodes of current members and count how many live within a 15 minute drive. That count shows how big your local pool is before you spend anything, and it tells you where a local ad should stop.

## How Should a Studio Set Up Its Google Business Profile?

The Association of Fitness Studios explains that Google's Map Pack lists relevant local businesses for queries such as personal training studios near me. Its [article on standing out on Google](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google) makes local visibility the main goal for a studio's search presence. Getting into that block depends on a profile that answers what a searcher wants to know: is this place close, and is it open when they can train?

Keep the category, hours, photos and booking link accurate. Update class times as soon as the timetable changes. Google's naming and review rules sit in its help pages, and they change, so check the live text before you edit the listing. ## Which Social Channels Work for Promoting Classes?

A guide from Appointy, published in December 2020, notes that Instagram and Facebook let businesses add a Book Now button to their profile. The date matters, since platform features change, but a booking button still removes a step between seeing a class and reserving a place. The same guide advises listing 15 to 20 businesses or brands to study before starting, then preparing at least 5 to 10 posts in each content category in advance for busy days.

Mariana Tek, a studio software vendor, advises posting short-form video three to five times a week at a steady cadence rather than in bursts. Its suggested class highlight clips run 15 to 30 seconds. ## How Do You Keep Members Past Their First Months?

Retention is where marketing spend pays back. Vertical Impression, citing Accelo's research, states that increasing customer retention by just 5% can boost profits by 25 to 95%. That figure comes from general business research rather than fitness studios, so test it against your own numbers before using it in a budget.

Mariana Tek suggests win-back campaigns for members who have not booked in 30 or 60 days, and an SMS reminder a few hours before class to reduce no-shows. For trial visitors, it advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades fast after a pop-up ends. The [email campaigns guide for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-email-marketing-campaigns) covers the message sequence in detail.

People are more willing to join a list when the value is clear. StackAdapt, citing a BCG study from 2022, reports that 90% of consumers say they would share personal information for clear value in return, while 57% believe companies sell their personal data. Tell new members what they get for their email address. Use the [email marketing guide for fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-fitness-studio) for setup.

## How Should a Pre-Sale or Referral Offer Be Priced?

Health Club Consultants, in an article published in January 2018, describes a three-month pre-sale and says the best offer should be at the start of it. The same article advises changing the pre-sale offer every two weeks or month, so staff can tell prospects the price is going up. Use the timing logic, but only send a price-rise message when the price will actually rise.

Referrals are another lever. AdWave, a vendor, suggests paying trainers $50 to $150 per successful member referral. It also advises planning $300 to $2,500 per month for influencer and trainer-driven marketing, which it says typically costs $30 to $120 per new member. Any trainer doing this work should hold a certification, since the US Bureau of Labor Statistics notes personal trainers usually must be certified before working with clients or gym members.

## What Does a Referral Budget Look Like?

The inputs below are assumptions for one studio, chosen to sit inside the vendor ranges above. Replace each one with your own figures before you decide anything.

| Monthly referral spend | $300 |
| Cost per new member from referrals | $75 |
| Membership revenue per member over the stay | $900 |
| Revenue per $1 of referral payout | $12 |

These figures are revenue, not profit. Before treating $12 as a return, subtract the cost of serving the member, including class time and trainer pay. If average months stayed drops to 3 (assumption), revenue per member falls to $450, still six times the $75 payout, but the margin shrinks once costs are included. Retention changes the result more than the payout does.

## What Should a Studio Do This Month?

1. Pull the postcodes of current members and count how many live within a 15 minute drive.
2. Check that your Business Profile shows correct hours, classes and a booking link.
3. Set win-back messages for members who have not booked in 30 or 60 days.
4. Write a referral payout inside the $50 to $150 range, then test whether it lowers your cost per new member.
5. Price any pre-sale so the best offer runs at the start.

## FAQ

### How far should a local ad reach around a studio?

Base the radius on your member addresses. Vertical Impression reports that people leave gyms with commutes over 15 to 20 minutes, so a 15 minute drive is a sensible starting point to test, not a fixed rule.

### Is the Wellhub survey representative of US studios?

No. It covered 662 gym and studio owners across 11 countries. Use it to understand the direction of the market, and use your own cost and retention figures for decisions.

### How much should I pay trainers for referrals?

AdWave suggests $50 to $150 per successful referral. Set your own figure by working out revenue per member and average months stayed, then test it against your cost per new member.

### Do personal trainers need certification?

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics says personal trainers usually must be certified before they begin working with clients or with members of a gym or health club. Check any additional state or local requirements that apply to your studio.
