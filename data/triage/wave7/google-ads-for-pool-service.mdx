Under one set of assumed numbers, a weekly pool customer costs about $269 in Google Ads: a $5.50 click, a 6.2% lead rate and one new customer for every three leads. Those inputs are illustrations, not measured results, and your own figures will differ. What decides whether the spend pays is how long a weekly customer stays. With a $150 monthly fee at a 40% margin (both assumptions), that example repays its ad cost in about 4.5 months. This guide shows how to run the calculation with your own numbers, then set up the campaign, track calls and find the break-even bid.

## How Should a Pool Service Campaign Be Structured?

Build the account around the services you sell, not around one general "pool" campaign. Built-Right Digital describes building its campaigns around specific replacement services so the budget stays on profitable jobs instead of generic inquiries, and the same logic applies to pool work.

| Ad group | Example keywords | Send clicks to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly service | pool cleaning service, weekly pool maintenance, pool service near me | Recurring plans page |
| Green pool | green pool cleaning, algae removal pool | One-off cleanup page |
| Opening and closing | pool opening service, pool closing service | Seasonal page |
| Repair | pool pump repair, pool heater repair | Repair page, only if you do repairs |

Keep weekly plans and one-off jobs apart. A weekly customer and a one-time green pool clean have different values, so their cost per lead should be judged separately. Use phrase and exact match at first. Broad match casts a wider net than a small budget can usually monitor.

## Which Keywords and Negatives Should You Start With?

Start with service-plus-town terms and the weekly phrases in the table. Seasonal terms can be worth testing, but judge them on your own leads rather than on anyone else's averages.

Add negatives on day one. Useful starting terms include diy, how to, tips, robot, supplies, chemicals, store, jobs, hiring, course, certification, salary, builder and construction. Remove any you need for real work, such as repairs or construction, if you offer those services.

A managed window-replacement service advertises negative keyword lists of 200 to 500 terms, updated weekly, based on search term analysis. A pool account starts much smaller, but the habit is the same: every irrelevant search term you block is budget kept for a route stop.

## Which Locations Should You Target?

Google says the cost of ads depends on industry, target audience and the keywords chosen. Location is one more lever in the same cost picture. Sunnystorm's agency blog notes that cost per click varies by where the advertiser is located, with big cities more competitive and more expensive.

Target the areas your trucks actually cover, and exclude the ones you will not drive to. Then review which areas produce enquiries and which produce clicks with no calls. Dense routes are usually more profitable than scattered ones, because a customer two towns away can cost more in fuel and time than the margin on the job.

## How Do You Track Calls and Form Enquiries?

Pool customers often phone, so call tracking is not optional. Without it, you cannot tell which keyword produced the job. Decide what length of call counts as a real enquiry, check it against your own call log, and treat short calls as a separate category.

Track form submissions through the thank-you page. The ad platform sees the click and the call, but it cannot see which enquiry became a route stop. Log each lead's outcome in a simple sheet, with date, source keyword, service, quote and whether they signed up.

Winback Engine's guide recommends reviewing 10 to 15 percent of call recordings each week as part of quality assurance. For a small team, that review shows whether callers are being quoted properly, which affects the close rate in the payback calculation below. If most enquiries are calls, a [call-only campaign](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns-service-businesses) can suit a business that answers during advertised hours.

## What Does the Payback Math Look Like?

The example below uses a $1,000 monthly budget. Every input is an assumption for illustration, and the scenarios bracket the 6.2% lead rate used in the opening. Replace the inputs with your own before making any decision.

Assumed inputs for the base case:

- Monthly budget: $1,000 (assumption)
- Cost per click: $5.50 (assumption), giving about 182 clicks
- Click to lead rate: 6.2% (assumption), giving 11.3 leads and a cost per lead of about $89
- Lead to customer rate: 33% (assumption), giving 3.7 new weekly customers and an ad cost of about $269 each
- Monthly fee: $150 at a 40% gross margin (assumptions), giving $60 margin per month
- Months to repay: $269 divided by $60, about 4.5 months

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $6.80 | $5.50 | $4.20 |
| Clicks | 147 | 182 | 238 |
| Click to lead rate (assumption) | 4% | 6.2% | 8% |
| Leads | 5.9 | 11.3 | 19 |
| Cost per lead | $170 | $89 | $52.50 |
| Lead to customer rate (assumption) | 25% | 33% | 40% |
| New weekly customers | 1.5 | 3.7 | 7.6 |
| Ad cost per customer | $680 | $269 | $131 |
| Months of margin to repay (assumption: $60 per month) | 11.3 | 4.5 | 2.2 |

In the tough case, a customer has to stay over eleven months just to repay the ad cost. Before adding budget there, shrink the area, tighten keywords or improve the close rate.

Your break-even bid follows from the target: maximum cost per click equals the most you will pay per customer, multiplied by the lead rate and the close rate. At $300 per customer, 6.2% and 33%, that is about $6.14. Above that bid, the average customer costs more than the margin will repay.

Local Services Ads can change the cost picture, but the lead cost depends on the category. ## How Often Should You Optimise the Account?

Review the search terms report weekly. INSIDEA's guide recommends doing this weekly during the first month of a campaign, and Define Digital's five-step strategy recommends weekly review to refine targeting and reduce irrelevant clicks. Keep that rhythm after the first month for a small account.

1. Read the search terms report and add negatives for irrelevant searches.
2. Pause keywords that have spent well past your target cost per lead with no leads.
3. Reduce spend in locations whose cost per lead runs well above the rest.
4. Test one new headline at a time, and give it enough leads to judge.
5. Increase the budget only where cost per customer is already on target.

Also keep the landing page matched to the ad. A weekly-service click should land on the recurring plans page, with a tap-to-call number, a short quote form and clear pricing. Serving a generic home page wastes the click you paid for.

## Why Should You Chase Repeat Customers as Well?

Winback Engine's guide says reactivation campaigns typically cost 5 to 10 times less per converted customer than new acquisition, with contact costs of $5 to $20 against $50 to $200 for new customers. That is a vendor comparison, not a pool benchmark, but it points to a cheap source of work: your former clients and lapsed routes.

A related rule of thumb, from LivePlan's guide, says customer lifetime value should be more than three times the customer acquisition cost. That rule was written for SaaS companies, so treat it as a check rather than a target. For weekly plans, it is a useful question: does a customer's expected months of margin cover the cost of winning them, with room to spare for the ones who leave early? ## FAQ

### How much should a pool service company spend on Google Ads?

No source here gives a pool-specific budget. The $1,000 monthly figure in this guide is an assumption. Use the break-even formula with your own fee, margin, close rate and cost per click to see what your numbers support.

### Are Local Services Ads cheaper than search ads for pool companies?

Lead cost varies by category. Ryze's figures show routine maintenance leads well below emergency plumbing leads, but that is plumbing, not pool service. Check Google's current eligibility list for your country and category before deciding.

### Which keywords should I start with?

Begin with service-plus-town terms, weekly service phrases and green pool cleaning, in phrase and exact match. Add a negative list from the first day, and judge each ad group on its own leads.

### How long before I know whether ads work?

No source gives a fixed period. Judge the account on cost per customer once it has produced enough leads to be meaningful, and check the payback calculation with your real close rate and retention before deciding to add budget.
