A New Orleans hair salon ranks in the Google Maps pack by treating its Google Business Profile as the core asset. The pack is the top three business listings in local search results, according to [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today). Complete every profile field accurately, keep photos and posts current, collect reviews at a steady pace, and keep your name, address and phone number identical across the web. ## What does a New Orleans salon need to appear in the Google Maps pack?

Adwave states that the top three businesses in the map pack are pulled directly from their Google Business Profile listings ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business)). That makes the profile the working asset, not a side listing. Adwave also reports that GBP signals were 32% of local pack ranking weight in 2025, citing a multi-location benchmark ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit)).

Treat that figure as directional. The same weighting appears on another Adwave page without a named source ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). Google states that reviews appear next to a business's profile in Maps and Search ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)), so reviews are visible to searchers as well as to ranking systems.

## Which Google Business Profile fields should a New Orleans salon fill in first?

Start with the primary category. Adwave recommends checking the top three to five businesses in local map results for a service search and noting which category they use ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones)). Adwave also says the primary category is the number one ranking factor for entering the Local Pack, though it does not name the analysis behind that claim.

Next, write the description. Adwave's guide notes that a Business Profile description allows 750 characters ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). Lead with the service and the New Orleans neighbourhood, then state what makes the salon different. Keep the wording natural rather than repeating keywords.

Hours, phone number and address must be accurate. Adwave says they should be 100% accurate ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers)). If you work from home or serve customers at their location, you can hide the street address and be designated a Service Area Business ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). A salon with a walk-in front desk should keep its address visible.

## How many photos, posts and reviews does a salon need?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, saying a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers)). A separate Adwave to-do list suggests at least 10 ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). Use 15 as the target and refresh photos as the salon's services change.

Posts need a live status to count. Google says a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, and Pending or Not approved posts do not show ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after publishing. Adwave advises at least one new post a week to keep the profile fresh ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers)).

Reviews carry the strongest numeric signal in this set. A third-party analysis of 2 million Business Profile pages found businesses with over 200 Google reviews significantly more likely to appear in the local pack ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business)). That is a correlation, not a guarantee, and the [review benchmarks for Denver hair salons](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026) show how the spread varies by market.

## Why does NAP consistency matter for salon citations?

NAP means name, address and phone number. Adwave says the NAP must be exactly the same on the website, the Business Profile and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses)). Small formatting differences count. The NAP on the website must match the listing right down to punctuation and whether "St." or "Street" is used ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)).

Audit existing listings before adding new ones. A mismatched listing is harder to fix once several directories have copied it. Check your Business Profile, website footer and booking pages side by side, then correct each directory to match the Business Profile exactly.

## Which keywords and service terms should a salon target?

Google's AI-driven answers draw on business profile data, website content and reviews, according to an Accrisoft summary cited by Adwave ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a)). The practical consequence is that service names must appear consistently across the profile, the website and the review text customers write.

Use neighbourhood names in the description and on service pages, where they are accurate. For paid search terms, the [best Google Ads keywords for hair salons](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026) cover the commercial side. Organic and paid terms should use the same service vocabulary so the salon's messaging stays consistent.

## How do you plan a realistic review target? (worked example)

The table below compares each sourced benchmark with an example salon. Every figure in the last column is an assumption for illustration.

| Signal | Sourced benchmark | Example salon (assumption) |
|---|---|---|
| Google reviews | Over 200 linked to higher local pack odds | 40 reviews |
| Photos | 15 recommended | 9 uploaded |
| Posts | At least 1 per week | 1 every 2 weeks |
| Description | Up to 750 characters | 600 characters |

Worked calculation for reviews, using assumed inputs:

- Current reviews: 40 (assumption)
- Benchmark target: 200 (Adwave citing Blogging Wizard, secondary)
- New reviews collected per week: 6 (assumption)

Weeks needed = (200 - 40) / 6 = 26.7, so about 27 weeks. At 10 new reviews a week, the same gap closes in 16 weeks. The pace of review requests, not the benchmark itself, is the variable the salon controls. Ask for reviews after each appointment and mention specific services in the request, since customers write the text that search systems read.

## Where else should a salon be listed beyond Google?

Adwave makes several Apple Maps claims that its own page does not attribute to a source: over 1.5 billion affluent iOS users, and an 88% versus 12% usage split between Google Maps and Apple Maps ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring)). Use these as context only. The same page says Apple Business Connect has a 7% share in a maps-only comparison and that Apple Maps Actions can connect booking services to a profile.

Booksy states that Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search or Maps in under 30 seconds ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing)). Check whether your booking tool connects to that feature. For a salon that already runs a [Google Maps embed](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) on its contact page, the embed and the profile should show the same address and hours.

## How should a salon measure local SEO progress?

Adwave lists six local search metrics worth tracking: Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift over time ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent)). Record them monthly so you can see which changes moved which metric. Adwave also says a business may begin ranking for its own name a few weeks after verification, which is a vendor estimate rather than a guarantee ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)).

If paid search runs alongside organic work, Google says location asset data in Google Ads comes directly from Google Maps and the Business Profile, and corrections made there flow into linked accounts ([Google](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). ## FAQ

### Does a Google Business Profile for a New Orleans salon cost anything?

Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up on Google Search and Maps ([Google](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). Paid placements such as Google Ads are separate and optional.

### How many reviews does a salon need to compete in the map pack?

A third-party analysis reported that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business)). It is a correlation from one dataset, so use 200 as a planning target rather than a threshold.

### Should a salon hide its street address on its profile?

Only if it works from home or serves customers at their location. In that case the address can be hidden and the business designated a Service Area Business ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). A walk-in salon should keep the address visible.

### How long does it take a new profile to rank for its own name?

Adwave's guide says a business often starts ranking for its own name a few weeks after verification. That is the vendor's own estimate, so check your profile's visibility monthly rather than relying on a fixed date ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)).
