Mailchimp can send a rebooking email to every client who is due for a groom, but the setup works only when three things are right: the plan you pick, the three emails you write, and the consent you hold for each address. Start with one nudge for overdue clients, add a short welcome for new clients, and track bookings for 30 days before adding anything else. The figures below are assumptions, so swap in your own.

## Which Mailchimp plan do you need for rebooking emails?

Choose the plan after you know what you will send. A manual newsletter needs very little, while an automated rebooking flow needs a tier that includes automation. Mailchimp's current pricing page lists which features each tier includes and the price at your contact count, so check it there. Prices change.

For the budget below, this guide uses an assumed $20 a month as the plan cost. That is an assumption, not a Mailchimp price.

## How much does one extra booking need to cover?

Start from profit, not revenue. Assumptions: an average groom of $75, a gross margin after supplies and time of 60%, so $45 profit per booking, and 60 overdue clients at any time. Break-even on a $20 plan is $20 divided by $45, about 0.44 bookings a month. One extra booking covers the plan.

The manual row shows the trade-off. Without a paid plan you still send the email, but you handle timing yourself, which takes roughly an hour a month at these volumes (assumption).

Look at the lifetime value too. A client who books one groom every 6 weeks gives about 8 grooms a year (assumption), or about $600 at $75. If that client stays a year, one reactivated client covers a full year of a $20 plan several times over.

## Which three emails should a groomer send first?

Skip the monthly newsletter at first. Three messages do most of the work, and each needs a clear trigger.

The due-for-a-groom nudge comes first. Send it when a client's last visit is past their usual gap, for example 5 weeks for a dog that normally books every 6 (assumption). Keep the body to two sentences, the dog's name, three real open times and one booking button.

The welcome comes second. When a new client books, send one email covering drop-off and pick-up times, what to bring and your cancellation policy. Written rules reduce confusion at the door.

The win-back comes third. For clients who have gone quiet, send one honest message offering a held slot, then stop. Do not send a sequence of reminders to someone who has ignored the first.

## When should the rebooking email go out?

Booksy's blog suggests sending automated SMS or email rebooking reminders 48 hours after a client declines to rebook in the salon. That advice is written for salons, so treat 48 hours as a starting test rather than a rule for groomers.

Timing also depends on your capacity. Mobile groomers usually do fewer dogs per day than a busy salon because of drive time, setup and one-on-one handling. A nudge that lists three real open times protects a tight schedule better than a generic "book now".

Seasonality matters as well. AdWave notes that November and December are traditionally a very busy time for groomers, sometimes too busy. Send the nudge for early-winter slots before the rush, not after the diary fills.

## How do you keep your list legal and clean?

This is general information, not legal advice. Email only clients who gave you their address, ideally with a clear tick box at booking that says what you will send. Make every message easy to opt out of, and act on opt-outs straight away.

Check the rules in your own country before a large send. Consent and opt-out rules differ across the US, UK, Australia and Canada, and this article does not summarize them. Read your regulator's own guidance. Never import a list you bought.

## FAQ

### Is Mailchimp's free plan enough for a pet groomer?

It can work for manual newsletters to a small list. Check Mailchimp's pricing page for the contact and send limits on the tier you use. If you want an automated rebooking flow, confirm that automation is included in the tier before you pay.

### How many emails should a groomer send each client?

No fixed rule applies. Tie emails to the dog's grooming cycle: one when the dog is due, one when it is overdue, then stop. Test whether that rhythm works for your clients and adjust from your booking data.

### Should I text or email clients to rebook?

Use both for different jobs. Lutily's secondary figures suggest texts get far more opens, so use them for short-notice slots. Use email for planning ahead and for the due-for-a-groom nudge.

### Do I need a lawyer to send marketing emails?

Not for routine messages to clients who gave you their address, but read your regulator's guidance on consent and opt-out. For a large or unusual send, ask a qualified adviser first.
