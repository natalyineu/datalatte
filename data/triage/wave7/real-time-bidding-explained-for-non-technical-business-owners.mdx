Real-time bidding (RTB) is an automated auction that sells a single ad impression the moment someone visits a website or opens an app. Advertisers bid for that impression, the highest bid wins, and the winning ad is shown. StackAdapt describes the process as an instant auction that takes less than a second. A small business does not run these auctions itself. A buying platform bids on its behalf, using the budget, targeting and creative the business sets. This guide covers the steps, the buying models, and how a local business could test RTB on a small budget.

## How Does Real-Time Bidding Work?

The whole sequence finishes while the page loads. Adwave puts the cycle from ad request to served ad at about 100 milliseconds, and StackAdapt notes that RTB auctions usually finish within one-tenth of a second.

1. A person opens a page or app that has ad space for sale.
2. The seller's system sends a bid request describing the impression.
3. Advertisers' buying platforms answer with a bid and an ad.
4. The highest bidder wins the placement.

StackAdapt says the bid request can include demographic data, location information and browser history. Publishers can set minimum bids and advertisers can set maximum bids. The winning bid also sets the final cost, which depends on how much competition there is for that impression.

## Who Buys and Who Sells in an RTB Auction?

Two kinds of platform make the auction work. Supply-side platforms (SSPs) are used by publishers to sell their ad space automatically to the highest bidders. Demand-side platforms (DSPs) do the buying: they use real-time bidding and machine learning to bid on impressions that match the advertiser's targeting criteria.

A small business usually works with a DSP. The business sets the audience, locations, budget and creative in the platform, and the DSP decides which impressions to bid on. StackAdapt says AI models assess historical and real-time auction data to estimate the clearing price of each impression. It also cites a Google claim, relayed through a secondary blog, that AI-powered bidding can cut cost per acquisition by up to 30%. Treat that figure as a vendor-supported claim, not a guarantee.

## Is RTB the Same as Programmatic Advertising?

No. Programmatic advertising is the wider term for automated buying and selling of ads. StackAdapt defines it as a subset of digital advertising that uses automated technology and data-driven algorithms to buy and place ads in real time, often across several platforms. RTB is one method within that category, the one where each impression is auctioned.

For a broader comparison of local-business options, read [display advertising vs programmatic for local business](/blog/display-advertising-vs-programmatic-local-business). It explains where each approach fits.

## How Does RTB Differ From Guaranteed and Private Deals?

Programmatic buying comes in several models. The main difference is whether the price and volume are agreed in advance.

| Buying model | How the price is set | Commitment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real-time bidding (open auction) | Impression by impression; highest bid wins | No upfront commitment or pre-negotiated price |
| Programmatic guaranteed | Pre-negotiated price | Upfront commitment |
| Private marketplace (PMP) | Auction among a selected, limited group of buyers | A subset of RTB, with invited buyers only |

An open marketplace, also called an open ad exchange, is where these open auctions run. A PMP lets a publisher offer premium inventory to a small group of buyers, so it is still RTB but with a narrower audience of bidders. For a small business, the open auction is the simplest starting point because nothing is committed in advance.

## Does RTB Work for TV and Other Screens?

Yes, in some cases. Adwave describes programmatic connected TV (CTV) as using real-time bidding to place ads on premium streaming channels while the advertiser controls who sees the ad and how much is spent. Adwave also states that bidding decisions for a CTV impression are made in less than 100 milliseconds.

Out-of-home screens are also sold this way. StackAdapt says programmatic DOOH inventory can be bought through demand-side platforms, with marketers bidding on digital screens. For a full overview of TV-style buying, see [what CTV advertising is for local business owners](/blog/what-is-ctv-advertising-guide-for-local-business-owners-2026).

## What Does a Small Test Budget Look Like?

All inputs below are **assumptions**, not market rates. Replace them with real quotes from your platform.

| Input or result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget (assumption) | $500 |
| CPM, cost per 1,000 impressions (assumption) | $10 |
| Impressions bought | $500 / $10 x 1,000 = 50,000 |
| Click-through rate (assumption) | 0.2% |
| Clicks | 50,000 x 0.2% = 100 |
| Cost per click | $500 / 100 = $5.00 |

If the same $500 bought impressions at a $20 CPM (assumption), you would get 25,000 impressions and roughly 50 clicks at the same click-through rate. The CPM is therefore the first number to ask a vendor about. Compare the output with your own conversion rate before you decide whether a second test is worth running. ## Is RTB Right for Your Local Business?

RTB suits reaching people before they search: a new shop opening, a seasonal offer, or an event. It fits poorly when your customers are ready to buy now, because search ads capture that demand more directly. Builtright Digital reports that paid search can start producing calls and form fills within the first few days, while automated bidding needs a few weeks of consistent data to optimize. That timing matters for a small budget.

Pricing is also a factor. Adwave states that Meta and Instagram ads have no technical monthly minimum, but most small businesses need $500 to $2,000 per month per channel. StackAdapt says it has no minimum spend requirement, while many enterprise platforms are built for larger accounts. Ask each vendor about minimums, pricing and reporting before you sign.

Check your foundations first. If your Google Business Profile, website and contact details are not ready, a paid campaign will send people to a weak page. ## FAQ

### What does RTB stand for?

RTB stands for real-time bidding. It is an auction for one ad impression, held when the impression becomes available.

### How fast is an RTB auction?

StackAdapt describes RTB as an instant auction that takes less than a second, and states that auctions usually finish within one-tenth of a second. Adwave gives a figure of about 100 milliseconds for the full request-to-served cycle.

### Do I need a developer to run RTB?

No. A buying platform handles the bidding. You set the budget, audience, locations and creative.

### Is there a minimum budget?

It depends on the vendor. StackAdapt states it has no minimum spend requirement. Many enterprise platforms require contact with sales first, so ask before you commit.

### Can I run RTB on TV screens?

Some connected TV inventory is sold through real-time bidding. Adwave describes programmatic CTV as placing ads on premium streaming channels this way.
