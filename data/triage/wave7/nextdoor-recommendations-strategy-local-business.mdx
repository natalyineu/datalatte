## Why Do Nextdoor Recommendations Matter for Local Businesses?

A recommendation is a public endorsement from a neighbor, and the people who read it are often the ones who might call you next. MediaPost reported in 2019 that Nextdoor had 40 million recommendations on its platform, and that 76% of members said they had been influenced by a neighbor's suggestion. Both figures come from Nextdoor itself and are several years old, so read them as direction rather than a forecast for your street.

Recommendations also connect to broader local goals. Salesforce's local marketing guide lists higher local brand awareness, more foot traffic, more qualified local leads, better customer retention and positive word of mouth as benefits of local marketing. Recommendations feed several of those at once, which is why they are worth a steady routine.

Word of mouth more generally carries weight. A secondary summary of referral research, citing McKinsey, says word of mouth is the primary factor behind 20% to 50% of purchasing decisions. Those figures are global, not Nextdoor-specific, so do not apply them directly to your own sales. For the wider approach, read our [word of mouth marketing strategy](/blog/word-of-mouth-marketing-strategy).

## Is a Nextdoor Business Page Free to Use?

The core tools are free. A vendor guide to Nextdoor says a Business Page, recommendations and posting updates are free to use. Nextdoor's own small business page says its Business Posts are free and appear in the neighborhood newsfeed, which it calls the most visible part of Nextdoor.

Advertising is a separate, paid product. Nextdoor's Local Deals, introduced in 2019, lets businesses and service providers target ads to people in their neighborhood. Free recommendations and posts can be built first, and you can decide later whether paid reach is worth the cost.

Some businesses face an extra step. Nextdoor says casual service providers, such as dog walkers or babysitters, need official business documents to verify their Business Page before they can post to the neighborhood newsfeed. Check this before you plan your posting calendar.

## Does Your Neighborhood Setting Decide Who Sees a Post?

Yes, and it is easy to miss. Nextdoor states that if you do not select your own neighborhood as part of a post's audience, you will not be able to see that post from a member account. A post can therefore be live but invisible to the account you use to check it.

Before you publish, confirm your neighborhood is selected in the audience settings. After publishing, open the post from a member account to confirm it appears. This takes about a minute and avoids weeks of posting that nobody sees.

## How Many Recommendations Should a Page Aim For?

A secondary summary of Nextdoor guidance, reported by AdWave from Surefire Local, says Business Pages with 5 or more recommendations achieve 30% higher engagement than pages with fewer. Treat 5 as a first milestone. It is a summary of guidance, not a controlled test, so it shows an association at most.

The worked example below uses assumptions, not Nextdoor data. Assume the page has 3 recommendations today and a shop with 40 customer visits a month.

| Monthly customers asked (assumption) | Recommendations gained per month (assumption) | Time to reach 5 from 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 40 | 10 (25% leave one) | About 1 week |
| 10 | 2.5 (25% leave one) | About 3 to 4 weeks |

The gap between the two rows is the point. Asking more customers, consistently, moves the page toward the 5-recommendation mark much faster than occasional requests. Track your real conversion rate for a month and replace the assumed 25% with your own figure.

## How Long Does It Take for Word of Mouth to Pay Off?

Google's SEO Starter Guide describes word of mouth, where people familiar with a site tell their friends about it, as one of the most effective and lasting ways to promote a site, though it can take time. The same logic applies to Nextdoor recommendations. Expect a slow build rather than an immediate spike.

Word of mouth also works best alongside other channels. Our article on [why word of mouth alone will not grow a local business anymore](/blog/why-word-of-mouth-alone-wont-grow-your-local-business-anymore) explains where it stops being enough. Use recommendations to strengthen trust, and use other channels to reach people who have not yet heard of you.

## What Does a Simple Weekly Routine Look Like?

A routine works better than a campaign. Use this checklist as a starting point and adjust the times to your own workload.

1. Claim your free Business Page and confirm the neighborhoods you serve.
2. Before each post, select your own neighborhood in the audience settings, then check it from a member account.
3. After each completed job or sale, send your page link by text or email to customers who were happy with the work.
4. Reply to each new recommendation within a window you set, such as one working day (assumption).
5. Once a week, publish one Business Post that features a real recommendation, since these posts are free.
6. Ask new customers how they heard about you and count those who name Nextdoor.
