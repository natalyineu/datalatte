A referral program rewards existing customers for sending you new customers. To build one, choose a reward that fits your margin, make sharing easy, record which customer sent each referral, and pay the reward after a real first visit. It works because people trust people they know. An [impact.com study](https://impact.com/referral/benefits-of-referral-marketing/) reports that 86% of consumers consider personal recommendations crucial in their purchasing decisions, while only 2% find traditional advertisements important.

## What Makes Referral Marketing Work for Local Businesses?

Referral marketing is structured word of mouth. A customer shares your business, a friend becomes a customer, and you thank the person who sent them. Without a clear offer, an easy way to share and a way to see who referred whom, referrals still happen, but you cannot repeat or measure them.

The value of a referred customer is the main reason to invest. Impact's referral growth analysis, as summarised on [Adwave's referral guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-build-a-referral-program-that-actually-drives-growth) (secondary), says referred customers have a 16% higher lifetime value. Wharton School research, cited on the [impact.com page](https://impact.com/referral/benefits-of-referral-marketing/), puts the gap at 16 to 25 percent over customers from traditional channels.

These are broad studies, not a forecast for your shop. Treat them as direction and measure your own referral rate.

## How Should You Choose the Reward?

Pick a reward you can afford on a first visit and explain in one sentence. Two common formats are a credit for the existing customer and a first-visit offer for the friend. A reward can be a credit that brings the referrer back, which suits appointment-based businesses such as salons, clinics and trades.

Pay the reward after the new customer completes a first paid visit, not at sign-up. This ties your cost to real revenue and makes it harder for people to refer themselves. Write the terms down so both sides know when the reward is paid.

Keep referral rewards separate from review requests. Ask for honest reviews on their own terms, and check your review platform's current help page before you mix the two.

## Should You Start Manually or Buy Software?

Start manually. Adwave's referral guide, which summarises Osmu's advice (secondary), recommends that businesses process referrals by hand for their top 10 to 20% most engaged customers for weeks or months before investing in automation software. Manual tracking also shows you which offer works before you pay for a tool.

A spreadsheet with a unique code per customer, the referrer's name, the new customer, the first-visit date and the reward status is enough to start. Once you can see which referrals convert, you can judge whether a tool is worth it. ## Does a Referral Reward Pay Back?

The table below uses assumed inputs. Replace them with your own sales and margin figures before you decide anything.

| Input or result | Value | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Average first-visit spend | $60 | Assumption |
| Repeat visits in 12 months | 3 more visits at $60 each | Assumption |
| Offer to the new customer | $10 off first visit | Assumption |
| Reward to the referrer | $15 credit | Assumption |
| First-year revenue from one referred customer | $60 - $10 + (3 x $60) = $230 | Calculated |
| Revenue after the $15 reward | $230 - $15 = $215 | Calculated |
| Gross margin on service revenue | 40% | Assumption |
| Gross profit after the reward | (0.40 x $230) - $15 = $77 | Calculated |

On these assumptions, one referral still returns $77 in gross profit after the reward. The payback changes quickly if the new customer never returns, so track repeat visits from the first month. A reward larger than your first-visit margin can still pay back if repeat rates hold, but only your own data can confirm that.

## How Do You Set Up a Referral Program in Six Steps?

1. Write the offer in one sentence, with the reward for both sides.
2. Create a unique code or link for each current customer.
3. Add the offer to receipts, booking confirmations and your email footer.
4. Tell your best customers first, in person or by message.
5. Log every referral and its first-visit date.
6. Review after 60 days and adjust the reward or the wording.

## How Do You Know It Is Working?

Measure three numbers: referrals per 100 active customers per quarter, the share of referrals that complete a first paid visit, and repeat visits from referred customers at 12 months. Compare them to the worked example above.

Adwave's guide on asking for referrals (secondary) reports that nurturing referral relationships has been shown to accelerate client growth by 150% in five weeks, according to Southwestern Consulting. That is a single cited figure, so use it to justify staying in touch with referrers, not to set targets.

Review the numbers every 60 days. If referrals convert but few return, improve the second visit. If few people share, make the code easier to find or the reward clearer.

## FAQ

### How do I track referrals without software?

Give each customer a unique code and log it at checkout or booking. A spreadsheet with referrer, new customer, first-visit date and reward status is enough to start.

### Should the reward be cash, credit or a discount?

Credit suits service businesses because it brings the referrer back. Choose the format that fits your margin and that you can explain in one sentence.

### When should I pay the referral reward?

Pay after the new customer completes a first paid visit. This ties the cost to real revenue and reduces self-referral.

### Can I use referrals and review requests together?

Keep them separate. Ask for honest reviews on their own terms, and check your review platform's current policy before linking any incentive to them.
