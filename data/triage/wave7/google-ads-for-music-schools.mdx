## What Do Local Advertising Costs Look Like?

Setting up a Google Business Profile as a service-area business and collecting reviews are both described as free, which makes the profile the first step for any studio ([AdWave mobile mechanics guide](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-mobile-mechanics), secondary). Printed direct mail is estimated at $0.75-2.00 per piece including design, printing and postage ([AdWave local political advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-political-advertising-city-county-school-board), secondary).

TV and streaming budgets work on a different scale. The vendor says local city, county and school board campaigns typically allocate $5,000-50,000 for TV and CTV advertising ([AdWave political ad rates 2026](https://adwave.com/resources/political-ad-rates-2026), secondary). That is a political figure and sits far beyond a lesson studio budget.

## Who Should the Ads Reach?

There are two main buyers: parents searching for child lessons, and adults looking for beginner or evening lessons. Their searches use different words and need different landing pages, so split them into separate ad groups rather than sending both to one generic page.

Write the parent ads around age ranges and trial lessons. Write the adult ads around schedule and skill level. On both pages, keep the next step obvious, such as booking a trial lesson.

## How Do You Structure Campaigns Across Locations?

A studio with one address can run a single campaign. A school with several branches or teaching areas can follow the pattern in the seoplus+ multi-location guide: each physical location or major service area gets its own Google Ads campaign within a single account ([seoplus+ multi-location PPC guide](https://seoplus.com/paid-ads/the-ultimate-guide-to-multi-location-ppc-advertising-on-google-ads/), secondary).

Separate campaigns make budgets and results visible per branch, so you can see which town fills trial lessons. The same guide notes that regions can have their own advertising rules, such as data privacy laws, which affect campaign setup and how data is handled. Check those rules in each country you advertise in before copying a template across borders. If managing this yourself is not realistic, [how to find a Google Ads manager for a local business](/blog/best-google-ads-manager-local-business-how-to-find) explains what to look for.

## Which Keywords and Negatives Work for Lessons?

Start with instrument, intent and place: "guitar lessons" plus your town, "piano teacher near me", or "violin lessons for kids". Give each instrument its own ad group so the ad and landing page name the same instrument.

Review search terms every week for the first two months. Add negatives for searches you do not serve, such as free self-study, job and salary searches, or products you do not sell. Add plural and singular forms yourself where they matter.

## Should You Track Calls and Trial Bookings?

Yes. Track the action that brings a student in. For most studios that is a trial lesson booking, or a call that leads to one. Ask every new student how they found the school and record the answer next to the campaign data. That lets you match instruments and towns to students who stay, not just to people who clicked.

A tidy Business Profile supports every ad, and its setup is described as free, so there is little reason to leave it incomplete.

## How Do You Launch in the First Month?

1. Create one campaign for each teaching location, inside a single account.
2. Split parent and adult ad groups, then create one ad group per instrument.
3. Send each ad to a page that offers a trial lesson, and set up call and form tracking.
4. Book a weekly search terms review for the first two months and add negatives as they appear.
5. Record how each new student found the school, then compare that against the campaign data each month.

## What Does a Budget Buy? A Worked Example

Every input below is an assumption, not a published figure. An enquiry here means a call or form submission. A new student is someone who enrols after a trial.

| Input | Value (assumption) |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $500 |
| Cost per click | $3.00 |
| Clicks | about 167 |
| Enquiry rate from clicks | 8% |
| Enquiries | about 13 |
| Cost per enquiry | about $37.50 |
| Enquiries that enrol | 25% |
| New students | about 3.3 |
| Cost per new student | about $150 |
| Monthly lesson fee | $160 |

At these assumed numbers, a new student at $150 in ad cost pays for themselves in their first month of $160 lessons. If only 10% of enquiries enrol, the cost per student rises to about $375, roughly 2.3 months of lesson fees. Measure enrolment from enquiry first, because it moves the result more than the click price does.

## Where Do Other Channels Fit?

## FAQ

### How much should a music school spend on Google Ads?
This guide does not give a music school benchmark. The worked example uses a $500 monthly budget as an assumption. Start with an amount you can sustain for two months, and judge the result on cost per new student.

### Can I target young students directly?
Reach parents and adult learners through search terms instead. The search terms show who is actually looking for lessons, which is a better guide than building audiences around young students.

### Is a Google Business Profile free?
Setup as a service-area business and review collection are described as free in the AdWave mobile service guide, so it is the lowest-cost step to take first ([AdWave mobile mechanics guide](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-mobile-mechanics), secondary).

### Do I need a separate campaign for each branch?
Each physical location or major service area can have its own campaign within one account, according to the seoplus+ guide ([seoplus+ multi-location PPC guide](https://seoplus.com/paid-ads/the-ultimate-guide-to-multi-location-ppc-advertising-on-google-ads/), secondary). That makes results visible per branch.
