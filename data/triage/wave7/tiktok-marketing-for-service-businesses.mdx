A service business should film the work itself: a before and after, a short answer to a question customers ask, and a normal day on the job. Meta research, as reported on AdWave's pest control page, says video ads generate 1.2x higher engagement than static images, and local service businesses see 3-5x higher recall from branded video content. Those are secondary figures from 2024, so treat them as a reason to test, not a forecast for your account. This guide covers what to film, a four-week start, and what a weekly routine costs.

This guide is for plumbers, cleaners, salons, groomers, trainers and similar local services. No audience-share figure is used here, so check a current survey before choosing TikTok over another channel.

## Is Video Worth Testing for a Service Business?

Yes, as a test with a fixed end date. The two video figures above come from Meta's research as reported by AdWave, so they describe video in general and local service brands as a group, not TikTok specifically. Use them as a reason to try one format for four weeks, then compare bookings with the weeks before.

Video suits services because the result is visible. A repaired gutter or a cleaned kitchen is a clear before and after in a few seconds. Video works best as a route to your details, not a replacement for them: the service page on your website still needs prices or price ranges, service areas and booking steps. Our [video marketing guide for local businesses](/blog/video-marketing-local-businesses-getting-started) covers the wider set of formats if you want to compare them.

## What Should a Service Business Film First?

Start with formats that show a result or explain a process. Five fit most trades:

1. A before and after of one job, shot from the same angle both times.
2. One question a customer asks often, answered briefly on camera or in captions.
3. A normal day on the job, from arrival to finish, with no script.
4. The tools you use and why, with hands only if you prefer not to show your face.
5. What changes a quote, without naming a price you cannot keep.

Blackstone Informatics' guide to pet shop social media lists six content types that work for pet stores: user-generated content, behind-the-scenes moments, tips and advice, seasonal content, interactive features, and products shown in context. Groomers and pet sitters can adapt that list, because it was written for retail rather than services. ## How Should a Four-Week Start Look?

AdWave's TikTok SEO plan for local businesses runs over four weeks. Week one reviews customer age, ZIP codes, service value and current search behaviour. Week three builds one search-ad group per service theme. The plan is written for search-led local marketing, so borrow its planning step rather than the whole approach.

For a service business, week one can be a short list: the three jobs that earn the most, the suburbs those customers live in, and the search terms they used when they called. Film the service that scores highest first. Then use the search terms as on-screen captions so that viewers see the words they would type. For more on local search itself, read our [SEO guide for local service businesses](/blog/seo-for-local-service-businesses).

## How Do You Balance the Content Mix?

AdWave's guide to local Facebook groups recommends four content pillars: expert guidance, member spotlight content, behind-the-scenes posts and light participation prompts. That is a group model, not a TikTok one, but it maps well onto a service account. Expert guidance becomes a how-to clip. A member spotlight becomes a customer's before and after, filmed with permission. Behind-the-scenes becomes a day on the job. A participation prompt becomes a question in the caption.

Using four pillars keeps the account from becoming a stream of job photos. It also gives you a way to plan a week: one clip per pillar, filmed in a single visit where possible. ## What Should You Check Before You Post?

Get written permission before filming a customer's home, pet or family, and do not show addresses or vehicle plates. Reviews you repost must be real, and do not offer a discount that depends on a positive review, because it can bias the reviews you collect. Disclose free services or products in the video itself, and check the FTC's current endorsement guidance before you publish, since rules and examples change.

Sounds are the other check. TikTok's live Business help page sets out which sounds a business account can add, and that list can differ from what older posts show. Check it before planning a trend-based video. Avoid copying a sound you cannot confirm you may use. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

## What Does a Weekly Routine Cost?

All inputs below are **assumptions**, not benchmarks. Replace them with your own numbers.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Filming time per week | 1 hour, 3 clips |
| Editing and posting per week | 1 hour |
| Your hourly value | $40 |
| Weekly cost | 2 hours x $40 = $80 |
| Monthly cost (4 weeks) | $320 |
| Average job value | $150 |
| Jobs needed to break even | $320 / $150 = 2.13, so 3 jobs |

If editing takes longer than the assumed hour, the cost rises. ## How Do You Know the Videos Worked?

Track three things for each post: calls, messages and booking-form submissions that mention or follow it. Ask every new customer how they found you, and record the answer on the booking form. A video that gets views but no calls is a content win, not a sales win, so judge by bookings.

Set a stop rule before you start. Under the worked example, if the routine brings no new jobs after three months, move that hour to asking for Google Business Profile reviews. ## FAQ

### Do I need to show my face on TikTok?
No. Hands, tools and finished results can carry a video, with voiceover or captions explaining the work.

### Is TikTok the right first channel for a service business?
It depends on where your customers already spend time. This guide does not use an audience-share figure, so check a current survey for your area before deciding.

### How do I measure whether a video worked?
Record calls, messages and booking-form submissions for each post, and ask new customers how they found you.

### Can I repost a customer's review in a video?
Only if the review is real and the customer agrees. Check the FTC's current endorsement guidance before publishing.
