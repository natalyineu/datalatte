A Google My Business listing, now managed as a Google Business Profile, is how many prospects find your fitness studio on Maps and in local search. A complete, accurate profile is the foundation. Reviews are the lever that takes longer to pay off. Mariana Tek reports that Google review volume can start influencing visibility within a few weeks, while the typical payoff for local SEO and Google reviews runs 6 to 12 months ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). Treat the listing as ongoing work, not a one-time setup.

## What should your studio fix on the listing first?

Start with the details that describe your studio accurately. Confirm your business name, address, phone number and hours. Then write a clear description that names the class types you teach, such as Pilates, indoor cycling or strength training. Google can only match your studio to searches it can understand, so vague wording costs visibility.

Upload current photos of the studio floor, the front entrance and your instructors, and keep the cover image up to date. Check that your booking link works on both desktop and mobile. A wrong number or a broken link sends prospects straight to a competitor.

Compare your categories and description with the studios ranking above you in your area. The [fitness studio Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization) walks through each field in more detail.

## How do reviews change visibility over time?

Reviews have the clearest timeline of any part of the listing. Mariana Tek states that Google review volume can start influencing visibility within a few weeks, which makes review requests worth building into your post-class routine ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)).

The same source places the typical payoff for local SEO and Google reviews at 6 to 12 months, with low ongoing effort but a slow build. That pattern favours steady habits over occasional pushes. Ask for reviews every week, reply to every review, and avoid waiting for a seasonal campaign to start.

Make the request easy. Send a direct review link after a first visit or a class, and keep the message short. Reply to positive reviews by naming the class or instructor where it fits. Reply to negative reviews calmly and invite the member to continue the conversation offline. Drafting these replies faster is covered in [AI for Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization).

## What budget and payoff should you expect?

Fixing the listing costs time rather than media spend. Many studios pair it with paid search while organic reviews build. Vertical Impression suggests a $1,000 to $2,000 monthly Google Ads budget for a well-optimized fitness campaign, with a target cost per trial lead under $30 and a cost per converting member under $150 to $200 ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026)).

Here is a worked example using those targets. The inputs are assumptions, not measured results for any studio.

- Assumption: your monthly Google Ads budget is $1,000, the low end of the range.
- Trial leads needed to hit the $30 target: $1,000 ÷ $30 = 33.3, so at least 34 trial leads a month.
- Converting members at the $150 to $200 target: $1,000 ÷ $200 = 5 and $1,000 ÷ $150 = 6.7, so at least 5 to 7 new members from that spend.

Now set the timeline against the listing work. Assumption: weeks 1 to 4 cover the listing fixes, weeks 3 to 8 are when review volume starts to show, and months 6 to 12 are the window for full payoff. Paid search fills the gap while the organic side builds. ## Why does competition make the listing matter more?

Competition is a real pressure for many owners. In a Wellhub-commissioned survey of 662 gym and studio owners across 11 countries, 95% said competition has intensified over the past three years, and 86% said members have become more price-sensitive this year ([Health & Fitness Association](https://www.healthandfitness.org/86-of-gym-and-studio-owners-grew-membership-this-year-even-as-costs-and-competition-rise/)). The same survey found 86% of owners grew their membership base that year, so the market is not standing still.

Those figures do not measure Google specifically, and the survey covers a global sample rather than the US, UK, Australia and Canada alone. They do explain why a listing that shows value clearly, with current photos, accurate class details and recent reviews, is worth the effort when members compare options.

## Which Insights numbers should you track each month?

Google's Insights dashboard shows how people find and act on your listing. Track three groups of numbers each month: how many people saw your listing through Search versus Maps, how many requested directions, and how many called or clicked through to your website.

If Maps views are low, check your location accuracy and categories first. If direction requests are high but bookings are flat, look at whether your entrance and parking are clear in your photos and posts. If calls are strong but website clicks are weak, make sure the booking path is easy to find from your profile. Compare these numbers month to month rather than week to week, since the review timeline is measured in months. ## FAQ

### How soon will reviews start to affect my visibility?

Mariana Tek states that Google review volume can start influencing visibility within a few weeks. The typical payoff for local SEO and Google reviews is listed at 6 to 12 months, so judge progress over a quarter or two rather than a single month ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)).

### How much should a small studio budget for Google Ads?

Vertical Impression gives monthly benchmarks by studio size. It puts Google Ads at $600 to $1,200 for a small independent studio under 200 members, and at $1,500 to $2,500 for a mid-size gym with 200 to 600 members ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026)). Treat these as starting points and test against your own cost per trial lead.

### Should I rely on the listing alone without paid ads?

The listing is the base layer, but its payoff is slow. The 6 to 12 month window for reviews and local SEO means paid search can cover new-member goals while organic signals build ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). Many studios run both, with paid search giving faster feedback on which offers and classes convert.

### What should I check if I move my studio to a new address?

Check Google's current help pages for the steps needed to update your address and re-verify the listing, since verification rules can change. Keep your hours and phone number consistent across your website and other directories during the move.
