## How much should a hair salon spend on Meta ads?

Bookeo's starting range is $750 to $1,500 per month for Facebook and Instagram. [AdWave's channel comparison](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels) puts a standalone Meta buy at $300 to $1,000 per month, which gives a practical floor for a single offer. A salon testing one promotion can start at the lower end. A salon running several promotions has more reason to use the higher range.

Results take time. [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-small-business-2026) says Meta takes weeks to optimize but offers good targeting. Judge a campaign after several weeks of data, not after the first few days.

## What does a Meta ad cost per click and per 1,000 views?

[AdWave's small business guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-small-business-guide) puts typical Meta costs at $5 to $15 per thousand impressions and $1 to $3 per click. Those ranges are wide because cost depends on creative, audience, season and competition. Your own numbers will differ, so track cost per booking, not only cost per click.

Bookeo also claims that a client who has seen a salon's brand on Facebook 5 to 10 times before searching "hair salon near me" converts at 2 to 3 times the rate of a cold searcher. The page gives no source for that figure, so treat it as a claim to test against your own booking data.

## Which ad formats work best for salon Meta ads?

[AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners) cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, as summarized by the vendor, saying short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram. For a salon, that points toward short Reels showing a real cut, colour or finish filmed in your own chair, with the booking link in the ad.

Stories suit time-limited offers. [Associated Hair Professionals](https://www.associatedhairprofessionals.com/updates/blog-posts/power-social-media-your-hairstyling-brand) describes Instagram and Facebook stories as temporary content, so use them for a same-week opening and retire the creative afterwards.

Organic posting supports paid ads. Bookeo says most salon owners find three to four Instagram posts and one to two Facebook posts weekly sustainable. For more on organic cadence, see [Instagram marketing for hair salons](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-hair-salons).

## How should you target clients near your salon?

Bookeo advises a 5 to 10 mile radius around the salon for Facebook and Instagram ads. For a single location, start at the narrow end and widen only if cost per booking stays acceptable.

[Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising) describes Meta Ads Manager as a centralized point for running ads across Instagram and Facebook, so one campaign can cover both. [The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/) notes that Meta owns both platforms, which lets advertisers identify detailed customer profiles and set a budget for each ad. Use interest or behaviour options only where they match the service you sell, and test them against each other. For setup detail, see [Facebook ads for hair salons](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons).

## What does a worked budget look like for one salon?

The table below estimates what a $750 monthly budget could return. The budget comes from Bookeo's starting range. Every other input is an assumption to replace with your own numbers.

Inputs (assumptions unless stated):
- Monthly budget: $750 (sourced, Bookeo starting range)
- CPM: $10 (assumption, within the $5 to $15 range)
- Click-through rate: 1% (assumption)
- Click-to-booking rate: 4% (assumption)
- Average ticket: $60 (assumption)

Working: $750 divided by $10, multiplied by 1,000, gives 75,000 impressions. At a 1% click-through rate that is 750 clicks, so cost per click is $750 divided by 750, or $1.00. At a 4% booking rate, 750 clicks produce 30 bookings. Thirty bookings at $60 is $1,800 in revenue, or 2.4 times the spend.

| Scenario (assumption) | Cost per click | Clicks | Bookings | Revenue | Return on spend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base case | $1.00 | 750 | 30 | $1,800 | 2.4x |
| Weaker clicks | $2.00 | 375 | 15 | $900 | 1.2x |

The weaker row doubles cost per click, which is still inside the $1 to $3 range cited above. Revenue here ignores product costs, staff time and repeat visits. The point is that a small change in click cost moves the result sharply, so check cost per click weekly during the first month.

## How do you track which bookings come from Meta ads?

Check three things before spending. First, the Meta Pixel is installed and fires on the booking confirmation page. Second, a conversion event is set up, such as a Lead event when a booking form is submitted. Third, UTM parameters are on every ad URL so your analytics can report Meta traffic separately.

Without these, you cannot calculate cost per booking, and decisions default to reach and impressions. For the rebooking side once a client is tracked, see [AI-powered email marketing for hair salons](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-for-hair-salons).

Local context matters more than national ad copy. Write ad text around the services and areas your clients actually book, and make sure the offer matches what your booking page shows.
