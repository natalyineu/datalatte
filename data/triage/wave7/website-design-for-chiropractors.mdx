A chiropractic website should answer three questions quickly: which conditions you treat, where the clinic is, and how a new patient books. Build six pages (Home, Services, Conditions, About, Contact and Booking), put the booking button on every page, and keep mobile pages fast and readable. Sources on trust and speed are cited below, but none publishes a booking-rate lift for chiropractic sites, so none is claimed here.

This guide covers page structure, testimonials, mobile speed, privacy and a worked running-cost example for solo chiropractors and small clinics in the US, UK, Australia and Canada.

## What Pages Does a Chiropractic Website Need?

Six pages cover most new-patient questions. Home states who you treat and in which town. Services lists adjustments, soft-tissue work and anything else you offer. Conditions gives each complaint a short, plain-language section. About introduces the practitioners and the clinic. Contact shows the address, phone, hours and a map. Booking links to your scheduling tool.

Consumers still treat the website as a serious decision point. A Storyblok survey of 1,000 consumers in 2023 found 43% said a brand's website was more valuable than its mobile app or social accounts when making purchases ([Adwave trust signals guide](https://adwave.com/resources/trust-signals-on-website), secondary). Use the [local business website checklist](/blog/local-business-website-checklist) to confirm each page before launch.

## How Should Testimonials and Health Claims Work?

Adwave's testimonial guide describes a structured evidence stack: short quotes for quick scanning, longer case-study style stories for depth, and video placed near the top of the page or close to the main call to action ([Adwave testimonial guide](https://adwave.com/resources/testimonial-pages-that-build-trust-and-drive-sales), secondary). One roundup it cites says adding positive testimonials to sales pages can raise conversions by 34% (same source, secondary).

Health claims need more care than retail reviews. Do not imply typical results from one patient's story, and publish only testimonials you have permission to use. Rules on outcome claims and before and after images vary by state, so check your state chiropractic board's current advertising rules before publishing them.

## Is the Site Fast Enough on a Phone?

Adwave cites a SOASTA study, included in Google's mobile site speed playbook, which found mobile pages that loaded one second faster achieved up to a 27% increase in conversion rates ([Adwave booking page guide](https://adwave.com/resources/appointment-booking-page-design), secondary). For a clinic, a heavy hero video or a chat widget can slow the booking page enough to cost appointments.

Compress photos, avoid autoplay video and test the booking page on a real phone before launch. Keep the first screen simple, following the [above-the-fold design guide](/blog/above-the-fold-design-local-business). Once the site runs, [A/B testing for local businesses](/blog/a-b-testing-for-local-business) shows how to compare button wording without guessing.

## Which Trust Signals Belong on Contact and Booking Pages?

Adwave's about-page guide recommends short paragraphs, bullet points, easy-to-find contact information and reduced visual noise. It also cites basic trust signals from Orientation Agency: HTTPS, accessible contact information and published policies ([Adwave about page guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-create-an-about-page-that-builds-trust), secondary).

Adwave's product description guide cites the Edelman Trust Barometer as finding only 12% of SMB product pages include specific, verifiable local trust markers (secondary). For a clinic, that gap is an opening: name the street, parking arrangements and the practitioners by name rather than using generic phrases.

## Does Privacy Language Affect Bookings?

A survey discussed by Clutch found 33% of users named the browser lock icon as their strongest security cue, against 8% who cited clear privacy standards and 5% who cited transparent data practices ([Adwave trust signals guide](https://adwave.com/resources/trust-signals-on-website), secondary). Serve the whole site over HTTPS and place a plain privacy note beside every form. For data rules, read the [GDPR guide for local business websites](/blog/local-business-website-gdpr-compliance), and check with a privacy adviser before adding tracking to booking pages or collecting symptoms in a form.

## Should You Use AI to Write Clinic Copy?

Consumer-trend findings cited in Adwave's 2026 microcopy review say only about 7% of consumers said visible AI-generated marketing increased trust, while roughly 31% said it reduced trust ([Adwave microcopy guide](https://adwave.com/resources/website-microcopy-that-converts), secondary). Write conditions and about text in the clinic's own voice, and have a qualified practitioner check every health statement. The [copy guide with examples](/blog/how-to-write-website-copy-local-business-examples) includes templates you can adapt.

## FAQ

### How many pages does a chiropractic website need?

Six is a practical start: Home, Services, Conditions, About, Contact and Booking. Add pages only when you have real content for them.

### Should I publish prices on the site?

That is a business decision. If you publish a starting price, state what it covers so the claim is clear to visitors.

### Can I use patient reviews on my website?

Only with permission, and within your state board's rules. Keep reviews honest and avoid implying typical results.
