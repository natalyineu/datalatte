A local business hero section needs to answer three things in the first screen: what you do, where you do it, and what the visitor should do next. Add one real photo, one proof point and one button. In field data cited by [Adwave's hero section guide](https://adwave.com/resources/website-hero-section-design), text heroes passed Core Web Vitals 48.1% of the time, against 44.5% for image heroes. The gaps are small, so clarity and speed matter more than format. Eye-tracking research cited there found users spent 80.3% of their viewing time above the fold, in 2010.

## What must a hero section include?

Start with the service, the place and the next step. Say what you do in plain words, name the town or neighbourhood you serve, and give the visitor one main action, such as a call or a booking form. The headline and button do most of the work. A supporting line can add a detail the headline cannot fit without becoming hard to scan.

Proof comes next. A review count, a star rating or a years-in-business line gives a cautious visitor a reason to keep reading. Use only numbers you can verify, and link to the review profile where you can. Placement matters too. Adwave's summary of Trustmary's testimonial guide says the most effective testimonial placements sit in homepage hero sections, next to explicit calls to action, as described in [its testimonial page guide](https://adwave.com/resources/testimonial-pages-that-build-trust-and-drive-sales).

## What do good hero patterns look like?

Each pattern below follows the same formula: service, place, proof and action. The bracketed text is a placeholder.

| Business | Sample headline pattern | Button |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coffee shop | "[Specialty] coffee and pastries on [street], open from [time]" | Order ahead |
| Hair salon | "[Service] for [hair type] in [neighbourhood]" | Book online |
| Pet groomer | "Gentle grooming for dogs of every size in [town]" | Book a groom |
| Fitness studio | "Small-group [class type] classes in [town]" | Try a class |
| Trades | "[Trade] in [area]. Call for same-day help" | Call now |

Pair each headline with a photo of your real space or team, and a line of proof such as "[X] Google reviews, [Y] average rating". Button labels should be short. [StackAdapt's landing page guide](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/landing-page-conversion-rate) cites "Learn More", "Shop Now" and "Sign Up" as effective because they are short and to the point. For more layouts, see [local business landing page examples](/blog/local-business-landing-page-examples-that-convert).

## How long should the headline be?

Keep it short enough to read in one glance on a phone. Lead with the service and the place rather than a slogan, so a visitor knows within seconds whether the page fits their need. "Plumbing in [town], same-day callouts" does this. "Your trusted partner for home comfort" does not say what you do.

Test one headline change at a time. Changing the headline, the photo and the button together makes it impossible to tell which change helped. Copy length and tone are covered in more detail in [how to write website copy for local businesses](/blog/how-to-write-website-copy-local-business-examples).

## Should the hero use a big image or video?

A large image is fine if it loads quickly. Adwave cites Google's Core Web Vitals guidance, which treats a Largest Contentful Paint of 2.5 seconds or less as good. If your hero image is the largest element on screen, its load time sets that score, so compress it and check the timing before you add effects.

Video needs more caution. In the same field data, video heroes had the lowest pass rate at 42.1%. If you use one, keep it short. Adwave's [promo video guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-make-a-promo-video) recommends 30 to 60 seconds for a website hero or homepage promo. A still photo of your real space often does the job with less risk to speed.

## How do you keep the first screen readable?

Put headline text on a solid colour or a darkened overlay rather than over a busy part of the photo. Check contrast with a free contrast checker and view the page on a phone as well as a laptop. Text that looks clear on a large monitor can disappear on a small screen in daylight.

Also check that the button sits where a thumb can reach it on mobile, and that the page still shows a button when the hero image has not yet loaded. The [local business website checklist](/blog/local-business-website-checklist) includes these checks in a format you can work through page by page.

## FAQ

### What is a hero section?

It is the first block visitors see on a page, usually a headline, a supporting line, an image and a button.

### How many buttons should a hero have?

One main action. Add a secondary link only if the page has a second real goal, such as a call and a booking.

### Should the hero show prices?

A clear "from" price can help if it is accurate and current. Test it against a version without the price before you rely on it.

### What photo should I use?

A real photo of your space, team or work. Stock images rarely show what you actually offer, and visitors notice.

### Is a hero video worth it?

Only if it is short and loads fast. Adwave suggests 30 to 60 seconds for a homepage promo, and video heroes had the lowest pass rate in its cited field data.
