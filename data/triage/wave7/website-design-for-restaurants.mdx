A restaurant website should show the menu as readable text, opening hours, address, phone number and one clear order or reservation button on the first screen. Most diners check the menu before they visit, so the menu has to load fast and read well on a phone. Where you can, send ordering and bookings through your own site, because marketplace orders carry commission. The sections below cover the home page, the menu, booking, and a worked example of what commission costs over a month.

## What should a restaurant home page show?

Keep the first screen simple. State what the restaurant is, show a real photo of the food or room, and put today's hours and a tap-to-call phone number beside the buttons. Grubhub advises restaurants to include as much information as possible in their Google Business Profile, including an address, phone number, website URL, operating hours and photos, so the site and the profile should match. Ordering and reservation buttons belong above the fold, not buried in the footer.

If you have a Google rating, place it near the buttons. Diners who are comparing places will look at reviews as well as the menu. Adwave also advises responding to every review on Google and Yelp within 24-48 hours, which signals that the business is active.

## Why should the menu be text, not a PDF?

A text menu loads faster on a phone than a scanned file and is easier for search engines to read. Grubhub notes that optimizing a website for mobile devices can help it appear higher in local search results, and Google has used mobile-first indexing since late 2020, which means it evaluates the mobile version of your site when ranking pages.

Write each dish as a line of text with a short description and a price. Update prices whenever the menu changes, and keep the web menu and your Google Business Profile menu in sync, since two different prices will confuse guests. A PDF can be an extra download, but it should not be the only menu on the site.

## How much does commission change the cost of an online order?

Marketplace apps take a share of each order, and that share can change how much a direct order is worth. Adwave's example of an Austin Thai restaurant reports that adding DoorDash Storefront reduced commission on direct orders from 30% to 6%, while keeping the discovery benefits of the marketplace. That figure is one vendor example, so check current rates on your own account before relying on it.

The worked example below uses assumed volumes and an assumed order value. The 30% and 6% rates are the Adwave example, and the $30 order value is an assumption.

| Input or result | Marketplace at 30% | Direct at 6% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orders per month (assumption) | 100 | 100 |
| Average order value (assumption) | $30 | $30 |
| Commission per order | $9.00 | $1.80 |
| Commission per month | $900 | $180 |
| Monthly difference | | $720 saved |

In this model, moving 100 orders a month from the marketplace to your own ordering page keeps about $720 in the business. It does not include payment processing or the cost of ordering software, and the marketplace may still bring new customers you would not reach otherwise. Compare the cost per new customer for each channel before you move orders.

## How should reservations and bookings work?

Show the booking method you actually use, whether that is a form, a phone number or a booking provider link, and keep it one tap from the home page. Put private dining and events on their own page with a request form, so a large-party enquiry does not get lost among standard reservations.

If you take deposits, make the policy clear before the guest pays. Salon Today's 2022 guide describes Fresha Deposits as a way to make sure online bookings are not confirmed until a payment is made, which reduces no-shows for businesses that depend on fixed appointments. Restaurants with set-menu events can use the same approach for prepaid seats.

## How much should you spend driving traffic to the site?

Adwave estimates Google Ads for restaurants costs $300 to $1,500 per month, and it cites WordStream's average cost-per-click for restaurants at $1.84. Marketing LTB reports that restaurant PPC campaigns convert 6-12% of clicks into reservations or orders. Those figures let you estimate what a monthly budget buys.

Take an assumed budget of $600 a month. At $1.84 per click, that buys about 326 clicks. At a 6% conversion rate, that is about 20 orders or bookings, roughly $30 each. At 12%, it is about 39, or about $15 each. The cost per order falls as conversion rises, which is why the landing page matters as much as the ad. ## FAQ

### Do I need online ordering on my own site?

It is optional. The benefit is that direct orders avoid marketplace commission. The Adwave example shows the rate falling from 30% to 6% on direct orders, but your own rates will depend on your providers.

### Should the menu be on the home page?

Link to it from the home page, and keep the full menu as readable text on its own page. A visitor should reach the menu in one tap.

### How do I get more diners to book directly?

Show the booking button above the fold, keep the phone number tappable, and make the booking page quick to complete. Our [coffee shop conversion tips](/blog/coffee-shop-website-cro-tips) cover similar layout changes for small food businesses.

### Is TV advertising worth it for a restaurant site?

Adwave advises launching TV commercials 2-3 weeks before a promotion begins and mapping key campaigns 6-8 weeks ahead. Pair any TV spend with a promo code, as covered in our [CTV ads guide for restaurants](/blog/ctv-ads-for-restaurants).
