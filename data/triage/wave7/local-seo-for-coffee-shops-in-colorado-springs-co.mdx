## Why does the Google Business Profile carry so much weight?

Google describes its Business Profile as a free profile for Search and Maps, in its [Business Profile listing page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/). Setup costs nothing, so the real cost for a coffee shop is staff time for posts, photos and replies.

Ahrefs, summarizing a 2021 BrightLocal survey, reports that 36% of SEOs consider the profile the most important ranking factor for the map pack, up from 23% in 2013 ([secondary](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo)). A survey measures opinion rather than Google's formula, so treat it as direction, not proof.

Verification comes first. Per Diem notes that without verification, updates will not show publicly and customers may not find the business ([secondary](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). BrewRound describes the older process, where Google mails a letter with a code, and says the listing does not appear in results until it is verified ([secondary](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/)). Check Google's current help pages for today's steps. The [coffee shop profile optimization guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization) covers the setup screens in more detail.

## Which category and description should a coffee shop choose?

Per Diem recommends choosing "Coffee shop" or a related term rather than something broad like "Restaurant" ([secondary](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). BrewRound's 2020 walkthrough says you can select only one category when you create the listing, and more can be added later ([secondary](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/)). Start with the most specific term that matches what you sell, then add related categories afterward.

The description should describe the shop as it is: drinks, roast style, seating, Wi-Fi and food. Mention Colorado Springs and your neighbourhood where they fit naturally. Avoid repeating the same phrases, because customers read the description before they decide to visit. More setup ideas are in [Google Business Profile tips for coffee shops](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips).

## What should you post and answer every week?

The Restaurant HQ recommends that coffee shops post on social media at least once per week, ideally one to two times per day ([secondary](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/)). For Business Profile posts, Google's help page is the rule that matters: a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts stay hidden, so check the status after publishing.

Seed the Q&A section with the questions customers ask most. Per Diem suggests answering proactively whether you offer free Wi-Fi, have dairy-free options or allow dogs on the patio ([secondary](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide)). Google also lets people message businesses directly from their listing, so someone should own that inbox every day. For handling those messages, see [AI customer service for local coffee shops](/blog/ai-driven-customer-service-for-local-coffee-shops).

## How do reviews and listing details shape your map position?

Google states that reviews show next to a business's profile in Maps and Search ([Google review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Customers compare shops at that moment, so the review count and rating are part of the first impression. Ask for reviews that name specific drinks or features, since the written text is what people read. [How coffee shops dominate Google Maps](/blog/coffee-shops-dominate-google-maps) explains how that content fits the wider picture.

Citations are listings on other sites that repeat your name, address and phone number. A sensible habit is to keep those details identical everywhere and fix conflicting listings before adding new ones. If you run Google Ads, location data comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and corrections made there flow into linked Ads accounts ([Google Ads help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). When the standard option does not work, you can select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link with your Ads account, and those locations must be yours. For ad terms, see [keywords for coffee shops in 2026](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-coffee-shops-in-2026).

## What does a monthly profile routine cost? (Worked example)

The example below uses assumption-labelled inputs, not measured results. Replace them with your own figures.

| Input | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Posts per month (assumption, meets the weekly minimum) | 4 |
| Minutes per post including photo (assumption) | 15 |
| Owner or staff time value (assumption) | $20 per hour |
| Extra visits per month from the profile (assumption) | 12 |
| Average spend per visit (assumption) | $6 |

Time spent is 4 posts × 15 minutes = 60 minutes, or 1 hour, costing 1 × $20 = $20. Extra revenue is 12 visits × $6 = $72. Net monthly gain is $72 − $20 = $52. Break-even is $20 ÷ $6, about 3.3 visits, so roughly 4 extra visits per month cover the time at these assumptions. Because setup is free, there is no platform fee in this model.

## How do you tell whether the routine is working?

Track impressions and clicks by page in Google Search Console, and review the performance data in your Business Profile dashboard each month. Keep a simple sheet of your top keywords and where the shop appears for each one. Search volumes change with season and neighbourhood, so your own data should outrank national averages.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a coffee shop?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up for Search and Maps. The ongoing cost is the time spent on posts, photos and replies.

### Does verification affect whether customers see the shop?

Yes. Per Diem notes that without verification, updates will not show publicly and customers may not find the business. Check Google's current help pages for the verification steps.

### Can customers message a coffee shop through Google?

Yes. Google allows users to message businesses directly through their listings, so someone needs to check that inbox regularly.

### Do reviews appear on the profile itself?

Yes. Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search.
