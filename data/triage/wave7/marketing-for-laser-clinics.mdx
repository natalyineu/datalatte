## Why Do Packages Matter More Than Single Sessions?

Because a single visit does not finish the treatment. [Plastix's laser hair removal page](https://plastixmarketing.com/laser-hair-removal-marketing/) says permanent results need 6-8 sessions, so a clinic that sells one visit at a time is asking clients to come back without a commitment. The same page describes memberships as monthly payments that include annual touch-up access, which gives the clinic a predictable revenue line.

Plastix also states that 85% of revenue should come from packages. That is the vendor's own target, not measured data, so treat it as a goal to test in your own numbers rather than a benchmark. The page names the Candela GentleMax Pro and Cynosure Elite iQ as platforms clinics commonly invest in. If you describe your equipment in ads, match the wording to what your device's own labelling supports.

Your consultation should therefore end with a package recommendation and a clear price, not an open question about "maybe later".

## When Should You Plan Your Campaigns?

Plastix states that hair removal searches spike 73% between March and June as patients prepare for summer. The figure is a vendor's own number with no source given, so use it as a planning signal rather than proof. Check your own search and booking data for the same pattern before you move budget.

Local visibility takes time. [MedSpaMarketPro](https://medspamarketpro.com/laser-hair-removal-marketing-ideas/) puts local SEO and Google Business Profile work at a time to traction of 60-120 days. If the peak starts in March, a profile that begins work in March may only start to rank as the season is ending. Work on your [Google Business Profile posts](/blog/hair-salon-marketing-google-my-business-posts) and service listings in the winter, and use paid search to cover the spring gap.

Campaigns timed to treatment schedules are the other option. The same vendor lists budgets of $500-$2,000 per campaign for seasonal work, which lets a clinic test one spring push before committing to a longer plan.

## What Does One Month of Advertising Return?

All inputs except the package value are **assumptions**. The $2,400 package value comes from Plastix, and the $25-$60 cost range from [Wizerunek](https://wizerunekwsieci.com/local-advertising-on-google-for-small-businesses-ready-to-grow/) (secondary) covers healthcare and dental clinics, not laser clinics specifically.

| Step | Input or source | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | Assumption | $1,000 |
| Cost per booked consultation | Assumption, inside the $25-$60 healthcare and dental range | $40 |
| Consultations booked | $1,000 divided by $40 | 25 |
| Share of consultations that buy a package | Assumption | 20% |
| Packages sold | 25 multiplied by 20% | 5 |
| Package value | Plastix average | $2,400 each |
| Package revenue | 5 multiplied by $2,400 | $12,000 |
| Ad cost per package sold | $1,000 divided by 5 | $200 |

Package revenue is not profit. Devices, staff time and consumables come out of the $12,000. If only 10% of consultations buy, the same month gives 2.5 packages and an ad cost of $400 per package, which is why the close rate matters more than the click price. Track consultations and packages separately from day one.

## How Should You Handle Partners and Reviews?

Reviews are the cheapest lead source you control. [Plastix's competitor-facing pages](https://plastixmarketing.com/laser-hair-removal-marketing/) do not cover reviews, so the useful evidence here comes from [For Vets](https://www.forvets.au/services/websites), which states that it reads every review of the client clinic and of nearby clinics, not only the handful Google displays. Monitor the review sites your clients actually use, and reply to each one.

Partnerships need the same discipline. [AdWave's dental guidance](https://adwave.com/resources/dental-clinic-marketing-strategies) recommends a formal written agreement covering goals, responsibilities, marketing activities and success metrics. A salon or other local business that refers clients to you should sign one before the first referral. The [influencer guide for hair salons](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-hair-salons-how-to-get-free-bookings-from-local-creator) shows how to structure a trade that is easy to track.

## FAQ

### How many sessions does laser hair removal need?
Plastix says laser hair removal requires 6-8 sessions spaced weeks apart for permanent results. Confirm the schedule for your own device and protocol with your clinical team.

### What is a typical package price?
Plastix states that its average 6-session laser hair removal package value is $2,400 or more. Your price will depend on your market and protocol.

### How much should a first campaign cost?
MedSpaMarketPro lists budgets of $500-$2,000 per campaign for seasonal work. Its first-visit service charges $299 for the first month and $1,000 a month after that, with ad spend paid to Meta directly.

### Is the one million patients a year figure reliable?
Weave attributes the estimate of about one million laser hair removal procedures a year to the American Society of Plastic Surgeons, but the page is a secondary source. Check the original society material before you quote it.

### Should my clinic follow the March to June search pattern?
Plastix says searches spike 73% in that window, with no source given. Confirm the pattern in your own search and booking data before you commit budget.
