OpenRouter's free tier lets a small business call models marked with a `:free` suffix through one API, which is enough for batch work such as review replies, social captions and draft emails. The `:free` variant is a free version of a model with its own rate limits, so an automation has to handle limit errors and slow replies. No single free model wins every task, so pick by task. Check the live model list before you commit, because the most recent benchmark of free models was last tested in September 2026.

## What does the :free suffix on OpenRouter mean?

OpenRouter is an AI platform that gives access to a wide variety of language models from different providers through a single API, as [Kilo's OpenRouter documentation](https://kilo.ai/docs/ai-providers/openrouter) describes it. Kilo also states that OpenRouter charges based on the underlying model's pricing.

The [OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq) says the `:free` variant is a free version of the model with its own rate limits. That matters more than the price. A free variant can run out of capacity while the paid version of the same model keeps working, so a workflow that assumes every call succeeds will fail quietly on busy days.

Build for that from the start. Log each call, record which model answered, and send anything that failed to a person rather than posting nothing.

## Which free model fits each automation task?

Published results do not rank free models for review replies or captions, so the choice has to come from a short test on your own work. The steps below take about an afternoon.

Once you have a chain, match it to the work. Review replies and social captions suit a fast model with a generous limit. Customer FAQ answers need more care, because a wrong price or opening time reaches the customer directly. ## How do you keep a free-tier automation running?

Free-tier reliability comes from three habits: a fallback chain, a pause between calls, and a manual path for anything that fails. The snippet below is a minimal version. Replace the placeholder IDs with `:free` models from the live list at openrouter.ai/discover.

```python
import openai
import time

client = openai.OpenAI(
    base_url="https://openrouter.ai/api/v1",
    api_key="YOUR_OPENROUTER_KEY",
)

# Replace with :free model IDs from openrouter.ai/discover
CHAIN = ["primary-model-id:free", "fallback-model-id:free"]

def draft_reply(prompt):
    for model in CHAIN:
        try:
            resp = client.chat.completions.create(
                model=model,
                messages=[{"role": "user", "content": prompt}],
                max_tokens=300,
            )
            return {"text": resp.choices[0].message.content, "model": model}
        except openai.RateLimitError:
            time.sleep(2)
        except openai.APIError:
            continue
    return None  # route to a person for a manual reply
```

Add a short pause between items in a batch so you do not exhaust the limit in one burst. Worked example, with inputs labelled as assumptions: a salon receives 150 reviews a month (assumption), and the script waits 1.5 seconds between calls (assumption). The pause alone adds 150 × 1.5 = 225 seconds, or about 3.75 minutes a month. Model response time comes on top of that, so measure it in your own test.

Schedule large batches for quieter hours. Cache answers to questions that repeat, such as opening hours, so they do not trigger a fresh call each time.

## Can free models write customer-facing text safely?

Hightouch reports, citing Stanford's accuracy benchmarks, that hallucination rates across 26 top-tier frontier models ranged from 22% to 94% ([Hightouch, AI agents for marketing campaign automation](https://hightouch.com/blog/ai-agents-marketing-campaign-automation), secondary). That source covers frontier models in general and does not split them into free and paid variants.

A spread that wide means the model choice alone does not guarantee accurate replies. Treat free-model output the same way you would treat a new hire's first drafts. Keep a person in the loop for anything that quotes prices, hours, policies or medical or legal details.

## When does paying beat staying on free models?

Stay on free models when the work is batch-based and a delay of a few minutes is harmless. Move to paid when a customer is waiting on a live reply or a time-sensitive post must go out on schedule.

OpenRouter's FAQ sets out one cost to check. For BYOK usage above the plan's allowance, the fee is 5% of what the same model and provider would normally cost on OpenRouter. Worked example, with the inputs labelled as assumptions: you bring your own provider key and spend $200 a month above the allowance (assumption). The fee is 5% × $200 = $10 a month. Ask whether $10 buys the reliability you need. If a live chatbot is missing replies, it probably does. If the work is overnight batches, it probably does not.
