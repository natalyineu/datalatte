A FAQ page helps a local business when it answers what customers ask before they book: price, timing, location, cancellation and what to expect. Write each question in the words customers use, group the answers by topic, and link each answer to the page where a visitor can act. Keep the answer text visible in the page itself. Search engines work by improving visibility in Google and other engines, so an FAQ that is readable and crawlable does more than one that only helps people already on the site.

## Why does a local FAQ page still matter for search?

Search engines judge a page by its text, its speed and whether its main content is easy to find. Google's 2025 guidance, as summarised by Adwave, says pages reached from classic or AI search must be easy to move through and must clearly distinguish the main content. A FAQ page that is well organised meets that test more easily than a long block of unstructured text.

The core business case is about visitors, not rankings alone. A restaurant SEO guide reports that more than half of the people who visit restaurant websites arrive through organic search. That figure is for restaurants, so treat it as a hypothesis to check in your own analytics before assuming it applies to your trade.

AI answers add another reason to write clear question-and-answer text. Pew Research, as cited by StackAdapt, found that about 15% of Google searches without an AI Overview result in a click to a website. For more on how this affects local visibility, read our [guide to Google AI Overviews for local businesses](/blog/google-ai-overviews-local-business-seo).

## Where should the questions come from?

Start with the questions customers already ask. Calls, voicemails, emails, contact forms, social messages and the questions in your reviews are the most direct sources. Log them for two weeks and count repeats. The questions that appear most often belong at the top of the page.

Search tools can confirm how people phrase those questions. Google describes Keyword Planner as a free keyword search tool that helps advertisers build keyword lists for search campaigns. Use it to compare how customers phrase a question with how you have written it.

Search Console adds evidence from your own site. Google Search Console is a free tool for monitoring a website's search performance. In the Performance report, an impression means your site appeared in a result, and a click means someone selected it. Use the report to see which searches already reach your site, then match those searches to FAQ questions you have not answered yet.

## How should the answers be built and displayed?

Put the answer in the first sentence, then add the detail a customer needs to act. Give numbers when you have them. Link each answer to the booking, pricing or policy page that covers it. Our [pricing page guide](/blog/website-pricing-page-local-business) shows how to present ranges clearly.

Be careful with accordions. Google states that Googlebot does not reliably activate content that depends on swiping, clicking or typing. If an answer only appears after a click, it may not be used for indexing or ranking. Keep the answer text in the page's visible HTML, and use the accordion only as a visual layer over text that is already there.

Mobile use matters too. Google switched to mobile-first indexing in late 2020, so it evaluates the mobile version of your site. Google also warns that content present only on desktop may not be used for Search indexing or ranking. Check that every answer appears on a phone. For speed, Google's Core Web Vitals guidance treats Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5 seconds and Interaction to Next Paint under 200 ms as good. The [Google Maps embed guide](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) covers one common source of slow mobile pages.

## Worked example: how many calls could a FAQ page save?

Every input below is an **assumption**. Replace them with your own call log before you use the result.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Calls a week asking the same questions | 12 |
| Minutes per call | 3 |
| Share of those calls avoided by the FAQ | 30% |
| Staff cost per hour | $20 |

Calls avoided: 12 x 30% = 3.6 a week. Minutes saved: 3.6 x 3 = 10.8 a week, or about 47 minutes a month. In hours that is about 0.78 hours a month, worth roughly $16 at $20 an hour. The direct saving is small. Any gain in bookings from prospects who find prices and policies answered is not measured here, so track it with your own booking data.

## How do you measure whether the page works?

Choose metrics that match the page's purpose. Adwave lists six local search metrics worth tracking: Google Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift over time. Compare these before and after publishing. Google's SEO guidance says changes can take effect in a few hours or take several months, so a single week is too short to judge the page.

Set review points at 30, 60 and 90 days after publication. Adwave describes this cadence as a way to decide whether to optimise again, promote the page harder, or leave it alone. If you merge older FAQ pages into a new one, keep any page that has traffic, rankings, links or conversion history until there is a replacement plan. [Google Analytics 4 setup for local businesses](/blog/google-analytics-4-setup-local-business) explains how to track visits and calls.

Check consistency across channels. Adwave says the business name, address and phone number must be exactly the same on your website, your Google Business Profile and major directories. If your FAQ lists hours or an address, copy them exactly from those sources.

## How do you update the FAQ over time?

1. Log every new customer question for two weeks, then group them by topic.
2. Write a visible answer for each repeated question, starting with the answer itself.
3. Link each answer to the booking, pricing or policy page.
4. Check that every answer appears on mobile without a click.
5. Compare the metrics above at 30, 60 and 90 days, then decide whether to edit, promote or leave the page alone.

## FAQ

### Should the FAQ answers be hidden in accordions?

Not if the answer is needed for search. Google says Googlebot does not reliably activate content that depends on swiping, clicking or typing. Keep the answer text visible in the page and use the accordion only as a layout option.

### How many questions should the page have?

No source here sets a number. Let your own call log and enquiry records decide. Add a question when it repeats, and remove or merge questions nobody asks.

### Should FAQs go on service pages too?

Yes, for questions tied to that service. Keep one general page for questions that apply across the business, and link to it from the service pages.

### How long before an FAQ change shows in search?

Google says changes can take effect in a few hours or take several months. Review the page at 30, 60 and 90 days rather than judging it after one week.
