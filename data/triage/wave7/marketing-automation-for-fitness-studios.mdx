A fitness studio should automate four follow-ups first: replies to new leads, trial-to-member sequences, missed-visit check-ins and win-back messages for lapsed members. Lapsing can be measured. [Winback Engine's guide](https://winbackengine.com/blog/customer-reactivation-guide/) treats gym and studio members who visit two to four times a week as lapsed after 30 to 60 days of inactivity. Set those windows in your booking software first, then automate the message that fires when a member crosses one.

## Which Automations Should a Fitness Studio Build First?

Start where a delay costs the most. [Mariana Tek advises](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) following up with pop-up contacts within 24 hours using an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades quickly after a pop-up ends. That makes the new-lead reply and the trial follow-up the first two sequences to build.

Build the four sequences in this order:

1. New lead: an instant reply with the class schedule and a booking link.
2. Trial or intro class: a reminder before each session, then a check-in and an offer to join.
3. Missed visits: a short message once a member passes the lapse window you set for their visit pattern.
4. Lapsed members: a win-back message that gives a specific reason to return, such as a new class time.

Keep each sequence to two to four messages. A message that invites a reply still needs a person to answer it, so decide who handles replies before you switch anything on. Assumption: a studio with $20,000 monthly revenue would have a marketing budget of $1,000 to $2,000. Tools, staff time and ads all come out of that figure.

## Worked Example: A Missed-Class Sequence

Every input below is an **assumption** for a studio with 200 members paying $120 a month. The 30 to 60 day lapse window comes from the sourced guidance above; the other figures are not sourced.

| Item | Assumption or calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Members in the studio | Assumption: 200 | 200 |
| Members who hit the lapse window in a month | Assumption: 10% | 20 |
| Share who return after a check-in message | Assumption: 15% | 3 |
| Extra months those members stay | Assumption: 3 | 9 member-months |
| Revenue kept | 9 x $120 | $1,080 |
| Tool cost (email plus connector) | Assumption: $40 a month | $40 |
| Net monthly gain | $1,080 - $40 | $1,040 |

If only one member returns instead of three, revenue kept falls to $360, which still exceeds the tool cost, but staff time spent answering replies is an extra cost this table does not include. Measure returns for 90 days against a month without the message, so you can see whether the result holds.

## How Should You Measure the Result?

The [Wellhub-commissioned survey](https://www.healthandfitness.org/86-of-gym-and-studio-owners-grew-membership-this-year-even-as-costs-and-competition-rise/) of 662 gym and studio owners across 11 countries found that 86% grew their membership base and 56% improved their profit margin. The same survey found that 40% say revenue is growing faster than costs, while 37% say costs are growing faster. Growth and margin are not the same thing, so track both.

Survey results describe the industry, not your studio. Track your own return rate, trial-to-member conversion and cancellations before and after each sequence goes live. ## What Consent and Message Rules Apply?

Email and text rules differ by country and by channel. A booking confirmation is not the same as a marketing text, and the rules for each can differ. Check the official guidance for your country, the consent settings in your platform's help pages, and whether your message type needs written consent before you launch.

Do not assume a rule from one country applies in another. This guide does not state specific legal requirements because those depend on your location and the message type. Speak to a lawyer before sending marketing texts.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid?

Do not automate a sequence before you can answer its replies. Do not text people who gave you only an email address. Do not send a promotion inside a message members expect to be a reminder. Do not connect tools before checking where member data is stored.

Test every sequence on your own phone before it goes live, and keep one list of members and their consent in a single place. If you use an AI assistant for replies, keep a person in charge of cancellations and complaints. ## FAQ

### What is marketing automation for a fitness studio?

It is software that sends an email or text when something happens, such as a new lead, a first class or a missed week. Staff do not need to follow up by hand for each member.

### How much does it cost?

Prices vary by vendor and plan and change often, so check each vendor's live pricing page. A common planning benchmark is 5 to 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing, according to Mariana Tek.

### Which sequence should I build first?

Start with the new-lead reply, because speed matters most there. The trial follow-up comes next.

### Do I need consent to send marketing texts?

Consent rules depend on your country and the type of message. Check the official guidance for your location and speak to a lawyer before launching text marketing.

### How do I know it is working?

Compare return rates, trial conversion and cancellations before and after each sequence, over at least one quarter.
