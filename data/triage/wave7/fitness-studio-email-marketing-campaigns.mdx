A fitness studio should plan about six one-off email campaigns a year on top of automated sequences: a January join drive, an April challenge, a summer attendance push, a September restart offer, a holiday gift pass and a win-back for lapsed members. Send each campaign to one segment, with one offer and one button. [Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) lists an immediate payoff for automated email and SMS once set up, and the calendar below is an assumption to test against your own join dates.

## What should a fitness studio email calendar include?

Tie each campaign to a decision point. People tend to commit to a join or a restart around January and the start of a new season, so those slots deserve your strongest offers. The months below are a starting assumption; move them to match your own join and cancellation data by month.

Suggested calendar (assumption, to test):

| Month | Campaign | Segment | Single goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | New-year join drive | Prospects and lapsed | Book a trial class |
| April | Pre-summer challenge | Prospects and active | Sign up for a 4-week challenge |
| June or July | Summer attendance streak | Active | Keep attendance up |
| September | Back-to-routine restart offer | Lapsed and prospects | Book a restart session |
| November or December | Gift pass or holiday schedule | Active | Buy a gift pass |
| Any quiet month | Win-back | No booking in 30 or 60 days | Return for one class |

If a membership pre-sale runs for three months, [Health Club Consultants](https://www.healthclubconsultants.com/blog/9-keys-to-marketing-a-gym/) advises putting the best offer at the start of the window. Use that principle for the January or September drive rather than saving the strongest offer for the end.

Automated sequences run alongside this calendar. [Vertical Impression's guide](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026) describes a retention sequence with emails at Day 7 (welcome and tips), Day 30 (milestone celebration) and Day 60 (habit encouragement with a referral ask). The full set of flows is covered in [email marketing for gyms and fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-fitness-studio).

## How much can one campaign realistically return?

No sourced open or click benchmark for fitness studios is available here, so set your expectations from your own last three sends. A [Gleantap case study](https://gleantap.com/how-integrated-sales-crm-and-marketing-automation-can-drive-unprecedented-growth/) reports a 50% open rate on personalized fitness campaigns for Studio Svelte. That is a vendor-reported single case, so use it as an example of what personalization can do, not as a target.

Litmus, as cited in the Vertical Impression guide (secondary), puts email marketing returns at $36 for every $1 spent. The figure is not broken down by fitness, so treat it as context rather than a forecast for your studio. Track bookings per send, because a high open rate that produces no trial sign-ups does not pay the bills.

## What does email cost against the members it brings in?

Tool cost is the first line to check. The Vertical Impression benchmark puts email and SMS tools at $100 to $200 a month for an independent studio under 200 members. The worked example below uses assumptions for everything else, so swap in your own numbers before deciding.

| Line | Amount | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Email and SMS tools, low | $100 per month | Sourced benchmark |
| Email and SMS tools, high | $200 per month | Sourced benchmark |
| Annual tools | $1,200 to $2,400 | Monthly figure × 12 |
| Members won through email in year one | 1 | Assumption |
| Monthly membership fee | $120 | Assumption |
| First-year fees from that member | $1,440 | Assumption: $120 × 12 |

At the low tool cost, one email-won member covers the year's tools in gross fees. At $200 a month, two members are needed: $2,880 in fees against $2,400 of tools. These are gross figures before your own costs, so the margin will be lower.

## How should I segment and time each send?

Send each campaign to a segment you can name from your booking system. Active members are those who have attended recently; the 30-day window is an assumption you can change. Prospects are people on the list who have never booked a class.

Lapsed members need a separate message. [Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) suggests win-back campaigns for members who have not booked in 30 or 60 days. Export last-visit dates monthly and tag contacts so each campaign reaches the right group. For setup in a larger platform, see [using Klaviyo for email and SMS in local business](/blog/using-klaviyo-for-email-and-sms-marketing-in-local-business).

## How do I grow the list that email depends on?

Email only works if people are on the list. The Vertical Impression guide suggests a $1,000 to $2,000 monthly Google Ads budget for a well-optimized fitness campaign. It projects 300 to 600 clicks with 3% to 6% conversion to trial sign-ups. That is a projection, not a guarantee, so check results against your own costs before committing. If you want help setting this up, see [Google Ads services](/services/google-ads).

## Do the legal rules change how I send?

Marketing email rules differ by country, and a message that is acceptable in one market can need consent in another. Check your regulator's current guidance for every market you email before the first send. Keep a clear opt-out in every message and process unsubscribes promptly, since complaints and unsubscribes affect whether future emails reach the inbox.

## How do I write a campaign people click?

Follow one rule: one segment, one offer, one button. Put the date and outcome in the subject line, for example "Restart week: 3 beginner classes, Sept 8 to 12". Place the button on the first screen and name the instructor or class so the message does not read like a mass blast.

Send a test to your phone before every campaign. After each send, log opens, clicks, unsubscribes and bookings in one sheet so the next campaign can beat the last. Short-notice offers, such as a same-week class, may suit text better than email; compare the two in [SMS vs email marketing for local business](/blog/sms-vs-email-marketing-local-business-which-is-better). For subject-line drafts, [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) can speed up the first version, but check every line against your offer.

## FAQ

### How many email campaigns should a fitness studio send a year?

About six one-off campaigns plus automated sequences is a workable starting point. This is an assumption; adjust it once you see your own join and cancellation dates by month.

### Is a 50% open rate realistic for a fitness studio?

It is one vendor-reported case for Studio Svelte, so it should not be used as a target. Measure your own opens across three sends before setting goals.

### How much should a small studio budget for email tools?

The Vertical Impression benchmark puts email and SMS tools at $100 to $200 a month for an independent studio under 200 members. Compare that figure with what you expect each member to be worth.

### Does paid search help fill an email list?

The Vertical Impression guide projects that a well-optimized $1,000 to $2,000 monthly Google Ads budget can generate 300 to 600 clicks with 3% to 6% conversion to trial sign-ups. Treat that as a projection and verify it with your own campaign data.
