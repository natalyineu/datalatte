A small business can start video marketing with a phone, natural light and one repeatable format, such as a 15 to 60 second tour of a service. Keep clips short, because [Adwave's social media video guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-make-a-marketing-video) says anything under 60 seconds tends to do well in feeds. Pick one platform where your customers already spend time, post for 30 days and track one action per video before spending money on promotion.

People are spread thin across platforms. [Spotify's podcast advertising guide](https://ads.spotify.com/en-AU/news-and-insights/podcast-advertising-guide/) reports the average person uses 6.7 social media platforms per month. A small team is usually better served by mastering one platform than posting thinly on all of them.

## Which Platform Should You Start With?

Choose the platform your customers already use, then the one you can post on every week. Start where you already have followers, reviews or a Google Business Profile. A short video there can sit next to the details people look for.

Google's guidelines allow businesses to post their own contact information, such as a phone number, email or social handle, on their profile or in responses. They do not allow businesses to solicit personal or confidential information from people, so keep requests for details out of your videos and captions. Check the current [Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) before you publish.

These are recommendations, not platform limits. Check the current maximum length in each platform's help centre before you upload, because limits change and differ by format.

## What Videos Should a Small Business Make?

Start with formats that answer questions your customers already ask:

- A tour of the space or a service in action
- One common customer question, answered in one take
- A staff introduction covering who they are and what they do
- A before and after, filmed only with the customer's permission
- A seasonal offer or event reminder

Give each clip one idea. The one-tip format Bookeo recommends is a good test: if a clip covers three things, split it into three clips. State who you are and what the viewer will see in the first few seconds, and add captions, since many viewers watch without sound.

## How Often Should You Post?

Post on a schedule you can keep for three months. A [salon marketing guide](https://salonbizsoftware.com/blog/daily-salon-social-media-marketing-ideas-how-to-engage-your-audience/) advises starting with at least one post a day and adjusting from there. A [fitness marketing guide](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/) suggests creating at least 5 to 10 posts in each content category in advance, for busy days.

Batching protects you from missed weeks. If you can only film weekly, [Adwave's trends article](https://adwave.com/resources/social-media-trends-for-small-business-in-2026) suggests one 15 to 20 minute live session a week to test format, timing and audience questions. The plan below uses one video per week to start.

| Week | Video | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tour or introduction | Show who you are |
| 2 | Answer a common question | Build trust |
| 3 | Behind the scenes of a service | Show the process |
| 4 | Offer or event reminder | Drive a visit or booking |

For a full calendar template, see our guide to a [social media content calendar for small businesses](/blog/how-to-create-social-media-content-calendar-small-business).

## How Long Until Video Shows Results?

Expect a learning period. Adwave says social media campaigns typically need two to four weeks to optimise and start delivering consistent results, so judge a format over a month of posts rather than after one video.

Completion rates give an early signal on whether people stay to the end. Adwave says social media video completion rates often range from 15% to 50%, depending on format and platform. Track your own completion figures in the platform's analytics and compare each video against your own earlier clips, not against someone else's numbers.

## What Equipment Do You Need?

A recent phone, a window for natural light and a way to keep the phone steady are enough. Clean the lens, face the light source and film short clips you can join together later. A free editing app or the built-in tool on your phone covers most needs.

Check each app's export settings and watermark rules before you commit to a workflow. For music, use tracks the platform provides for business accounts or music you have the rights to, and check the current business music terms in the platform's help centre.

## How Do You Know If Video Is Working?

Pick one action per video: a booking, a phone call, a coupon redemption or a request for directions. Views and likes show interest, but they do not show sales. Give each video a unique link, phone extension or offer code so you can connect it to a result.

Brand recall can matter too. Universal Ads' [Beyond the Scroll study](https://www.universalads.com/blog/how-tv-advertising-supercharges-social-media-performance-for-emerging-brands) reports 5x higher recall when a TV ad is paired with an ad in a social video feed, as of 2025. That is a paid, multi-channel result, so treat it as a reason to repeat a message, not as a benchmark for organic clips.

## How Much Should You Spend to Boost a Video?

Start with a test budget. Adwave's [local advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-small-business) says even $50 to $100 can show which messages connect with a local audience. Once a format earns clear actions, a [personal brand promotion guide](https://adwave.com/resources/promote-my-personal-brand) from the same publisher suggests a few hundred dollars a month to boost top-performing content.

For reach, Adwave's [TV advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/first-tv-ad-beginners-guide) says most local service businesses start with a 10 to 15 mile targeting radius. Set your radius to the area where your customers actually travel, and keep the first test small enough to stop without strain.

## Is a Weekly Video Worth Your Time?

All inputs below are **assumptions** for illustration. Replace them with your own figures.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Time per video, including filming and posting | 90 minutes |
| Videos per month | 4 |
| Monthly time | 6 hours |
| Value of your time | $30 an hour, so $180 a month |
| New customers you can trace to video | 3 a month |
| Value per customer | $70 |
| Monthly return | $210 |
| Net after time cost | $30 a month |

At these numbers, break-even is about 2.6 customers a month ($180 divided by $70), so you need three traced customers to come out ahead. If you trace only one, the video costs you time in the short term. Judge the format over three months and record which clips bring enquiries.

## FAQ

### Do I have to be on camera?

No. Film your hands at work, the product, the space or the team. Voiceover and captions work well for a first set of clips.

### How long should my videos be?

Sources suggest 15 to 60 seconds as a practical range, and Bookeo suggests 30 to 45 seconds for single-tip clips. Check each platform's current maximum length in its help centre before you upload.

### How often should I post?

A salon marketing guide suggests at least one post a day, but a schedule you can keep for three months matters more. A fitness marketing guide suggests batching at least 5 to 10 posts per content category in advance.

### Do I need to spend on ads?

No. Organic posting costs only your time. Once a format works, test a small paid boost, such as $50 to $100, to see which messages connect with your local audience.
