## How Do Clients Find a Nail Salon in Europe?

Clients who want a salon nearby often start with a local search. Trafft gives "nail salon in [city]" as an example of the phrases people type into Google when they want a salon close by. Local searches reward a listing that is accurate, current and easy to act on, because the person searching usually wants an appointment soon, not a long research process.

Booksy says that with Reserve with Google, clients can book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds. That removes a step between seeing your listing and holding an appointment. Keep your opening hours, service menu and photos current, since an out-of-date listing gives a client a reason to try someone else. Test the booking flow yourself before relying on the claim, because Booksy's guide is a vendor source.

## What Is a Repeat Client Worth to a Nail Salon?

Gel or acrylic nails need a visit every 2 to 3 weeks, according to Salonist's rebooking guide. Two to three weeks works out to roughly 17 to 26 visits a year, which is why a repeat client can outweigh a single new one. The table uses assumptions: a £40 visit price and a 30% marketplace commission on the first booking, which is a placeholder, not a published rate.

| Scenario (assumption) | Visits per year | Year revenue at £40 per visit | Marketplace commission on first visit (30%) | Commission as share of year revenue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visits every 3 weeks | about 17 | £680 | £12 | 1.8% |
| Visits every 2 weeks | about 26 | £1,040 | £12 | 1.2% |
| One visit, never returns | 1 | £40 | £12 | 30% |

An own booking link carries no commission on any of these visits. Read the table as a trade-off. If the client stays, the first-visit commission becomes a small share of the year. If the client never returns, the commission equals 30% of everything that client spent. Track rebooking before deciding whether a marketplace pays off. Clients who drift away can be reached again through a [direct mail campaign](/blog/direct-mail-marketing-local) if that suits your area.

## Are Booking Marketplaces Worth the Commission?

Marketplaces sell visibility to people who were not searching for your salon by name. Fees differ: some take a share of a new client's first booking, others charge a subscription, and both can change. Check whether repeat bookings and your own booking link are charged, because that answer shows how much of your takings the platform can claim.

Compare any marketplace fee with the cost of winning a new client another way. Use your own figure, since this guide does not give a European benchmark. Premier Salon Marketing, a Google Ads vendor, claims up to a 33% increase in leads and calls for beauty salons. Treat that as a vendor claim and test it with a small budget first. ## How Should You Use Reviews and Email?

Booksy says over 90% of consumers check reviews before trying a new stylist. That figure appears on the vendor's own blog without a third-party source, so treat it as a direction rather than a measurement. Booksy also says its software prompts clients to leave a review after their appointment, which is a practical way to ask every client without writing each request by hand.

Email is the other owned channel. Booksy says email returns an average of £38 for every £1 spent in the beauty industry, another vendor claim worth testing on your own list. Meevo's examples include a reminder text 24 hours before an appointment and a birthday message with a 10% off coupon. Both are illustrations, not benchmarks. Consent rules for marketing messages differ by country, so check your national regulator before sending a campaign.

## Where Does Social Media Fit?

Bookeo states that potential salon clients spend over 17 hours per week on social media, but it gives no source, so use that figure as context rather than a planning input. Booksy recommends short formats: Before and After reveal videos that capture attention in the first two seconds, and about 15-second clips that explain how to maintain a style or use a tool at home. For a nail salon, a 15-second clip showing a nail-art step or a at-home aftercare tip fits that format. The [TikTok guide for beauty salons](/blog/tiktok-marketing-beauty-salons-europe) covers the format in more detail.

Paid social can also target locally. Booksy gives an example of a Meta ad for bridal styling shown to newly engaged women within a 10-mile radius of a salon. A nail salon could adapt that targeting for a bridal manicure offer. Test one offer at a time and measure bookings, not views.

## What Should a Nail Salon Track Each Month?

1. Count new clients by source: Google, Instagram, a marketplace, a referral or a walk-in.
2. Count how many clients in each group rebook within the 2 to 3 week interval.
3. Calculate the cost per new client for each paid channel, using your own invoices.
4. Ask each client for a review after the visit, and note how many arrive.
5. Draft review requests and rebooking reminders with the [prompts in our ChatGPT guide](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing).

## FAQ

### Which search phrases bring nail salon clients?

Trafft gives "nail salon in [city]" as an example of a local search phrase people use on Google. Make sure your Google listing answers those searches first, with accurate location and service details.

### How often do gel clients need to come back?

Gel or acrylic nails need a visit every 2 to 3 weeks, according to Salonist. Rebooking at each visit is the biggest lever on what one client is worth over a year.

### Are marketplace commissions the same in every country?

No. Fees differ by platform and by country, and they change over time. Check the current partner page for your market and confirm whether repeat bookings are charged.

### Do I need consent to send marketing emails to clients?

Consent rules differ by country, so check your national regulator before sending a campaign to a client list.

### Are short videos worth it for nail salons?

Booksy suggests Before and After reveal videos that capture attention in the first two seconds, and about 15-second clips that explain at-home care. Test them against bookings, not views.
