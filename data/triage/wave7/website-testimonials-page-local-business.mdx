## What do the FTC rules prohibit on testimonials?

The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule was approved in August 2024 and became effective on October 21, 2024. It prohibits creating, selling, buying or knowingly distributing fake reviews, including AI-generated reviews falsely presented as genuine customer experiences, according to [Adwave's summary of the rule](https://adwave.com/resources/trust-signals-on-website) (secondary). It also bars sentiment-conditioned payments and undisclosed insider testimonials.

Enforcement is not limited to large brands. According to [ArentFox Schiff's review of 2026 advertising law](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/the-fine-print/advertising-law-compliance-2026-five-developments-every-advertiser) (secondary), the FTC's 2025 action against telehealth company NextMed alleged fake testimonials and manipulated reviews.

The FTC's Endorsement Guides, which cover reviews and testimonials in advertising, were revised in 2023, as the [FTC's endorsements guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews) explains. This article is a summary, not legal advice. If you operate in the UK, Australia or Canada, check your own consumer-protection regulator.

## What belongs on each testimonial entry?

Each entry should let a stranger judge whether it is genuine:

1. The customer's own words, lightly corrected for typos, with their written permission to publish.
2. A first name and last initial, or a business name for trade clients.
3. The service they booked and the month and year.
4. A link to the original review when it came from Google or another platform.
5. A check that you can still stand behind the quote. Remove entries that are no longer accurate.

Lead with your three strongest quotes, add a short list of the next ones, and finish with a booking button. Run the page through the [local business website checklist](/blog/local-business-website-checklist) to confirm it sits in your main navigation and loads quickly on a phone.

## Do paid or rewarded customers need a disclosure?

Square's loyalty software page states that the businesses featured in its testimonials [were fairly compensated by Square for their time](https://squareup.com/us/en/software/loyalty). That plain statement is the model to copy if you give a discount, a free service or a gift in return for a quote.

According to [Adwave's solar advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/solar-company-advertising) (secondary), testimonials fall under the FTC's Endorsement Guides, and a paid or rewarded customer's relationship must be disclosed. Label any incentive on the page. Never tie a reward to whether the review is positive, because the rule prohibits sentiment-conditioned payments.

## How do you ask for testimonials without breaking the rules?

Ask every customer the same way, at the same point in the job, and do not reward only the happy ones. Asking for a review is ordinary practice. Paying for a particular sentiment is not.

Ask for website permission separately: "May we quote your review on our website with your first name?" Keep the reply in your job file. A simple follow-up after each completed job helps, and the steps in [how to automate customer follow-ups](/blog/how-to-automate-customer-follow-ups-local-business) show how to schedule that message.

Never use AI to write testimonials for customers. The FTC rule specifically covers AI-generated reviews presented as genuine experiences. Check the live review policy on each platform's help page before you change your collection process.

## Where should testimonials appear besides one page?

Place a short quote beside the booking button on your home page and on each service page. That puts proof where a visitor is deciding, not three clicks away. A dedicated testimonials page then works as a destination for links from posts, emails and your listing.

Adwave's social proof guide gives a practical format: a three-slide sequence of claim, full review, then a photo, team member or service context. It also recommends two or three templates reused consistently, which avoids a designer for every post ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/using-reviews-and-testimonials-as-social-proof-on-social-media), secondary). The same guide suggests Stories for fresh proof, Feed posts for evergreen proof and pinned posts for the strongest trust signal.

Pair the page with [landing page examples that convert](/blog/local-business-landing-page-examples-that-convert) so the quotes sit inside a clear offer. Do not build the page from screenshots alone. Typed text is easier to read on a phone and can be indexed.

## FAQ

### How many testimonials should I show?

The sources give no fixed number. Start with three strong, specific quotes and add more as real reviews arrive. Quality and recency matter more than volume.

### Can I write testimonials myself or use AI to draft them?

No. The FTC rule prohibits fake reviews, including AI-generated reviews falsely presented as genuine customer experiences. Use only words from real customers who agreed to be quoted.

### Do I have to show negative feedback?

The sources do not set this rule. Keep the testimonials page to quotes you have permission to publish, and reply politely to public criticism on the platform where it was posted.

### Can I put Google reviews on my site?

You can quote them with permission and link to the original review. Check the platform's live help page before embedding review widgets, because display policies can change.
