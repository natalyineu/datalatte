Word-of-mouth marketing for a local business is a plan to get customers talking about you, in person and online, and to make it easy for them to do so. Extole defines it as customers sharing information about a product or service they love with friends or family. A workable plan has three parts: give people something worth mentioning, make sharing simple, and ask at the right moment. Review42's summary of referral research, republished by AdWave, estimates word of mouth represents about 13% of purchases worldwide, so the habit is worth building on purpose.

This guide is for owners of cafes, salons, studios and trade businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Where a figure is secondary or unverified, the article says so, and where no source gives a number, it does not offer one.

## What Counts as Word-of-Mouth Marketing for a Local Business?

Extole's guide most commonly credits psychologist George Silverman as the pioneer of the field. It gives an anecdote attributed to him in which skeptics' opinions of a drug were swayed by a positive endorsement from one or two of their peers. The mechanism is the point for a shop: a trusted person speaks, and a doubtful listener changes their mind.

Salesforce lists the benefits of local marketing as increased local brand awareness, higher foot traffic, more qualified local leads, better retention from the community, and positive word of mouth. Word of mouth is one result of local marketing, not a separate budget line. For how local marketing is framed in other markets, the [New Zealand local marketing guide](/blog/local-marketing-new-zealand-small-business-guide) gives a useful comparison point.

## How Do You Give People a Reason to Talk?

Recommendations follow experiences worth describing. Pick one thing your team does the same way every time, such as a signature item, fast service or a small unexpected gesture. Write it down so a new hire repeats it. Consistency matters more than novelty, because a customer can only repeat what they can describe.

SmallBizClub presents eight ideas for enhancing word of mouth, written by Megan Totka, and VerticalResponse presents three highly effective strategies. Neither list attaches a figure to its ideas, so use them as prompts to test in your own business rather than as proven results.

VerticalResponse quotes Brad Fay, chief operating officer of Keller Fay Group, who says local banks typically have stronger customer satisfaction scores than big banks. No figures are given. For a local business, the lesson is that closeness to customers is itself a reason to be recommended. For timing a push around seasonal demand, see the [holiday marketing strategy for local business](/blog/holiday-marketing-strategy-local-business).

## How Should You Ask for Reviews and Referrals?

Ask at a good moment, such as after a finished appointment, a completed job or a pickup, when the customer has just had a result they are happy with. Make the link simple: send a direct link to your Google review page by text or email, or print a QR code at the till. Keep the request neutral and do not script what the review should say.

Google's SEO Starter Guide describes word of mouth, where people familiar with a site tell their friends about it, as one of the most effective and lasting ways to promote a site, though it can take time. The same patience applies to a local listing. If your customers recommend through messaging apps rather than reviews, the [Brazil WhatsApp guide](/blog/local-marketing-brazil-small-business-whatsapp-meta) shows how one market builds that channel.

Check the current help pages of the review platform you use before launching any request campaign. Platform rules change, and this article does not restate them.

## What Principles Should Guide Your Program?

The Word-of-Mouth Marketing Association developed a code of ethics checklist, as summarised by Peekage. It says the greatest word-of-mouth strategies are credible, social, repeatable, measurable and respectful. Use those five words as a test for every idea you consider.

Ask three questions. Would a customer who learned of the offer feel the recommendation was honest? Could you repeat it every month without strain? Could you measure whether it works? If an idea fails any of these, rework it before you spend money on it.

Rewards for referrals should be costed before launch. Avoid tying any reward to the content or rating of a review, and confirm the current terms of any platform you use, since this article does not restate them.

## What Does a Referral Reward Actually Cost a Salon?

All inputs below are assumptions for a hypothetical salon, not published figures. Replace them with your own numbers before deciding on a reward.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Average first-visit price | $80 |
| Gross margin on that visit | 50% |
| Credit for the referrer | $10 |
| Credit for the new customer | $10 |

| Result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Margin on first visit ($80 × 50%) | $40 |
| Credits paid per referral ($10 + $10) | $20 |
| Net per referral before repeat visits | $20 |

If the margin on the same visit were 20% ($16), the same $20 in credits would lose $4 per referral. Repeat visits and credits that are never redeemed change the result, so run the numbers with your own data before you commit to a reward size.

## How Do You Track Whether Word of Mouth Works?

Add a "How did you hear about us?" question to booking forms and ask at the till. Record the answer, including any referral name the customer offers, and review the counts monthly. Compare referrals against rewards paid, using the worked example above as a template.

Word of mouth tends to show up slowly in the data, which fits Google's note that the approach can take time. Give a new programme at least a few months before judging it, and change one variable at a time so you know what caused any change.

## FAQ

### Is word-of-mouth marketing free?

Not completely. Staff time, training, review links and any rewards all cost money. Cost each referral reward using the margin on the first visit before you launch.

### Can I offer a discount in exchange for a review?

Avoid it. Check the current terms of the review platform you use, since rules change and this article does not restate them.

### How many reviews do I need?

The sources here give no target. Compare your review count and rating with the three nearest competitors on Google Maps and aim to keep your reviews steady over time.

### How do I measure word of mouth?

Ask every new customer how they found you and record the answer. Track the results monthly, and use referral codes only if you can tie them to a booking.
