## What Decides Whether a Boston Salon Makes the Map Pack?

The primary category is the strongest single lever. Adwave states that analysis of millions of profiles shows the primary category is the number one ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, and it recommends checking the categories used by the top 3-5 businesses in local map results for your service search ([Adwave, categories guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones), secondary). Boston competition differs by neighborhood, so run that check from a few spots across the city, not only from your storefront.

Reviews are the other visible signal. Google states that reviews show next to a business's profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Adwave also reports that GBP signals represent 32% of local pack ranking weight in 2025 ([Adwave, multi-location audit](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit), secondary). Treat that as a directional benchmark. The same vendor's beginner guide repeats the 32% figure without naming a source ([Adwave, map pack guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).

## How Do You Set Up the Google Business Profile for a Boston Salon?

Setup costs nothing. Google describes its Business Profile as free on Google Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile, Canada page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). The work is in the details:

1. Pick a primary category that matches the top local results for your main service, then add secondary services as the profile allows.
2. Write a description of up to 750 characters, leading with your service and your Boston neighborhood. Adwave's beginner guide gives this 750-character limit.
3. Make sure hours, phone number and address are 100% accurate ([Adwave, Google Maps marketing](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary).
4. Upload at least 15 high-quality photos. Adwave says a robust gallery of that size gets far more clicks and calls.
5. Publish at least one Google Post a week. Posts appear publicly only when their status is Live; Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help, posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).
6. Turn on messaging and check it from a desktop. Adwave notes that GBP chats can be managed from the dashboard on desktop, not only the Maps app ([Adwave, messaging](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on), secondary).

A storefront salon with walk-in clients should keep its street address visible. Hiding it is an option only for businesses that work from home or serve customers at their location ([Adwave, beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).

## Which Citations and NAP Rules Matter for Boston Salons?

NAP means name, address and phone number. Adwave says it must be exactly the same on your website, your GBP, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places ([Adwave, voice search guide](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses), secondary). The same vendor's guide adds that the match must hold right down to punctuation and whether the address says "St." or "Street" ([Adwave, beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).

Apple Maps deserves a check. Adwave reports that 88% of people use Google Maps compared with 12% for Apple Maps, but the page names no source for either figure ([Adwave, Apple Business Connect](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), secondary). Use the figures as context, not as targets. Claim your Apple Business Connect listing anyway, because it uses the same NAP and sits in the same directory set. ## How Many Reviews Does a Boston Salon Need?

Adwave cites a Blogging Wizard analysis of 2 million GBP pages, which found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack ([Adwave, reviews guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business), secondary). Review count is therefore a useful milestone, but the page gives no formula connecting a specific count to a specific rank.

Here is a worked example. All inputs are assumptions for illustration, not measured results for any salon:

- Assumption: the salon currently has 120 reviews.
- Assumption: it gains 4 reviews a month with no request system.
- Assumption: a text request sent 24 hours after each appointment lifts that to 12 reviews a month.
- Target: 200 reviews.

At 4 a month, reaching 200 takes (200 − 120) ÷ 4 = 20 months. At 12 a month, it takes (200 − 120) ÷ 12 ≈ 6.7, so about 7 months. The request system does the same job in a third of the time. Hair salons can also compare their review counts with other cities in the [Denver hair salon review benchmarks](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026).

## How Should Salons Name Their Services Online?

Google's AI-driven answers are reportedly built from business profile data, website content and reviews, according to a summary of Google's transition that Adwave cites ([Adwave, Q&A guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a), secondary). That means the service names a customer would type, such as balayage, color correction or men's cuts, should appear in four places: the GBP service list, the website service pages, the reviews, and the owner's replies to reviews.

Google Ads location assets pull their data from Google Maps and GBP, and corrections made there flow into linked Ads accounts ([Google Ads Help, location assets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). Fixing the profile therefore improves paid and organic results together. ## How Do You Track Whether Local SEO Is Working?

Adwave lists six local search metrics it considers useful: GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time ([Adwave, local search intent](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent), secondary). Record a baseline for each before changing anything. Check the local pack for your top keywords from more than one location, because results vary by where the searcher stands.

## FAQ

### Does a Boston hair salon need to pay to appear on Google Maps?

No. Google describes its Business Profile as free on Google Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile, Canada page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). Paid placement is a separate product through Google Ads.

### Do Google Posts appear right away?

Not always. Google reviews each post, and it appears publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help, posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).

### How many photos should a salon upload?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos and says a gallery of that size gets far more clicks and calls ([Adwave, Google Maps marketing](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Refresh them regularly with real work and the salon interior.

### Can salons manage customer messages from a computer?

Yes. Adwave states that GBP messages can be managed from the Business Profile dashboard on desktop ([Adwave, messaging](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on), secondary).
