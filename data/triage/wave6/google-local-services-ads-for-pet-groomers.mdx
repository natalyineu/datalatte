## Is Pet Grooming Eligible for Local Services Ads Where You Live?

Eligibility is set by category and country, so a listing in one market does not carry over to another. Open Google's Local Services help page for your country and search for pet grooming before you build any plan around the ads. Do not assume eligibility because a competitor runs an ad or because a guide describes the US list.

Australian groomers should run the same check. The sources behind this guide do not list Local Services eligibility by country, so treat any category list you find online as a prompt to verify, not as proof.

If pet grooming is not eligible, the budget still has somewhere to go. The [Local Services Ads complete guide](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide) explains the mechanics, and Search campaigns are the usual fallback, covered in the [dog groomer keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-dog-groomers-in-2026).

## What Budget Does a Groomer Need to Test an Ad?

Start from capacity, not from a guess. Vanspeed PRO puts a typical mobile groomer at 20 to 22 working days a month, and the industry standard at 5 to 8 dogs per day for one groomer. The table below uses those ranges and labels every input that is not directly sourced.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Working days per month | 21 | Assumption, within the 20-22 range |
| Dogs per day | 6 | Assumption, within the 5-8 range |
| Dogs per month | 126 | Calculated: 21 times 6 |
| Ad budget per day | $15 | Assumption, within the $10-$20 starting range |
| Days in month | 30 | Assumption |
| Ad spend per month | $450 | Calculated: $15 times 30 |
| New clients from ads | 10 | Assumption |
| Cost per new client | $45 | Calculated: $450 divided by 10 |
| Average groom price | $75 | Assumption |
| First-visit price with 10% off | $67.50 | Calculated, using the 10% offer in the Barklead sample copy |
| Share of monthly dogs from ads | about 8% | Calculated: 10 divided by 126 |

At these assumptions, each new client costs $45 against a $67.50 first visit, so the test covers its cost on the first booking. Change the new-client figure to 5 and cost per client doubles to $90, which is above the first visit. Run the numbers with your own conversion rate before you scale.

## Which Channel Fits a Salon and Which Fits a Mobile Van?

A mobile service has a smaller daily ceiling. Aacenter notes that mobile groomers usually do fewer dogs per day than a busy salon, because of drive time, setup and one-on-one handling. That makes each new client worth more to a van than the same ad would be to a salon with more chairs.

Targeting also changes. Barklead suggests a 10 to 15 mile radius for Facebook ads. An older example from Groomer to Groomer describes a customer base within about 12 miles, from a single business in 2016, so treat it as one data point, not a benchmark. Keep the radius tight enough that a van can reach every booking in a working day.

## Who Are You Trying to Reach With Paid Ads?

Barklead suggests targeting pet owners aged 25 to 60 who are likely to have disposable income, and it cites Facebook's reach of over 2.9 billion active users. Those are platform-level figures, not proof that your town has enough of those owners.

Industry sources point to social discovery too. Love Groomers cites Pet Business Magazine saying 60% of pet owners find new pet care services through social media, a secondary figure you should check against the original. A separate guide from uprinting, citing the National Library of Medicine, reports that 30% of dog owners use full-service grooming salons, which helps set expectations for how much of your market buys in a salon at all.

## How Fast Do Customers Judge Your Listing?

Speed matters before a click turns into a booking. EnvisionNow, citing CXL Institute research, says it takes just 50 milliseconds for a potential client to form an impression of a pet grooming studio. That is a secondary figure, and the study's method is not described on the page, so use it as a reason to make your first screen clear rather than as a measured benchmark.

Make the first thing a visitor sees easy to read: the service, the area you cover, and how to book. A paid ad that sends people to a slow or unclear page wastes the budget you tested above.

## What Should You Check Before You Spend?

1. Confirm that pet grooming is an eligible category for your country on Google's Local Services help page, and note the date you checked.
2. Set a starting daily budget inside the $10 to $20 range from Barklead, and cap it so the monthly total matches your worked example.
3. Define which staff member or station can take the next booking, using the EnvisionNow-style definition of a counter as a front desk or groomer station that can take the next pet for the services it covers.
4. Pick a booking or messaging tool that complies with data privacy rules and handles client information securely, as Build Growth Engine recommends.
5. Track the first-visit offer separately, such as the 10% off used in Barklead's sample copy, so you can compare cost per new client with the first groom.
6. Review the results after the test period you set, and compare them with your assumptions, not with a competitor's figures.

Google's own rules on pricing and lead charges change, so read the current help pages before you rely on any price. ## FAQ

### Can pet groomers use Local Services Ads?
It depends on your country and category. Check Google's Local Services help page for your country and confirm that pet grooming is listed before you plan a budget.

### How much should a groomer spend to test ads?
Barklead suggests starting Facebook ads at $10 to $20 a day and increasing once you find high-performing ads. The worked example above uses $15 a day as an assumption.

### How many dogs can a mobile groomer handle in a month?
Vanspeed PRO gives an industry standard of 5 to 8 dogs per day for one groomer and 20 to 22 working days a month. Multiplying the two gives a rough monthly range, which the example rounds to 126 dogs.

### Does a mobile van need a different ad radius than a salon?
Barklead suggests a 10 to 15 mile radius for Facebook targeting. Mobile groomers do fewer dogs per day than salons, so a tighter radius usually makes routes easier to manage.
