A fitness studio can test podcast ads as a small, tracked experiment, but not as a first marketing step. Produced podcast spots are typically 15 to 30 seconds long, and pre-recorded podcast ads often run 15 to 60 seconds. Many boutique studios budget 5 to 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing, so a podcast test has to fit inside that line. Pick one local or niche show, give listeners one offer, and count every join from it before you buy more episodes.

## Do Podcast Ads Suit a Local Fitness Studio?

Podcast audiences are large, but a studio only earns from listeners who can reach it. A national show spreads your spend across people far from your door. A city or neighbourhood show, or a show aimed at runners, cyclists or new parents in your area, is easier to justify because more of its listeners could walk into your studio.

Reach is only one test. Listeners who look you up need a clear next step. Check that your Business Profile is accurate and that your booking page has a trial offer. If the site only shows a membership price, a podcast ad sends people to a dead end. Fix that before any audio goes live.

## How Long Are Podcast Ad Spots?

Spot length changes what you can say. [Matinée's podcast advertising guide](https://matinee.co.uk/blog/podcast-advertising-guide/) describes produced spots as pre-recorded, professionally mixed audio, typically lasting 15 to 30 seconds. [Tinuiti's Spotify guide](https://tinuiti.com/blog/tv-audio/spotify-ads/) says pre-recorded podcast ads are typically clips of 15 to 60 seconds, inserted within episodes. In 30 seconds you can name the class, the place and one offer. A 60-second spot leaves room for a coach quote but costs more attention.

Spotify's own audio ads are 15 to 30 seconds and play between songs and podcast episodes on the free tier, according to [ClicksGeek](https://clicksgeek.com/best-advertising-platforms/). [Spotify describes Ad Studio](https://ads.spotify.com/en-AU/help-center/ad-studio-overview/) as its self-serve platform for building audio or video campaigns. That is a second route if sponsorship is too costly for a test. The listener hears the ad between songs rather than from a host they already trust, so the two channels work differently. For a wider comparison, read the [digital audio advertising guide for 2026](/blog/digital-audio-advertising-2026-spotify-pandora-podcast-ads).

## How Much Should a Studio Spend on a Test?

Many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing, according to [Mariana Tek's group fitness guide](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/). That is the whole marketing budget, not an extra. A podcast test should be a slice of it, and the rest of the budget should go to channels you already measure.

Set the test budget before you talk to any show. Ask each one for a rate card that shows the fee per episode and the listens or downloads it reports. Flat fees and CPM quotes are hard to compare side by side, so convert each one to a cost per thousand listens. Do not commit to a long run until one episode has produced a tracked join. ## What Should the Ad Say and How Do You Follow Up?

Keep the ad to one offer and one action, such as a trial class, a first-week pass or a free assessment. Give each show its own link or code, so you can match joins to the podcast. Describe the class, the coach and the schedule. Do not promise weight loss or body change that you cannot back up.

Timing matters after the click. Mariana Tek advises studios to follow up with pop-up contacts within 24 hours using an automated welcome sequence, since interest fades fast after a pop-up ends. The same logic applies to podcast leads, so a welcome sequence should start as soon as a listener claims the trial. The [marketing automation guide for local fitness studios](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-fitness-studios) covers the setup. Mariana Tek also describes three to five short-form videos a week as a realistic pace for most studios, so treat podcasts as one channel within a wider plan, such as the [social media strategy for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-marketing-strategy).

## Should You Label Podcast Ads as Sponsored?

Yes. Ask each host to say a plain phrase, such as "sponsored by", close to the endorsement, so listeners know the ad is paid. Vague tags are weaker than plain wording. Advertising rules differ by country, so check the requirements where you operate before you record anything.

Get the host's script in writing before the first episode. Confirm the offer, the link or code, the disclosure wording and the length of the spot. Written terms also make it easier to compare what each show actually delivered.

## What Does a Six-Episode Test Cost? A Worked Example

Every input in this example is an **assumption**. The $100 episode fee and $60 monthly price are placeholders for testing the arithmetic, not market rates. Replace them with figures from your own quotes and member data.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly revenue (assumption) | Studio's own figure | $20,000 |
| Marketing budget at 5% | $20,000 x 5% | $1,000 |
| Test budget (assumption) | Share of the 5% floor | $400 |
| Fee per episode (assumption) | Flat quote, placeholder | $100 |
| Episodes | $400 / $100 | 4 |
| Trial sign-ups (assumption) | Listeners who claim the offer | 8 |
| Trial-to-member rate (assumption) | Share of trials who join | 25% |
| New members | 8 x 25% | 2 |
| Cost per new member | $400 / 2 | $200 |
| Member value, six months (assumption) | $60 x 6 | $360 |
| Revenue from new members | 2 x $360 | $720 |

On these assumptions the test returns $720 for $400 spent. Now lower the trial rate to 10%. The test produces 0.8 members, the cost per member rises to $500, and the revenue of $288 falls short of the $400 spent. The result depends entirely on the trial-to-member rate, so measure it after the first episode rather than assuming it.

## How Do You Run the Test?

1. Set the test budget as a share of your monthly marketing line.
2. Shortlist two or three shows with a local or niche audience, and request rate cards.
3. Write one offer and a unique link or code for each show.
4. Agree the sponsorship wording and the spot length with each host.
5. Run four episodes, then count trial sign-ups and joins per show.
6. Keep, change or stop each show based on cost per new member.

If your booking software already records how each new member found you, use it for the join counts, as described in the [guide to using Mindbody for fitness studio marketing](/blog/how-to-use-mindbody-for-marketing-fitness-studio). Timing a test around a membership push can also help, so see the [New Year membership marketing guide](/blog/fitness-studio-new-year-membership-marketing) if January is your busiest period.

## FAQ

### How much does podcast advertising cost for a studio?

Costs depend on the show and its audience, so there is no single price to quote here. Ask each show for a rate card and convert the fee to a cost per thousand listens. The worked example uses an assumed $100 episode fee.

### Is podcast advertising better than Google Ads?

They do different jobs. Search captures people already looking for a class, while podcasts build awareness among listeners who may not yet be looking. Start by checking that your search ads and profile are set up, then add audio once you can track joins. See the [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads) for help with search.

### How long should a podcast ad be?

Produced spots typically run 15 to 30 seconds, and pre-recorded podcast ads often run 15 to 60 seconds. Shorter spots force a single clear offer.

### How many episodes should a test include?

The four-episode plan in the worked example is an assumption chosen to fit a small budget. It is not a published rule. Run enough episodes to see a pattern in trial sign-ups before deciding.

### Do I need to say the ad is sponsored?

Yes. Ask the host to use a plain sponsorship phrase close to the endorsement, and check the advertising rules that apply where you operate.
