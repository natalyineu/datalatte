Programmatic DOOH means buying ad plays on digital screens through a platform, targeted by location, time and audience. For a local business, published prices vary widely. Some self-serve platforms list campaign minimums of $1,500 to $3,000, while programmatic campaigns generally require $10,000 to $50,000. Programmatic CPMs range from $3 to $20 depending on inventory quality and location. The practical approach is a small, time-boxed test with one offer, screens near your customers, and a comparison area that did not see the ads.

## What Does Programmatic DOOH Cost for a Local Business?

AdQuick lists monthly digital billboard costs from $1,200 in smaller markets to $15,000 or more in major cities. Indoor screens in malls typically run $750 to $2,500 per month, which can suit a single shop or clinic better than a roadside board. These are ranges from a vendor page, not quotes for a specific location.

Digital billboard CPMs are listed at $5 to $10, while programmatic DOOH CPMs range from $3 to $20, depending on inventory quality and location. The cheapest CPM is not automatically the best buy. A screen in the wrong place can waste every impression it delivers.

## Which Minimum Should You Expect?

Minimums depend on the buying route, not only on the screen. The table below puts the figures from the sources side by side so you can ask each platform which route its minimum applies to.

| Buying route | Published minimum | Where the figure comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMB-focused DOOH platforms | $1,500 to $3,000 per campaign | AdQuick; Vistar Media starts around $1,500 |
| Self-serve digital billboards | No monthly minimum, no contract | Blip pricing page |
| Programmatic DOOH campaigns generally | $10,000 to $50,000 | AdQuick cost guide |
| Enterprise DSPs such as The Trade Desk and DV360 | Five-figure monthly minimums | AdQuick, small business platform page |
| Traditional managed DOOH | $10,000 to $25,000 or more | Take 10 Media comparison, vendor framing |
| Recommended programmatic budget | At least $1,000 | Adpersonam guide for small businesses |

The gap between $1,500 and $50,000 is wide. Ask every vendor for its minimum in writing, and confirm whether it applies per campaign, per month or per market.

## How Do Self-Serve and Managed Buys Differ?

Take 10 Media describes traditional managed DOOH as requiring rigid, long-term contracts. Its self-serve programmatic option is pay-as-you-go and can be paused or adjusted at any time. This is the vendor's own framing, so check the terms before you sign anything.

Blip says it gives businesses access to thousands of digital billboards and that its advertising is available on any budget. Its pricing page states there is no required minimum and no contract, and that you can start with a few dollars per day to test a single market. Blip also claims to be trusted by over 200,000 independent businesses.

Adomni claims access to more than 1 million digital screens for DOOH advertising. Treat screen counts as marketing claims. What matters for a local business is whether the screens are within reach of your customers.

## How Many People Actually See the Ad?

Take 10 Media says highway billboard viewers often have dwell times of only 3 to 5 seconds. That suggests roadside messages must be readable at a glance, with one name and one offer. This is a vendor claim, not an independent measurement.

The same vendor says a video ad on a convenience store or ATM screen can capture attention for 1 to 3 minutes in a distraction-free environment. Longer attention favours venue screens over roadside boards for a message that needs more reading time.

According to Vertical Impression, citing OAAA and Harris Poll data from 2024, 73% of adults view digital out-of-home advertising favourably. That is a secondary citation, so use it as context rather than proof that your ad will work.

## How Should You Read CPM Figures?

AdQuick's marketplace reports a median delivered-impression CPM of $8.00 for roadside digital venues, with a range of $7.50 to $8.00. AdQuick also states that these CPMs are computed from delivered ad plays, not full-loop traffic, and are not directly comparable to advertiser-share CPMs on directly booked digital inventory.

In practice, compare like with like. Ask each vendor whether its CPM is based on delivered impressions, full-loop traffic or advertiser share. A $6 CPM and an $8 CPM mean little if the two numbers measure different things.

BidWaves states that it optimises bids, audiences, placements and creative each month for programmatic clients, then reports results in plain language. Ask any vendor what its monthly optimisation cycle includes and how often you will receive reporting.

## What Does a Two-Week Test Look Like?

The numbers below are a worked example. Every input is an assumption, so replace each one with a vendor quote and your own baseline before you decide.

| Input | Value (assumption) |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget | $2,500, inside the $1,500 to $3,000 SMB range |
| Average CPM | $8.00, based on AdQuick's roadside median |
| Test length | Two weeks |
| Baseline new bookings, same area, prior two weeks | 25 |
| New bookings, same area, test two weeks | 40 |

Delivered impressions equal $2,500 divided by $8.00, multiplied by 1,000, which gives 312,500 impressions. Incremental bookings equal 40 minus 25, which gives 15. Cost per incremental booking equals $2,500 divided by 15, or about $167.

That $167 figure is only as reliable as the baseline. If bookings in the area would have risen anyway, the lift is overstated. A [geofencing guide for local businesses](/blog/geofencing-advertising-local-business-guide) covers how to define the target area more tightly.

## How Do You Check Whether It Worked?

Set the comparison area before the test starts. Pick a similar area that did not see the ads, then compare bookings or visits across both areas over the same weeks. Record the baseline in writing so the result cannot be reshaped afterwards.

Set a decision rule in advance. A sensible rule is to continue only if cost per incremental booking is lower than what you would pay for the same result from another channel. ## FAQ

### What is programmatic DOOH?

It is the automated buying of ad plays on digital out-of-home screens through a platform, targeted by location, time and audience.

### What is the smallest budget a local business can use?

SMB-focused platforms list campaign minimums of $1,500 to $3,000. Blip states it has no monthly minimum and no contract, and an agency guide recommends at least $1,000 for programmatic advertising.

### Is self-serve DOOH the same as a managed buy?

No. Take 10 Media describes self-serve programmatic as pay-as-you-go and adjustable at any time, while traditional managed DOOH typically involves long-term contracts. Confirm the actual terms with each vendor.

### How do I know if people saw my ad?

Ask for delivered-impression reporting and confirm how each vendor calculates its CPM. Measure results against a comparison area that did not see the ads, rather than relying on impression counts alone.
