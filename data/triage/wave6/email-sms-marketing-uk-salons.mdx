[Booksy's UK salon marketing guide](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-marketing-ideas) suggests an automated SMS or email 48 hours after a client declines to rebook, a "Come back and see us" incentive once a regular has not booked within 12 weeks, and a birthday treat seven days before the big day. The same page says email returns an average of £38 for every £1 spent in the beauty industry. Before any marketing message goes out, check the Information Commissioner's Office guidance on electronic marketing.

## What Should a UK Salon Check Before Sending Marketing?

A confirmation of a booking the client has already made is different from a promotion, and the rules treat marketing messages more strictly. The Information Commissioner's Office publishes [guidance on electronic mail marketing](https://www.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/) under the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations. Read it before you build a list, because it sets the consent test this article does not restate in detail.

Keep a record of when and how each person agreed to hear from you. Leave out anyone who has not agreed, give every message a clear way to opt out, and act on opt-outs straight away. Check that your booking tool stores this against each client profile. [SalonIQ states](https://www.saloniq.com/article/grow-your-salon-with-sms/) that its system lets salons adhere to GDPR for every client in their own profile, so ask any tool you choose how it records consent.

## Which Three Sequences Bring Clients Back?

Start with messages that serve the client. Booksy's examples give each sequence a clear trigger, which makes them easy to automate in whatever booking system you use.

| Sequence | Trigger | Channel | Example content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rebooking nudge | Client declines to rebook | SMS or email, 48 hours later | Link to book with a preferred stylist |
| Win-back | No booking within 12 weeks | Email with a "Come back and see us" incentive | Next available slots and one offer |
| Birthday offer | Seven days before the birthday | Email or SMS | Complimentary luxury treatment |

Booksy's birthday example is a complimentary luxury treatment, and its win-back example uses the 12-week trigger for a regular client. These are examples from a vendor's guidance, not tested results, so compare the timings with your own gap between visits. The sequences only need one clear trigger each, so you can set them up before you change any other part of your marketing. ## How Often Should a Salon Send Texts and Emails?

The sources here do not set a safe frequency, so the limit has to be your own choice. A reasonable starting point is two marketing messages a month, labelled as an assumption, with unsubscribes tracked against that limit. Reminders for an existing booking do not count toward it, because they only restate a booking the client made.

Booksy also suggests a last-minute tool: an automated SMS blast to a "Top 50" client list can fill a cancelled chair quickly. Keep that message for true gaps in the diary rather than a weekly promotion. If a text starts to read like a sales broadcast, clients will opt out, and the list you have built is worth more than a single discount. ## When Should a Salon Use Text Instead of Email?

Text suits short, time-sensitive messages. Meevo gives an example of an automated system that texts a client 24 hours before their appointment, so the client can confirm or reschedule. Meevo presents this as an illustration rather than a measured result, so test it on your own bookings before you rely on it.

Email gives more room for anything longer, such as aftercare advice, a seasonal menu or a gift voucher. Booksy's £38 figure for every £1 is the only return figure in the sources here, and it refers to email, not text. No comparable SMS return figure is given, so measure both channels in your own diary. ## What Could a Win-Back Sequence Earn? A Worked Example

Lutily cites a Boulevard industry report saying most salons lose about 30% of their client base every year from natural attrition. Applied to a salon with 300 active clients, that is roughly 90 clients a year. The inputs below are assumptions, except the attrition rate, which comes from the secondary source.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Active clients | 300 (assumption) |
| Annual attrition | 30% (secondary, Boulevard via Lutily) |
| Clients lost each year | 300 x 30% = 90 |
| Share of lapsed clients the win-back brings back | 10% (assumption) |
| Clients brought back | 90 x 10% = 9 |
| Visits per returning client a year | 3 (assumption) |
| Average spend per visit | £40 (assumption) |
| Revenue recovered | 9 x 3 x £40 = £1,080 a year (assumption-based) |

Subtract your software fee and per-message cost before you decide. Those costs vary by tool, so check the live pricing page of the platform you plan to use. The example assumes the win-back is the only change to your marketing, which is a simplification, and it treats the 10% return rate as a hypothesis to test rather than a benchmark.

## FAQ

### Do UK salons need consent before sending marketing texts?
Marketing texts and emails are covered by the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, so check the ICO guidance on electronic marketing before you send them. This article does not set out the consent test in detail.

### When should a win-back message go out?
Booksy suggests a "We Miss You" trigger once a client has not booked within 12 weeks. Match the window to your typical gap between visits, which is an assumption to test in your own diary.

### Are birthday messages worth sending?
Booksy suggests sending a birthday offer, such as a complimentary luxury treatment, seven days before the client's birthday. Test it on a small group first and compare rebookings with a control group of clients who receive nothing.

### Should a salon text clients as often as it emails them?
The sources here do not set a frequency. Use text for time-sensitive messages such as reminders and last-minute openings, and keep marketing texts rare.
