## Does Shopify Fit a Local Store or a Service Business?

The clearest fit is a shop with stock, a counter and a steady flow of small orders. Shopify is built around selling products, with a catalogue, checkout and order management. A business that sells appointments, quotes or project work will spend time forcing its offer into a product layout, so a booking-focused site is often simpler.

Many businesses sit in between. A café might sell coffee beans online and take bookings for a roastery tour. The retail side fits Shopify well, while the tour side may need a separate tool. Test the online share of your sales before you move the whole operation.

A published example shows what a retail brand can achieve. [23 Digital's Bottega Coffee case study](https://www.23digital.com.au/portfolio/bottega-coffee/) reports a 3.8x increase in monthly online sales within 6 months of launch. The same page lists a $25K budget and a campaign length of 12+ months. That is one agency's account of one project, not a typical result.

## How Much Does Shopify Cost Each Month?

Monthly cost has three parts: the plan fee, payment processing on each sale, and any paid apps or themes. Shopify publishes plan prices by tier and billing cycle, and billing yearly usually lowers the monthly figure. Check the current table before you budget, because it changes.

Card processing is the cost owners most often underestimate. It applies to every sale, including small ones, and online and in-person rates are usually different. A shop that sells many low-value items pays more in fees relative to its takings than a shop with fewer, larger sales.

## How Do Pickup and Local Delivery Work?

Pickup and local delivery are set up per location, so a shop with two addresses needs each one configured. Shopify's help center covers the setup steps, including how orders are marked ready and picked up. Check whether local delivery is available on your plan, because feature access varies by tier.

The operational questions matter more than the software. Decide who packs each order, how stock is counted at each location, and what the customer sees when an item is sold out. A product that is listed online but unavailable at the counter creates refunds and complaints. Write these answers down before you launch.

Customers who ask about pickup times often message first. If you run that conversation on WhatsApp, see [WhatsApp Business API for local marketing automation](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-local-marketing-automation) for how messaging fits with orders.

## Is Shopify POS Enough for Counter Sales?

Shopify POS handles in-person sales on the same catalogue as the online store, so stock and sales records sit in one place. Whether the included features cover you depends on how many staff log in, how many locations you run, and whether you need multi-location inventory or detailed staff permissions.

Those extras sit in higher tiers or paid add-ons, so your cost comparison has to include them. A single-counter shop with one or two staff may not need more. A business with several sites and a team usually will. Check Shopify's POS pricing page for the current add-on price before you assume the base cost is the full cost.

## What Are the Main Pros and Cons?

Pros:

- One catalogue for online orders, pickup and counter sales.
- Stock and orders visible in one admin area, which reduces manual reconciliation.
- A large app ecosystem for email, reviews and ads.

Cons:

- A monthly plan fee plus card fees on every sale, so volume has to justify the cost.
- Add-ons such as POS features and paid apps can raise the total quickly.
- A service business that mainly takes bookings will often do better with a simpler site.
- Local delivery and pickup depend on setup and plan features, so confirm them before you promise them to customers.

## How Do You Connect Shop Data to Marketing?

Once orders flow into Shopify, the marketing question is what happens next: a reminder email, a repeat-purchase offer, or a local ad aimed at people near the shop. Ecommerce-Platforms.com lists HubSpot Marketing Hub with 2,000 apps and deep two-way data sync with tools such as Shopify, Slack and Stripe. Retention matters as much as first orders. ## What Does a Shopify Month Cost in a Worked Example?

Every input below is an assumption for a small retail shop on a basic paid plan. Replace them with your own figures and the current Shopify rates.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan fee, monthly billing (assumption) | Assumed $39 per month | $39 |
| Online orders (assumption) | 100 orders at $80 average | $8,000 |
| Online card rate (assumption) | 2.9% + $0.30 per order | $262 |
| In-store sales (assumption) | 200 sales at $20 average | $4,000 |
| In-person card rate (assumption) | 2.6% + $0.10 per sale | $124 |
| Total platform and card cost | $39 + $262 + $124 | $425 |
| Share of $12,000 total sales | $425 / $12,000 | about 3.5% |

On these assumptions the platform and card costs equal about 3.5% of sales, before apps, a domain or a theme. Card fees are the largest part. The real question is whether online orders bring in sales you would not otherwise have had. If they mostly move existing counter sales online, the extra cost buys little.

## FAQ

### How much does Shopify cost for a local shop?

The plan fee is published by tier and billing cycle on Shopify's pricing page, and card processing applies on top. Prices change, so check the live page. The worked example in this guide shows how to total both for your own sales.

### Does Shopify support local pickup?

Yes. Pickup is set up per location through Shopify's help center. Each location that fulfills online orders needs its own configuration, so check the steps before you promise same-day collection.

### Can Shopify handle local delivery?

Local delivery is available in Shopify, but access can depend on your plan. Confirm the current delivery features on the help center before you rely on them for a delivery promise.

### Is Shopify better than a booking site for a service business?

Usually not. Shopify is built around product sales, so a business that sells appointments or quotes will likely need a booking tool and may only use Shopify for its product shop.
