In-app ads are banners, video or other ad units shown inside third-party mobile apps. A local business usually reaches them through an ad network rather than buying app space directly. The sources cited in this guide do not publish costs for in-app placements, so no price benchmark is given here. Treat in-app advertising as a retargeting and awareness test to run after search ads already produce leads, and keep it on a small, separate budget.

## How Does In-App Advertising Work?

App owners sell space in their apps through ad platforms. On the supply side, publishers add ad units such as banners, interstitials and rewarded videos, and the network decides which advertiser's ad fills each unit. An advertiser buys access to that inventory through a network, not through the app itself.

For a local business, this means you choose the audience, not the app. Location radius, interests and past website visitors shape who sees the ad. The app is largely outside your control, which is why placement reports matter. They show where your money went, and they are the only reliable way to see whether a given app brings leads or just spend.

Because no benchmark is available, judge any in-app test by your own numbers. Record your cost per click, your conversion rate and the value of one customer, then decide whether the channel pays for itself. The worked example below shows how to do that with clearly labelled inputs.

## Is a Small In-App Test Worth Running? A Worked Example

Every input in the table below is an assumption, not a sourced figure. Replace them with your own account data before deciding.

| Item | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly test budget | $300 | Assumption |

| Clicks | 200 | $300 divided by $1.50 |
| Conversion rate, click to lead | 5% | Assumption |
| Leads | 10 | 200 times 5% |
| Cost per lead | $30 | $300 divided by 10 |
| Lead-to-customer rate | 25% | Assumption |
| Customers | 2.5 | 10 times 25% |
| Average job value | $120 | Assumption |
| Revenue | $300 | 2.5 times $120 |

At these assumptions the test only breaks even, returning $300 on $300 spent. If your cost per click is higher or your close rate is lower, the test loses money. If you can raise the conversion rate or job value, the same budget becomes worthwhile. Run this calculation with your real figures before approving any spend.

## Who Should Use In-App Advertising?

In-app placements suit a business that already gets website visits, has a clear offer, and can follow up with people who looked but did not book. Retargeting is the strongest use, because it reaches people who already know the business. Awareness campaigns can also help a new business become recognised, though their results are harder to measure in leads.

Skip in-app placements if you have no conversion tracking, if your budget is too small to produce a meaningful number of leads, or if your offer only suits people searching right now, such as emergency repairs. In those cases, search ads capture existing demand, and display adds little. ## What Should You Check Before Launching?

Work through these steps before approving any in-app or display spend:

1. Confirm that calls, form submissions or bookings are tracked as conversions, and test each one.
2. Set the location to the area customers can realistically reach from your premises or service route.
3. Run search ads first and let them produce leads for at least two weeks.
4. Only then start a small in-app or display test on a separate budget line.
5. Open the placement report and note any apps or sites that take spend without producing leads.
6. Exclude weak placements, and compare cost per lead with the figure you calculated in the worked example.

Poor placements and accidental clicks are common concerns with app inventory. No rate for these problems is published in the sources reviewed here, so do not assume one. Judge the test on its own cost per lead after two weeks of data.

## FAQ

### What is in-app advertising?
Ads shown inside mobile apps, bought through ad networks rather than directly from the app owner.

### Is in-app advertising worth it for a local business?
It is usually worth testing only after search ads are producing leads. Whether it pays off depends on your own cost per click, conversion rate and customer value.

### Can I choose which apps show my ad?
Most advertisers choose audiences and locations rather than individual apps. Check your ad account for any placement controls it currently offers.

### Is there a cost to list my business on Yelp or Facebook?
According to the Agile Store Locator article, a basic Yelp listing and a Facebook business page are free, with paid features available on Yelp.
