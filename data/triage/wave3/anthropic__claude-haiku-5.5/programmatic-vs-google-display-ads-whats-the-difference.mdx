Display is an ad format, programmatic is a way of buying ads, and the Google Display Network (GDN) is one network you can buy inside Google Ads. Google describes Display campaigns as serving visually engaging ads across a network that reaches 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail. Programmatic buying uses automated auctions across many exchanges. For a local business, the practical choice is between Google Display and a programmatic route that can reach beyond Google's ecosystem. Google Display is itself a form of automated buying, so the real differences are reach, targeting control and cost.

## Is Google Display Network programmatic advertising?

Technically, yes. Fluency notes that GDN is a basic form of programmatic advertising because it uses automation to buy and place ads. It also describes GDN as a "walled garden" limited to Google's ecosystem, while true programmatic uses independent demand-side platforms (DSPs) to bid on inventory across the open web [S2]. Awarity makes the same split: GDN is limited to Google's ecosystem, whereas programmatic can access broader inventory across multiple ad exchanges [S1].

So the distinction is mostly about access, not mechanics. Both routes buy impressions by software. The difference is how many sellers the buyer can reach from one set of controls.

## What are display, programmatic and GDN?

These three words get mixed up, so keep them separate:

- Display is the format: visual ads on websites and apps. Google describes Display campaigns as serving "visually engaging ads on the Google Display Network" [S3].
- Programmatic is the buying method: software buys placements through automated auctions. Awarity says it uses algorithms and AI to deliver targeted ads across multiple platforms [S1].
- GDN is one network. Google says its Display campaigns reach people across 35 million websites and apps and on Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail [S3].

Fluency adds that many advertisers have traditionally relied on GDN for programmatic media, which limits access to inventory outside Google [S2]. A programmatic setup can cover display, but it is not limited to it.

## How do Google Display and programmatic compare on reach and targeting?

Reach is the clearest gap. Awarity says programmatic can access a much wider assortment of placements than GDN, across numerous sites and apps [S1]. Fluency describes DSPs as bidding on inventory across the entire open web [S2].

Targeting differs in the same direction. Awarity says GDN targeting mainly uses interests and keyword-based tactics, while programmatic can add behavioural and contextual targeting [S1]. Google's own page describes optimized targeting, which finds audience segments with the highest likelihood to meet your conversion goals [S3]. Both claims come from different sources with different interests, so test them in your own account. For a shop-level view of placements, see [audio ads vs display ads for local business](/blog/audio-ads-vs-display-ads-local-business).

## What does programmatic cost compared with Google Display?

Fluency says programmatic may involve higher costs than GDN, which typically requires smaller budgets [S2]. Awarity, a programmatic vendor, says programmatic reaches the right audience at a lower cost than Google Display [S1]. These statements conflict, and the vendor claim is unverified. Treat neither as a promise.

Google's Smart bidding uses AI to optimise for conversions in each auction, which can reduce manual work [S3]. The right test is cost per result on your own offer, not cost per thousand impressions across routes. A simple test is to run both routes for the same offer over the same period, then compare cost per booking or call, not impressions alone.

## What would $600 a month buy?

The figures below are assumptions for arithmetic only, not benchmarks. A salon wants $600 a month for awareness.

- Assumption: total monthly budget is $600.
- Assumption: a DSP takes a 15% platform fee on top of media, so media spend is $600 / 1.15 = $521.74.
- Assumption: both routes win impressions at a $4 CPM.

The DSP buys $521.74 / $4 x 1,000 = about 130,435 impressions. If Google's route carried no separate fee, the same $600 would buy $600 / $4 x 1,000 = 150,000 impressions. The gap comes only from the assumed fee. Real CPMs vary by inventory and targeting, so measure your own before you scale. Local marketing budgets are easier to plan once you set a target cost per result.

## Which should a local business choose?

Use a simple rule set rather than a feature list:

- Start with Google Display if you want a fast launch from an account you already run, with limited ad-tech experience.
- Consider Google Display for remarketing and broad awareness, since Google positions Display campaigns for people at every stage of the buying cycle [S3].
- Consider a DSP only if you want reach outside Google's network, such as connected TV, and you can fund a proper test. For that route, see [connected TV advertising in Phoenix](/blog/ctv-advertising-phoenix-az-local-business-guide).
- If your goal is calls and bookings rather than awareness, compare against [Google Local Service Ads](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) before adding display.
- Do not switch routes until you can say what a lead is worth to you. Neither route fixes weak measurement.

## FAQ

### Is Google Display programmatic?

Mechanically, yes. Fluency describes GDN as a basic form of programmatic advertising because it uses automation to buy and place ads [S2]. Google's own help pages describe Display campaigns without using the word programmatic [S3].

### Is programmatic better than Google Display for a small business?

Not by default. Programmatic offers wider reach, while Google Display is simpler and typically suits smaller budgets [S1][S2]. The better choice depends on your budget, your goal and whether you can measure results.

### Does programmatic cost more than Google Display?

Fluency says programmatic may cost more [S2], while Awarity claims it costs less [S1]. Neither source gives a verified rate, so compare cost per result in a controlled test.

### Can I run Display ads only in my local area?

Google Display supports audience and placement targeting [S3]. Check the location settings in your own account, because the sources here do not confirm local radius options.
