Good retargeting shows a relevant follow-up to people who already visited your site, for a limited time, and stops once they book or buy. The rules below cover audience size, time windows, frequency, exclusions and measurement. The most useful numbers are a 180-day Facebook retargeting window, a 28-day conversion attribution window, and a 100-user minimum for Google Analytics 4 audiences. Those figures come from secondary sources, so check them against the platform before you build a campaign.

The sections below explain each rule and include a worked budget example you can adapt to your own numbers.

## Is Your Audience Big Enough to Retarget?

Retargeting only works if the list is large enough for the platform to use. Whitehat reports that the GA4 audience minimum was cut from 1,000 users to 100 users across all networks. That is a secondary report, so confirm the current threshold in your own ad account before you plan spend.

Small local businesses often have a few hundred site visitors a month, and only a fraction of them fall into any one segment. A segment of service-page visitors may take weeks to reach 100 people. If the list is small, do not force a campaign. Use a longer window so more visitors count, or spend on traffic sources first.

## How Long Should the Retargeting Window Be?

WasteNot states that on Facebook you can only retarget users who visited your properties within the last 180 days, and that a conversion can only be attributed to an ad if it happens within 28 days of exposure. Those two numbers set the outer limits of the window.

A short window suits quick decisions, such as a table booking or a same-day appointment. A longer window suits considered purchases, such as a gym membership or a home project. Compare a 7-day and a 30-day window on the same budget. The [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads guide for local business](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) explains which platform fits which decision speed.

## How Often Should People See Your Ads?

None of the sources gives a safe frequency number, so treat any cap as a test rather than a rule. Begin with a low cap on how often one person sees a retargeting ad each week, and raise it only if bookings rise. If the platform does not offer a cap, shorten the window instead.

Watch for signs of fatigue, such as rising cost per click with falling clicks, or the same few people appearing in every report. When those signs appear, pause the ad rather than spending more to reach the same audience. Rotating two or three creative versions, such as a reminder, a review and an offer, helps keep the message fresh.

## Who Should You Exclude From Retargeting?

Exclude anyone who has already booked, bought or submitted a form. Showing a "come back" ad to a paying customer wastes budget and can annoy them. This is advice, not a sourced figure, but it is one of the cheapest fixes available.

Then match the message to the step. A visitor who looked at prices needs an offer or proof. A visitor who abandoned a booking needs a reminder and a direct link. The [SMS vs email comparison for local business](/blog/sms-vs-email-marketing-local-business-which-is-better) shows when each channel fits, and [AI-powered email marketing tools](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-tools) covers automation for follow-ups that ads cannot reach.

## How Much Reach Do You Lose to Privacy Changes?

Retargeting reach is smaller than it used to be. Whitehat reports that 30-50% of users remain unreachable through traditional tracking, and that Apple's App Tracking Transparency has reduced iOS tracking to 14-35% opt-in rates. Both are secondary figures, but they point the same way: browser and app tracking miss a large share of visitors.

The practical response is to build lists from data you collect directly, such as email sign-ups and booking records. Whitehat also reports that 71% of marketers are increasing first-party data collection. Plan the budget on the assumption that the pixel will see only part of the audience.

## How Do You Measure Whether Retargeting Works?

Count bookings, calls and visits, not clicks. A high click-through rate means little if no one books. Whitehat reports an average retargeting return of 4.2x ROAS in 2026, but that average comes from a single secondary guide and will vary widely by business type.

To judge your own results, compare retargeted visitors with similar visitors who did not see ads, if you can. Otherwise, track cost per booking over a fixed period and review it every month.

## FAQ

### What is retargeting?

Retargeting shows ads to people who have already visited your website or interacted with your business. The aim is to bring back people who did not convert on their first visit.

### How many users does a retargeting audience need?

Whitehat reports that GA4 now requires 100 users per audience, down from 1,000. Confirm the current threshold in your own Google Ads account, since secondary sources can lag behind platform changes.

### How long should the window be?

WasteNot reports a 180-day limit on Facebook retargeting audiences. Choose a shorter window for quick decisions and test a longer one for bigger purchases.

### How often is too often?

No source gives a safe number. Start with a low frequency cap, watch bookings, and pause ads that show falling clicks.

### Do I still need retargeting if privacy rules are tightening?

Yes, but plan for smaller audiences. Whitehat reports that 30-50% of users are unreachable through traditional tracking, so direct sign-ups and email lists matter more.
