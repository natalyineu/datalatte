To track marketing ROI for a local business, record what each channel cost, count the customers it brought in, measure what those customers spent, and subtract the cost. ROI is the gain from a channel, after its cost, divided by that cost. ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend, so it ignores your costs and margin. A 400% ROAS can still lose money once cost of goods and fixed costs are counted. This guide sets out five steps that work for a shop, studio or service firm without a data team. ## What Is the Difference Between ROI and ROAS?

ROAS and ROI are different measures. The LinkedIn post by Ken Lyons gives ROAS as (Revenue ÷ Ad Spend) x 100, which shows how much revenue each ad dollar brings back. ROI uses (Revenue - Cost) ÷ Cost x 100. In his example, $1,000 of ads that produced $5,000 of revenue gives an ROI of 400% ([Ken Lyons, LinkedIn, secondary](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bostonseo_the-10-kpis-local-business-owners-need-to-activity-7294752560974856192-8Xw1)).

ROAS stops at revenue. ROI subtracts what it cost to earn that revenue. A business with thin margins can post a strong ROAS and still make no money. Use ROAS to compare ads, and use ROI to decide whether a channel deserves more budget.

| Measure | Formula | Answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost per lead | Spend ÷ leads | Is the channel cheap to try? |
| Cost per acquisition | Spend ÷ paying customers | Does it bring buyers? |
| ROAS | Revenue ÷ ad spend x 100 | Which ads sell most? |
| ROI | (Revenue - cost) ÷ cost x 100 | Should spend increase here? |

## How Do You Tag Every Link So the Source Is Clear?

Each customer should arrive with a label that shows where they came from. The LinkedIn list recommends segmenting traffic by source, whether organic, paid or social, so you can see which channel earns the visit ([Ken Lyons, LinkedIn, secondary](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bostonseo_the-10-kpis-local-business-owners-need-to-activity-7294752560974856192-8Xw1)). Apply the same discipline to every link you control: emails, social bios, printed QR codes and ad destinations.

Keep the labels consistent. Use lowercase names, record them in one shared sheet, and never invent a new name for an existing channel. Two spellings of one channel split your data into two channels and make the comparison meaningless.

If you schedule posts with [Hootsuite or Sprout Social](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business), check whether the tool fee belongs to that channel's cost. Usually it does.

## How Do You Set Up Conversions in Google Analytics?

A conversion is an action that matters to the business. The LinkedIn list defines conversion rate as the share of visitors who take an action, such as buying or signing up. It then treats cost per lead and cost per acquisition as the efficiency checks that follow ([Ken Lyons, LinkedIn, secondary](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bostonseo_the-10-kpis-local-business-owners-need-to-activity-7294752560974856192-8Xw1)). Write down your own version: a booking form sent, a quote requested, a product bought.

Set up that one action in Google Analytics first, then check it weekly. The same list names Google Analytics and Google Search Console as tools for monitoring these metrics. Start with one conversion, because adding five at once makes the numbers harder to trust.

Web analytics only sees people who visit your site. Customers who walk in or phone are invisible to it, which is why the next step matters.

## How Do You Track Calls and Walk-Ins?

For many local firms, the phone and the front door bring the most sales, and web tools miss both. The ClicData guide advises asking customers why they chose your business, which fills that gap ([ClicData, secondary](https://www.clicdata.com/blog/marketing-roi-tracking-guide/)). Add one question at checkout or on the booking form: "How did you hear about us?"

Offer five answers to pick from, such as search, social media, a friend, a flyer or other. Log each answer in your till or a notebook. A promo code for each channel gives a second signal, although no single code captures every customer. Treat the tally as a direction, then compare it with your analytics data.

The LinkedIn list also names attributed store visits, which count in-store visits that follow an online campaign ([Ken Lyons, LinkedIn, secondary](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bostonseo_the-10-kpis-local-business-owners-need-to-activity-7294752560974856192-8Xw1)). Treat it as a goal, and measure it with your own log.

## How Do You Compare Channels Fairly?

Compare channels over the same period and with the same definition of a customer. Count everything you paid for, not only ad spend. Tool fees, printing and discounts belong in the cost. Include the owner's time as well, or the cheapest channel will always look best.

| Channel | Cost to count | What to log |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paid search ads | Ad spend, agency fee | Calls, forms, cost per customer |
| Social ads | Ad spend, design, boosts | Tagged link clicks, bookings |
| Email and SMS | Tool fee, discounts given | Revenue from tagged links or codes |
| Local listings | Owner's time | Calls, direction requests, bookings |
| Referrals | Rewards given | Referred customers and their spend |

For service firms, job revenue is the figure that matters, so the software that records jobs should feed your numbers. A [Jobber and ServiceTitan comparison](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business) shows how field-service tools handle job records. Bookkeeping software records costs, and a [FreshBooks versus QuickBooks comparison](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business) covers that side.

## What Does a $1,000 Ad Month Return?

This example applies the ROI formula from the LinkedIn post, with gross profit in place of revenue so that margin is counted. Every input is an assumption. Replace the inputs with your own figures before deciding anything.

| Line | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Ad spend (assumption) | $1,000 |
| Leads (assumption) | 15 |
| Cost per lead | $66.67 |
| Share of leads who buy (assumption) | 40%, so 6 customers |
| Spend per customer, first visit (assumption) | $150 |
| Revenue | $900 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 40% |
| Gross profit | $360 |
| ROAS (revenue ÷ spend x 100) | 90% |
| ROI on first visit ((profit - spend) ÷ spend x 100) | -64% |

If each customer returns three times in a year (assumption), revenue becomes $2,700 and gross profit $1,080. ROI then becomes +8%. The same campaign moves from a loss to a small gain because of repeat visits, so track returns as carefully as first sales.

## How Often Should You Review the Numbers?

Review the figures on a fixed schedule. The ClicData guide recommends regular team meetings to review campaign metrics and discuss budget changes, and it advises documenting the strategy so the reasoning is not lost ([ClicData, secondary](https://www.clicdata.com/blog/marketing-roi-tracking-guide/)). A one-person shop can manage with a 20-minute weekly review.

Each review should answer three questions: which channel cost the least per customer, which returned the most profit, and which needs a new message rather than more money. Write the answers in the same sheet as the data, so the next review starts from these decisions.

The ClicData guide also separates goals from priorities. A goal is the measurable outcome, such as a 15% rise in UK website traffic in Q4. A priority is the order of work that reaches it ([ClicData, secondary](https://www.clicdata.com/blog/marketing-roi-tracking-guide/)). Set one ROI goal for the quarter and let it set the priorities.

Firms in [Australia](/blog/local-marketing-australia-small-business-2026) can use the same method. Check how local sales tax is treated in your own accounts before calculating profit, since that changes the cost line.

## FAQ

### Does a one-person shop need all of this?

Start with one question: did this channel make more profit than it cost? A tally of how customers found you, plus ad spend, answers it. Add analytics and call logging once spend grows.

### Why does the ad platform show a strong return when the bank balance does not?

ROAS counts revenue, not profit. A 400% ROAS means $4 of revenue for every $1 of ads. If margin is 25%, that is only $1 of gross profit. Subtract cost of goods and fixed costs before judging the channel.

### Can web analytics track everything?

No. It records activity on your website only. Phone calls and walk-in sales need a separate log, such as a checkout question or a call record.

### How do you measure a channel that sends no clicks, such as a flyer?

Give the flyer its own promo code or phone number, then count the customers who use it. Compare that count with the checkout question answers. Expect the result to be an estimate.
