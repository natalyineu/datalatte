## What do the available sources confirm about Starbucks email?

Very little about email itself. A 2016 retelling in [diginomica](https://diginomica.com/starbucks-spills-coffee-on-its-digital-strategy-with-loyalty-changes) (secondary) reports that Starbucks moved its Rewards program from a frequency-based model, where visits earned points, to a spend-based model, where customers earned free drinks by spending over time. According to the article, Starbucks said the change ended customers splitting orders to collect points faster. The article describes the company as having 25 straight quarters of growth before the change ended that run.

The article also reports that the change coincided with the Frappuccino Happy Hour promotion, and that customers reacted negatively. It does not mention email.

The Starbucks loyalty dashboard, which is a primary source, reports 35.8 million US 90-day active members in Q3 FY26, up from 33.9 million a year earlier, and says Rewards members accounted for 59% of tender dollars at US company-operated stores ([Starbucks Investor Relations](https://s203.q4cdn.com/326826266/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/Q3-FY26-Digital-IR-Dashboard.pdf)). These are loyalty figures, not email figures. Other sources returned for this guide, a Pinterest page with a title and no content and a government page showing "Page not found", support no figures and are not used. Any article quoting Starbucks email open rates should be checked against a primary source before it is repeated.

## Why did the 2016 loyalty change backfire?

The retelling quotes CEO Howard Schultz admitting the timing was wrong. He said the company underestimated the interdependence of the Rewards change and the Happy Hour promotion, and that two initiatives competing for attention during the same period disrupted the launch.

The lesson for a cafe is simple: do not send a new loyalty offer in the same week as a seasonal drink launch. Customers can act on only one message at a time, and the louder message wins. Schedule loyalty emails around your own promotions so each message has room to be read.

Schultz also said the company should have built awareness before the promotion, as it had done successfully in the past. For a small shop, that means teasing a new offer one week before it starts, using the counter sign, the receipt and one email, then sending the offer itself once.

## Should a cafe reward visits or spend?

The 2016 change suggests the choice matters, because customers react to how points are earned. A visit-based stamp card is easy to understand and rewards regulars who come often, even if they buy small drinks. A spend-based program rewards larger orders but is harder to explain at the till.

For most single-site cafes, a visit-based card works best because staff can explain it in one sentence and customers can see their progress. Keep the rules unchanged for at least a full season. Changing them mid-cycle is the kind of move that drew criticism in 2016.

Whichever model you use, put the rule in every email. A short line such as "Collect 8 stamps for a free drink" reduces questions at the counter and makes the offer easier to act on.

## Which tactics work for timing and offers?

1. Send a loyalty email only when no other promotion is running, following the 2016 lesson about competing messages.
2. Give every offer a clear end date, such as "this Friday only." Make the deadline real and avoid fake urgency, since customers who feel misled stop opening mail.
3. Use one offer per email. A single free item or small discount is easier to redeem than three choices.

Timing and clarity matter more than design. A plain-text email with one dated offer, your address and your opening hours often does the job. Keep a short calendar of your own promotions so loyalty emails never land on top of a launch.

## Which tactics work for personalization and sign-up?

5. Ask for a birthday month, not a full date, and send a small treat in that month. The month is enough for a planned offer and collects less personal data.
6. Split your list into three groups: new sign-ups from the last 14 days, regulars who visited in the last 30 days, and lapsed customers with no visit in 45 to 90 days. The 45 to 90 day window is a suggestion, not a standard.
7. Send a welcome email the day someone joins. It is the moment of highest interest, and you write it once.

Personalization does not need expensive data tools. A tag in your email software and a note at the counter are enough to start. ## Which email rules apply to a small cafe?

Commercial email rules set requirements for honest headers, clear subject lines, a valid postal address and a working opt-out. In the US, the [FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) covers these points and requires opt-out requests to be honored promptly. Check the current text before you send.

UK, EU, Canadian and Australian rules differ on consent and unsubscribing. If you email customers in more than one country, confirm the requirements for each. The [Canada local marketing guide](/blog/local-business-marketing-canada-guide-2026) covers region-specific context for Canadian businesses.

## What should a cafe do this week?

1. Put a sign-up QR code on the counter and receipt with one line explaining the reward.
2. Add a usual-order question and a birthday-month field to your sign-up form.
3. Write one welcome email: a thank-you, your best seller, one dated offer, your address and your hours.
4. Check your promotion calendar and move any loyalty email that falls within a week of a major launch.
5. Confirm every email includes a working unsubscribe link and your business address.

## FAQ

### Does Starbucks publish email open rates?

Not in the sources reviewed for this guide. The usable article discusses the loyalty program, not email metrics, and the Starbucks dashboard covers membership and spend. Treat any open-rate figure attributed to Starbucks as unverified until you find the original.

### Is email marketing worth it for a small cafe?

It can be. The worked example shows a positive monthly gain under assumed inputs, but the result depends on your own redemption rate and offer cost. Run one offer for a month, then compare visits and revenue before expanding.

### Do I need an app to run a cafe loyalty program?

No. A paper stamp card, a point-of-sale loyalty feature or a spreadsheet with a monthly email covers the core idea. Local search visibility works alongside email, and the [coffee shop Google Business Profile tips](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips) cover that side.

### Do the email rules differ by country?

Yes. Rules on commercial email, consent and unsubscribing vary between the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Check the rules in your own country before sending.
