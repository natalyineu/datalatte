A hair salon gets the most from SMS by using it for four jobs: appointment reminders, filling a cancelled slot, rebooking nudges and handling replies. A UK salon-marketing consultancy recommends reminders 24 to 48 hours before the visit, and warns that blanket texting to every client is expensive and often irritating. Keep each text short, personal and tied to a single action, then measure it with a unique code.

## What Should a Hair Salon Text Clients?

Lockhart-Meyer says SMS works best because it is instant and personal. Its recommended uses are reducing no-shows, sending occasional flash offers to a small set of selected clients, and reminding people about promotional events. Blanket texting to the whole list sits outside those uses.

That leaves four jobs for your text list:

- Reminders for booked visits.
- Gap fills when a slot opens at short notice.
- Rebooking nudges for clients who are due back.
- Replies, including confirming, rescheduling and answering questions.

General news and promotions for everyone fit better in email or social posts. Keeping SMS for these four jobs protects the channel from the overuse that makes clients stop reading.

## When Should You Send an Appointment Reminder?

Lockhart-Meyer prefers a reminder 48 hours before a visit and says a text sent the day before is too late for a client who needs to rearrange. The reasoning is simple: the reminder only helps if it leaves time to act on it.

SalonIQ, a salon software vendor, also recommends sending a reminder two days before and asking the client to reply "yes" to confirm. Its article dates from 2019, so use it as a starting point and test the timing against your own no-show log for a month.

For US salons, that test matters more than any single recommendation. Your own no-show rate before and after reminders is the number that tells you whether the window works.

## How Do You Fill a Cancelled Slot?

Send gap-fill offers late, because urgency helps. Lockhart-Meyer says last-minute texts create a sense of scarcity and make a phone call more likely. On same-day cancellations, text as soon as you know the gap exists.

Send the offer to the few clients who fit the service and the time, not to the whole list. Name the service, the time, how to book and the offer. Add a code so you can count results. Clients who get a relevant text are more likely to reply, and clients who get irrelevant texts are more likely to opt out.

## How Do You Keep Texts Short and Personal?

Use the client's first name and get to the point quickly. The consultant advises staying close to 160 characters, avoiding text-speak and keeping spelling, punctuation and grammar as clean as your other branding. Say what is on offer, how to take it up and what to do next.

Example for a gap fill (illustrative wording, not a tested message):

"Hi Jess, a colour slot has opened tomorrow at 2pm. Book on our link or reply YES. Code GAP1."

The same logic applies to rebooking. A short text naming a time window and a booking link does more work than a long message about your story.

## How Do You Handle Replies and Opt-Outs?

One-way texts leave replies unanswered, and unanswered replies are lost bookings. Bella Booking, an Australian salon software vendor, describes a shared two-way inbox in which YES or CONFIRM can confirm an appointment and STOP ends all SMS to that client. Its design also offers a separate opt-out for marketing-only messages.

That is one vendor's design, but the principle holds for any salon. Someone must read replies, and a STOP request must always work. Assign the job to a named person or a shared inbox, not to whoever happens to have the phone.

Clean the list too. Lockhart-Meyer warns that texting old numbers or clients who have moved away wastes money. Check delivery reports regularly and remove numbers that fail.

## Comparison: Which Text Job Fits Which Goal?

| Text job | Best timing | Who gets it | What to measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Appointment reminder | 24 to 48 hours before | Everyone booked | No-show rate |
| Gap fill | Same day, as soon as the gap is known | Clients who fit the service and time | Codes redeemed, slots filled |
| Rebooking nudge | When the client is due back | Lapsed or due-back clients | Rebooking rate |
| Reply handling | Within the working day | Anyone who texts back | Response time, unanswered messages |

The table makes one point clear: each job has a different audience and a different measure. Mixing them into one blast is the most common way to waste the channel.

## FAQ

### How many texts per month is too many?

No source gives a fixed number. The consultant warns that overuse is what gives SMS a bad name, so send texts only when you have a specific reason, such as a reminder, a gap or a due-back client.

### Should I text all clients about a promotion?

Usually not. Target the clients who fit the service, and use email or social posts for general news. Blanket texts are the pattern the consultant calls expensive and irritating.

### How do I measure SMS results?

Put a unique code in every offer and count redemptions in your booking system. Track no-show rates before and after reminders to see whether the reminder window is working.

### Who should handle client replies?

Assign replies to a named person or a shared inbox with history, so STOP requests and rescheduling messages are never missed. Personal phones make this hard to manage across a team.
