Grammarly's Free plan covers spelling, grammar and basic tone signals. Pro adds full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustment and about 2,000 AI prompts per member each month, at roughly $12 per seat when billed annually. For most salons, Free handles day-to-day writing. Pro is worth testing only when long pieces, such as a monthly newsletter or several service pages, take real hours. The three texts where a typo does the most damage are appointment reminders, public review replies and service pages. Grammarly can catch spelling and grammar in all three. It cannot check a price, a date or whether a post sounds like the salon.

## Where does a typo hurt a salon most?

Three texts matter most, and each needs a different check.

Appointment reminders with a wrong day or time cost more than any typo. Read the date and time against the calendar by hand. Grammarly will not notice a wrong Saturday that reads perfectly well. If an AI tool drafts reminders, test one message on your own phone first.

Review replies are read by future clients as well as the reviewer. Keep them short, thank the person, mention one real detail and do not confirm anything about the reviewer's appointment. Grammarly's tone feedback helps here. Grammarly Business says it detects over 40 tones ([Grammarly Business](https://www.grammarly.com/business/learn/the-value-of-live-agents/), vendor), which is more than a salon needs. Aim for calm and specific, not clever.

Service pages are often written once and never reviewed. Check that each one states the service, how long it takes, a price or "from" price, who it suits and a booking link. A tidy page only helps if people find it, which is covered in the [hair salon local SEO guide](/blog/hair-salon-local-seo-guide).

## How do you set up Grammarly in about ten minutes?

1. Install the browser extension. Grammarly lists Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge, plus desktop apps for Windows and macOS ([eesel AI review](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/grammarly-reviews), secondary).
2. Check that the language setting matches the market you write for, so local spellings are not flagged wrongly.
3. Add salon terms to the personal dictionary: service names, product brands and stylist names.
4. Write a half-page style sheet with the tone, standard booking and cancellation lines, and the sign-off.
5. Read the privacy section below before pasting anything about clients.

## Is it safe to paste client details into an AI writing tool?

Treat any online writing tool as a place where client details should not go. Keep client names, phone numbers and treatment notes out of it. Check the data-use settings in the account, and read the provider's current privacy policy before pasting anything sensitive. Local data rules also apply to a salon, so check those as well. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

## Where does Grammarly fall short for a salon?

- It cannot verify prices, availability or the date of a booking.
- It does not learn a brand voice, so a punchy caption can come back blander ([eesel AI review](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/grammarly-reviews), secondary).
- Its generative output can read as generic. Edit any AI rewrite hard.
- It flags trade terms until they are added to the dictionary.
- It does not know the area, the clients or the waiting list.

## Does Pro pay for itself? A worked example

The inputs below are assumptions except the Pro price, which comes from the secondary review above.

| Input | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seats on Pro | 2 stylists | Assumption |
| Pro price, annual billing | $12 per member a month | Layer3 Labs review (secondary) |
| Pro price, monthly billing | $30 per member a month | Layer3 Labs review (secondary) |
| Average ticket per booking | $90 | Assumption |
| Share kept after products, chair rent and wages | 40% | Assumption |
| Profit per booking | $36 | $90 x 40% |

On annual billing, Pro costs 2 x $12 x 12 = $288 a year. That needs $288 divided by $36, or 8 kept bookings a year, which is under one a month. On monthly billing, the cost is 2 x $30 x 12 = $720, which needs 20 kept bookings a year.

The test is not the price. It is whether Pro's rewrites save real hours on long work. If it only tidies short captions, Free is enough. A single wrong reminder that loses one booking is worth more than the software fee, so check the facts by hand either way.

## FAQ

### Is the free Grammarly plan enough for a salon?

For most day-to-day writing, yes. Free covers spelling, grammar and basic tone signals, with about 100 AI prompts a month. Pro suits people who write long pieces often.

### Will Grammarly make my posts sound robotic?

It can, if every suggestion is accepted. Decline changes that strip out personality, and read each post aloud before publishing.

### Can Grammarly help me rank higher on Google?

Not directly. It checks writing, not local search signals. Work on the Business Profile and website pages separately, using the [hair salon local SEO guide](/blog/hair-salon-local-seo-guide).

### Should client information go into Grammarly?

No. Keep client names, phone numbers and treatment notes out of any online writing tool, and check the data-use settings in the account.
