Responsive search ads (RSAs) let you supply up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, and Google mixes them into combinations that match each search. The best results come from writing each asset as a distinct benefit, pinning only the text that must appear, and judging the ad by conversions rather than by a label. Google's Quality Score, a separate 1 to 10 diagnostic, shows where relevance is weak.

## What changes when you switch from expanded text ads to RSAs?

An expanded text ad (ETA) gives you two headlines and one description. An RSA displays up to three headlines and two descriptions, drawn from a larger pool that Google rotates and tests. According to [Ten Thousand Foot View](https://www.tenthousandfootview.com/how-to-implement-googles-responsive-search-ads/) (secondary, written when RSAs launched), the format offers 90% more text, and Google's framing is that it captures searches a fixed ad might miss.

| Feature | Expanded text ad | Responsive search ad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headlines shown | 2 | 3, drawn from up to 15 |
| Descriptions shown | 1 | 2, drawn from up to 4 |
| Status | Announced as retired in June 2022 ([Gravitate](https://www.gravitatedesign.com/blog/writing-effective-responsive-search-ad-copy-for-google-ads/), secondary) | Current format |
| Who controls combinations | You, fixed by position | Google, based on query and user data |

Because Google chooses which combinations appear, the copy you write is the main control you have. That makes the writing step matter more, not less.

## How do you write headlines that Google will actually use?

Fill every slot with a different idea. Ten Thousand Foot View advises that each snippet should be a unique benefit or feature, because similar headlines get rotated into the same message more than once. Three versions of one promise waste assets and give the system nothing new to test.

Put your core keyword into the headlines. Gravitate recommends giving headlines priority for keyword matches, since a headline that repeats the search term reads as more relevant. For a worked example of picking terms for a specific local service, see [Google ad keywords for acupuncture practices](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-acupuncture-practices-in-2026).

Gravitate also recommends short calls to action in descriptions, and asking or answering a question in a headline. Keep each line specific to what you sell, and only state prices, ratings or offers that are true on the landing page.

## Should you pin headlines and descriptions?

Pinning fixes a piece of text to a position, so it always runs there. Ten Thousand Foot View recommends pinning boilerplate that must appear, such as the main offer or call to action. You can pin several snippets into one position, so Google runs one of them each time, which keeps some testing alive.

Pin only what cannot be left out. Every pinned position reduces the combinations Google can test. If all three headline slots and both description slots are pinned, the remaining assets get little or no chance to show, and the ad loses the flexibility that makes RSAs useful.

## How does Quality Score relate to your RSAs?

Quality Score is a diagnostic from 1 to 10, set at the keyword level. Google's [Quality Score help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118) (primary) says it combines three components: expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing page experience. Each is rated Above average, Average or Below average against advertisers showing for the same search over the last 90 days.

Google states that Quality Score is not a key performance indicator and is not an input in the ad auction. It is a tool for finding weak spots. Ad relevance and landing page experience are the components that RSA copy and the landing page affect most directly, so a headline that matches the search term is a practical starting point.

## What does a worked RSA budget example look like?

The figures below are illustrative assumptions, not Google benchmarks. Take a local plumber with these inputs, each labelled as an assumption:

- Monthly clicks: 500 (assumption)
- Conversion rate before changes: 4% (assumption)
- Average cost per click: $3 (assumption)
- Monthly spend: 500 × $3 = $1,500

Conversions before changes: 500 × 0.04 = 20. Cost per conversion: $1,500 ÷ 20 = $75.

Now assume a rewritten RSA lifts the conversion rate to 5% on the same clicks and spend. Conversions become 500 × 0.05 = 25, and cost per conversion falls to $1,500 ÷ 25 = $60. The $15 saving is hypothetical. Measure your own account over several weeks before deciding a change worked.

## How often should you review RSAs, and what should you check?

A short monthly routine keeps changes logged so their effect can be traced:

1. Confirm each ad group has at least two RSAs, so one ad is not carrying the whole group.
2. Replace any headline that repeats an idea already in the set with one new angle.
3. Check the search terms report for irrelevant queries and add negative keywords.
4. Compare cost per conversion before and after each change, not the ad strength label.
5. Log the change date and what changed, so the next review shows cause and effect.

Ten Thousand Foot View notes that RSA reporting was limited when the format launched and suggests comparing cumulative results against existing ETAs. Check current reporting in the account before relying on that approach.

## FAQ

### Do you need all 15 headlines?

The format allows up to 15, which gives the system more to combine. Add only headlines that carry genuinely different messages, so no two repeat the same benefit.

### Does a low ad strength label stop an ad from showing?

The sources used here do not cover ad strength in detail, so do not treat the label as a guarantee either way. Judge the ad by cost per conversion and by whether it matches the search term.

### Is pinning a bad idea?

Not always. Pin the offer or call to action that must appear in every ad, but leave most positions unpinned so Google can test combinations.

### Can RSAs work for a branded campaign?

Ten Thousand Foot View reports that Google recommends against RSAs in branded ad groups, because branded queries vary little and ETAs usually cover them well. Check current Google guidance before deciding.

### How does Quality Score differ from ad strength?

Quality Score is a keyword-level diagnostic from 1 to 10, built from click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Google says it is not an auction input, so use it to find weak spots rather than as a target.
