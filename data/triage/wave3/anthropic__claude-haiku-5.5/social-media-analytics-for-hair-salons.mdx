A hair salon should track five numbers from social media: reach, profile visits, website or booking-link taps, booking-page visits and actual bookings. The first three measure attention, and the last two measure intent and revenue. Likes and follower counts are left off the list because they do not show whether anyone booked. Tagging every link and asking new clients how they heard about the salon closes the gap that app dashboards leave open.

## Why Track Bookings Instead of Followers?

Competition for clients is growing. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 670,800 barbers, hairstylists and cosmetologists in 2025 and projects employment to grow 7 percent from 2025 to 2035, much faster than the average for all occupations. About 80,300 openings are expected each year, many from workers retiring or moving to other jobs.

More stylists and more salons compete for the same feeds. Trade press reporting on a ResearchandMarkets survey says the hair extensions market is forecast to grow about 8% a year through 2031, with companies forming more partnerships with salons and influencers.

A bigger audience does not guarantee a fuller chair. Followers are an input, and bookings are the outcome a salon needs to measure.

## Which Instagram Numbers Lead to a Booking?

Think of social media as a funnel with five steps: someone sees a post (reach), looks at the profile (profile visits), taps the link (website taps), reaches the booking page (booking-page visits) and completes a reservation (bookings). Each step loses people, so the drop between steps shows where the process breaks.

If reach is high but profile visits are low, the content may attract the wrong viewers or the profile may not explain the service. If profile visits are steady but taps are low, the bio or link is the problem. Associated Hair Professionals argues that a following is only useful when it belongs to people who appreciate what the stylist offers, so check whether viewers match target clients before changing the content.

App menus for these metrics change, so look for the current location of each number in Instagram settings rather than relying on an old screenshot.

## How Do You Tag Links So Traffic Can Be Traced?

Instagram can show taps on a link, but it cannot show who booked. A tagged link lets an analytics tool record where the visitor came from. Use three standard parameters: utm_source for the platform, utm_medium for the type of traffic and utm_campaign for the promotion.

For example, a link might read yoursalon.example/book?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=november-colour. Use lowercase throughout and a new campaign name for each promotion, so the report keeps them separate. Use the same spelling every time, or the same source will appear as several rows.

If the booking tool sits on another domain, make a test booking through the tagged link and check whether the parameters arrive in the analytics report. If they do not, ask the booking provider how it passes campaign data. A simple way to start is to read the [social media management guide for hair salons](/blog/social-media-management-for-hair-salons) before choosing which channels to tag first.

## What Does a Monthly Check Look Like?

Use a short routine and one shared sheet:

1. Export reach, profile visits and website taps for the month from Instagram.
2. Open the analytics traffic report and filter to the Instagram source.
3. Count new bookings from the booking software for the same dates.
4. Count how many new clients named Instagram in the "how did you hear about the salon" answers.
5. Note which posts or Stories ran in the best weeks, then decide one thing to repeat and one to stop.

Compare weeks rather than single posts. A single post can spike for reasons unrelated to content. Paid promotions need the same routine, so the [Meta ads guide for hair salon marketing](/blog/using-meta-ads-for-hair-salon-marketing) explains how to read paid results alongside organic ones.

## Should You Ask Clients How They Found the Salon?

Yes, because no tool sees the whole path. A client may see a Reel, search the salon name a week later and book from a search result. Analytics will record that as a search, not as Instagram. A booking form question catches it.

Add a "How did you hear about us?" field with five fixed options, such as Instagram, Google, a friend, walking past and other. Fixed options make the answers easy to count. Keep the question in place for a full month before judging results, since one or two weeks of answers can mislead. ## FAQ

### What social media metrics should a salon track?
Track reach, profile visits, website taps, booking-page visits and bookings. Follower counts and likes say little about whether a post brings clients.

### How can I tell if Instagram brings bookings?
Use a tagged booking link, check the Instagram source in the analytics report, and ask new clients how they found the salon. Using all three gives a fuller picture than any single tool.

### Why not use follower count as a success measure?
Followers do not show whether people want the services or will book. Bookings and booking-page visits connect directly to revenue.

### How often should I review the numbers?
Review the full report monthly and check bookings weekly during a promotion. Weekly checks catch problems early, while monthly reviews show the trend.

### What if my booking tool does not pass campaign data?
Test with a real booking through the tagged link. If the source does not arrive, ask the provider how it handles campaign parameters, or treat the "how did you hear" answers as the main source.
