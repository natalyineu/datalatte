## What Does the Montreal Online Market Look Like?

Local directories are a real part of how Montreal businesses get found and recommended. Alignable's Montreal page shows members recommending each other, a Recent Products and Services section, and a forum where owners answer questions about getting clients. The page lists 7,136 recommended businesses and 11,444 services at the time it was opened.

Those counts show the competition for attention inside one directory. They do not show your search rankings. Treat directories as one channel among several. A Google Business Profile and a clear website still do most of the work when a local customer searches on a phone, and a directory listing works best as a supporting source of recommendations and referrals.

## Should Montreal Marketing Be in French, English or Both?

Pick one default language for each channel and test the second. The sources reviewed for this version do not include census or language-use figures, so the split should come from your own bookings, call logs and website analytics rather than from population averages. A decision based on your own enquiries will be more reliable than a city-wide estimate.

| Channel | Default approach | Add the second language when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Main language matches your core customers | Bookings or contact forms show demand in the other language |
| Google Business Profile | One primary description, one secondary | Reviews or searches come in mostly in one language |
| Paid ads | Separate ad groups per language | Cost per enquiry is lower in one language |

An agency case study from Archipel for Startup Montreal used the tagline "Business as casual" to present Montreal's friendly approach to business to international audiences. Tone can reflect local culture, but the wording still needs review by a native speaker before it goes live.

## How Should You Set Up Google Business Profile in Montreal?

Keep the business name, address and phone number identical across the profile, the website and any directories. Write the description in the primary language first, then add the second language. A machine translation pasted into the second field does not make a listing bilingual, and it often reads poorly to the people you want to attract.

Reply to reviews in the language each reviewer used. A short reply in the same language shows that a real person reads them. Before adding services or a description, check that every field matches the storefront or service area you actually cover, and remove any service you cannot deliver in the listed area.

## How Do You Run a Bilingual Ad Test?

Give each language its own ad group or ad set, with its own landing page and booking form. That keeps the ad, the page and the booking step in one language, so you can see which language converts. Have a native speaker review every headline and call to action before launch.

Run the test for a full month before moving budget. Compare cost per enquiry, not cost per click, because a cheap click can still produce few bookings. Track both languages in the same report so the comparison is fair. For broader setup steps, see [local business marketing in Canada](/blog/local-business-marketing-canada-guide-2026).

## Worked Example: A CA$800 Bilingual Test

The inputs below are all **assumptions**: CA$800 a month, a 60/40 French/English split, a cost per click of CA$2.50 in both languages, and a 4% enquiry rate. Replace them with your own account data after the first week, because real click costs and enquiry rates will differ by keyword and by page.

| | French | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget (assumption split) | CA$480 | CA$320 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | CA$2.50 | CA$2.50 |
| Clicks | 192 | 128 |
| Enquiry rate (assumption) | 4% | 4% |
| Enquiries | about 7.7 | about 5.1 |
| Cost per enquiry | CA$62.50 | CA$62.50 |

The assumed rates are equal on purpose so the arithmetic is easy to follow. In a real account they will differ, and finding that difference is the point of the test. After a month, move budget toward the language with the lower cost per enquiry and keep a small test running in the other language, so the comparison stays current.

## What Legal Checks Come Before Signs, Ads and Email?

Language rules for signs and commercial advertising in Quebec, and consent rules for commercial email in Canada, are specific and change over time. This version does not state their thresholds or penalties, because the sources reviewed here do not confirm them. Check the current text on the official government and language-authority pages, then confirm with a Quebec lawyer before printing signs or launching a campaign.

Before sending any marketing email or text, confirm that each recipient opted in, that each message identifies the sender, and that an unsubscribe option works. Send messages in the language the customer chose at sign-up, and keep a record of when and how consent was given.

## FAQ

### Should I advertise in French in Montreal?

Check Quebec's language rules for your specific signs and ads before you decide. This guide does not state the requirement, so confirm it with the official language authority or a Quebec lawyer.

### Is a bilingual website worth it for a Montreal business?

It depends on your customers. Use your own enquiry data to decide. If you receive requests in both languages, a bilingual site with separate pages is usually easier to run than mixed-language pages.

### How much should a Montreal business spend on a bilingual test?

Start with a budget you can measure for a full month. The CA$800 example above is an assumption for illustration. Use your own cost per click from Google Ads once the campaign is running.

Yes, for commercial messages you should have consent, identify yourself and include a working unsubscribe link. Confirm the current rules with a lawyer before sending your first campaign.
