No published figure sets a standard Google Ads budget for a barbershop. What can be checked is the cost per click and what a booked appointment is worth. Focus Digital's 2025 study of more than 150 campaigns lists a mid-tier average cost per click of $6.80, which puts $300 a month at about 44 clicks. A sensible approach is to set a test amount you can afford to lose, track bookings rather than clicks, and raise the budget only when cost per booking stays below what a client is worth. Barbershops are not a category in that study, so every figure here is a starting point.

## What Does a Click Cost a Barbershop?

Focus Digital groups campaigns into three cost-per-click tiers. High-tier categories average $11.30 a click, mid-tier categories average $6.80, and low-tier categories average $2.90. The report says mid-tier costs are typical in professional services and some B2B work. Barbershops are not listed, so assuming the mid tier for local searches such as "barber near me" is an assumption, not a finding.

A day spa case published by Sunny Storm (secondary) shows a lower price point. That campaign paid $175 for 92 clicks, about $1.90 each. It was a single-offer ad for a couples massage, so its click price came from a narrow search rather than a broad category. Neither figure is a barbershop benchmark. Measuring your own clicks for a month is the only reliable test.

## What Budget Do the Benchmarks Suggest?

The same report lists recommended monthly budgets. Hospitality starts at $2,700, home services at $3,500, and the mid CPC tier at $3,200. It also states that campaigns below these thresholds typically underperform because they lack volume and exposure. A $300 budget sits well under all of them.

That gap matters. A $300 budget is best read as a test that shows which searches produce calls, not as a steady campaign that will meet the report's thresholds. The report also says metro campaigns form the baseline, while rural and suburban campaigns can run about 15 percent lower because they reach fewer searches. A shop in a dense city therefore needs more budget to reach the same volume as one in a quieter area.

## What Does a $300 Test Look Like?

The table below uses the mid-tier CPC and the day spa booking rate. Every input is an assumption, so it shows the arithmetic, not a forecast.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $300 | Assumption |
| Average CPC | $6.80 | Focus Digital mid-tier average, applied as assumption |
| Clicks | about 44 | $300 divided by $6.80 |
| Booking rate | 34% | Sunny Storm day spa case, applied as assumption |
| Bookings | about 15 | 44 x 34% |
| Cost per booking | about $20 | $300 divided by 15 |
| First-visit value | $35 | Assumption |

At these inputs, 15 bookings at $35 bring in about $525 before costs. If the booking rate falls to 10 percent, bookings drop to about 4.4, cost per booking rises to about $68, and a single first visit no longer covers the cost. The booking rate, not the click price, decides whether the test pays.

## How Should a Shop Run the First Three Months?

Month one is research. Choose one or two services, such as a skin fade or beard trim, and run them on a small budget. Check which search terms led to calls or bookings, and stop paying for the ones that did not. Google's Quality Score, a 1 to 10 diagnostic, rates expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. It is not an auction input, but a weak rating on relevance or the landing page points to a mismatch between the search and the ad.

Month two keeps the terms that booked and cuts the rest. Month three raises the budget only if cost per booking stays below the first-visit value. The [hair salon Google Ads budget guide](/blog/how-much-should-a-hair-salon-spend-on-google-ads) applies the same test-then-scale logic to a chair-based business.

## Does the Landing Page Matter as Much as the Budget?

The day spa case relied on a landing page built for one offer. It had no menu links, and the main focus was a booking button. A barbershop can copy that structure with a page for one service, one price and one booking link. Sending every ad to the homepage gives people more choices than they need and makes the booking step harder.

The same case study notes that 72 percent of potential clients prefer to find local business information through search, citing a secondary retelling of industry statistics. Treat that figure as direction, not as a measured rate for barbers.

## How Do You Know the Campaign Is Paying Off?

Divide the monthly spend by the number of bookings to get cost per booking. The day spa case reported $5.60 per booking on $175 of spend, which shows how wide the gap can be between a strong offer and a weak one. A Toronto agency's planning formula (secondary) multiplies cost per click by clicks per lead and by the monthly lead goal, then refines the estimate after two to three months of results. The same discipline works for bookings: set a goal, track real results, then adjust.

## FAQ

### How much should a barbershop spend on Google Ads per month?
No source publishes a standard figure for barbershops. At a $6.80 mid-tier average click, $300 buys about 44 clicks. Pick an amount you can afford to lose during a test and judge it on cost per booking.

### Is $50 a month enough?
At $6.80 a click, $50 buys about 7 clicks, which is too few to show what works. A small budget may still suit one tightly targeted service, but expect slow results and little data.

### Do Google Ads work for walk-in barbershops?
They can, if you count calls, direction requests and online bookings. Walk-ins are harder to track, so asking new customers how they found the shop is a practical addition.

### Should I spend on Google Ads or fix my profile first?
Fix the basics first, because every ad sends people to them. A current profile with accurate hours and recent reviews costs nothing to maintain, and paid ads add speed to that foundation.
