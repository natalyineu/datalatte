A home service business builds reviews by asking every customer on the day the job ends, using a text with a direct Google review link. Google's ranking guidance says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking, but it also says there is no way to request or pay for a better ranking. The sustainable approach is a steady, honest request routine, not a one-time push or a paid shortcut.

This guide sets out a simple request process for plumbers, electricians, HVAC and other trades, the practices to avoid, and a worked example. The Google help pages reviewed here do not publish review-rate statistics, so the figures in the worked example are labelled assumptions.

## What Does Google Say Drives Local Ranking?

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well a profile matches what someone is searching for. Distance is how far each business is from the searcher. Prominence is how well known a business is, and Google links it to how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has. [Google's ranking tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) state that more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking.

Reviews are therefore one input among several. The same guidance says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to appear in local results, so a review routine works best when hours, service area and categories are also current. Google's [edit guide](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617) shows where each of those fields sits. A trade business can list up to nine additional categories beyond its primary one, so choosing the right primary category matters before any review push.

## When Should a Trade Business Ask for a Review?

Ask when the work is finished and the customer has seen the result, ideally before the technician leaves or within the same day. This timing is a practical recommendation, not a published statistic. The customer still remembers the visit, and the technician can mention that a text will follow, so the message does not arrive as a surprise.

Make the request easy to act on. A text sent from the job-management tool already in use avoids asking the technician to type anything. If the tool cannot send texts, a printed card with a QR code in the invoice envelope is a reasonable fallback. Keep the same routine for every job so the process runs without decisions in the moment, and the team does not have to remember who was asked.

## What Should the Request Message Say?

Keep the message short, name the person who did the work and link straight to the review form. A sample message to adapt:

> Hi Dana, thanks for choosing us today. If the repair went well, would you leave a short Google review? It takes about a minute: [link]

Do not mention the star rating you hope for, and do not write a message that asks only for praise. Sending the same wording to every customer keeps the process honest and easy to audit. If a CRM is in use, the same template can run automatically after each completed job. ## What Should a Trade Business Avoid?

Do not offer a discount, gift or free service in return for a review. Google's ranking help says there is no way to request or pay for a better ranking, and paying for reviews undermines the credibility that makes reviews useful to customers. Treat any offer of cash or goods for posting as off limits.

Do not ask only customers who seem happy. Filtering requests this way makes a profile look selective and skews what prospective customers read. Ask every customer after every completed job. Rules on reviews vary by country and state, so check the platform's current policy and ask a qualified adviser if it is unclear what applies to the business.

## How Should Replies to Reviews Work?

Google's ranking help says positive reviews and helpful replies can help a business stand out, so reply to every review. Thank the customer, use their first name and mention the type of job. Keep replies short and specific so they read as human and do not repeat the same sentence across every review.

For a negative review, reply once, stay factual and offer an offline route to fix the problem. Do not argue in public or accuse the reviewer without evidence. Paid channels can bring more leads, but they do not create reviews, so a reply routine is the part of reputation work that a small team can control directly. ## How Long Does It Take to Reach 50 Reviews?

All inputs in the table below are **assumptions** for a small plumbing company, not measured results.

| Input or result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Completed jobs per month (assumption) | 40 |
| Customers sent a request (assumption) | 100% |
| Share who post a review (assumption) | 15% |
| New reviews per month (40 × 100% × 15%) | 6 |
| Current reviews (assumption) | 8 |
| Months to reach 20 reviews ((20 − 8) ÷ 6) | 2 |
| Months to reach 50 reviews ((50 − 8) ÷ 6) | about 7 |

The 15% is a cautious placeholder. Track the real rate for two months and replace it with the measured number. If the rate is lower, the timeline stretches, so check whether the request is sent promptly and whether the message is clear. Reviews also carry more weight when they are recent, so keep the routine running after the 20-review mark rather than stopping there. ## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a home service business need?
The Google pages reviewed here do not set a minimum. Twenty is a practical first target for a trade business, with recent reviews counting most. Track the count monthly and keep requesting after reaching it.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?
No. Offering goods, services or discounts in exchange for reviews is a practice to avoid. Google's ranking help says there is no way to pay for a better ranking, and paid reviews damage trust with customers.

### Should I ask only happy customers?
No. Filtering requests makes a profile look selective and skews what customers read. Send the same request after every completed job.

### What if a customer leaves a bad review?
Reply once, politely and factually, and offer to fix the problem offline. Do not threaten or accuse the reviewer.
