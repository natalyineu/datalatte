A Facebook lookalike audience is a group of people Meta finds because they resemble a source list you supply, such as past customers. For a local business, the source list matters more than the audience size. Upload your best customers, keep the ad location tied to your service area, and judge the test by cost per lead and cost per customer, not by reach. Meta's current source minimums and size options change, so check them in Ads Manager before spending.

This guide covers which source to upload, how to build and test one, and what a small test might cost. Figures are sourced where possible, and worked examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Is a Facebook Lookalike Audience?

A lookalike audience starts with a custom audience built from your own data. Meta reads that list, identifies shared traits, and shows your ads to people who are similar but not already on your list. Klientboost describes the source as website visitors, social media followers, [email subscribers](https://klientboost.com/email/how-to-build-an-email-list/), or current customers (secondary).

The approach rests on a simple point. People who resemble your customers are more likely to respond than a cold audience chosen by interests alone. Brolik notes that the method matters more as targeting options shrink, because it relies on data the business already owns (secondary).

## Which Source List Should a Local Business Upload?

The strongest source is the group of customers who are most valuable to the business. Brolik suggests narrowing the list to customers who order several times a year or who spend above the average order value (secondary). A salon might use clients who rebook every six weeks. A gym might use members who renewed twice.

Brolik also points to Customer Value Audiences, where a business can add lifetime spend or another ranking to its list so Meta can weight the most valuable customers (secondary). A smaller, cleaner list usually performs better than a large list of people who only liked the page.

Remove staff, suppliers and duplicates before upload. Confirm the current minimum number of people required from a single country in Ads Manager, because the requirement can change.

## How Do You Build the Source in Ads Manager?

The steps below follow the general path Klientboost describes (secondary). Menu labels shift over time, so match them to the screen in front of you.

1. Install the Meta pixel on the website so events are tracked. The [Facebook pixel setup guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-setup-guide) covers this step.
2. Open the audiences section of Ads Manager and create a custom audience. Choose customers if a list exists, or website visitors if the business is new.
3. Name the audience with the source type and date, such as "Repeat customers, May."
4. Create a lookalike audience from that custom audience and select the country or region.
5. Set the ad set location to the service area, so the ads do not reach people who cannot visit.

For more detail on source types, read the [custom audiences guide](/blog/facebook-custom-audiences).

## Which Audience Size Should You Test First?

Start with the narrowest size setting Ads Manager offers. A narrower lookalike is a closer match to the source, which usually suits a local business with a small list. Widen the audience only if delivery stalls or the budget cannot be spent.

Do not judge the size by reach alone. Compare the lookalike with a plain interest or broad audience at the same budget. Track three numbers: cost per lead, the share of leads who become paying customers, and cost per customer. Those figures show whether the source is working.

## When Should You Skip a Lookalike?

Skip the lookalike if the source list is small, mostly made of cold leads, or cannot be tracked after the form. A list of 60 customers may fall below the minimum Meta requires, and a wider setting does not fix a weak source.

In that case, run a simple local campaign first and collect leads for several weeks. If leads arrive faster than the team can respond, a [customer service agent for local businesses](/blog/ai-agent-for-customer-service-local-business) can handle first replies while a better source is built. Gyms and studios with recurring memberships may find the [Facebook ads guide for gyms and fitness studios](/blog/facebook-ads-for-gyms-fitness-studios-complete-guide) useful for choosing a source list that reflects renewals.

## FAQ

### How many people does a lookalike source need?
Meta sets a minimum number of people from one country. Check the current figure in Ads Manager, because it can change.

### Which lookalike size should I start with?
Start with the narrowest setting available. Widen only if the audience cannot spend the budget or delivery is poor.

### Can a local shop use lookalike audiences?
Yes, if it has a source list. Limit the ad location to the service area so the ads reach people who can visit.

### Do lookalikes beat broad targeting?
No general answer exists. Test both at the same budget and compare cost per customer.

### What does a lead cost on Meta?
It depends on industry, offer and location. Use your own test data, and treat the worked example above as an assumption.
