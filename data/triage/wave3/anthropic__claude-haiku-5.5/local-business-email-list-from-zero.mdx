To build a local business email list from zero, ask customers while they are in front of you, make joining one tap or one line, and send only to people who agreed. A target of 500 subscribers in 90 days works out to about 39 sign-ups a week. Treat that as a planning goal, not a benchmark. ## What Rules Apply When You Collect Email Addresses?

Consent rules differ by country, and the rules that apply depend on where your customers are, not only where your business is. A local business in the US, UK, Australia or Canada should check the official guidance for its own market before it starts collecting. This article does not restate those rules, so treat any sign-up plan as incomplete until that check is done.

One habit protects you in every market. Record that the person chose to join, when they joined, and how. Keep the sign-up wording next to the record so you can show what they agreed to. Never add someone to a list because you have their phone number from a booking or a receipt, even if the number was given for a different purpose.

## Which Free Tool Should You Start With?

You do not need a paid platform on day one. A Google Contacts label can work as a simple list for small sends. A Scrap.io guide describes this method: you select contacts, create a label, then type the label name into the To or Bcc field in Gmail to send to the whole group ([Scrap.io](https://scrap.io/how-to-create-an-email-list-in-gmail), secondary).

The same guide notes the trade-off. Replies go only to the sender, and unsubscribe handling is manual. A dedicated email tool handles unsubscribes and tracking automatically. Once a business sends a regular newsletter, the manual version becomes a risk, so the move to a dedicated tool should be planned before the list grows.

## Where Do the First 100 Addresses Come From?

Start with people already in contact with the business. They are warm, they have already chosen to deal with it, and they are the easiest group to ask.

| Where | How to ask | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter, desk or till | Staff ask, or a QR code sits beside the till | Highest intent, asked at the moment of payment |
| Receipts and booking confirmations | Link to the sign-up page | Customers see it right after the visit |
| Website and Google Business Profile | Sign-up form or link | Needs existing traffic to pay off |
| Social profiles | Link in bio | Followers are not subscribers until they sign up |

Give a clear reason to join that the business can deliver, such as early access to a seasonal menu or an offer on the next visit. State what will be sent and how often. Avoid vague promises that cannot be kept, because a subscriber who feels misled is unlikely to stay.

## What Should the First Email Say?

Send a welcome message within a day of sign-up, while the person still remembers the business. Keep it short. Thank them, deliver the offer that was promised, say what to expect and how often, and include a way to unsubscribe and a business address.

After that, choose a rhythm that can be sustained. A fortnightly email that actually goes out beats a weekly plan that stops in week five. Each message should do one job, such as announcing a new product, an event or a change to opening hours. The [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) article includes prompt patterns that can be adapted, but every draft should be checked against the real offer before it is sent.

## Can a Counter Reach 500 in 90 Days?

All inputs below are **assumptions** for a single-location shop. Replace them with real counts once they are available.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Customers served per week (assumption) | 400 |
| Share asked to join (assumption) | 50% |
| Share of those asked who agree (assumption) | 15% |
| Counter sign-ups per week | 30 |
| Other sources per week (assumption) | 5 |
| Total new subscribers per week | 35 |
| Total after 13 weeks | about 455 |

At 35 a week, the shop reaches about 455 after 90 days, a little short of 500. Asking 70% of customers instead of 50% adds about 12 sign-ups a week. That lifts the total to about 47 a week and roughly 611 over 13 weeks.

Now take a smaller shop. Assume 150 customers a week with the same rates. That gives about 11 counter sign-ups plus 5 from other sources, or 16 a week, and about 208 after 13 weeks. For that shop, a 200-subscriber goal fits the data better than 500.

## What Does the 90-Day Checklist Look Like?

Use this sequence to turn the plan into weekly actions. Each step is a task to complete, not a result to expect.

1. Week 1: Write the sign-up line, the offer and the welcome email. Record where and how consent is captured.
2. Week 1: Print a QR code for the sign-up page and place it at the counter with a one-line reason to join.
3. Week 2: Add a sign-up link to receipts or booking confirmations.
4. Week 2: Choose a tool. If a Google Contacts label is used first, set a date to move to a dedicated tool.
5. Weeks 3 to 6: Ask every customer at checkout for a fortnight and count the yes answers each day.
6. Week 6: Compare the weekly count with the target. Adjust the offer or the way the ask is worded, but never the consent process.
7. Weeks 7 to 12: Send one newsletter per fortnight. Track which messages get replies or visits.
8. Week 13: Review the total against the goal and set the next target.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to build an email list from zero?

It depends on how many customers a business sees and how many it asks. The example above reaches about 455 in 90 days at 400 customers a week, using stated assumptions. The sources behind this plan do not give a typical figure for local businesses.

### Can I email customers who gave me their address at the till?

Only if the rules where the customer lives allow it and the business can show what the customer agreed to. Record the consent and the wording the customer saw. If the position is unclear, check the official guidance for the market before sending.

### Is it okay to buy an email list?

No. People on a bought list never asked to hear from the business, so they are unlikely to read its emails, and the consent position is weak. Growing the list from customers who choose to join is the approach this plan takes.

### Do I need a paid email tool from the start?

Not necessarily. A Google Contacts label can handle small sends ([Scrap.io](https://scrap.io/how-to-create-an-email-list-in-gmail), secondary). Move to a dedicated tool once automatic unsubscribe handling, tracking or regular newsletters are needed.
