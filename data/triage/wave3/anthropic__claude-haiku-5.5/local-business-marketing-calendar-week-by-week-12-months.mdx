A local marketing calendar has two layers: a weekly routine you repeat all year, and a short list of seasonal peaks you prepare for four to six weeks ahead. The routine has four tasks: ask recent customers for reviews, post one update to your Google Business Profile or social page, send one message to your list, and check calls, directions and bookings. Peaks are fixed dates such as Valentine's Day, Mother's Day and the holiday season, plus any local period your sales history shows is busy. The sources used here describe calendar structure but contain few quantified claims, so this guide avoids sales forecasts. Every number in the worked example is an assumption.

## What Should a Marketing Calendar Include?

A marketing calendar turns strategy into a schedule. [MarketingCoach.ie](https://marketingcoach.ie/marketing-calendar/) says a calendar shows whether your channel mix relies too heavily on one channel, where workload peaks, and how the budget is spread across the year. It also helps you decide which tasks to hand to an outside provider.

The second benefit is shared visibility. [Boltic](https://www.boltic.io/blog/marketing-calendar-template) describes a single calendar view as a way to see publishing timelines and asset deadlines, which reduces missed deadlines. For a local business where one or two people handle marketing, the calendar becomes a record of what is due and what has been done.

If you schedule posts with software, compare tools before committing. The [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social guide](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) covers how the two differ for small teams.

## What Goes Into the Weekly Routine?

Keep the routine to four tasks so it survives a busy month. Each week, ask recent customers for a review using your Google review link or a QR code. Post one update to your Business Profile or social page. Send or schedule one message to your email or text list. Then spend ten minutes checking calls, direction requests and bookings against the week before.

Four tasks is a planning choice, not a research finding. The point is consistency: a routine you can repeat for a year beats a detailed plan you abandon in March. If bookings run through field-service software, check how the scheduling tool handles appointments before you plan promotions around it. The [Jobber vs ServiceTitan guide](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business) compares the two for service businesses.

Review requests should be honest and unincentivised. Do not offer discounts or rewards in exchange for reviews.

## Which Dates Deserve Extra Planning?

Peaks are dates when local demand for a service or product rises. The table lists common peaks and what to prepare for each. The dates follow standard calendar conventions. None of the sources used here confirms them for a given year, so check each one before planning.

| Peak | Timing | Why it matters locally | Prepare |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Valentine's Day | February 14 | Couple bookings and gifts | Gift cards, couple offers, extra slots |
| Mother's Day | Second Sunday in May | Gifts and treat bookings | Gift cards, booking slots, a simple offer |
| Back to school | Late summer | Family bookings and routines | Family offers, school-term memberships |
| Small Business Saturday | Saturday after Thanksgiving | Local shopping day | Local offer, window sign, email |
| Holiday season | November to December | Gift buying and year-end bookings | Extended hours, gift cards, reminders |

Do not treat every peak as equal. Your own sales history matters more than any national calendar. Compare last year's calls, bookings and sales for each period, then put effort into the peaks that actually moved your numbers.

## How Should Each Quarter Be Themed?

Spreading themes across the year keeps no month empty. A simple pattern works well:

- Winter (January to March): new-year routines, a Valentine's offer, and a profile clean-up with fresh photos.
- Spring (April to June): Mother's Day gift cards, outdoor events, and a review drive.
- Summer (July to September): quieter months for many shops, so use them for list building and loyalty offers, then back-to-school promotions late in the season.
- Autumn (October to December): holiday hours, Small Business Saturday and gift card sales.

These themes are a framework, not a finding from the sources. Adjust them to match your busy and slow months. If you run a business in Australia, the local guide to [Australia local marketing](/blog/local-marketing-australia-small-business-2026) covers timing and competition for that market.

## How Far Ahead Should You Prepare?

None of the sources gives a standard lead time, so treat the following as a working rule. Start planning four weeks before each peak, and finish the creative two weeks before. Set special hours and offer details as soon as you know them, so customers see them in time.

Paid campaigns need more lead time. Build and check them early enough to run a test before the peak starts. Keep a short checklist for each peak: the offer, the dates, the hours, the creative, the booking or landing page, and the number you will compare afterward.

## What Does a Peak Plan Cost? A Worked Example

The inputs below are all assumptions for a nail salon preparing for Mother's Day, which falls on a Sunday in May.

- Assumption: the offer is a $50 gift card with a 10% bonus, so the bonus is $5 per card.
- Assumption: the target is 30 gift cards sold.
- Assumption: ads cost $60 in total.

Cash taken in is 30 × $50 = $1,500. The bonus cost is 30 × $5 = $150. Total cost is $150 plus $60, which is $210. The cost ratio is 210 ÷ 1,500, about 14%. Replace these assumptions with your own numbers before deciding anything.

| Weeks before | Task |
| --- | --- |
| 6 | Choose the offer and set a cap on gift cards |
| 5 | Update the Business Profile with hours, gift card link and photos |
| 4 | Email the list and post the offer |
| 3 | Ask recent clients for reviews |
| 2 | Send a reminder and post the slots still open |
| 1 | Send a final reminder and check bookings against capacity |
| After | Count gift cards sold and bookings, then note what to change |

The success measure must be set before the peak: gift cards sold, or appointments booked from the offer. Without a number fixed in advance, you cannot tell whether the plan worked.

## FAQ

### Do I need a full-year marketing calendar?

A calendar helps you avoid last-minute work, but the weekly routine matters more than a detailed year plan. Start with the routine, then add the peaks that fit your trade.

### Which dates should a local business plan for first?

Start with the dates your own sales history shows as busy. Valentine's Day, Mother's Day and the holiday season are common starting points for many businesses.

### How do I know whether the calendar is working?

Compare calls, directions, bookings and sales during each peak with a normal period. Use the same measure each time so the comparison is fair.

### Should I post on social media every day?

No source used here sets a daily posting standard. Choose a rate you can keep up for a full year, then stick to it.
