## Why Should Window Companies Optimize for Sold Jobs Instead of Leads?

Netembark, a Google Ads agency, argues that window companies should optimize toward appointments that are held and sold, not form fills, and qualify prospects before a rep drives out. Its guide suggests qualifying on the form or call by asking the number of windows, whether the home is owned or rented, the timeline, and whether all decision-makers can attend ([Netembark, secondary](https://www.netembark.com/ppc/home-improvement/window-companies/)).

Speed matters too. A quick confirmation call or text reduces no-shows, though texting must follow the consent rules that apply where you operate. Netembark also recommends reporting cost per lead, cost per appointment set, cost per appointment held and cost per sale, separately for each campaign.

This framing changes the decision. A campaign that produces cheap enquiries from homeowners who never answer the phone is worse than a dearer campaign that fills the diary with decision-makers. Treat lead count as a diagnostic and the sold job as the result.

## How Should Window Campaigns Be Split by Search Theme?

Home Service Direct, another agency, and Netembark both separate window searches into distinct themes. Replacement searches such as "window replacement near me" or "window installation company" are usually the highest-value core. Type and material searches, such as "vinyl replacement windows" or "bay window replacement", need product-led pages. Repair searches such as "foggy window repair" are smaller jobs and may need their own budget or a negative keyword ([Netembark, secondary](https://www.netembark.com/ppc/home-improvement/window-companies/)).

Give each theme its own campaign or ad group and its own landing page. Brand bidding is a special case: Netembark notes that brand use is for dealers only, and ad-text use needs authorization under Google's [trademark policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6118).

Egress searches, such as "egress window installation", are code-driven and often pair with basement finishing. Cost-planning searches like "cost to replace windows in a house" show research intent and tend to need a lower bid. The same split logic applies to other home trades, as in the [Google Ads for garage door companies](/blog/google-ads-for-garage-door) guide.

## Which Negative Keywords Stop Wasted Spend?

Window campaigns attract off-topic searches, so block them before launch. Netembark's list covers software (microsoft, windows 10, windows update, laptop, pc), cleaning (window cleaning, window washer), auto (window tint, car window, auto glass, windshield) and appliances (window ac, window air conditioner) ([Netembark, secondary](https://www.netembark.com/ppc/home-improvement/window-companies/)).

Add your own exclusions as well. A company that does not do repairs should block repair, fix and broken. If no financing is offered, block financing searches. Hobby and job-seeker terms such as "diy", "jobs" and "salary" are common leakage points.

Review the Search Terms report weekly. Each new irrelevant term should go onto a shared negative list, so the fix applies across every campaign rather than one ad group.

## How Do You Track Appointments Through to the Sale?

Netembark recommends separate conversion actions for each stage: appointment set, appointment held and sold, with contract value. Capture the GCLID on forms, use call conversion tracking for calls from ads and the landing page, and upload CRM outcomes through offline conversion import or enhanced conversions for leads ([Google Ads Help: offline conversions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2998031)).

Set the conversion window to cover the sales cycle. Netembark points to Google's guidance of up to 90 days ([Netembark, secondary](https://www.netembark.com/ppc/home-improvement/window-companies/)). It also notes that uploads move to the Data Manager API starting June 15, 2026, so any custom integration should be checked now.

If sold volume is too thin to bid on, use appointment held as the primary bidding conversion. Move to sold with value once there are enough outcomes. Review requests after a completed install also build trust for the next lead, and the guide to [AI agents for Google reviews management](/blog/ai-agent-for-google-reviews-management) covers how that work can be handled.

## What Do Quality Score and Landing Pages Change?

Google's Quality Score runs from 1 to 10 at the keyword level. It combines expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience, each rated above average, average or below average against advertisers showing for the same search over the last 90 days ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118)).

Google is explicit that Quality Score is a diagnostic, not a key performance indicator, and it is not an input in the ad auction. Do not optimize it in isolation. Use it to find where an ad or landing page is weak. A window page that names the service, the town and a tap-to-call number usually scores better on relevance than a generic homepage.

Changing match type does not change Quality Score, because the score is based on exact searches. A dash in the column means there are not enough exact searches to score the keyword yet.

## What Does a $3,000 Budget Return?

The table below is a worked example. Every input marked assumption is illustrative. Replace it with your own figures after a month of data. Cost per click, click-to-lead rate and lead-to-sold rate decide whether the account pays.

| Line | Tough | Base |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $15 | $10 |
| Click-to-lead rate (assumption) | 3% | 5% |
| Clicks | 200 | 300 |
| Leads | 6 | 15 |
| Lead-to-sold rate (assumption) | 10% | 18% |
| Sold jobs | 0.6 | 2.7 |
| Ad cost per sold job | $5,000 | $1,111 |
| Average job value (assumption) | $8,000 | $8,000 |
| Gross margin after materials and installers (assumption) | 30% | 30% |
| Gross profit per job | $2,400 | $2,400 |
| Gross profit from sold jobs | $1,440 | $6,480 |
| Profit after ad spend | -$1,560 | +$3,480 |

Two formulas turn this into targets. Maximum cost per lead equals gross profit per job multiplied by lead-to-sold rate. In the Base column that is $2,400 × 18%, or $432. Maximum click price equals maximum cost per lead multiplied by click-to-lead rate, so $432 × 5% gives about $21.60 a click. The Tough column fails on both counts: its maximum click is about $7.20, yet it pays $15.

Use gross margin after materials and installers, not the sale price. A thinner margin lowers every ceiling quickly. Track outcomes by booked job, since one job often requires several quotes.

## What Should You Do in the First 30 Days?

Work through these steps before scaling spend.

1. Calculate gross profit per window or door job and your realistic close rate on quoted leads, then use the formulas above to set a maximum cost per lead.
2. Build one search campaign for one service area, with ad groups for replacement windows, patio doors and entry doors, using phrase and exact match. Add the negative list above.
3. Create one landing page per service, each with a tap-to-call number and a short quote form.
4. Set up conversion tracking for form submissions, ad calls and landing page calls, with stage-based CRM uploads, before the ads go live.
5. Set a callback rule for every new lead, and book a weekly 20-minute slot to read the Search Terms report and pause wasteful keywords.

## FAQ

### How much should a window company spend on Google Ads per month?

There is no published standard in the sources for this guide. Decide from the number of jobs you want, your gross profit per job and your close rate. The worked example above shows how a $3,000 budget performs under different assumptions.

### What if $500 a month brings no leads?

Check location targeting, search terms, tracking and the landing page first. At the click prices in the example, $500 buys only a few dozen clicks, which may be too few to judge. Narrow to fewer, more specific keywords before concluding that the market does not work.

### Should I use broad match?

Google states that Smart Bidding works best with broad match, per Netembark's summary of Google's guidance ([Netembark, secondary](https://www.netembark.com/ppc/home-improvement/window-companies/)). Without enough recorded conversions, start with phrase and exact match and review the Search Terms report weekly.

### How long before ads bring jobs?

Calls can arrive within days, but window quote cycles are longer. Plan for several weeks before judging cost per booked job. This is a planning rule of thumb, not a published Google figure.
