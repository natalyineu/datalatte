## What Does Google Say Decides Local Ranking?

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity. Relevance means how well a profile matches a search, so complete and detailed business information helps. Distance is how far each business is from the person searching, and Google uses its own location knowledge when the searcher does not share one.

Prominence is the third factor. It reflects how well known a business is, and Google bases it partly on how many websites link to the business and how many reviews it has. More reviews and positive ratings can raise a local ranking. Google also states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking, so reviews have to come from customers who choose to leave them.

## How Do You Keep a Profile Complete?

Google says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local results. If the information is wrong, the profile may not appear for relevant searches. The table below covers the fields Google lists, with an action for each.

| Field | What Google says | Action this month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business name | Use the name on real signage and stationery | Match the storefront exactly |
| Primary category | The first category is the primary one | Pick the most specific category that fits |
| Additional categories | Up to 9 more can be selected | Add only categories that describe main services |
| Service area | Set the cities or zip codes served | Be specific and accurate |
| Hours | Special hours can be set for holidays | Update holiday hours in advance |
| Phone numbers | Up to 2 more numbers, mobile or landline | Add a second line if it takes bookings |
| Attributes | Details such as parking or Wi-Fi | Fill in every attribute that applies |

Google may occasionally contact the verified phone number on a profile by call, text or WhatsApp to confirm business details. Any answer given may update the profile. The collected information can be reviewed under Edit profile, where its source and date are shown. Items can be deleted, though deleting them does not change other details already on the profile.

## Should You Reply to Reviews?

Google says replying to reviews shows that a business values customer feedback, and that positive reviews and helpful replies can help it stand out. A reply is the one review-related action fully within the owner's control, and it does not ask anyone for anything.

Keep replies specific. Name the service or detail the customer mentioned, thank them for the feedback, and describe the change made if a complaint was valid. Dispute details belong in a private channel rather than a public argument. Prospective customers also read replies to see how problems are handled before they visit. ## What Should a Business Avoid?

The clearest line in Google's guidance is that no one can buy a better local ranking. Any offer of a paid ranking boost, a guaranteed star rating or a set number of reviews should be treated as a warning sign. A profile that is flagged or emptied of reviews loses far more trust than a slow review count costs.

Staff, friends and family should not post reviews under their own names. Discounts or gifts in exchange for feedback should not be offered on any platform. A tactic that works around a platform's rule about asking for reviews is likely to be found and penalised. The safer approach is to make visiting easy and let customers decide whether to write anything.

## How Do You Check Yelp Rules Before Acting?

The sources used for this guide do not include Yelp's policy text, so no Yelp rule is stated here. Before running any request, reminder or incentive on Yelp, read Yelp's own content guidelines and recommendation policies in full, and follow the most recent version.

Where a rule is unclear, leave the tactic out until it is confirmed. A review count that disappears from a rating because a tactic broke a rule costs more than a few weeks of slower growth. The Google-based steps in this guide, such as a complete profile and prompt replies, do not depend on Yelp's wording.

## Worked Example: A 90-Day Plan for a Salon

All inputs below are **assumptions** for planning. They are not Google or Yelp data.

- Customers per month (assumption): 400.
- Share who leave a review without being asked (assumption): 2%.
- Reviews per month: 400 × 2% = 8.
- Reviews over 90 days: 8 × 3 = 24.

| Week | Step | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Complete every field in the profile table, with 10 photos and the full service list | All fields filled and checked |
| 2 | Confirm the verified phone number and review any collected info | Collected info reviewed, unwanted items deleted |
| 3 to 12 | Reply to each new review within one working day | No review left unanswered past the first working day |
| 3 to 12 | Add one new photo of the work or the space each week | Ten new photos by week 12 |
| Monthly | Log the services customers mention most and fix any repeated complaint | One fix recorded per month |

The 2% is a guess. Track the real rate for at least one quarter and judge the plan against that figure. ## FAQ

### Does Google let businesses pay for a better local ranking?
No. Google's help pages say there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Local results are based on relevance, distance and prominence.

### Do reviews affect local ranking on Google?
Google lists reviews and ratings as part of prominence. More reviews and positive ratings can help a business's local ranking.

### Should I reply to reviews?
Google says replies show that a business values customer feedback, and that helpful replies can help it stand out.

### What if Google contacts my phone number?
Google may occasionally contact the verified phone number by call, text or WhatsApp to confirm business information. The collected information can be reviewed and deleted under Edit profile.

### Why does this article not list Yelp rules?
The source set for this guide contained only Google help pages. Yelp's policy wording was not verified, so it is not stated here. Check Yelp's own guidelines before acting.
