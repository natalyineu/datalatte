To start TikTok ads for a small business, first check whether your customers use the app. Then set a fixed test budget and a success number, and run one campaign for a set period. Pew's 2026 survey found 37% of US adults use TikTok, so the audience is large, but the channel does not suit every local business. The practical test is simple: decide how many bookings or calls would justify continuing, fund the test long enough to measure them, and compare the cost per result with other channels.

This guide covers a first campaign only. Usage figures come from Pew's 2026 survey and testing principles come from Meta's and Google's help pages. Every example number is labelled as an assumption.

## Are Your Customers on TikTok?

Pew's survey covered 5,511 US adults, fielded between 30 January and 18 June 2026, with an overall margin of error of plus or minus 1.8 percentage points. In that survey, 37% of US adults said they use TikTok, the same share as in 2025. YouTube stood at 84% and Facebook at 70%, while Instagram rose to 51%. The fact sheet covers the United States only, so a business in the UK, Australia or Canada should check local usage data before deciding.

National usage does not show whether your local customers are on the app. A café, salon or gym near a university may find a large share of its customers there. A dental practice or mortgage broker serving mostly older clients may find the reverse. Before spending anything, ask ten or so current customers which apps they use. That check costs nothing and tells you more than a national average.

## How Much Should You Test With?

Pick a test budget you can afford to lose completely. It should be large enough to produce a meaningful number of results and small enough that a failed test does not hurt the business. Platform minimums and budget rules change, so confirm the current figures in TikTok Ads Manager before you set a number.

Break-even on first visits alone needs 14 bookings ($600 divided by $45, rounded up), so decide in advance which view you will use. If customers rarely return, the test has to produce more bookings before it can pay off.

## How Do You Set Up the First Campaign?

Change one thing at a time so you can tell what caused a result. The steps below follow a standard order for a first test.

1. Set up the account. Create a business account and give access to the person who will manage the ads.
2. Pick one goal. Choose a specific action, such as a booking, a call or a form fill. Avoid vague goals like "awareness" for a first test, because they are hard to tie to revenue.
3. Set the location. Target only the area you can serve. Keep the area realistic, since very small areas can limit delivery.
4. Choose a broad audience. Start with your location and a general age range. Add narrower targeting only after you have results.
5. Make a short vertical video. Show the service or product in the first few seconds and state the offer early.
6. Set the budget and schedule. Use the fixed amount from your test plan and do not change it mid-test.

Organic posts that already attract comments and saves are a sensible starting point for paid tests. Planning and publishing those posts consistently is easier with a tool, and the [comparison of social scheduling tools for local businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) shows the trade-offs.

## How Do You Run a Fair Test?

A fair test compares two versions under the same conditions. Meta's guidance on A/B testing says each version is shown to a separate segment of the audience so that nobody sees both. It also recommends using the same budget for both versions. Meta advises against informal testing, such as switching ad sets on and off manually, because overlapping audiences produce unreliable results.

That guidance is written for Meta Ads, but the principle carries over to any platform: split the audience cleanly, keep budgets equal and change one variable at a time. For a small business, this usually means testing two videos against the same audience and budget. Testing a video, a different offer and a new landing page in one go makes it impossible to know which change mattered.

## How Do You Know If the Test Worked?

Set the success number before spending. Cost per booking matters more than views, likes or follower growth. Use your own results to judge the test, since no published figure for typical local-service costs is reliable enough to set your target.

Use this decision rule with your own numbers:

- If cost per result is below your break-even figure on the view you chose, run a second test with a higher budget or a second video.
- If cost per result is close to break-even, change one thing, such as the offer or the first three seconds of the video, and test again.
- If the test produces zero results after the full period, stop and review the offer and the booking page before spending more.

Some businesses also compare TikTok with other paid channels. Google's help page describes Display campaigns as reaching people across [35 million websites and apps](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190), including YouTube and Gmail, which makes it a useful comparison for the same offer. Run both tests on separate budgets and compare cost per result rather than impressions.

## FAQ

### How much budget do I need to test TikTok ads?

Choose an amount you can lose completely, then fund it long enough to see a meaningful number of results. Check the current minimums in TikTok Ads Manager, since platform rules change.

### Do I need a lot of followers to run TikTok ads?

Paid campaigns do not depend on a follower count. Followers matter for organic reach, but paid results depend on the offer, the audience and the video.

### Is TikTok good for older customers?

Pew's 2026 survey reports 37% of US adults use TikTok overall. The fact sheet extract does not break this down by age, so check your own customers first. If most of them are older, test the channel on a small budget before committing.

### Can I target only my own city?

Location targeting is available, but very small areas can limit how many people see your ads. Choose an area you can actually serve.

### How long should a first test run?

Set the length before you start and run it long enough to collect a meaningful number of results. A 30-day test is a common starting point, but the right length depends on your budget and expected customer volume.
