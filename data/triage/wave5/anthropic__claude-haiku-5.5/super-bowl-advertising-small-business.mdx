Small businesses rarely buy the Super Bowl broadcast itself, because national airtime is priced for national advertisers. The practical alternative is a small, local, time-limited paid test that runs in the weeks before game day. The sources checked for this article do not cover Super Bowl ad rates, viewer counts or spending projections, so those figures are left out. What the sources do support is how Google Display campaigns reach people and what typical costs look like. That is enough to plan a measurable test with a fixed budget.

## Can a Small Business Advertise During the Super Bowl?

Yes, but not in the broadcast on a typical small budget. The practical route is display or video advertising aimed at local people in the days around the game. Google describes Display campaigns as reaching people "as they browse millions of websites, apps, and Google-owned properties (such as YouTube and Gmail)" according to its [Display ads help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190). The Display Network reaches 35 million websites and apps, which is wider than Search alone.

This article treats the game as a timing anchor, not as a placement. The test starts early, ends before kickoff and is judged on orders or enquiries rather than on how many people saw an ad.

## What Does Google Display Offer a Local Business?

Google lists three goals for Display campaigns: driving sales, leads and website traffic, or building awareness. For a local shop, sales and leads matter most. Campaigns can be built around a specific outcome rather than only impressions, which suits a test with a clear offer.

Display campaigns use AI for bidding, creative and targeting. Google states that Smart bidding "uses AI to optimize for conversions or conversion value in each and every auction" and that Display campaigns "optimize within days". A short test should run long enough for the system to learn, which argues against a single weekend.

## What Do the Benchmark Costs Mean for a Test?

WordStream's industry averages give a rough planning range. Across all industries it reports a search cost per click of $2.69 and a display cost per click of $0.63. It notes both averages rose slightly from earlier figures of $2.32 and $0.58. These are averages across many advertisers, not a forecast for one area.

Industry differences matter. The same [WordStream benchmark page](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks) lists Legal at $6.75 and Consumer Services at $6.40 for search, against $1.53 for Travel and Hospitality. Restaurants and food are not a separate category in that table, so the all-industry average is the closest available proxy. Check actual results in your own account.

## How Do You Budget a Local Test?

The worked example below uses assumptions for a pizzeria selling party-tray pre-orders. The display cost per click is the only sourced input. Every other input is an assumption to replace with your own numbers.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test budget | $600 | Assumption |
| Display cost per click | $0.63 | WordStream all-industry average, secondary |
| Clicks bought | About 952 | $600 divided by $0.63 |
| Click to pre-order rate | 2% | Assumption |
| Pre-orders | 19 | Assumption, rounded from 19.05 |
| Average order | $85 | Assumption |
| Gross margin | 35% | Assumption |
| Revenue | $1,615 | 19 times $85 |
| Gross margin earned | $565 | Revenue times 35% |
| Result before other costs | Minus $35 | $565 minus $600 |

Under these assumptions the test loses about $35. Each pre-order earns about $29.75 in margin ($85 times 35%), so break-even needs about 21 pre-orders. At a 2% click rate that means roughly 1,050 clicks, which implies a cost per click near $0.57. Cheaper clicks, a higher click to order rate or a larger average order are the levers to test.

## What Steps Make the Test Measurable?

Use this checklist before spending anything. Each step is a decision, not a guarantee of results.

1. Set the pre-order deadline and the game date in one calendar, and keep the test inside that window.
2. Create one landing page or short link used only in the ads, so traffic can be attributed.
3. Choose a small geographic area around the shop and a single offer.
4. Set a fixed daily budget and a total cap, and decide in advance what result would justify a second test.
5. Record each order against its source and log spend weekly. Tools such as [FreshBooks or QuickBooks](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business) can hold the spend records.
6. Share the offer on social channels with a scheduler if several accounts are running. A comparison such as [Hootsuite versus Sprout Social](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) can help choose one.
7. Review results after the game and decide whether to repeat, change the offer or stop.

## Is Live Sports Advertising Worth Testing?

It can be, if the test is small and the results are tracked. A broader look at how local businesses approach live events is in [live sports advertising for local business](/blog/live-sports-advertising-local-business). The same discipline applies: a fixed budget, one offer and an attribution method.

Google's help pages describe tools that can control cost once an account has conversion data. Google notes that "pay for conversions" is available on Target CPA Display campaigns, which ties spend to results rather than to clicks. Smart bidding, described in the [Smart bidding help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882), works the same way and needs enough conversions to learn from.

## FAQ

### Can a small business really advertise during the Super Bowl?
Yes, in the sense of running paid ads around game week on display or video. Buying the broadcast spot itself is a national purchase whose price the sources checked do not cover, so it is not included here.

### How big is the Google Display Network?
Google says Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail.

### What does a local test cost per click?
WordStream's all-industry average is $0.63 for display and $2.69 for search. These are averages across many advertisers, so a local cost may be higher or lower.

### How do I know if the test worked?
Use a code or short link that appears only in the ads, count orders against it and compare the margin earned with the spend. The worked example shows the break-even arithmetic.
