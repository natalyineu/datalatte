Pew Research's 2026 survey found 37% of US adults use TikTok, so the audience is large enough to test. Posting is free, so the real cost is staff time. This guide covers which platforms your customers use, what to film, a 12-week review routine, and a worked break-even calculation with labelled assumptions.

## Who Uses TikTok in the US?

Pew Research's 2026 survey of 5,511 US adults, fielded January 30 to June 18, 2026, found 37% use TikTok. That is the same share Pew reported in 2025 and up from 33% in 2024. YouTube (84%), Facebook (70%) and Instagram (51%) remain larger in the same survey, so TikTok is a supplement to those channels for most businesses, not a replacement.

The fact sheet's age breakdown is not in the source text reviewed for this guide, so no age figure is quoted here. Check the live [Pew social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) for age splits before choosing TikTok for a customer base assumed to be older or younger.

## Does Follower Count Decide Who Sees Your Videos?

TikTok's own explanation of how its feed ranks videos could not be verified from the sources available for this rewrite, so this guide does not rely on it. Any claim that follower count does or does not affect reach should be checked against TikTok's current help pages before a plan is built around it.

The plan below does not depend on that answer. It measures each video by what it does for the business: people who watch to the end, people who message or visit, and bookings that can be traced back to the platform. Follower totals are a weak measure for a business whose customers live within a few miles of the shop.

## What Should a Local Business Post?

Show the work and the place, with a clear local cue. Film real jobs, real products and real staff, and name the town or neighbourhood in the caption so local viewers recognise it. Ask permission before filming any customer or pet, and never show someone's face or home without agreement.

| Business | Video idea | Local cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hair salon | A colour transformation in one take | Service name and town in caption |
| Café | One drink made start to finish | Price on screen, street name in caption |
| Pet groomer | Before and after, with owner's permission | Neighbourhood name in caption |
| Fitness studio | A 20-second class clip or one coaching cue | Studio location and class time |

Start each clip with the most interesting second. For scripts and captions, [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) can speed up drafting, but every line should be checked for accuracy before posting.

## How Should You Run a 12-Week Test?

Pick a posting rate that can be kept for 12 weeks. Consistency matters more than volume, so if only one clip a week is realistic, plan one clip a week. Use this routine:

1. Choose one posting day and one filming slot per week, and write them in the calendar.
2. Batch three captions, each with the town name and one offer or service.
3. Post on the same days for the full 12 weeks.
4. Every two weeks, list which videos held viewers, which were shared, and which led to a message or booking.
5. Keep the formats that brought bookings and drop the rest.
6. Log every new client's source at the point of booking, so TikTok mentions are counted, not guessed.

A broader plan for how the platform fits with other content is in [blog content strategy for local business](/blog/blog-content-strategy-local-business).

## Is It Worth It? A Worked Example

All inputs below are **assumptions** for an illustrative hair salon. They are not benchmarks and should be replaced with the business's own numbers.

| Input | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Videos per week | 3 (assumption) |
| Hours per video, including filming and editing | 1 (assumption) |
| Hours per month (3 videos x 4.33 weeks = 13) | about 13 |
| Value of your time | $20 per hour (assumption) |
| Monthly cost of time (13 x $20) | about $260 |
| Margin per new client visit | $35 (assumption) |
| Extra bookings needed to break even ($260 / $35 = 7.4, rounded up) | 8 per month |

On these inputs the salon needs about eight extra bookings a month, roughly two a week, to cover the time. That may be realistic if before-and-after clips reliably bring in local clients. If the videos attract views from outside the area and no bookings, the time is not paying back, and the format should change or the posting should stop.

Count bookings for 12 weeks before deciding. If paid promotion is added later, test it separately and treat any ad spend as its own cost. ## FAQ

### Can I get a large following without ads?

No source reviewed for this guide promises a follower target. Judge the account on bookings and visits over at least 12 weeks instead of on follower totals.

### Do hashtags matter?

The sources reviewed for this rewrite do not establish a hashtag rule, so this guide does not recommend a number. Use hashtags that name your town and service, and keep captions accurate.

### How long until TikTok brings bookings?

No verified timeline exists. Set a 12-week test window and review it every two weeks against the booking log.

### Which local businesses suit TikTok best?

Businesses whose work is visual and whose customers can be reached through a short clip, such as salons, cafés, studios and pet services. Check the Pew age data before assuming the customers are on the platform.
