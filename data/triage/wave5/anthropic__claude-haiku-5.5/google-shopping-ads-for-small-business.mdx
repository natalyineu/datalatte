Google Shopping ads are product listings. They are matched from product data rather than keywords the advertiser picks, so they only suit businesses that sell physical goods a feed can describe. The cost question matters more than the format. The source set contains no Shopping-specific figures, so the closest benchmarks are WordStream's all-industry averages: about $2.69 per search click and a 3.17% search click-through rate. At those averages, a product business with a thin margin can spend more winning a first sale than it earns from it. Run the numbers before setting up a feed.

## What Do Google Shopping Ads Cost?

Costs depend on how competitive your product category is. WordStream's table shows a wide spread. Search cost per click ranges from $1.16 for e-commerce to $6.75 for legal, with Consumer Services at $6.40. A product in a crowded category will likely cost more per click than a niche product, so a benchmark from a different category can mislead you.

Cost per click is only the first number. Conversion rate and order value decide whether the spend pays. A cheap click that never converts costs as much as an expensive click that does. Before launch, write down three figures for each product group: cost per click, expected conversion rate, and gross profit per order. Those three numbers tell you whether a campaign can break even.

## How Are Shopping Ads Different From Search Ads?

Search ads run on keywords the advertiser chooses and show a headline and description. Shopping ads draw on product data, so the titles and descriptions in your feed do much of the matching work that keywords do in a text campaign. Those titles are the part of a Shopping setup you control most directly, so write them the way customers search for the product.

Performance Max can also serve product listings alongside other formats. Google's Display help page says the Display Network reaches people across 35 million websites and apps, plus Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail. Google's help page also notes that Optimized targeting is the default for Display campaigns, which makes Display less controllable than a standard Search campaign. For other automated campaign types, Google suggests considering Performance Max or Search.

## Which Products Suit a Shopping Campaign?

A product suits a Shopping campaign when it can be described in a feed and sold at a price that leaves room for ad costs. Work through this checklist before you commit budget.

1. List the products you sell online and the price of each.
2. Work out the gross profit per order for each product after cost of goods.
3. Compare that profit with the cost per order from a worked example like the one below.
4. Start with the products that clear the test, and leave the rest out of the first campaign.

## Is Shopping Worth It for a Local Business?

It depends on whether customers can buy the product. A shop that sells gifts or supplies online can test Shopping. A salon, café or plumber sells appointments and time, so there is no product to list in a feed. Those businesses should look at Local Services Ads and local search first. The [Google Local Service Ads guide](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) covers that route.

Stores that sell in person can still look at Performance Max. The [Performance Max complete guide](/blog/google-performance-max-for-local-business-complete-guide-2026) explains how it works and where it fits. Businesses in Canada and Australia should also compare their channel mix against the [Canada guide](/blog/local-business-marketing-canada-guide-2026) and the [Australia guide](/blog/local-marketing-australia-small-business-2026).

## What Should You Check Each Week?

1. Check whether any product has spent budget without a sale after a set number of clicks. Write the threshold down before you start.
2. Review which search terms and products drive clicks, and pause the ones that do not convert.
3. Confirm that prices and stock levels in the feed match your website, since mismatches waste clicks and damage trust.
4. Compare the week's cost per order with the gross profit per order from your worked example, and cut spend on products that fail that test.

These steps are a process, not a promise of results. Adjust the thresholds to your own margin and sales cycle.

## FAQ

### Do Google Shopping ads use keywords?
Not in the way Search ads do. Shopping ads draw on your product data, so product titles and descriptions carry much of the matching work. Check Google's current Shopping help pages for the exact rules before you set up a feed.

### What does a Shopping click cost?
WordStream's all-industry search average is $2.69 per click. Legal is listed at $6.75 and Consumer Services at $6.40. Your own category will vary, so check benchmarks for your products.

### Can a service business use Shopping ads?
Usually not. Shopping ads sell products, and a service has no product to list. Local Services Ads or standard Search are usually better fits.

### Is the 35 million figure about Shopping ads?
No. Google's Display help page says Display campaigns reach 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail. It describes Display, not Shopping.

### Should I run Performance Max or a standard Shopping campaign?
Test whichever fits your goals. Google's Display page suggests considering Performance Max or Search for other automated campaign types. Compare cost per order against your worked example before you commit.
