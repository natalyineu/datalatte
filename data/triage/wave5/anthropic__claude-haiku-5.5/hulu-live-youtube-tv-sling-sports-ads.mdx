## Which video platforms do US adults use most?

Pew Research Center's 2026 survey of 5,511 U.S. adults, fielded January 30 to June 18, 2026, found that 84% of adults say they ever use YouTube and 70% use Facebook ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)). Instagram reached 51%, TikTok 37%, WhatsApp 33% and LinkedIn 31%. Snapchat (26%), Reddit (25%) and X (18%) trail those platforms.

The survey's margin of error is plus or minus 1.8 percentage points. It measures whether adults use each platform, not whether they watch live television or respond to ads. Pew's figures are useful for deciding where your audience already spends time, but they do not show that a streaming TV buy will reach the same people.

## Can a small business buy live TV channel ads directly?

The reviewed sources do not confirm this. Nothing in them sets out ad sales terms, minimum spends or channel lists for Hulu Live TV, YouTube TV or Sling. Any figure a seller quotes for these services should be treated as unverified until it appears in a written proposal.

Ask a seller to name the exact channels, the program types and the placements included. Live sports inventory is a separate decision with its own timing, so the [live sports advertising guide for local businesses](/blog/live-sports-advertising-local-business) covers how to match a message to a game. If a seller cannot list channels, stop there. Vague inventory makes results impossible to judge.

## What does Google Ads document about display and video reach?

Google's help page on Display campaigns says they can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)). That is the documented scope. It does not mention YouTube TV, the subscription service.

The same page says Smart Display campaigns have been folded into the standard Display campaign, and that existing Smart Display campaigns migrated without any action needed. Display campaigns use Smart Bidding and optimized targeting by default, and ads are responsive, meaning Google AI assembles combinations of your uploaded assets. You can switch these settings off if you prefer manual control.

For video file requirements before you upload anything, read the [CTV creative specs guide for 2026](/blog/ctv-advertising-creative-specs-guide-2026). Confirm the placement list with Google in writing before assuming TV-screen reach.

## How do you test a streaming TV ad without wasting budget?

Meta's guidance on A/B testing is the clearest primary source available on how to run a fair comparison. It says to choose a hypothesis first, change one variable such as the ad creative, audience or placement, and use the same budget for both versions so the comparison is fair ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669)). It also warns against informal testing by switching ad sets on and off, because audiences can overlap and results become unreliable.

A practical test plan for a small business:

1. Write one hypothesis, for example, that a discount code aired on a live channel will bring more calls than a brand message.
2. Build two versions that differ in one variable only.
3. Give each version the same budget and a unique tracking number, QR code or landing page.
4. Set a stop rule before launch, such as a fixed budget and a fixed number of days.
5. Count bookings, calls or redemptions, not impressions.

Worked example (all inputs are assumptions, not sourced figures): a bakery sets a $1,000 test budget. It assumes a $25 CPM, which buys 40,000 impressions. If each household sees the ad three times on average, that is about 13,333 households reached, or roughly 7.5 cents per household. The bakery's real CPM will depend on the seller, the channels and the ZIP codes, so replace these inputs with the seller's written rates before deciding.

## What should you ask a seller before paying?

Use this checklist to compare proposals side by side. A seller who cannot answer any row in writing is not ready for your budget.

| Question to ask | Why it matters | Written answer needed |
|---|---|---|
| Which channels and placements are included? | Confirms whether live TV is actually included | Named list of channels |
| What is the minimum spend, per campaign or per flight? | Shows whether a test is affordable | Dollar amount and billing date |
| How are impressions, reach and frequency reported? | Lets you check delivery against the plan | Report format and frequency |
| How will conversions be tracked? | Needed to judge the test | Tracking method for calls, codes or visits |
| What are the cancellation and makegood terms? | Protects you if inventory underdelivers | Contract clause |

Compare each seller's answers against the platform's own documentation. Where the platform does not list a placement, ask for proof before you pay.

For a second opinion on a proposal before you sign, the [Google Ads management service](/services/google-ads) includes a review of placements, tracking and budget.

## FAQ

### Can I advertise on YouTube TV with Google Ads?

The Google documentation reviewed here describes display and video reach across the Google Display Network, YouTube and Gmail. It does not name YouTube TV. Ask Google or an authorized reseller in writing which placements are included before you launch.

### Is there a self-serve option for Hulu Live TV?

None of the reviewed sources confirm one. Confirm with the seller whether live channels are included and what the minimum spend is.

### Why is platform usage data useful for this decision?

Pew's 2026 figures show how many U.S. adults use each platform, which helps you choose where to look first. They do not measure ad response or television viewing, so use them to narrow options, not to forecast results.

### How do I judge whether a streaming TV test worked?

Set the success measure before launch, such as bookings or redemptions per tracked offer, and compare the test against the stop rule you set. Do not judge the test on impressions alone.
