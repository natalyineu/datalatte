A fitness studio social media strategy has four parts: choose the one or two platforms your members actually use, post a steady mix of class, coach and member content, make one clear offer such as a trial class, and measure trial bookings instead of likes. [Pew Research's 2026 survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) of 5,511 US adults found 84% use YouTube, 70% use Facebook and 51% use Instagram. Those shares show where general reach sits, but a studio's own member list should decide which platforms receive time and budget.

This guide covers platform choice, a weekly content mix, how to test paid social without wasting budget, a trial-class funnel worked through with assumption-labelled inputs, and the numbers to track each month.

## Which Platforms Should a Studio Use?

Pew's 2026 fact sheet is a national measure of platform use in the United States. Fieldwork ran from January 30 to June 18, 2026, with an overall margin of error of plus or minus 1.8 percentage points. The figures are shares of all US adults. Pew does not break usage down by gym membership or by fitness-studio member age, so the numbers are a starting point rather than a studio-specific benchmark.

| Platform | 2025 | 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | 84% | 84% |
| Facebook | 71% | 70% |
| Instagram | 50% | 51% |
| TikTok | 37% | 37% |
| Reddit | 26% | 25% |
| Snapchat | 25% | 26% |

Pew notes that YouTube and Facebook are the most widely used social media platforms in the US, and that about half of adults use Instagram. For a studio, the practical test is simpler: check where current members already spend time. If the membership skews younger, read the [TikTok guide for fitness studios](/blog/using-tiktok-for-fitness-studio-marketing) before adding a short-video channel. Two platforms maintained well usually beat four maintained poorly.

## What Should a Studio Post Each Week?

Repeatable content types stop a studio from starting each post from a blank page. The list below is a suggested structure, not a tested formula. Adjust it once trial bookings show which posts bring people through the door.

1. Class clips of 15 to 30 seconds from a real session, with the class name and time on screen.
2. Coach moments that explain one tip or one movement.
3. Member stories, posted only with written permission from the member.
4. Offer posts that promote the trial class and link to a single booking page.
5. Schedule posts listing the week's classes and any open spots.

Keep one booking link in the profile bio and point every offer post to it. Two or three posts a week on one platform is easier to sustain than daily posting that stops after a month. If a member receives a free class or perk for posting, that connection should be disclosed in the post, and the disclosure should not be buried in hashtags.

## How Should You Test Paid Social Ads?

Paid social only pays off when the studio can tell which version works. Meta's help centre recommends A/B testing rather than switching ad sets on and off by hand. Manual switching can create overlapping audiences and unreliable results. An A/B test splits the audience so that nobody sees both versions, then measures cost per result or cost per conversion lift.

Meta also advises writing a hypothesis before choosing a variable. For a studio, a usable hypothesis is that a trial-class offer will outperform a general membership message for the same audience. Use the same budget for both versions so the comparison is fair. For search campaigns, the [Google Ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-google-ads-guide) applies the same logic to keywords.

If paid reach is new, compare it with free posting first. The [organic versus paid social comparison](/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media) explains when each makes sense for a small business. Run the test for long enough to collect a meaningful number of trial bookings before deciding a version has won.

## What Does a Trial-Class Funnel Return?

The worked example below uses assumptions for every input, including cost per lead. Replace them with figures from your own ad account and sign-up form. The purpose is to show how each stage narrows the audience down to a cost per new member.

| Step | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $400 (assumption) |
| Cost per lead | $25 (assumption) |
| Leads | 16 |
| Leads who book a trial | 40% (assumption) = 6.4 |
| Trial attendees who show up | 70% (assumption) = 4.5 |
| Attendees who join | 40% (assumption) = 1.8 |
| Cost per new member | about $220 |
| Monthly membership price | $120 (assumption) |
| Months retained | 6 (assumption) |
| Revenue per new member | $720 |

On these inputs, each new member returns about 3.2 times the cost to acquire them. Lower the join rate to 15% and the cost per member rises to about $600, close to the $720 revenue per member. The join rate and the speed of follow-up after the trial matter more than the ad itself. Retention after joining is covered in the [membership retention tactics guide](/blog/fitness-studio-membership-retention-tactics).

## Which Numbers Should You Track?

Track five numbers each month and leave the rest aside until these are stable:

- Trial bookings by source, asked at sign-up
- Show rate for trial classes
- Join rate within two weeks of a trial
- Cost per new member, if paid ads run
- Cancellations by month

Followers and likes do not show whether a studio is growing. Add a source question to the booking form so each trial can be traced to a post or ad. If software is used to automate follow-up messages, the [AI tools for fitness studio marketing](/blog/fitness-studio-ai-powered-marketing-tools) article compares options.

## FAQ

### Which platform is best for a fitness studio?

It depends on the membership. Pew's 2026 data shows YouTube at 84% and Facebook at 70% of US adults, but it does not break usage down by studio member age, so check the member list before committing.

### How often should a studio post?

No source gives an ideal number. Choose a rhythm that can be kept for three months, then compare trial bookings across that period before changing it.

### Is paid social necessary?

Not at the start. Organic posts and a free business listing cost only time. Paid ads make more sense once the trial offer has been shown to convert a reasonable share of visitors.

### Can a studio repost member photos?

Only with the member's written permission. If the member receives a free class or perk for posting, the relationship should be disclosed in the post. Check current advertising rules in the country where the studio operates before running a member programme.

### How can a studio tell if an ad test worked?

Run both versions with the same budget at the same time, then compare cost per result. Meta advises against informal on-and-off switching because it produces unreliable results.
