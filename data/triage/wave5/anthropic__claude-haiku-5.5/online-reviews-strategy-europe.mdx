## Do Customers Still Read Reviews Before They Choose a Business?

Yes, and the habit is getting stronger. According to the [BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), 97% of consumers read online reviews for local businesses. The share who always read them rose to 41% in 2026, up from 29% the year before.

The survey links this shift to wider economic pressure. It notes that consumers facing higher prices and variable product quality tend to do more research before they spend. For a small business, this means reviews sit in the path of almost every new customer. A steady flow of recent reviews, plus visible replies, gives a prospect the reassurance they look for before they call, book or visit.

## Which Review Sites Do Customers Use in 2026?

Customers do not rely on one site. The survey reports that the average consumer uses six different review sites when choosing a business, so a single profile is no longer enough. Google is still the largest source, but its share fell from 83% in 2025 to 71% in 2026.

Other platforms are gaining ground. The survey notes a resurgence at traditional review sites and growing use of video platforms such as YouTube, Instagram and TikTok for quick, visual recommendations. It also lists Facebook and AI tools such as ChatGPT among the most common sources for local recommendations.

The survey also reports that only 35% of SMBs have a Google Business Profile. If that holds for your market, many customers cannot read or write Google reviews at all. Start with Google because it is the largest source, then add a second platform where your own customers already look.

## Why Do Star Ratings and Recency Matter So Much?

Customers now expect higher ratings and fresh reviews. The survey describes a sharp increase in customers who only use businesses rated 4.5 stars or above. A profile with a few old five-star reviews and nothing recent can look inactive, even when the business is performing well.

Recency is the other half of the picture. A steady trickle of reviews each month gives a clearer current picture than a burst after a campaign followed by silence, which is why a routine tends to outperform a one-off push.

Ratings also depend on who gets asked. Asking only the people you expect to be pleased skews the profile, and it runs against the platform rules covered later in this article. The more defensible approach is to ask every customer in the same way and then respond to whatever comes in.

## How Fast Should You Reply to Reviews?

Customers notice silence. The survey says slow or generic review responses are increasingly seen as a red flag, and consumers expect a business to acknowledge feedback almost immediately. A reply that arrives days later, or reads like a template, works against the reassurance the review profile is meant to provide.

Set a fixed response window that you can actually keep, such as the same working day or within 24 hours. Mention a specific detail from the customer's experience, thank them, and for a negative review, offer a direct route to resolve the problem. Keep the tone calm and avoid arguing in public.

If volume is the constraint, tools can draft replies, but a person should check each draft before it goes live. In practice, that leaves one conservative rule: do not offer rewards for reviews, and do not steer requests toward happy customers only. Before running any review campaign, read the current policy for each platform you use and check the consumer rules in each country you serve. ## What Does a 30-Day Review Routine Cost in Time?

The worked example below uses assumption inputs. Replace them with your own numbers before you plan staffing.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completed appointments or jobs per month | 120 | assumption |
| Customers asked for feedback | 120 | assumption: every customer |
| Share who leave a review | 5% | assumption |
| New reviews per month | 6 | calculated: 120 x 5% |
| Time to write each reply | 5 minutes | assumption |
| Reply time per month | 30 minutes | calculated: 6 x 5 |

Under these assumptions, the business gains about six reviews a month and spends roughly 30 minutes a month replying. If the reply rate is higher, the reply time rises in step, so set the reply window to match real volume.

The routine itself is short:

1. Write one neutral request message and save it as a template.
2. Send it to every customer at the same point after each visit.
3. Log each request with a date so the flow is visible.
4. Reply to each new review inside the chosen window.
5. Check the monthly review count and average rating once a month.

## Where Do AI Tools Fit Into Review Discovery?

The survey reports that AI tools such as ChatGPT have moved into third place for local business recommendations. A customer may ask an assistant for options before opening any review site, so the information the assistant can find about a business matters.

Keep business details accurate on the platforms that feed these tools, and keep reviews flowing so the public record stays current.
