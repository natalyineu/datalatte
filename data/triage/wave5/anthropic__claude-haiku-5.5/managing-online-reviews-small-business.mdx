Managing online reviews means asking customers for feedback in one consistent way, replying to every review promptly, and tracking the themes customers repeat. BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses and 41% always do, up from 29% the year before. Reviews now shape how people choose a local firm, so a small business benefits most from a steady weekly routine rather than an occasional push for feedback.

This guide covers small local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Consumer figures come from [BrightLocal's 2026 survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), published February 11, 2026. Any figure taken from another source is labelled where it appears.

## Why Do Online Reviews Matter for a Small Business?

Reviews are often one of the first things a new customer checks. BrightLocal describes them as evidence that a business is active and reliable, and says they now influence visibility in both traditional Google search and AI search tools. The survey also reports that consumers look beyond text reviews, with video platforms and AI tools rising as places people check before they decide.

Trust works in both directions. BrightLocal's commentary notes that higher prices and concerns about product quality push consumers to research more before choosing a business. A small business with few reviews, old reviews or unanswered reviews gives that research little to confirm, and the customer often moves on to a competitor with a clearer record.

## Where Do Customers Actually Read Reviews?

The average consumer now uses six review sites when choosing a business. Google is still the largest source, but its share fell from 83% in 2025 to 71% in 2026. Traditional sites such as Tripadvisor, Better Business Bureau and Healthgrades are seeing a resurgence, and video platforms including YouTube, Instagram and TikTok are gaining traction.

That spread matters for a small firm. A single profile means missing customers who check another site first. Choose the two or three platforms your customers actually use, keep business name, address and phone details identical on each, and use the same reply tone everywhere. ## How Do You Ask for Reviews Without Breaking Platform Rules?

Ask every customer in the same way, shortly after the job is finished, with one clear message and one link. Asking only satisfied customers, offering rewards, or setting staff a target for reviews carries risk on most platforms. The sources used for this guide do not set out each platform's exact wording, so read the current contribution policy for every site you use before launching any request campaign.

Service businesses can trigger the request from their scheduling software when a job is marked complete. [Jobber and ServiceTitan](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business) compare two systems many trades use for this. Keep the trigger identical for every customer, so the request does not become selective by accident.

## How Fast Should You Reply to Reviews?

BrightLocal reports that slow or generic review responses are increasingly seen as a red flag, and that consumers expect businesses to acknowledge feedback almost immediately. A weekly check is the least a small business should do. A reply within two working days is a safer working target for most owners.

Keep replies short and specific. Thank the reviewer, mention the service they used, and for a complaint say what will be checked and invite them to get in touch directly. Avoid sharing private details about the visit or the customer's account. Copy-and-paste replies are the pattern that makes a profile look inattentive, so vary the wording while keeping the tone consistent.

## What Should You Do About a Fake or Unfair Review?

Respond to a genuine unhappy customer calmly and offline where possible. Other readers judge the reply as much as the review itself, so arguing in public rarely helps a business.

For a suspected fake review, use the platform's reporting tool rather than replying with accusations. BrightLocal's survey found that consumers want review platforms and businesses to be held accountable, with severe consequences for anyone caught manipulating feedback. Credibility that does not depend on one review site also helps, so [local press coverage](/blog/how-to-get-local-press-coverage-for-your-small-business) can add third-party proof to your profile.

## Worked Example: A Weekly 30-Minute Review Routine

All inputs below are **assumptions** for a one-person service business, not benchmarks.

| Step | Time (assumption) |
| --- | --- |
| Read new reviews on your main platform and one other site | 8 min |
| Reply to each new review, about 5 a week | 15 min |
| Send review requests for the week's completed jobs | 5 min |
| Note repeated themes in a spreadsheet | 2 min |

That is 30 minutes a week, or about 26 hours a year (assumption: 52 working weeks). If the business has 12 reviews and gains 5 a month (assumption), it reaches 20 reviews in about two months. Use the theme notes to find repeat complaints, such as slow scheduling, and fix them at source so later reviews reflect real change rather than reply templates.

If your own numbers differ, change the inputs in the table and keep the structure. The routine matters more than the exact minutes. ## FAQ

### How many reviews does a small business need?

The sources used here do not set a required number. Focus on steady volume and a recent flow of reviews, and track your own count each month against your own history.

### Can I offer a discount in return for a review?

Avoid it. Incentives tied to reviews are restricted on most platforms, so check the policy for each site before running any offer.

### Can I delete a bad review?

Not as the business owner. Report reviews you believe break a platform's rules, and reply to the rest.

### How quickly should I reply?

BrightLocal reports that consumers expect a fast acknowledgement and dislike slow or generic replies. Aim to reply within two working days.

### Should I ask only happy customers?

No. Asking selectively is risky on most platforms and undermines trust. Ask every customer with the same message.
