A solo pet groomer can test Google Ads for 30 days on a small budget, but only if tracking, booking capacity and lead quality are set up first. At a $600 monthly budget and an assumed $6.40 average click, the account buys about 94 clicks. Whether those clicks fill slots depends on how many enquiries arrive, how many turn into bookings and whether the calendar has room. The plan below covers the setup, the tracking, a tight first campaign, a 30-day schedule and a break-even check.

## What Should You Fix Before Spending Anything?

Start with capacity. Count the open appointments you want to fill next month. If you want 8 and roughly half of enquiries typically book (use your own number, not a benchmark), you need about 16 enquiries. Work backward from that to a budget, not forward from a budget to hope.

Next, set up three conversion actions: calls from the ad, calls from the website and your booking form or confirmation page. Choose a minimum call length that reflects a real enquiry. Forty-five to sixty seconds is a reasonable starting point, and it is a suggestion, not a platform rule.

Then build one landing page for one offer. Put the service and town in the headline, add one booking button, a tappable phone number, real photos, your rating, prices as ranges and a plain list of breeds or services you do not take. That last item screens out poor-fit callers before they ring.

Finally, make a lead log. One row per enquiry with the date, the source (call or form), the service asked about, the town and an outcome: booked, quote only, out of area, not a fit, or no answer. You will read this in week 4.

## How Should the First Campaign Be Built on a Small Budget?

Keep it to one campaign and one location. Pull the postcodes or zip codes of your last 50 clients and draw a radius that covers most of them. Mobile groomers should draw the radius around the area they will realistically drive, not the whole region.

Use exact and phrase match only in month 1. [Google's own guidance on automated campaigns](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190) points to Search campaigns when you want control over keywords, which suits a small test. Leave broad match out until you have enough conversion data to judge it.

Use three ad groups: core grooming terms with your town, specialty terms such as doodle or senior dog grooming, and brand. Add negatives from day one for words like school, course, jobs, DIY, supplies, clippers, free and how to. The [keyword guide for pet groomers](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-pet-groomers-in-2026) has candidate lists for each ad group.

Match the ad schedule to when you can answer the phone. If you cannot take calls while a dog is on the table, send people to the booking form at those hours instead.

Revenue is not profit. Suppose running the table, including your time, supplies and rent, takes an assumed 60% of revenue. Then a $255 client leaves about $102. In the Tough column the first year does not cover the ad cost. In Base it does, and the repeat visits are where the margin comes from. The Strong column books more clients than many solo groomers have room for, so the right move there may be a smaller budget.

The cheapest lever is the booking rate, not the click. At the same enquiry volume, moving from 35% to 60% booked cuts the cost per client by about 42%. That means answering fast, returning missed calls the same day and having online booking that works on a phone.

## What Is Your Break-Even Click Price?

The break-even check is simple: maximum cost per click equals the contribution per new client, multiplied by the enquiry rate, multiplied by the booking rate. Using the assumed $102 contribution per client, a 10% enquiry rate and a 50% booking rate gives $102 x 0.10 x 0.50 = $5.10. Above that price, the average click loses money. Recalculate with your own margin, because a higher-priced service or a better repeat rate raises the ceiling.

## How Do You Run the 30-Day Plan?

Days 1 to 7 are for launch. Turn the ads on, then test each conversion action with a real call and a real form submission. Do not judge results yet.

Days 8 to 14 are for cleaning the traffic. Read the search terms report, add negatives and pause any keyword that has spent money without producing an enquiry.

Days 15 to 21 are for fixing leaks. If clicks arrive but enquiries do not, rewrite the page or the ad. Keep the lead log current.

Days 22 to 30 are for the decision. Compare cost per booked client against your break-even figure, then scale, hold or stop. Judging on day 5 is a common mistake, because a handful of enquiries can swing a small account either way.

## What Should Week 4 Tell You?

Sort the lead log by outcome and look for patterns. Many out-of-area enquiries mean the radius is too wide. Many not-a-fit enquiries mean the breed or service needs a negative keyword and a clearer line on the page. Many no-answers point to hours or the ad schedule. Many quote-only enquiries suggest the page should show price ranges earlier.

Each pattern has one fix, and changing several things at once makes results hard to read. Change one thing, wait a week and check the log again.

## FAQ

### How much should a solo groomer spend on Google Ads in the first month?

There is no official figure. The $600 used here is an assumption. Choose a budget from your slot target and your break-even click price, not from an industry average.

### Should I use Performance Max or a Search campaign?

For a first month on a small budget, a Search campaign gives you the search terms report and control over which keywords you pay for. [Google's Display campaign help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190) suggests Performance Max or Search as the options for other automated campaign types. Read the [Performance Max guide for local business](/blog/google-performance-max-for-local-business-complete-guide-2026) once you have steady tracked bookings to compare against.

### Should I use Google Local Services Ads instead?

Local Services Ads work on a different model and have their own rules and costs. Compare the two before committing, using the [Local Services Ads complete guide](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide) and the [worth-it analysis](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business).

### Why am I getting calls but no bookings?

Check the lead log. The usual causes are out-of-area callers, price shoppers, breeds you do not take and missed calls. Each has a fix: radius, price ranges on the page, negatives and a tighter ad schedule.

### How long until I know if it works?

Enquiries can arrive within days, but judge after about 30 days and 100 or more clicks. The 30-day mark is a rule of thumb, not a Google figure.
