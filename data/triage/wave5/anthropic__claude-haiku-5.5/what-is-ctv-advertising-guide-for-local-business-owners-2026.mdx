CTV advertising means video ads shown on a television screen through streaming, using a smart TV, streaming stick or game console. Unlike a broadcast spot, a CTV campaign is bought in a dashboard, usually on a cost-per-thousand-views basis, and can be aimed at a chosen area and schedule. The format is video, so the planning questions are the same as for other paid media: what it costs per result, how results will be measured, and whether the offer is ready for traffic. This guide explains the terms, the cost logic and a small first test for a local business.

## What Is CTV Advertising?

CTV is short for connected TV. A CTV ad plays before or during a streamed show on a television set, in the way a pre-roll ad plays on a phone or laptop, but on a large screen with sound on. The buying is digital. The advertiser sets a budget, picks an audience and chooses a schedule, rather than negotiating a spot with a broadcaster.

The sources reviewed for this guide cover Google's Display Network, not CTV specifically. The benchmarks below therefore describe display and search advertising, the nearest measured comparison. They help set expectations for a digital test, but a CTV campaign needs its own quotes from the platform being used.

## How Is CTV Different From Google Display Ads?

Google describes Display campaigns as ads shown across the Display Network, which reaches people as they browse websites, apps and Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail. Google states that Display campaigns can reach people worldwide across 35 million websites and apps. The campaign type supports images and video on those placements, with AI handling targeting, bidding and formats.

The difference for a local owner is the screen and the viewing context. A display ad on a phone competes with the page it sits on, while a CTV ad plays in a living room with sound on. Google's Display help page does not cover TV screens, so any CTV vendor's device and reporting definitions should be checked before money is committed. ## What Do Benchmarks Say About Digital Ad Costs?

WordStream's industry benchmarks put the average search cost per click across all industries at $2.69, and the average display cost per click at $0.63. The average search click-through rate is 3.17% and the display rate is 0.46%. WordStream says it updates this data regularly, so its current report should be checked before a budget is set.

Costs vary widely by industry. The same benchmark lists legal at $6.75 and consumer services at $6.40 per search click, while dating and personals reaches a 6.05% average search CTR. A local business should compare its own quotes against the figures for its category, not the all-industry average, because a single average hides large differences.

## What Does a $600 CTV Test Cost? A Worked Example

Every input below is an **assumption** for illustration. None comes from the sources.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Budget | $600 |
| CPM (cost per 1,000 views) | $30 |
| Ad views bought | 20,000 |
| Average views per household | 4 |
| Households reached | about 5,000 |
| Tracked visits from the ad | 10 |
| Profit per visit | $40 |

Under these assumptions the test costs $60 per tracked visit ($600 divided by 10) and returns $400 in first-visit profit. That is a $200 shortfall on first visits alone. The campaign would pay back only if some of those customers return, or if the ad lifts search and map activity that a tracked link does not capture.

The calculation also shows why tracking matters. If the owner records only one visit in five, the cost per tracked visit rises and the result looks worse than the true effect. Judge the test over repeat visits and alongside other marketing, not on a single week of bookings.

## How Do You Run a First CTV Test?

A first test should answer one question: do people in the target area visit after seeing the ad? A small setup keeps the answer clear.

1. Write one offer and one area. A single service, such as a same-week booking, is easier to track than a general brand message.
2. Add a source question to the booking form or till script, with a streaming TV option. This count is needed to judge the test.
3. Create a tracked link or QR code that points only to the offer page, so visits from the ad can be separated from other traffic.
4. Set the budget before launch, and decide in advance what result would justify a second test.
5. Record new customers by source each week, then compare them with a baseline for the same period.

Weekly records should be kept even when the numbers look weak. A test with no written threshold tends to be judged by mood, so the decision rule should be fixed before the first view is bought.

## Is CTV Right for a Local Business?

CTV suits a business with a clear offer, a defined service area and a short path to action, such as a booking page or a phone number. It is a weak first move if the Google Business Profile is incomplete, the website cannot take bookings, or new customers cannot be traced to a source. Those basics matter more than the ad format.

## FAQ

### What does CTV stand for?
CTV stands for connected TV, meaning a television set that receives video over the internet through a smart TV, streaming stick or game console.

### Is CTV the same as OTT?
No. OTT, or over-the-top, is a wider term for video delivered over the internet to any device. CTV refers to the TV screen, so a vendor should be asked which devices its numbers include.

### Is CTV cheaper than Google Display?
The sources reviewed do not give CTV prices, so no comparison can be stated as fact. Google's display benchmark average is $0.63 per click, but CTV is priced by cost per thousand views, so the two are not directly comparable.

### How do I know if a CTV test worked?
Track new customers by source, use a tracked link or QR code, and compare results over several months. A single campaign rarely settles the question.
