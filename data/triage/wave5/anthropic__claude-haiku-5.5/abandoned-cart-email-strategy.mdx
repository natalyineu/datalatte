A small shop should send one or two abandoned cart emails to shoppers who gave their email address at checkout and then left. Start the first one within hours, keep it plain and link straight back to the cart. Email benchmarks from [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-marketing-benchmarks/) give a reference point: its ecommerce average open rate is 29.81% and its click rate is 1.74%. Those are general campaign figures, not cart recovery figures, so measure your own results from the start.

This guide is for local shops with an online store, such as a bakery, pet supply shop or salon retail range. It covers subject lines, a setup checklist, what each email should say and a worked example with every input labelled.

## What Do Email Benchmarks Tell a Cart Recovery Plan?

Mailchimp's benchmark tables compare open, click and unsubscribe rates by industry. For ecommerce, the average open rate is 29.81%, the click rate is 1.74% and the unsubscribe rate is 0.19%. Across all users the open rate is 35.63%. Mailchimp says the data covers campaigns sent to at least 1,000 subscribers, so it does not describe a single automated cart email sent to a handful of shoppers.

Two cautions apply. Mailchimp's data was last updated in December 2023, and the company warns that Apple's Mail Privacy Protection can affect open rates. Treat open rate as a rough signal. Click rate and orders from the email are more reliable measures of whether a cart reminder is doing its job.

## How Should a Cart Recovery Subject Line Read?

Mailchimp's guidance is specific enough to use as a checklist. Keep the subject line to no more than 9 words and 60 characters, because many shoppers read on phones. Use no more than 3 punctuation marks, since heavy punctuation can make an email look like spam. Use no more than 1 emoji, and use it to support the words rather than replace them.

Be descriptive rather than clever. A line such as "Your cart is waiting" tells the shopper little. A line that names the product, such as "Your sourdough loaf is still in your cart", gives a specific reason to open. Mailchimp also recommends merge tags to personalise subject lines with the recipient's name, which many cart platforms support.

## How Do You Set Up Cart Recovery Emails?

Setup depends on your store platform and your email tool, so start by checking what the platform already sends. Many store platforms include a built-in abandoned checkout message, and an email tool may add a second one. Two reminders for the same cart look careless and confuse the shopper.

1. Open your store platform and find its abandoned checkout settings. Note whether a built-in message is already on.
2. Decide which system will send the cart emails. Keep only one active.
3. Confirm the trigger: a shopper who entered an email address and left before paying.
4. Set the first email's delay. Choose a few hours rather than a day, then test a second option later.
5. Write the first email using the content rules in the next section.
6. Send a test order, stop at payment and check that the email arrives and the cart link opens the correct basket.
7. Record the date you switched it on so you can compare orders before and after.

## What Should Each Email Say?

Keep the first email plain. Show the item or items in the cart, the price and a link back to the basket. Do not add pressure language such as countdown timers or stock warnings unless stock is genuinely limited. Shoppers who were interrupted usually need a reminder, not a sales pitch.

A second email can answer likely worries, such as delivery times or the returns policy, if those are clear on the site. Use this email to address the reasons shoppers commonly leave: unexpected costs, unclear delivery or a complicated checkout. If the checkout itself causes problems, fix that on the site. A reminder cannot repair a form that frustrates people.

Discounts are optional. A discount in the first email can train shoppers to abandon carts in the hope of one, so reserve any incentive for a later email and only if margins allow it.

## What Is a Cart Recovery Email Worth? A Worked Example

All inputs below are assumptions except the two Mailchimp benchmarks, which are labelled. The example uses campaign-level rates, so it is a rough estimate of the ceiling rather than a forecast.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Checkouts started per month | 200 (assumption) |
| Share of checkouts abandoned | 70% (assumption) |
| Abandoned carts with an email address | 60% (assumption) |
| Cart emails delivered per month | 84 (calculated) |
| Ecommerce avg. open rate | 29.81% (Mailchimp) |
| Ecommerce avg. click rate | 1.74% (Mailchimp) |
| Share of clickers who complete an order | 1 in 3 (assumption) |
| Average order value | $40 (assumption) |

The calculation runs as follows. With 200 checkouts and 70% abandoned, 140 carts are left. If 60% include an email address, 84 shoppers receive the message. At a 1.74% click rate, about 1.5 people click. If one in three of those completes an order, the result is about half an order, or roughly $20 a month.

That figure is small, and the example is built to show that. Real results may be higher or lower. The number to watch is orders from the email, not the number of emails sent. For a shop with more traffic, the same logic scales directly with the number of abandoned carts that have an email address attached.

## How Do You Measure Whether It Works?

Compare orders from recovery emails against the carts they were sent to, over the same period before and after you switched them on. Use a period long enough to smooth out normal swings in order volume, and avoid comparing a busy holiday week with a quiet one. Most email tools report emails sent, clicks and orders attributed to a campaign. Use the same definition each month so the numbers stay comparable.

Track unsubscribes too. Mailchimp's ecommerce average unsubscribe rate is 0.19%. If the rate for cart emails is well above that, the message may be too frequent or too pushy. Remove the second email first and see whether the rate falls.

## FAQ

### How many abandoned cart emails should a small shop send?

Start with one and add a second only if your unsubscribe rate stays low and you can see orders coming from it. Two is a sensible maximum for most small shops.

### How soon should the first email go out?

Within hours is the usual starting point. Test a second delay after the first set of results comes in, and compare orders for each.

### Should the first email include a discount?

Not usually. Save any incentive for a later email, and only if margins allow it. Discounts sent to everyone can train shoppers to leave carts on purpose.

### Are open rates reliable for cart emails?

Not fully. Mailchimp notes that Apple's Mail Privacy Protection can affect open rates. Use clicks and orders from the email as the main measures.

### What if my shop also sells in person?

Cart recovery only applies to online checkouts. For in-store customers, consider a welcome sequence or booking reminders instead, and measure them the same way.
