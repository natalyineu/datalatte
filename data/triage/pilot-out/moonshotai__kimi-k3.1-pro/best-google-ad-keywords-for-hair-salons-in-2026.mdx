The best Google Ads keywords for hair salons combine a service, a location and clear booking intent. Start with terms such as “balayage [city]”, “hair salon near me”, “women’s haircut [city]”, “hair extensions [city]”, “bridal hair [city]” and “curly hair specialist near me”. These phrases communicate a service more clearly than broad inspiration searches such as “hairstyles”.

Build ad groups around the services the salon wants to book, send each ad to a matching page, and review actual searches for irrelevant queries. Salon Guru’s campaign process includes keyword variations, match types, negative keywords and ongoing analysis ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/)).

## Which keywords show strong booking intent?

Use the following phrases as test candidates, not proven winners. Neither supplied source publishes hair-salon search volumes, click prices or conversion benchmarks. The right list depends on the services offered, the salon’s catchment and the bookings that follow.

| Service theme | Keyword ideas | Matching landing page |
|---|---|---|
| General local searches | hair salon near me; hair salon [city]; book hair appointment [city] | Main services and booking |
| Colour | balayage [city]; hair colourist [neighbourhood]; hair colour correction [city] | Colour services and starting prices |
| Cutting and styling | women’s haircut [city]; same-day haircut near me; express men’s haircut [city] | Relevant cutting or styling service |
| Treatments | keratin treatment near me; Japanese straightening near me | Individual treatment page |
| Extensions | hair extensions [city]; hair extension consultation [city] | Extension methods and consultation |
| Occasions | bridal hair [city]; wedding hair trial [city]; party hair updo [city] | Occasion services and booking process |
| Specialisms | curly hair specialist near me; organic hair salon [city] | Relevant expertise or product approach |

A phrase such as “hair colour correction [city]” gives the campaign more context than “hair ideas”. It identifies the service and lets the landing page answer the corresponding need. That does not guarantee a booking, but it makes keyword, advertisement and page alignment easier.

Only include qualifiers that are accurate. “Same-day”, “open late”, “organic” and “specialist” can attract unsuitable enquiries if the salon cannot fulfil them.

## How should match types and negative keywords be used?

The sources do not establish a universal match-type rule for salons. Salon Guru includes match-type variations and negative keywords in campaign preparation. The secondary LinkedIn example uses broad-match keywords and explicitly recommends negatives to filter irrelevant searches ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/); [secondary guide](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/step-by-step-guide-running-google-ads-salon-services-hi4df)).

The practical lesson is that expansion and exclusion belong together. Broad match in an example campaign is not evidence that it suits every hair salon. A salon without reliable conversion tracking should resolve that gap before using its budget to discover large numbers of unrelated queries.

Candidate negative themes include:

- DIY and learning searches, such as “diy”, “at home”, “how to” and “tutorial”.
- Employment and training searches, such as “jobs”, “career”, “salary”, “school” and “course”.
- Product and supply searches, such as “wholesale”, “supplies”, “kit” and “tools”.
- Services the salon does not provide, including nail, barber or pet-grooming terms where relevant.
- “Free” when no suitable free service exists.

Do not apply that list mechanically. A salon offering nail or barber services should retain those searches and can compare the dedicated keyword guides for [nail salons](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-nail-salons-in-2026) and [barbershops](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-barbershops-in-2026).

## How should campaigns and landing pages be organised?

Separate ad groups by service when the advertisements and landing pages need different content. Colour, cutting, extensions and bridal searches can all belong to the same salon without belonging in the same ad group.

Salon Guru’s setup sequence starts with groups of phrases, then aligns landing-page keywords and client hooks, builds keyword variations and negatives, creates ads and ad groups, and expands using analysed results ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/)). That supports a simple structure:

- Send colour searches to a colour page with relevant treatments and booking information.
- Send extension searches to extension methods, maintenance information and consultation details.
- Send bridal searches to trial, availability and occasion-service information.
- Keep salon-name searches separate if a brand campaign is tested.

The secondary example recommends call, sitelink, callout, structured-snippet, image and location assets. Its sitelink ideas include booking, services, location and business information. Call assets are scheduled during business hours, while the location asset connects to the business profile ([secondary guide](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/step-by-step-guide-running-google-ads-salon-services-hi4df)).

Before launch, that guide also recommends conversion tracking for forms and telephone calls, linked Google Ads and Analytics accounts, competitor research and a team ready to follow up. Those preparations make lead outcomes visible instead of leaving the campaign to be judged by clicks alone.

## How can an assumption-based budget compare keyword intent?

Worked example: every input below is an assumption created for arithmetic. None is a market benchmark, forecast or client result. The comparison isolates booking intent by holding the assumed budget, click price and client value constant.

| Input or calculation | Specific service keyword | Broad inspiration keyword |
|---|---:|---:|
| Monthly budget, assumption | $630 | $630 |
| Cost per click, assumption | $3.50 | $3.50 |
| Calculated clicks | 180 | 180 |
| Booking rate, assumption | 5% | 1% |
| Calculated bookings | 9 | 1.8 |
| Calculated cost per booking | $70 | $350 |
| Revenue per new client, assumption | $120 | $120 |
| Calculated revenue | $1,080 | $216 |

Under these assumptions, the specific keyword produces five times the bookings from the same budget. The fractional booking is expected-value arithmetic, not a literal appointment. The comparison illustrates sensitivity to intent; it does not predict that “balayage [city]” will outperform “hairstyles” for a particular salon.

Replace every assumption with actual campaign and booking information before making budget decisions. Revenue also needs context: service costs, repeat visits, cancellations and capacity can change whether an apparently inexpensive booking is commercially useful.

## Which seasonal and long-tail ideas are worth testing?

Seasonal phrases can connect an existing service with an occasion, but the sources provide no seasonal search-volume data. Treat the calendar as a source of test ideas rather than evidence of demand.

| Occasion | Keyword ideas to assess |
|---|---|
| New Year | new year haircut [city]; hair colour refresh [city] |
| Valentine’s Day | Valentine’s hair updo [city]; date night hair [city] |
| Weddings | bridal hair [city]; wedding hair trial [city] |
| Back to school | kids haircut [city]; back to school haircut [city] |
| December celebrations | party hair updo [city]; hair salon open Christmas Eve [city] |

Longer phrases can also clarify a constraint. Candidates include “curly hair specialist near me”, “hair salon with wheelchair access [city]”, “hair salon with parking [city]”, “hair salon open late [city]” and “organic hair salon [city]”.

These phrases are not automatically cheaper or more likely to convert. Their value comes from accurately describing an available service and making the landing page more specific. Remove any seasonal advertisement when the corresponding appointment or availability is no longer offered.
