A hair salon Facebook ads strategy should connect each campaign to a booked, attended appointment, not just clicks or form fills. Define a new-client offer, target the area the salon can realistically serve, send people to a clear booking page, and record which enquiries become paying visits. Set the budget from unit economics: divide ad spend by attended new clients, then compare acquisition cost with gross profit over a chosen period. No salon-specific Facebook benchmark is asserted here; the worked cost example uses labelled assumptions.

## What should a hair salon Facebook campaign achieve?

Start with the business result rather than the campaign format. A useful primary outcome is an attended appointment from someone who fits the salon’s service area and offer. Enquiries, booking starts and clicks can help diagnose the funnel, but they should not be mixed together in the acquisition-cost calculation.

Choose whether the campaign is for new clients, lapsed clients or a specific service. Each group needs a different baseline because existing clients may have booked without advertising. Keep the reporting cohort consistent: if the offer is for new clients, exclude appointments from people already in the client database.

Facebook can be tested alongside other channels rather than treated as the default. The [Google Ads versus Facebook Ads comparison](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) helps frame that channel choice. The decision should depend on measurable bookings and contribution, not on which dashboard reports the cheapest click.

Before launch, write the offer, measurement period and stop condition in the campaign notes. That prevents a cheap lead from being celebrated while the front desk is seeing no attended appointments.

## How should tracking be set up before launch?

Create a measurement dictionary before creating ads. State what counts as an enquiry, what counts as a booking and when a booking becomes an attended new client. Use those definitions in the advertising report and the salon’s appointment records so the same person is not counted under several outcomes.

If the website and booking flow support it, install the Meta Pixel and configure an event representing the chosen action. A booking-button click may be useful for diagnosis, but it should remain separate from a completed booking. Ask the website or booking provider which events can be passed accurately.

Where the platform supports server-side event sharing, ask whether Conversions API is available and how duplicate browser and server events are handled. Avoid assuming that a particular integration exists. The provider’s documentation or support team should confirm the available setup.

Complete a test journey before spending. Open the ad destination on a phone, submit a test enquiry or booking, and check that the intended event appears once. Then confirm that the appointment system can identify the test record.

Add a source question to the booking or intake process and keep a simple front-desk record of enquiries, appointments and attendance. Campaign names and offer codes can help connect records, but staff should not have to reconstruct every result from memory.

## Who should see the ads, and what should the offer say?

Build the geographic targeting around the salon’s actual catchment. Review the locations of suitable existing clients and consider the travel pattern around the salon. A radius that looks sensible on a map may still produce enquiries from people unwilling to travel for the advertised service.

Let the creative explain who the appointment is for. Show the salon’s own work where permission has been obtained, identify the service clearly and avoid presenting a result that the advertised stylist or appointment length cannot deliver. The landing page should repeat the same service and offer.

The offer should state what is included, who qualifies, any price condition and how the appointment can be requested. A consultation, a service-specific introduction or an added treatment may be tested without assuming that a discount will produce profitable clients.

## How can budget and cost per client be calculated?

Start with economics rather than an arbitrary monthly allowance. The [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) provides broader budgeting context. For a salon forecast, the essential comparison is acquisition cost with gross profit generated during a clearly defined measurement period.

| Input | Assumed value |

| Monthly ad spend | $600 |

| Output | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Leads | $600 ÷ $30 | 20 |
| Attended new clients | 20 × 40% | 8 |
| Ad cost per attended new client | $600 ÷ 8 | $75 |
| Gross profit per new client | $90 × 2 × 50% | $90 |
| Cohort gross profit | 8 × $90 | $720 |
| Cohort gross profit after ad spend | $720 − $600 | $120 |
| Break-even cost per lead | $90 × 40% | $36 |

The break-even cost per lead equals gross profit per new client multiplied by the lead-to-attended-client rate. Replace every assumption with salon records before setting a real budget. Also keep product costs, stylist capacity and the selected period visible; revenue alone does not show whether the campaign contributed profit.

## What should happen after a click or enquiry?

The landing page should make the advertised service, suitability and booking action easy to understand on a mobile connection. Avoid sending every visitor to a generic homepage if a relevant service or booking page exists. Test the complete journey whenever the page, form or booking provider changes.

Give enquiries an owner. The responsible person should know where messages arrive, how availability is checked and what happens when the requested stylist or time is unavailable. An advertising report cannot fix enquiries that remain unanswered or bookings that cannot be scheduled.

Record whether each lead booked, attended and matched the campaign’s client definition. Note cancellations and no-shows separately so the salon can see where the funnel changes. Avoid treating every submitted form as a new client.

At checkout, offer the appropriate next appointment and explain the maintenance or rebooking option for that service. The worked example depends on later visits, so retention should be measured rather than presumed. If later visits do not occur, replace that assumption before increasing spend.

## How should results be reviewed and improved?

Review spend, valid leads, attended new clients and acquisition cost together. Creative metrics can help diagnose attention, but they should not override appointment records. Keep campaign, audience and creative names consistent enough to connect results with the offer being tested.

Change a limited part of the campaign at a time and record what changed. A new image, a different offer and a revised landing page introduced together make the cause of a result difficult to identify. Check tracking again after substantial website or booking-flow changes.

Use the campaign workflow that exposes the objective, event and reporting controls required by the measurement plan. If a simplified promotion workflow cannot provide that information, it is unsuitable for evaluating acquisition cost.

Set the stop condition from the salon’s economics. Investigate when tracking breaks, enquiries cannot be handled or acquisition cost remains incompatible with gross profit. Do not use an invented industry average as the reason to continue.

## FAQ

### What should count as a Facebook lead for a salon?

Use a definition that matches the campaign and keep it consistent. An enquiry, booking request and attended appointment represent different stages. Acquisition cost should ultimately use attended new clients when the campaign’s purpose is new-client growth.

### Is there a reliable Facebook cost benchmark for every salon?

No universal salon figure is established by the supplied source. Location, offer, booking flow and lead quality can change the result. Use clearly labelled assumptions for planning, then replace them with the salon’s own advertising and appointment records.

### When is an acquired client too expensive?

Compare ad cost per attended new client with gross profit over the selected measurement period. If acquisition cost exceeds that gross profit, the campaign does not cover its ad spend within that period under the stated assumptions.

### Should campaign performance be judged by clicks?

Clicks can help diagnose whether an ad generates visits, but they do not establish client acquisition. Connect click data with valid enquiries, bookings, attendance and gross profit before deciding whether the campaign should continue.
