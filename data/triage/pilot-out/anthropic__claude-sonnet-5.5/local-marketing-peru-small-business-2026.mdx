Local marketing in Peru works best when it starts with one specific problem and tests one channel at a time. The problem might be a brand that no longer matches the business, a website with visits but few inquiries, weak visibility in Google, or ads that miss the right audience. Two Lima agency sites, [Onza Marketing](https://onzamarketing.com/) and [Manya](https://manya.pe/), each list six starting points along these lines. A small shop can pick one, run it for four weeks and measure it.

This update does not quote Peruvian audience sizes, ad prices or payment volumes. None of those figures could be tied to a source reviewed for this version, so they are left out. Check current numbers in each platform's own tools before setting a budget.

## What are the main starting points for a Peruvian small business?

Onza frames its offer as six business challenges: a brand that no longer reflects the business, a website that does not generate enough inquiries, difficulty appearing in Google, a wish to reach more customers with ads, a wish to sell online, and social media without a clear strategy. The site presents these as different problems that need different fixes.

Manya's six goals overlap on brand, website, search and lead generation. It adds website support, SEO aimed at AI-driven search engines, and automation of repetitive tasks. Its homepage also promises a personalised diagnosis before recommending a solution.

Taken together, four themes recur across the two agencies: brand clarity, a website that converts, being found in search, and paid reach. Online selling, social media and automation appear on one list or the other, so they are better treated as second-stage projects.

A shop with a physical location and a handful of products rarely needs all of them at once. The practical reading is that the diagnosis matters more than the service menu. A business that cannot say which of the six problems it has is not ready to compare quotes.

## Which services do Lima agencies sell for local visibility?

Onza's solutions list has seven blocks: branding, web design, organic positioning (SEO), online stores, social media management, paid media and graphic design. Each links to its own service page, and the homepage shows a Google Partner badge.

[Marketing Branding PERU](https://www.marketing-branding.pe/casos-de-exito-marketing-peru-y-latinoamerica/) leans harder on Google products. Its case-study page names Google Ads, Google Shopping, SEO, analytics and market studies, and tags individual cases with services such as Performance Max, Demand Gen, Google Analytics 4 and web hosting.

Two things follow for a small business. First, the vocabulary differs between agencies, so "paid media" at one firm may be "Google Ads" at another. Ask for the exact deliverables in writing.

Second, many local-visibility tasks do not need an agency at all. Completing a Google Business Profile, posting a payment option and answering messages quickly cost time rather than fees. An agency adds most where the work is technical, such as tracking setup, campaign structure or site rebuilds.

## What do agency track-record claims actually prove?

Every number on these sites is the agency's own. Onza reports activity since 2013, more than 1,000 projects, support from more than 15 countries and more than 10 sectors served. Manya reports more than 800 clients and shows named testimonials. Marketing Branding PERU reports serving more than 400 companies across Peru, Chile and Colombia using Google tools.

Those figures show volume and age, which is useful for judging whether a firm is established. They do not show results. A project count says nothing about whether the projects produced calls, bookings or sales.

The case-study page at Marketing Branding PERU lists client names with service tags. In the portion reviewed, no revenue or conversion figures were attached to the entries, so the page works as a list of past work, not as evidence of outcomes.

Before relying on any claim, ask for a reference in a similar sector and city. A dentist in Lima and a restaurant in Cusco face different search behaviour, and a shop should weigh the claim accordingly. A fair test is whether the agency can name the metric it moved and the period it was measured over.

## Which problem should a small shop fix first?

The order below is a reasoning aid, not a sourced rule. It follows the logic of Onza's own challenge list: each problem sits at a different point in the customer's path.

1. If customers search for the product and find competitors first, fix the free Google listing and basic search visibility.
2. If visitors arrive but do not call or message, fix the contact path on the website before buying traffic.
3. If the contact path works, test a small ad budget to see whether paid reach brings messages at a tolerable cost.
4. If the shop can state clearly what it sells and to whom, then plan social posts around it.

Ads sit third because they send people to whatever page exists. A weak page makes paid clicks expensive in proportion to the sales they produce.

Messaging also deserves attention. Onza's main call to action opens a WhatsApp conversation, which suggests that agencies expect prospects to prefer chat to forms. A shop can copy that by putting a WhatsApp link on every profile and page. ## How could a Peruvian shop test channels? (worked example)

All numbers in this example are **assumptions** for illustration, not benchmarks. A bakery in Arequipa has 10 hours a week and a small test budget.

| Step | Assumption | Check after 4 weeks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete Google Business Profile | 2 hours | Calls and direction taps |
| Facebook page posts with a Yape or Plin payment option | 3 hours | Orders mentioning Facebook |
| Short video for TikTok or Reels | 3 hours | Views and messages |
| Ask each customer how they found the shop | 1 hour | Tally on a sheet |
| Test ad | 1 hour | Cost per message from the ad account |

The hours add up to 10, which matches the weekly limit.

Now the ad test. Assume the bakery spends 150 soles and receives 25 messages, so cost per message is 150 / 25 = 6 soles. Assume 6 of those messages become paid orders averaging 40 soles, which gives 240 soles of sales and a cost of 25 soles per order. With an assumed gross margin of 35%, gross profit is 240 x 0.35 = 84 soles, which is 66 soles short of the ad spend.

That result does not make ads a failure. It shows that a first-order loss needs repeat purchases to pay back, so the shop should track return customers. If the Facebook or Instagram test works and posting becomes a chore, [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) compares scheduling tools. ## What should a business ask an agency before signing?

Both Onza and Manya say they start with a diagnosis, so use that stage to test the agency. Questions worth putting in writing:

- Which of my problems is the first one to fix, and what is the evidence?
- Which result have you produced for a business of similar size and sector, and can I speak to that client?
- Who owns the ad account, analytics property and domain if the contract ends?
- Which numbers will be reported each month, and which count as success?
- What is the smallest paid test you would run first, and what would make you stop it?

Answers that cite a metric and a time period are more useful than answers that cite a client logo. Onza's page lists logos from several sectors, and Manya's lists testimonials. Both show relationships, not outcomes.

An agency that refuses to hand over account ownership or avoids naming a first test deserves caution. A business that asks these questions of two or three firms will also learn what a fair scope looks like in its own city.

## FAQ

### What is the first step in local marketing for a Peruvian small business?
Name the single biggest problem, such as low visibility in Google or a website that produces few inquiries, and test one fix for four weeks. Both Lima agency sites reviewed here begin with a diagnosis of this kind.

### Are agency client counts reliable?
They are self-reported. Onza claims more than 1,000 projects, Manya more than 800 clients and Marketing Branding PERU more than 400 companies. Treat them as signs of experience and ask for sector-specific references to judge results.

### Do I need an agency to start?
Not for the basics. Completing a Google Business Profile, adding a payment option to profiles and tracking how customers found the shop need only time. Agencies add the most value for tracking setup, campaign structure and website rebuilds.

### How much should I spend on a first ad test?
No sourced Peruvian ad price is given here. Choose an amount you can lose, read cost per message from your own ad account, and compare it with the margin per order as in the worked example above.
