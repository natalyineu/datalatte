The best Google Ads keywords for hair salons pair a service with a place: "balayage [city]", "hair salon near me", "women's haircut [city]", "bridal hair [city]" and "hair extensions [city]". These searchers are close to booking, unlike people typing "hairstyles".

[Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/) starts a campaign by choosing a group of phrases such as hair extensions, balayage or hair colour, then builds a list with variations, match types and negative keywords. Click costs and search volumes differ by city, so check Keyword Planner for local numbers.

## Which high-intent keywords should a hair salon start with?

The 11 terms below are starting points. The reasoning column is DataLatte's judgement, not measured data, and no click costs are quoted because they vary by city.

| Keyword idea | Why it may work |
|---|---|
| "hair salon near me" | Broad local intent, likely competitive |
| "hair salon [city]" | Clear location, easy to target |
| "balayage [city]" | Specific, higher-value service |
| "women's haircut [city]" | Core service |
| "book hair appointment [city]" | Booking intent |
| "hair colourist [neighbourhood]" | Very local |
| "hair extensions [city]" | Higher-ticket service |
| "bridal hair [city]" | Event bookings, plan ahead |
| "curly hair specialist near me" | Niche people search by name |
| "affordable hair salon [city]" | Price intent, only if pricing fits |
| "same-day haircut near me" | Urgency, mostly on phones |

Look up each term in Keyword Planner for local volume and suggested bids. Then begin with the few that match the services the salon most wants to sell.

## Why does intent matter more than search volume?

"Hairstyles for curly hair" is inspiration. "Curly hair specialist near me" is an appointment request. Budget should reach the second group first, which is why a service-plus-place structure beats generic terms.

Salon Guru lists landing page keywords and client "hooks" as a step that lowers click costs. In practice, each service needs a page matching the search, with a starting price and a clear booking button, not the homepage.

The same source warns that Google Ads can be costly and wasteful if not managed correctly. A loose keyword list is the fastest route to that outcome. A tight list will not rescue a weak page, but a loose one wastes a good page.

Campaign structure can follow the same logic: separate ad groups for cuts, colour, extensions and bridal work, so ad text mirrors the search. Salon Guru's last step is to add more ad groups and phrases as results are analysed, which means the first list is a draft.

## How much can a specific keyword save?

Every figure in this example is an assumption chosen for the arithmetic. None is a market statistic or a client result.

Assumptions: a monthly budget of $630, a cost of $3.50 per click and an average new-client visit worth $120 for both keywords. Only the keyword type changes.

| | "balayage [city]" | "hairstyles" |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks ($630 / $3.50, assumption) | 180 | 180 |
| Share who book (assumption) | 5% | 1% |
| Bookings | 9 | 1.8 |
| Cost per booking | $630 / 9 = $70 | $630 / 1.8 = $350 |
| Revenue at $120 (assumption) | $1,080 | $216 |

The same spend produces five times the bookings, but only because the 5% and 1% were assumed. Real booking rates will differ. Thirty days of the salon's own click and booking data will show the actual split, and cost per booking is the number to track.

## Which long-tail and seasonal keywords are worth testing?

Longer phrases carry lower volume but clearer intent. Ideas to test: "organic hair salon in [city]", "best hair colourist for balayage in [neighbourhood]", "keratin treatment near me", "Japanese straightening near me", "hair salon open late [city]" and "hair salon with wheelchair access [city]".

Seasonal terms deserve their own ad groups so they can be switched on a few weeks before demand rises:

- New Year: "new year new hair", "hair colour refresh"
- Spring and summer: "wedding hair [city]", "bridal updo [city]"
- Late summer: "back to school haircut"
- December: "party hair updo", "hair salon open Christmas Eve [city]"

This calendar is a planning suggestion, not search data. Google Trends for the salon's region shows when searches actually climb.

Salons that cut men's hair can borrow ideas from the [barbershop keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-barbershops-in-2026). Salons that do not should list "barber" as a negative keyword.

## Which negative keywords stop wasted spend?

The San Diego example uses broad match and says to apply negative keywords to filter out irrelevant searches ([LinkedIn, secondary](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/step-by-step-guide-running-google-ads-salon-services-hi4df)). Salon Guru also builds negatives into the keyword list from the start ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/)).

Useful themes for a hair salon:

- DIY and learning: "diy", "at home", "how to", "tutorial", "what is"
- Jobs and training: "jobs", "career", "salary", "school", "course"
- Supplies: "wholesale", "supplies", "kit", "tools"
- Services not offered: "dog grooming", "nail salon", "barber"
- Price-only searches such as "free", when they do not fit the offer

A cautious approach is to run tighter match types on the core terms and widen only after real search data shows what converts. Review the actual search terms weekly in the first month and add new negatives each time irrelevant queries appear.

## How does a published salon campaign set up the account?

The San Diego example targets a 5-15 km radius around the salon and selects "People present in this area". It sets the goal to Leads, uses Maximise Conversions bidding and unticks Google Search Partners and Display Partners. Budget is $50-$100 per day for search and $10-$25 per day for local listing ads.

Ads follow Google's character limits: headlines up to 30 characters and descriptions up to 90. Call assets are scheduled for business hours, alongside sitelinks, callouts, a location asset and images.

Hair salons can adapt that structure with service-specific ad groups and a local campaign using "near me" terms. The [nail salon keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-nail-salons-in-2026) shows the same approach for a neighbouring trade. Scale the budget to the salon's own booking value rather than copying the figures above.

## What should be in place before the ads go live?

The same LinkedIn example lists five preparations before spending anything:

1. A complete Google Business Profile with photos, reviews and a phone number.
2. Conversion tracking for both form submissions and phone calls.
3. Google Ads linked to Analytics for accurate reporting.
4. Competitor research on local pricing and services.
5. A team ready to follow up on incoming leads.

The last item gets skipped most often. Ads that generate calls nobody answers waste the budget as surely as bad keywords. Match ad schedules to the hours the phone is staffed, or offer online booking that works around the clock.

Other appointment-led trades face the same follow-up problem, as the [chiropractor keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-chiropractors-in-2026) shows.

## Should a salon use Google Ads or SEO first?

Salon Guru says the answer depends on goals. Google Ads can generate enquiries almost immediately by placing an advert at the top of results. SEO builds long-term visibility in organic results. Its view is that the strongest approach combines both ([Salon Guru, secondary](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/)).

The same agency says many owners start with ads for quick business while SEO gains momentum, then reduce ad spend as organic rankings improve. It adds that getting to the top organically usually takes a few weeks. That is a vendor claim, so timing will vary by market.

Keyword research serves both channels. The terms that convert in paid campaigns are good candidates for service pages and Google Business Profile descriptions.
