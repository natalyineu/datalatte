Hair salon Facebook ads cost $50.91 per lead on average for Beauty & Personal Care, according to [LocaliQ's Facebook advertising benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/facebook-advertising-benchmarks/) (updated September 23, 2026). If 40% of those leads become paying clients, each new client costs about $127 in ads. A typical client generates roughly $128 in gross profit over six months (assumption, see worked example below). That means break-even, not profit. Success depends on three things: tracking, the offer, and follow-up.

This guide covers benchmarks, tracking setup, local targeting, offers to test, and the math that tells you whether to keep spending. Dollar figures are US benchmarks. Use the same logic with your local currency.

The beauty cost per lead ($50.91) is nearly double the all-industry average ($27.39). The conversion rate is lower (5.63% vs 8.54%). Traffic campaigns report no conversion rate or cost per lead. A "lead" in the benchmark is a form or similar action, not a client in your chair. Your own lead-to-client rate decides the real cost.

## Step 1: How Do You Set Up Tracking?

Without tracking, you cannot see which ads produce bookings.

- Install the **Meta Pixel** on your website. Add an event for a completed booking or a click on your booking button.
- Add the **Conversions API** alongside the Pixel. [Meta recommends](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/conversions-api/deduplicate-pixel-and-server-events.md) a redundant setup. Deduplication prevents double-counting. Meta matches events by event ID and deduplicates events received within 48 hours.
- Check events arrive in Events Manager after a test booking.
- Add "How did you hear about us?" to your booking form. Give each ad a unique offer code. Platform reports show leads; your front desk knows which became clients.

## Step 2: How Do You Target Locally?

Most salon clients come from a short drive. Pull the postcodes of your last 50 new clients. Use that to set a radius in Ads Manager. Keep age and interest targeting broad. Let the creative do the filtering. If you have a client list with permission, build a custom audience and exclude current clients from new-client offers.

## Step 3: What Offers Should You Test?

The offer carries more weight than the audience. Test one at a time.

| Offer idea | Why it can work | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New-client consultation | Low commitment, builds trust | Needs a quick-to-book calendar |
| Free add-on with first colour | Protects your price | Costs product and time |
| Service-led ad (balayage, extensions) | Reaches high-ticket buyers | Needs real photos of that service |
| Quiet-slot offer (weekday mornings) | Fills empty chairs | Do not train regulars to wait for deals |

Use your own work: before-and-after photos, a 10-20 second video, a stylist introducing herself. Get client consent. State the offer, price, and end date.

## Step 4: What Objective and Budget Should You Choose?

If tracking works, choose a leads or sales objective. If not, start with traffic to your booking page and fix tracking in week one. Work backwards from what a client is worth. The maximum cost per lead equals gross profit per client multiplied by your lead-to-client rate. See our [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) for more.

Meta's delivery system needs conversion events to learn. Third-party guides cite about 50 optimisation events within 7 days per ad set. Treat that as a reason to run one or two ad sets, not ten.

## Step 5: How Do You Follow Up?

Reply to every enquiry the same day, ideally within an hour during business hours. Make the first available slot visible on the landing page. If you take bookings by phone, ensure someone answers the number in the ad.

## Step 6: How Do You Retarget?

Build an audience of people who visited your booking page or engaged with your page. Show them a different message: a client review, a before-and-after, or "two appointments left this Thursday". Rotate the creative.

## Step 7: What Numbers Should You Read Weekly?

Open Ads Manager once a week. Look at:

1. Cost per lead or booking.
2. Which ad and audience produced the most bookings.
3. Click-through rate.

Pause the weakest ad. Keep the best. Test one new thing. Give an ad one to two weeks before deciding.

## The Math

Take a $600 monthly ad budget. Only the "Average" cost-per-lead uses a published figure ($50.91, LocaliQ). Every other number is an **assumption** for illustration. Replace them with your own.

| | Tough | Average | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend (assumption) | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per lead | $80 (assumption) | $50.91 (benchmark) | $30 (assumption) |
| Leads | 7.5 | 11.8 | 20 |
| Lead to attended client rate (assumption) | 25% | 40% | 50% |
| New clients | 1.9 | 4.7 | 10 |
| Ad cost per new client | $320 | $127 | $60 |
| Average ticket (assumption) | $85 | $85 | $85 |
| Visits in first six months (assumption) | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 50% | 50% | 50% |
| Gross profit per client over six months | $128 | $128 | $128 |
| Gross profit minus ad spend | -$361 | +$1 | +$675 |

At the benchmark cost per lead and a 40% lead-to-client rate, you roughly break even over six months. Profit comes from the second and third visit. The Strong column is possible but better than the benchmark.

Two formulas worth keeping:

- **Maximum cost per lead** = gross profit per client x lead-to-client rate = $128 x 0.40 = about $51.
- **Maximum cost per new client** = gross profit per client over your chosen period.

The lever that moves the result most is the lead-to-client rate. Going from 25% to 50% halves your cost per client with no change to the account.

## Common Mistakes

- Targeting a whole county. People will not drive that far for a haircut.
- No tracking, then arguing about results.
- Sending traffic to a slow or confusing booking page. Test on your phone.
- Judging a creative on reach or likes. Judge on bookings.
- Discounting too deeply. A free add-on protects pricing better.
- Turning ads off after one quiet week. Allow a fair test.

## Do This Today

1. Pull your last 50 new clients' postcodes and decide your ad radius.
2. Work out your gross profit per new client over six months and your maximum cost per lead.
3. Check your booking link works on a phone. Install the Meta Pixel and a booking event. Ask about the Conversions API.
4. Write one specific new-client offer with real photos. Add an offer code or "how did you hear about us" question.
5. Launch one campaign with one or two ad sets. Set a four-week window and a written continue-or-stop number.

## FAQ

### How much does a lead cost for a hair salon on Facebook?
LocaliQ's benchmarks list $50.91 per lead for Beauty & Personal Care lead campaigns (updated September 2026). Your number depends on your offer, area, and landing page.

### Do I really need the Meta Pixel?
You can run ads without it, but Meta cannot optimise for bookings and you cannot see which ads produce them. Meta recommends running the Conversions API alongside the Pixel.

### How long should I run an ad before deciding?
One to two weeks as a rule of thumb. Meta's learning phase is widely reported at about 50 optimisation events in 7 days. A small salon will often fall short, so judge on cost per booking.

### Should I boost posts or use Ads Manager?
Ads Manager for bookings. Boosts give less control over objective and tracking.

### Facebook or Instagram?
Meta ads run on both from the same account. Test placements together first. See our [Instagram ads guide for hair salons](/blog/using-meta-ads-for-hair-salon-marketing).
