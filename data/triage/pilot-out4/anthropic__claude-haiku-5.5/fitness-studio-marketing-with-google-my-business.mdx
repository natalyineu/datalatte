Google My Business (GMB) is the free listing that shows your fitness studio in Google Search and Maps. It is where many local searchers decide whether to call, visit or ask for directions. To start, claim or create your listing, verify it, and complete every field with the same name, address and phone number you use elsewhere. Then add photos, collect reviews and post regular updates. The listing costs nothing to run, but it needs a weekly routine to keep it working.

## How much does a Google listing matter for a fitness studio?

Most studio owners underestimate how often local searches start with a category rather than a brand name. A search for "yoga near me" or "CrossFit gym" is a discovery search, and the businesses that appear are the ones with a complete listing. According to a BrightLocal study cited by [Dublin Digital Agency](https://dublindigitalagency.weebly.com/blog/google-my-business-for-gym-marketing) (secondary), 84% of searches are discovery searches.

## How do you claim and verify your listing?

Start at google.com/business and sign in with a business Google account. The Dublin Digital Agency guide lists the setup sequence: enter the business name, your name, address and phone number, the website link, then choose a category (for example, CrossFit, personal trainer or fitness centre). Verification follows, using the methods Google offers for your listing (secondary).

1. Sign in with the business Google account and open the listing setup.
2. Enter the studio name exactly as it appears on your signage and website.
3. Add the address, phone number and website link.
4. Choose fitness-specific categories that match your main services.
5. Complete verification and confirm the details are live.

Use the same studio name across every profile. The Appointy guide to fitness social media recommends uniform naming so your brand looks consistent wherever clients find you (vendor).

## Which fields and photos should you fill in first?

Accuracy comes first. The Dublin Digital Agency guide stresses matching name, address and phone (NAP) information, operating hours and a short description with a call to action (secondary). Stale hours are a common problem. A listing that shows a holiday schedule you no longer follow sends people to a closed door.

Photos carry weight too. The same guide cites Main Street ROI for more than 40% more requests and more than 30% more website visits for listings with photos (secondary). Use bright, clean shots of the studio floor, class sessions and trainers. Skip mirror selfies, which the Appointy guide advises against for profile images (vendor).

## How should you handle reviews?

Reviews shape both ranking and conversion. The Association of Fitness Studios article recommends a standing system: ask happy members for reviews, make leaving one easy, and reply personally to each one. Personal replies also give you a chance to use relevant terms naturally (association).

Reply to negative reviews too. The Dublin Digital Agency guide notes that a calm, responsive reply shows prospects you take feedback seriously (secondary). Avoid over-asking. Requesting reviews in every email or class reminder can annoy members, so ask at moments when they are happiest, such as after a milestone class.

## Should you add paid ads to your listing strategy?

Build organic presence first. The Appointy guide advises new studio owners to test free channels before paying for promotion, since paid results often do not last without a plan behind them (vendor). Once your listing is complete, paid search can target the same neighbourhoods. The Dublin Digital Agency guide suggests linking your listing to a Google Ads account for location-based targeting (secondary).

## What is a realistic listing return? A worked example

The inputs below are assumptions for illustration, not benchmarks. Replace them with your own tracked figures.

Assumptions:
- Direction requests per month: 60 (assumption)
- Share of direction requests that book a trial class: 25% (assumption)
- Share of trial clients who join: 1 in 3 (assumption)
- Average monthly membership fee: $120 (assumption)
- Cash cost of the listing: $0, since GMB is a free tool (association)

Calculation:
- 60 direction requests × 25% = 15 trial bookings
- 15 trial bookings × 1/3 = 5 new members
- 5 members × $120 = $600 in new monthly recurring revenue

The cost is time. If a weekly 30-minute routine covers photos, posts and review replies, the listing returns roughly $600 a month in recurring revenue for that time. Run the same calculation with your own numbers each quarter.

## Which listing metrics should you track each month?

Track a short set of numbers in a spreadsheet: direction requests, phone calls, website visits from the listing, and new members who mention finding you on Google. Compare them month on month, and note any changes you made, such as a new post, a holiday closure update or a review drive.

Your monthly review should ask three questions. Did direction requests change after you added photos? Did calls rise after you corrected the phone number? Did the members who joined cite the listing? Small, consistent tracking beats a one-off audit.

## How does the listing connect to your website and social channels?

The listing works best as a hub. Link it to a booking page rather than the homepage, and make the website's address and hours match the listing exactly. The Association of Fitness Studios article also recommends adding local references, such as a partners page or nearby landmarks in your directions, to help search engines understand your location (association). ## FAQ

### Is Google My Business free for fitness studios?

Yes. The Association of Fitness Studios describes the Google Business Profile as a free tool that displays hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos directly in search results (association). Paid ads are optional and sit on top of it.

### How long does verification take?

The sources in this article do not give a timeline, so check the verification prompts Google shows during setup. Once verified, make sure your hours and phone number are accurate before you promote the listing.

### How many reviews should a studio aim for?

The sources do not set a target number. Focus on a steady flow of genuine reviews and reply to each one. The Association of Fitness Studios notes that consistent, organic growth in reviews is more useful than sudden bursts.

### Should I hire someone to manage my listing?

Start yourself, as the Appointy guide suggests, and measure what changes. If your monthly tracking shows the listing is not producing enquiries, a local SEO review can identify the gaps (vendor).
