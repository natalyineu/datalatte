Google My Business, now called Google Business Profile, is the free listing that puts a coffee shop on Google Search and Maps. A [BrewRound guide](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/) (secondary) notes that 46% of all Google searches are for local businesses. To benefit, claim and verify your profile, fill in accurate details, add real photos, post updates such as offers and events, and reply to every review. Google's own [Business Profile page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) lists these same tools as free to use.

## How do you claim and verify a coffee shop listing?

Start by signing in with a Google account and searching for your shop. If the listing already exists, claim it. If not, create one. [Per Diem's guide](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide) (secondary) advises choosing "Coffee shop" as the primary category rather than a broad term like "Restaurant," because the narrower category tends to match more relevant searches.

Verification comes next. Per Diem describes the usual method as a postcard with a code sent to the business address, with some businesses offered phone or email verification instead. BrewRound adds that you can finish filling in your profile while you wait, but it will not appear in results until it is verified. Plan for the postcard delay before a launch or a promotion.

## Which profile details matter most for a coffee shop?

Accuracy comes first. Per Diem recommends that your address, phone number, hours and website match what appears on your other platforms, such as Instagram and your own site. Conflicting details across the web can confuse both Google and customers.

Photos come second. Per Diem suggests going beyond the storefront to show the drinks, the seating area, the barista counter and the general mood of the space. BrewRound says your logo and cover photo are a must, with further shop photos adding context.

Service options are the third area. [Google's Business Profile page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) notes that you can show whether you offer options such as curbside pickup or delivery, and let customers place orders or book through the profile. For a café selling beans or bottled drinks, list those products too.

## Which Google post types should a coffee shop use?

Google lets businesses publish several post types. BrewRound describes the ones most relevant to a café. Use the table below to match each type to a job.

| Post type | Best use for a coffee shop | Notes from source |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | Discounts such as a pastry with any drink on a slow weekday | Add a photo, title, dates, terms and voucher code |
| Event | Coffee tastings, latte art classes or evening events | Add a date range, details and a button linking to booking |
| What's New | Seasonal drinks, new beans, weekend specials | Similar to a Facebook update, and once a week is enough |
| Product | Bags of whole bean or branded items | Add a price, description and link to order |
| COVID-19 update | Only for health-related operating changes | Outdated for most shops now, so avoid using it for drink launches |

Source: [BrewRound](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/) (secondary).

Use a weekly rhythm rather than a monthly one. For a worked budget, assume one post per week and 15 minutes per post including the photo (assumption). That gives 52 posts and 780 minutes, or 13 hours a year. For a shop run by one owner, that is a realistic commitment, and it can be split across the week so no single session is long.

## How often should you reply to reviews?

BrewRound recommends setting aside time once a week to reply to every review, both positive and negative. Google's [Business Profile page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) lists managing and responding to reviews as a core feature, so this is part of the standard profile workflow rather than an extra.

Replies take less time than most owners expect. Assume a shop receives 30 reviews a month and spends 3 minutes writing each reply (assumption). That is 90 minutes a month. Use a short template for positive reviews, then write a specific reply for any complaint, mentioning the fix. Keep the reply short and factual, since readers see the reply beside the review.

## Why do coffee shops need a local SEO plan beyond the profile?

The profile alone does not guarantee a place in local results. It works best when it matches your website, your menu and your social accounts. If your menu lists one set of prices on the site and another on the profile, customers notice and reviews suffer. Treat the profile as one part of a broader local visibility plan that includes consistent listings and steady, honest updates.

## FAQ

### Is Google Business Profile really free for a coffee shop?

Yes. [Google's Business Profile page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) describes the profile as a free listing that you can personalize with photos, manage reviews and use to share what makes your business unique. Paid options such as ads are separate.

### How long does verification take?

The sources do not give a fixed timeframe. Per Diem says verification usually uses a postcard with a code to the business address, while some businesses can verify by phone or email. Your profile will not appear in results until it is verified, so start the process before any planned opening or promotion.

### Which category should a café choose?

Per Diem recommends "Coffee shop" or a closely related term rather than a broad category like "Restaurant," so the profile matches more specific searches. Add secondary categories only where they accurately describe what you sell.

### What should I post first?

Start with an Offer or What's New post that fits a real, specific slot, such as a weekday pastry deal or a new seasonal drink. Keep the photo real and the terms clear. BrewRound recommends one What's New update a week as a sufficient rhythm.
