Small business marketing in Manitoba works best when it starts local: a complete Google Business Profile, a Google Ads campaign aimed at nearby customers, and a clear offer that people in your town can act on. Manitoba's market is concentrated. A 2021 province-wide campaign for the Manitoba Chambers of Commerce targeted about 10,000 local businesses, and it shows how far coordinated local buying messages can reach. This guide covers the steps, one sourced grant, and a budget example you can adapt.

## What Should a Manitoba Business Set Up First?

Start with the free assets. A complete [Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/business/) lists your hours, services, and service area, and gives local searchers a reason to call. Add photos of your space, your team, and your work, then reply to every review. Reviews carry weight in smaller communities, where word of mouth spreads quickly and one poor experience can be repeated across a neighbourhood.

Next, set a service radius that matches how far your customers actually travel. A Winnipeg bakery and a rural plumber need different radii, so check where your last twenty customers came from before you choose one. Brandon, Steinbach, and Dauphin each have their own service areas, and a radius that works in the city may be too small for a regional trade.

Finally, make sure your website states the town you serve, your phone number, and one specific offer. Search ads and profile visits both send people to that page, and a page that names the community converts better than a generic "serving Manitoba" line.

## How Did One Manitoba-Wide Campaign Perform?

The Manitoba Chambers of Commerce campaign ran from late summer 2021 and used the hashtag #BuyLocalMB with the tagline "Buy. Brag. Tag." Ads ran on billboards, TV, newspapers, and online. According to [the agency's case study](https://6pmarketing.com/case-study/mcc-buy-local/), the ads were seen 37 million times, including 5.6 million times on Google Ads and 3.7 million on Facebook.

The campaign also gave local chambers 80 downloadable files to print and use, and it ran a weekly prize of a $100 Visa gift card for eligible posts. Those tactics kept the message in front of residents in their own communities, not just in the provincial media.

The results are reported by the agency that ran the campaign, so treat them as a vendor account rather than an independent audit. The useful lesson is structural: a shared message, a call to action, and a local reason to participate. A single business can copy that structure at smaller scale with a neighbourhood offer and a review request.

## Can a Manitoba Owner Get Help Paying for Marketing?

Yes, but only for specific owners. [Community Futures Westman](https://cfmanitoba.ca/westman-about/westman-image-gallery/small-business-supports-digital-marketing-or-business-mentorship-ibds) states that Indigenous business owners in Manitoba, in rural areas and Winnipeg, can apply for up to $750 in supports. The eligible services include digital marketing, such as web design, social media setup, photography, and video creation, or a business mentor.

Funding is paid directly to the service provider, not to the owner. Providers must be registered businesses and must supply a business number. The page also says applications are reviewed and approved as funding allows, so there is no guarantee of approval or timing.

Owners who do not qualify should check the [Canada-wide overview](/blog/local-business-marketing-canada-guide-2026) for other options and budgeting guidance.

## What Does a Google Ads Budget Look Like?

The example below uses assumptions, not sourced rates. Replace each assumption with your own numbers from Google Ads reports after two to four weeks of data.

| Line item | Assumption | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly Google Ads budget | CA$400 (assumption) | CA$400 |
| Average cost per click | CA$2.00 (assumption) | 200 clicks |
| Click-to-lead rate | 5% (assumption) | 10 leads |
| Cost per lead | CA$400 ÷ 10 leads | CA$40 |
| Lead-to-job rate | 30% (assumption) | 3 jobs |
| Average job value | CA$180 (assumption) | CA$540 revenue |
| Return on ad spend | CA$540 ÷ CA$400 (assumption) | 1.35x |

A 1.35x return is thin, and it shows why tracking matters. If your leads convert at 50% instead of 30%, revenue rises to CA$900 and return to 2.25x. Measure calls and form fills separately, and pause any ad group whose cost per lead stays above your margin for a full month.

## Should You Hire a Marketing Partner?

A partner can make sense if you lack time for campaigns, content, and reporting. One agency, [Code Kraft Solutions](https://codekraftsolutions.com/blog/digital-marketing-small-business-canada), lists digital marketing packages starting at $499 per month. That is a vendor's own price, so compare it with the quote from any provider you consider, and ask what reporting and ad spend are included.

A partner is less useful if you have no clear offer and no tracking. Set up conversion tracking first, so you know which calls and forms come from ads. Then decide whether to run the work yourself or outsource it. Scheduling and posting tools are one option for keeping social updates consistent, as covered in this [comparison of social tools](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business).

Trades businesses often need job management alongside marketing. The [comparison of field-service software](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business) explains how booking and dispatch tools fit with lead generation.

## What Mistakes Hurt Manitoba Local Businesses Most?

The most common mistake is targeting the whole province. A plumber in Winnipeg gains little from clicks in Thompson, so set your geography to the towns you actually serve and review the search terms report each week.

The second mistake is ignoring reviews. A negative review left unanswered can stay visible for months. Reply within a day, state what you did to fix the problem, and invite the customer to contact you directly.

The third mistake is collecting email addresses without clear permission. Ask customers to opt in at the counter or during booking, and record when and how they agreed. Keep the first message short, with one offer and a way to unsubscribe.

## How Do You Run a 30-Day Plan?

Week one: complete your Google Business Profile, add photos, and answer every open review.

Week two: launch a small Google Ads campaign with a single service and a local radius, then add call and location extensions.

Week three: set up conversion tracking for calls and form submissions, and test both yourself.

Week four: review the first reports, pause weak ad groups, and add one retargeting or awareness campaign if your budget allows.

Repeat the cycle monthly, and change one variable at a time so you can see what caused a result.

## FAQ

### How much should a Manitoba small business spend on Google Ads?

There is no sourced benchmark for Manitoba, so start with an amount you can sustain for at least two months. Use the worked example above to test whether your cost per lead fits your margin before you increase spend.

### Can I get Community Futures funding for marketing?

Only if you are an Indigenous business owner in Manitoba who meets the program rules. The page states support is up to $750, paid directly to an eligible provider, and approved as funding allows.

### Is a Google Business Profile enough for local marketing?

It is a strong starting point, but not a full strategy. Pair it with a page on your website that names the communities you serve, a review process, and at least one tracked channel such as call tracking or a form.
