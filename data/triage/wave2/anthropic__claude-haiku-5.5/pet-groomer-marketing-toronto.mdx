Marketing a pet grooming business in Toronto works best neighborhood by neighborhood. Build a Google Business Profile that names the areas you serve, add a Nextdoor page that neighbors can recommend, and time offers to Toronto's winters and spring shedding. Your competition is partly the bathtub: according to figures cited by the [National Library of Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8907591/), 41% of dog owners groom at home, 30% use full-service salons, and 9% choose mobile groomers. Your goal is to win a larger share of the salon and mobile groups within your own postal area.

## Is there long-term demand for pet grooming work?

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects that employment of animal care and service workers will grow 11% from 2025 to 2035, which it describes as much faster than the average for all occupations. Its page lists grooming among the core duties, alongside bathing, nail trimming, and clipping. That projection covers the US, not Ontario specifically, so treat it as directional for a Toronto business rather than a local forecast.

For a single shop, the practical takeaway is that demand is steady enough to justify a permanent marketing habit, not a one-time launch. Groomers who keep their listings current and their reviews coming in are better placed to capture that growth than those who market only when the diary is empty.

## How should a Toronto groomer set up local SEO across neighborhoods?

Start with the Google Business Profile. Hibu's grooming guide recommends completing every field, including address, phone, email, and website, then adding photos of the space, before-and-after results, and the groomer themselves. Keep hours current around holidays and respond to every review, positive or negative. These basics are the foundation for any neighborhood strategy, because Google decides which searches your profile can answer.

Name the specific areas you serve in your description and website copy. A groomer near Leslieville is not competing for the same searches as one near Yorkville, so a blanket "Toronto" claim is weaker than the streets, parks, and transit stops your clients actually use. If you are weighing a broader strategy, the [Canada local marketing guide](/blog/local-business-marketing-canada-guide-2026) covers how these signals fit together.

## Where does Nextdoor fit before paid search?

Nextdoor is useful for Toronto groomers because neighbors recommend businesses to each other in public threads. According to [a Nextdoor pet-business guide from Franpos](https://franpos.com/nextdoor-pet-business-marketing) (secondary), the Business Page and recommendations are free, and a neighbor asking "who's a good groomer?" will see your recommendations. Claim your page, choose accurate categories such as pet grooming or mobile grooming, and list the neighborhoods a mobile route covers.

Post helpful content once or twice a week rather than ads. Seasonal safety tips, offers to share lost-pet posts, and genuine availability updates perform better than promotions. Link your online booking from the page so a neighbor can go from recommendation to appointment in one tap.

## How do reviews and referrals build trust?

Reviews and referrals work together. Hibu suggests collecting reviews through QR codes in the waiting room, follow-up emails, or text messages after an appointment, then responding to each review. A referral program can reward regular clients with discounts or small gifts for new clients they bring in. Hibu cites one retelling of referral research claiming referred customers spend 200% more than average, a figure from a secondary source that should be checked before you quote it.

For Toronto, the most useful habit is asking at the right moment: when a client collects a freshly groomed dog, or when a daycare parent mentions a calmer pet at home. A steady trickle of reviews matters more than a single campaign, because neighbors read recent feedback when they compare groomers.

## When should winter and spring offers run?

Toronto's winters are a natural hook for paw care. Road salt and slush are hard on paw pads, so a winter paw-care offer combining balm application with extra washing gives clients a clear reason to book. Schedule the campaign for the coldest months and promote it in the neighborhood areas where dogs walk the most.

Spring is the opposite problem: double-coated breeds shed heavily and clients may wait too long. Uprinting's seasonal guide suggests promoting deshedding services in spring with flyers and posters. Send that offer before the peak, using email and social posts in late winter so the appointments spread out instead of crowding into one week. Printed door hangers are a practical way to reach nearby streets; the [direct mail guide for local businesses](/blog/direct-mail-marketing-local) explains how to target them.

## FAQ

### How much should a pet groomer in Toronto spend on marketing?

The worked example above uses 750 CAD a month, but that is an assumption, not a benchmark. Start with a budget you can track by channel, then adjust based on cost per new client and repeat visits. Many groomers begin with free channels and add paid search only after their profile and reviews are in order.

### Should I target all of Toronto or only my neighborhood?

Target your immediate neighborhood and two or three adjacent ones. Clients tend to book groomers they can reach easily, so concentrated campaigns usually convert better than broad city-wide ones. Name those areas in your profile and on your website so both search and local ads match where clients live.

### Is Nextdoor still worth using for a grooming business?

Nextdoor is worth testing because the basic Business Page and recommendations are free, according to Franpos's guide (secondary). Its value depends on consistent, helpful posts and reviews from real neighbors. Track bookings from the platform with a dedicated code before deciding whether to spend on its paid options.

### Does winter really change grooming demand?

Winter changes the services clients ask for, especially paw care and wash frequency during salt season. Groomers who promote winter paw care proactively, rather than waiting for requests, can build a reputation as specialists. Measure the difference by tracking bookings for that service month by month.
