The best Google Ads keywords for a day spa are service-led, local searches such as "massage near me", "facial [city]" and "couples massage". Group them by intent, run them on phrase or exact match to start, and add negatives that block hot tub, job and do-it-yourself searches. No source reviewed gives a spa-only cost per click, so the budget example below uses labelled assumptions. Check your own estimates in Keyword Planner before committing a budget.

## What Do US Labour Figures Say About Spa and Salon Demand?

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics groups barbers, hairstylists and cosmetologists together. Its figures cover people who work in barbershops and salons, not spas specifically. Even so, they show a large, growing service sector. Employment in the group is projected to grow 7 percent from 2025 to 2035, much faster than the average for all occupations.

Those workers are also the people a spa hires, so the numbers hint at competition for staff. They do not show how many clients search for a spa or what a click costs. Treat them as market context only.

## Which Keyword Groups Should a Spa Start With?

Group keywords by what the searcher wants, because each group needs its own ad text and landing page. A search for "massage near me" is a booking query, while a search for "hydrafacial" may be a research query that needs a treatment page.

| Group | Example keywords | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core services | "massage near me", "facial near me", "day spa [city]" | Strongest booking intent |
| Specific treatments | "deep tissue massage", "hydrafacial", "body wrap" | Send each to a matching page |
| Occasions | "couples massage", "bridal spa day" | Higher ticket, planned in advance |
| Gifts | "spa gift card", "spa gift certificate" | Seasonal peaks around holidays |
| Brand | [your spa name] | Protects your name in results |

Secondary sources in the Sources list make the same point: a searcher who types a service plus a place is closer to booking than one who types a general term. Only list treatments the spa actually offers, and avoid broad single words like "spa" on their own.

## Which Match Types Should a Spa Use?

Start with phrase and exact match on the core and treatment groups. Exact match gives the most control, and phrase match catches close variations, such as "deep tissue massage near me" when the keyword is "deep tissue massage". Both reduce the chance of paying for unrelated searches. The [nail salon keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-nail-salons-in-2026) shows the same match-type approach for another appointment-based service.

Broad match can come later, once there is conversion data. Max Conversion recommends exact and phrase match for high-intent terms (secondary), and the same cautious sequence is used here as a recommendation, not a Google rule.

## Which Negative Keywords Protect a Spa Budget?

Negative keywords stop an ad from showing for searches that are not wanted. Two secondary sources list overlapping core negatives for service businesses, including "free", "DIY", "how to", "jobs", "salary", "school" and "at home" ([Max Conversion](https://www.max-conversion.com/best-keywords-to-use-for-med-spa-google-ads-campaign), [Scaling Wellness](https://scalingwellness.com/best-keywords-to-target-with-google-ads-for-massage/)).

Spa-specific negatives depend on the search terms report. The word "spa" is also used for hot tubs and pool equipment, so add terms like "hot tub", "parts" and "cover" if they appear. Check the report weekly during the first month, because new unwanted phrases tend to appear early.

- Jobs and training: "jobs", "hiring", "salary", "school", "course"
- Do-it-yourself: "diy", "at home", "how to"
- Equipment: "hot tub", "parts", "cover", "filter"
- Price shoppers, only if the spa does not discount: "free", "cheap"

## How Much Could an $800 Monthly Budget Return?

Google's Keyword Planner gives suggested bid estimates for each keyword and a forecast of clicks, conversions or impressions based on spend. Its example shows a $1.72 suggested bid for "clothing store", which is a retail example and not a spa benchmark. Use Keyword Planner for the spa's own city and treatments.

| Input or result | Value | Basis |

| Leads | about 13 | 160 × 8% |

On these inputs the first visit does not cover the ad spend. Profit depends on repeat visits, packages and gift cards. If the average first booking is $160 instead, first-visit revenue rises to $960, which covers the spend. Run the same table with real prices before setting a budget.

## What Should a Spa Check in the First 30 Days?

1. Confirm conversion tracking for bookings, calls and form submissions before spending the full budget.
2. Build three to five ad groups from the core and treatment keywords, each sent to a matching page.
3. Add the negatives above at launch, then review the search terms report every week.
4. Compare cost per booking, not only cost per click, for each keyword group.
5. Pause any group with no bookings after a fixed spend limit set in advance.
6. Test one gift or occasion group separately so its results are not mixed with everyday services.

Local salon and fitness guides use similar group structures: see the [barbershop keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-barbershops-in-2026) and the [personal trainer keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-personal-trainers-in-2026).

## FAQ

### Should a spa bid on the word "spa" alone?

Usually not. It is broad and attracts hot tub, equipment and job searches. Pair it with a service or a place, such as "day spa" or "massage [city]", and add negatives.

### What is a good cost per click for spa keywords?

No spa-specific figure appears in the sources reviewed. Use Keyword Planner's estimates for the spa's own city and treatments, then test with a small budget.

### How many keyword groups should a small spa start with?

Three to five is enough for a first campaign. Start with core services, one or two treatment groups and brand, then add occasion and gift groups once there is booking data.

### Do gift card keywords work?

They can peak before holidays, but no source here reports their performance. Track gift searches as a separate group and compare cost per sale.
