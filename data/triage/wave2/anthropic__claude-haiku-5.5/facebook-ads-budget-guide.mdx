## How much should a small business spend on Facebook ads each month?

Most businesses spend $1 to $500 a month on Facebook ads, according to a survey of 250+ US marketing professionals reported by [WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/social-media/pricing/how-much-does-facebook-advertising-cost/) (secondary). The same survey found that most businesses allocate 0 to 15% of their total advertising budget to Facebook, with the exact share depending on goals and return.

A useful starting point is a budget you can afford to lose for six to eight weeks while you test. The UK agency [Bons & Frazer](https://bonsandfrazer.com/insights/facebook-ads-on-a-budget/) (secondary) suggests £12 to £25 a day for UK lead generation, which is roughly £360 to £750 a month. Below about £10 a day, enquiries are so few that you cannot tell a good ad from a lucky one.

## What does a Facebook ad cost per click and per lead?

WebFX reports that Facebook ads generally cost $0.30 to $4.00 per click (CPC) and $3 to $20 per 1,000 impressions (CPM). The average CPC it cites is around $0.80. The typical cost per lead (CPL) ranges from $8 to $25, depending on industry and season. Cost per action (CPA) ranges from $8 to $55 (all [WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/social-media/pricing/how-much-does-facebook-advertising-cost/), secondary).

Cost per lead matters more than cost per click for most local businesses. A cheap click that never books is not a bargain. Track the number that ends in a paying customer, and set your budget from there.

## What is the minimum daily budget that actually works?

Bons & Frazer summarises Meta's own floors as about $1 a day per ad set when charged per impression, about $5 a day when charged per click or video view, and around $40 a day for rare events (secondary, citing Meta's help centre). Those figures are the least Meta will accept, not the least that produces results.

For lead generation, the same agency suggests a working floor of £10 to £12 a day, and a rule of thumb of spending at least half your expected cost per lead each day. At a £20 cost per lead, that means about £10 a day. Each ad set has its own floor, so splitting a small budget across several ad sets leaves each one too little to learn from.

Meta typically needs around 50 optimisation events a week before a campaign exits its learning phase. Bons & Frazer notes that at £20 a lead this would require roughly £140 a day, which almost no small business spends. Keep the structure simple: one campaign, one ad set, and leave it alone for at least a week.

## Worked example: what budget does a local service business need?

The inputs below are assumptions for an illustrative hair salon. Replace them with your own figures.

Assumption inputs:
- Cost per lead: $15 (assumption, within the $8 to $25 range WebFX reports)
- Lead-to-paying-customer rate: 25% (assumption)
- Target: 10 new customers a month (assumption)
- First-year revenue per customer: $300 (assumption)

Calculation:
1. Cost per customer = $15 ÷ 0.25 = $60
2. Monthly budget for 10 customers = 10 × $60 = $600
3. Daily budget = $600 ÷ 30 = $20

Revenue ROI = ($3,000 − $600) ÷ $600 × 100 = 400%. This uses revenue, not profit, so subtract your margin before deciding. The $20 a day sits inside the working range Bons & Frazer gives for lead generation, which is a useful sanity check.

## When should you increase or pause your Facebook ad budget?

Increase spend only after a campaign has held its cost per lead for a full week. Increase gradually, by 20 to 30% at a time, rather than jumping from $20 to $100 a day, which resets the learning process. Hold each new level for several days before the next step.

Pause or reduce if cost per result stays above your break-even for several days in a row. Also watch frequency: if the same people see your ad repeatedly and click-through rate falls, the audience is saturated. Refresh the creative or narrow the audience before adding money.

Track results beyond the first click. Offline conversions, promo codes and repeat visits show whether ad-driven customers are worth the spend. ## How do you set your first budget step by step?

1. Write your target number of new customers per month.
2. Estimate your cost per lead from a two-week test, or start with a benchmark from WebFX (secondary) and replace it as soon as you have data.
3. Divide your target customers by your close rate to get leads needed.
4. Multiply leads by cost per lead to get the monthly budget.
5. Divide by 30 for the daily figure, and check it against Meta's floor for your currency.
6. Run one campaign for at least seven days before changing anything.

## FAQ

### How much should a small business budget for Facebook ads?

Start with a budget you can test for six to eight weeks. Bons & Frazer suggests £12 to £25 a day for UK lead generation, and WebFX reports most US businesses spend $1 to $500 a month. Build your number from your cost per lead and customer target rather than copying a benchmark.

### Can I run Facebook ads on a tight budget?

Yes, but a small budget must be run differently. Use one campaign and one ad set so the money is not split too thinly. Bons & Frazer notes that at £5 a day you are testing an offer rather than generating a steady flow of leads.

### What is the minimum daily budget for Facebook ads?

Meta's own floor is about $1 a day per ad set for impression-based billing and about $5 a day for click-based billing, according to Bons & Frazer (secondary). That is the least Meta accepts, not the level that produces enquiries. A working floor for lead generation is closer to £10 to £12 a day.

### How long should I run Facebook ads before judging results?

Give a campaign at least seven days to gather data, and three to four weeks before judging cost per lead in lead generation. Bons & Frazer suggests waiting that long at smaller budgets so one lucky week does not mislead you.

### Does cost per click or cost per lead matter more?

For most local businesses, cost per lead matters more. WebFX reports CPC averages around $0.80, but a cheap click that never becomes a customer does not help your budget. Measure the number that leads to a paying customer.
