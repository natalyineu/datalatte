## What Actually Decides Your Local Ranking?

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity. Relevance means how well a Business Profile matches what someone searches for. Distance is how far each business is from the searcher, and Google uses its own location data when the searcher does not share one. Prominence is how well known a business is. Google links it partly to how many websites link to the business and how many reviews it has, along with the ratings.

None of these factors can be bought. A tool that promises a rank boost is selling something Google says it does not sell. Buyers should treat any such promise as a warning sign and ask what the tool actually measures.

## How Can a Tool Help Without Changing the Rules?

Tools help with the inputs a business controls. A profile can be made more complete and specific, hours and addresses can be kept accurate, and reviews can be answered. Google's guidance says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local results, and that an inaccurate profile may not show for relevant searches. A tool that flags mismatched details or missed updates saves a manual check of every field.

Tools can also show how results appear from different spots around a shop. Because distance matters, a view from several locations around the premises can be more useful than a single city-wide ranking. These views measure current position. They do not prove that a particular change will raise rank.

## Which Features Are Worth Paying For?

Start with features that match Google's ranking factors. Position tracking by location fits relevance and distance. Listing consistency checks fit the accuracy Google asks for. Review monitoring and reply tools fit prominence, since Google ties reviews and ratings to how well known a business is.

Skip anything that will not be opened at least monthly. Many tools add reports, AI summaries or content generators that do not change profile data. Before paying for those, ask whether the output would lead to action. If not, the feature is a cost rather than a system. ## What Is a Free Setup That Works?

A free setup for one location needs only a few routine tasks. Google's help recommends completing and verifying the Business Profile, then keeping each field current. The steps below follow that guidance.

1. Verify the business and confirm the name matches signage and branding, as Google asks.
2. Choose the most specific primary category, then add up to 9 more categories that describe what the business does.
3. Enter the service area by the cities or zip codes served, as precisely as possible.
4. Add up to 2 more phone numbers if they are used, and keep hours current, including holiday hours.
5. Reply to reviews, positive and negative, every week.
6. Once a month, search the main terms from the shop and from nearby streets, and note the position.

This routine covers most of what a single-location tool would track. For how it fits into a wider plan, read the guide on [generative engine optimization for local business](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-local-business), which explains local visibility beyond the standard results page.

## When Should You Upgrade to a Paid Tool?

Upgrade when the manual routine takes more time than a tool would. The usual triggers are several locations, a need for shared reports, or a team that must see the same data. Multiple locations multiply the checks, and a single dashboard can save far more time than it does for one shop.

If a general automation platform is already in use, check whether it covers listings or reviews before adding a separate local tool. ## FAQ

### Do I need AI tools for local SEO?

No. Google's Business Profile guidance focuses on complete, accurate information, reviews and photos. A free routine handles these, and a paid tool helps only if it saves time.

### Can a tool improve my local ranking by itself?

No. Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. A tool can show position and help keep the profile accurate, but the improvements come from the business's own actions.

### How many categories can a Business Profile have?

One primary category, plus up to 9 more. Google asks businesses to pick a specific primary category that represents the main business, and to avoid using categories as keywords.

### Is a free setup enough for one location?

Often, yes. A monthly check of main searches, a complete profile and regular review replies cover most of what a single location needs. Upgrade when the manual work takes longer than a tool would.
