Google's help pages say local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and prominence. You influence relevance and prominence directly, through complete profile details, verification and reviews. You cannot control how far a searcher is from you. ## What does local SEO for small businesses depend on?

Google describes local results as based mainly on relevance, distance, and popularity. Relevance is how well your profile matches what someone is searching for, so complete and detailed business info matters. Distance depends on where the searcher is, and if a customer does not share their location, Google uses what it knows about it. You cannot change either of those two factors.

Prominence is the popularity factor. Google says it reflects how well known a business is, including how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have. More reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. Your effort is therefore concentrated on two things: accurate profile information and a steady flow of genuine reviews.

## How do you verify and complete your Business Profile?

Start by making sure your profile is claimed and verified. Google says you must add or claim your business and then verify your Business Profile before it shows on Search, Maps, and other Google services. Verification also tells Google you are authorized to represent the business, which makes it more likely to appear in results. Some businesses must verify before they can edit their info at all.

Once verified, fill in every field that applies. Google recommends a full address for businesses customers can visit, accurate hours including special holiday hours, and a clear category. You can add up to 9 more categories beyond your primary one, but the primary category sets the main identity of the listing. Do not use categories as keywords or to describe features of your business.

## Which profile fields should you check first?

Use this checklist to audit your listing in the order that affects matching and trust. Each row reflects what Google's help pages say about that field, and the final column is the step to take this week.

| Profile field | What Google's help pages say | Your action this week |
|---|---|---|
| Business name | Match the name on your real signage and stationery | Remove keywords or taglines from the name field |
| Primary category | Choose a specific category for your main business | Confirm it describes the core service |
| Service area | Enter cities or zip codes you serve, as accurately as possible | List only places you actually visit or deliver to |
| Hours | Set special hours for holidays and temporary changes | Add next month's holiday hours now |
| Phone numbers | Up to 2 extra numbers beyond the primary one, not fax | Confirm each number reaches a person or working voicemail |

Google may also send automated calls, texts, or WhatsApp messages to the verified phone number to confirm business info. Any answers you give can update your profile, so keep that number monitored. Check the Collected info section regularly and delete anything that is wrong.

## How should you handle reviews and photos?

Google's guidance is to reply to customer reviews, because positive reviews and helpful replies help a business stand out. Treat each reply as a short, factual answer that thanks the customer or addresses the issue. Service businesses that schedule jobs and collect feedback in one system often find reviews easier to request on time, which is one reason to compare tools such as [Jobber vs ServiceTitan for small service businesses](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business).

Google also says photos and videos help you show what you offer and tell your story. Add real photos of your work, team, and location rather than stock images. In-store product listings are only available to retail businesses in eligible countries or regions, so check whether that feature applies before spending time on it.

## Can you pay to rank higher on Google?

No. Google's help page states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Any offer to guarantee a top spot in Maps for a fee should be treated with caution. Google also keeps its search algorithm details confidential so that ranking stays fair for everyone.

Your practical focus is the same for every business: keep information accurate, verified, and complete, and respond to customers. ## FAQ

### Do I need to verify my Google Business Profile before local SEO works?

Google says you must add or claim your business and verify your Business Profile before it shows on Search, Maps, and other Google services. Verification also helps your listing appear in results, so treat it as the first step.

### Can I set a service area instead of a storefront address?

Yes. Google says that if your business serves customers within a specific local area, you can enter your service area by cities, zip codes, or other places you serve. Google asks you to be as specific and accurate as possible.

### How many categories can I add to my profile?

You can select a primary category and up to 9 more categories. Google advises choosing a specific category that represents your main business, and not using categories as keywords or to describe features.

### Does a bigger review count help local ranking?

Google says prominence is based partly on how many reviews you have, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. Reply to reviews, as Google recommends, rather than focusing only on the count.

That depends on your time budget. The worked example above suggests around 6 hours per month for a single-location service business, using assumed inputs.
