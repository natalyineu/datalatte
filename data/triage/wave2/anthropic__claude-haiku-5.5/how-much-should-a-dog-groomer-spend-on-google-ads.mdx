A dog groomer should set a Google Ads budget by working backward from cost per booking. No source in this review publishes a dog-groomer spend benchmark, so this article does not give one. What the sources do publish is agency pricing: [Perform Pets lists ongoing marketing from $2,000 a month for one location](https://performpets.com/how-much-does-grooming-salon-marketing-cost/), a paid-ads program requires at least $600 a month in ad spend, and [mobile grooming booking software runs $49 to $159 a month](https://performpets.com/how-much-does-grooming-salon-marketing-cost/). Use those as cost inputs, then test them against your own bookings.

## What does a dog groomer Google Ads budget have to cover?

Ad spend and management are separate costs. The same Perform Pets pricing review says ad spend is paid to Google or Meta on top of any agency fee, and that no published agency fee includes it. A quote of $2,000 a month is therefore not the total cost of running ads.

Four lines make up the real budget: ad spend, any management fee, booking software, and your own time. Your time has a floor value. The [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a 2025 median pay of $17.08 an hour](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/animal-care-and-service-workers.htm) for animal care and service workers. If you assume two hours a week on search term reviews and bid changes (assumption), that is about 8.7 hours a month, or roughly $148 of labour at the median rate.

Software deserves a check too. Many grooming shops already pay for booking software. Confirm whether your current plan includes reminders and messaging before buying another tool.

## How do you work out a worked budget example?

Start with what one booked client is worth on the first visit. The inputs below are assumptions for a worked example, not published figures. Replace them with your own numbers.

- Average grooming ticket: $60 (assumption)
- Product and labour cost per visit: $15 (assumption)
- Average cost per click: $2.50 (assumption)
- Click-to-booking rate: 10% (assumption)

First-visit profit per booking is $60 minus $15, which is $45. That is your break-even ceiling: any cost per booking below $45 pays back on the first visit. Repeat visits are not counted here, which keeps the test conservative.

| Monthly ad spend (assumption) | Clicks | Bookings | Cost per booking | First-visit profit minus ads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $300 | 120 | 12 | $25 | $240 |
| $600 | 240 | 24 | $25 | $480 |
| $1,000 | 400 | 40 | $25 | $800 |

The table holds cost per click flat, which is optimistic. Bids usually rise as you buy more clicks, so the $1,000 row is the least reliable. Track real cost per booking each month and compare it to the $45 ceiling.

## How do Quality Score and ad relevance affect what you pay?

Google's [Quality Score diagnostic](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118) runs from 1 to 10 at the keyword level. It combines three components: expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing page experience. Each is rated Above average, Average, or Below average against other advertisers whose ads showed for the same search over the last 90 days.

Google states that Quality Score is a diagnostic tool, not an input in the ad auction, and should not be optimised as a key performance indicator. Use it to find weak spots. If landing page experience is rated Average or Below average, send "dog groomer near me" clicks to a page that shows the same service and a booking form, not your homepage.

## What steps should you take before you spend?

1. Write down first-visit profit for one booked client.
2. Set a maximum cost per booking at or below that figure. In the example, that is $45.
3. Choose a test budget. If you hire a paid-ads program, check its stated minimum first, such as the $600 that one agency lists.
4. Count bookings, not clicks. Record phone calls and form submissions as separate sources.
5. Review after one full month of data. Compare cost per booking to your ceiling.
6. Keep spending only while cost per booking stays under the ceiling. Cut keywords and ad groups that do not.

Service businesses with a similar appointment model can apply the same test. ## Should you hire an agency or run the account yourself?

Published agency pricing varies widely. Perform Pets lists ongoing marketing from $2,000 a month for one location, TailWerks lists $997 to $3,247 across three tiers, and Pet Engine Marketing lists generally from $2,500 a month. Each of these excludes ad spend.

Set those fees against your ad budget. A $2,000 monthly fee on top of a $600 ad budget means the agency cost is more than three times the media spend. That can still be worth it if the account generates enough bookings, but only your cost-per-booking numbers can show whether it does. If you run the account yourself, the time estimate above and the checklist are the minimum you need.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is $600 a month enough for a dog groomer's Google Ads?

It is enough to test only if cost per booking stays under your first-visit profit. In the worked example, $600 at an assumed $2.50 cost per click produces 24 bookings. Run the same check with your own prices and click costs before deciding.

### Do I need Facebook ads as well as Google?

The vendor case used both channels, and it did not separate results by channel. Start with the channel that captures people already searching, then read the [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) before adding a second platform.

### Does a higher Quality Score lower my cost per click?

Google says Quality Score is not an input in the ad auction. Treat it as a diagnostic that points to weak ad text, weak landing pages, or low expected click-through rate. Fix those three components rather than chasing the number itself.

### How long before I judge results?

The sources do not set a time frame. Use at least one full month of booking data, then compare cost per booking to your ceiling.
