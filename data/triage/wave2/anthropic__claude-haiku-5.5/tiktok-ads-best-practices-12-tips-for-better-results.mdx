The most effective TikTok ads for local businesses are short, vertical, and built to look like native TikTok content rather than polished commercials. Sources recommend ads under 20 seconds, vertical 9:16 framing, and a fresh creative every week or so, because ads fatigue quickly on the platform. Before spending, set up a conversion event, test several creatives against the same audience, and judge results by cost per booking or sale rather than views.

## Should your TikTok ad look like an ad?

Usually not. Evoluted advises making ads "look organic" with native elements such as text overlays, text-to-speech, and visual effects so the content blends into the feed. The same agency notes that TikTok relies heavily on video that feels authentic, and that a polished Facebook or Instagram asset often underperforms when moved to TikTok unchanged ([Evoluted](https://www.evoluted.net/blog/marketing/our-top-tips-for-making-tiktok-ads-work-for-your-brand), secondary).

For a local business, this usually means filming on a phone in the real workspace. A salon can show a cut in progress with an on-screen price. A coffee shop can show a barista working a busy counter. Many sources treat the opening seconds as the decisive moment, so the hook should appear immediately.

A cross-platform comparison of creative and audience behaviour is covered in the guide to [TikTok ads versus Facebook ads for local business](/blog/tiktok-ads-vs-facebook-ads-which-is-better-for-local-business).

## How long should a TikTok ad run?

AdRoll recommends developing ads shorter than 20 seconds, because the app is built around a steady stream of short, punchy content ([AdRoll](https://www.adroll.com/blog/tiktok-ads-what-to-know-plus-tips-and-tricks), secondary). Shorter runtime also forces a single clear message, which suits a local offer such as an introductory class price or a booking discount.

Length is not the only variable. Evoluted recommends shooting vertically so the content is not masked, and keeping captions and overlays inside the platform's safe zones, away from the profile picture and the like, comment, and share buttons ([Evoluted](https://www.evoluted.net/blog/marketing/our-top-tips-for-making-tiktok-ads-work-for-your-brand), secondary).

Audio also needs checking. Evoluted advises using non-copyright audio to avoid ad disapprovals, and adding captions when the audio is speech.

## Who should you target on TikTok?

AdRoll suggests casting a wide audience net and avoiding age targeting, since the app skews toward younger users ([AdRoll](https://www.adroll.com/blog/tiktok-ads-what-to-know-plus-tips-and-tricks), secondary). Narrow targeting limits how many people see the ad, which makes it harder to learn which creative works.

Start with a broad location radius around your service area, then let the results guide any narrowing. Some local businesses find their customers skew older, so check the actual audience breakdown in the ad platform before assuming the app's reputation applies to your customers. Insense notes that results depend on how closely your target audience overlaps with the platform's user demographics ([Insense](https://insense.pro/blog/tiktok-ad-strategy), secondary).

## How often should you refresh TikTok creative?

Test several ideas at once. Evoluted notes that ads can fatigue quickly on the platform and suggests a new video about once a week if capacity allows ([Evoluted](https://www.evoluted.net/blog/marketing/our-top-tips-for-making-tiktok-ads-work-for-your-brand), secondary). AdRoll's advice is similar in spirit: launch, observe which idea resonates, then double down on the winner rather than spending hours polishing one ad.

A practical rhythm for a small budget is to run two or three creatives with the same audience and budget, review them after about a week, pause the weakest, and replace it with a new variation. Keep a simple log of hook, offer, and result so each test teaches you something.

Creative production can be slow for a single-owner business. Tools for drafting scripts and captions can help, as covered in [how AI tools may replace some marketing assistant work](/blog/ai-tools-replace-marketing-assistant-local-business).

## How much should a first TikTok test cost?

The sources in this review do not give a universal starting budget, so the example below uses assumed inputs. Replace them with your own numbers.

**Worked example (all inputs are assumptions):**

- Daily budget: $15 (assumption)
- Test length: 14 days (assumption)
- Creatives tested: 2 (assumption)
- Total spend: $15 × 14 = $210
- Bookings from TikTok traffic: 5 (assumption)
- Average booking value: $40 (assumption)
- Revenue: 5 × $40 = $200
- Cost per booking: $210 ÷ 5 = $42

In this scenario the test does not pay back on its own. Cost per booking ($42) sits slightly above the assumed booking value ($40), which signals the offer, creative, or landing page needs work before scaling. A test like this is judged by whether it teaches you which creative and offer produce bookings at an acceptable cost, not by the immediate profit.

## Is TikTok still worth it for US local businesses?

LeadsBridge reported 170 million monthly active users in the United States and described the platform as a major advertising channel, while also noting that the US ban had been under legislative and court review ([LeadsBridge](https://leadsbridge.com/blog/tiktok-ads-optimization/), secondary). The relevant law is the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act, which the [congressional bill record](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/7521) documents. Because that status changed repeatedly, check the current position before committing a large budget.

Comparison with search advertising matters here. Paid search captures people who already want a service, while TikTok builds awareness and can drive impulse bookings. Many local businesses use both, with search covering intent and TikTok covering new reach. Our [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads) page explains how that split works in practice.

## FAQ

### Is TikTok worth testing for a small local business?

It can be, if your customers overlap with the platform's audience. Start with a small test and a clear conversion event, then judge the result on cost per booking or sale. The worked example above shows how to compute that.

### How long should my first TikTok ad be?

Sources recommend keeping ads shorter than 20 seconds. The first two or three seconds need a clear hook, and the call to action should appear early.

### Should I age-target my TikTok ads?

AdRoll suggests avoiding age targeting and casting a wide net. Check the audience breakdown in your ad account and let the results guide any narrowing.

### Can I reuse my Facebook or Instagram video on TikTok?

Evoluted's advice is to avoid it, because content that looks like a polished feed ad tends to get scrolled past. Refilm a version that looks native to TikTok.
