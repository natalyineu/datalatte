A customer reactivation campaign works by messaging people who stopped buying, but before you write anything, you need to know whether you are allowed to contact them. Canada's anti-spam law says a past purchase creates an "existing business relationship" only if it happened "within the two-year period immediately before the day on which the message was sent." The customers you most want back are often the ones whose permission has quietly expired. Sort your lapsed list by permission first, then run a short three-touch sequence and measure booked revenue.

This guide is for owners of cafes, salons, studios, groomers and similar businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It assumes you already know the basics of subject lines and offers. It covers how to sort customers by what you can legally send, how to structure the sequence, and how to work out whether it pays. This is general information, not legal advice, and rules change, so check with an adviser before a large send.

## Why Win Back Lapsed Customers at All?

Harvard Business Review's Amy Gallo wrote that "acquiring a new customer is anywhere from five to 25 times more expensive than retaining an existing one," and noted that the range depends on "which study you believe" and the industry. The same article attributes to Bain's Frederick Reichheld the claim that "increasing customer retention rates by 5% increases profits by 25% to 95%." These are broad figures, not local-business benchmarks, and they concern retention rather than winning back people who already left.

Use them as a reason to test, then replace them with your own numbers. The cost per reactivated customer should come from your records, as the worked example below shows. ## How Do You Define Lapsed From Your Own Data?

Export customers from your POS, booking system or CRM. Take the usual gap between visits for your active customers and multiply it by about 1.5. That multiplier is an assumption, not a published standard. A client who books every six weeks looks lapsed at about nine weeks, while a coffee shop's window is far shorter than a dentist's.

Then add a second data point for each person: the date of their last purchase or enquiry, and how they gave you their contact details. You need both for the permission step, so do not skip the second column.

## Who Are You Allowed to Contact?

The rules differ by country and by channel. The table summarises the official pages listed in Sources, so open them for the exact wording before you send.

| Where | Email or text rule | Opt-out deadline | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US email (CAN-SPAM) | Covers all commercial email, including a message to former customers. Requires accurate header, honest subject line, ad disclosure, valid postal address and an opt-out | Honour within 10 business days | [FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) |
| US texts (TCPA) | Separate consent rules apply to marketing texts | Not verified for this article | Ask an adviser |
| UK (PECR) | Prior consent, unless the soft opt-in applies to similar products from a sale | Offer an opt-out in every message | [PECR 2003, regulation 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22) |
| Canada (CASL) | Express consent, or implied consent from a purchase within 2 years or an enquiry within 6 months | Take effect no later than 10 business days | [CASL ss.10, 11](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html) |
| Australia (Spam Act) | Express or inferred consent, such as giving your address where marketing is reasonably expected | No later than 5 working days | [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/marketing-and-advertising/promoting-your-business-by-email-or-text-messages) |

A few points from those pages are worth spelling out. US email has no exemption for ex-customers, so a "we miss you, here is 10% off" message is commercial email. It needs the full set of disclosures and a working opt-out. In the UK, the soft opt-in has three conditions: you collected the details in a sale or negotiations, the marketing is for your similar products only, and the person was offered a simple way to refuse. Bought-in lists do not qualify. Canada's clock is explicit in s.10(10), and the regulator's current guidance should be read too.

## How Do You Sort Lapsed Customers Into Permission Buckets?

Once you know the rules for your country, put every lapsed contact into one of three buckets. Green means the person opted in clearly, bought recently and is not on your suppression list, so you can run the full sequence. Amber means an old purchase, an unclear source, or details collected without an opt-out. For amber contacts, do not send marketing yet. Use a non-marketing channel such as an in-person conversation, or take advice on a permission-refresh approach. Red means unsubscribed, bounced, complained, or bought-in. Never email or text these people, and keep them on a suppression list.

The last point matters more than people expect. If you move to a new email tool, import your suppression list too. Opt-outs belong to the person, not to the software, so a fresh import without them can break the law.

## What Does a Three-Touch Sequence Look Like?

Run the sequence only for the green bucket, and split that group by past spend. High-value customers can receive a personal note or a call from the owner, though calls follow different rules, so check yours. Medium-value customers get the three touches below. Low-value customers get one automated message.

Touch 1, on day 1, is a warm note from a named person. Say who you are, why you are writing, what is new and include one link, with no offer yet. Touch 2, around day 7, gives one specific reason to come back, with a date. Touch 3, around day 14, is a final reminder. After that, stop and move the person to your normal low-frequency list.

Every message needs a single-click unsubscribe that suppresses the person across every list. If you also use texts, they need their own consent trail. You can draft the wording with the prompts in [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing), but check each draft against your country's rules.

## Which Offer Can You Afford?

A deep discount trains customers to wait for sales. Cheaper options include a small add-on that costs you little, bonus loyalty points, early access to something new, or a personal note. Work out the cost per redemption before you send anything. The worked example below assumes $4 per redemption.

For people who booked and did not turn up, a different message usually works better than a discount. Keep that case separate from the lapsed list.

## Why Did They Leave? Ask Once

Put one question in the first or second message, with four or five choices and an "other" box. Useful options are: moved, found another place, price, a bad experience, and just busy. The answers show whether you have a marketing problem or a service problem.

Someone who had a bad experience needs an apology, not a coupon. Reply to every complaint personally, on the same day. If replies start arriving in volume, a tool can help sort them, but a person should still write the answer. ## Does a Reactivation Campaign Pay? A Worked Example

Take a lapsed list of 800 customers. Every input below is an assumption for illustration, except the bucket shares, which should come from your own permission sort. Replace them with your numbers before deciding.

| Line | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lapsed customers on file (assumption) | 800 | 800 | 800 |
| Share in green bucket (assumption) | 50% | 65% | 80% |
| Customers messaged | 400 | 520 | 640 |
| Reactivation rate (assumption) | 5% | 8% | 12% |
| Customers who return | 20 | 42 | 77 |
| First-visit spend (assumption) | $45 | $45 | $45 |
| Revenue | $900 | $1,890 | $3,465 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 60% | 60% | 60% |
| Offer cost at $4 per return | $80 | $168 | $308 |
| Tool and staff time (assumption) | $110 | $110 | $110 |
| Campaign profit | $350 | $856 | $1,661 |
| Cost per returning customer | $9.50 | $6.62 | $5.43 |

Profit is revenue multiplied by gross margin, minus offer cost and tool and staff time. The calculation counts only the first return visit, so it understates the value of customers who stay. It also ignores customers who lapse again, so track your re-lapse rate.

Compare the cost per returning customer with what you pay to win a new customer through ads. If a new customer costs you $40 (also an assumption), even the Tough column wins on cost per customer. The bigger risk is the green-bucket share in row two. If only 30% of your list is green, you simply have a smaller campaign, and no subject line fixes that. The lasting fix is collecting clear permission from the start.

## What Should You Measure?

Open rates are unreliable, so track outcomes instead. Reactivation rate is the share of messaged customers who buy again within about 30 days. Reactivated revenue is their total spend. Cost per reactivation is offers, tools and staff time divided by customers won back. Re-lapse rate shows how many lapse again within a couple of months, and unsubscribe and complaint rate shows whether your list or message is off.

Pull these from your sales or booking records, not only from email reports. For a comparison of channels, [SMS vs email marketing for local business](/blog/sms-vs-email-marketing-local-business-which-is-better) sets out the trade-offs.

## What Should You Do This Week?

1. Export your lapsed customers and add two columns: date of last purchase and how you collected the contact details.
2. Sort them into green, amber and red buckets using the table above, and put every red contact on a suppression list that travels with you between tools.
3. Choose your offer and work out its cost per redemption.
4. Write the three messages, with sender name, postal address and a one-click unsubscribe in each, and schedule them for days 1, 7 and 14.
5. Four weeks later, calculate reactivation rate, revenue, cost per returning customer and unsubscribes, then decide whether to repeat the campaign next quarter.

## FAQ

### How long should I wait before calling a customer lapsed?

Use your own buying cycle. About 1.5 times the usual gap between visits is a workable assumption. Coffee shops often measure in weeks, while salons and dentists measure in months.

### Can I email everyone who ever bought from me?

Not automatically. In the US, CAN-SPAM covers emails to former customers and requires an opt-out. In the UK, you need prior consent unless the soft opt-in applies. In Canada, implied consent from a purchase lasts two years. In Australia, you need express or inferred consent. Check each contact against your records.

### Do the rules apply to text messages?

Treat texts as a separate channel with their own consent trail. US texts fall under separate TCPA rules, and UK and Canadian rules also cover electronic messages. Get advice before texting a lapsed list.

### Should I offer a discount?

Not first. Try a personal note or a small add-on, and check what each redemption costs you before deciding whether a discount is worth it.

### What if a customer replies "stop"?

Treat it as an unsubscribe and suppress them everywhere, within the deadlines in the table or sooner. Do not argue with the reply.
