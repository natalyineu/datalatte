## Where do New Zealand small businesses get found first?

Most local searches in New Zealand start with Google, and a business that appears in a local directory adds a second route to customers. Directory counts show how competitive some categories are. LocalBizNetwork lists 16 beauty salons and 13 automotive businesses in Christchurch alone, so a bare listing is rarely enough to stand out.

Directories also differ in scope. The Christchurch page covers one city, while the national LocalBiz.nz category pages count listings across the whole country. Check which geography a directory serves before you count it as local visibility. A nationwide listing does not help a Wellington cafe if its customers only search for Wellington suburbs.

Start with the platforms your customers already use. Then fill the gaps with directory profiles that match your city.

## Is a free directory profile worth setting up in New Zealand?

Buy Local NZ describes its profiles as free to join and says a profile adds a local business citation that can help in search results and AI search. That is the vendor's own claim, so treat it as a reason to test rather than a guaranteed result. Because the profile costs nothing, the test is low-risk.

Regional sites operate under the Buy Local NZ umbrella, including Northland, Auckland, East Coast, Waikato, Tauranga, Hawke's Bay, Taranaki, Manawatu, Wellington, Nelson, Christchurch and Dunedin. Pick the site that matches where your customers actually live, rather than joining every region.

Keep your business name, address and phone number identical across every profile and your website. Inconsistent details weaken the citation and make it harder for customers to confirm they have found the right business.

## How much should a small NZ business budget for Google Ads?

The sources in this guide do not publish New Zealand cost-per-click or conversion rates, so the example below uses inputs labelled as assumptions. Replace them with your own account data after your first 30 days of spend.

Worked example: a pet groomer in Christchurch with a monthly paid search budget of NZD 900 (assumption).

- Average cost per click: NZD 2.00 (assumption)
- Clicks per month: 900 divided by 2.00 = 450
- Booking conversion rate: 4% (assumption), so 450 times 0.04 = 18 bookings
- Value of one booking: NZD 80 (assumption), so 18 times 80 = NZD 1,440 in booking value
- Return on ad spend: 1,440 divided by 900 = 1.6

At these assumed inputs, each dollar of ad spend brings back NZD 1.60 in booking value. If the real conversion rate is 2%, the same budget produces about 9 bookings and a return near 0.8, which is a loss. Test the conversion rate before you scale the budget.

## Which suburb keywords work across Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch?

Suburb and neighbourhood terms usually match local intent more closely than city-wide terms. A searcher typing "hairdresser Ponsonby" is usually closer to booking than one typing "hairdresser Auckland". Build ad groups around suburbs your customers actually travel from, and write separate headlines for each.

Use the same logic in each city. Central Wellington and central Christchurch can behave differently from suburban Auckland, so split campaigns by location. Each campaign can then carry its own bid and message. A single account with mixed locations hides which area is producing bookings.

- "hairdresser Ponsonby Auckland"
- "coffee Cuba Street Wellington"
- "gym Christchurch CBD"
- "dog groomer Hamilton"

Five to ten keywords per campaign is enough to start. Add more only after the first set shows which terms convert.

## Should a New Zealand business use Facebook and Instagram too?

Social channels suit businesses that depend on visual proof, such as cafes, salons and fitness studios. Short, unpolished video often works better than studio shots for local audiences, but test this against your own results. Track what each post sends to your booking page, not just likes or reach.

Paid social and paid search do different jobs. Search captures people already looking for your service, while social creates demand among people who are not searching yet. ## What should a NZ business check before launching paid search?

Setup mistakes waste more money than weak creative, so check the basics before any campaign goes live. The table below lists the steps in order.

| Step | Action | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Claim a free profile on the regional Buy Local NZ site | Name, address and phone match your website |
| 2 | Verify your Google Business Profile with the same details | Profile shows your correct suburb |
| 3 | Set location targeting per city or suburb | Campaigns are split by location |
| 4 | Pick 5 to 10 suburb-plus-service keywords | Each keyword maps to a page on your site |
| 5 | Set a monthly cap based on your margin per booking | Cap is written down before launch |
| 6 | Track bookings, not just clicks | Conversion goal fires on each booking |
| 7 | Review results after 30 days | You have enough data to change one variable |

Service businesses with jobs booked through a phone call or form should also check whether a paid listing type fits. The question is covered in [Google Local Service Ads: Are They Worth It for Local Businesses?](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business).

## FAQ

### Is New Zealand a good market for small local businesses?

The sources here show active local directories in Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch, but they do not publish market size or spend data. Test with a small budget and track bookings before you commit more money.

### Do I need separate campaigns for Auckland and the rest of New Zealand?

Separate campaigns make sense when each city has different customers and different competition. Splitting campaigns by location lets you set bids and messages for each area and shows which city is producing bookings.

### Is a free directory profile enough on its own?

No. A profile adds a citation and a listing, but customers still need a website, reviews and a way to book. Treat the profile as one part of a wider local presence.

### Should I use Facebook or Google first?

If customers are already searching for your service, start with Google Search because it captures existing demand. Add Facebook or Instagram once you know which bookings converted and can measure social separately.
