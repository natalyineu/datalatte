Local marketing in Latvia works best when small businesses combine a complete Google Business Profile, Latvian-language Google Ads, and a supplementary social channel such as Instagram or Draugiem.lv. One Latvian agency cites research showing 97% of users search online for local businesses, and many of those searches lead to action soon after. The practical starting point is to make your listing and ads easy to find for people searching in Latvian, Russian, or English, then measure which channel brings bookings or calls.

## How much do Latvian customers search for local businesses online?

Local search is the main entry point for most nearby purchases. The Latvian agency Aurian reports that 97% of users look for local businesses online, and that 76% of people who run a local search visit the business the same day. Both figures come from a secondary source that summarises unnamed studies, so treat them as directional rather than precise benchmarks for your own category.

For a small business, the implication is simple. If someone searches for a hair salon or dog groomer near them, the business that appears in the map results with complete details and fresh reviews has the advantage. Ads help you appear above organic results, but the profile is what converts the click into a visit.

## Which channels should a Latvian small business start with?

Start with the channels closest to purchase intent, then add social channels once bookings are tracked. Google Business Profile and Google Search Ads capture people already looking for your service. Instagram and Facebook support awareness and repeat visits. Draugiem.lv is a Latvian-built network that can add reach to a local audience, but it should be tested after your core channels are measured.

| Channel | Best for | Effort | Language note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Map results, calls, directions | Low, one-off setup plus replies | Use Latvian as primary, add Russian or English where your customers need it |
| Google Search Ads | Immediate bookings from search | Medium, needs keyword and location setup | Separate Latvian, Russian, and English campaigns |
| Instagram | Visual services, salons, cafés | Medium, weekly posting | Bilingual captions work well for mixed audiences |
| Facebook | Community groups, events, retargeting | Medium | Target by city and interest |
| Draugiem.lv | Supplementary reach to Latvian users | Low to medium | Test with a small budget and a tracked link |

Google Ads and Business Profile should come first in most cases because they are measurable. Social channels are harder to attribute, so set a tracked link or a booking code for each one before you increase spend.

## How do you set up a Google Business Profile for Latvian searches?

The profile should match what customers type into search, in the language they use. Confirm the business name, address, phone number, and opening hours. Add your services in Latvian, and where relevant in Russian or English. Upload current photos, then reply to reviews, because responses show that the business is active.

1. Claim or verify the profile and check that the category matches your main service.
2. Write a short description that names your city or district, such as Centrs, Āgenskalns, or Purvciems in Riga.
3. Add services with clear names, for example "dog grooming" or "hair colouring," in each language you serve.
4. Post an update or offer at least monthly, and keep opening hours correct around national holidays.
5. Reply to every new review within a few days, in the language the reviewer used.

Local map ranking depends on relevance, distance, and prominence, so consistency across your website and directories matters as much as any single change.

## What does a Google Ads budget look like in euros?

Latvia uses the euro, so budgets are set in EUR. Public Latvia-specific cost-per-click data was not available in the sources for this guide, so the example below uses assumption inputs. Replace them with figures from your own account after two to four weeks of data.

Example: a Riga hair salon with a monthly marketing budget of €600 (assumption). Assume 60% goes to Google Ads (assumption), which is €360. Split it €200 for a Latvian campaign and €160 for a Russian campaign, both assumptions. Assume an average cost per click of €0.60 (assumption), giving about 600 clicks per month (€360 ÷ €0.60). Assume a 4% booking rate on those clicks (assumption), producing 24 bookings. At an average booking value of €45 (assumption), that is €1,080 in revenue, or a return of 3.0 times ad spend (€1,080 ÷ €360).

The point of the calculation is the method, not the numbers. Divide spend by cost per click to get clicks, multiply by booking rate to get bookings, then multiply by booking value. If the result is below your margin, adjust keywords, districts, or landing pages before increasing budget. ## Should you run separate Latvian and Russian campaigns?

Separate campaigns are the cleaner approach when your customers speak both languages. A single campaign mixing Latvian and Russian keywords makes it harder to see which language is converting, and it complicates ad copy. Build one campaign per language, each with its own keywords and landing page text.

In practice, Latvian campaigns cover terms such as "frizētava Rīgas centrs" or "suņu frizētava Rīga." Russian campaigns cover equivalent searches such as "парикмахерская Рига." English campaigns make sense if you serve expats or remote workers in Riga. Keep each campaign's budget visible so you can compare booking rates by language after a month.

## How should you track bookings from social channels?

Social channels are harder to attribute than search, so track them before scaling. Use a unique tracked link for each channel in your bio, posts, or Draugiem.lv page, and a separate booking code or question such as "mention Instagram when booking." Review these weekly against your booking system.

For a service business that takes bookings by phone, WhatsApp, or an online calendar, record the source at the time of booking. If you use a scheduling tool, check whether it can report lead sources, because that decides whether you can compare channels fairly. ## FAQ

### Is Latvian or Russian better for local marketing?

Use both, in separate campaigns. Latvian is needed to reach Latvian-speaking customers, and it should be the default language on your Google Business Profile. Russian campaigns reach a distinct group of searchers, especially in parts of Riga. Compare booking rates by language after a month and shift budget toward the stronger one.

### How important is Draugiem.lv for a Latvian business?

Treat it as supplementary. Set up your Google Business Profile, Google Ads, and Instagram or Facebook first, and measure them. Then add Draugiem.lv with a small test budget and a tracked link. If it produces few bookings, keep the page up but move the spend back to search.

### Can a small Latvian business compete with larger firms online?

Yes, in most local categories. A complete Google Business Profile with recent reviews, clear Latvian service descriptions, and a landing page for each district can compete well for nearby searches. Larger firms may outspend you on broad terms, so focus on district-specific and service-specific searches where your location gives you an advantage.

### What should I track each month?

Track profile calls, direction requests, and website bookings from Google. Track ad spend, clicks, and bookings by language campaign. Track tracked-link bookings from social channels. Compare booking cost and revenue per channel against your margin, then reallocate budget every month rather than every quarter.

### Do I need a marketing agency to run this?

Not necessarily. The profile setup and tracking steps above can be done in-house by a small team. An agency or freelancer can help with Latvian and Russian copywriting, ad management, or a website landing page. If you hire one, ask for weekly reporting on bookings, not only clicks and impressions. For ongoing paid search support, see the [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads).
