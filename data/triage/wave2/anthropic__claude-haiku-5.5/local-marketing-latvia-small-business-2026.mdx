Local marketing in Latvia comes down to three moves: be easy to find in Google Search and Maps, run Latvian and Russian language campaigns where your customers use those languages, and treat Draugiem.lv as a supplementary channel for older, locally oriented audiences. A Latvian agency report cites research showing 97% of users search online for local businesses. Start with a complete Google Business Profile, then add paid search once you can track calls and bookings. Latvia-specific public data is thin, so each figure below is labelled by source, and the worked budget uses assumptions.

## How often do Latvian customers search for local businesses online?

Most local demand starts with a search. The [Aurian agency article on local Google advertising](https://aurianagency.lv/lokala-reklama-google-ka-piesaistit-klientus-tuvakaja-apkartne/) reports that 97% of users look for local businesses online, and that 76% of people who run a local search visit the business the same day. Both figures are secondary: the article does not name the underlying study, so treat them as directional rather than precise.

## Which platforms should a Latvian small business use first?

Start with search, then add social channels that match your customers. Google is the first priority because it is where local intent is expressed: someone types a service and a town, and the Maps results follow. Paid Google Ads and an optimised Business Profile work together, and the profile costs nothing beyond time.

Meta platforms come second. Facebook and Instagram suit visual businesses such as cafés, salons and groomers, where photos of the product drive enquiries. Use them to remind past customers and to build a local audience, not as the only source of leads.

Draugiem.lv and WhatsApp are useful for direct contact. WhatsApp works for bookings once a customer has reached you, while Draugiem.lv adds reach among Latvian-speaking users. Add these after your primary channels are measured. Adding a channel before you can count its leads makes the results impossible to judge.

## How should a Latvian business split Google Ads by language?

Latvia has several language groups, and a single English or Latvian campaign may miss customers who search in another language. The practical approach is one campaign per language, each with keywords written in that language. The checklist below sets out what each campaign should cover.

| Language | Who it reaches | What to set up |
|---|---|---|
| Latvian | Native Latvian-speaking customers | Latvian keywords and ad copy, Latvian Business Profile description, Latvian landing page |
| Russian | Russian-speaking residents in your service area | Russian keywords (for example, parikmaherskaya Riga), Russian ad copy, a matching Russian page or section |
| English | Expats and remote workers who buy your service | English keywords only if your service is bought by English speakers, with a short English landing page |

Run each language campaign with its own budget so you can see which one brings leads. Review search terms monthly and add negative keywords for terms that bring clicks but no enquiries. Keep the Business Profile in Latvian and add the second language in the description or services list.

## What does a Latvian local Google Ads budget look like?

The worked example below uses a hypothetical Riga café. All inputs are assumptions, not sourced figures, and you should replace them with your own data after a first month of testing.

Assumed monthly Google Ads budget: €500 (assumption). Assumed average cost per click: €0.80 (assumption). Assumed landing page conversion rate, meaning the share of visitors who call, book or message: 4% (assumption).

Clicks = €500 ÷ €0.80 = 625 clicks.
Leads = 625 × 4% = 25 leads.
Cost per lead = €500 ÷ 25 = €20.

Split the budget across languages before you start. An assumed split of €300 Latvian, €125 Russian and €75 English gives each campaign enough spend to produce a reading within a month. If the Russian campaign produces leads at a lower cost per lead than the Latvian one, shift budget toward it. The point of the calculation is to compare cost per lead across campaigns, not to predict a specific result.

Once you have a month of real numbers, compare them with the assumptions. A higher cost per click than €0.80 means your keywords or bidding need tightening. A lower conversion rate than 4% usually points to the landing page or the Business Profile rather than the ad.

## Where does Draugiem.lv fit in a Latvian marketing plan?

Draugiem.lv is a Latvian social network and a supplementary channel. Use it when you want to reach Latvian-speaking users who may not be fully captured by Meta targeting. It suits businesses whose customers are older or more locally rooted, such as tradespeople, local retailers and services with a repeat clientele.

Do not start here. Set up the Business Profile, Latvian Google Ads and one Meta channel first, then test Draugiem with a small monthly budget of your own choosing. Measure enquiries from it separately, using a distinct link or a question at booking, so you can decide whether to keep it.

## Which local events should shape a Latvian marketing calendar?

Latvia's national calendar gives businesses natural campaign moments. Midsummer, known in Latvian as Jāņi, is the country's most important cultural holiday, with traditions that include bonfires, singing and oak-leaf garlands. Food, drink and beauty businesses often see demand rise around it, so plan promotions and posts in the weeks before. Independence Day is a national day of pride, and content that references Latvian heritage and identity can resonate when it is authentic and not overstated.

Plan these moments into your calendar rather than improvising. Set a content deadline, prepare Latvian-language posts and check that your Google Business Profile hours and special notices are current before the holiday. ## FAQ

### Should a Latvian business advertise in Latvian or Russian?

Both, in separate campaigns. Latvian reaches native Latvian-speaking customers and should be the default. Russian reaches Russian-speaking customers in your area, so run it where your local customer base includes them. Keep each campaign's keywords, ad copy and landing page in its own language.

### How important is Draugiem.lv compared with Google?

Google is the priority. Draugiem.lv is a supplementary channel for Latvian-speaking users who are not well reached through Meta. Set up Google Ads and your Business Profile first, then test Draugiem with a small budget and measure its enquiries separately.

### Can a small Latvian business compete with larger firms on Google?

Yes, through a complete and active Business Profile, local keywords and reviews in Latvian. Competitive advantage here comes from relevance and detail rather than budget. Use specific keywords tied to your location, such as the district or town you serve, and keep your hours and photos current.

### What should I measure in the first month?

Track cost per click, clicks, enquiries and cost per lead for each language campaign, plus the number of calls and bookings from your Business Profile. Use the worked example above to set a target cost per lead, then compare the actual figures after 30 days.
