A 2026 survey of 662 gym and studio owners found 86% grew their membership this year, yet 82% said customer acquisition costs rose. Most owners also handle marketing themselves. The survey covers 11 countries and does not break out New York, so treat its figures as directional and test each tactic against your own studio data.

## How do you stand out when rivals are on every block?

Competition is intensifying almost everywhere. [The Health & Fitness Association report](https://www.healthandfitness.org/86-of-gym-and-studio-owners-grew-membership-this-year-even-as-costs-and-competition-rise/) found 95% of owners say competition has intensified over the past three years. In a dense neighborhood, a generic "premium studio" message disappears quickly.

Start with positioning. [A health club marketing guide](https://www.healthclubconsultants.com/blog/9-keys-to-marketing-a-gym/) recommends defining your target market, studying competitor pricing, and choosing a unique selling proposition. Pricing should match that positioning, so a studio that claims to be exclusive needs prices to match.

Members are also changing what they want. The same report found 66% of owners report requests for more flexible or shorter contracts, 65% see demand for better digital or hybrid options, and 48% see requests for recovery services. Lower prices ranked fourth, at 46%. Competing on value and flexibility is often more defensible than competing on price.

## Which referral and offer structure brings members in?

A double-sided referral program works best when both people benefit. [Mariana Tek's guide to group fitness marketing](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) suggests rewarding the existing member, giving the new friend a reason to say yes such as a free first class or intro pack, keeping redemption to a simple code or link, and tracking who refers the most.

Time-limited pre-sales also drive sign-ups. [The 9 Keys guide](https://www.healthclubconsultants.com/blog/9-keys-to-marketing-a-gym/) recommends opening a pre-sale with the strongest offer and changing it every two weeks or month, so prospects have a real reason to join now. Referral rewards only work if current members are satisfied enough to recommend the studio, so retention comes first.

## How should Google Business Profile support local search?

A Google Business Profile is often where local prospects first find a studio. [Loyalsnap's fitness marketing guide](https://www.loyalsnap.com/post/15-effective-fitness-studio-marketing-ideas-to-get-more-clients) notes that a listing places a studio on Google Maps, and that contact details, reviews, photos and videos all build trust on the page.

Ask happy members to leave reviews that naturally mention the search phrases prospects use, such as "best gyms around me" or a specific class type. Keep the profile complete and matching the website. ## Where do short video and social posts fit?

Video works well for showing a studio's atmosphere, instructors and equipment. Loyalsnap's guide suggests testimonial videos that address common objections, since video can be more emotional than text. Keep clips short and focused on one class type or one member story.

Social posting is also part of the instructor role. [The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/fitness-trainers-and-instructors.htm) describes fitness trainers promoting their facilities through social media, newsletters, blog posts, posters and flyers. ## Which channel should a New York studio fund first?

Channels do different jobs, so the right mix depends on whether a studio needs new trials, more referrals or fewer cancellations. The table below is a general framework rather than survey data.

| Channel | Main job in a NYC studio | What to track |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Captures people already searching for a class | Calls, direction requests, review count |
| Instagram and TikTok | Builds awareness with real class footage | Profile visits, trial bookings |
| Search and social ads | Reaches people ready to book near the studio | Cost per booked trial |
| Referral program | Turns satisfied members into recommenders | Referred sign-ups per member |
| Retention follow-up | Protects revenue you already have | Monthly cancellation rate |

Most studios should fund the retention row and the listing first, because those costs are lowest and their results are easiest to measure.

## How much does keeping a member save compared with recruiting one?

Retention math is often more useful than another ad test. The example below uses assumption inputs, not survey data, so replace them with your own numbers.

Assumption inputs: 150 active members, $160 average monthly fee, 5% monthly cancellation rate, and 12 months of value for a retained member.

- Members lost per month: 150 × 5% = 7.5
- Monthly revenue lost: 7.5 × $160 = $1,200
- Annual value of one retained member: $160 × 12 = $1,920
- If a retention change cuts cancellations to 3% (assumption), losses fall to 4.5 per month, saving 3 members.
- Value of 3 saved members over 12 months: 3 × $1,920 = $5,760

On these assumptions, a small cut in cancellations protects more annual revenue than a comparable rise in sign-ups often adds. For specific tactics, see the [membership retention guide](/blog/fitness-studio-membership-retention-tactics).

## Should a studio invest in automation and AI tools?

Adoption is already widespread. The Health & Fitness Association report found 65% of owners invested in new technology in the past year, including CRM platforms, booking systems, automation and AI tools, and 52% say AI will be essential to competing for members over the next 12 months.

Automation is most useful where timing matters. Mariana Tek's guide notes that a CRM can deliver a referral reward the moment a friend books a first class, and event sign-ups fade quickly without follow-up. ## Can off-peak capacity fill through corporate partnerships?

Many studios pay to run facilities that sit empty. The same report found 85% of owners say at least a quarter of their facility is unused during off-peak hours, but only 20% have a developed strategy to monetize it.

Corporate wellness is one option. Among owners partnered with a corporate wellness platform, 76% said the revenue was new income rather than shifted from existing members. This figure comes from a vendor that runs its own partnership program, so test any partnership against your own margins before committing.

## FAQ

### Do these statistics apply specifically to New York studios?

No. The Wellhub survey covers 662 gym and studio owners across 11 countries and does not report New York results. Use it as directional context, and measure acquisition cost, retention and referral rates in your own studio.

### How often should a studio change its pre-sale offer?

The [9 Keys guide](https://www.healthclubconsultants.com/blog/9-keys-to-marketing-a-gym/) suggests changing the offer every two weeks or month, so prospects see a real reason to act before the price changes.

### What should a studio fix first?

Start with the Google Business Profile and reviews, because it is often the first touchpoint. Then tighten the referral reward and retention follow-up. Paid ads tend to work better once the offer and listing are solid.

### Is video necessary for a small studio?

It is not required, but short clips help prospects see the room, instructors and energy before they visit. Keep production simple and focus on one class or one member story.
