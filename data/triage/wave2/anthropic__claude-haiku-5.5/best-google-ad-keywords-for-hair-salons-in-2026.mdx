The best Google Ads keywords for hair salons pair a service with a place: "balayage [city]", "hair salon near me", "women's haircut [city]", "bridal hair [city]" and "curly hair specialist near me". Someone typing a specific service and location is closer to booking than someone typing "hairstyles". Salon Guru warns that poorly managed Google Ads can be [enormously costly and wasteful](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/), so the keyword list is the place to start.

## Which keywords should a hair salon start with?

The competition for local clients is large and growing. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 670,800 barbers, hairstylists and cosmetologists in 2025 and projects 7 percent growth through 2035 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/barbers-hairstylists-and-cosmetologists.htm), primary). A generic term like "hair salon" puts you against all of them at once. Start with a service plus a place, and add a clear qualifier where you have one.

| Keyword idea | Intent | Good fit for |
|---|---|---|
| "hair salon near me" | Broad local | Salons with a strong map profile |
| "hair salon [city]" | Clear location | Single-location salons |
| "balayage [city]" | Specific, high-value service | Colour specialists |
| "women's haircut [city]" | Core service | Full-service salons |
| "book hair appointment [city]" | Booking intent | Salons with online booking |
| "hair colourist [neighbourhood]" | Very local | Salons in a named area |
| "hair extensions [city]" | High-ticket service | Extension specialists |
| "bridal hair [city]" | Plan-ahead event bookings | Salons with bridal packages |
| "curly hair specialist near me" | Named niche | Curl specialists |
| "affordable hair salon [city]" | Price intent | Salons with clear entry prices |
| "same-day haircut near me" | Urgency | Salons with open walk-in slots |

The intent column is a judgement, not measured data. Check volume and bid ranges in Keyword Planner for your area before committing budget.

## How do you check which keywords earn their cost?

Salon Guru's setup process starts with a group of target phrases, then a landing page with those keywords, then a full keyword list with match types and negatives ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/), secondary). Each phrase should send the searcher to a page about that exact service. A "balayage" click that lands on a general homepage wastes the visit and the money.

Once ads run, read the search terms report every week. Look for terms that brought clicks but no bookings, and for unexpected terms that did convert. Move the good ones into their own ad group with matching ad text. Pause or negate the rest. Judge the list on several weeks of data, not the first few days.

## Which negative keywords should hair salons add?

Negative keywords filter out searches that will never become clients. LinkedIn's Google Ads walkthrough for salons recommends them explicitly when running broad match, because broad match casts a wide net ([LinkedIn, Shubham Chordiya](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/step-by-step-guide-running-google-ads-salon-services-hi4df), secondary). Start with these themes:

- DIY and learning: "diy", "at home", "how to", "tutorial"
- Jobs and training: "jobs", "career", "salary", "course", "school"
- Supplies: "wholesale", "supplies", "kit", "tools"
- Services you do not offer, such as "barber" if you do not serve men
- Price-only terms such as "free" if they do not fit your offer

Add plural and singular forms yourself, because a negative only blocks the exact wording you list. Add new negatives from the search terms report each week.

## How should keywords map to ad groups and landing pages?

Group keywords by service, then write ads and landing pages for each group. Salon Guru's process creates ad groups, titles, descriptions and links for each service group, then adds more groups as results come in ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/), secondary). Use the same logic for your sitelinks. LinkedIn's example for a salon recommends "Book an Appointment", "View Services" and "Our Location" sitelinks, plus a call asset and callouts such as "Walk-ins Welcome" ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/step-by-step-guide-running-google-ads-salon-services-hi4df), secondary).

The same structure applies to other appointment businesses. For example, [nail salons](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-nail-salons-in-2026) and [barbershops](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-barbershops-in-2026) follow the service-plus-place pattern, and [fitness studios](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-fitness-studios-in-2026) need the same tight landing pages.

## What budget should a salon start with?

Set the budget from the number of new clients you need and what each one is worth, not from a fixed rule. LinkedIn's illustrative plan for a San Diego nail salon used $50 to $100 per day for a search campaign and $10 to $25 per day for local listing ads, with a 5 to 15 km radius ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/step-by-step-guide-running-google-ads-salon-services-hi4df), secondary). That is one consultant's example, not a benchmark for your city.

Salon Guru prices its own work as a setup fee plus monthly management and analysis for three months, quoted per campaign ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/), secondary). Ask any agency for a quote that separates setup from ongoing management, and check what the three-month period covers.

## Which mistakes waste salon budget?

Salon Guru notes that Google Ads can waste money if not managed correctly ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/), secondary). In practice, the common errors are:

1. Running broad match without a negative list, so ads show for DIY and job searches.
2. Sending every click to the homepage instead of a service page with a booking button.
3. Ignoring mobile, where call buttons and a fast booking page matter most.
4. Running ads outside opening hours or when nobody can answer the phone.

SEO works on a slower timescale. Salon Guru says a well-optimised site usually takes a few weeks to climb for key phrases, so ads and SEO can run together while rankings build ([Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/), secondary).

## FAQ

### What are the best Google Ads keywords for hair salons?

Specific service-plus-location phrases such as "balayage [city]", "hair salon near me", "women's haircut [city]" and "bridal hair [city]" work best. Broad terms like "hairstyles" attract clicks but convert less often.

### What negative keywords should a hair salon use?

Start with DIY, how-to, jobs, courses, supplies and wholesale terms, plus any service you do not offer. Add more from the search terms report each week.

### Should a hair salon use broad match?

Only with a strong negative list and conversion tracking in place. Phrase match is a safer starting point for most service keywords.

### How long before hair salon Google Ads show results?

Clicks can appear quickly, but judge the keyword list on several weeks of data. SEO usually takes a few weeks to build rankings for key phrases.
