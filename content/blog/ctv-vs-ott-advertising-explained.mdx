OTT (over-the-top) is video delivered over the internet instead of cable, broadcast or satellite, on any screen. CTV (connected TV) is the large TV screen that plays it, either a smart TV or a TV with a streaming stick or console. IAB UK puts it in one line: "CTV is a subset of OTT." Video on a phone is OTT but not CTV. The same video on a living-room TV is both.

For a local business the practical question is what you can buy and for how much. Roku lists a $500 minimum lifetime budget, and Amazon says its streaming TV campaigns have no minimum.

## What Is the Difference Between CTV and OTT?

OTT describes how content travels: over the internet, bypassing cable, broadcast or satellite platforms. IAB UK's 2022 definitions say it covers all devices and environments and "is not exclusive to connected TV (CTV)." CTV describes the screen: a large-screen TV connected to the internet, directly or through a console or streaming device.

Everyday usage is looser than the definitions, and vendors often use the two terms for the same product. When a vendor says "OTT ads," ask which screens the campaign actually runs on.

| | OTT | CTV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it describes | Delivery: video over the internet | The device: a TV connected to the internet |
| Screens | TV, phone, tablet, desktop | TV only |
| Relationship | The wider category | A subset of OTT |
| Example | Streaming app on a phone | The same app on a smart TV |

## Why Does the Difference Matter When You Buy Ads?

The screen changes how the ad is seen. A TV ad plays in a lean-back setting and a phone ad plays in a person's hand. Platforms may price and report these separately, so a campaign sold as OTT can include phone and desktop impressions.

Before you pay, ask three things: whether the buy is limited to TV screens, whether you can see results by device, and whether you can limit delivery to your ZIP codes. The answers matter more than the label.

Pricing is not published as a single CTV or OTT rate. Amazon's local TV guide gives a rough range of $5 to $50 per thousand views for streaming and $5 to $30 for traditional TV spots. That is a wide range, and it is one vendor's guide, not a market average.

## What Do the Other Acronyms Mean?

These terms describe how the service earns money, not the screen.

- **AVOD:** free to watch, paid for by ads. This is where advertisers buy.
- **SVOD:** paid by subscription. Some services also offer a cheaper plan with ads.
- **FAST:** free streaming channels that run like scheduled TV, with ad breaks.
- **Linear TV:** scheduled cable or broadcast television.

For a small business, the inventory you can buy is AVOD, FAST and ad-supported plans. Which services allow small advertisers differs by platform and country.

## What Can a Local Business Actually Buy?

Two self-serve routes are documented. Roku Ads Manager states that "Lifetime budgets are required on all campaigns and must be $500 or more." Its location targeting offers states, ZIP codes and DMAs, one geographic type per selection. If you do not narrow it, Roku says the entire United States is included.

Amazon's streaming TV guide says campaigns are billed per thousand impressions with "no minimum budget required," suggests a $150 daily budget as a starting test, and lists the US, Canada, Brazil, Mexico and the UK. Its DSP page lists a $10K recommended minimum for self-service packages and $50K for managed service.

## Worked Example: Reach for a $1,500 Test

All inputs are **assumptions** for illustration, not quotes from any platform.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Budget | $1,500 |
| CPM | $25 (inside Amazon's cited $5-50 range) |
| Impressions | 60,000 |
| Repeat views per household | 4 |
| Households reached | about 15,000 |

At $25 per thousand, $1,500 buys 60,000 impressions. If each household sees the ad four times, that is about 15,000 households. At a $10 CPM the same budget gives 150,000 impressions, and at $50 it gives 30,000. Your real CPM depends on targeting, season and platform, so treat your first campaign as the measurement.

## Should You Buy CTV or OTT?

For most local businesses, ask for TV-screen delivery inside your ZIP codes. Screen size is the main thing the ad has going for it, and broad OTT can spread a small budget over phones where the video is easy to ignore.

A practical order: start with a small, ZIP-targeted test, give it a landing page or a unique phone number to count response, and compare it with search before scaling. ## FAQ

### Is OTT the same as CTV?

No. CTV is a subset of OTT. OTT covers internet-delivered video on any device, while CTV means the TV screen. Vendors often use the terms loosely, so confirm the screens in any quote.

### Is YouTube CTV or OTT?

YouTube is delivered over the internet, so it is OTT. When it is watched on a TV, it is also CTV. The same applies to any streaming app.

### Can a small business advertise on streaming TV?

Yes. Roku Ads Manager requires $500 or more for a lifetime budget. Amazon says its streaming TV campaigns have no minimum budget. Both are US-focused products, so check availability in your country.

### Which is cheaper, CTV or OTT?

No source gives one price for each. Amazon's local TV guide cites $5 to $50 per thousand views for streaming. Ask for the CPM by device before you commit.
