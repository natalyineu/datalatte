## Which listings fill the Google Maps pack for a Nashville groomer?

The local pack is defined as the top three business listings in local search results, [according to Adwave's local SEO audit checklist](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today). Adwave's lawn care guide adds that these top three are pulled directly from their Google Business Profile listings ([Adwave, how to market a lawn care business](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business)). For a search such as "dog groomer near me," the profile is the first place Google looks.

Your website still matters. Google's AI-driven answers draw on business profile data, website content and reviews, according to [Accrisoft's summary as reported by Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a). Treat the profile as the foundation and the website as the place where your services and location are described in more depth.

Before choosing categories, look at the businesses that appear for your core search. Adwave recommends reviewing the top 3-5 results in local map listings to see which category they use ([Adwave, GBP categories](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones)).

## How should a pet groomer set up its Google Business Profile?

Start by claiming the profile. Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). Adwave states that 11.1% of profiles are unclaimed, though the figure has no named source, so treat it as a rough signal rather than a benchmark.

Fill in the business description. The vendor page [Adwave's beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) states the description allows 750 characters. Write it in plain language that names your services and Nashville neighbourhood once, without repetition.

Upload photos. Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, and says a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls ([Adwave, Google Maps marketing](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers)). Hours, phone number and address should be 100% accurate on the same page.

Publish posts. Google's help page explains that a post is public on Search and Maps only once its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Adwave advises at least one new post a week.

If you visit customers rather than seeing them at a storefront, the profile lets you hide your street address and become a Service Area Business ([Adwave, beginner's guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). Mobile groomers should check this setting first.

## Does a pet groomer's name, address and phone have to match everywhere?

Yes, and small differences count. Adwave's voice search guide says your NAP must be exactly the same on your website, your Business Profile, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places ([Adwave, voice search](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses)).

The beginner's guide gives an example: the website and profile must match down to the comma and whether you write "St." or "Street" ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack)). Audit your existing listings before adding new ones. A conflicting listing added today creates a new inconsistency to fix later.

Google's own business messaging can also be managed from a desktop dashboard, not only the mobile app ([Adwave, GBP messaging](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on)). Assign one person to check that replies and details stay consistent.

## How many reviews does a Nashville groomer need?

Google's guidance says reviews appear next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google, tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). They are visible to people deciding whether to book, so review volume matters beyond ranking.

On ranking, a Blogging Wizard analysis of 2 million profile pages found businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. The analysis is reported secondhand by Adwave, so treat 200 as a useful target rather than a guaranteed threshold ([Adwave, how to get more Google reviews](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business)).

Reviews come from requests made at the right time. The table below shows how long a steady request process takes to reach 200 reviews.

## Worked example: how long to reach 200 reviews?

The inputs below are assumptions, not sourced figures. Each scenario starts with 40 existing reviews (assumption) and asks for a review after each groom.

| Scenario | Grooms per month (assumption) | Review rate (assumption) | Reviews per month | Months to reach 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 40 | 20% | 8 | about 20 |
| Middle | 60 | 25% | 15 | about 11 |
| High | 90 | 30% | 27 | about 6 |

The middle scenario works out as 60 grooms × 25% = 15 reviews a month, or roughly 3-4 a week. Reaching 200 from 40 needs 160 more reviews, so 160 ÷ 15 is about 11 months. Adding a follow-up message 24 hours after each appointment is the cheapest way to move from the low row toward the middle row.

## Which local signals should a groomer track?

Adwave lists six local search metrics it calls useful: Google Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift over time ([Adwave, local search intent](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent)). Record them monthly in a simple spreadsheet so you can compare changes after each step.

Apple Business Connect also reports Profile Views, which counts how many people saw your business card in Apple Maps ([Adwave, Apple Business Connect](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring)). Apple Maps is a second place where your details need to be accurate.

Google Business Profile is not the only factor. Ahrefs reports a BrightLocal survey in which 36% of SEOs rated the profile as the most important ranking factor for the map pack, as of 2021 ([Ahrefs, local SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo)). Survey results describe opinion, not measured ranking weight.

## Should a pet groomer also run paid ads?

Paid placements and organic listings draw on the same profile data. Google's help page says location asset data used in Google Ads is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and corrections made there flow into linked ad accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). A cleaner profile therefore helps both channels.

A Marketing 360 case study describes a mobile pet groomer that advertised on Facebook and Google to choose its channels ([Marketing 360](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/)). ## FAQ

### Does a Nashville pet groomer need a website as well as a Business Profile?

Yes. A profile answers the "near me" search, but your website carries your full service list and location details. Keep the name, address and phone identical on both.

### How many photos should a pet groomer upload?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos. Use real images of your space and your work, with customer consent where dogs or owners are shown.

### How often should a groomer post to its profile?

Adwave advises at least one new post a week. Check that each post shows a Live status, since Pending and Not approved posts do not appear publicly.

### Can a mobile groomer hide its home address?

Yes, if you serve customers at their location. The profile allows you to hide your street address and be designated a Service Area Business.
