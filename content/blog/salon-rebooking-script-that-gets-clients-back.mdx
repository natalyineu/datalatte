The salon rebooking script that works is simple: before the client leaves the chair, the stylist recommends a specific return date and the front desk offers two available slots, rather than asking "Would you like to book again?". Clients who leave with a booked appointment are far more likely to come back than clients who say "I will call you".

An 80% rebooking rate is a goal some salons aim for, not a promise, and results vary with your services, clientele and location. What you can control is the process. Below is a script, a timing guide and the follow-up routine around it.

## Why the rebooking moment matters

Winning a new client costs time and ad money. Keeping one costs a conversation. Most clients mean to come back and simply forget, or put it off until their hair no longer looks good. A booked appointment turns good intentions into a commitment, and it fills your calendar weeks ahead.

Track your own number so you know where you stand. A simple measure: of the clients you serve in a week, how many left with their next appointment already booked? Your booking software (Square Appointments, Vagaro, Fresha, Booksy, GlossGenius and others) can usually report this, or you can tally it on paper for a month.

## Step 1: Recommend, do not ask

Stylists are the experts, and clients expect a recommendation. A question invites a "no" or a "maybe". A recommendation gives a plan.

**At the chair, near the end of the service:**

"Your cut will look its best for about six weeks. I would like to see you the week of the 14th to keep the shape. Does a morning or an afternoon suit you better?"

**For colour:**

"To keep your roots fresh, I recommend a touch-up in about five weeks. I have Thursday at 10 or Saturday at 2. Which works?"

**For a first-time client:**

"It was lovely to meet you. I would like to see you again in about six weeks so we can keep building on this. Shall I put you down for the 23rd?"

The pattern is always the same: say how long the result lasts, name a target date, and offer two options. Avoid open-ended "when would you like to come back?" because it makes the client do the planning.

## Step 2: Use sensible timing by service

Return times depend on the service, the hair and the client's goals. These are typical starting points to adapt to your own salon:

| Service | Typical rebooking window |
| --- | --- |
| Short cuts and fades | 3 to 5 weeks |
| Longer cuts and trims | 6 to 10 weeks |
| Root colour touch-up | 4 to 6 weeks |
| Highlights or balayage | 8 to 12 weeks, with toner visits in between |
| Blowouts and styling | As needed, often weekly or for events |
| Treatments (keratin, gloss) | Per product guidance |

Always use your stylist's judgement. A client with fast-growing hair or a high-maintenance colour has a shorter window than someone with a low-maintenance cut.

## Step 3: Hand off smoothly at the front desk

The stylist makes the recommendation, and the front desk completes it. Both should say the same thing.

**Stylist to desk:** "Jordan is due in five weeks for colour. Could you look for Thursday morning?"

**Desk to client:** "I have the 18th at 10 or the 20th at 2. Which would you prefer?"

Then confirm it out loud and by text: "You are booked for Thursday 18th at 10 with Sam. You will get a reminder two days before."

If the client is unsure, offer a hold rather than a no: "I can pencil you in and send you a text. You can move it up to 24 hours before without a fee." Check your cancellation policy first and make sure it is clear and fair.

## Step 4: Handle common objections

Prepare short answers so staff do not freeze.

- **"I do not know my schedule yet."** "No problem. I will book you in so you have a slot, and you can move it free until 24 hours before."
- **"I will call you."** "Of course. Our week fills up fast, so I will pencil in a spot for you and text you tomorrow to confirm."
- **"It is too expensive."** Listen first. Perhaps suggest a lower-cost maintenance service between big appointments, such as a gloss or a trim.

Do not push. A client who feels pressured will not rebook. A friendly, brief second offer is plenty.

## Step 5: Follow up automatically

The script handles clients in the chair. A simple message routine handles the rest.

1. **Confirmation text** straight after booking.
2. **Reminder** 24 to 48 hours before, with an easy reply or reschedule link.
3. **Thank-you message** the day after, with a prompt to leave a review if the client is happy.
4. **Lapsed-client nudge** when someone has passed their usual window with no booking, such as "We have not seen you in a while. We have a few openings this month."

Most booking apps include automated texts and emails. Check your plan, and make sure clients have opted in to messages. ## Step 6: Review the numbers monthly

Once a month, sit down for ten minutes and look at:

- Rebooking rate per stylist, as a coaching tool and not a ranking contest.
- No-show and late-cancel rates.
- How many lapsed clients returned after a nudge.

If one stylist has a lower rate, shadow a few checkouts. The gap is usually a missing recommendation, not a missing skill. If your overall rate is low, check whether the desk is offering two specific slots every time.

## Rebooking checklist for your team

Print this and keep it by the front desk:

- The stylist states how long the result will last.
- The stylist gives a target date, not a vague "soon".
- The desk offers two specific slots.
- The appointment is booked before payment is taken.
- A confirmation text goes out immediately.
- The next-visit plan is saved in the client notes.
- Clients who leave without booking get a friendly text within two days.

Run through the list at your weekly team meeting for a month. Small habits repeated every day do most of the work, and clients tend to appreciate being looked after rather than chased.

## FAQ

**Q: Is an 80% rebooking rate realistic?**

A: It depends on your clientele and services. Some salons aim for it, but treat it as a target. Measure your current rate first and aim to improve it steadily.

**Q: Should clients pay a deposit to rebook?**

A: Some salons use deposits for long colour appointments or peak times. Keep policies clear, fair and visible at booking. Many salons prefer a free-change window instead.

**Q: What if a client wants to wait and see?**

A: Offer to hold a slot with free changes until a set time, and send a gentle follow-up text. That keeps the door open without pressure.

**Q: Do I need special software?**

A: Not to start. A paper tally and a clear script work. Software makes reminders and reporting easier as you grow.
