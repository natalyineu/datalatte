Google says Local Services Ads (LSA) screening takes 3 to 4 weeks on average after you submit your documents. That is three to four weeks of leads you are not getting, so the owners who do best are the ones who start the paperwork before they have decided on a budget.

This guide is the setup manual: what to gather, what to click, which settings matter and what the first month looks like.

It is written for local businesses in the US, UK, Canada and Australia. ## Can Your Business Run Local Services Ads?

LSAs are limited to certain service categories, and the list differs by country. Google's [US getting-started page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS) lists categories well beyond the trades, including Hair Salon, Barber Shop, Nail Salon, Personal Trainer, Yoga Studio, Pet Grooming, Pet Training, Veterinarian and Coffee Shop.

A handful (such as tattoo studio and interior designer) are limited to California and Florida. The same page says the Google Verified badge is currently unavailable for verticals in the auto, beauty and dining categories.

Being on the list does not guarantee ads in your city. Run Google's eligibility check with your category and location. If you are in the UK, Canada or Australia, expect a shorter, trade-heavy list or no listing at all; Google's own sign-up flow is the final word.

## Which Documents Do You Need Before Applying?

Google's [verification requirements page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS) says requirements vary by category, state and user, and that on average the process takes 3 to 4 weeks after you submit documents. For a US advertiser, depending on category, the checks can include:

- **A verified, public Google Business Profile.** Every advertiser needs one, and the person completing the checks must be an owner or manager of it. Fix name, address, phone and hours first; the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) walks through it.
- **Business registration.** Google's [screening page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6226575?hl=en) says this is verified through a D-U-N-S number, with alternatives including a Secretary of State registration, an IRS EIN or a Department of Revenue registration.
- **Representative identity.** An unexpired government photo ID, with names matching exactly across your documents, which Google's screening page stresses. Check spellings before you upload.
- **Background checks.** The business, its owners and, in applicable categories, the service professionals who perform the core service. Office and clerical staff are excluded. Google says businesses are not charged for the check.
- **Insurance.** A certificate of insurance that is active for at least 14 days after you submit it, with the policy number visible.
- **Licences.** Checked against state or country databases, and county or city licences can be required too.

One exception matters for cafes: Google's requirements page says restaurants and coffee and dessert shops only need a verified Business Profile, and that food and beverage is a pilot limited to specific regions that the page does not name.

If you fail a background check and receive a final disqualification, Google says you must wait 30 days to reapply, and 180 days after a second denial. Check names and dates carefully before you submit.

## Which Badge Will You Get?

Older guides talk about "Google Guaranteed" and "Google Screened". Google's [Verified badge page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/16498018) says it is launching a single badge for all advertisers, ending the reimbursement programme tied to the old Guarantee badge, and that ranking is not affected. New advertisers must complete screening to receive the Google Verified badge.

Some advertisers are put through risk-based verification. Google's screening page says these advertisers can get a quick badge but then have 14 days to complete the remaining steps to keep their ad serving. Treat any early badge as a countdown, not a finish line. Google also says advertisers are re-evaluated regularly and that ignoring a requested review can cost you the badge, a pause or suspension.

## Which Account Settings Matter Most?

Once approved (or while you wait), these are the settings that decide whether your first month is useful.

### Job types

In the lead inbox, open the menu, choose Profile & Budget and edit the job types card, switching on only the jobs you actually want. Google's [edit page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12491364?hl=en) says to update these settings whenever your location or services change, and that you must keep at least one job type on when adding an industry.

A groomer who does not do mobile visits, or a plumber who refuses roof leaks, should leave those job types off from day one.

### Service areas

Add areas by county, city or ZIP code. Google's [performance tips](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12492201?hl=en) suggest setting areas broadly, for example a whole county, rather than individual postcodes. That raises lead volume but also travel time, so use your own break-even distance, not Google's default enthusiasm.

### Business hours and ad schedule

Google's [billing page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en) says a missed call is charged only when it comes during business hours and the caller stays on for more than 20 seconds. Google's [ad scheduling page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/9251526?hl=en) lists three options (all day, only during my business hours, or custom hours) and says the default is all day. If nobody answers the phone after 6pm, consider limiting the schedule to your business hours.

### Lead types

Google's tips page says message leads typically cost less than phone leads and catch customers when you cannot answer, such as nights and weekends. Booking leads need an integrated CRM partner. Switch on messages unless you really cannot reply within the hour.

### Photos and reviews

Add clear photos of your team and your work. Google's tips page suggests at least five reviews, and notes that some business types need that many before reviews show.

Its [ranking page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en&ref_topic=11555266) says the auction weighs your bid and overall profile quality, which includes rating, review count, average response time, images and completed verification checks. The page also lists responsiveness to customers, and says missed calls may hurt it.

## How Do Bidding and Budget Work?

Google's [bidding page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/10125017?hl=en) lists three bid modes: Maximize Leads (recommended), Target Cost Per Lead and Max Per Lead (manual ceiling). It suggests a budget that supports at least 10 leads a week for Maximize Leads and says results take about two weeks to become accurate.

There is a significant change underway. Google's [transition page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585?hl=en) says existing LSA advertisers are being moved to Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, in phases: US home and storefront service advertisers from August 2026, broader groups in late 2026, and non-US accounts and remaining categories in 2027.

According to Google, manual bidding is being deprecated, weekly budgets become daily averages (weekly amount divided by 7), with monthly spend capped at the daily average times 30.4, and lead history, messages and call recordings move to the Leads page in Google Ads.

Billing stays pay-per-valid-lead, ads still run only on Search and Maps, and a Verified badge you have already earned transfers.

Practical effect: do not build a plan that depends on a hard per-lead cap, and expect your screens to look different from older tutorials. Your account will show which setup applies to you.

## What Are the Billing Rules Before the First Lead?

From Google's billing page: you pay for valid leads, including answered calls, returned calls, texts, emails, voicemails and (US and Canada only) booking requests. A missed call during business hours is charged if the caller stays on for more than 20 seconds.

Repeat contact from the same number or email within 15 days of a charged lead is not charged again. Charged leads are reviewed for 30 days and poor-quality, duplicate or invalid ones may be credited automatically; credits are not available for health care verticals, tax specialists or EMEA advertisers.

Set up call handling now, and note which calls become jobs, because Google only counts leads.

## What Does a Launch Month Cost? A Worked Example

The figures below are for planning a launch month, not a promise. Cost per lead for the middle case uses the $53 home-services average from a February 2026 SearchLight Digital benchmark ($6.72M spend, 888 contractors, 126,650 leads), which comes second-hand through [The Valley Marketing Group](https://thevalleymarketinggroup.com/blog/google-local-services-ads-cost-per-lead-2026/).

It covers US trades, so treat it as a ceiling-style reference for salons or studios, where no verified public benchmark exists. Every other row is an **assumption**.

Two lessons. First, the 10-leads-a-week guideline is not cheap: in the Base case you are committing about $2,300 a month before you know whether your phone handling converts. If that is too much, you may not be ready for LSAs yet. Second, the setup delay has a price.

Four weeks of verification is four weeks of lost Base-case profit potential (about $440 on these assumptions), which is why you start the paperwork first and decide on the budget while Google checks you.

## What Should You Do First?

1. Confirm your category and location in Google's eligibility check, and note whether your country has a listing at all.
2. Make sure your Google Business Profile is verified, with the same business name, address and phone number as your registration documents.
3. Gather the paperwork: D-U-N-S number or equivalent registration, government photo ID, insurance certificate, licences and the owner details for the background check.
4. Decide who answers the LSA number, during which hours, and what happens when nobody can. Set a callback rule of five minutes.
5. Work out your break-even cost per lead (close rate x profit per job) using the [worth-it guide](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business), then start the application so verification runs while you plan the budget.

## FAQ

### How long does Google Local Services Ads verification take?

Google's requirements page says the process takes 3 to 4 weeks on average after you submit your documents. Some advertisers get a quick badge through risk-based verification but then have 14 days to complete the remaining steps.

### Can I run LSAs and normal Google Ads together?

Yes, they are separate products. Many eligible businesses use LSAs for ready-to-hire callers and Search ads for specific services. Search ads give you keyword and copy control that LSAs do not.

### Do pet groomers, salons or cafes qualify?

In the US, Google lists Pet Grooming, Hair Salon, Barber Shop, Nail Salon and Coffee Shop among its categories, but the Verified badge is unavailable for beauty and dining verticals. See the [LSA guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-local-services-ads-for-pet-groomers).

### What is Google doing with Local Services Ads in 2026?

Google says it is migrating LSA advertisers to Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, in phases starting with select US advertisers in August 2026. You still pay per valid lead.

### How much should I budget?

Google suggests a budget that supports at least 10 leads a week for Maximize Leads. Multiply by the cost per lead Google shows in your account; the table above shows what that looks like at three price points.
