Google Local Services Ads (LSA) are pay-per-lead ads that appear at the top of Google search results for certain local service searches. Instead of paying per click, you pay when a customer contacts you through the ad (typically a call or message), and your listing shows a Google verification badge after your business passes screening. Availability, categories and prices vary by country and city, so check Google's current eligibility tool before planning around them.

## What Makes LSA Different from Google Search Ads

Traditional Google Search Ads charge per click: someone searches, clicks, and you pay whether or not they become a customer.

Local Services Ads generally charge per **lead**, such as a phone call or message from a potential customer. If a lead is not valid (wrong number, spam, outside your service area, or a service you do not offer), Google provides a way to dispute it, and valid disputes can be credited. Dispute rules and time windows change, so check the current help documentation.

The other major difference is the verification badge. To run LSA, Google verifies your business through checks such as background checks, licensing and insurance, depending on the category and region. Once approved, your listing displays a badge that signals Google has vetted you. Depending on category the badge is called "Google Guaranteed" or "Google Screened."

<Callout type="tip">
Google Guaranteed is generally used for home services, and Google Screened for professional services. Names and rules can change, so confirm in your Google Ads account.
</Callout>

## Which Business Types Are Eligible for LSA

LSA eligibility is limited and varies by country and region. It has historically focused on home services and professional services, and Google expands it over time. Examples that have commonly been eligible in some markets include:

* **Home services:** plumbers, electricians, HVAC, cleaners, locksmiths, pest control, roofers, movers
* **Professional services:** lawyers, financial planners, tax professionals, real estate agents
* **Other categories** that Google has added in some regions

Many other local businesses, including coffee shops, many beauty and fitness businesses and some pet services, may not be eligible in your market. Always check Google's LSA eligibility information with your business category and location. If you are not eligible, standard [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and a strong [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) are your main local options. ## How Much LSA Costs

LSA pricing is set by the auction and varies a great deal by category and location. Cost per lead tends to be lower for simpler, lower-ticket services and higher for high-value services like legal or home repair, and higher in competitive metro areas. Google may show you an estimated range for your category and area while you set up, which is the best source for planning.

Rather than relying on a generic figure, work backwards from your own numbers:

1. What is a typical job worth to you?
2. What share of leads do you close?
3. What cost per lead can you afford while staying profitable?

You set a weekly or monthly budget in your account. Start modestly, track the leads you receive and the jobs you book, and adjust.

## Getting Verified: Step by Step

The verification process is the biggest barrier to entry, and also what makes LSA leads feel more trustworthy to customers. The exact steps vary by category, but typically include:

1. **Create your LSA profile** in your Google Ads account. Enter your business category, service area and contact details.
2. **Background checks** for the owner and sometimes staff, run through a third-party provider.
3. **License and insurance verification**, where required for your category. Have clear copies ready.
4. **Google's review** of your application. Timing varies from days to several weeks, and resubmitting unclear documents slows it down.
5. **Billing and go-live**, where you set your budget, business hours and lead preferences.

## Managing LSA Leads

Getting approved is just the start. How you handle leads determines whether LSA pays off.

* **Answer every call quickly.** Google has said responsiveness can influence how your ads are shown, and a missed call is a lost lead you have paid for or will not get.
* **Mark leads as booked** in the dashboard, which gives Google feedback about which leads are good.
* **Dispute invalid leads promptly,** such as spam, wrong numbers or leads outside your service area. Check the current time limit in Google's documentation.
* **Keep asking for reviews.** Review count, rating and recency are widely believed to influence ranking.

Google does not publish a full list of LSA ranking factors, but proximity, reviews, responsiveness and business hours are commonly cited as important. Treat any claimed percentage weighting with skepticism.

## LSA vs Google Search Ads: Which Should You Run?

If your category is eligible and you can afford both, they can work together: LSA for high-intent, local service searches and Search Ads for broader keyword coverage. If you must choose:

**Choose LSA if:**
- Your category is eligible in your area
- Your main goal is calls and bookings
- You have a good number of reviews
- You can answer calls quickly

**Choose Search Ads if:**
- Your category is not LSA-eligible
- You sell products rather than services
- You want more control over keywords and audiences
- You want to build awareness alongside leads

## FAQ

**Q: Can I run LSA and regular Google Ads at the same time?**
A: Generally yes. They are separate products and show in different parts of the results. Many eligible businesses run both, using LSA for high-intent local searches and Search Ads for wider keyword coverage.

**Q: What happens if I get a lead outside my service area?**
A: Dispute it through your LSA dashboard, and make sure your service area is set accurately so fewer such leads arrive. Check Google's current dispute rules and time limits.

**Q: How long does it take to get approved for the Google badge?**
A: It varies. Background checks, license and insurance verification, and Google's own review can take from a few days to several weeks. Clear, complete documents speed it up.

**Q: Can I pause LSA without losing my badge?**
A: You can pause ads, but long pauses or lapsed licenses or insurance may require re-verification. Check Google's current policy before a long break.

**Q: Do I need to answer every call?**
A: You should answer as many as you can, quickly. Consider call forwarding to several phones or a receptionist service so you do not miss paid leads.
