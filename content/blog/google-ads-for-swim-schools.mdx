## Why Does Peak Season Cost More Per Family?

**Worked example (assumption: every input below is invented, not a market benchmark; swap in your own).** Assume 5% of clicks become an enquiry (call or form), 50% of enquiries enroll, and a family pays $140 a month for 4 months ($560 lifetime value). Assume clicks cost $2.50 in the off-season and $4 in peak, because more schools are bidding.

| | Off-season, $500 | Peak, same $500 | Peak, $800 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per click (assumed) | $2.50 | $4.00 | $4.00 |
| Clicks | 200 | 125 | 200 |
| Enquiries (5%) | 10 | 6.25 | 10 |
| Families enrolled (50%) | 5 | 3.1 | 5 |
| Cost per enrolled family | $100 | $160 | $160 |
| Lifetime value of families | $2,800 | $1,750 | $2,800 |

Read it row by row: $500 / $2.50 = 200 clicks, and $500 / $4 = 125 clicks. To buy the same 5 families in peak season you need $800, which is $300 more. Even then each family costs $160 against a $560 lifetime value.

If your price or retention is lower, redo the last row with your own numbers. The lesson is simple: plan the peak budget before the peak, because the same money buys fewer families.

## Why Do Google Ads Work for Swim Schools?

Parents search close to home and compare a few options. They care about location, class level, schedule and safety. Ads put you in front of those exact searches, and a complete Google Business Profile helps you show in map results. Start with our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

Swim demand is seasonal: it usually rises ahead of summer and drops in colder months, though indoor pools can fill classes all year. Campaigns should follow that rhythm. For the wider picture see our [Google Ads guide](/guides/google-ads-guide).

## How Should You Split Seasonal Campaigns?

Build separate campaigns so you control budget and messaging by season.

**Peak (typically spring and summer)**
- Raise daily budgets a few weeks before demand climbs.
- Target "summer swim lessons", "kids swim classes [city]" and "swim camp".
- Add a bid adjustment for mobile, since parents often search on phones.

**Off-season (typically autumn and winter)**
- Lower budgets and use tighter keywords: "indoor heated pool swim lessons", "winter swim classes toddlers", "adult swim lessons indoors".
- Lead with winter benefits: warm indoor pool, small classes, year-round enrollment.

**Ad schedule**
- Check when calls and bookings actually arrive (often weekday evenings and weekends for parents) and weight budget toward those windows.

## Which Keywords Should a Swim School Use?

Group by theme and match each group to a landing page:

| Theme | Keyword ideas |
|---|---|
| General local | swim lessons near me, swim school [city], swimming classes [city] |
| By age | baby swim classes, toddler swim lessons, kids swim lessons, adult swim lessons |
| By level | beginner swim lessons, learn to swim, stroke development |
| By setting | indoor swim lessons, heated pool lessons, private swim lessons |
| Seasonal | summer swim lessons, swim camp, winter swim classes |

Start with phrase and exact match. Add negatives: "jobs", "lifeguard certification", "pool installation", "pool supplies", "free swim", "swimsuit" and "how to swim" (informational searches).

## Should a Swim School Use Remarketing?

Most visitors will not book on the first visit. Google Ads lets you show ads to people who visited your website by creating an audience segment of website visitors ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2454064), checked Oct 2026), for example on other websites or YouTube.

1. Install the Google Ads tag on your site, or ask your marketer to.
2. Create lists for all visitors and for people who reached your booking page but did not finish.
3. Run a small campaign with a clear offer, such as "Still thinking about swim lessons? Book a free trial class this week" (only if you offer one).
4. Use frequency capping to avoid ad fatigue.

Do this after your search campaigns produce steady traffic.

## Which Ad Assets Help Parents Act?

Google now calls what used to be extensions "assets" ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7331111)). They make your ad larger and more useful:

- **Call assets:** parents want to ask about levels and availability. Make sure someone answers during the hours your ads run.
- **Location assets:** promote your address so nearby parents can call, visit or find you.
- **Sitelinks:** link to schedule, pricing, free trial and instructor pages.
- **Structured snippets and callouts:** indoor pool, small class sizes, certified instructors, age ranges. Only what is true.

Review asset performance regularly and keep what earns clicks.

## What Should the Landing Page and Tracking Include?

- Send clicks to a page that matches the search (a "baby swim classes" page, not the homepage).
- Show age groups, schedule, price, location, safety information and one clear booking or enquiry button.
- Track phone calls (with a minimum call length), form submissions and booking-software enrollments as conversions.

If families phone to book, read call tracking for local business.

## How Do You Size the Budget?

Size the budget backward from what you can pay to enroll one family. Estimate a student's lifetime value and your margin, then use your expected conversion rate to work out the clicks you need (see the table above). Larger cities and peak season usually cost more per click.

Review results after 60 to 90 days, not a few days. If you cannot sustain a budget for a few months, build your Google Business Profile and website first.

## What Should You Do Before You Launch?

1. Work out your lifetime value per family: monthly price x months they stay.
2. Pull your enquiry and enrollment counts for the last 3 months. That gives you your own two rates.
3. Open Keyword Planner and write down the click estimate for "swim lessons near me" and "baby swim classes".
4. Build one landing page per age group, with price and level on it.
5. Add the 7 negative keywords above before spending a dollar.
6. Call your own tracking number once to confirm it rings and is recorded.
7. Schedule a review at day 60 and day 90. Do not touch budgets in week one.

## What Mistakes Waste Budget?

1. **One campaign all year.** Separate peak and off-season campaigns give you control.
2. **No negative keywords.** Job and informational searches waste budget.
3. **No call or enrollment tracking.** Without it you are guessing.
4. **A weak landing page.** Parents leave when they cannot see levels, price and how to book.
5. **Judging too early.** Give it a few weeks of data before big changes.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a swim school spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no standard figure. Work backward from what you can pay to enroll one family, choose an amount you can sustain for at least a couple of months, and review after 60 to 90 days. Costs per click are usually higher in large cities and in peak season.

**Q: What keywords should a swim school bid on?**
A: Local, high-intent terms such as "swim lessons near me", "kids swim classes [city]", "baby swim classes" and "indoor swim lessons", grouped by age and level. Add negatives for jobs, supplies and informational searches.

**Q: Should I use broad match or exact match?**
A: Start with phrase and exact match for control. Consider broad match later, with conversion tracking and a strong negative list.

**Q: Do I need a website, or can I send ads to a Facebook page?**
A: Use a website or dedicated landing page. Parents want classes, prices, location and a booking option fast, which social pages and directory listings rarely show well. Our [website service](/services/website) can help.

**Q: I tried Google Ads before and it did not work. What should I change?**
A: Check conversion tracking, keyword specificity, negatives, landing-page relevance and whether you read search terms weekly. Small, steady changes beat big, rare ones.
