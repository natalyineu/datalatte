One Google account, one verified profile, one tap. That is roughly what it takes to connect your Business Profile to Gemini, according to Google's own help page, and if you manage two or more verified profiles you are locked out. Google announced the connection on June 10, 2026, and most "AI features for Google Business Profile" articles are still vague about what it does, who gets it, and where it stops.

This guide separates three things: what Google's AI does to your listing without asking you, what the new Gemini connection lets you do, and what you should still do by hand. It is written for owners of local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. One early warning for UK readers: the Gemini connection is not available there.

## Google My Business or Google Business Profile?

Same thing. Google My Business was renamed Google Business Profile, and you now manage it by searching your business name while signed in to Google, or through Google's Business Profile tools. People still search the old name, so this article uses both.

## What Google Announced for Gemini

On June 10, 2026, Google published a post by Vishnu Sivaji, Senior Director of the Gemini app, announcing two things for small businesses: a Business Profile connection and "Business notebooks". [Google's announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-features-for-businesses) says that once you connect, Gemini can use your customer reviews, customer questions and performance data. Google's examples:

- Ask how the month went, and Gemini looks at search impressions, direction requests, call data and engagement.
- Ask for a drafted reply to a recent review that responds to what the customer wrote.
- Ask Gemini to update hours, post seasonal updates, or find gaps in the profile.

Business notebooks are a workspace holding your chats, sources, Business Profile and website, so Gemini keeps context between sessions. Google says notebooks can flag things like unanswered customer questions or holiday hours that are not set yet.

### The fine print that decides whether you get it

Google's [Gemini help page for Business Profile](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17142585) lists the limits, and they matter more than the announcement:

- You must own or manage **only one** verified Business Profile. Multi-location owners and agencies are out.
- You must sign in with a **personal** Google account. Work or school accounts are not supported.
- **Keep Activity** must be turned on, otherwise Gemini cannot connect.
- It is **not available in the EEA or the UK**.
- It works in the Gemini web app, not the mobile apps, and is supported in English and several other languages.
- Rollout is gradual, so it may not have reached your account.

The same help page says Gemini can update hours, contact info, action links, menus, photos and attributes, draft review replies, create posts, summarise reviews and report performance metrics and search keywords. Check the page for current availability, because Google changes these limits.

For a US, Canadian or Australian owner with one location, this is a genuinely useful free assistant. For a UK owner, a multi-site chain or an agency, you will be doing this by hand or with third-party tools.

## What Google's AI Does Without You

Separate from the Gemini connection, AI also shapes how searchers see your listing. Google does not publish a how-to for this, and what is visible varies by country and category, so treat the following as things to watch, not features to switch on:

- **Review-based summaries and highlights** in Search and Maps. You cannot write them. You influence them through what real customers write.
- **Fake edit and fake review detection.** Google's [prohibited content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) says fake engagement is not allowed and will be removed.

The practical consequence is simple: Google's systems read your profile, your reviews and your photos. Wrong or thin inputs produce wrong or thin outputs.

## The Inputs That Matter

Google's help page on [business information](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that prominence includes the number and rating of reviews. It also says inaccurate information can keep your profile from showing for relevant searches, and that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. So there is no AI shortcut around the basics:

| Profile area | What to do | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary category | Pick the most specific accurate one | It tells Google what kind of business you are |
| Hours | Set regular and holiday hours | Google says to list special hours |
| Attributes | Fill in everything true (parking, Wi-Fi, access) | Google names attributes for details like these |
| Services and products | Short, factual descriptions with prices where you can | Gives summaries real facts to use |
| Photos | Current and real, not stock | Customers judge you on them |
| Reviews | A steady flow, each one answered | Prominence, plus the source of any summary |

## Use Gemini for These Four Jobs

If you qualify, these are the tasks where an assistant earns its keep.

### 1. The monthly readout

Ask: "How did my business perform last month compared with the month before? What changed?" Then check the numbers yourself against the Performance screen in your profile. An AI summary of your calls and direction requests is only as good as its reading of the data, and it can be wrong. Our guide to [Google My Business insights](/blog/google-my-business-insights) shows what each metric means.

### 2. Review reply drafts

Google's [review help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) says replies are public and should be polite, clear and brief, and that generic thank-you messages are not helpful; reply where you can add an update or answer a question. A good prompt: "Draft a reply to this review. Mention the dog's name and the nail trim. Keep it under 50 words. Offer to fix the wait time they mentioned." Then edit it so it sounds like you. Read our review reply workflow before automating anything.

### 3. Gap spotting

Ask Gemini to list what is missing from your profile: attributes, services, menu items, holiday hours. Then fill the gaps and verify each edit in the dashboard. Do not let it edit hours without you reading the result, because wrong hours generate bad reviews fast.

### 4. Post drafts

Ask for three short posts for next month based on real events: a new class, a seasonal menu, a booking deadline. Add a real photo. See our guide to [Google Business Profile posts](/blog/google-business-profile-posts).

<Callout type="coffee" title="Facts In, Edit Out">
Give the assistant facts you know are true and ask it to write around them. Never ask it to invent awards, prices or claims, and never publish a draft unread.
</Callout>

## What Not to Do

Google's [contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) is blunt about reviews, and AI makes it easier to slip:

- No incentives. The policy bars offering "payment, discounts, free goods and/or services" in exchange for reviews, and says content posted due to an incentive will be removed.
- No review gating. You may not "selectively solicit positive reviews". Ask every customer, not just the happy ones.
- No pressuring customers to write a review while on your premises.
- No fake or AI-written reviews, and no pasting the same reply everywhere.

You can make asking easy, though. Google says to give customers a link or QR code to your review form. See how to get more 5-star Google reviews for scripts that stay inside the rules.

## Limits and Cautions

- AI summaries can be wrong or out of date. Fix the profile and report the issue to Google.
- Availability is uneven by country, account type and number of locations.
- AI cannot fix a suspended profile. Misleading names, fake addresses and incentivised reviews cause suspensions.
- Customers still trust real photos and honest replies more than polished text.

## Do This Today

1. Check whether you qualify: one verified profile, a personal Google account, Keep Activity on, and not in the UK or EEA. Read Google's Gemini help page for the current rules.
2. If you do, connect the profile in the Gemini web app and ask for last month's performance. Compare it with your Performance screen before believing it.
3. Ask Gemini to list gaps in your profile, then fix at least three: categories, attributes, services or holiday hours.
4. Reply to your five newest unanswered reviews, using AI drafts you rewrite with one specific detail each.
5. Put a 20-minute reminder in your calendar for the first Monday of every month to repeat steps 2 to 4.

## FAQ

**Q: Does Google Business Profile have a built-in AI assistant?**
A: Since June 2026, Google lets you connect a Business Profile to the Gemini app, where it can draft review replies, analyse performance and help update the listing. It has eligibility limits: one verified profile, a personal account, and no availability in the UK or EEA at launch.

**Q: Can I control the AI summary of my reviews?**
A: Not directly. You influence it through the reviews real customers leave, the accuracy of your profile and the replies you write. Do not incentivise reviews or ask only happy customers.

**Q: Is it safe to let Gemini change my hours or details?**
A: Google says it can update hours, contact info, photos and more, but check every change in the dashboard afterwards. Wrong hours are an expensive mistake.

**Q: I run several locations. Can I use this?**
A: Not at the moment. Google's help page says the feature is unavailable if you have access to more than one verified profile.

**Q: How often should I update my profile?**
A: Review it monthly, and update straight away when hours, services or contact details change.
