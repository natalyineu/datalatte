AI features in Google Business Profile can only be as accurate as the listing they read. Google's guidelines set the baseline: keep one profile per business, use your real-world name, choose the fewest categories that describe your core business, and keep the description free of links, promotions and prices. Before you rely on any AI feature, fix those basics, then check the current rules for the feature itself on Google's help pages. This article covers the profile rules that apply whatever tools you use.

## What rules does your profile have to follow?

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say that a profile can be suspended if it does not follow them. The core rules are short. Represent your business as it is consistently known in the real world, on signage, stationery and other branding. Make your address or service area accurate and precise. Choose the fewest categories that describe your overall core business. Keep only one profile per business, because duplicates can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search.

These rules matter more when AI is involved. Any summary or draft built from your listing repeats whatever the listing says, including errors. A profile with a duplicate, a wrong address or a stack of loosely related categories gives automated tools weak material to work with.

## How should you name and categorise the profile?

Google's name rules are strict. The name should match the business as customers know it. Taglines, store codes, hours, phone numbers, website addresses and fully capitalised words are not allowed in the name field. Google's own example is a 24-hour coffee shop called Shelly's Coffee, entered with the name Shelly's Coffee, the street address, hours of "Open 24 hours" and the category Coffee shop. The hours go in the hours section, not the name.

Categories follow the same logic. Google asks for the fewest categories that describe the core business, so a pet groomer that also sells treats should pick the grooming category first and add secondary categories only where they truly describe a service. ## What should the business description leave out?

The description is the one free-text field an AI tool is most likely to reuse, so Google's limits here matter. Google's guidelines say the description should use useful information on services, products, mission and history. It should not include low-quality or gimmicky text, special promotions, prices or sale offers, and it should not include links of any type. Google's examples of disallowed copy include "Everything on sale, -50%" and "Best bagels in town for $5!"

Write the description as plain facts about what you do and who you serve. Put prices and offers in the services or posts sections, where they can be checked and updated, rather than in the description. Check the text for misspellings too, since Google lists those as a quality problem.

## How long before changes show up?

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says every change takes time to be reflected. Some take effect within hours, others take months. It recommends waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change worked, and says that not every change will show a visible impact. That guidance is written about websites, but the same patience applies to a listing.

The practical rule: make one batch of profile fixes, record the date, then review your Google Business Profile performance and search results after a few weeks. Changing the same fields every few days makes it impossible to tell which edit helped.

## Which checks should you run this week?

1. Confirm there is only one profile for your business, and that your address and service area match where you work.
2. Compare the business name with your signage and invoices. Remove any tagline, store code, hours or phone number from the name field.
3. Review your categories and keep only the fewest that describe your core business.
4. Read the description and remove any links, promotions, prices or sale wording.
5. Note today's date, then recheck the profile and your search visibility after a few weeks.

## Which AI feature rules should you check yourself?

This article does not list eligibility rules, availability by country or account requirements for individual AI features, because the sources used here do not cover them. Google changes these rules, so check the current Google Business Profile and Gemini help pages before you plan around any feature. Do not assume a feature is available in your country or on your account type.

Whatever the feature, the same limits apply. An AI tool cannot override Google's content guidelines, so a draft that adds links, prices or false claims to the description breaks the rules regardless of who wrote it. Edit every AI output against the guidelines above before publishing.

## FAQ

### Does a single profile matter for AI features?

Yes. Google's guidelines say there should be only one profile per business, because duplicates can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search. Any tool reading your listing will then pull from a clean, single source.

### Can I put my prices in the business description?

Google's guidelines say the description should not focus on prices or special offers. Put prices in the services section, where they can be updated and checked more easily.

### How long should I wait before judging a profile change?

Google's SEO Starter Guide recommends waiting a few weeks before assessing a change, since some changes take hours and others take months to show. Record the date of each batch of edits so you can compare results.

### Do I need to add links to my description?

No. Google's guidelines say no links of any type are allowed in the business description. Keep your website link in the website field instead.
