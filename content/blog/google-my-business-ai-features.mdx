Google does not offer a single "AI mode" inside Google My Business (now called Google Business Profile), but AI now shapes how your listing appears and gives you a few tools to manage it. Your job is to keep your profile accurate, review-rich and complete so Google's AI features have good material to work with.

Features change often and roll out unevenly by country and business category, so treat this article as a guide to what is typically available and how to prepare, and check your own dashboard for what you can see.

## A quick note on the name

Google My Business was rebranded to Google Business Profile, and the old standalone app has been retired. You now manage your profile directly in Google Search and Maps (search for your business name while signed in) or through the Google Business Profile dashboard. We use both names here because many owners still search for "Google My Business".

## Where AI touches your profile

AI shows up in a few distinct places, and it helps to separate what Google does to your listing from what you can use yourself.

### What Google does with AI

- **Summaries of reviews and place details**: Google surfaces short summaries and highlights in Maps and Search drawn from reviews, photos and profile information. You cannot write these directly. You influence them by what customers say and what your profile contains.
- **AI-generated answers in Search and Maps**: Searches phrased as questions (for example "quiet cafe with laptop-friendly seating near me") can return AI-assisted results built from listings, reviews and web content.
- **Image and attribute understanding**: Google analyses photos and text to match your business to specific needs, such as dishes, amenities or services.
- **Spam and fake-review detection**: Automated systems flag suspicious reviews, edits and listings.

### What you can use

- **Profile suggestions and prompts**: The dashboard may suggest missing information, such as attributes, services or hours.
- **Review reply helpers**: Depending on your account and region, you may see suggested replies. Always edit them so they sound like you and refer to specifics.
- **Performance insights**: You see how people found you, which searches they used, and how many tapped for calls, directions or website visits. See our guide to [Google My Business insights](/blog/google-my-business-insights).
- **Third-party AI tools**: Many tools use AI to draft posts, replies and descriptions, and you paste the result in. These are not Google features, but they are often more flexible.

## Why the inputs matter more than the tools

AI-driven results rely on structured, consistent information. If your profile is incomplete or contradicts your website, Google has less confidence about when to show you. The checklist below covers the basics:

| Profile area | What to do | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary category | Choose the most specific accurate one | Drives which searches you match |
| Secondary categories | Add those that genuinely apply | Broadens relevance |
| Services and products | List them with short descriptions and prices where possible | Gives AI concrete things to match |
| Attributes | Fill in everything that applies (Wi-Fi, outdoor seating, wheelchair access) | Matches "near me" needs |
| Description | Plain, factual, specific | Helps humans and algorithms |
| Photos | Real, current, varied (exterior, interior, team, products) | Used in image understanding |
| Hours | Accurate, with holiday updates | Prevents bad experiences and negative reviews |
| Reviews | Steady flow with specific detail, with replies | Feeds the summaries customers see |

## Shape the review summary people see

Because review summaries are built from what customers write, the best way to influence them is to ask more customers for reviews and make it easy for them to be specific.

- Send a direct review link by text or email soon after the visit.
- Ask a natural question, such as "What did you enjoy most?", rather than asking for a certain wording. Never script or incentivise reviews, which breaks Google's policies.
- Reply to every review, mentioning relevant details like the service or product. This also gives readers context.

More detail is in our guide to [getting more 5-star Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-5-star-google-reviews-local-business).

## Use AI tools wisely for drafting

AI writing tools such as ChatGPT or Claude can save time on routine tasks. Reasonable uses include:

- Drafting a first version of your business description from a few bullet points
- Writing short product or service descriptions
- Suggesting post ideas for each month
- Drafting replies to reviews that you then personalise

Rules of thumb:

1. Give the tool facts. Do not ask it to invent claims, awards or prices.
2. Edit everything so it sounds like you.
3. Never paste generic text that could describe any business.
4. Do not use AI to write fake reviews or to respond to reviews with copy-paste text.
5. Keep descriptions within Google's character limits and avoid stuffing keywords.

## Posts, photos and Q&A

Regular activity signals a business that is open and cared for. Aim for:

- A post every week or two: offers, events, new items, seasonal hours. See [Google Business Profile posts](/blog/google-business-profile-posts).
- New photos monthly. Real, well-lit images outperform polished stock images in terms of customer trust.
- Answers to the common questions customers ask, on your profile and website.

## Watch your insights and act on them

Check performance monthly. Look at:

- Which queries lead people to your listing (branded vs. generic)
- Calls, direction requests and website clicks
- Photo views compared with similar businesses
- Review count and rating trends

If direction requests drop after a hours change, or generic searches rise after you add services, you have learned something useful. Our guide to [Google My Business analytics](/blog/google-my-business-analytics) explains how to read them.

## Limits and cautions

- AI summaries can be wrong or outdated. If you spot an error, update your profile and, where possible, report the issue to Google.
- Not every business sees every feature. Availability depends on country, category and account status.
- Policy compliance matters. Misleading names, fake addresses or incentivised reviews risk suspension, which no AI tool will fix.
- Automation is not a substitute for a real local presence. Customers still trust real people, real photos and honest replies.

## FAQ

**Q: Does Google My Business have a built-in AI assistant?**
A: Google has added AI-assisted features across Search, Maps and the business dashboard, and what you see varies by region and account. There is no single toggle. Check your profile dashboard for suggestions and prompts.

**Q: Can I control the AI summary of my reviews?**
A: Not directly. You influence it indirectly through the reviews customers leave, the information on your profile and the content on your website.

**Q: Is it safe to use ChatGPT to write my business description?**
A: Yes, as a drafting aid, as long as you supply accurate facts, edit the output and make sure it follows Google's guidelines.

**Q: How often should I update my profile?**
A: Review it at least monthly, and update immediately when hours, services or contact details change.
