A hair salon should run Facebook ads as a small, local lead or traffic campaign pointed at one offer, such as a first-visit price or a colour consultation. LocaliQ's benchmarks, updated September 23, 2026, put Beauty and Personal Care lead campaigns at $2.97 per click and $50.91 per lead, and traffic campaigns at $0.50 per click. Test one offer, track bookings, and keep the ads going only if cost per new client is lower than the client's value.

This guide covers the objective to pick, what Meta ads cost for salons and a worked budget. Meta's own policy and help pages could not be read for this article, so policy details are not stated. Figures come from the sources at the end, and examples are labelled assumptions.

## How Much Do Facebook Ads Cost for a Hair Salon?

For Beauty and Personal Care, LocaliQ lists traffic campaigns at a 2.73% click-through rate and $0.50 per click. Lead campaigns have a 1.35% click-through rate, $2.97 per click, a 5.63% conversion rate and $50.91 per lead. Across all industries lead campaigns average $1.80 per click and $27.39 per lead.

The beauty category is above the all-industry lead average. LocaliQ does not define a "lead" for each industry, and a form fill is not a booked appointment. Treat the numbers as a planning range.

## Should a Salon Run Traffic or Lead Campaigns?

A traffic campaign sends people to your booking page and is cheap per click. A lead campaign collects a name and phone number inside Meta, which costs more per click but gives you a contact to call or text.

Pick traffic if your online booking works well on a phone. Pick leads if you book by phone or message. The wrong choice here wastes more money than any targeting tweak. Our [guide to Facebook ads budgets](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) explains how to size either one.

## What Offer Should the Ad Make?

Use an offer a new client can act on in one step, such as a first-visit price, a colour consultation or a gap-fill slot this week. Show real work, not stock photos, and name the neighbourhood.

Do not claim results you cannot show, and do not invent reviews. Check Meta's current advertising standards before using before-and-after images, since the policy could not be read for this article. Our [personalised Facebook ads guide](/blog/personalized-facebook-ads-for-hair-salons) covers message variants.

## Who Should You Target?

Start with a radius around the salon, set in the ad's location setting, and an age and gender range that matches your clients. Keep interests broad. Add custom audiences from your client list and past messages only after you have enough data.

An existing client list can seed a lookalike, but the source must be big enough. Our [lookalike audiences guide](/blog/facebook-lookalike-audiences-guide) covers the limits Meta states.

## How Do You Know It Worked?

Track form leads, booking-page visits and phone calls separately, and ask every new client how they found you. Compare cost per new client to what you earn from a client over a year.

## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Facebook ads?
No source publishes a standard budget. With a $2.97 lead-campaign click, $400 buys roughly 135 clicks. Choose a test amount you can lose and scale on cost per new client.

### Do Facebook ads work for hair salons?
They can bring new clients when the offer is clear and follow-up is fast. No public source reports salon-specific results, so test and measure.

### Should I run lead ads or send people to my booking page?
Use leads if you book by phone or message, and traffic if online booking works on mobile.

### How long should I run a test?
Long enough to collect a meaningful number of leads. Judge on booked clients, not clicks.

### Do I need Instagram too?
Meta ads can run on both. Organic Instagram helps clients check your work before booking.
