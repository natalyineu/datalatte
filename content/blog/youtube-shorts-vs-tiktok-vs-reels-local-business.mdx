For most local businesses, start with Instagram Reels if customers already follow you there, add YouTube Shorts for search and longer shelf life, and use TikTok to reach new people. You do not need to pick one: film once, post to all three, and keep the platform that brings bookings. On the example below, a 5.5-hour monthly routine across three apps breaks even at about two new customers.

## Which Short-Form Video Platform Reaches More Local Customers?

By raw reach, YouTube is largest. Pew Research Center's [Americans' Social Media Use 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/) survey, as summarised by [JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/pew-latest-social-report/) (second-hand), found 84% of US adults use YouTube and 50% use Instagram. [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/413192/tiktok-draws-over-a-third-of-us-adults-pew-repo.html) reported TikTok at 37%, roughly double its 2021 level.

Daily habits differ. The same JCK summary gives 48% daily use for YouTube and 24% for TikTok, from a separate February to March 2025 survey of 5,123 adults. It gives no daily figure for Instagram.

Reach is not the same as local reach. All three apps show videos to people outside your area, so a large audience does not mean nearby customers. Your own customers matter more: where do they already look for businesses like yours?

## How Do the Three Platforms Compare?

| | YouTube Shorts | TikTok | Instagram Reels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Share of US adults who use the app | 84% (YouTube) | 37% | 50% (Instagram) |
| Maximum length | 3 minutes | Check in the app | 3 minutes |
| Business music rule | Shorts over 1 minute with a copyright claim are blocked | Business accounts must use the Commercial Music Library | Check Meta's current music rules |
| Ad route | Google Ads (several campaign types) | TikTok Ads Manager | Meta Ads |
| Local ad targeting | Radius, set at campaign level in Demand Gen | City, DMA, county, zip code | Through Meta Ads location settings |

Length limits are set out on YouTube's [three-minute Shorts page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en). Instagram announced three-minute Reels on 18 January 2025, according to [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-officially-expands-reels-length-3-minutes/737766/) (second-hand), which also said Instagram's advice was still to keep Reels under 90 seconds. That advice may have changed, so check it in the app.

For all three, a short, vertical, captioned clip with the result in the first two seconds is the safe default.

## Where Do Small Accounts Get More Views?

Only partial evidence exists, and it is not a like-for-like test. Socialinsider's [TikTok benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/tiktok) show accounts with 1,000 to 5,000 followers averaged 350 views per post in 2025, down from 860 in 2024. Its [Instagram benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram) show Reels from accounts with 1,000 to 5,000 followers averaged 580 views in 2025.

Both datasets are large (about 2 million TikTok videos, 35 million Instagram posts) but cover different accounts and methods, so the gap of 580 against 350 views is a hint, not a ranking. Socialinsider does not publish a comparable YouTube Shorts benchmark.

Engagement rates are not comparable either. TikTok's 4.40% for small accounts is measured against views. Instagram's 0.52% for Reels in 2025 is measured differently. Do not read one as ten times better than the other.

The practical takeaway: expect hundreds of views per video on a small account on any platform. Judge each app by calls, bookings and direction requests, not by views.

## Which Platform Fits My Type of Business?

These are decision rules based on how each app is used, not guaranteed outcomes:

- **Your customers already follow you on Instagram:** start with Reels. Existing followers see the video, and booking links sit in the same app. See our [Instagram Reels for business guide](/blog/instagram-reels-for-business).
- **Customers search for how-to answers before they call** (plumbing, electrical, cleaning, pet care): lead with YouTube Shorts. YouTube is also where longer answers can live later.
- **You want to reach people who do not know you yet** (a new studio, café or salon): test TikTok, then compare it with Instagram in our [TikTok vs Instagram ads guide](/blog/tiktok-vs-instagram-ads-local-business-2026).
- **You have under two hours a week:** run one platform for eight weeks before adding another.

Whichever you choose, set up the account properly first. Our [TikTok for business account guide](/blog/tiktok-for-business-account-guide) covers TikTok, and a business or creator account on Instagram gives you insights.

## What Do the Music Rules Mean for a Business?

Music trips up more local accounts than editing does, because each app treats business use differently.

On TikTok, [the Commercial Music Library page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library) says businesses cannot use the general music library for commercial purposes. It offers a pre-cleared catalogue of 1 million songs and lists organic content, including duets and stitches, among the covered activities.

On YouTube, the [Shorts help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en) says most songs in the Shorts audio library can be used for up to 90 seconds of a three-minute Short. Shorts over one minute with an active copyright claim are blocked globally, along with their monetisation. Royalty-free tracks from the YouTube Audio Library do not receive a Content ID claim.

The simplest rule for all three: use your own voice or licensed business-safe audio, and add captions so the video works with the sound off.

## Can You Run Ads on Shorts, TikTok and Reels?

Yes, and the entry costs differ. TikTok's [budget page](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/budget?lang=en) requires a daily budget above $20 at ad group level, so a one-month test is about $600. Its [location targeting page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/location-targeting) lists city, DMA, county and zip code options.

Google's [Shorts ads page](https://business.google.com/us/ad-solutions/youtube-ads/shorts-ads/) lists Video Reach, Video View, Demand Gen, App and Performance Max campaigns as routes. Only Video Reach and Video View can run exclusively on Shorts. It recommends 9:16 video at 1080x1920 and under 60 seconds, and says to keep text and calls to action out of the top 10%, bottom 25% and right 10% of the frame. For a radius around your shop, [Demand Gen](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13695389?hl=en) offers it at campaign level only.

For Reels, you buy through Meta Ads. Our [Meta Ads service](/services/meta-ads) page explains how DataLatte sets up local campaigns.

## What Does a Three-Platform Routine Cost? A Worked Example

Every input is an **assumption**. Replace it with your own.

| | One platform | Three platforms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Filming (6 clips, one session) | 2 h | 2 h |
| Editing the clips once | 2 h | 2 h |
| Posting (6 clips x 5 min per platform) | 0.5 h | 1.5 h |
| Total monthly time | 4.5 h | 5.5 h |
| Time cost at $25/h | $112.50 | $137.50 |
| Profit per new customer ($150 first job x 40% margin) | $60 | $60 |
| Customers needed to break even | 1.9 | 2.3 |

The extra two platforms add one hour, about $25, a month, or 0.4 of a customer. That is why filming once and posting everywhere is a reasonable test. After eight weeks, compare booking-link clicks and "how did you hear about us?" answers per platform, then drop the weakest. ## FAQ

**Q: Do I need all three platforms?**
A: No. One platform done consistently beats three done badly. Start with the one your customers already use, then repost the same clips elsewhere if it costs under an hour a month.

**Q: Which is best for a café, salon or restaurant?**
A: Visual businesses usually start with Reels or TikTok because customers browse them for ideas. Test for eight weeks and compare bookings, not views.

**Q: How long should a Short or Reel be?**
A: Shorts and Reels can now run to three minutes. Instagram advised keeping Reels under 90 seconds, according to Social Media Today in January 2025. Short clips with the result up front are the safer start.

**Q: Can I post the same video on all three?**
A: Yes, but check each app's music rules and remove watermarks or text that sits in the screen areas apps cover. Re-export the clip for each platform.

**Q: Should I run ads or stay organic?**
A: Stay organic until you know which clips produce bookings. Then promote the best one. TikTok's ad minimum is about $20 a day per ad group.
