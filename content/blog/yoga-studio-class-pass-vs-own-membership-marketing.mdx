**Is ClassPass or your own membership better for a yoga studio?** For most studios, your own membership is the better long-term growth engine because you keep the customer relationship, the data and the full price. ClassPass can still be useful in a limited role: filling off-peak classes and introducing new people to your studio, as long as you treat it as a lead source and not as your business model.

The right answer depends on how full your classes are, what ClassPass pays you per visit in your market, and whether you have a way to convert drop-ins into members. Below is how to compare them using your own numbers.

## How does ClassPass work for a studio?

ClassPass is a marketplace where customers book classes across many studios. Studios are paid per booking under terms that vary by market and contract, so always read your agreement carefully.

**Pros**

- Exposure to a large pool of people actively looking for classes
- Can fill empty off-peak spots at little marginal cost
- No upfront marketing spend to get a first visit

**Cons**

- Payout per visit is usually lower than your drop-in or membership rate
- You get limited customer data and weak control over the relationship
- You depend on a third party's pricing, algorithm and policy changes

## What does building your own membership involve?

An in-house membership gives you recurring revenue and the full relationship, but you carry the marketing and retention work yourself.

**Pros**

- Full control of customer data, communication and pricing
- Higher revenue per visit, since there is no marketplace cut
- Flexibility to design tiers, intro offers and perks

**Cons**

- You pay to acquire members (ads, local SEO, referrals)
- You need systems for billing, booking and follow-up
- Growth is slower at the start

## How to compare revenue using your own numbers

Instead of trusting generic percentages, plug your own figures into a simple comparison. The table below shows the inputs to gather.

| Input | ClassPass visit | Own membership |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|
| Revenue per visit | Your ClassPass payout per booking (check your contract) | Monthly fee divided by average visits per member per month |
| Acquisition cost | Often none per visit | Ad spend plus time per new member |
| Typical visits per customer | Varies; many users rotate between studios | Usually steadier for committed members |
| Customer data you keep | Limited | Full |
| Repeat purchase | Not guaranteed to you | Recurring billing |

An illustrative example with round numbers (replace with yours): if a member pays $120 a month and attends 6 times, your revenue per visit is $20. If ClassPass pays you $12 per booking, you need about two-thirds more ClassPass visits to match that revenue, and you still do not own the relationship.

<Callout type="tip">When building your own membership program, consider offering tiered pricing, such as an intro pack, a limited monthly plan and an unlimited plan, to suit different customers.</Callout>

## Can you use both?

Yes, and many studios do. A practical hybrid approach:

1. Limit ClassPass to off-peak or low-attendance classes so it never displaces full-price customers.
2. Capture every ClassPass visitor's attention in person: a welcome card, a first-month offer, and a clear path from drop-in to membership.
3. Review your numbers every quarter and reduce ClassPass inventory as direct memberships grow.
4. Keep your direct price clearly better value per class than the marketplace, so regulars have a reason to switch.

## Drawbacks and limits to keep in mind

ClassPass users are often exploring many studios, so loyalty can be harder to build. In-house programs ask more of you in upfront marketing and ongoing retention. Neither is a shortcut.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does ClassPass pay a studio per visit?**
A: It varies by market, studio and contract, and terms can change. Ask for the current payout structure in writing and compare it to your own drop-in and membership revenue per visit.

**Q: Can I get my client data out of ClassPass?**
A: Typically you get limited information about people who book your classes, which makes retargeting and long-term relationship building harder. Check your agreement for what you may use and how.

**Q: Should I price my membership above or below ClassPass?**
A: Your membership should offer clearly better value per class for regular attendees while feeling like a premium experience, with perks such as priority booking or member-only events. Avoid pricing so low that you undercut your own drop-in rate.

**Q: How long does it take for an in-house membership to pay off?**
A: It depends on your starting audience and acquisition cost. Track cost to acquire a member against monthly revenue and expected months of membership to see your payback period.

**Q: Do I need special software to run a membership?**
A: You need reliable recurring billing, class booking and email. Choose a studio platform that fits your size and budget, and upgrade as you grow.
