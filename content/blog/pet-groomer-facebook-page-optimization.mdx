Socialinsider's 2026 Facebook benchmarks, built on 25 million posts from 130,683 pages, show that a page with 1,000 to 5,000 followers averages about 60 views for a status or image post and 85 for an album. That is not a typo. Your Facebook page is not a billboard, and nobody is going to stumble onto it and book a full groom. What it does well is something quieter: it is where a new client checks you out after a friend says "you should try her", and where an existing client sees "two Thursday slots just opened".

This guide is for salon-based and mobile groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It sticks to what Meta's help pages and published benchmarks confirm, and labels the rest as my advice. If you are still setting up your Google listing, do that first: our [Google Business Profile guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers) covers the channel that usually sends strangers to you. Facebook is the trust layer on top.

## Why Bother With Facebook at All

Two reasons, both from data rather than hype.

First, your clients are there. Pew Research Center's social media fact sheet (survey fielded February to June 2025) found 71% of US adults use Facebook, including 80% of 30 to 49 year olds and 74% of 50 to 64 year olds. Those are the ages most likely to own a dog that needs regular grooming. The American Pet Products Association says 95 million US households own a pet. Results will differ in the UK, Australia and Canada, but the pattern of older, pet-owning Facebook users is similar.

Second, reach is tiny, which tells you how to use it. Socialinsider puts average engagement at 0.15% (reactions, comments and shares divided by fans), and views by page size rise sharply from the 60 to 85 range for 1,000 to 5,000 followers to well over 800 for pages of 100,000 or more. A small page cannot win a reach contest. It can win a credibility and rebooking contest, because a client who sees a post is already half sold.

## Step 1: Complete the Page Foundations

Open your Page and fill in every field. Each is worth a minute.

- **Name:** your real business name. Add your town only if it is part of how you are known ("Paws & Bubbles Dog Grooming, Leeds").
- **Category:** choose the closest grooming or pet service category Facebook offers.
- **Profile photo and cover photo:** a clear logo or you with a dog for the profile photo, which shows small. A bright real photo of the salon or a freshly groomed dog for the cover.
- **Address, phone, hours, website:** match your Google listing exactly. If you are mobile, state your service area rather than showing a home address.
- **About text:** what you groom (dogs, cats, large breeds, anxious pets), which areas you cover and how to book, in the first two lines.
- **Username:** short and easy to print on a card.

## Step 2: Add a Booking Path With the Action Button

Meta's help page says to add the button from your Page: click Options, then Add action button or Edit action button, pick a button, and save. The help page does not list which buttons are available for your Page, and that depends on your setup, so look at the options on your own screen.

Choose the button that matches how you really take bookings:

- Booking software (Square Appointments, Fresha, MoeGo, Gingr): link the button to your booking page if a booking or link option is offered.
- Phone or message bookings: use a call or message button.
- Test it on your phone, logged out, once a month. A dead button is worse than no button.

Then pin a post that says how to book, your "from" prices, and what a new client needs to bring (vaccination records, previous groomer notes). Pinned posts are my recommendation, not a ranking factor.

## Step 3: Set Up Messages the Right Way

Most pet owners message before booking: "How much for a doodle?" "Do you take anxious dogs?" Fast clear answers win those bookings.

- **Instant reply:** thank them, ask breed, size, coat condition and the service they want. Meta Business Suite handles automated responses in the Inbox. Menu names change, so search the inbox settings for "Automations" or "Instant reply".
- **Saved replies:** write them once for prices, vaccination rules, how long a groom takes, deposits and cancellations.
- **Response time:** Meta says the response time shown on your Page defaults to your calculated average, and admins can control what shows publicly ("within minutes", "within an hour", "within hours" or "within a day"). It also says messages received while your Page is set to Away do not count toward your response rate. So if you are mid-groom with a nervous terrier, set Away deliberately instead of letting messages sit.

Set a rule for yourself: every message gets a human reply within a few hours in business hours. Within an hour is better. That is my target, not a Meta rule.

## Step 4: Post What Groomers' Clients Respond To

On a page averaging 60 views a post, you are posting to the people who matter most: your existing clients and their friends. Aim for 8 to 12 posts a month (my suggestion), mixed:

- **Before and afters** with the owner's permission. Albums are the format Socialinsider found got the most views at both small and large page sizes (85 on average for 1,000 to 5,000 followers), so a three-photo reveal is worth the extra minute.
- **Availability posts:** "Two Thursday slots just opened, message me." Specific beats "book now".
- **Meet the client:** pet name, breed, one line of story.
- **Seasonal care:** matting after wet weeks, paw care in winter, shedding in spring.
- **Behind the scenes:** the salon, your tools, how you calm an anxious dog.

Avoid link-only posts. Socialinsider's 2025 data had link posts at 0.05% engagement, a third of the 0.15% overall average. Put the booking link in the pinned post and the button, not every caption.

Always ask permission before posting a client's pet or the owner. If you also use Instagram, schedule both from Meta Business Suite and read our Instagram marketing guide for pet groomers.

## Step 5: Earn and Answer Recommendations

Meta's help page says that turning on Recommendations and reviews lets any logged-in user publish a Recommendation on your Page, and that turning the setting off removes the rating and reviews. The setting is a toggle labelled "Allow others to view and leave reviews on your Page?" under Page and tagging in Settings, according to that help page. Meta's developer changelog notes that in 2018 it replaced the 1 to 5 `rating` field with a positive or negative `recommendation_type`, which is why Facebook feels like "yes or no" rather than stars.

What to do:

- Ask at pick-up, when the dog looks great and the owner is smiling. A small card with a QR code that links to your Page's recommendations works.
- Reply to every recommendation. Be specific: use the pet's name.
- Do not offer discounts or free add-ons in exchange for recommendations; Google explicitly bans that for Google reviews, and it is a bad habit on any platform. Our guide on asking grooming clients for Google reviews has wording that stays inside the rules.

## Step 6: Use Local Groups Carefully

Groups like "[Town] Dog Owners" are where recommendations get asked for. Admins set each group's rules, so read them first. Many restrict promotion to a specific thread or day. Answer questions helpfully for a few weeks before mentioning your business, and let happy clients tag you. A neighbour saying "go to her" does more than any post.

## Step 7: Paid Ads Come Last

Once your page, button, messages and recommendations are working, small ads can fill quiet slots. Good starting jobs: a new-client offer to people within a few miles, a rebooking reminder to past clients (uploaded list), or a seasonal package such as de-shedding. Meta does not publish a "right" budget, so any daily figure is a guess. Start small, keep a hard weekly cap and judge it by cost per booked groom. ## The Math

What is a month of posting worth? Treat every row except views as an **assumption**. The 60 views per post is the Socialinsider average for status and image posts on pages with 1,000 to 5,000 followers; use your own Page Insights views instead.

| | Low | Base | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Posts per month (assumption) | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| Views per post (Socialinsider, 1-5K followers) | 60 | 60 | 60 |
| Total views | 480 | 480 | 480 |
| Views that turn into a message (assumption) | 0.5% | 1% | 2% |
| Messages | 2.4 | 4.8 | 9.6 |
| Messages that book (assumption) | 30% | 50% | 60% |
| New bookings | 0.7 | 2.4 | 5.8 |
| Price of a groom (assumption) | $75 | $75 | $75 |
| First-visit revenue | $54 | $180 | $432 |
| 12-month value at 4 visits each (assumption) | $216 | $720 | $1,728 |

Time cost: 8 posts at about 15 minutes each is 2 hours, plus message replies. In the Base case that is $360 of 12-month value per hour of posting, before your costs. In the Low case it is $108 an hour, which is still reasonable but not a reason to neglect the Google listing. Replace the views with your own and watch how many new bookings say "I saw you on Facebook". The result is only as good as your close rate on messages, which is why Step 3 comes before Step 4.

## A Monthly Page Checklist

- Hours, holiday closures and phone number are current
- Pinned post reflects your current offer and booking instructions
- Action button tested on a phone
- Every message and recommendation has a reply
- Eight to twelve posts published, at least two with photo albums
- Page Insights checked: views, messages, link clicks

## FAQ

**Q: Is Facebook still worth it for a pet groomer?**
A: As a credibility and rebooking channel, yes. Pew found 71% of US adults use Facebook. As a way to get new strangers, no: average views are in the dozens for small pages. Treat it as a complement to your Google listing.

**Q: How often should a pet groomer post on Facebook?**
A: Two to three posts a week is a sustainable rhythm for most solo groomers. That is my suggestion, not a platform rule. Consistency matters more than volume.

**Q: Should I use Facebook ads or just post organically?**
A: Organic posting reaches people who already know you. Ads reach new local owners. Fix your page and your message replies first, then test a small budget on one clear offer.

**Q: Do I need Recommendations turned on?**
A: Meta says turning them on lets any logged-in user publish a Recommendation and see your rating, and turning them off removes them. Most groomers leave them on because owners read them, but you need to be ready to reply to each one.

**Q: What if Facebook shows "away" or a slow response time?**
A: Meta says admins control the public response time label and that messages received while Away do not count toward your response rate. Use Away honestly, then clear the backlog when you are out of the salon.
