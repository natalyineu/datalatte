To track marketing ROI for a local business, record what each channel cost, count the customers it brought, multiply by what those customers paid, and subtract the cost. ROI is profit from a channel divided by its cost. ROAS, the figure ad platforms show, is revenue divided by ad cost, so it ignores your margin. A 4x ROAS can still lose money.

This guide shows five tracking steps that work for a shop, studio or service firm without a data team. Figures come from the sources at the end; examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Is the Difference Between ROI and ROAS?

Google's own help defines target ROAS as conversion value divided by ad spend: its example is $5 in sales for $1 of ads, a 500% ROAS. That is revenue, not profit.

ROI subtracts costs. If a job earns $150 and costs you $90 to deliver, the profit is $60, and ROI is measured against the money you put into marketing. Use ROAS to compare ad campaigns and ROI to decide whether a channel deserves more money.

| Measure | Formula | Answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost per lead | Spend divided by leads | Is the channel cheap to try? |
| Cost per customer | Spend divided by new customers | Does it bring buyers? |
| ROAS | Revenue divided by ad spend | Which ads sell most? |
| ROI | (Profit minus spend) divided by spend | Should I spend more? |

## How Do You Tag Every Link with UTM Parameters?

Add UTM tags to every link you control: emails, social bios, QR codes, ad URLs. Google Analytics help says to always use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, with utm_term and utm_content optional.

The values are case sensitive, so "google" and "Google" show as two sources. Pick lowercase names and keep a short spreadsheet of them. Our [UTM guide](/blog/utm-parameters-track-every-marketing-campaign) has a ready naming scheme.

## How Do You Set Up Conversions in Google Analytics 4?

Mark the actions that matter as key events: a booking form sent, a call button tapped, an order placed. Google defines a key event as an event that measures an action particularly important to the success of your business.

You can create a Google Ads conversion from a GA4 key event so both tools count the same action. Google notes that Google Ads conversions do not appear in standard Analytics reports, so expect two views that need reading side by side.

GA4 sees web visits only. For walk-ins and phone orders, use the next two steps. See also the [analytics guide for local businesses](/blog/local-business-analytics-and-tracking).

## How Do You Track Phone Calls?

For many local firms the phone is the main conversion. Google Ads help lists five phone conversion types, among them calls from ads, calls to a forwarding number on your website, and imported call conversions from a CRM. You set a minimum call length, and every call at least that long counts.

Forwarding numbers are needed for calls from ads and website calls. Choose a length that reflects a genuine enquiry, not a wrong number. Then mark in your own log which calls became customers.

## How Do You Track Walk-Ins and Offline Sales?

Ask one question at checkout or on the booking form: "How did you hear about us?" Give five answers to pick from, such as Google, Instagram, a friend, a flyer, other. Log them in your till or a notebook.

A promo code for each channel adds a second signal, but no code system captures everyone. Treat the tally as a direction, then compare it with GA4 and call data. When they disagree, trust the one closest to cash received.

## How Do You Compare Channels Fairly?

Use the same period and the same definition of a customer for every channel. Include everything you paid: ad spend, tool fees, printing, and your time at a fair rate.

| Channel | Cost to count | What to log |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Ads | Ad spend, agency fee | Calls, forms, cost per customer |
| Social ads | Ad spend, design | Link clicks with UTM, replies, bookings |
| Email and SMS | Tool fee, discounts | Revenue from tagged links or codes |
| Google Business Profile | Your time | Calls, direction requests, bookings |
| Referrals | Rewards given | Referred customers |

The [metrics guide](/blog/which-marketing-metrics-matter-local-business) explains which of these numbers to watch weekly.

## The Math: A $1,000 Ad Month

All inputs are **assumptions** except the cost per lead, which is the LocaliQ all-industry average of $66.69.

| | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Ad spend | $1,000 |
| Leads at $66.69 each | about 15 |
| Leads who become customers (assumption) | 40% |
| New customers | 6 |
| Revenue per customer, first visit (assumption) | $150 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 40% |
| Revenue | $900 |
| ROAS | 0.9 |
| Gross profit | $360 |
| ROI on first visit | minus 64% |

Same spend, but if each customer returns three times in the year (assumption), revenue is $2,700, gross profit $1,080 and ROI is about plus 8%. Repeat business decides the result, so log returns as well as first orders.

## FAQ

### I am a one-person shop. Do I need all of this?

Start with one question: did this channel make more profit than it cost? A tally of how customers found you, plus ad spend, answers it. Add GA4 and call tracking when spend grows.

### What if customers do not use promo codes?

Codes only capture some customers. Add the checkout question and compare. Expect gaps and use them to spot direction, not exact totals.

### Why does the platform say 4x ROAS when my bank balance disagrees?

ROAS counts revenue, not profit, and platforms may count conversions you would have had anyway. Subtract cost of goods and fixed costs. At a 25% margin, $4,000 of revenue from $1,000 of ads leaves $1,000 gross profit, which only breaks even.

### Can GA4 track everything?

No. It records web activity. Phone calls need call tracking and in-person sales need your till or a checkout question.

### How do I track Yelp or directory ads?

Use a separate tracking number or landing page for each source, and ask callers how they found you. Compare with the platform report.

### Should seasonal businesses track differently?

Track the same metrics year round and compare cost per lead and per customer across seasons, not total spend. See also [how to measure website ROI](/blog/website-roi-local-business).
