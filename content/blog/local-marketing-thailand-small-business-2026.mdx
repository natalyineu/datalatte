For most Thai small businesses, the working mix is a LINE Official Account (LINE OA) for direct customer messages, Facebook for discovery and community, and Google for local search. A [CloudTech market overview](https://cloudtech.co.th/blog/digital-marketing-thai-market) (secondary, December 2024) cites 54 million internet users, 51 million Facebook users, 47 million LINE users, and Google search share above 95%. Each channel does a different job, so budget should follow the job rather than the trend.

## Which platforms reach Thai customers in 2026?

Thailand is a large, heavily connected market. The same CloudTech overview also names 24 million TikTok users and describes TikTok as growing quickly among Gen Z and Millennial consumers. These are national user counts, not local audience sizes.

A salon in Bangkok and a café in Chiang Mai reach different people on the same platforms. Treat the national figures as context, and measure your own reach through LINE OA, Facebook Insights, and Google Business Profile data. The source is a secondary retelling from late 2024, so check current platform figures before setting a long-term budget.

Messaging-first markets follow a similar pattern. The guide to [local marketing in Brazil with WhatsApp and Meta](/blog/local-marketing-brazil-small-business-whatsapp-meta) explains how messaging apps become the customer relationship channel, which is the role LINE plays in Thailand.

## Why is a LINE Official Account the core channel?

A LINE OA lets a business message customers who have added it. The CloudTech overview (secondary) lists direct customer communication, push notification campaigns, rich menu customisation, and chatbot integration as its main capabilities.

LINE OA works best for retention rather than first contact. A regular customer who has added the account can receive a booking reminder, a new menu, or a promotion without the business paying for reach each time. Messages stay short, specific, and tied to one action such as booking or reordering.

Setup follows a fixed order. Complete the business profile in Thai, with English added where tourists form a real share of customers. Build a rich menu around the four actions most customers take: book, view services, see promotions, and get directions. Place "add friend" QR codes at checkout, on receipts, and on the website. Then set automated replies for opening hours and common questions, and send one broadcast per week with a single offer and a deadline.

## How should Facebook fit into the mix?

Facebook suits finding new customers and building local community. The CloudTech overview (secondary) reports that carousel ads and video perform best in Thai Facebook advertising, that Facebook Groups are highly valued for customer engagement, and that peak engagement falls around 7-9 PM and 12-1 PM.

Facebook Live is also cited as a strong engagement format. Test a live session on a fixed weekday and compare the leads it produces against normal posts before committing to a schedule. A business that cannot staff live sessions should rely on short video ads and active participation in relevant groups.

Most local businesses need only one page and one posting schedule. If a team manages several pages, the [Hootsuite and Sprout Social comparison](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) sets out the trade-offs for small teams.

## What does Google search mean for a Thai local business?

Google holds a search share above 95% in Thailand, according to the CloudTech overview (secondary). For a local business, the practical work is a complete Google Business Profile, reviews in Thai and English where relevant, and a dedicated location page for each branch. The same source also mentions Thai directory listings and schema markup.

Search ads capture people who already want the service. Write Thai keywords that match how people actually type, such as "ร้านทำผม ทองหล่อ" (hair salon Thonglor) or "คาเฟ่ อารีย์" (café Ari). Run English keywords in a separate campaign if tourists are a segment you serve, so each can be measured on its own.

## Which channel does which job?

The table below separates the four main channels by job, the reach figure the CloudTech overview gives for each (secondary), and the metric to track. Choosing one primary channel per goal stops budget spreading thinly across all of them.

| Channel | Main job | Reach figure (secondary) | Metric to track |
|---|---|---|---|
| LINE OA | Retention, bookings, repeat orders | 47M users | Repeat bookings per 100 followers |
| Facebook | Discovery and community | 51M users | Cost per new enquiry |
| Google Search and Maps | Capturing people already searching | 95%+ search share | Calls and direction requests |
| TikTok | Young-adult awareness, short video | 24M users | Profile visits and saves per video |

Start with LINE OA and Google, because they capture existing demand. Add Facebook once those two have a tracking method. Add TikTok only if the customer profile is young adults and the business can produce short, personality-led video.

## Does culture change how Thai businesses should advertise?

Yes. The CloudTech overview (secondary) names respect for hierarchy, the value of "sanuk" (fun), and family and community themes as recurring patterns. It recommends appropriate honorifics such as คุณ and ครับ/ค่ะ, avoiding aggressive or confrontational tactics, and using humour and bright visuals.

Hard-sell language often reads poorly. A polite, helpful offer with a clear next step usually works better than urgency-heavy copy. Test both tones on a small budget before choosing one for the main campaign.

## How do Thai customers prefer to pay?

The CloudTech overview (secondary) gives payment shares of 40% for cash on delivery, 25% for mobile banking, and 20% for credit cards, with e-wallets such as TrueMoney and Rabbit LINE Pay also in use. The source gives no date for these shares, so treat them as directional rather than current.

Put payment options in LINE OA auto-replies and on every product or booking page. If many customers pay on delivery, the order confirmation message should say so plainly.

## FAQ

### Is LINE or Facebook more important for a Thai local business?

Both matter, but they do different jobs. LINE OA is the retention channel because it reaches customers who have already opted in. Facebook is the discovery channel for reaching new people through posts, groups, and ads. If only one can be funded at the start, a LINE OA with a clear offer usually gives the better return on repeat business.

### Should a Thai business market to tourists as well as locals?

If tourists form a real share of customers, run bilingual content with Thai first and English second. Keep separate Google Ads campaigns for Thai and English keywords so each can be measured. Add English to the Google Business Profile and to the LINE OA rich menu.

### Does TikTok work for Thai businesses?

The CloudTech overview (secondary) puts TikTok at about 24 million Thai users and describes it as growing quickly among younger consumers. It suits businesses whose customers are young adults and who can produce short, personality-led video. For older customers, Facebook is the more practical choice.

### How should a business compare its marketing results?

Compare cost per new customer across channels rather than total reach. Use the worked example above as a template: list monthly spend, count new customers by source, and divide. After three months of data, shift budget toward the channel with the lowest cost per customer.
