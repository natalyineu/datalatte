Brunei's official 2025 population estimate is 458,600 people, and DataReportal counts about 300,000 Facebook and 297,000 Instagram ad-audience users there. Those two numbers set the rules for local marketing in the sultanate: your whole national market is smaller than many single US suburbs, and almost everyone you want to reach is already on Meta's apps. You win by being specific about place, language and offer, not by chasing volume.

This guide is for local businesses in Brunei: cafes, salons, clinics, studios, groomers and shops. It sticks to numbers from official statistics and published data reports, labels everything else as an assumption, and shows how to turn a modest budget into a measurable test. There is no verified public benchmark for Google Ads or Meta Ads costs in Brunei, so the cost model below uses assumptions you replace with your own results.

## What the Official Numbers Say

**Population.** The Department of Economic Planning and Statistics (DEPS) put Brunei's 2025 population at 458,600, up from 455,500 in 2024 (0.7% growth). Brunei citizens are 76.7% (351,700), permanent residents 5.8% (26,500) and temporary residents 17.5% (80,400). Brunei-Muara district holds 72.5% (332,700 people), Belait 14.8%, Tutong 10.6% and Temburong 2.1%. The median age is 32.8, and 19.8% of people are under 15. By ethnicity, Malays are 73.5%, Chinese 9.7% and others 16.8%. Source: [DEPS Population Estimates 2025 media release](https://www.deps.gov.bn/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/POP_2025_PR.pdf).

**Online.** DataReportal's [Digital 2026: Brunei Darussalam](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-brunei-darussalam) reports about 463,000 internet users (a penetration its publisher caps at 99.0%), 300,000 social media user identities, and a median mobile download speed of 184.86 Mbps. **Income.** The [World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/country/brunei-darussalam) lists Brunei's GDP per capita at about US$32,235 and population at 466,330 for 2025. Note that the World Bank's population figure differs slightly from DEPS's, which is normal because they use different methods. For local targeting, use the DEPS figure.

**Currency.** Singapore's central bank explains that the Brunei and Singapore dollars [can be exchanged at par and without charge](https://www.mas.gov.sg/currency/brunei-singapore-currency-interchangeability-agreement). That makes pricing and budgeting stable if you also sell to Singapore visitors, though Brunei remains its own market. Our Singapore guide shows where the two diverge.

## What This Means for Your Marketing

### 1. Your real audience is one district

With 72.5% of residents in Brunei-Muara, a cafe, salon or clinic in Bandar Seri Begawan is chasing roughly 330,000 residents at most, and in practice only those within a short drive. Do not set a "Brunei-wide" campaign unless you ship nationally. Draw a radius around your premises, or target Brunei-Muara only, and use the [Google Ads location targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) options, which Google says include countries, areas within a country and a radius around a location.

### 2. Facebook and Instagram have nearly equal reach, but different audiences

DataReportal shows Facebook and Instagram at 64.1% and 63.7% of the population. The adult gender splits differ: Facebook ads reach 40.6% women and 57.1% men, while Instagram reaches 53.1% women and 45.4% men. So a salon, bakery or wellness studio selling mainly to women has a stronger audience skew on Instagram; a tools, car or sports business may find more of its buyers on Facebook. Treat that as a starting hypothesis and test both.

Messenger, at 135,000, reaches less than half as many people as Facebook itself. DataReportal's table does not include WhatsApp, so WhatsApp's role is not measured here. Using WhatsApp as the main enquiry channel is common practice for local businesses in the region, but that is a recommendation, not a sourced figure. See our guide to [WhatsApp Business for local business marketing](/blog/whatsapp-business-marketing-local-business) for setup.

### 3. Many of your customers are not citizens

17.5% of residents are temporary residents, and 16.8% fall in the "other" ethnicity group. If your customers include expatriate workers or foreign professionals, publish English alongside Malay on your profile, ads and menu. How much English versus Malay to use is a judgment call; test with your own audience.

### 4. Halal is a legal matter, not just a marketing claim

Brunei's rules on halal food are official, so check them before you advertise. A [BizBrunei report from July 2017](https://bizbrunei.com/2017/07/changes-halal-certification-apply-businesses) said all businesses producing, supplying and serving food and beverages must apply for halal certification, with a separate permit for each manufactured product and an exemption only for businesses serving non-Muslims. A [Star report from August 2026](https://thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/08/09/brunei-strengthens-halal-compliance-amid-rising-applications) quotes the Minister of Religious Affairs saying the Halal Food Control Division received 1,004 applications between January and July 2026, with 614 approved, 122 in process, 190 awaiting the Brunei Islamic Religious Council's decision, 23 exempted and 55 rejected. The 2017 report may be out of date, so confirm current requirements with the Ministry of Religious Affairs before you make any halal claim in an ad.

On wider cultural norms, keep imagery and wording modest and respectful, particularly for beauty, fitness and pet businesses. If you are unsure, ask a local adviser or a local agency before launching.

## A Channel Plan That Fits a Small Market

**Google Business Profile first.** It is free, and Google's own [local ranking page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, with complete business info helping relevance. Fill in hours, phone or WhatsApp number, photos, services and your category, and ask every happy customer for a review. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers each field.

**Google Search ads second.** Search volume for any local phrase will be small, but intent is high. Start with one campaign for your main service, in English and Malay phrases where relevant, such as "hair salon Bandar Seri Begawan" or "cafe Brunei". Track calls, WhatsApp clicks and form submissions. Review search terms weekly.

**Meta ads third.** Use Instagram and Facebook for offers that make people act: a launch, a trial class, a seasonal menu. Send clicks to WhatsApp or a booking page, not just the home page.

**Measure by customers, not clicks.** Ask every new customer how they found you. In a small market this simple habit beats any dashboard.

## The Math

The model below tests a Meta-to-WhatsApp campaign for a single Bandar Seri Begawan business. **Every row is an assumption.** There is no public Brunei cost benchmark to cite, so the CPC, enquiry and close rates are illustrative. Replace them after your first month.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget (assumption) | BND 500 | BND 500 | BND 500 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | BND 0.80 | BND 0.50 | BND 0.35 |
| Clicks | 625 | 1,000 | 1,429 |
| Click to WhatsApp enquiry rate (assumption) | 4% | 6% | 8% |
| Enquiries | 25 | 60 | 114 |
| Enquiry to customer rate (assumption) | 20% | 30% | 35% |
| New customers | 5 | 18 | 40 |
| Ad cost per new customer | BND 100 | BND 27.8 | BND 12.5 |
| Revenue per visit (assumption) | BND 12 | BND 12 | BND 12 |
| Visits needed to repay the ad cost (revenue basis) | 8.3 | 2.3 | 1.0 |

Two checks keep this honest. First, reach: if roughly 72.5% of Facebook's 300,000 reach lives in Brunei-Muara (an assumption that Facebook use is spread like the population), that is about 217,000 accounts, so 1,000 clicks a month is nowhere near saturation. Second, the table counts revenue, not profit, so real payback is slower. A coffee at BND 4 needs far more repeat visits than a haircut at BND 40; use your own price and margin.

Your break-even click price is: maximum CPC = target cost per customer x enquiry rate x close rate. If you will pay BND 25 for a new customer at 6% and 30%, your ceiling is 25 x 0.06 x 0.30 = BND 0.45 a click.

## Do This Today

1. Open your Google Business Profile and fill every empty field, including the primary category, hours, phone or WhatsApp number and at least ten real photos.
2. Write down your average spend per visit, how many times a customer comes back in a year, and the most you would pay for one new customer.
3. Set up WhatsApp Business with a greeting, quick replies for price, hours and location, and a note saying when you reply.
4. Launch one small Search campaign and one small Meta campaign, each aimed at a radius around your premises, with a fixed monthly budget you can afford to lose.
5. From today, ask every new customer how they found you and write the answer in a spreadsheet. Review it on the first of next month.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Brunei's small population a problem for digital marketing?**
A: It caps volume, not effectiveness. DEPS counts 458,600 residents and 72.5% live in Brunei-Muara, so a tight radius and a specific offer matter more than broad reach.

**Q: Which platform should I start with, Instagram or Facebook?**
A: DataReportal shows near-equal reach (about 300,000 and 297,000), but Instagram skews female and Facebook skews male among adults. Start where your main buyer is, then test the other.

**Q: Is Google Ads worth it with small search volume?**
A: It can be, for high-intent services, if you keep the campaign narrow and track real enquiries. There is no public Brunei benchmark, so run a capped test before committing.

**Q: Do I need halal certification to advertise food?**
A: Reports say food businesses serving Muslims need certification or an exemption. Check current rules with the Ministry of Religious Affairs and do not use halal wording in ads without it.

**Q: Can I copy Singapore or Malaysia tactics?**
A: Some ideas transfer, such as Google Business Profile and WhatsApp enquiries, but the market size, religion-linked rules and audience differ. Adapt and test.
