To email people in the EU under GDPR, you generally need consent that is freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous. Pre-ticked boxes and silence do not count, you must be able to prove each consent, and people must be able to withdraw as easily as they agreed. In the UK, a separate rule allows emailing existing customers about similar products if you offered a simple way to opt out.

This is a practical summary of the legal texts, not legal advice. It focuses on small businesses that send newsletters and offers. Where a rule differs by country, check your national regulator.

## Does GDPR Apply to a Business Outside the EU?

It can. Article 3(2) says GDPR covers processing of personal data about people in the EU by a business not established there, where the processing relates to offering goods or services to those people. A US café with a tourist newsletter is not automatically in scope, but an online shop that ships to Europe and collects addresses there can be.

A simple test is whether you target EU residents, for example with euro prices, EU delivery or local-language pages. If you do, apply the rules below to that segment of your list.

## What Counts as Valid Consent for Marketing Email?

Article 4(11) defines consent as any freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication of the person's wishes, given by a statement or a clear affirmative action. Recital 32 adds that "silence, pre-ticked boxes or inactivity should not therefore constitute consent."

In practice that means an empty checkbox the person ticks, next to plain wording such as "Send me offers by email." Do not bundle it with accepting terms of service, and do not buy a list and treat it as consent. Each person must have agreed to hear from you.

## What Must You Prove and Tell People?

Under Article 7(1), you must be able to demonstrate that the person consented. Keep a record of who agreed, when, through which form and what wording they saw. Article 13 also requires you to tell people, when you collect data, who you are, why you process it, how long you keep it and that they can withdraw consent at any time.

Under Article 7(3), withdrawing must be as easy as giving consent. A working unsubscribe link in every email is the minimum. Withdrawal does not make earlier sending unlawful.

## Can People Opt Out of Marketing Entirely?

Yes. Article 21(2) gives people the right to object at any time to processing of their data for direct marketing, including profiling linked to it. Under Article 21(3), once someone objects the data may no longer be processed for that purpose.

For a small business this means a suppression list. When someone unsubscribes, keep their address on a do-not-email list so an old import does not add them back by accident.

## What Is the UK Soft Opt-In?

UK Regulation 22 of the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations covers email to individual subscribers. It allows marketing without prior consent if you obtained the address during a sale or negotiations for a sale, and you market only your similar products and services. You must also offer a free, simple way to refuse, both when you collect the address and in every later message.

A salon that took a booking by email can promote its own services this way. It cannot send third-party offers on the same basis. The rule is UK-specific, so do not assume it applies in EU countries.

## What Are the Fines?

Article 83(5) sets the upper tier at EUR 20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, for breaches of the basic principles including the consent conditions in Article 7 and data subject rights in Articles 12 to 22. Article 83 sets ceilings, not typical amounts. We did not find a verified average fine for small businesses sending newsletters, so none is quoted.

## What Does a Compliance Check Look Like? (Worked Example)

All figures are **assumptions**. A hair salon has 300 addresses: 180 from a website form with a ticked box and a date stamp, and 120 from an old paper sheet with no wording or dates.

## FAQ

### Do I need double opt-in for GDPR?

GDPR does not name double opt-in. It requires that you can demonstrate consent. Double opt-in, where the person confirms through a link, is one way to create that evidence. A form submission with a stored timestamp and wording is another.

### Can I email people who gave me their card at an event?

Only if the card or sign-up sheet made clear they agreed to marketing emails and you kept a record. A business card handed over does not by itself prove consent to a newsletter.

### Is B2B email treated differently?

The UK soft opt-in quoted above is written for individual subscribers, so the rules for emailing companies differ. We could not open the UK regulator's B2B page, so check its guidance before relying on any business-to-business exception.

### Do I have to delete someone who unsubscribes?

You must stop using their data for marketing. Keeping the address on a suppression list so you do not email them again is a sensible, common practice.

### Does this replace US rules?

No. US senders still follow CAN-SPAM, which requires a physical address and honouring opt-outs within 10 business days. See our [holiday email guide](/blog/holiday-email-marketing-campaigns) for those basics.
