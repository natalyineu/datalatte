## How Much Should an LA Fitness Studio Spend on Marketing?

Many boutique studios budget 5 to 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing, according to [Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/). For a small independent studio under 200 members, [Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026) (secondary) gives a total monthly benchmark of $1,500 to $2,900 across Google Ads, Meta ads, out-of-home placements, and email or SMS tools.

The worked example below uses assumptions, not Los Angeles market data. Assumption: the studio's monthly revenue is $30,000. At 5 to 10 percent, the marketing range is $1,500 to $3,000. The plan keeps spend inside both that range and the benchmark total.

| Channel | Benchmark per month | Plan per month (assumption) |
|---|---|---|
| Google Ads | $600 to $1,200 | $1,000 |
| Meta ads (retargeting and local reach) | $400 to $800 | $650 |
| Out-of-home (one elevator or transit placement) | $400 to $700 | $0 (not used) |
| Email and SMS tools | $100 to $200 | $200 |
| Total | $1,500 to $2,900 | $1,850 |

Check: $1,850 divided by $30,000 is 6.2 percent, inside the 5 to 10 percent range. If revenue rises, the budget should rise with it.

## Why Does Radius Matter More in a Spread-Out City?

[BrandRocket](https://www.brandrocket.net/resources/gym-google-ads-settings-location) (secondary) suggests starting paid campaigns at a 3 to 5 mile radius for a budget gym and 5 to 8 miles for a boutique studio, adjusted for how people actually drive in your area. Los Angeles has many drive-time patterns within a single city, so treat those figures as a starting test, not a fixed rule.

Set one radius per campaign, then compare booking rates from each area over the same period. Where bookings cluster, narrow the radius. Where they are thin but the studio is nearby, test messaging that names the neighborhood.

Your Google Business Profile should reflect the same local focus. Name your neighborhood in the business description and ask recent members to mention it in reviews when they describe the studio.

## How Often Should a Studio Post Short-Form Video?

[Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) advises studios to post at a steady cadence rather than in bursts, with three to five times a week described as a workable target. A steady schedule is easier to keep than a burst of posts that stops after a few weeks.

Film instructor-led clips, class previews, and studio spaces in natural light when your room allows it. Keep one recurring format, such as a 30-second class preview each Monday, so followers know what to expect.

## Which Paid Ads Fit a Studio's Budget?

The Google Ads and Meta lines in the table above come from the same benchmark: $600 to $1,200 for Google and $400 to $800 for Meta for an independent studio under 200 members. Keep both inside those ranges until you have your own conversion data.

Use the radius from the previous section for each campaign. Retargeting visitors who viewed your pricing page is a common use of Meta spend, and the step-by-step setup is covered in [fitness studio marketing with Facebook ads and AI](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-facebook-ads-and-ai).

Out-of-home placements carry a $400 to $700 benchmark per month, which is more than many small studios can justify. The worked example leaves them out for that reason.

## How Do You Win Back Members Who Stop Booking?

[Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) suggests sending win-back campaigns to members who have not booked in 30 or 60 days. [Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026) (secondary) recommends automated win-back sequences for members who have missed three or more classes. Both triggers can run in the same email tool.

Retention matters because boutique churn is steep. According to [Second Measure](https://secondmeasure.com/?p=364) (secondary), more than 80 percent of boutique studio customers do not spend in the last quarter of their first year, with one exception.

A win-back message should be short, name the class the member last took, and offer one easy next booking. For tool setup, see [email marketing for fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-fitness-studio).

## What Happens to Pop-Up Leads and Prospects?

Interest fades quickly after a pop-up event ends. [Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence for contacts collected at a pop-up.

Build the sequence before the event. It should include a welcome message, a first-class offer, and a booking link that works on a phone. Mariana Tek also reports that over 90 percent of group fitness members book their own classes through a studio's branded app, so the booking path matters as much as the message.

## What Order Should a Studio Work In?

1. Set a monthly marketing budget at 5 to 10 percent of revenue, then check it against the $1,500 to $2,900 benchmark for an independent studio under 200 members.
2. Update your Google Business Profile with your neighborhood, current hours, and a review request that asks members to name their area.
3. Choose a starting radius of 5 to 8 miles for a boutique studio and set one campaign per neighborhood cluster.
4. Publish short-form video three to five times a week in one recurring format.
5. Create a win-back sequence for members who have not booked in 30 days or who have missed three classes.
6. Automate a 24-hour welcome sequence for every pop-up or event lead.
7. Review booking rates by radius and channel each month, then move budget toward what converts.
