Website conversion rate optimization is the work of finding where visitors stop short of a booking, call or purchase, and fixing the biggest gap first. Start by recording one action as a key event in Google Analytics, watch real visits for free in Microsoft Clarity, and change one thing at a time. Baymard's review of 50 studies puts average cart abandonment at 70.22%, and its list of reasons shows that cost surprises, forced accounts and long checkouts matter most.

This guide is for owners of local service and small retail sites. It explains a measurement setup that costs nothing, which fixes the evidence supports, and a worked example with labelled assumptions. It gives no "average conversion rate" for local businesses because none of the sources opened publishes a reliable one.

## How Do You Find Where a Website Loses Visitors?

Count first, then look. Decide which single action matters most, such as a booking, a tap on the phone number or an order. In Google Analytics 4 you mark an event as a key event, and Google's help says a key event can also become a Google Ads conversion, which keeps counts consistent between the two.

Then add Microsoft Clarity. Microsoft states it is free forever and offers session recordings, heatmaps and funnels. Watch twenty recordings of mobile visitors who left without acting. Note where they stop scrolling, what they tap that is not a link and where they leave. Clarity says it should not be used on sites aimed at people under 18.

## What Do Visitors Abandon Checkouts For?

Baymard's list is for online shops, but the reasons apply to any form or booking flow. Among US shoppers who abandoned a cart (excluding those just browsing), it reports:

| Reason | Share |
|---|---|
| Extra costs too high (shipping, tax, fees) | 40% |
| Delivery too slow | 20% |
| Did not trust the site with card details | 19% |
| Had to create an account | 18% |
| Checkout too long or complicated | 17% |
| Website errors or crashes | 17% |
| Total cost not visible up front | 12% |

Baymard also reports the average US checkout shows 23.48 form elements, while an ideal flow can be 12 to 14. For a salon or service business, treat each booking field the same way. Ask only for what you need to confirm the appointment.

## Which Fixes Come First?

Fix the biggest and cheapest problems before testing button colours. A reasonable order:

- **Speed on mobile.** Google's good Core Web Vitals thresholds are an LCP of 2.5 seconds or less, INP of 200 ms or less and CLS of 0.1 or less at the 75th percentile (web.dev).
- **One clear action above the fold.** A visible Book, Call or Order button.
- **Price transparency.** Show prices or ranges and any extra fees early.
- **Fewer form fields.** Remove anything you can ask later.
- **No forced accounts.** Allow guest booking or ordering.
- **Trust signals.** Real photos, reviews and your address.
- **Working forms.** Submit every form yourself weekly.

## How Do You Know a Change Worked?

Change one thing, wait for enough visitors and compare the same weekdays. A week with a holiday is not comparable with a normal week. Keep a log with the date, the change and the result. With low traffic, a small site cannot detect a few percentage points of difference, so choose changes with a large expected effect and judge them on bookings, not on impressions. No source opened gives a minimum sample size, so use common sense and repeat before you conclude.

## FAQ

### What is a good website conversion rate?

No source opened gives a reliable figure for local businesses. It varies by industry, traffic source and offer, so compare your own weeks.

### How much does conversion rate optimization cost?

The tools can be free: Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity. The cost is your time or an agency's. Ask for a fixed scope.

### Which tool shows why visitors leave?

Session recordings and heatmaps, such as those in Microsoft Clarity, show what visitors do. They do not say why, so follow with a question or a test.

### Should I run A/B tests?

Only with enough traffic. A small site is better off fixing obvious problems first.

### Does page speed affect conversions?

Google defines thresholds for a good page experience, but the sources opened here do not provide a salon or local-business conversion figure for speed.
