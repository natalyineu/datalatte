## What Do Pet Services Pay Per Click and Per Lead?

LocaliQ's 2026 search benchmarks (updated 1 June 2026) list Animals and Pets, which the article also labels Pet Services, at $4.06 per click, 7.49% click-through rate, 16.22% conversion rate and $31.50 per lead. For all industries the figures are $5.42 per click, 6.64% click-through rate and $66.69 per lead.

So pet services sit below the all-industry average on click cost and well below on cost per lead. The category is broad and includes vets, boarding and pet stores, so a groomer's own numbers can differ. Treat the benchmark as a starting range.

## Is There a Standard Budget for Groomers?

No. A published budget "per business size" would be invented, because costs depend on your city, competitors and how many keywords you can afford. What is published is how Google spends money.

Google's help center says the average daily budget is a per-day target. Spend can exceed it on busy days, with a daily limit of about two times the average, and a monthly limit of 30.4 times the average daily budget for most campaigns. A $20 daily budget therefore caps near $608 a month. Set budget from your goal, then let the account show what is realistic.

## How Do You Work Out Your Own Number?

Use this formula, then replace each input with your own.

1. Decide how many new grooming clients you want a month.
2. Estimate the share of leads (calls, forms, bookings) who become clients.
3. Leads needed = clients wanted divided by that share.
4. Budget = leads needed x cost per lead.

If you want 8 new clients and 40% of leads book (an assumption), you need 20 leads. At the LocaliQ average of $31.50 per lead that is $630 a month, or about $21 a day.

## The Math: Three Budget Levels

Cost per client stays the same because the budget does not change the rate. What changes is volume. Now the value side, also an assumption: a $70 groom every 8 weeks is about 6.5 visits a year, or roughly $455 a year per regular client. At $79 per new client the ads pay back within two or three visits, if the benchmarks hold for you. If your first month shows $200 per client, the picture changes.

## How Many Clients Are Looking for a Groomer?

APPA's 2026 State of the Industry report cites 95 million US households owning a pet, according to its 2025 National Pet Owners Survey. APPA lists 2024 spending on "Other Services," which includes grooming, boarding and training, at $14.3 billion, but gives no grooming-only figure.

That is a large market, but you only compete within driving distance. Use location targeting and review your search terms report to see where clicks really come from. ## What Else Affects What You Pay?

- **Location.** A small radius can reduce wasted clicks, though Google notes location targeting is best effort.
- **Keywords.** Broad terms like "dog" bring in non-customers, so use phrase and exact match for service terms and add negative keywords.
- **Landing page.** A page with prices, hours and a booking button converts better than a home page. This is a recommendation, not a published figure.
- **Tracking.** If you cannot count calls and bookings, you cannot tell which dollars worked.

## FAQ

### What is the minimum Google Ads budget for a groomer?

Google sets no practical minimum for a campaign, but a very small budget produces too few clicks to learn from. At $4.06 per click, $300 a month buys roughly 74 clicks.

### How much does one new grooming client cost in ads?

LocaliQ's pet cost per lead is $31.50. If 40% of leads book, which is an assumption, a client costs about $79. Your own booking rate sets the real figure.

### Should I start with Google Ads or Facebook?

Google reaches people searching for a groomer now. Facebook reaches people who might need one. This guide suggests starting with Google for direct bookings, but your area may differ.

### How long should I run ads before judging them?

Long enough to collect meaningful call and booking counts. No official number applies, so set a minimum number of leads, such as 20, before deciding.
