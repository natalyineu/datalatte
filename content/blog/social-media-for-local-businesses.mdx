Organic growth is slow, so the weekly plan has to be small enough to keep running for months.

## Which Platforms Should a Local Business Start With?

Pick the one or two platforms where your customers spend time, and do them well. Madsun Media gives this advice to [Australian small businesses](https://www.madsunmedia.com/blog/small-business-marketing-australia), and it applies in the US, UK and Canada too. Spreading thin across five networks usually means no single channel gets steady posts.

Facebook and Instagram are the usual starting pair for local services. A Solveo summary cites 2.9 billion monthly active users on Facebook and 500 million daily active users on Instagram. Those numbers show scale, not relevance to your town. A [salon advertising guide from Trafft](https://trafft.com/salon-ads/) names both platforms for showing work, running targeted ads and offering promotions.

Check where your recent customers say they found you. Their answer matters more than any global figure. Once you have chosen, a [scheduling tool comparison for small businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) can help you decide whether paid software is worth it.

## What Content Should a Local Business Post?

Mix formats rather than repeating one. AdWave's [restaurant marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/restaurant-marketing-tips) recommends photos, short videos such as Reels or TikToks, and customer-generated stories. It gives no numeric minimum for that mix, so treat the balance as your own decision.

A [pet store guide from Blackstone Infomatics](https://blackstoneinfomatics.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-pet-shops/) lists six content types that work: user-generated content, behind-the-scenes moments, tips and advice, seasonal content, interactive features, and product highlights in context. For a plumber or salon, those translate into finished jobs, a quick how-to, a seasonal reminder, and a customer photo.

Build the calendar before the week starts. The [content calendar guide for small businesses](/blog/how-to-create-social-media-content-calendar-small-business) turns these formats into a weekly grid, and the [local content creation guide](/blog/local-business-social-media-content-creation) covers shooting on a phone.

## What Is the Ten-Step Checklist for Local Posting?

Use this list as a weekly routine. The steps are ordered so the first ones cost nothing but time.

1. Choose one or two platforms where your recent customers already look for local services.
2. Photograph or film finished jobs, and name the neighbourhood or town in the caption.
3. Put the service name and location in your captions, bio and profile so search can match them.
4. Post real work, not reposted stock images or someone else's content.
5. Keep videos short and show the finished result first.
6. Reply to every comment and direct message on the day it arrives.
7. Ask recent customers for a review using your Google review link or a QR code.
8. End each post with one action: book, call or visit, with the link in the bio or post.
9. Keep your Google Business Profile current with the same photos, hours and services.
10. Review your insights once a month and note which format earned the most saves and replies.

After a month, keep the format that worked and drop the weakest one.

## Do Reviews Matter More Than Posts?

For many local searches, yes. A third-party study cited by [Mayfly Internet Marketing](https://www.may-fly.co.uk/resources/how-to-generate-leads-with-seo/) reports that 72% of customers use Google reviews to find businesses. Review requests belong in the weekly plan, not in a separate task you get to later.

The same Mayfly article cites BrightEdge, a third-party source, saying SEO drives 1000%+ more traffic than organic social media. That is a vendor-cited comparison of traffic, not customers, and it says nothing about your shop. Use it to justify a complete search profile, not to stop posting.

Ask in person or with a short follow-up message. Do not offer discounts or free items in exchange for reviews without first checking the platform's current policy.

## How Long Before Social Media Brings Customers?

Organic social is slow. AdWave's [local advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-advertising-ideas-beat-social-media) says organic growth typically needs 6-12 months of consistent effort before it generates meaningful traffic. Plan for that gap, and do not judge the channel after three weeks.

[UpSwell Marketing](https://upswellmarketing.com/industry/fitness), a gym marketing agency, makes a faster claim: social media drives leads within days, and most gyms see measurable growth within two months. That is a vendor statement for one niche, so track your own enquiries from the first month.

Paid social behaves differently. The IAB's January 2026 outlook, as reported by AdWave, forecast social media ad spend growth of 14.6% in 2026. That is a market figure, not a return for your shop. Test paid promotion only after the organic posts run steadily. Need help setting the plan up? You can [book a free audit](/free-audit).

## What Does a Realistic Weekly Plan Take? A Worked Example

The inputs below are assumptions for a single-site local shop with three hours a week for social media. Replace them with your own numbers.

| Task | Times per week (assumption) | Minutes per session (assumption) | Weekly minutes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shoot photos and clips of finished work | 1 | 60 | 60 |
| Write captions and post | 3 | 10 | 30 |
| Reply to comments and messages | 3 | 15 | 45 |
| Ask one customer for a review | 1 | 5 | 5 |
| Check insights and note the top post | 1 | 20 | 20 |
| Total | | | 160 |

The total is 160 minutes, or 2 hours 40 minutes, which leaves 20 minutes of the three-hour budget as a buffer. If you hand the shoot to a freelancer in the US, [AdWave's hiring guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-hire-and-manage-a-social-media-intern-or-freelancer), citing Indeed, notes that a 1099 form is required for freelancers hired for social media work.

For batching captions and visuals, the [AI content guide for local businesses](/blog/ai-driven-social-media-content-for-local-businesses) shows how to prepare a week's material in one sitting.

## Can AI Agents Handle the Routine Posting?

Some routine work can be automated. HubSpot quotes Tim Hanson, CMO at Penfriend, saying his teams spend 64% less time on process work after using AI for administrative tasks. That is a self-reported vendor figure from one company, so treat it as a single data point.

[Sintra's roundup](https://sintra.ai/blog/ai-social-media/best-social-media-ai-agents) cites a third-party claim from nexos.ai that teams using AI agents for content work save 10 to 15 hours a week per creator. Sintra says this is not its own figure. Measure the time you actually save in your first month before counting on it.

Keep approval with the owner. The [AI agent posting guide](/blog/ai-agent-for-social-media-posting-local-business) covers setup, and the [analytics guide](/blog/ai-powered-social-media-analytics-for-local-businesses) explains which insights numbers are worth tracking.

## FAQ

### How often should a local business post?
No source gives a universal figure. The worked example uses three posts a week as an assumption. Choose a number you can keep up for months, then review it in insights.

### Is Instagram or Facebook better for a local business?
Neither is better by default. Trafft's salon guide names both for showing work and running targeted ads. The platform your recent customers mention is the best guide.

### Do I need a scheduling tool?
Not at the start. Compare options once you have a steady weekly routine, using the [tool comparison for small businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business).

### How long until social media brings customers?
AdWave says organic growth typically takes 6-12 months of consistent effort. Some vendors claim faster results, but those claims are not local benchmarks.
