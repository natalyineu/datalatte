Set your digital marketing budget by working backward from the customers you want, not by picking a percentage of revenue. Take the new customers you need per month, divide by the share of leads that become customers, then multiply by your category's cost per lead.

LocaliQ's 2026 search-ad benchmarks put that cost at $30.57 for restaurants, $39.25 for beauty and $67.36 for health and fitness, so ten leads cost roughly $306, $393 or $674 in ad spend.

## How much should a small business spend on digital marketing?

Start with a percentage only as a sanity check. An [SBA blog post](https://www.sba.gov/blog/how-get-most-your-marketing-budget) cites two second-hand figures: an average of 1.08% of revenue spent on advertising (Small Business Trends, 2018, with retailers near 4% and restaurants at 1.93%) and 7.9% of revenue on marketing overall (Web Strategies, 2018).

The Web Strategies breakdown shows 11.8% for B2C services. These are dated and cover all marketing, not only digital, so the spread is wide.

Worked example (assumption): a salon with $15,000 monthly revenue.

| Rule | Share | Monthly budget |
|---|---|---|
| Low (advertising only) | 1.08% | about $162 |
| Average marketing | 7.9% | about $1,185 |
| B2C services average | 11.8% | about $1,770 |

A range from $162 to $1,770 is too wide to act on. That is why the next section works from outcomes.

## How do I back-solve a budget from the customers I want?

Use four numbers: customers wanted, lead-to-customer rate, cost per lead and a test buffer.

**Budget = (customers wanted / lead-to-customer rate) x cost per lead.**

Worked example (assumptions: you want 10 new clients a month and 1 in 2 leads books):

- Leads needed: 10 / 0.5 = 20.
- Beauty and personal care at $39.25 per lead: 20 x $39.25 = $785 per month.
- Health and fitness at $67.36 per lead: 20 x $67.36 = about $1,347 per month.
- Restaurants and food at $30.57 per lead: 20 x $30.57 = about $611 per month.

The costs come from [LocaliQ's 2026 search advertising benchmarks](https://www.localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/), which are Google Ads search averages across many businesses. Your cost will differ by city and competition, so treat the formula as a planning model and replace the benchmark with your own cost per lead after 60 days.

## Is the budget worth it? Check customer value first

Compare the cost per customer with what a customer is worth. Cost per customer equals cost per lead divided by the lead-to-customer rate.

Worked example (assumption): a fitness studio with a 1 in 3 lead-to-member rate and $67.36 per lead pays about $202 per new member. If an average member stays 6 months at $90 a month, that is $540 of revenue, so the ad cost is about 37% of first-year revenue before service costs. If your retention is lower or margin is thin, the channel may not pay back.

Decision rule: if cost per customer is above one third of the customer's gross margin over the first year, fix the offer, the landing page or the targeting before raising spend. ## What are the minimum budgets by channel?

Platforms set minimums, and below them you collect too little data to learn from. A verified example: TikTok's [help centre](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/budget-and-bidding-faq) states a $50 minimum at the campaign level and $20 at the ad group level.

For channel-by-channel limits and splits, see:

- [Facebook ads budget for local business](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend)
- [Local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide), which compares Google Ads, Meta Ads and local SEO at different spend levels

Practical rule: fund one channel at the level that produces at least a few leads a week before adding a second. Spreading $500 across five channels gives each too few results to judge. This is a judgment call, not a platform rule.

## How should I split the budget between channels?

Split by purpose rather than a fixed pie:

1. **Capture demand:** Google search ads and your Google Business Profile, because people are already looking.
2. **Create demand:** Meta or TikTok ads, for services people do not search for daily.
3. **Keep customers:** email or SMS, which cost little per send.

Worked example (assumption): a $1,000 monthly budget for a salon could put $700 into Google search, $200 into Meta retargeting and $100 into email tools. Review after 60 days: move 20% of the budget from the weakest channel to the strongest, and repeat. For a broader template by business type, read the [small business marketing budget guide](/blog/small-business-marketing-budget-guide-2026).

## What should I measure each month?

Track four numbers per channel: spend, leads, customers and cost per customer. Use a booking question such as "How did you hear about us?" to catch customers who came from ads but booked by phone.

Review monthly, change one thing at a time, and keep a note of what you changed so you can see its effect.

## FAQ

### What percent of revenue should a small business spend on marketing?

Sources vary widely. An SBA blog cites 1.08% for advertising and 7.9% for total marketing (both 2018, second-hand), so use a percentage only as a rough check against your outcome-based budget.

### How much does a lead cost for a local business?

LocaliQ's 2026 search benchmarks list $30.57 for restaurants, $31.50 for animals and pets, $39.25 for beauty and $67.36 for health and fitness. The all-industry average is $66.69.

### What is the minimum I can spend on ads?

It depends on the platform. TikTok lists $50 per campaign and $20 per ad group. Check each platform's help page before you plan.

### Should I spend more in busy months?

Yes if your cost per customer stays stable. Add budget only to the channel that is already paying back.

### How do I start if I have no data?

Run a 60-day test with one channel and the formula above. Replace the benchmark with your own cost per lead afterward.
