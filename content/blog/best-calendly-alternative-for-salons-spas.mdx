Calendly's pricing page lists $10 per seat for Standard and $16 for Teams. A salon booking tool such as Fresha lists $14.95 per team member, Vagaro $30 for the first calendar and Booksy $29.99. So the difference between a meeting scheduler and a salon system is often under $25 a month for a small team. The question is not price. It is what each tool does when a client wants a balayage with a deposit and a text reminder.

This guide is for salon, barbershop and spa owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Every price below was read on the vendor's own page. Where a page was unclear, incomplete or contradicted itself, the entry says "confirm with vendor". Prices are shown in US dollars unless stated, exclude sales tax, and change often.

## Where Calendly Fits, and Where It Stops

Calendly is built around meetings. Its [pricing page](https://calendly.com/pricing) lists a Free plan with one event type and one calendar connection, Standard at $10 per seat per month, Teams at $16 per seat and Enterprise from $15,000 a year for at least 50 seats. The page does not say whether the $10 and $16 are annual or monthly figures, so confirm with vendor. Standard includes unlimited event types, automated reminders and Stripe or PayPal payment connections.

That is a decent kit for a stylist who only needs consultations. The limits show up in the details:

- **Payments are per meeting.** Calendly's [Stripe help page](https://calendly.com/help/calendly-stripe) says you turn on "Require payment to book this meeting" for each event type and set an amount. It does not describe deposits or partial payments for bookings; it says payment links that can collect deposits are in private beta for select users. Refunds are not processed by Calendly: you issue them through Stripe or PayPal, and Calendly's [payments FAQ](https://calendly.com/help/payments-faq) says it does not hold the money.
- **Tax is on you.** The Stripe help page says Calendly does not calculate or collect VAT or sales tax, so tax has to be inside the price or explained in your terms.
- **Text reminders.** The pricing page does not mention SMS for any plan. Calendly's own materials describe text reminders through Workflows, but they disagree on which plan includes them, so confirm with vendor.
- **Salon features.** The pricing and help pages reviewed describe event types, calendars, payments and reminders. They do not describe per-stylist service menus, retail inventory, tips or client colour history. That may change, but those are the jobs a salon system exists to do.

If you are happy with Calendly for supplier calls and team meetings, keep it for that. Use a beauty tool for clients.

## What to Compare Before You Pick

1. **Calendars and pricing per stylist.** Is the fee per bookable person, or per location?
2. **Deposits and no-show fees.** Who can charge them, on which plan, at what processing rate?
3. **Reminders.** Are texts included or metered?
4. **Marketplace fees.** Free listings can carry a commission on new clients. Our [Booksy, Vagaro and Fresha comparison](/blog/booksy-vs-vagaro-vs-fresha-best-booking-app-salons-2026) works through those fees.
5. **Processing rates.** A 0.1 point difference on $20,000 of monthly card sales is $20. Our [Fresha, Square and Vagaro fee breakdown](/blog/fresha-vs-square-vs-vagaro-salon-booking-software) has the numbers.

## The Eight Options, on Published Prices

### Acuity Scheduling: the closest step up from Calendly

Per Acuity's [pricing page](https://www.acuityscheduling.com/pricing): Starter is $16 a month on annual billing or $20 monthly for one calendar; Standard is $27 or $34 for up to 6 calendars; Premium is $49 or $61 for up to 36. Payments through Stripe, Square, PayPal and Venmo, email reminders and custom client forms are on every plan. Text reminders, an appointment waitlist, packages and memberships, and gift certificates start at Standard. Every plan has a 7-day free trial. Best for a stylist or small spa that likes Calendly's simplicity but needs forms, packages and texts.

### Setmore: cheapest per head, with a pricing page that needs checking

Setmore's [pricing page](https://www.setmore.com/pricing) lists Free for up to 4 users and Pro at $5 per user a month on annual billing. Free includes online and in-person payments but no deposits and one email reminder per appointment; Pro adds deposits, two reminders per appointment and 500 text credits a month for Pro team members. A Live Receptionist add-on is $99 a month. The page also shows other figures ($12 and $5, and an inconsistent table entry), so confirm with vendor which price applies to monthly billing.

### Fresha: low subscription, pay-as-you-go extras

[Fresha's pricing](https://www.fresha.com/pricing) shows Independent at $19.95 a month for one bookable team member and Team at $14.95 per team member, with a 7-day trial. Online payments are 2.79% + $0.20 and in-person payments 2.29% + $0.20. The marketplace charges a one-time 20% commission (minimum $6) on a new client; bookings from your own link, Facebook or Instagram are free. Each plan includes 100 free automated texts and 50 free marketing emails a month. Add-ons include Client Loyalty at $59.95 per location and an AI Concierge at $99.95 per location. No-show and cancellation fees are listed as a feature with no amount, so confirm with vendor how they work.

### Vagaro: reminders and listing included

[Vagaro's pricing page](https://www.vagaro.com/pro/pricing) shows $23.99 a month as an exclusive rate for the first 6 months beside a $30 list price, plus $10 per bookable calendar up to seven. Unlimited notifications and appointment reminders and a free marketplace listing are included, with a 30-day trial. Online booking works from Vagaro, Instagram, Facebook and Apple Maps. Payments run through Vagaro Merchant Services, and the page says exclusions apply.

### Booksy: a flat fee plus per-user charges

Booksy's [pricing page](https://biz.booksy.com/pricing) lists $29.99 a month plus $20 for each extra user, with a 14-day trial. Confirmations and reminders are free, and plans include 2,000 free SMS marketing messages a month. No-show protection features are included, with standard mobile payment processing fees applying. Boost, its paid discovery product, is free to turn on and charges a one-time 30% of a new client's first visit, capped at $100.

### GlossGenius: flat processing, tiered plans

[GlossGenius](https://glossgenius.com/pricing) lists Standard at $28 a month ($24 on annual billing), Gold at $56 ($48) for up to 9 staff, and Platinum at $168 ($148) for 10 or more. Processing is a flat 2.6% on all plans, with no extra fee for Tap to Pay, card on file or manual entry. Standard includes a custom booking site, client notifications, email and text marketing, inventory and packages; Gold adds forms, Google booking and an automated waitlist. The page does not state Standard's staff limit, so confirm with vendor. A Reception add-on for AI call and text answering is $50 a month.

### Square Appointments: strongest if you already use Square

Square's US appointments page lists Free, Plus and Premium tiers, but the monthly prices did not display, so confirm with vendor. Square's [help article on processing fees](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5068-square-appointments-pricing) lists tap, dip or swipe at 2.6% + 15 cents on Free, 2.5% + 15 cents on Plus and 2.4% + 15 cents on Premium, and online payments at 3.3% + 30 cents on Free and 2.9% + 30 cents on Plus and Premium. Card on file and manual entry are 3.5% + 15 cents on every tier.

### Mindbody: classes and memberships

[Mindbody's pricing page](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/business/pricing) shows Starter from $79 per location per month, with higher tiers on request. Starter includes integrated payments, booking widgets and a listing on the Mindbody app; the Ultimate tier adds automated email and text campaigns and a 24/7 AI front desk. It makes sense for a spa that sells classes and memberships. For a four-chair hair salon it is more than you need.

## The Math

Take a three-stylist salon. Every client figure is an **assumption**; the subscription numbers come from the pages above at list monthly prices, before processing and tax.

| Tool | Monthly subscription for 3 bookable stylists |
| --- | --- |
| Setmore Pro | $15 (3 x $5, annual billing; confirm) |
| Calendly Standard | $30 (3 x $10; billing cycle not stated) |
| Acuity Standard | $34 (monthly billing, up to 6 calendars) |
| Vagaro | $50 ($30 + 2 x $10) |
| Fresha Team | $44.85 (3 x $14.95) |
| GlossGenius Standard | $28 (staff limit unconfirmed) |
| Booksy | $69.99 ($29.99 + 2 x $20) |
| Mindbody Starter | $79 |

The spread between the cheapest and priciest is under $65 a month. Now test it against no-shows, using assumptions: 150 appointments a month, a $70 average ticket and an 8% no-show rate, which is 12 missed visits or $840. If deposits and reminders prevent a quarter of them, that is 3 saved visits, $210 a month. A tool that costs $25 more per month pays for itself if it saves just 0.4 extra appointments ($25 divided by $70). The subscription is not what decides this. Whether the tool lets you take a deposit and send a text without a manual step is what decides it.

## Which One Should You Choose?

- **Solo stylist on a tight budget:** Setmore or Fresha. Check deposit availability on the exact plan.
- **Small salon with colour services and walk-in retail:** Vagaro or GlossGenius, so inventory, reminders and checkout live in one place.
- **Already taking payments on Square:** Square Appointments, once you confirm the US plan prices.
- **Social-media-driven barber or stylist:** Booksy, with its fees understood. See our guide to [turning Instagram into bookings](/blog/hair-salon-instagram-bookings).

## Switching From Calendly

1. Export your client contacts using Calendly's current instructions and check for duplicates.
2. Set up services with real durations (including processing time for colour) and prices before going live.
3. Turn on reminders and decide your cancellation policy and deposit rule.
4. Book three test appointments yourself.
5. Replace the booking link in your Instagram bio, Facebook page and Google listing. Google's [Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7475773) says you can enable bookings from a provider in the Bookings section, that providers can take up to a week to appear, and that custom links give no performance data.
6. Message your clients with the new link, and keep Calendly running for a few weeks so nobody gets stranded.

After the switch, use the reminders to chase rebooking. Our [rebooking script guide](/blog/salon-rebooking-script-that-gets-clients-back) and [no-show article](/blog/why-salon-clients-ghost-you-and-how-to-stop-it) show what to send.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Calendly good enough for a solo stylist?**
A: It can work for consultations. Its pricing page lists payments through Stripe or PayPal on Standard and above, but help pages do not describe deposits for bookings. For a full service menu, retail and tips, a beauty tool is a better fit.

**Q: Which booking tool is cheapest?**
A: On published figures, Setmore Pro at $5 per user on annual billing and Fresha Team at $14.95 per member are lowest. Both have caveats, listed above, so compare totals including processing.

**Q: Can I keep Calendly for non-client scheduling?**
A: Yes. Many owners keep it for supplier calls and team meetings while clients book through the salon tool.

**Q: Which tool works best if clients find me on Google?**
A: Look for a provider that connects to your Google Business Profile bookings. Google's help page says you can add a provider or your own booking link, but performance data is not available for custom links.

**Q: Where do SMS marketing and email fit in?**
A: Several tools include a monthly allowance of texts and emails, but allowances differ. See our guide to [SMS marketing for hair salons](/blog/sms-marketing-for-hair-salons) and our [email and SMS service](/services/email-sms).
