A Montana small business that serves visitors should plan marketing around the summer peak, keep its Google Business Profile accurate, and run a small, tightly targeted Google Ads campaign. About 13.8 million nonresident visitors came to Montana in 2024, and 46% of them arrived between July and September, according to University of Montana tourism research as reported by the Daily Montanan.

No source publishes a Montana-specific click price. The WordStream by LocaliQ 2026 all-industry average is $5.42 per click. Use it as a starting range for cafes, studios, salons and service firms in Billings, Missoula, Bozeman and smaller towns.

## How Big Is Tourism for Montana Businesses?

The Institute for Tourism and Recreation Research (ITRR) at the University of Montana estimated 13,787,000 nonresident visitors in 2024, the highest on record. They spent about $5.0 billion, roughly 10% less than in 2023, and that spending directly supported an estimated 38,240 jobs, down from 48,340 a year earlier. The full ITRR report could not be opened, so these numbers come from the Daily Montanan's account of it (secondary).

For a small business this means two customer groups: residents who buy year round, and visitors who arrive in a short peak. The figures do not show your share, so mark in last year's monthly sales which months visitors drive.

## When Do Visitors Arrive, and How Long Do They Stay?

The same report puts the average nonresident stay at 4.87 nights, rising to 6.21 nights in summer. Only 11% of visitors came from January to March, while 46% came from July to September.

Plan a quiet-season offer for residents in winter and put your ad budget where the demand is in summer. A restaurant near Glacier Country can use the long summer stay to promote repeat visits within one trip. Airport arrivals give a hint of where visitors enter: Bozeman had about 753,000, Glacier Park International about 369,000 and Missoula about 301,000.

## How Should Seasons Shape Your Ad Settings?

Google Ads lets you schedule ads and adjust bids by day and time. Google describes "Presence or interest" targeting as reaching people in or interested in your location, and "Presence" as reaching only people physically there. A guide outfitter might use the first for trip planners who live in other states. A Great Falls plumber needs only "Presence".

If visitors book ahead, run ads in spring for the summer peak rather than during it. After a few weeks of data, shift spend toward the days and hours your phone rings.

## How Do You Win the Map Listing in a Small Market?

Google says relevance, distance and prominence drive local results and that you cannot pay for a better ranking. Complete every field, add photos and answer reviews. Use special hours for holidays and seasonal closures instead of editing regular hours, because visitors read hours before driving.

## FAQ

### How much should a Montana business spend on Google Ads?

No state benchmark exists. At the national average of $5.42 a click, $400 a month buys about 74 clicks. Begin small and measure cost per customer.

### Does tourism help a small local shop?

Only if visitors buy from you. Statewide nonresident spending of about $5.0 billion in 2024 does not show your share, so compare your own seasonal sales.

### When should a Montana business advertise to visitors?

Demand is concentrated in July to September, so plan spring campaigns for trip planners and keep a smaller winter budget for residents.

### Is Google Business Profile worth it?

Yes. It is free and sits where people compare nearby options. Keep hours accurate for seasonal changes.
