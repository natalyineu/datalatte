Growth hacking is the use of resource-light, cost-effective marketing tactics to grow and retain an active base, sell products and gain exposure, according to [Optimizely's glossary](https://www.optimizely.com/optimization-glossary/growth-hacking). For a local business, that means small, cheap, measurable tests on channels customers already use: a complete Google Business Profile, reviews, referrals, an owned email or SMS list, a local partnership, and one tracked paid test. No published study ranks these by return for local shops, so the practical way to choose is to test them with your own numbers.

## What Does Growth Hacking Mean, and Where Did the Term Come From?

The term was first coined by Sean Ellis, founder and chief executive of GrowthHackers.com. [Optimizely](https://www.optimizely.com/optimization-glossary/growth-hacking) states that he was head of growth at Dropbox.com. In a 2010 blog post, Ellis wrote that a growth hacker is a person whose true north is growth, and that everything they do is scrutinized by its potential impact on scalable growth.

The same glossary says growth hacks are often developed and implemented by a single person on the product or engineering team, without needing an entire marketing team. For a small shop, that is the useful part: one owner can run a test without a department. A secondary summary by [Consult Me](https://consult-me.co.uk/growth-hacking-for-small-businesses-practical-tips-and-tricks/) describes the approach as learning fast rather than launching big.

## Why Do Small Businesses Suit Growth Hacking?

Optimizely says growth hacking is most commonly associated with start-ups and small businesses, which lack large cash reserves but need quick results. That fit explains why cheap tests appeal to a shop with a tight budget.

[OnDeck's guide](https://www.ondeck.com/resources/growth-hacking), a secondary source citing Small Business Association advice, says companies worth less than $5m invest around 8% of their revenue in promoting themselves to achieve growth. Treat that figure as a reference point for planning, not a target a single shop must hit. Your own margins and customer lifetime value should decide how much you spend on each test.

## Which Six Tactics Should a Local Business Test First?

Start with the Google Business Profile, because editing it costs nothing. Check the details customers rely on: hours, categories, phone number and booking link. Google's live help pages set the current rules for representing a business, so read them before you change anything.

Reviews come next. Ask customers for feedback when they are most satisfied, and make the request a routine part of checkout. Keep review requests separate from any reward, and check the platform's live help page before offering anything in return.

Referrals give an existing customer a reason to bring a friend. Give each referrer a unique code so you can count results. The mechanics resemble [affiliate marketing for local business](/blog/affiliate-marketing-for-local-business), where a partner earns for each customer they send, so the same tracking habits apply.

An owned list is the fourth tactic. Collect sign-ups at the till and booking page, and state clearly what you will send. Check the consent rules that apply where you operate before you add anyone.

Partnerships come fifth. Swap offers with a neighbouring business whose customers match yours, print a card with a code on each side, and agree dates and limits in writing. Joint days and pop-ups are covered in [event marketing for local business](/blog/event-marketing-for-local-business).

The sixth tactic is one paid test. Choose one channel, one offer and one landing page, and set a fixed budget you can afford to lose. Discount marketplaces carry their own costs, so review [the Groupon cost breakdown](/blog/groupon-for-local-business-cost) before adding one as a test.

## How Do You Run Each Test as a Short Cycle?

The [Iskali guide](https://iskaliconsultoria.com/en/growth-hacking-in-digital-marketing-in-mexico/) presents its experiment cycle as six steps, ending with measuring and deciding. Its final step reads: if it works, it becomes a process. That is a useful test of whether a tactic earns a permanent place in your week.

For web pages, emails or ad creatives, [Consult Me](https://consult-me.co.uk/growth-hacking-for-small-businesses-practical-tips-and-tricks/) names tools such as Google Optimize and VWO as ways small teams can run split tests. Check whether each tool is still offered, because product lines change. Draft test copy faster with [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing), then check every claim yourself.

Fix the stopping date and the booking target before you start. Stopping early on a lucky week is the most common way a test misleads.

## What Does a Worked Test Budget Look Like?

All inputs below are **assumptions** for an imaginary hair salon. The point is the method: cost per booking is spend divided by new bookings.

| Test | Spend (assumption) | New bookings (assumption) | Cost per booking |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Review request at checkout | $20 printing | 5 | $4 |
| Partner card swap | $30 printing | 3 | $10 |
| Paid ad test | $300 | 6 | $50 |

On these numbers the checkout request is cheapest per booking, but it only works if happy customers already exist. The ad test costs the most per booking, yet it can scale with budget. Repeat each test for a second period before deciding, because one period of data can mislead.

[AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/upper-funnel-vs-lower-funnel), a secondary summary citing a Measured article, notes that businesses relying only on lower-funnel tactics often see stagnant or rising cost per lead. Upper-funnel investment, by contrast, can reduce acquisition costs over time. Keep that in mind before judging the paid test on a single month.

## Can a Referral Offer Grow a Local Shop the Way Dropbox Grew?

Consult Me's secondary summary says Dropbox's referral programme gave users extra storage space in exchange for inviting friends, and it describes this as a growth hacking example. The company grew by rewarding the invitation itself, which is the mechanism to copy.

A local version might give a credit to both people after the friend's first visit. No source checked here measures referral conversion for local shops, so run it with unique codes and count the results. Keep the referral reward separate from any review request, and check current platform policies before you launch.

## FAQ

### What is growth hacking in plain terms?

It is running small, quick, measurable experiments to find what brings customers in cheaply. Optimizely describes it as resource-light, cost-effective marketing. For a local business, that usually means the profile, reviews, referrals and a list.

### Who usually runs growth hacks?

Optimizely says growth hacks are often developed and implemented by a single person, without needing an entire marketing team. That makes the approach practical for a small owner.

### Is there a sourced ranking of which tactic works best for local shops?

No. None of the sources used here ranks these tactics by return for local businesses. Test them with your own booking counts and costs.

### How long should a test run?

The sources here give no required duration. Set a stopping date and a booking target before you start, and repeat promising tests for a second period before deciding.

### Where should a business check current review and listing rules?

Use the platform's live help pages, since policies change. For US businesses, the Federal Trade Commission also publishes rules on fake reviews.
