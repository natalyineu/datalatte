A local business website needs five things to meet GDPR: a privacy notice that says what you collect and why, consent before non-essential cookies, forms that collect only what you need, a way to answer data requests within one month, and a plan to report a breach within 72 hours. These come from the text of the regulation. This guide explains each in plain language. It is general information, not legal advice.

The rules apply when you handle personal data of people in the EU, and the UK has a near-identical UK GDPR. A café with a booking form, a salon with a newsletter box and a groomer with a contact page all qualify.

## What Does GDPR Require From a Small Website?

Article 83 sets two fine tiers. Breaking the core principles, the lawful basis and consent rules, or the rights of individuals can cost up to EUR 20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Other duties, such as security and record keeping, carry up to EUR 10 million or 2%.

Those are ceilings, not typical penalties for a small shop. They do show which duties regulators take seriously. The rest of this guide follows that list: what you tell people, what you ask consent for, what you keep, and how you respond.

Start by listing every place your site touches personal data. If you are choosing a platform, our [WordPress and Webflow comparison](/blog/webflow-vs-wordpress-small-business) shows what each handles. A contact form, booking widget, newsletter box, analytics script, live chat, embedded map and social pixel each count. You cannot write an accurate notice until you know the list.

## What Must a Privacy Notice Say?

Article 13 lists what you must tell people when you collect their data from them. The key items are who you are and how to contact you, why you use the data and your legal basis, who receives it, any transfer outside the EU, how long you keep it, and their rights to access, correct, erase, restrict, object and move their data.

You must also say that people can withdraw consent at any time, that they can complain to a supervisory authority, and whether giving the data is required.

Give this information when you collect the data, so link the notice next to every form. Write it in short sentences, one section per purpose: bookings, newsletter, analytics. A copied template that names tools you do not use is worse than none.

## When Do You Need Cookie Consent?

Consent is the rule for non-essential cookies and similar trackers. The UK regulator's guidance on storage and access technologies lists exceptions where consent is not needed: communication, strictly necessary, statistical purposes, appearance, emergency assistance and simple means of objecting. Marketing pixels and ad trackers are not on that list.

The regulator's page notes recent changes to the UK rules, and the detail of the statistical exception sits in its full chapters, so read those before relying on analytics without a banner. The EU position is set by each country's ePrivacy law, so check your local regulator.

Under Article 7, consent must be provable, clearly separate from other terms, and as easy to withdraw as to give. A banner with a large Accept button and a hidden reject option fails that test. Our [cookie consent guide](/blog/website-cookie-consent-small-business) covers banner setup step by step.

## How Do You Handle Data Requests and Objections?

People can ask what you hold, ask you to correct or delete it, or object. Article 12(3) requires a response without undue delay and within one month of receipt. It can be extended by two further months for complex or numerous requests, but you must tell the person within the first month and explain why.

For marketing, Article 21 gives people a right to object at any time, and you must stop using their data for direct marketing once they do. Put an unsubscribe link in every email and remove people the same day.

Keep a simple log: date received, what was asked, what you did, date answered. A shared spreadsheet is enough for a small business. The [GDPR email marketing guide](/blog/gdpr-compliant-email-marketing) goes deeper on lists and consent wording.

## Which Tools Need a Contract?

If a company handles personal data on your behalf, Article 28 treats it as a processor and requires a binding contract. That contract must say it acts only on your documented instructions, keeps data confidential and meets the security requirements of Article 32. Booking systems, email platforms and hosting are typical processors.

Ask each vendor for its data processing terms and save a copy when you sign.

Article 30 asks for records of processing, but organisations with fewer than 250 people are exempt unless the processing is likely to risk people's rights, is not occasional, or includes special category or criminal data. A business that runs regular newsletters or customer bookings is processing regularly, so assume you need a short record: what data, why, which tool, how long.

UK businesses should also check the data protection fee. The ICO says organisations, including sole traders, that use personal information need to pay the fee unless exempt. Its page did not list the tiers when checked, so confirm the amount on the ICO site.

## What Do You Do If Data Leaks?

Article 33 says to notify the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach. A later report must explain the delay. No notification is needed if the breach is unlikely to risk individuals' rights and freedoms.

Prepare before it happens. Know who on your team decides, where passwords are stored, and which tools hold customer lists. A lost phone with a booking app logged in is a breach. So is an email sent to the wrong list.

## FAQ

### Does GDPR apply to a small local business?
Yes, if you process personal data of people in the EU or UK. Size does not exempt you, though some record-keeping duties are lighter for organisations under 250 people.

### What is the maximum GDPR fine?
Up to EUR 20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, for the most serious infringements. Other duties carry up to EUR 10 million or 2%.

### Do I need a cookie banner?
You need consent before non-essential cookies such as marketing trackers. Strictly necessary cookies are exempt, and the UK regulator lists other exceptions, including statistical purposes, under conditions.

### How long do I have to answer a data request?
One month from receipt, extendable by two months for complex cases if you tell the person within the first month.

### How fast must I report a breach?
Within 72 hours of becoming aware, where feasible, unless the breach is unlikely to risk individuals.
