Google says AI Overviews need no special optimization or extra tags. For a local business, the work is ordinary local SEO: one accurate Business Profile, pages Google can crawl and read, and honest reviews and content. The real change is the click. Pew Research Center found that users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, compared with 15% without one. That study covered general searches, so it shows a direction rather than a forecast for any single local site.

## Do AI Overviews Reduce Clicks to Local Websites?

Pew tracked the browsing of 900 US adults in March 2025 and analysed 68,879 Google searches, 12,593 of which produced an AI summary. Users clicked a link inside the summary in about 1% of visits. Sessions ended after the page in 26% of cases with a summary, compared with 16% without one, so more visits stopped without a further step.

Pew studied general searches, not local business queries. Local searches often show a map pack, which may change the pattern. The figures are useful for planning, but they do not measure what your own site will see. Check your own analytics before assuming a loss of the same size.

## What Does Google Say About Getting Included?

Google's [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says a page must be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet. It states there is no extra requirement and no special structured data for AI features. Structured data should match the text a visitor sees on the page.

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) adds that sites following the Search Essentials are more likely to show up in results, although no site is guaranteed a place. For a local business, this means the reliable work is clarity, accuracy and crawlability. Anyone selling an AI-only tag is selling something Google does not describe.

## Does the Local Pack Still Matter?

Yes, and the two do not always agree. A secondary report, republished by MarTech Series in June 2026, compared Google AI Mode with the Local Pack across 1,120 queries. It found that 28.5% of Local Pack businesses were missing from AI Mode for the same query. Among the top three results, overlap was 48%.

That study covers AI Mode rather than AI Overviews, and it focuses on verticals such as plumbers and dentists. The full methodology was not reviewed for this article. Even so, it suggests that a strong map pack position does not guarantee a mention in AI answers. Maintain the map pack basics and do not treat them as a substitute for AI visibility.

## What Should a Local Business Check First?

Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) give a practical starting list. Work through it in order before changing anything else:

1. Confirm there is only one profile for the business, since duplicates can cause display problems on Maps and Search.
2. Match the name to the real-world name on signage, stationery and the website. Do not add taglines, hours or phone numbers to it.
3. Make the address or service area accurate and precise.
4. Choose the fewest categories that describe the core business.
5. Keep the business description free of links, prices and promotions, which the guidelines do not allow.

Google's guidance also says published content should highlight what makes a business unique. Write for customers first, and let the search benefit follow.

## Can Google Find and Read Your Pages?

Start with a site: search. If your pages appear, they are in the index. If they do not, check the technical requirements in Google's documentation. Google mainly finds pages through links from pages it has already crawled, and a sitemap can help it discover the rest.

Google aims to see a page the way an average user does. If CSS or JavaScript is hidden, Google may not understand the page. If content changes by location, check what Google sees from its usual US crawler location. The URL Inspection Tool in Search Console shows how Google renders a specific page. For more on describing a business to search engines, read the [local business schema markup guide](/blog/local-business-schema-markup-rich-snippets-google).

## Should You Add Special Markup for AI Answers?

No AI-only markup is described by Google, so there is nothing AI-specific to install. Google's LocalBusiness structured data documentation explains how to describe a business on a page. Use it to describe what is already visible and accurate, not to chase a feature.

Structured data only helps when it matches the page and the real business. If the page embeds a map, it should still state the address in plain text. The [Google Maps embed guide](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) covers that setup in detail.

## How Long Before Changes Show in Results?

Google says every change takes time to reflect. Some take effect within a few hours, while others can take several months. The SEO Starter Guide suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether work had a beneficial effect.

Not every change produces a visible result. If results fall short and the strategy allows it, adjust and test again. Record the date of each change so later results can be matched to the work that caused them.

## How Do You Track Results Without AI Reporting?

Do not expect a clean AI-only report. Google folds AI feature traffic into its standard search reporting, so the split is not available in Search Console. Track a fixed set of searches your customers use, check them on the same day each month, and note whether the business appears and how the results look. Keep the list stable so months can be compared.

Pair those checks with your own records: calls, direction requests, form fills and bookings. These come from the phone system or booking tool, so they do not depend on how Google labels its traffic. A vendor blog post on AI visibility trackers exists, but it names no independent study, so treat its claims as marketing.

## FAQ

### Do I need special markup for AI Overviews?

Google does not describe an AI-only tag. Structured data should describe the visible page accurately and match the real business.

### Will one Business Profile help with AI answers?

Google's guidelines call for one profile per business with accurate details. Duplicate or inaccurate profiles can cause display problems, so fixing them is a sound first step.

### How long should I wait before judging changes?

Google suggests waiting a few weeks. Some changes show within hours, while others take several months.

### Can I see AI traffic separately in Search Console?

The sources reviewed for this article do not confirm a separate AI report. Track results with fixed searches and your own call and booking records instead.
