## What Do AI Social Media Analytics Tools Do?

They collect numbers from your accounts (reach, engagement, clicks, follower changes) and add automated features: summaries, suggestions for post wording, sentiment labels on comments and reviews, or a chat assistant that answers questions about your data. The AI layer sits on top of the same figures you could read yourself.

What they cannot do is know which post sold a haircut or a latte. That link comes from your own tracking. Our guide to [data analytics for small business](/blog/data-analytics-for-small-business) covers the basics, and [predictive analytics for small business](/blog/predictive-analytics-small-business) explains why forecasts need history to work.

## How Do You Connect Social Posts to Bookings?

Tag every link you post. Google Analytics Help says to always use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign on a campaign URL, and that values are case sensitive, so utm_source=instagram and utm_source=Instagram appear as different sources. Use lowercase throughout. Without tags, reports show "(not set)".

A simple naming scheme: source is the platform, medium is social, campaign is the promotion, such as spring-colour-offer. Put the tagged link in your bio, Stories and posts. Then add a "how did you hear about us" field to your booking form. The two together show which posts led to bookings. See also [Google Analytics 4 setup for a local business](/blog/google-analytics-4-setup-local-business).

## What Should You Look at Each Month?

Keep a short list:

1. Link clicks from social to the website, by platform
2. Bookings or calls that name social as the source
3. The five posts with the most saves, shares and clicks
4. The five posts with the least, and what they had in common
5. Follower change, as context only

AI summaries can speed up steps three and four. Treat any prediction (best time to post, "your audience prefers video") as a hint to test, not a fact, because a small account gives a small sample. We did not find a published minimum sample size for these tools, so do not trust a conclusion drawn from a handful of posts.

## Is It Worth It? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions** for a salon owner who tags links and asks the source question.

| Input | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Social posts in a month | 16 |
| Link clicks to booking page | 120 |
| Bookings that mention social | 9 |
| Share from the top 4 posts (assumption) | 6 of the 9 |
| Margin per booking | $35 |
| Margin from social bookings | $315 |
| Analytics tool cost (Metricool Starter) | $20 |

On these assumptions the owner learns that four posts produced most bookings. Repeating that type, and dropping the weakest, is a free change. The $20 tool pays for itself only if the knowledge changes what is posted; the free tier may be enough to start. The numbers are illustrative, not benchmarks.

## FAQ

### Do I need AI for social media analytics?

No. Native platform insights, tagged links and a source question answer most small-business questions. AI summaries save time once you have data.

### What is the cheapest option?

Metricool offers a free plan for one brand with 30 days of analytics. Check what your social apps already show before paying.

### Can AI predict my best posting time?

Tools suggest times from your history, but a small account gives little data. Test a suggestion for a few weeks before trusting it.

### How do I know if a post caused a booking?

Use tagged links and a booking-form question. Neither is perfect, but both beat guessing.

### Is customer data safe in these tools?

We did not review vendor privacy terms. Read them before connecting customer messages or reviews.
