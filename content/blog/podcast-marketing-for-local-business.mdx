Podcast marketing for a local business means publishing your own show, or appearing on others, to build trust with people who may hire you. It is slow and works best for businesses selling expertise: advisers, trades, clinics, coaches. Hosting is cheap. Buzzsprout has a free plan with 2 hours of uploads a month, and its Audio plan is $15 a month.

The audience exists: Edison Research's Infinite Dial 2025 found 55% of Americans 12 and older are monthly podcast consumers. This guide covers the setup, the costs, what to say and how to measure results. ## Is a Podcast Worth It for a Local Business?

It depends on your goal. A show will not bring customers within weeks. It can build trust slowly, give you content to share and make you easy to recommend. National listening figures say little about your town, and no reliable data on local-business podcast results was found.

If you only have time for one marketing channel, fix your Business Profile and reviews first; see our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist). A podcast is an add-on once those work.

## What Does It Cost to Start?

Equipment can be a headset and your phone. Hosting is the recurring cost. Buzzsprout's page lists a free plan with 2 hours of uploads a month, and paid plans of $15 (Audio), $25 (Audio + Video) and $30 (Multi-Podcast) a month billed annually. The Audio plan includes up to 72 hours of uploads per year. Spotify for Creators offers distribution tools, but its page did not state pricing, so check before you rely on it.

To list your show on Apple Podcasts you need an Apple Podcasts Connect account and an RSS feed that follows RSS 2.0, is publicly accessible and has artwork and at least one episode. Most hosts generate this feed for you.

## What Should the Show Be About?

Answer real customer questions. A plumber can explain what a water-heater quote should include. A vet can explain what to ask at a first visit. Keep episodes short and plain, and repeat the next step: book, call or visit.

Interview local people too: suppliers, community groups, other shop owners. Guests share episodes with their own audience, which helps reach.

## How Do You Turn Episodes Into Leads?

- Put a short link in every episode description and say it aloud.
- Embed episodes on a page of your website with a transcript, which also gives search engines text to read.
- Send each new episode to your email list. See our email marketing for restaurants guide for list-building ideas that apply to any local business.
- Cut a short clip for social posts.

Edison's 2025 release notes that YouTube is the platform used most often by 33% of US weekly podcast listeners, so consider a video version if your host supports it.

## The Math: What Does a Year Cost?

All inputs are **assumptions**.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Episodes per year | 26 (every two weeks) |
| Length | 25 minutes each |
| Upload total | about 11 hours, within a 72-hour plan |
| Hosting | $15 x 12 = $180 |
| Your time per episode | 3 hours |
| Your time per year | 78 hours |

Your own time is the larger cost. If one extra customer worth $500 comes from the show in a year, hosting is covered. A show with no customer path cannot pay back, so decide what action you want before episode one. ## How Do You Measure It?

Track visits to your show link, calls that mention the podcast and form answers to "How did you hear about us?" Your hosting dashboard shows downloads, but downloads are not customers. Review after six months.

## FAQ

### Do I need expensive equipment?

No. A decent microphone or headset and a quiet room are enough to begin. Quality of content matters more.

### How often should I publish?

Pick a schedule you can keep. A steady, shorter cadence beats a burst followed by silence.

### Is podcasting better than blogging?

They do different jobs. Audio builds familiarity; text ranks in search. Use the transcript on your site for both.
