ChatGPT can help a small business with marketing by drafting and editing copy, brainstorming ideas, summarising customer feedback and planning content, but it works best as a fast first-draft assistant, not an autopilot. You still supply the facts, the local detail and the final judgement.

Below are 20 practical uses, grouped by the job they do, with example prompts you can adapt. Everything here works with the free version or a paid plan (typically around $20 per month for ChatGPT Plus, though plans change). Other assistants such as Claude or Gemini can do similar jobs.

## Before you start: how to get better output

Generic prompts produce generic copy. Give ChatGPT context every time:

- Who you are: business type, neighbourhood, size, vibe.
- Who you are talking to: your regulars, new parents, office workers.
- What you want: format, length, tone, call to action.
- What to avoid: clichés, claims you cannot back up, discounts you do not offer.

Never paste private customer data (names, emails, health details about people or pets) into a public AI tool, and always check facts, prices and opening hours before publishing. ## Content and social media (1-6)

**1. Social post drafts.** Ask for five post ideas for the week based on what is actually happening in your business: a new menu item, a quiet Tuesday slot, a team member's birthday.
*Prompt:* "I run a neighbourhood café. Write five Instagram captions for next week. Facts: new cardamom bun on Wednesday, live music Friday 6 pm. Warm, short, one emoji max."

**2. Content calendars.** Have it build a four-week plan with themes, then you drop in real photos and details. Our [content calendar guide](/blog/content-calendar-for-small-business) shows a template.

**3. Caption variations.** Turn one idea into versions for Instagram, Facebook, Google Business Profile and email.

**4. Short video scripts.** Ask for 15 to 30 second Reel or TikTok outlines: hook, three beats, call to action.

**5. Hashtag and keyword ideas.** Useful as a starting list. Check them in the platform before relying on them.

**6. Repurposing.** Paste one blog post or newsletter and ask for a handful of social snippets.

## Website and SEO (7-11)

**7. Service page drafts.** Provide your services, area and what makes you different, then edit heavily so it sounds like you and contains accurate details.

**8. Title tags and meta descriptions.** Ask for several options under 60 and 155 characters respectively.

**9. FAQ sections.** Ask for the questions customers typically ask, then answer them with your own policies.

**10. Blog outlines.** Request outlines, not finished posts. Your local knowledge is what makes content rank and convert. See [blog content strategy for local business](/blog/blog-content-strategy-local-business).

**11. Google Business Profile posts and descriptions.** Draft updates and the business description (750 character limit) then trim and personalise. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers what to include.

## Reviews and customer communication (12-15)

**12. Review replies.** Paste a review (without personal details) and ask for a polite, specific reply in your voice. Always edit so replies do not sound templated.

**13. Handling a negative review.** Ask for a calm, non-defensive draft that acknowledges the problem and moves the conversation offline.

**14. Email newsletters.** Draft subject lines and body copy for a monthly update. See [email marketing for small business](/blog/email-marketing-for-small-business).

**15. Booking and reminder messages.** Draft SMS and email templates for confirmations, reminders and rebooking nudges.

## Ads and offers (16-17)

**16. Ad copy variations.** Generate several headlines and descriptions for Google Ads or Meta Ads, then test them. Check character limits and policy rules, because AI sometimes oversteps both.

**17. Offer ideas.** Brainstorm promotions for slow periods, holidays and local events, then pick ones that fit your margins.

## Research and operations (18-20)

**18. Summarising feedback.** Paste (anonymised) reviews or survey comments and ask for themes. Treat it as a pointer, not proof.

**19. Competitor comparison prompts.** Describe what you know about nearby competitors and ask what gaps you could fill. ChatGPT does not know your local market, so supply the facts.

**20. Standard operating procedures.** Turn your messy notes into step-by-step checklists for opening, closing or onboarding new hires.

## What ChatGPT is not good at

- **Facts about your business.** It does not know your prices, hours or policies unless you tell it, and it can state wrong things confidently.
- **Local specifics.** It may invent landmarks or events.
- **Live data.** Unless browsing is on, it cannot see current search results or competitor pages.
- **Your voice.** Without examples it defaults to a bland, upbeat tone.
- **Compliance.** Health, finance and legal claims need human review.

## A simple workflow that works

1. **Brief:** Write down the goal, audience, key facts and tone.
2. **Draft:** Ask for two or three options.
3. **Edit:** Cut clichés, add real details, check every claim.
4. **Publish:** Add real photos and local references.
5. **Review:** Note what performed and feed that back into your next prompt.

Keep a "voice sheet" with three samples of your own writing and your do-not-say list, and paste it at the start of new chats.

## FAQ

**Q: Is ChatGPT good enough for small business marketing?**
A: Yes for first drafts, ideas and editing, especially when you give it detailed context. It is not a replacement for knowing your customers, and every output needs a human check.

**Q: Will Google penalise AI-written content?**
A: Google has said it focuses on content quality and helpfulness rather than how content is produced. Thin, generic or inaccurate pages perform poorly whoever wrote them, so add real local expertise.

**Q: Is the free version enough?**
A: Often yes for occasional use. Paid plans add higher limits and more capable models, which can help if you use it daily.

**Q: Is it safe to put customer information into ChatGPT?**
A: Avoid pasting personal or sensitive data. Anonymise reviews and messages, and check your tool's data settings and your local privacy rules.

## Mistakes that waste time

The most common problem is publishing the first draft. AI copy tends to lean on the same phrases, such as "elevate your experience" or "look no further", and customers notice. Another is asking for too much at once: a prompt that requests a full month of content usually returns something shallow, while a prompt for one specific post with real facts returns something you can actually use. Finally, keep a short log of which prompts gave you good results so you can reuse them instead of starting from scratch each week.
