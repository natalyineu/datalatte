If you're considering audio advertising for your local business, the three realistic self-serve options are Spotify, Pandora, and iHeartMedia. Each platform attracts different audiences, charges differently, and works better for specific business types.

This guide cuts through the marketing speak — here's how they actually compare for a local advertiser spending $500–$3,000/month, not a national brand with a $500,000 budget.

## Platform Overview: The Key Differences

Before diving into details, here's the honest one-line summary of each:

- **Spotify:** Best targeting, biggest audience, most design-forward — great for reaching under-40 customers
- **Pandora:** Best for local geo-targeting and commute-time intent — great for food, coffee, and drive-to destinations
- **iHeartMedia:** Best for brand trust and broadcast credibility — great for higher-ticket services where trust matters

## Spotify Ads: Deep Dive

### Who listens to Spotify?

Spotify's audience skews young and urban — 60%+ of active users are under 35. They're listening on mobile, at the gym, commuting, cooking, and studying. Playlist and mood-based listening means the context of your ad is predictable (someone hearing your ad in a "focus work" playlist is in a different mindset than someone in a "party" playlist).

### Spotify Ad Studio: What you get

- **Targeting options:** Age, gender, location (city-level), interests, playlist genres, devices, listening behaviour
- **Ad formats:** Audio (15s or 30s), display banner shown during ad, podcast ads (separate buy)
- **Geo-targeting:** City-level — good for local businesses in most US and UK markets
- **Minimum budget:** ~$250/campaign
- **Self-serve or managed:** Self-serve via Ad Studio; managed service requires ~$25,000 minimum spend

### Spotify ad production

Spotify provides a free voice-over tool in Ad Studio — you type your script, choose a voice, and add background music from their library. The quality is serviceable for testing. For a polished campaign, hire a voiceover artist on Voices.com or Voice123 ($50–200 for a 30-second spot).

### When Spotify works for local businesses

**Strong fit:**
- Coffee shops and cafés (younger audience, lifestyle alignment)
- Fitness studios and gyms (active audience, gym playlist context)
- Fashion and lifestyle retail
- Bars, restaurants targeting under-35 drinkers
- Mobile apps or tech-forward services

**Weaker fit:**
- Home services targeting homeowners (skews older)
- Healthcare-adjacent services
- B2B services

### Spotify CPM reality

Spotify quotes CPMs of $15–30 for self-serve campaigns. In practice, local campaigns with tight geo-targeting tend to land at the higher end ($22–28 CPM) because a smaller audience pool is being targeted. A $500 budget at $25 CPM delivers 20,000 impressions — in a city of 500,000 people, that's a small but concentrated hit.

<Callout type="tip">
Spotify's "frequency cap" setting is important for local campaigns. Set it to 3–4 exposures per user per week maximum — audio ads become irritating quickly if the same person hears your ad 10 times in a week.
</Callout>

## Pandora Ads: Deep Dive

### Who listens to Pandora?

Pandora's audience is primarily US-based, older than Spotify (median age ~35–45), and dominated by drive-time and at-work listening. A significant share of Pandora's listening happens in cars — making it uniquely strong for "on my way" local intent. Someone hearing your coffee shop ad during their morning commute is in precisely the right mindset.

### Pandora Ad Studio: What you get

- **Targeting:** Location (city, DMA, zip code), age, gender, genre preferences, device
- **Unique advantage:** Audio + visual — Pandora shows a display banner alongside the audio ad that can be tapped to visit your website or call
- **Minimum budget:** ~$250/campaign
- **Listener quality:** Pandora's free tier has high ad load, meaning listeners are accustomed to audio interruptions and don't tune out as aggressively

### Pandora's local business advantage

Pandora has historically been more popular with local direct-response advertisers than Spotify — partly because of its drive-time audience and partly because its visual companion ad turns an audio spot into a clickable unit. A listener who hears "Coffee 2 minutes from your exit" can tap the banner and get directions immediately.

**Best local business fits for Pandora:**
- Coffee shops and drive-thrus
- Quick-service restaurants and fast casual
- Gas stations and auto services
- Any business where "on my way" captures a high-value moment

**Pandora CPM range:** $10–18 for self-serve local campaigns — the most affordable of the three platforms.

## iHeartMedia Ads: Deep Dive

### Who listens to iHeartMedia?

iHeartMedia's digital streaming audience uses the iHeartRadio app, which aggregates streaming stations across the US. Unlike Spotify and Pandora, iHeartMedia also sells traditional broadcast radio — the digital buy gives you streaming only, but the brand carries broadcast credibility.

The audience skews slightly older than Spotify but is diverse — iHeartMedia reaches more rural and suburban listeners than Spotify's urban-heavy base.

### iHeartMedia digital self-serve

- **Minimum budget:** ~$500 for digital self-serve
- **Targeting:** DMA (designated market area), city, zip, age, genre
- **Ad formats:** 15s, 30s, 60s audio + display companion
- **Note:** True broadcast radio requires a direct buy with a local iHeart station representative — this has much higher minimums but reaches a different (older, larger) audience

### When iHeartMedia works for local businesses

**Strong fit:**
- Home services (plumbing, HVAC, roofing) — older homeowner audience
- Healthcare and dental practices
- Legal services and financial advisors
- Auto dealerships and mechanics
- Any business where "trustworthy brand" matters more than immediate impulse

**Weaker fit:**
- Businesses targeting under-30 customers
- Highly visual products/services (audio doesn't do the product justice)

<Callout type="info">
iHeartMedia's local broadcast radio buy (separate from digital) can reach 60–80% of adults in a market — significantly broader than any streaming platform. If you're a home service business doing $500K+/year in revenue, the broadcast buy is worth a conversation with your local station.
</Callout>

## Creative: What Makes a Good Audio Ad for Local Businesses

The biggest reason local audio campaigns underperform isn't the platform — it's the creative. A bad audio ad wastes money on any platform.

**What works:**
- **Lead with the local hook:** "In [City Name]?" or "If you're near [landmark]..." creates immediate relevance
- **One message, one action:** "Visit us this week" is better than "Visit us, follow us, and check out our app"
- **Say your name twice:** Once at the start, once at the end
- **15 seconds > 30 seconds for most local businesses:** Listener patience for audio ads is limited
- **Real voice > AI voice:** A genuine local accent or personality outperforms generic voiceover

**What doesn't work:**
- Reading your entire menu or service list
- Jingles that take 10 seconds to get to the point
- No call to action
- Complex URLs — use your business name only ("find us on Google" or "visit [YourBusinessName].com")

## Running a Fair Three-Way Test

If you have the budget to test all three simultaneously:

1. **Equal budget split:** $300 each minimum per platform for 30 days
2. **Unique tracking per platform:** Use platform-specific promo codes or UTM-tagged landing pages
3. **Same core message:** Different platforms, same offer — so you're testing the platform, not the creative
4. **Measure cost-per-response:** Not CPM — actual calls, visits, or redemptions
5. **Give it 30 days:** Audio builds frequency and recall, not immediate clicks

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which audio ad platform is cheapest for a local business?

Pandora typically has the lowest CPM ($10–18) of the three self-serve platforms. However, the cheapest cost-per-result depends on your specific audience and market — in some cities, Pandora's audience may be smaller, making Spotify more efficient despite the higher CPM.

### Do I need professional audio production to advertise on these platforms?

No. All three platforms offer free creative tools — you type a script, choose a voice, and the platform generates the audio. The quality is adequate for testing. Once you find a message that works, invest $150–300 in a professional voiceover for your best-performing ad.

### Is audio advertising worth it compared to Google or Meta Ads?

For most local businesses, Google Ads and Meta Ads should come first — they capture higher intent and convert more directly. Audio advertising works best as an awareness layer that makes your Google and Meta ads more effective (people who've heard your name are more likely to click when they see you in search results).

### Can I target specific neighbourhoods on these platforms?

Pandora and iHeartMedia allow zip code targeting (US). Spotify offers city-level targeting but not zip-level for self-serve campaigns. For hyper-local campaigns within a specific neighbourhood, Meta Ads with radius targeting gives you better precision than any audio platform.

### Do people actually listen to audio ads?

Completion rates for Pandora's 30-second ads average 85–90% because the free tier doesn't allow skipping during ad breaks. Spotify's completion rates are similar for non-skippable formats. Compare this to video ads (30–40% completion for 30-second video) — audio completion is actually very high.

## The Bottom Line

- **Spotify** is the most sophisticated platform with the best targeting — ideal for lifestyle-oriented local businesses reaching under-35 customers
- **Pandora** offers the best CPM and strongest drive-time/commute-time local intent — ideal for coffee shops, restaurants, and drive-to destinations
- **iHeartMedia** carries the most brand trust and reaches an older, more suburban audience — ideal for home services, healthcare, and higher-ticket local services

For most local businesses starting with audio advertising: **begin with Pandora**, run a 30-day test at $300–500, measure redemptions or calls, then decide whether to expand to Spotify.

Audio advertising works best alongside, not instead of, your core intent channels. If your Google Ads and Google Business Profile aren't dialled in yet, fix those first — then add audio as a brand-building layer.
