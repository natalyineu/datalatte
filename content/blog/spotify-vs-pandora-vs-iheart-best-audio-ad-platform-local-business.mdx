For most local businesses, Pandora is the best first audio ad platform because of its drive-time listening and local targeting, Spotify is best for reaching under-35 lifestyle audiences such as gym-goers and café regulars, and iHeartMedia is best when brand trust and an older, suburban audience matter (home services, healthcare). All three offer self-serve tools with modest minimum budgets, but pricing and minimums change, so confirm current figures in each platform's ad tool.

This guide compares them for a local advertiser spending a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month, not a national brand with a six-figure budget.

---

## Platform Overview: The Key Differences

Before diving into details, here's the honest one-line summary of each:

- **Spotify:** Best targeting, biggest audience, most design-forward — great for reaching under-40 customers
- **Pandora:** Best for local geo-targeting and commute-time intent — great for food, coffee, and drive-to destinations
- **iHeartMedia:** Best for brand trust and broadcast credibility — great for higher-ticket services where trust matters

| Platform | Audience skew | Self-serve tool | Targeting | Ad lengths |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spotify | Younger, urban, mobile | Spotify Ad Studio | City, age, gender, interests, genres, devices | Typically 15s and 30s |
| Pandora | US-focused, drive-time and at-work listening | Pandora Ad Studio | City, DMA, zip code, age, genre | Typically 15s and 30s |
| iHeartRadio digital | Broader, more suburban and older | Digital self-serve or sales rep | DMA, city, zip, age, genre | Typically 15s, 30s and 60s |

Costs are quoted as CPM (cost per thousand listens) and vary by market, targeting and season. As a rough planning guide only, Pandora has tended to be the most affordable of the three and Spotify the most expensive for tightly geo-targeted campaigns. Minimum budgets are usually a few hundred dollars per campaign; check each tool for the current minimum and pricing.

## Spotify Ads: Deep Dive

### Who listens to Spotify?

Spotify's audience skews young and urban compared with the other two platforms. They're listening on mobile, at the gym, commuting, cooking, and studying. Playlist and mood-based listening means the context of your ad is predictable (someone hearing your ad in a "focus work" playlist is in a different mindset than someone in a "party" playlist).

### Spotify Ad Studio: What you get

- **Targeting options:** Age, gender, location (city-level), interests, playlist genres, devices, listening behaviour
- **Ad formats:** Audio (15s or 30s), display banner shown during ad, podcast ads (separate buy)
- **Geo-targeting:** City-level — good for local businesses in most US and UK markets
- **Minimum budget:** a few hundred dollars per campaign (check current minimum)
- **Self-serve or managed:** Self-serve via Ad Studio; a managed service is available at much higher spend levels

### Spotify ad production

Spotify provides a free voice-over tool in Ad Studio — you type your script, choose a voice, and add background music from their library. The quality is serviceable for testing. For a polished campaign, hire a voiceover artist on a freelance marketplace (typically a modest one-off fee for a 30-second spot).

### When Spotify works for local businesses

**Strong fit:**
- Coffee shops and cafés (younger audience, lifestyle alignment)
- Fitness studios and gyms (active audience, gym playlist context)
- Fashion and lifestyle retail
- Bars, restaurants targeting under-35 drinkers
- Mobile apps or tech-forward services

**Weaker fit:**
- Home services targeting homeowners (skews older)
- Healthcare-adjacent services
- B2B services

### Spotify CPM reality

Spotify's self-serve CPMs are generally higher than Pandora's, and local campaigns with tight geo-targeting tend to land toward the upper end because a smaller audience pool is being targeted. Do the math before you launch: divide your budget by the CPM and multiply by 1,000 to estimate impressions, then compare that to the size of your city. A small budget in a large city is a concentrated but limited hit.

<Callout type="tip">
Spotify's "frequency cap" setting is important for local campaigns. Set it to 3–4 exposures per user per week maximum — audio ads become irritating quickly if the same person hears your ad 10 times in a week.
</Callout>

## Pandora Ads: Deep Dive

### Who listens to Pandora?

Pandora's audience is primarily US-based, generally older than Spotify's, and dominated by drive-time and at-work listening. A significant share of Pandora's listening happens in cars — making it uniquely strong for "on my way" local intent. Someone hearing your coffee shop ad during their morning commute is in precisely the right mindset.

### Pandora Ad Studio: What you get

- **Targeting:** Location (city, DMA, zip code), age, gender, genre preferences, device
- **Unique advantage:** Audio + visual — Pandora shows a display banner alongside the audio ad that can be tapped to visit your website or call
- **Minimum budget:** a few hundred dollars per campaign (check current minimum)
- **Listener quality:** the free tier includes ad breaks, so listeners are used to audio ads

### Pandora's local business advantage

Pandora has historically been more popular with local direct-response advertisers than Spotify — partly because of its drive-time audience and partly because its visual companion ad turns an audio spot into a clickable unit. A listener who hears "Coffee 2 minutes from your exit" can tap the banner and get directions immediately.

**Best local business fits for Pandora:**
- Coffee shops and drive-thrus
- Quick-service restaurants and fast casual
- Gas stations and auto services
- Any business where "on my way" captures a high-value moment

**Pandora CPM:** typically the most affordable of the three for self-serve local campaigns, but verify in Ad Studio for your market.

## iHeartMedia Ads: Deep Dive

### Who listens to iHeartMedia?

iHeartMedia's digital streaming audience uses the iHeartRadio app, which aggregates streaming stations across the US. Unlike Spotify and Pandora, iHeartMedia also sells traditional broadcast radio — the digital buy gives you streaming only, but the brand carries broadcast credibility.

The audience skews slightly older than Spotify but is diverse — iHeartMedia reaches more rural and suburban listeners than Spotify's urban-heavy base.

### iHeartMedia digital self-serve

- **Minimum budget:** typically higher than Spotify and Pandora for digital self-serve (check current minimum)
- **Targeting:** DMA (designated market area), city, zip, age, genre
- **Ad formats:** 15s, 30s, 60s audio + display companion
- **Note:** True broadcast radio requires a direct buy with a local iHeart station representative — this has much higher minimums but reaches a different (older, larger) audience

### When iHeartMedia works for local businesses

**Strong fit:**
- Home services (plumbing, HVAC, roofing) — older homeowner audience
- Healthcare and dental practices
- Legal services and financial advisors
- Auto dealerships and mechanics
- Any business where "trustworthy brand" matters more than immediate impulse

**Weaker fit:**
- Businesses targeting under-30 customers
- Highly visual products/services (audio doesn't do the product justice)

<Callout type="info">
iHeartMedia's local broadcast radio buy (separate from digital) can reach a much larger share of a market than streaming alone. If you're an established home service business, a broadcast buy is worth a conversation with your local station rep.
</Callout>

## Creative: What Makes a Good Audio Ad for Local Businesses

The biggest reason local audio campaigns underperform isn't the platform — it's the creative. A bad audio ad wastes money on any platform.

**What works:**
- **Lead with the local hook:** "In [City Name]?" or "If you're near [landmark]..." creates immediate relevance
- **One message, one action:** "Visit us this week" is better than "Visit us, follow us, and check out our app"
- **Say your name twice:** Once at the start, once at the end
- **15 seconds > 30 seconds for most local businesses:** Listener patience for audio ads is limited
- **Real voice > AI voice:** A genuine local accent or personality outperforms generic voiceover

**What doesn't work:**
- Reading your entire menu or service list
- Jingles that take 10 seconds to get to the point
- No call to action
- Complex URLs — use your business name only ("find us on Google" or "visit [YourBusinessName].com")

## Budget Recommendations for Local Audio Advertising

| Budget | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Very small budgets | Audio needs enough frequency to build recall. If you cannot sustain a campaign for several weeks, put the money into Google Ads first. |
| A few hundred to about $1,000/month | Test Pandora only: usually the lowest CPM and easiest local targeting for a first audio buy. |
| About $1,000 to $2,500/month | Pandora as the primary platform, Spotify as a secondary test. |
| $2,500+/month | Test all three, with different creative for each audience. |

---

## Running a Fair Three-Way Test

If you have the budget to test all three simultaneously:

1. **Equal budget split:** the same budget per platform for 30 days, high enough to reach meaningful frequency
2. **Unique tracking per platform:** Use platform-specific promo codes or UTM-tagged landing pages
3. **Same core message:** Different platforms, same offer — so you're testing the platform, not the creative
4. **Measure cost-per-response:** Not CPM — actual calls, visits, or redemptions
5. **Give it 30 days:** Audio builds frequency and recall, not immediate clicks

## FAQ

**Q: Which audio ad platform is cheapest for a local business?**
A: Pandora has typically had the lowest CPM of the three self-serve platforms. The cheapest cost per result depends on your audience and market, though. In some cities Spotify may be more efficient despite a higher CPM, so test before you commit.

**Q: Do I need professional audio production to advertise on these platforms?**
A: No. The platforms offer free creative tools where you provide a script and choose a voice. That is fine for testing. Once you find a message that works, consider paying for a professional voiceover.

**Q: Is audio advertising worth it compared to Google or Meta Ads?**
A: For most local businesses, [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and [Meta Ads](/services/meta-ads) should come first because they capture higher intent. Audio works best as an awareness layer that makes your other ads more effective.

**Q: Can I target specific neighborhoods with audio ads?**
A: Pandora and iHeartMedia have offered zip code targeting in the US, while Spotify has focused on city-level targeting for self-serve. For hyper-local campaigns, Meta Ads with radius targeting is usually more precise. Check each tool for current options.

**Q: Do people actually listen to audio ads?**
A: Audio ads in non-skippable breaks tend to have high completion rates, often higher than video ads. Actual numbers depend on the format and listener, so use each platform's reporting to see your own results.

## The Bottom Line

- **Spotify** has the most sophisticated targeting and suits lifestyle-oriented local businesses reaching younger customers.
- **Pandora** is typically the most affordable and has strong drive-time intent, ideal for coffee shops, restaurants and drive-to destinations.
- **iHeartMedia** carries brand trust and reaches an older, more suburban audience, ideal for home services, healthcare and higher-ticket services.

For most local businesses starting with audio: **begin with Pandora**, run a 30-day test, measure redemptions or calls, then decide whether to add Spotify. Audio works best alongside your core intent channels, so get your Google Ads and Google Business Profile in shape first.
