For most local businesses, the best first audio buy is the platform where you can set a small budget and target your own city or zip code: Spotify Ads Manager and Pandora (sold through AudioGO) both start at $250, while iHeartMedia offers a self-serve tool called AdBuilder plus sales-assisted broadcast, streaming and podcast buys. Which one wins depends on who your customers are, not on a universal ranking, and we could not find any official CPM comparison, so be skeptical of claims that one platform is always cheapest.

Say you run a dog grooming shop and you have $500 to try audio. Someone hears your 30 seconds on a walk, thinks "I should book Biscuit in," and picks up their phone. What do they type? Your name. Then Google shows them your profile. In the 50 pet groomers we pulled from Google Maps in Chicago in Oct 2026, 13 had fewer than 50 reviews and the median was 114. If you are one of the 13, fix that first. Audio sends people to search for you. It does not close them.

This guide compares the three platforms for a local advertiser spending a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month, and flags what we could and could not verify. Platform details were checked in October 2026 and change often.

## The key differences at a glance

| Platform | Self-serve tool | Minimum (published) | Local targeting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spotify | Spotify Ads Manager (formerly Ad Studio) | From $250 | In the US: country, state, city, postal code or DMA |
| Pandora | AudioGO (sold by SiriusXM Media) | From $250 | In the US: state, county, city, DMA and zip code |
| iHeartMedia | AdBuilder (self-serve radio) plus sales team | Not published on pages we could open | Geo-location, demographics and listener behavior |

Sources: [Spotify](https://ads.spotify.com/en-US/news-and-insights/small-business-self-serve-ad-tips/), [Spotify location targeting](https://ads.spotify.com/en-GB/help-center/location-targeting/), [AudioGO](https://www.audiogo.com/pandora), iHeartMedia (see Sources below), checked Oct 2026.

CPMs (cost per thousand listens) vary by market, targeting and season, and none of the three publishes a rate card for local campaigns. Get a quote or an in-tool estimate for your own city before you compare.

## Spotify

Spotify's self-serve tool lets small businesses start from $250 ([Spotify Ads](https://ads.spotify.com/en-US/news-and-insights/small-business-self-serve-ad-tips/), checked Oct 2026). Audio files can be WAV, MP3 or OGG and should be 30 seconds or less, so a 15 or 30 second spot fits.

Location targeting depends on your account's country. For US accounts, Spotify lists country, state, city, postal code or DMA, and it determines location from IP address ([Spotify Help](https://ads.spotify.com/en-GB/help-center/location-targeting/), checked Oct 2026). That means Spotify can target neighborhoods more tightly than many advertisers assume, though IP-based location is approximate.

Spotify tends to suit businesses whose customers listen on phones around workouts, commutes and downtime: cafes, gyms and studios, salons, bars. We did not find an official audience-age breakdown to quote, so check the audience estimate in Ads Manager and ignore stereotypes about who listens.

## Pandora (via AudioGO)

Pandora's self-serve buying is now offered through AudioGO, which says you can start from $250 and offers hundreds of demographic and audience targeting options ([AudioGO](https://www.audiogo.com/pandora), checked Oct 2026). In the US, AudioGO lists targeting by state, county, city, metro area or zip code, which makes it a good fit for businesses with a tight service area ([AudioGO](https://www.audiogo.com/local-advertising), listed in search results, checked Oct 2026).

For creative, AudioGO says you can bring your own audio file, buy professional production voiced by actors, or create a free ad with a synthetic voice. If you have no audio yet, that removes a common barrier to a first test.

Pandora suits businesses that want zip-code level reach, such as coffee shops, restaurants, groomers and auto services. We could not verify how much of Pandora listening happens in cars, so we would not build a campaign around a commute-only assumption.

## iHeartMedia

iHeartMedia sells broadcast radio, digital audio and podcasts, and asks advertisers to contact its team for quotes ([iHeartMedia Advertise](https://www.iheartmedia.com/advertise), checked Oct 2026). It also promotes iHeart AdBuilder as the first entirely self-service way to buy radio advertising, aimed at small businesses ([iHeartMedia AdBuilder guide](https://share.iheartmedia.com/iHeartAdBuilder_YourGuideToRadioAdvertising), checked Oct 2026). Its pages we could open did not state a minimum budget, so ask for it before you plan.

Its main difference is that one provider can sell broadcast stations, streaming and podcasts. A local broadcast buy reaches a station's whole market, while streaming can be targeted more narrowly. Home services, healthcare, legal and auto businesses often find mainstream radio formats a natural fit, but that is a judgment based on format audiences, not a published statistic.

## Which platform for which business?

These are starting hypotheses to test, not rules:

| Business type | Test first | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee shop or cafe | Pandora or Spotify | Zip-code reach (Pandora), lifestyle context (Spotify) |
| Fitness studio or gym | Spotify | Workout and activity context |
| Hair salon or beauty | Spotify | Lifestyle fit and postal-code targeting |
| Pet groomer or restaurant | Pandora | Tight local radius by zip code |
| Home services, dental, legal | iHeartMedia | Broad market reach and mainstream formats |

## The math: why CPM alone settles nothing

Every number here except the $250 minimums is an assumption for illustration. They are not platform prices and not real results. Swap in the quote you get.

Example: $250 on each of Spotify and Pandora. Assume CPMs of $15 and $20, that each listener hears the ad 4 times, and the average first sale is $40. The only thing we change between rows is how many listeners act.

| Row | Impressions | Listeners (÷ 4) | Action rate | Customers | Cost per customer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spotify, $15 CPM | 16,667 | 4,167 | 0.3% | 12.5 | $20.00 |
| Pandora, $20 CPM | 12,500 | 3,125 | 0.3% | 9.4 | $26.67 |
| Pandora, $20 CPM, tighter zip targeting | 12,500 | 3,125 | 0.5% | 15.6 | $16.00 |

Impressions = $250 / CPM x 1,000. On identical response, the cheaper CPM wins. But if tight zip-code targeting lifts the action rate from 0.3% to 0.5% (an assumption), the more expensive CPM wins. You find out which row you are in only by running both with separate tracking. At $40 a sale, you need 7 customers from a $250 test to cover the spend on first sales alone ($250 / $40 = 6.25).

## Writing audio ads that work locally

Creative is usually the bigger lever than platform choice. Some habits that tend to help:

- **Lead with the local hook:** "In [City]?" or "Near [landmark]?" signals relevance immediately.
- **One message, one action:** "Book this week" beats a list of offers.
- **Say your name twice:** once at the start and once at the end.
- **Keep it short:** a 15 second spot forces focus. Test it against a 30 second version.
- **Use a simple call to action:** "Search [Business Name] on Google" is easier to remember than a long URL.

Skip reading your whole menu or service list. Skip jingles that take ten seconds to reach the point.

## Budget guidance

| Monthly budget | Suggestion |
|---|---|
| Under $500 | Audio needs repeated exposure to build recall. If you cannot sustain it for several weeks, put the money into [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and your Google Business Profile first. |
| About $500 to $2,500 | Test one platform, likely Spotify or Pandora, for at least a month. |
| $2,500 and up | Test two or three platforms with the same offer and unique tracking for each. |

## Running a fair three-way test

1. Split the budget evenly for the same 30 days.
2. Give each platform its own promo code, landing page or tracked phone number.
3. Use the same core message so you test the platform, not the creative.
4. Compare cost per call, visit or redemption rather than CPM.
5. Keep the campaign running long enough to build frequency.

## Do this today

1. Ask iHeartMedia for its minimum in writing. Do not plan around a number you have not seen.
2. Open Spotify Ads Manager and AudioGO and write down the CPM estimate each shows for your zip codes.
3. Write a 15-second and a 30-second script. Time both aloud.
4. Make 2 promo codes, for example "SPOT" and "PAN", one per platform.
5. Set a calendar reminder for day 30 to compare cost per customer, not CPM.

## FAQ

**Q: Which audio ad platform is cheapest for a local business?**
A: There is no official comparison, so test. Spotify and Pandora both start at $250, and what you pay per thousand listens depends on your city, targeting and season. Compare cost per result, not CPM.

**Q: Do I need professional audio production?**
A: Not to start. AudioGO offers a free synthetic-voice ad, and you can upload your own file on Spotify. Once you find a message that works, consider a professional voiceover.

**Q: Is audio advertising worth it compared with Google or Meta Ads?**
A: For most local businesses, [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and [Meta Ads](/services/meta-ads) come first because they reach people who are already searching or browsing. Audio works as an awareness layer on top.

**Q: Can I target specific neighborhoods?**
A: Yes in the US on Spotify (postal code) and Pandora via AudioGO (zip code). Meta Ads with radius targeting can be similarly precise.

**Q: Does iHeartMedia have a self-serve option?**
A: Yes, iHeart AdBuilder is promoted as self-service radio buying. Broadcast, streaming and podcast packages are also sold through its sales team.
