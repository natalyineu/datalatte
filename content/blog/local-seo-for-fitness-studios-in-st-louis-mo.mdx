## How does the Google Map Pack choose fitness studios?

Adwave defines the Google Maps local pack as the top three business listings in local search results. For a query such as "personal training studios near me," the Association of Fitness Studios says Google lists relevant local businesses in the Map SERP features section. Those three slots are not bought through a separate ad system. Adwave states that the top three businesses are pulled directly from their Google Business Profile listings.

That makes the profile the first asset to fix, before any website work. Google describes its Business Profile as a free listing on Search and Maps, so getting found costs time rather than a listing fee.

Competitors in the same neighbourhood are usually using the same profile fields. A studio that fills every field accurately and keeps them current has a clear advantage over one that set up its listing years ago and never touched it again.

## Which Google Business Profile fields should a studio fill first?

Start with the primary category. Adwave recommends reviewing the top three to five businesses in the local map results for a service search and noting the category they use. It also states that analysis of millions of profiles shows the primary category is the top ranking factor for entering the Local Pack, though it does not name the source of that analysis.

Next, make your hours, phone number and address 100% accurate, as Adwave advises. Then write the description. Adwave's beginner guide says the description allows 750 characters. Lead with the class types and the neighbourhood, and mention St. Louis once in plain language rather than repeating it.

A studio that works from home or travels to clients can hide its street address, which Adwave says designates it as a Service Area Business. A studio with a public front desk should keep the address visible. For the full setup, read [the fitness studio Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization). Keyword ideas for profile copy and ads are in [the best Google Ads keywords for fitness studios in 2026](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-fitness-studios-in-2026).

## How many photos and posts keep a studio profile active?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos and says a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls. Its beginner guide sets a lower starting point of 10. Useful shots include the class floor, the coaches, the entrance and the parking area.

Post at least once a week. Adwave describes Google Posts as free mini-billboards that appear on the profile in Search and Maps, and it recommends a weekly post to keep the profile fresh. Google's help page says a post is public on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Check the status after each publish, because a post that is pending or not approved stays hidden.

## How many reviews does a studio need to compete?

Google says reviews show next to a business's profile in Maps and Search, so they are visible before a visitor clicks anything. The count matters too. A Blogging Wizard analysis of 2 million profiles, reported by Adwave, found that businesses with over 200 reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. That is a correlation in a secondary source, not a guaranteed rule.

Adwave's gym guide sets a different benchmark: gyms with 100+ Google reviews and 4.7+ ratings are said to dominate the local map pack, and a rating below 4.5 meaningfully suppresses conversion. Treat these as targets to measure against. For a regional comparison, see [the Denver fitness studio review benchmarks for 2026](/blog/denver-fitness-studios-google-reviews-benchmarks-2026).

Ask for reviews after a class series or a first visit, and reply to every review. Member wording such as "beginner-friendly" or "great coaches" tells prospective members what to expect.

## Do name, address and phone details have to match everywhere?

Yes. Adwave says the business name, address and phone number must be exactly the same on the website, the Google Business Profile and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places. Its beginner guide adds that punctuation counts, so "St." and "Street" must match the profile character for character.

A practical method is to write one master version of the details, then copy it into every listing and your website footer. Put the same address on your contact page with [a Google Maps embed](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

Apple also matters. Adwave reports that in a maps-only comparison, Apple Business Connect has captured a 7% share and is growing. Its Profile Views metric counts how many people saw the business card in Apple Maps, so claiming the listing lets a studio track that traffic and keep the details consistent there too.

## How long before a studio sees organic results?

Adwave states that organic channels such as Google Business Profile, referrals and community take 6-12 months to gain real traction, although they compound over time. Its beginner guide also says a business may begin ranking for its own name in the Map Pack a few weeks after verification. Both are vendor estimates, so measure your own results rather than relying on them.

Adwave's local search intent guide lists six metrics worth tracking: Google Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift over time. Check them monthly, and compare the same months year on year.

While organic results build, paid search can fill the gap. The [Google Ads guide for St. Louis fitness studios](/blog/google-ads-for-fitness-studios-in-st-louis-mo) explains that route.

## What does closing a review gap take in weeks?

The inputs below are hypothetical and labelled as assumptions. Replace them with your own counts.

Assumption: the studio has 85 Google reviews today, a target of 200 reviews, and collects 3 new reviews per week from a text request sent after each class series.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Gap to target | 200 minus 85 (assumption) | 115 reviews |
| Time at 3 per week | 115 divided by 3 (assumption) | about 38 weeks |
| Time at 6 per week | 115 divided by 6 (assumption) | about 19 weeks |

Doubling the request rate roughly halves the timeline. At 3 per week, the gap takes about nine months to close, which sits within Adwave's 6-12 month window for organic traction. The sensible lever is the request rate, because you control it directly.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a fitness studio?

Yes. Google describes its [Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) as free to set up and shows it on Search and Maps. The cost is the time needed to keep the details accurate.

### Can a studio hide its street address?

Only if it works from home or serves customers at their location. Adwave's [beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) says hiding the address designates the business as a Service Area Business. A studio with a public front desk should keep the address visible.

### Do Google posts show right away?

Not always. Google's [posts help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en) says a post shows publicly on Search and Maps when its status is Live. Check the status after each publish.

### How many reviews are needed before the Map Pack?

No single number is guaranteed. Adwave reports a [Blogging Wizard analysis](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business) finding that businesses with over 200 reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. Its [gym guide](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-gyms-fitness-centers) cites 100+ reviews and 4.7+ ratings. Use both as benchmarks and track your own position.
