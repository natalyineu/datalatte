Class schedules change weekly, so studio email fails when it announces a timetable that no longer exists. Link every send to the live booking page instead of pasting times into the message. The email sells the habit; the booking page carries the facts.

## Which emails should a yoga studio send first?

Send the welcome within an hour of the first signup. It carries the studio address, parking or entry details, what to bring, and one beginner-friendly class to try. New students deciding between two studios usually pick the one that removed the most uncertainty.

Send reminders from the booking system, not by hand. A reminder 24 hours before class, with a one-tap reschedule link, protects attendance better than a monthly newsletter. Studios using [AI-powered email tools](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-tools) can draft these, but trigger times should come from real bookings.

Send the win-back at 60 days of absence. One message, one gentle offer, no guilt copy. Students lapse because of work, travel and injury more often than dislike, so the tone should assume a welcome return rather than a lost sale.

## What should a studio newsletter contain?

Keep it monthly and short: one schedule change, one workshop or event, one useful practice note, and one clear booking link. Four blocks, readable in a minute. Long wellness essays compete with every other inbox; a studio newsletter wins on usefulness, not depth.

Segment by behaviour, not demographics. Intro-offer buyers need a path to a second class. Members need timetable and community news. Lapsed students need the win-back, not the newsletter. ## FAQ

### How often should a yoga studio email its list?

Three automated sends (welcome, reminder, win-back) plus one monthly newsletter is enough for most studios. More frequent newsletters usually repeat the timetable and train students to ignore the inbox. Event weeks justify an extra send; quiet weeks do not need filling.

### What is the best email tool for a small yoga studio?

At under 250 contacts, a free plan from Mailchimp or Brevo runs the core sends at no cost, on November 2026 vendor pricing. Brevo's 300 free emails a day suits daily reminders. Studios passing 500 contacts should compare entry tiers on automation steps and segmentation, not templates.

### Should class reminders come from email or the booking app?

Both, with different jobs. The booking system owns the transactional reminder because it knows the real booking. Email owns the welcome, the newsletter and the win-back because those need writing, not just a timestamp. Splitting them keeps cancellations accurate and marketing readable.

### How does a studio grow its email list legally?

Ask at booking, at the desk and on the website, with an explicit opt-in. Never import a booking system's full history into a marketing list without consent; those addresses did not agree to newsletters. A short signup incentive, such as a first-class guide, converts better than a discount pop-up.
