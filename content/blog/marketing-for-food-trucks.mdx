A food truck's main marketing problem is that customers cannot find it if they do not know where it is today. Fix that first: publish a schedule people can see in one place, keep your Google Business Profile current, and post each stop where followers will see it. This guide does not claim how most customers discover trucks, because the survey figures available online were secondhand or old, and it gives no benchmark for truck sales.

It covers US-style single-truck businesses. Permit rules are local, and the US Small Business Administration says local governments determine registration, licensing and permitting requirements. Illustrative numbers are labelled as assumptions.

## How Do Customers Find a Truck That Moves?

They need one reliable place to check. Pick it and repeat it everywhere: a pinned post, your profile link and a sign on the truck. Google Business Profile posts can be Updates, Offers or Events, and only live posts show on Search and Maps. Posts are archived after six months unless you set a date range, so tie each post to a date.

Google's guidelines do not name food trucks. They do say a business that travels to customers and has no storefront gets a service-area profile, and that you should describe your location with a precise address or service area. Check how your truck should be listed, as a fixed commissary address can look wrong to customers. Our [Business Profile guide for restaurants](/blog/google-my-business-for-restaurants) covers the basics.

## What Should You Post, and When?

Post the same three things each service day: where, when and the one item to order. Put the address and a landmark in the first line, since people read only the start. Keep a weekly schedule graphic and update it when plans change.

Repeat a short routine so followers learn it. For instance, a morning post with the stop, a reminder before opening, and a photo of the first plate. ## Should a Food Truck Run Paid Ads?

Only for specific goals, such as a launch week, a new stop or a private-event push. Google Ads radius targeting requires a radius of at least 1 km, and Google warns that small radii may show intermittently or not at all. A truck that moves would have to keep editing locations, so test with a fixed regular stop before paying for a moving target.

No source we opened publishes food truck ad costs, so do not assume a cost per customer. Start with a small cap and compare the sales on that day to a normal day. Our [Facebook ads guide for restaurants](/blog/facebook-ads-for-restaurants) has setup steps.

## What Do Card Fees Do to Your Margin?

For a low-ticket business, fees are a real cost. Square's US page lists in-person card payments at 2.6% plus 15 cents on the Free plan, 2.5% plus 15 cents on Plus and 2.4% plus 15 cents on Premium. Online or invoice payments are 3.3% plus 30 cents on Free and 2.9% plus 30 cents on Plus. Rates vary by account, so check yours.

On a $12 order at the Free plan in-person rate the fee is about $0.46, roughly 3.8% of the sale. Pre-orders through a website cost more per sale but cut your queue and food waste.

| Food cost | Assumption: 32% | $112 |

| Weekly contribution before labour | $350 - $112 - $19 | about $219 |

If posts bring only 10 extra pre-orders a week, contribution falls to about $88. The test is whether posting is cheaper than your time to earn the same elsewhere. Track pre-orders by source with a short code, such as "SCHED10", and keep the log for eight weeks.

## FAQ

### How do customers find food trucks?

No verified recent study is cited here. Older and newer surveys disagree, so track how your own customers heard of you with a quick question at the window.

### Do food trucks need a Google Business Profile?

It helps people find your hours and links. Google's guidelines do not name food trucks, so follow its address and service-area rules and check how it lists mobile businesses.

### Should I run Facebook or Instagram ads?

Only with a clear goal, and with small caps. No source here gives food truck ad costs.

### How much do card fees cost a food truck?

On Square's US Free plan, 2.6% plus 15 cents for in-person payments. Compare processors for your volume.

### What permits do I need?

They are set locally. The SBA says local governments determine licensing and permits, so ask your city or county.
