Local businesses can reach NFL fans with connected-TV ads by scheduling spots around game windows instead of buying a named game. Fans spread their viewing across several paid services and free local broadcasts, so a single streamer rarely holds the whole audience. Public price data for ad inventory is thin in the sources reviewed here, so the practical route is a small, measured test against a break-even number you set in advance.

Viewers are not paying much to watch. A one-time antenna picks up local CBS, FOX, NBC and ABC games, and streaming add-ons are priced for individual fans. Those costs shape where the audience sits, and that in turn shapes where an ad can reach it.

## Where Do NFL Games Stream in 2026?

Fans have several low-cost routes, and each one carries a different slice of games. According to a 2026 Yahoo Sports guide that syndicates Gadget Review, an over-the-air antenna gets local CBS, FOX, NBC and ABC games with no recurring cost ([S1](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/cheapest-ways-watch-every-nfl-161342220.html)). For streaming, the same guide lists NFL+ at $6.99 a month or $49.99 for the season, Prime Video at $14.99 a month for Thursday Night Football, and Peacock Premium at $10.99 a month.

The figures above are consumer prices, not ad rates. They matter to a buyer for one reason: fans who pay for one service are often not watching the others. A streaming-only buy therefore covers only part of the room, and a plan built on one platform should be treated as a partial reach estimate.

## Does Local Over-the-Air Still Matter for Ad Reach?

Yes, and it is the part most streaming-first plans miss. The S1 guide's advice to buy an antenna for local network games implies that a real share of football viewing still happens on free local broadcast ([S1](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/cheapest-ways-watch-every-nfl-161342220.html)). That is an inference from the fan guidance, not a measured audience figure, so no viewer share should be assumed from it.

For a local advertiser, the practical point is geography. A streaming buy can be aimed by ZIP or radius only if the platform offers those controls. Before buying, ask the seller whether the inventory can be limited to your delivery area, and whether the ad can run in the same window as a local over-the-air game in your market.

If your customers are local, judge the reach by households in your delivery area, not by a national game average. National viewership numbers describe the league, not your ZIP code.

## How Should a Local Business Buy Game-Window Reach?

There are three common routes. Each trades control against cost and complexity, and the cheapest route is often the one that matches a local business best.

| Route | What you get | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named-game package from a streamer's sales team | A specific game or slate | Highest control, but usually the highest minimums; get terms in writing |
| Programmatic live-sports package | Ads in sports content across several streamers | Easier entry point, but you buy a content category and may not get a specific game |
| Standard CTV scheduled to game windows | Regular streaming inventory on Thursday, Sunday or Monday evenings | Least sports-specific, but you reach football households by time and location |

The third route is the one many local advertisers overlook. A takeaway shop does not need an ad inside the game. It needs a spot in front of households deciding on dinner while games are on. ## What Does a $3,000 Test Need to Break Even?

The model below uses assumption-labelled inputs. None of the CPM or ticket figures comes from the sources reviewed here, so replace them with quotes from your own providers before deciding.

Inputs (all assumption): budget $3,000; average ticket $60; gross margin 40%, so each order contributes $24; frequency of 3 ad exposures per household.

| Line | Scenario A: CPM $25 | Scenario B: CPM $50 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impressions (budget divided by CPM) | 120,000 | 60,000 |
| Households at frequency 3 | 40,000 | 20,000 |
| Orders needed to cover $3,000 | 125 | 125 |
| Response rate needed | 0.31% | 0.63% |

Read it this way: at double the CPM, the same budget reaches half the households, so the response rate needed to break even doubles. Orders should be tracked with a promo code or dedicated URL, so the 125 figure can be checked against real sales. Over a four-week test, that is a clear go or no-go signal.

Check the household count against reality. If your delivery area holds 15,000 households and the plan shows 40,000 reached, the frequency or budget is too high for the market. ## What Do Search Benchmarks Suggest About Paid Reach?

Search and display are the comparison points most local advertisers already use. Google's help documentation says Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail, and that campaigns can be set to pay for conversions rather than clicks ([S5](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)). That makes display a useful control group for a CTV test, since both can be measured against the same promo codes.

Benchmark data from WordStream gives reference points, though its figures are dated. Its industry tables list an average search click-through rate of 3.17% and a display rate of 0.46%, with average cost per click of $2.69 for search and $0.63 for display ([S4](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks)). The page was first published in 2016 and should be treated as a rough baseline only. ## Which Local Businesses Should Test NFL Streaming Ads?

Some businesses fit this approach better than others. The test makes most sense when the offer can be redeemed quickly and the delivery area is large enough to hold a real audience.

- Restaurants and takeaway with a clear game-night offer and a short redemption window.
- Home services and auto businesses that serve the whole metro area and can absorb a higher cost per acquisition.
- Bars and venues where a single game-window promotion matches the product.

Skip it if you serve only a few blocks, if you have no video spot, or if search ads are not yet capturing people already looking for you. ## What Should You Do This Week?

1. Count the households in your real delivery area, and do not plan to reach more households than exist.
2. Replace every assumption in the break-even table with your own ticket, margin and at least two CPM quotes, one self-serve and one sports-specific.
3. Pick one game window per week for a four-week test, and write the break-even response rate down before launch.
4. Write creative that names your offer and town, and avoid league or team names, logos and branded phrases unless you hold a license.
5. Add a QR code and a unique promo code, then compare redemptions with the same weekdays from the prior month.

## FAQ

### Can a small local business afford NFL streaming ads?

A scheduled test can start well below the cost of a named-game package. The sources here do not give a sports CTV price, so the answer depends on quotes. The break-even model above shows how the required response changes with CPM, and that is the number to check before committing.

### Is the cheapest way to watch NFL games also the best way to advertise?

Not necessarily. Cheap viewing routes spread the audience across services, which is why a single platform gives only partial reach. Ad buying should be judged on your delivery area and redemption data, not on how cheaply fans watch.

### Can I target local households by ZIP code?

Some CTV platforms offer ZIP, city or radius targeting, but sports-specific inventory may offer fewer geographic controls. Ask the seller before buying, and confirm the controls in writing.

### How do I measure whether game-window ads worked?

Use a unique promo code, a dedicated URL or a QR code, and compare redemptions across the same weekdays before and after the test. Judge the result over several weeks rather than one game, because a single game is too noisy to read.
