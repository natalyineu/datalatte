An NFL regular-season game averaged 18.7 million viewers in 2025, the second-highest season average on record, according to the league as reported by Awful Announcing. That is the headline every sales rep will lead with. Here is the number that matters more for a local business: AdWave's Q2 2026 benchmark page puts "premium direct and live sports" CTV at about $45 to $65 per thousand impressions, against roughly $20 to $25 for standard programmatic inventory. Same football fans, same living rooms, a price gap of more than double.

This guide is for restaurants, bars, home services, dealers and other local businesses in the US (the NFL is a US audience play; if you are in the UK, Canada or Australia, treat this as a template for local sports windows). It covers what the audience data actually says, where the games stream, three ways to buy, the trademark trap in your creative, and a break-even table. Our other football pieces cover individual platforms: [Thursday Night Football on Prime Video](/blog/amazon-prime-video-thursday-night-football-ads) and [Peacock live sports](/blog/peacock-live-sports-advertising-local-business). This one is about deciding whether to buy football at all, and how to do it cheaply.

Sports Media Watch reports that Amazon's Thursday Night Football averaged 15.33 million viewers in 2025 and was the series' most-watched season, but there are three caveats a local buyer should know:

- **New measurement.** 2025 was the first season measured with Nielsen's Big Data + Panel method. On a like-for-like basis, TNF was up 8% (from 14.23 million), not the 16% Nielsen's official comparison shows against last year's panel-only figure. Awful Announcing notes it is unclear how much of the league-wide jump is real growth versus past undercounting.
- **Simulcasts are included.** The 15.33 million includes local over-the-air broadcasts in the two teams' home markets, so it is not all streaming.
- **It is a national number.** None of these figures says how many households in your delivery area are watching.

The one genuinely useful data point for a marketer is age: Sports Media Watch reports a median age of 49.4 for Prime Video's TNF audience versus 56.2 for NFL on linear networks. If your customers are 30 to 55, streaming football skews closer to them.

## Where the Games Stream in 2026

Rights are split, and one guide we checked (Sports Brackets, dated May 2026) contains inconsistencies on holiday games, so verify against the league schedule. As that guide describes it:

- **Amazon Prime Video:** Thursday Night Football every Thursday except Week 1 and Thanksgiving, plus a Black Friday game.
- **Netflix:** a Week 1 Thursday game in Melbourne, a Thanksgiving Eve game and Christmas Day games.
- **Peacock:** Sunday Night Football simulcast with NBC, plus select exclusives.
- **Monday Night Football:** ESPN and ABC, with streaming on ESPN+ and Disney+.
- **Sunday afternoons:** regional CBS and FOX broadcasts.

Two practical consequences. First, exclusive streaming games still air on local over-the-air channels in both teams' markets, so the audience you reach through a streaming-only buy is only part of the viewing. Second, which streamer carries which game changes your buying route, not your goal. You are renting attention in a three-hour window.

## Three Ways to Buy Football Attention

| Route | What you get | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named-game package from a streamer's sales team | A specific game or slate | Highest control, highest CPM and minimums; ask for both in writing |
| Programmatic live-sports package | Ads in sports content across several streamers | Lower entry point; you buy a content category, and a particular NFL game may not be included |
| Standard CTV, scheduled to game windows | Regular streaming inventory on Thursday, Sunday or Monday evenings | Cheapest, but you reach football fans only by time and geography, not by content |

The third route is the one most local advertisers overlook. Most self-serve CTV tools let you set a schedule and a ZIP or radius. A pizza shop does not need an ad inside the game; it needs a spot in front of the households deciding on dinner while the game is on. Our [CTV platforms comparison](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026) shows which self-serve platforms offer scheduling and geo controls, and the [CTV cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data) explains CPM math in detail. Older live-sports options such as [Hulu Live, YouTube TV and Sling](/blog/hulu-live-youtube-tv-sling-sports-ads) sell their own inventory too.

## Watch the Words in Your Creative

The NFL protects its marks aggressively. According to the Broadcast Law Blog, the league holds federal registrations not only for "Super Bowl" but for "Super Sunday," "Gameday," "Back to Football," "1st and Goal" and over a hundred other marks. It reports cease-and-desist letters over taglines like "Stock Up for the Super Bowl," over viewing parties that charge admission, and over giveaways of game tickets. A disclaimer such as "Not an Official Sponsor" will not stop a letter, per the same source.

What is safe? The same blog notes that the NFL abandoned its attempt to register "The Big Game" after more than twenty parties threatened to oppose it, and that its authors know of no reported claims over that phrase. That is an absence of reported claims, not a legal guarantee, and the blog is from 2020. For a regular-season spot, the safest route is to skip league and team names, logos and the protected phrases altogether and say what is true about your business: "Thursday night wings, ready in 15 minutes" works without a single trademark. Check with a trademark attorney if you plan anything bigger. This is general information, not legal advice.

Other creative rules:

- Use 15 or 30 seconds with one offer and one action.
- Put a QR code or short URL on screen in the last seconds; many viewers hold a phone.
- Name your town or neighborhood in the first five seconds.

## The Math: What a $3,000 Football Test Needs

Every figure below is an **assumption**. The CPMs are midpoints of AdWave's ranges ($22.50 for standard, $55 for live sports), which AdWave attributes only to "industry benchmark data." The $30 for scheduled game-window inventory is our assumption, because dayparting may carry a premium. The $60 average order is also an assumption, standing in for a restaurant or delivery ticket.

| | Standard CTV, no schedule | Scheduled to game windows | Premium live sports |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget | $3,000 | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| CPM (assumption) | $22.50 | $30 | $55 |
| Impressions | 133,333 | 100,000 | 54,545 |
| Households at frequency 4 | 33,333 | 25,000 | 13,636 |
| Customers to cover cost at $60 each | 50 | 50 | 50 |
| Share of households that must buy | 0.15% | 0.20% | 0.37% |

Read it this way: the live-sports route needs more than double the response of standard CTV to break even on revenue, before food cost, labor or rent. A premium audience can respond better, but nobody has published local-business evidence of that, so do not assume it. If you decide premium sports is worth it, you are paying for brand lift among people already in a buying mood.

Check the household count against reality too. If your delivery area has 15,000 households and the table says you can reach 25,000, you are overspending on frequency. Lower the budget, not the quality.

## FAQ

**Q: Can a small local business afford NFL CTV advertising?**
A: A scheduled standard-CTV test can start much smaller than a named-game package. The AdWave benchmark of $45 to $65 CPM for premium live sports suggests a $3,000 test buys about 55,000 impressions there versus about 133,000 at standard prices. Get written quotes before committing.

**Q: Can I use "Super Bowl" or the NFL logo in my ad?**
A: Not without a license, according to the Broadcast Law Blog, which documents objections to unlicensed "Super Bowl" advertising. Skip league and team names, logos and protected phrases such as "Gameday."

**Q: Which streamer has the most NFL games?**
A: It depends on the week. Amazon has Thursday Night Football, Peacock streams Sunday Night Football and Netflix has select games, per a May 2026 guide that you should check against the official schedule.

**Q: Can I target by zip code?**
A: Many CTV platforms offer ZIP, city or radius targeting, but sports-specific inventory sometimes has fewer geo controls. Ask the seller before you buy.

**Q: How do I measure it?**
A: Use a QR code, a promo code and a dedicated URL, and compare the same weekdays before and after. Judge over several weeks, not one game.
