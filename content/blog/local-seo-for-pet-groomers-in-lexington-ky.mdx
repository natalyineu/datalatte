Local SEO for a pet groomer in Lexington, KY is mostly a Google Business Profile (GBP) task. The Google Maps pack, the block of three listings shown for local searches, draws its listings directly from GBP data, so a complete and accurate profile is the starting point. Beyond that, a groomer needs name, address and phone details that match across the web, reviews that describe real services, and a steady rhythm of posts. ## What Is the Google Maps Pack and How Does It Choose Listings?

The local pack is the top three business listings in local search results, as [Adwave's local SEO audit checklist](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today) defines it (secondary). The same vendor notes that those three listings are pulled directly from their Google Business Profile entries in its [Google Maps marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) (secondary). For a grooming shop, the pack is shaped mostly by profile fields you control, not by a separate ad budget.

Google describes its profile as free to set up, in its [Business Profile sign-up page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) (primary). The ranking weight is harder to pin down. An [Adwave multi-location audit](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit) cites a 2025 benchmark placing GBP signals at 32% of local pack ranking weight (secondary). A BrightLocal survey cited by [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo) found 36% of SEOs ranked the profile as the most important factor in 2021 (secondary). Neither figure comes from Google, so treat both as direction, not precision.

## How Many Photos and Posts Does a Lexington Groomer Need?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, and says a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls (secondary). Its beginner guide sets a floor of 10 (secondary), so 15 is the safer target. Useful shots include finished dogs with owner consent, the grooming station, and the van if you run a mobile service. Refresh them with the seasons rather than uploading once and leaving them.

Posts need one more check. Google states that a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, and Pending or Not approved posts do not show ([Google posts help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), primary). Adwave advises at least one new post a week to keep the profile fresh (secondary). Set a recurring reminder and look at each post's status after publishing.

For the wider profile setup, [Google Business Profile for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-for-pet-groomers) covers the basics.

## Do Reviews and Review Replies Move the Map Pack?

Google says reviews appear next to a business's profile in Maps and Search ([Google review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), primary). Adwave cites an analysis of two million profiles by Blogging Wizard, which found businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack (secondary, via [Adwave's review guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business)). For a single groomer, 200 reviews is a long horizon. The practical goal is a steady stream of genuine reviews, not a burst.

Ask for reviews in a way that lets customers describe what they booked, such as a nervous dog groom or a puppy first appointment. Replies matter too. Keep them accurate and specific to the service. Do not offer incentives for reviews, and do not post invented ones.

## Does NAP Consistency Across Directories Still Matter?

Consistency is the basic check. Adwave's voice search guide says the name, address and phone number must be exactly the same on the website, the GBP listing, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places (secondary, [Adwave voice search guide](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses)). Its beginner guide goes further, requiring a match down to punctuation and to whether the address says "St." or "Street" (secondary).

A phone number written as (555) 123-4567 on one site and 555-123-4567 on another is the kind of mismatch to fix first. Correct existing listings before you add new ones.

Google Ads uses the same underlying data. Its help page says location asset data comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and corrections made there flow into linked Google Ads accounts ([Google location assets help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), primary). If you plan to run search ads, [Google Ads for pet groomers in Lexington](/blog/google-ads-for-pet-groomers-in-lexington-ky) explains how to set that up.

## Should a Pet Groomer Also Claim Apple Maps?

Apple Business Connect is the Apple counterpart to GBP. Adwave's article on it states that the platform reaches over 1.5 billion affluent iOS users, and that 88% of people use Google Maps compared with 12% for Apple Maps. The page names no source for either figure, so treat them as unverified (secondary, [Adwave Apple Business Connect article](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring)).

The same article describes Apple Maps Actions, which let a business connect services such as booking or ordering to its profile, and a Profile Views metric that counts how many people saw the business card. Claiming the listing is a small task, and it keeps the NAP data consistent. Whether it earns much traffic for your shop depends on how many of your clients use Apple devices, which only your own data can show.

## Does Google's AI Answer Use Your Profile Data?

Google launched Q&A in Maps in 2017, and Adwave's review of the feature notes that answers now sit alongside profile content (secondary, [Adwave Q&A article](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a)). A transition summary cited by Adwave says responses in Google's AI-driven answering are sourced from business profile data, website content, and reviews (secondary).

The practical step is to keep the three sources consistent. Write the profile description, the website service pages, and recent reviews around the same services and neighbourhoods. The embedded map on your contact page is covered in [Google Maps embed for a local business website](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

## What Does a Simple Local SEO Budget Look Like?

The profile itself costs nothing to set up, so the real cost is staff time. The example below uses assumed inputs, not sourced figures. Replace them with your own numbers.

Inputs (all assumption): average first groom price of $70; 8 new clients per month who arrive through GBP calls, directions, or website clicks; 4 hours per month on profile tasks; an owner time value of $30 per hour; no paid ads in this month.

Revenue from GBP-driven clients: 8 × $70 = $560 per month.
Time cost: 4 × $30 = $120 per month.
Net before other costs: $560 − $120 = $440 per month.

If only 4 clients come through the profile, revenue falls to $280 and the net to $160. The number that matters is the conversion from profile views to bookings. Track it for a quarter before deciding whether to add paid search. [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins) compares the two options for small budgets.

## Which Metrics Show Whether the Map Pack Is Working?

Adwave lists six local search metrics worth tracking: Google Business Profile impressions, map pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift over time (secondary, [Adwave local search intent guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent)). Record them monthly in a single sheet so the trend is visible.

Messages from customers can be handled from the Business Profile dashboard on a desktop, not only the Maps app, according to [Adwave's messaging guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on) (secondary). A reply that takes an hour is easier to manage on a larger screen.

## FAQ

### How long before a Lexington groomer appears in the map pack?

Adwave says a business may begin ranking for its own name a few weeks after its profile is verified, which it calls a vendor estimate (secondary). No reliable timeline for competitive searches is available from the sources used here, so track your own map pack position weekly for your top keywords.

### Is a Google Business Profile free?

Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up on Search and Maps ([Google sign-up page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/), primary). Any paid ads you run on Google are a separate cost.

### Should a mobile groomer hide its street address?

Adwave says a business that works from home or serves customers at their location can hide its street address, and is then designated a Service Area Business (secondary). Use that option only if it matches how you work, and make sure the service area you list is accurate.
