## What Does a CTV Ad Cost for a Nail Salon?

Published CTV rate cards for small salons are hard to find, so the sourced numbers above are the most useful reference. Local TV pricing depends on the market and the schedule, which is why the cost sits in a wide range. A nail salon should expect the same kind of variation in CTV and should not plan on the low end without a quote.

Ask each platform or streaming seller for a quote for your exact ZIP codes, schedule and ad length. Compare the quote with the number of new clients you need to cover it, using the break-even check later in this article.

## Where Can a Small Salon Buy CTV?

Streaming TV ads come in different forms. Ad-supported video-on-demand (AVOD) services are free or lower-cost streaming services that are paid for by advertising ([Paramount Advertising, vendor glossary](https://adsmanager.paramount.com/insights/what-is-ctv-advertising-definitions)). That distinction matters because the ads you buy run inside a particular service, and each one has its own audience and pricing.

Before you buy, check three things: the minimum spend, the creative specs and the fees on top of media costs. Minimums and specs change, so read the platform's current help pages rather than relying on a figure from a blog.

## How Do You Target Local Clients?

Start from how people search. Salon-related searches include phrases such as "hair salon near me" and "nail salon in [city]" ([Trafft, vendor guide](https://trafft.com/salon-ads/)). Those terms show that people look for a salon close to where they are, so your targeting should stay close to home too.

List the neighbourhoods or ZIP codes within a short drive of the salon. Then check that list against where your current clients live, using your booking software. Keep the area tight for the first test so the results are easy to read.

For rebooking, timing matters more than reach. Gel or acrylic nails need a visit every two to three weeks ([Salonist, vendor blog](https://salonist.io/blog/improve-salon-rebooking-rate)). If a viewer does not book the first time, your reminder emails and follow-up messages will do more of the work. Our [AI email marketing guide for salons](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-for-hair-salons) covers that follow-up.

## What Should the Ad Say and Show?

Keep the message simple: the salon name, the neighbourhood, one service and one way to book. Add a unique booking link or QR code so you can see which new clients came from the ad. Without that, you cannot judge whether the spend worked.

Local search still matters before anyone sees your ad. One vendor says it reviewed the local-search setup of more than 300 salons that stock its products ([RŌZ Hair, vendor blog](https://rozhair.com/blogs/education/hair-salon-advertising-ideas)). Check that your Google Business Profile lists correct hours, services and photos before you spend on any awareness channel.

Production costs can exceed media costs for a small test. A phone-shot video of real work is a cheap start. Get written permission from any client or staff member who appears on screen, and avoid health or safety claims you cannot support.

## What Setup Steps Come First?

Use this order. It keeps the budget focused on the steps that convert.

1. Confirm your Google Business Profile, booking link and reviews are current.
2. Pick one service to promote, such as gel manicure or a fill appointment.
3. List the ZIP codes or neighbourhoods within a short drive and match them to your client addresses.
4. Get a quote for your exact area, schedule and ad length from each seller.
5. Create one finished video and a unique booking link or QR code.
6. Set a fixed budget and a test window, then record every new booking by source.

Booksy's 30-day plan also starts with a digital audit in days 1 to 7 and leaves automation for days 22 to 30 ([Booksy, vendor blog](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-marketing-ideas)). The same logic applies here: fix the basics before you buy reach.

## FAQ

### How much do CTV ads cost for a nail salon?

Published CTV rates for small salons are scarce. The nearest sourced benchmark is local TV, which runs from a few hundred dollars to several thousand per airing, with production extra ([Adwave, secondary](https://adwave.com/resources/local-tv-ad-cost-2026)). Get a quote for your exact area and schedule.

### Can a salon target only nearby ZIP codes?

Check the platform you plan to use. Set your targeting to the ZIP codes or neighbourhoods within a short drive, then compare them with where your current clients live.

### What is an AVOD service?

An AVOD service is a free or lower-cost streaming service supported by ads ([Paramount Advertising, vendor glossary](https://adsmanager.paramount.com/insights/what-is-ctv-advertising-definitions)). It is one of the places a CTV ad can run.

### Should I try CTV before Google Ads?

Search reaches people already looking for a nail salon. Salon Guru charges a one-time set-up cost per Google Ads campaign, though it does not publish the amount ([Salon Guru, vendor page](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/)). Most salons set up search and their Google Business Profile first, then test CTV.

### How do I know if a CTV ad worked?

Give the ad a unique booking link or QR code and ask every new client how they found you. Compare the number of new clients with your break-even target.
