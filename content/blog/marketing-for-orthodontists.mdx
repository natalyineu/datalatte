Orthodontic marketing works when it makes your practice easy to find, easy to trust and easy to book. The American Association of Orthodontists (AAO) reports about 1.91 million adults in treatment with US members in 2024, up from 1.64 million in 2022. Adults and parents both compare options online, so your Google profile, reviews and consult booking page do most of the work.

## Who Are Your Orthodontic Patients in 2026?

Two groups decide differently:

- **Parents of children and teens:** they compare convenience, price and trust, and often ask other parents or the family dentist.
- **Adults:** they search privately, compare aligner and braces options, and care about appearance, time in treatment and monthly payment.

The AAO survey says [member practices treat about 1.91 million adults](https://www2.aaoinfo.org/member-survey-indicates-orthodontic-patient-numbers-at-all-time-high/), but its release did not give the adult share of all patients. Do not copy an adult percentage from a blog. Count the split in your own patient records and build one page for each group.

Practical split: a "Braces for teens" page with scheduling for after school and a "Clear aligners for adults" page with evening appointments and payment options.

## How Do You Show Up When People Search for Braces Near Me?

Start with your Google Business Profile, because it appears next to the searches that send people to you.

1. Choose the right primary category and add secondary categories for the services you offer.
2. Add real photos of the office, the team and the consult room.
3. Add a booking link that opens your consult form, not the home page.
4. Keep name, address and phone identical on your website and directories.
5. Write one service page per treatment: braces, clear aligners, retainers, early treatment.

## How Much Do Reviews Matter for an Orthodontist?

Reviews cost nothing but time. BrightLocal's 2026 survey reports:

- 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and 41% always do.
- 68% require at least 4 stars, and 31% only use businesses rated 4.5 or higher.
- 74% look for reviews from the last three months.
- 89% expect owners to respond, and 80% are more likely to use a business that responds to all reviews.

Set a routine. Ask every patient at debond or at a milestone visit, reply to every review within a week, and never reply with patient details. Reviews should come from real patients on a regular basis, not in one burst.

## Should You Run Google Ads and Meta Ads?

Run ads after the profile and booking page are working. Ads send more people to the same page, so a weak page wastes money.

Google Ads reach people searching with intent, such as "braces near me" or "Invisalign cost." Google's [personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) lists Health as a sensitive interest category, where advertiser-curated audiences such as Customer Match and remarketing lists are not supported, while predefined audiences and location targeting remain. Check how this applies to your campaigns before planning a retargeting strategy. See our [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads) for setup.

Worked example, labelled as an assumption: you spend $3,000 a month and get 30 consult requests. Half attend (15), and 40% of attendees start treatment (6). That is $500 per start. If the average case fee is $5,500, the ad cost is about 9% of revenue. Change each assumption to your own, and track the number of starts rather than clicks.

## What Do HIPAA Rules Mean for Patient Photos and Testimonials?

HHS's [marketing guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html), as summarized in search results, says a covered entity generally needs a patient's written authorization before using protected health information for marketing, with limited exceptions. Before and after photos or named testimonials can identify a patient, so get written authorization that covers the platforms you will use.

Our summary is not legal advice. Confirm with your compliance advisor and with 45 CFR 164.508, and keep signed forms with the patient record. Safer content: team intros, office tours, general answers to common questions and consent-based stories.

## How Do You Track Whether Marketing Works?

Track the full path from lead to start.

| Metric | Where to find it |
| --- | --- |
| Consult requests | Booking form and call tracking |
| Show rate | Practice management software |
| Starts | Practice management software |
| Cost per start | Ad spend divided by starts |
| Review count and rating | Google Business Profile |

Review monthly. If requests are high but starts are low, fix the consult experience and follow-up before raising the budget. If requests are low, fix the page and offer. Our [free audit](/free-audit) checks these points for your practice.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should an orthodontist spend on marketing?**
A: No verified industry benchmark was found. Set a budget from your target number of starts and your cost per start, then adjust each month.

**Q: Is Google Ads or Meta Ads better for orthodontists?**
A: Google Ads captures people already searching, while Meta builds awareness. Start with search, then test social once your tracking works.

**Q: How many reviews does an orthodontist need?**
A: There is no fixed number. BrightLocal found 74% of consumers look for reviews from the last three months, so keep a steady flow.

**Q: Can I post before and after photos?**
A: Generally only with written patient authorization under HIPAA. Check with your compliance advisor.

**Q: How do I attract adult patients?**
A: Make a dedicated adult page, offer evening appointments, show payment options and ask adult patients for reviews.
