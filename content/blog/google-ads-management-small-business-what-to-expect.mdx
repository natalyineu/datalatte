Google Ads management for a small business means a freelancer or agency builds the campaigns, sets up conversion tracking, reviews search terms and reports on cost per lead for a fee, while ad spend is paid to Google. No independent survey of management fees exists. Agency pricing guides quote 10% to 20% of ad spend or roughly $500 to $2,000 a month flat, and those are sellers describing their own market.

Good management shows up as working tracking and a stable or falling cost per lead, not as more clicks. This guide covers what is included, how fees are structured, which metrics matter and the questions to ask before you sign.

## What Does Google Ads Management Include?

**What you should receive:**

- **Account audit** before spending, or a sound structure built from scratch.
- **Keyword research** for your category and location.
- **Campaign build** with ad groups, match types and negative keyword lists.
- **Conversion tracking** for phone calls, form submissions and bookings.
- **Ad copy** with several headline and description variations (responsive search ads).
- **Assets** such as call, location, sitelink and callout.
- **Landing page review**, flagging conversion issues even if a redesign is out of scope.
- **Weekly search term reviews** to add negative keywords.
- **Monthly reporting** and a regular strategy call.

**What is usually not included:**

- Ad spend itself, which goes to Google.
- Landing page redesign or copywriting, usually a separate project fee.
- Creative production for Display or YouTube.
- Website development.

So a $600 management fee means $600 to the provider plus whatever ad budget you set, paid to Google.

## How Much Does Google Ads Management Cost?

The honest answer is that nobody publishes audited numbers. The figures below come from agency pricing guides, which are secondary sources written by sellers. Greenflow SEM, for example, says most small businesses pay about $500 to $2,000 or more a month and that percentage fees are commonly 10% to 20% of ad budget; it cites no data and bases the ranges on its own experience and published prices. Another guide read for this article returned an empty page and is not used.

| Fee model | How it works | Where it suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat monthly fee | Fixed amount whatever you spend | Small, stable budgets |
| Percent of spend | A share of ad spend, often 10% to 20% in guides | Budgets that move |
| Hybrid | Base fee plus a share of spend | Larger or growing accounts |
| Hourly or one-off audit | Paid per piece of work | Owners who manage day to day themselves |

Compare scope, reporting and contract terms before price. Some providers set minimums that make the effective rate high on a small budget.

## What Does "Working" Look Like?

**Metrics that matter:**

- **Cost per conversion or lead.** The most important number. Everything else is context.
- **Conversion rate.** The share of clicks that take the desired action. A low rate with plenty of clicks usually points to a landing page or targeting problem.
- **Search impression share.** Google defines it as impressions received divided by the total eligible impressions. Low share with an adequate budget points to bids or quality issues.

**Metrics that matter less on their own:**

- **Clicks and impressions.** More clicks are not better if they do not convert.
- **Click-through rate.** A high rate on a landing page that does not convert only raises what you pay for the same leads.
- **Quality Score.** Useful for spotting weak keywords and pages, but Google says it is not a key performance indicator and not an auction input.

## Which Five Questions Expose Weak Management?

1. **"What is your negative keyword process in the first week?"** A good answer names a starting list for your category and a weekly search terms review. "We monitor ongoing" is vague.
2. **"Which match types do you use and why?"** Broad match as the default deserves an explanation on a small local budget.
3. **"How do you track conversions from day one?"** Look for tag setup, call tracking and form tracking. Calls are often the main conversion for local businesses.
4. **"What happens if performance drops in month two?"** A diagnostic process beats waiting.
5. **"Can you show a recent search terms report from a similar client?"** An anonymised example shows real negative keyword work.

## What Does the First 90 Days Look Like?

**Weeks 1 and 2: setup.** Audit, keyword research, conversion tracking, campaign build and negatives. Nothing launches until a test call and a test form submission register.

**Weeks 3 to 6: data collection.** Search terms show what people typed. Expect negatives, paused weak keywords and ad tests. Do not judge the account on a few days of data.

**Weeks 7 to 12: optimisation.** With enough conversions, smarter bidding, location and schedule adjustments and landing page tests become possible. Timing varies by industry and budget.

## What Should the Contract Say?

- **Account ownership.** Keep the Google Ads account in your name, with the manager added as a user.
- **Contract length.** Month-to-month or a short first term is easier to leave than a 6 to 12 month lock-in.
- **Reporting.** Cost per lead, calls and form fills in plain language.
- **Guarantees.** Be wary of guaranteed rankings or lead counts; results depend on competition, budget and your site.
- **Spend transparency.** Google bills ad spend to your card with no hidden markup.

## How Do You Avoid Wasting the First Month?

Ask for a free audit first, confirm tracking works before launch and expect month one to produce conversion data, not perfect results. If month two arrives with no conversion data at all, setup is the problem, not the channel.

## FAQ

### How much should I budget for Google Ads management?

It depends on provider and scope. Flat fees and percentage-of-spend models are both common; get two or three quotes and compare what is included. Published ranges are agency self-reports, not audited data.

### Can I manage Google Ads myself?

Yes, especially at small budgets, but expect a learning curve around match types, negatives and conversion tracking. Many owners start with an audit and then decide.

### How long before Google Ads starts working?

Clicks and leads can come in the first weeks. A stable cost per lead usually needs a couple of months of data.

### Do I pay the manager or Google for ad spend?

Ad spend is paid to Google directly. The management fee is separate.
