## How Should a Business Choose Platforms Without Reach Data?

Without verified reach data, start from your customers rather than from a platform ranking. Ask recent customers how they found the business and which app they use to message it. A bakery in Baghdad and an electrician in Basra may rely on different channels, and only local enquiries can show that difference.

Shortlist two channels that match those answers. Limiting the comparison to two keeps the test readable and stops the budget from spreading too thin. If you already schedule social posts, a comparison such as the [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social guide for small businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) can help you choose a tool before adding paid spend.

## How Might Audience Gender Affect Targeting?

Audience gender splits differ by platform and can change over time, so they should not be assumed. A salon, a clothing shop and a hardware store may each attract a different mix of customers, and the same ad can perform differently for each group.

Use the audience breakdowns inside your own ad account once the test starts. Compare cost per enquiry for each segment, and avoid excluding any group until there are enough results to justify it. If the platform does not report the breakdown needed, a simple question at the point of sale, such as "how did you hear about us," gives a cheaper check.

In this example, Channel A produces more messages for the same spend, so it would receive most of the budget in the next period. A smaller share should stay on Channel B to confirm the result. Count a message as useful only when it asks about price, availability or a booking, so that casual reactions do not inflate the comparison.

## What Should You Set Up First?

Before paying for ads, confirm that customers can find and contact the business. The steps below cover this groundwork.

1. Complete the Google Business Profile, including the correct category, address pin, hours and phone or messaging number.
2. Confirm which messaging app customers actually use, by asking them rather than assuming.
3. Label each enquiry by channel, using a unique phone number, link or checkout question.
4. Set a total test budget and a daily cap, and write down the start date.
5. Review results after 14 days and compare cost per useful message, not reach or likes.

For a service business that books jobs, a field service tool may handle follow-up. The [Jobber vs ServiceTitan comparison](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business) shows how those systems differ.
