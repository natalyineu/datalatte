The best way to build an email list as a local business is to ask for an address at the moments people are already engaged with you, such as at checkout, when they book, or when they scan a code on your counter, and to give them a clear reason to say yes. Below are 20 methods that work for shops, salons, studios and groomers, grouped by where the sign-up happens.

Unlike social media followers, an email list is an audience you own. An algorithm change cannot take it away, and a message to people who chose to hear from you is usually the cheapest repeat-visit tool a small business has. The catch is that lists grow slowly unless you build sign-up into your daily routine.

## Before you start: consent and tools

Collect addresses only from people who agree to receive marketing. The rules differ by country: CAN-SPAM in the US, GDPR and PECR in the UK, CASL in Canada and the Spam Act in Australia. In practice, that means a clear sign-up message ("Get our monthly offers by email"), an unsubscribe link in every email, and no buying lists. Do not add customers to marketing emails just because they gave you an address for a receipt.

You also need somewhere to store the list. Mailchimp, MailerLite, Brevo and Constant Contact all have entry-level plans that are free or cost roughly $10 to $30 a month for small lists, and many booking and point-of-sale systems (Square, Vagaro, Fresha) include basic email tools. ## In-person methods

**1. Ask at the point of sale.** Train staff to say one sentence: "Want our offers and news by email? I can add you now." Keep it optional and quick.

**2. Put a QR code on the counter.** A small sign that links to a one-field sign-up form lets customers join while waiting. Test the code on a phone before you print it.

**3. Use a tablet or clipboard sign-up.** Works well for salons and studios where people sit and wait. Type nothing for them; let them enter their own address to avoid typos.

**4. Add a checkbox to your booking flow.** If clients book online, include an unticked "Email me offers and reminders" box. Booking tools like Square Appointments and Vagaro typically support this.

**5. Collect at class or appointment check-in.** Fitness studios and groomers see the same people repeatedly, which makes check-in a natural, low-pressure moment.

**6. Run a birthday club.** Ask for a birthday month and an email in exchange for a small treat. It gives you a reason to send an email every year.

**7. Print the sign-up on receipts and cups.** A short URL or QR code on packaging costs almost nothing and reaches everyone who buys.

## Website methods

**8. Add a sign-up form in your footer.** It is easy to build and stays visible on every page.

**9. Use one well-timed pop-up.** An exit-intent or delayed pop-up can work, but keep it to one, make it easy to close, and test it on mobile where intrusive pop-ups can annoy visitors.

**10. Create a dedicated landing page.** A simple page with one headline, one benefit and one field is easy to link from social bios, flyers and Google Business Profile posts.

**11. Offer a useful resource.** A seasonal menu, a grooming schedule, a class timetable or a "first visit" guide gives people a reason to subscribe that is not just a discount.

**12. Add a sign-up to your contact and thank-you pages.** People who just reached out are already interested.

## Offers and incentives

**13. Give a first-visit incentive.** A free drink, a small add-on or a modest discount is typical. Work out the cost per subscriber so the offer does not eat your margin.

**14. Run a giveaway with care.** A prize that only appeals to your real customers (a month of coffee, a free grooming) attracts better subscribers than a generic gift card. Follow the local rules on contests, and make the email opt-in a separate, optional tick box rather than a hidden condition.

**15. Try a referral loop.** Invite subscribers to forward your email or share a code, with a reward for both sides.

## Partnerships and channels

**16. Partner with neighbouring businesses.** A salon and a nearby boutique, or a gym and a juice bar, can each promote the other's offer with a shared sign-up page. Only share addresses if each person has explicitly agreed.

**17. Promote the list on social media.** Mention it in your bio link, Stories and posts. Our guide to [Instagram marketing for coffee shops](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-marketing) shows how to turn followers into regulars.

**18. Use paid ads sparingly.** A small Meta lead-generation campaign can collect sign-ups for a specific offer. See [Meta Ads](/services/meta-ads) if you want help with that.

**19. Add a link to your email signature and Google Business Profile.** Business Profile posts and your website link can point to the sign-up page at no cost.

**20. Host events or workshops.** Sign-up for event details doubles as list building, and attendees are typically warm leads.

## Make the list worth keeping

Growing a list matters less than keeping it engaged. Send a welcome email right after sign-up, because people are most interested then. Tell them how often you will write, and then stick to it. For most local businesses, one or two useful emails a month beats a weekly blast.

Segment when you can. Even two groups, such as new subscribers and regulars, let you tailor your message. Remove people who have not opened anything in six to twelve months; a smaller, engaged list tends to perform better than a large, ignored one.

## FAQ

**Q: How many subscribers does a local business need?**

A: There is no magic number. Even a few hundred engaged local subscribers can bring in measurable repeat visits. Focus on the percentage of customers you capture rather than total size.

**Q: Is it legal to email customers who gave me their address for a receipt or booking?**

A: Not automatically for marketing. Most regions expect clear consent for promotional emails, so use a tick box or a separate sign-up. Check the rules in your country or ask a local adviser.

**Q: Should I offer a discount to get sign-ups?**

A: It helps, but it is not required. Many businesses do well with useful content, early access or a loyalty perk. Test one offer for a month and see how many people join.

**Q: What is the fastest method to start with?**

A: A QR code at the counter plus a staff prompt at checkout. Both are cheap to set up in an afternoon.
