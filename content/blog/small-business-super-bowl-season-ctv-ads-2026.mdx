A local business cannot buy a national Super Bowl LXI spot, which sold for roughly $8 million per 30 seconds. The game is on February 14, 2027, and a small business can still advertise in its own city around it. That means game-window streaming ads, geo-targeted search and map ads, and a watch-party offer, tested over four weeks from mid-January on a budget of $300 to $1,000.

## What does an $8 million Super Bowl spot signal to a local business?

The price signals that national brands pay for one thing: a huge live audience, bought almost a year ahead. Disney first asked [$10 million](https://awfulannouncing.com/disney/seeks-10-million-super-bowl-lxi-ads.html) for a 30-second spot (secondary), then tried $9 million, and later sold a "significant chunk" at about [$8 million](https://wdwnt.com/2026/06/disney-selling-8-million-super-bowl-ad-spots-despite-advertiser-pushback/) each (secondary, retelling Variety).

Super Bowl ads sold for around [$5 million in 2016](https://wdwnt.com/2026/04/advertisers-push-back-on-disney-seeking-10-million-for-30-second-super-bowl-commercial/) (secondary), so the price has climbed steadily. Buyers still pushed back on the opening ask.

For a local business, the takeaway is not to copy the spot. It is that attention around this game is expensive nationally, and a neighborhood-sized version of that attention can be bought in small pieces.

The inventory is also gone. A [Moonb guide](https://www.moonb.io/blog/super-bowl-commercial-cost) (secondary) reports every 30-second slot sold by August 5, 2026, to 58 brands across 34 categories.

## Can a small business buy ads inside the game itself?

Mostly no, and the budget range explains why. The Moonb guide reports local broadcast spots in smaller markets from about $10,000 to $50,000. That is ten to fifty times a $1,000 test budget, for one break.

The same guide says streams have sold some of their own ads since 2024, but the sources give no price or availability for local advertisers on this game.

So the realistic plan treats the game as a calendar anchor rather than a placement. Viewers talk about the game, plan food around it, and search for places to watch or pick up supplies. A local business can meet that demand in the same city and the same week without bidding for the broadcast.

When asking a CTV platform about live-game inventory, request the answer in writing. Whether any is open to small local buyers is not documented in the sources, and a rep can confirm it faster than guessing. A broader view of buying live sports is in the [live sports advertising guide](/blog/live-sports-advertising-local-business).

## What can a local business buy around game day?

Three pieces fit a small budget and a four-week window.

Game-window CTV ads reach households in chosen ZIP codes. Adwave (secondary) says platforms now offer ZIP code-level and even household-level targeting, and reports streaming CPMs of $15 to $35, with free ad-supported channels toward the lower end. The [real CPM data guide](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data) breaks down what small advertisers see in practice.

Geo-targeted search and map ads catch people actively looking, such as "coffee near me" or "gym trial class". These carry the intent that CTV builds up.

Watch-party promotions turn the game into an offer. The game falls on Valentine's Day, which [Disney sees as a big opportunity](https://wdwnt.com/2026/04/advertisers-push-back-on-disney-seeking-10-million-for-30-second-super-bowl-commercial/) (secondary), so a couple-friendly or group-friendly offer has a natural hook. For NFL-specific tactics, see the [NFL CTV guide for local business](/blog/nfl-ctv-advertising-local-business).

## How should CTV be planned without local price data?

Start by asking, not predicting. The sources do not document whether local streaming rates move around the game, up or down, so any claim about that is a guess. Ask each platform for its current rate for the exact flight dates and ZIP codes, and get the CPM, minimum spend and frequency settings in writing.

A few planning rules keep a small test honest:

- Keep the targeted area to the real customer radius. A coffee shop with a ten-minute walk-in zone gains little from a metro-wide buy.
- Cap frequency, so a small budget reaches more households instead of repeating to a few.
- Put a QR code or a short offer URL on the ad. Adwave cites an LG Ad Solutions study (secondary) saying QR scan rates on CTV ads rose 42% year over year, with local service and retail advertisers showing the most engagement.
- Build the creative to the platform's specs before the flight starts. The [creative specs guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-creative-specs-guide-2026) lists the file requirements, and the [new formats article](/blog/new-ctv-ad-formats-2026-pause-ads-qr-codes-shoppable) covers QR and shoppable options.

If a platform cannot supply a quote for game week, a different platform or a pre-game flight is the fallback. Comparisons are in the [CTV platforms guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026).

## Which watch-party promotions fit a coffee shop or fitness studio?

The offer needs to be simple enough to explain in a 15-second ad and a search headline.

For a coffee shop, a game-day pre-order box, such as a drip coffee carrier plus pastries for a gathering, works because it is easy to bundle, pre-pay and pick up. Restaurants and cafes can borrow tactics from the [CTV for restaurants guide](/blog/ctv-ads-for-restaurants).

For a fitness studio, a Valentine's weekend partner class or a "get moving before the game" trial gives people a low-risk first visit. More ideas for studios are in the [fitness studio CTV guide](/blog/ctv-ads-for-fitness-studios).

Whichever type fits, the promotion needs three practical pieces: a cutoff date for orders or bookings, a unique code or URL per channel, and staff who know the offer. Without the code, there is no way to tell search from CTV later.

## What does the 4-week calendar from mid-January look like?

The calendar below is a suggested schedule. Dates are planning labels, and the only date taken from the sources is the game on February 14, 2027.

| Week | Dates (suggested) | Tasks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Week of Jan 18 | Fix the offer, price and pickup or booking limits. Build a landing page with a QR code and unique promo code. Update Google Business Profile hours and add an event post. Request written CTV quotes with ZIP lists and flight dates. Set up conversion tracking. |
| 2 | Week of Jan 25 | Launch geo-targeted search and map ads with a daily cap. Upload CTV creative and start a light flight. Print in-store signs with the QR code. |
| 3 | Week of Feb 1 | Compare real CPM and cost per click with the planned figures. Move budget toward the better channel. Send an email or text to existing customers. |
| 4 | Week of Feb 8 to game day, Feb 14 | Heaviest spend in the final days. Remind buyers of the order or booking cutoff. Staff and stock for the rush. After the game, record orders by code and decide what to keep. |

## What does a $800 test look like for a coffee shop?

Every input below is an assumption for illustration, not a benchmark. The CPM sits inside the $15 to $35 range Adwave reports; the other figures are placeholders to be replaced with real quotes.

| Line | Spend (assumption) | Assumed rate | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Game-window CTV, ZIP-targeted, weeks 2 to 4 | $400 | $25 CPM | 16,000 impressions |
| Geo-targeted search and map ads | $300 | $1.50 per click | 200 clicks |
| Signs, QR printing, small giveaway | $100 | not applicable | not applicable |
| Total | $800 | | |

Further assumptions: average frequency of 4 per household, so 16,000 impressions reach 4,000 households. Assume 0.2% of those households order a game-day box, which gives 8 orders. Assume 10% of the 200 search clicks become orders, which gives 20. The total is 28 orders.

At an assumed $45 per box, 28 orders bring in $1,260. At an assumed 60% gross margin, that is $756 against $800 of spend. Break-even is 30 orders ($800 divided by $27 margin per box, rounded up).

So the first test lands just under break-even, and repeat visits decide whether it pays off. At a $15 CPM the same $400 buys about 26,667 impressions, and at $35 about 11,429, so real quotes change the reach noticeably.

At the low end of the range, $300 is better spent entirely on search and map ads plus signage. At $1,000, extra money can go to CTV or a longer flight.

## How can the result be read afterward?

Compare orders or bookings by code, by QR scan and by source in the analytics tool, then divide spend by orders for each channel. The assumption table above gives the targets to beat: 30 orders at the example prices, or the break-even number for the shop's own margin.

CTV often works as the awareness layer, so search clicks may rise during the CTV flight. Adwave (secondary) reports that 72% of CTV advertisers now use multi-touch attribution, but a single-location shop can get most of the signal from codes and a short survey at pickup or check-in.

After the game, two decisions remain. Keep the channel that produced orders at an acceptable cost. Keep the email or text list built during the promotion, since a game-week campaign only pays for one weekend, while a list can be reused. ### What budget works for a game-week test?

The plan above uses $300 to $1,000 as a range. The $800 coffee shop example is built on assumptions, and the first run lands just under break-even at the stated inputs, so the real cost per order from the test matters more than the example.

Mid-January gives four weeks before February 14, 2027: one week of setup, then three weeks of ads that build toward game day.
