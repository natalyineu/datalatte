A local retail store should start Google Ads with a small Search campaign limited to the area people travel from, and connect its product feed to Google Merchant Center so nearby shoppers can see what is in stock. WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put Shopping, Collectibles and Gifts at $4.14 per click and Apparel, Fashion and Jewelry at $4.44. There is no single "retail" benchmark, so use those as a range.

This guide is for independent shops that sell from a physical location, in the US, UK, Canada and Australia. All figures come from the sources listed at the end. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Does Google Ads Cost for a Retail Store?

Retail clicks are cheaper than many service categories, but conversion rates are lower. LocaliQ defines conversion rate as leads divided by clicks, so for a shop a "lead" is usually a call, a direction request or a form, not a sale.

| Benchmark (US, 2026) | CPC | Conversion rate | Cost per lead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All industries | $5.42 | 8.18% | $66.69 |
| Shopping, Collectibles and Gifts | $4.14 | 4.01% | $49.40 |
| Apparel, Fashion and Jewelry | $4.44 | 4.50% | $97.51 |
| Furniture | $3.97 | 2.99% | $106.70 |

Furniture and apparel show the problem: a click is cheap, but each lead costs $97 to $107. Treat these as ranges and replace them with your own data after a month ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/)).

## Can You Show Your In-Store Stock on Google?

Yes, if your country is supported. Google Merchant Center describes two options: free local listings, which show in-store products across Google at no cost, and local inventory ads, which are paid. Local inventory ads are offered in about 90 countries and free local listings in about 45, including the US, UK and Canada. Retailers can also show pickup options, such as reserving a product and collecting it in store the same or next day ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/3057972)).

Start with the free listings. They need a product feed from your point-of-sale system, so ask your provider whether it exports one. Add paid local inventory ads once you know which products sell.

## Which Search Campaign Should You Start With?

Use one Search campaign for your best-selling category and one for your brand name. Brand searches are cheap and protect against competitors bidding on your name. For the category campaign, use the phrases a nearby shopper would type, such as the product plus a town or neighborhood.

Negative keywords stop waste. Google explains that they do not match close variants, so add plurals and synonyms yourself ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972)). Good negatives for a shop: "free", "used" if you sell new, "wholesale", "jobs", "pdf", and any brand you do not stock.

## How Do You Keep Ads Close to the Store?

Retail ads waste money when they reach people who will not drive to you. Google Ads lets you target by region, city, postal code or a radius of at least 1 km around a location. It recommends broad "Presence or interest" for Search but says "Presence" suits advertisers who want people in the specific area only ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722043)).

For a shop people visit, try Presence first. If you also ship, run a separate campaign for delivery with a wider area, so the local budget is not diluted.

## What Does Your Google Business Profile Need?

Your free profile appears next to the ad and in map results. Google says local ranking depends on relevance, distance and prominence, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). Keep opening hours, special hours and product photos current, because shoppers check them before they leave home.

Ask for reviews with a link or QR code at the till. Google prohibits offering free or discounted goods in exchange for a review ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). ## The Math: What Does a $500 Month Return?

All inputs are **assumptions** built on the Shopping, Collectibles and Gifts benchmark. A "lead" is a call, directions request or form.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $500 | assumption |
| CPC | $4.14 | LocaliQ benchmark |
| Clicks | about 121 | 500 / 4.14 |
| Conversion rate | 4.01% | LocaliQ benchmark |
| Leads | about 4.8 | 121 x 4.01% |
| Cost per lead | about $103 | 500 / 4.8 |
| Share of leads who buy (assumption) | 50% | assumption |
| Sales | about 2.4 | 4.8 x 50% |
| Average basket (assumption) | $70 | assumption |
| First-sale revenue | about $168 | 2.4 x $70 |

On the first sale alone, the ad loses money. It pays only if the customer comes back or the basket is larger, which is why many shops use ads for high-margin products and rely on the free profile and reviews for casual traffic. Our SMS marketing for retail guide covers repeat sales.

## FAQ

### How much should a local retail store spend on Google Ads?
There is no standard amount. At a $4.14 click, $500 buys about 120 clicks. Start with a sum you can afford to lose for a month and scale once you know your cost per sale.

### What is the difference between free local listings and local inventory ads?
Free local listings show your in-store products on Google at no cost. Local inventory ads are paid and show stock and pickup options to nearby shoppers. Both need your inventory data in Merchant Center.

### Is Google Ads or SEO better for a local shop?
Start with the free Business Profile and reviews, then add ads for products where margin covers the click cost.

### Should a store bid on its own name?
Often yes. Brand clicks are usually cheap and stop competitors taking the top spot. Check your own cost before you decide.

### What conversion rate should I expect?
The nearest benchmarks are 4.01% for Shopping, Collectibles and Gifts and 4.50% for Apparel, Fashion and Jewelry. Your rate depends on your pages and stock.
