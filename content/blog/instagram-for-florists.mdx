A florist should plan Instagram around the two biggest flower holidays and use the rest of the year to show real arrangements, prices and how to order. NRF forecasts US shoppers will spend $3.1 billion on flowers for Valentine's Day 2026 and $3.2 billion for Mother's Day 2026, so posts that make ordering easy before those dates matter most.

No source publishes Instagram conversion data for florists. This guide uses NRF holiday data, Instagram's own ranking guidance and LocaliQ ad benchmarks, and labels examples as assumptions.

## When Do Florists Need Instagram Most?

Holidays drive the calendar. NRF's Valentine's Day forecast was a record $29.1 billion in total, with flowers at $3.1 billion and 41% of consumers planning to buy them. For Mother's Day, NRF projected $38 billion in total and $3.2 billion on flowers, with 75% of shoppers planning to purchase flowers.

These are forecasts from consumer surveys of about 7,800 adults each, with a margin of error of 1.1 points, not shop sales. NRF also says 38% of Valentine's shoppers planned to buy online and 21% from specialty stores. Mother's Day shoppers named online at 33% and specialty stores at 29%. Specialty stores include florists, so you are competing with online sellers for the same buyers.

## What Should a Florist Post?

Instagram says Reels make up over 50% of time spent on the app, and that Reels it recommends to non-followers are judged on whether people watch to the end, reshare or like. Show the making, not only the finished bouquet:

- A bouquet from loose stems to wrapped, with the price in the caption
- Delivery or pickup on the day, with the cut-off time for orders
- A weekly "what is in the cooler" Story with available stems
- A holiday preorder post with dates and a clear order link

Instagram's guidance is to post original content and avoid reposts and watermarked clips. It also says relevant keywords in your caption, bio and hashtags help search, so write "birthday bouquet in [your town]" instead of only "blooms."

## How Do You Turn a Like Into an Order?

Put the order method where people already look. Use the bio for your area, delivery rules and order link. Instagram's creators site recommends using Insights to look for patterns in which content gets more reach, so check which formats lead to profile visits and message requests.

Keep your Google profile current as well, because many buyers search for a florist near them before choosing. See the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and our [florist marketing guide](/blog/marketing-for-florists).

## Should a Florist Run Instagram Ads?

Possibly for holiday preorders. WordStream by LocaliQ's September 2026 benchmarks do not list florists. The nearest category, Shopping, Collectibles and Gifts, shows a traffic CTR of 1.88% and a traffic CPC of $0.59 for Facebook ads. The all-industry traffic CPC is $0.60.

Worked example. All inputs are **assumptions**:

- Budget: $200 for the week before a holiday
- CPC: $0.59 (benchmark for the closest category)
- Clicks: 200 / 0.59 = about 339
- Share of visitors who order: 2% (assumption)
- Orders: about 6.8
- Average order: $65 (assumption)
- Revenue: about $440

Change the order rate and the result changes a lot, which is why you should track clicks and orders from the link. Google search ads can also catch holiday demand; see [Google Ads for florists](/blog/google-ads-for-florists).

## FAQ

### How often should a florist post on Instagram?
Instagram says to post original content consistently but gives no required number. Increase posting in the two weeks before Valentine's Day and Mother's Day.

### Do Reels or photos sell more flowers?
No data splits that for florists. Reels have more reach potential, while photos and Stories help people ready to order.

### Which holidays matter most for florists?
NRF forecasts $3.1 billion for Valentine's Day and $3.2 billion for Mother's Day flower spending in 2026. Check local demand for other occasions in your own order history.

### Should I show prices on Instagram?
Prices help buyers decide, and none of the sources here says otherwise. Test it and compare messages and orders.

### How much should I spend on ads?
There is no florist benchmark. Start with a small, time-limited budget tied to one preorder offer.
