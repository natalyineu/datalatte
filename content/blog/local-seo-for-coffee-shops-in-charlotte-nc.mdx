A coffee shop in Charlotte, NC moves up the Google Maps pack mainly by getting its Google Business Profile right: the correct category, a verified listing, answers to common questions, regular posts and a steady flow of reviews. Consistent citations, where your name, address and phone number match across directories, support that work. Google's own pages confirm the profile is free to set up and that reviews appear beside the listing in Maps and Search. This guide covers each step, with a worked review example and a monthly checklist.

## Why does the Google Business Profile carry so much of the map pack?

A BrightLocal survey, reported by Ahrefs, found that 36% of SEOs consider the Google Business Profile the most important ranking factor for the local pack in 2021. The same survey's share for that view was 23% in 2013 ([Ahrefs local SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), secondary). This reflects expert opinion, not a measured ranking formula, so read it as a sign of where attention belongs rather than a guarantee.

BrewRound's 2020 guide says 46% of all Google searches are for local businesses, but it cites no source for that figure ([BrewRound](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/), secondary). Treat it as a rough indicator. Google's Business Profile page describes the listing as free to set up on Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)), so the main cost of competing is time spent keeping the profile current.

## Which category and verification steps come first?

Start with the category. Per Diem recommends choosing "Coffee shop" or a related term rather than a broad one such as "Restaurant" ([Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide), secondary). BrewRound notes that you can select one category at setup and add more later ([BrewRound](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/), secondary). Pick "Coffee shop" first, finish setup, then add "Cafe" or "Espresso bar" if they match your menu.

Verification comes next. Per Diem states that without verification, updates won't show publicly and customers may not find the business ([Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide), secondary). BrewRound describes a 2020 flow in which Google mails a letter with a code, and the profile does not appear in results until it is verified ([BrewRound](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/), secondary). Check the current verification method in your dashboard, because that description is from 2020.

## What questions and messages should the profile handle?

Answer common questions before customers ask them. Per Diem recommends answering proactively whether you offer free Wi-Fi, have dairy-free options or allow dogs on the patio ([Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide), secondary). For a Charlotte shop, that means writing out your milk alternatives, seating for laptop users and outdoor seating rules, then keeping those answers current when the menu or policies change.

Google also lets people message businesses directly through their listings ([Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide), secondary). Assign one person to check messages on a fixed schedule so enquiries don't go unanswered. Per Diem also reports that Think Coffee, an independent brand, has thousands of app downloads after placing its app download link on its Google profile. That is a single reported case, not a benchmark.

For more detail on profile fields, see [our Google Business Profile tips for coffee shops](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips).

## How often should posts and photos go up?

Posts need a status check. Google's help page says a post appears publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, and Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Help: create and manage posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). After publishing, open the post and confirm it is Live. Any post that is not Live needs editing or resubmitting.

For cadence, The Restaurant HQ recommends posting on social media at least once a week, ideally one to two times a day ([The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/operations/coffee-shop-marketing/), secondary). That is social media advice, so use it as a minimum schedule for your own planning rather than a Google rule. Rotate photos seasonally, and see [our guide to coffee shop profile optimisation](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization) for a photo and post plan.

## How do reviews work toward the map pack in Charlotte?

Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google Help: tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). A shop with few reviews has less visible social proof on its listing. The figure of "over 50 reviews with a 4.7+ average" appears in an Accountability Now article for landscapers, which cites no source for it ([Accountability Now](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), secondary). Use it as a working target only.

Worked example (inputs are assumptions):
- Current reviews: 22 (assumption)
- New reviews per week: 3 (assumption)
- Target: 50 (from the secondary article above)

Reviews still needed: 50 − 22 = 28. At 3 per week, 28 ÷ 3 ≈ 9.3, so about 10 weeks. If the pace falls to 1.5 per week (assumption), 28 ÷ 1.5 ≈ 18.7, so about 19 weeks. The gap between those two timelines is the reason to set a fixed follow-up routine after each visit or order.

## Which citations and keywords are worth checking in Charlotte?

Citations work best when every listing matches exactly. Abbreviations such as "St." versus "Street" and formatting differences in phone numbers create mismatches that are easy to miss. Check Yelp, TripAdvisor, Foursquare, Bing Places, and local chamber of commerce or neighbourhood directories. One landscaping firm reported an 18% jump in local pack appearances after cleaning up its NAP data, according to Accountability Now. That is one anecdote, not a benchmark ([Accountability Now](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), secondary).

If you run several shops, Google's Ads help states that location data in Google Ads comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, so corrections made there flow into linked accounts. Advertisers can select up to 10 Maps locations to link when other options do not work ([Google Ads Help: location assets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)).

Match keywords to intent rather than chasing volume. Search volumes change, so check your own Search Console data for current numbers.

| Query pattern | Likely intent | Where to place it |
|---|---|---|
| coffee shop near me | Visit now | Category, hours, description |
| coffee shop open now | Visit now | Accurate hours on the profile |
| best café [city] | Research | Description, review wording |
| [neighbourhood] coffee | Hyper-local | Service pages, description |
| specialty coffee [city] | Discovery | Menu, posts |

## What should a Charlotte coffee shop check each month?

1. Confirm the profile is still verified and the primary category is "Coffee shop".
2. Re-read Q&A answers on Wi-Fi, dairy-free options and dog policy, and correct anything out of date.
3. Confirm every recent post shows Live status. Edit or resubmit any that do not.
4. Reply to each new review, naming the drink or neighbourhood where it fits naturally.
5. Swap in fresh photos for the current season.
6. Check name, address and phone on your main directories for transposed digits or abbreviation drift.
7. Log your review count and map pack position for your top keywords in one spreadsheet.

Embedding a Google Map on your location page helps visitors find the shop. For setup steps, see [how to embed a Google Maps listing on your website](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

## FAQ

### Does a Google Business Profile cost anything?

Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up on Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). BrewRound also notes that you need a Google account, which can be registered for free ([BrewRound](https://brewround.com/blog/coffee-shop-marketing/google-my-business-for-coffee-shops/), secondary).

### Which category should a coffee shop choose?

Per Diem recommends "Coffee shop" or a related term rather than a broad category such as "Restaurant" ([Per Diem](https://www.tryperdiem.com/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-guide), secondary). Add secondary categories after the primary one is set.

### Why isn't my post showing publicly?

Google says a post shows on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status and edit or resubmit the post.

### Do I need 50 reviews to rank on the map?

Google confirms reviews appear next to your listing but does not publish a review threshold in the sources used here. The 50-review figure comes from a secondary landscaping article and should be treated as a working goal ([Accountability Now](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), secondary).
