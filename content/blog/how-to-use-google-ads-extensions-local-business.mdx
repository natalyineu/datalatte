Google Ads extensions are now called assets. For a local business, the most useful one is the location asset, which can show your address, a map and the approximate travel distance. It reads its information from your Business Profile, so an out-of-date address or phone number appears in the ad. Call, sitelink, callout, price and promotion assets add links, facts and offers. Quality Score is a diagnostic, not an auction input, so the strongest results come when the ad and the landing page say the same thing. The sections below cover what to set up first, what the sources confirm, and how to check results with a worked budget.

## What changed when extensions became assets?

Google renamed ad extensions to assets. The features are the same, but the help pages and interface now use the new name, so a tutorial that says "extensions" describes the same options. Google's location asset help covers two cases: "Formerly Location Extensions" for businesses that own their stores, and "Formerly Affiliate Location Extensions" for businesses that sell through stores they do not own.

For a local business, the first case applies. Google's example is a bakery owner who adds a location asset so that nearby people searching on mobile for "best bakery nearby" or "fresh bread" can see the distance, the street address on a computer, and a Call button. Tapping through leads to a details page with hours, phone number, photos and directions.

Google's help also says location assets can work with Performance Max campaigns. If you run that format, check the location asset first. The [Performance Max guide for local businesses](/blog/google-performance-max-for-local-business-complete-guide-2026) explains how that campaign type is set up.

## How does the location asset get its data?

The location asset draws its information from your Business Profile, so the profile is the real setup task. Google's location asset help says the ads can show the store address, a map to the location and the approximate travel distance. It also says location assets can enable store visit conversions, which track when your ads lead to a visit, and that Google turns these on automatically when an account meets eligibility criteria.

A secondary source, Store Growers, which was updated in January 2026, gives older profile rules that are not official Google guidance. It advises using only one Business Profile for the business, because duplicate profiles cause location extension problems. It also says the profile should be managed by the owner or an authorised representative, and that the business should have a physical location customers can visit or travel to meet them.

Before linking, check the name, address, phone number and hours on the profile. The [Business Profile tips for a coffee shop](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips) list what to tidy first. If the profile is wrong, the asset shows the wrong information.

## How does Quality Score relate to assets?

Quality Score is a diagnostic tool, not a direct input to the ad auction. Google's help says it is measured from 1 to 10 at keyword level and is based on three components: expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Each is rated "Above average", "Average" or "Below average", compared with other advertisers whose ads showed for the same search over the last 90 days.

Google also lists factors that Quality Score may not capture, and assets are among them. An asset therefore does not raise your Quality Score on its own. The score still matters, because weak ad relevance or a weak landing page rating is usually the first thing to fix. Google says Quality Score is not a key performance indicator and should not be optimised or aggregated with the rest of your data. Use it to spot weak keywords, then judge assets by enquiries and visits.

## Which assets should a local business set up first?

Start with the assets that answer the searcher's next question: where you are, how to call, what it costs and what is on offer. The table gives a checklist for each one, with what the sources cover and what to check before you publish.

| Asset | What it does for a local business | What the sources support | Check before you publish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Shows address, map, distance and a details page | Google location asset help, primary | Business Profile address, hours and phone are correct |
| Call | Adds a Call button on the ad | Google location asset help, primary, for the Call button in the bakery example | Phone number matches your website and profile |
| Sitelinks | Adds links to key pages | Not covered in the sources reviewed here | Each link goes to a page that matches its label |
| Callouts | Adds short facts | Not covered in the sources reviewed here | Each fact is true and easy to confirm on your site |
| Price | Shows a menu of services | Not covered in the sources reviewed here | Each price is on your site, or left out |
| Promotion | Shows a time-limited offer | Not covered in the sources reviewed here | The offer has an end date and a matching landing page |

Where the sources give no limit or number, use the limits shown in your own Google Ads account when you create each asset. Do not rely on a figure from a tutorial that you cannot check against Google's current help.

## How do you check whether assets are working?

Look at impressions and clicks for each asset in the Assets report, then compare enquiries, calls and visits with the period before the changes. Store visit conversions can show when people who saw a location asset later visited the store, but they depend on eligibility, so confirm your conversion settings before relying on the figure.

Secondary source Blast Analytics, published in 2016, says the advertiser pays the same cost per click whichever action the user takes, whether that is a site visit, a map click or a call. That is a historical description and the current billing terms should be checked in your account. If you are unsure what to measure, start with calls and direction taps, since those are the actions a location asset is designed to produce. Google's Quality Score help also notes that the score is based on historical exact-match searches, so changing match types does not change it.

## What should you do this week?

1. Fix the Business Profile address, phone number and hours, and confirm the business has only one profile.
2. Link the profile to your Google Ads account and add a location asset for your physical location.
3. Add a call asset only if your phone line is staffed during the hours you publish.
4. Write sitelinks and callouts that match facts on your website, and send each sitelink to a page that matches its label.
5. Add a promotion only if you have a live offer, with an end date and a landing page that states the same offer.
6. Set a reminder for four weeks ahead to review the Assets report and compare enquiries with the period before.

## FAQ

### Are Google Ads extensions the same as assets?

Yes. Google renamed ad extensions to assets. The interface and help pages now use the new name, and the features are the same.

### Do assets make my Quality Score go up?

Not directly. Quality Score is a diagnostic from 1 to 10 based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Google lists assets among the factors Quality Score may not capture, so focus on relevance and the landing page.

### Who should use a location asset?

Any business with a physical location customers can visit, provided its Business Profile is accurate. Google's example is a bakery whose nearby customers see the distance, the address and a Call button.

### Should I run a promotion asset?

Only if you have a real offer that your landing page also states. Give it an end date so you never advertise an expired deal.
