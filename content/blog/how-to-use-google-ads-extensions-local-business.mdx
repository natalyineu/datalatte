Six assets, roughly an hour of work, and your Google ad stops being two lines of text and starts looking like a small shop front: your address, a call button, four links to the pages people actually want, and a price menu. Most local ad accounts we see use two of the six at best. This guide shows which to set up, the exact limits Google publishes for each, and how to tell whether they are earning their place.

**A note on the name.** What this article calls "assets" used to be called "ad extensions". Google announced the rename on its [Ads blog](https://www.blog.google/products/ads-commerce/create-engaging-ads/) ("ad extensions will now be called assets"), and the Google Ads interface and help pages now use "assets". The features themselves are the same. If a tutorial or a freelancer still says "extensions", they mean this. The rename went further than vocabulary: Google's help describes assets as the content pieces that make up an ad, which includes headlines, descriptions, images and the add-ons covered here.

## How Assets Work (and What Google Does Not Promise)

You create an asset once and attach it at account, campaign or ad group level. Google's [assets overview](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375499) says assets must be approved to show, and that they show only when Google predicts they will improve performance and your ad's position and Ad Rank are high enough. In plain terms: you supply the options, Google picks the combination, and no asset is guaranteed to appear with every ad.

That has two consequences for a small account:

- **Low Ad Rank means fewer assets.** If your bids or ad relevance are weak, you will see fewer extras. Our [Quality Score guide](/blog/google-ads-quality-score-guide-lower-cpc) covers the levers.
- **Give Google more to choose from.** More approved options means more chances to match the search.

For the more specific level to win: Google's help for [call assets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453991) states that the most specific level takes priority, so an ad group asset beats a campaign asset, which beats an account asset. Set generic assets at account level, and put niche ones (for example, "Colour correction from $120") on the matching ad group only.

## The 6 Assets to Set Up First

### 1. Location asset: your address, map and directions

This is the one that turns an ad into a store visit. Per Google's [location asset help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182), the asset pulls address and phone data from Google Maps and your Business Profile, and it can show on Search, Maps and other surfaces. Locations marked closed in your Business Profile will not show.

Setup: in Google Ads go to Tools, then Shared library, then Location Manager, add "Our locations", choose Google Business Profile and select the manager account. Newer accounts may be guided through Connected products instead. Google notes menu paths vary by account version.

Two warnings from Google's page: each account can use only one location asset type, and if you remove all locations you lose location targeting and store visit tracking. Fix your profile first, because the ad reads from it. Our [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers that.

### 2. Call asset: one tap to ring you

Google's [call asset help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453991) lists these rules, all worth knowing before you submit:

- A click on the call button costs the same as a headline click, which is your normal CPC.
- Turn on call reporting. It uses a Google forwarding number and is available only on the Search Network.
- Use the scheduling option to show the number only when someone answers.
- Vanity, premium and fax numbers are not accepted.
- Do not put your phone number in the ad text, as that can cause a disapproval.
- The exact number must appear in your website's source code, or you must verify the domain in Search Console. Dynamic number insertion or third-party call tracking can trigger "Unverified Phone Number" disapprovals.

That last point matters if you also run call tracking. Our [call tracking guide](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) explains the trade-offs, and [call-only campaigns](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns) are a separate format.

### 3. Sitelink assets: links to the pages people want

Google's [sitelink help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375416) gives the numbers:

- You need at least 2 sitelinks for them to show on Search; Google recommends at least 6, with descriptions, for high-volume campaigns.
- Sitelink text is limited to 25 characters in most languages.
- Up to 6 can show on desktop and up to 8 on mobile.

Add six for a typical local business: Book Now, Prices, Services, Reviews, Opening Hours, Contact. Each should land on a page that matches its label and loads fast on a phone. A sitelink called "Prices" that lands on the home page wastes a click.

### 4. Callout assets: short facts that remove doubt

Per Google's [callout help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6079510), callouts are limited to 25 characters in most languages and up to 10 can appear with an ad, depending on spacing, browser and device. Google advises concrete wording over vague claims, and shorter callouts so more fit.

Good: `Free Parking`, `Same-Day Appointments`, `Walk-Ins Welcome`, `Open Till 9pm`, `Dog-Safe Products`. Weak: `Great Service`, `Quality You Can Trust`.

### 5. Price assets: a scrollable menu under the ad

The [price asset help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065415) says you need a minimum of 3 items (5 or more recommended), a set shows up to 8 cards, and headers and descriptions are limited to 25 characters. You can add them at account, campaign or ad group level and edit them without resetting performance stats. Billing: a click on a price item costs the same as a headline click, and you are not charged for more than two clicks per impression.

Google's own example is a hair salon with broad items like "Haircut" and "Treatments" for general campaigns, and "Partial Color" and "Full Color" in a colour-specific ad group. Copy that pattern. Google's [price asset requirements](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7048464) bar price information and promo words like "Sale" from the header and description (the price goes in its own field), and say prices must be accurate and easy to find at the destination. Use "From $65" only if your site says the same.

### 6. Promotion assets: a time-boxed offer

Promotion assets show up to two lines of offer text. Google's [promotion help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7367521) says you choose a monetary or percentage discount, add optional dates and an occasion label, and that occasion-specific assets must be created or edited within 6 months of their start date or they stop serving. The asset is free; clicks cost the headline rate. Run one only for a real offer that your landing page also states, and put an end date on it so you never advertise an expired deal.

## Assets Worth Adding Later

- **Structured snippets** list options under a header, such as Services or Types. Google's help and API reference give value-count and length limits, but we could not open them to confirm the numbers, so check the limits shown in your own account when you create one.
- **Lead form assets** let people enquire inside Google. Google's [lead form help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9423234) requires a privacy policy link, a conversion-focused bidding strategy, and notes that Google Ads keeps leads for 60 days while manual downloads cover 30. If you cannot respond within minutes, skip them.
- **Image assets** work only if you have clean, real photos. Skip stock.

## The Math

Does an hour of asset work pay for itself? Here is a worked example. Everything below is an **assumption** for illustration, not a benchmark, because Google publishes no uplift figure for assets in the pages above. Replace with your own numbers.

| | Before assets | Modest gain | Stronger gain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $2.50 | $2.50 | $2.50 |
| Clicks | 240 | 240 | 240 |
| Click-to-enquiry rate (assumption) | 8% | 8.8% | 9.6% |
| Enquiries | 19.2 | 21.1 | 23.0 |
| Cost per enquiry | $31.25 | $28.41 | $26.04 |
| Value of one enquiry (assumption) | $45 | $45 | $45 |
| Extra enquiries per month | n/a | 1.9 | 3.8 |
| Extra value per month | n/a | $86 | $171 |

The "modest" column assumes assets lift enquiries by 10% and the "stronger" one by 20%. These are guesses to show the shape of the decision, not predictions. Even the modest case pays for an hour of your time within one month, and nothing in it assumes higher spend. The honest test is your own account: record enquiries for four weeks before and after, and compare.

One more check on a different axis. If staff miss 3 of every 10 calls, a call asset sends you paying clicks that go nowhere. Use call scheduling to match your answering hours before anything else.

## Check That They Are Working

In the Assets report, look at impressions and clicks per asset, then use the segment for click type to see how many clicks came from sitelinks, calls or directions versus the headline. Rewrite assets with almost no impressions after four weeks (a rule of thumb, not a Google threshold). Pair this with solid [conversion tracking](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) so you count enquiries, not just clicks. ## Do This Today

1. Open your Business Profile and fix address, hours and phone, then link it in Location Manager so your location asset reads accurate data.
2. Add a call asset with your real answering hours, and turn on call reporting.
3. Write six sitelinks of 25 characters or fewer, each pointing to a mobile-friendly page that matches its label.
4. Add five callouts that state concrete facts (hours, parking, same-day, payment options).
5. If you have a price list, add at least 3 price items; if you have a live offer, add a promotion with an end date. Put a calendar reminder four weeks out to review the Assets report.

## FAQ

**Q: Are Google Ads extensions the same as assets?**
A: Yes. Google renamed ad extensions to assets, and the interface and help pages now use "assets". The features are the same.

**Q: Do assets cost extra?**
A: Creating them is free. Per Google's help, clicks on call, price and promotion assets are charged at the same rate as a headline click, and for price and promotion assets you are not charged for more than two clicks per impression.

**Q: Why are my assets not showing?**
A: Google says assets show only when they are predicted to improve performance and your ad's position and Ad Rank are high enough. Also check for disapprovals, an unverified phone number, a closed location in your Business Profile, and too few assets (sitelinks need at least 2).

**Q: How many sitelinks and callouts should I add?**
A: Sitelinks: at least 2 are required, 6 recommended by Google for busy campaigns. Callouts: Google gives no minimum, but up to 10 can show, so write five or six.

**Q: Should I use lead form assets?**
A: Only if you will answer within minutes and can export or route leads. Google stores them for 60 days, so download or connect them to your CRM regularly.
