Google Ads extensions, now called assets, are extra pieces of information attached to your ads, such as your phone number, address, extra links, offers and key selling points. For local businesses the most useful are location, call, sitelink, callout and price assets, because they make an ad larger, give people more ways to act, and often improve click-through rate compared with a bare text ad.

This guide explains each asset type, when to use it, how to set it up and how to check it is working.

## Extensions are now "assets"

Google renamed ad extensions to "assets" and manages them from the Assets section in your Google Ads account. The idea is the same: you create the asset once, then Google chooses when to show it with your ads. Assets can be set at account, campaign or ad group level, and lower levels override higher ones.

Google does not guarantee that every asset will show with every ad. It tends to show them when they are expected to improve performance and when your ad position and rank allow. Higher quality ads and bids give assets more chances to appear. See our [Quality Score guide](/blog/google-ads-quality-score-guide-lower-cpc) for what influences this.

## The assets local businesses should set up first

### Location asset

Connects your Google Business Profile to your ads so the address, map pin and "Get directions" show. This is essential for any business that people visit. Link your accounts in Google Ads under Assets, then select the locations. It can also trigger the ad on Maps.

### Call asset

Adds a clickable phone number, which on mobile starts a call. Choose a number that reaches someone, set call hours to match when you answer, and consider call reporting to see how many calls come from ads. If you rely on calls, see our guide to [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) and [call-only campaigns](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns).

### Sitelink assets

Extra links below your ad to specific pages such as "Book online", "Pricing", "Services", "Reviews" or "Contact". Create at least four, with short titles and optional two-line descriptions. Make sure each link goes to a page that matches its title and works on mobile.

### Callout assets

Short phrases up to 25 characters, such as "Free parking", "Same-day appointments", "Family owned", "Locally roasted". They are not clickable. Use them for facts that reduce hesitation.

### Structured snippet assets

A header plus a list of values, for example "Services: Haircuts, Colour, Styling, Treatments" or "Types: Yoga, Pilates, Strength". Useful if you offer several options.

### Price assets

Show service names and "from" prices. Handy for salons, groomers and studios with clear price lists. Keep them updated, because mismatches between ad and landing page can disappoint users and may breach policies.

### Promotion assets

Highlight an offer such as "20% off first visit" with start and end dates. Use only when the offer is real and clearly stated on your site.

### Lead form assets

Let people submit their details inside Google Search, without visiting your site. They can suit service businesses that follow up quickly by phone, but you must handle leads promptly and respect privacy policies.

### Image assets

Images can show alongside text ads in some placements. Use clear, real photos that fit your business and meet Google's image guidelines.

## How to set them up

1. In Google Ads, open **Assets** from the left menu (the exact menu layout can change).
2. Click the plus button and choose the asset type.
3. Select the level: account for general assets, campaign or ad group for specific ones.
4. Enter text, links and details. Check character limits.
5. Save, then wait for Google's review. Rejected assets show a reason you can fix.
6. Review performance after a few weeks by opening the Assets report and sorting by clicks, impressions and conversions.

## Writing better assets

- **Be specific:** "Cut and colour from $65" beats "Great prices."
- **Match the landing page:** if the sitelink says "Pricing", the page should show pricing.
- **Keep it honest:** avoid claims such as "best in town" unless you can support them. Google may disapprove unsupported superlatives.
- **Use variety:** aim for four or more sitelinks and callouts so Google can rotate combinations.
- **Check spelling and capitalisation.** Policies prohibit gimmicky formatting.

## Check that assets are working

Use these reports:

- **Assets table** to compare performance of each asset by clicks, impressions, CTR and conversions.
- **Segments by click type** to see how clicks on sitelinks, calls or directions compare to headline clicks.
- **Call details** to see call duration and whether calls came from the ad.
- **Google Business Profile insights** to track direction requests from linked locations.

Replace assets that rarely appear or perform poorly. Keep those that show frequently and lead to conversions.

## Common mistakes

- Not linking your Google Business Profile, so no address or directions show.
- Using the same sitelinks across every campaign, even where they do not fit.
- Leaving old promotions running after they expire.
- Sending sitelinks to pages that are slow or broken on mobile.
- Forgetting call assets while staff are unavailable, causing missed calls.
- Writing assets that repeat the headline instead of adding information.

## A setup checklist

- Link your Business Profile and add a location asset.
- Add a call asset with accurate call hours.
- Create four to six sitelinks to real, mobile-friendly pages.
- Add four to six callouts that state concrete facts.
- Add a price or promotion asset only if you can keep it current.
- Review the Assets report after a few weeks and replace weak performers.

Working through this list once usually takes less than an hour.

## FAQ

**Q: Are Google Ads extensions free?**
A: Creating them costs nothing. You pay the normal cost per click when someone clicks the ad or an asset, and calls from call assets are billed according to your campaign settings.

**Q: Why are my assets not showing?**
A: Common reasons include low ad rank, policy disapprovals, too few assets, daily budgets running out, or Google judging that they would not improve performance. Check statuses in the Assets report.

**Q: How many sitelinks should I add?**
A: Add at least four, and consider six or more so Google can choose combinations. Quality matters more than quantity.

**Q: Do assets change my Quality Score?**
A: Google treats expected impact of assets as part of ad relevance and rank. They do not directly guarantee a higher score, but better assets usually support better performance.
