Instagram Stories ads work for a fitness studio when they promote one low-risk offer, such as a free first class, to people who live or work near the studio. Instagram is one of the most widely used platforms among US adults, so the audience is there. Whether the ads pay off depends on how many leads attend a class and join, not on the lead count alone. Start with a small test, track attendance from the first week, and compare cost per new member against your own margins.

## Do Instagram Stories ads work for fitness studios?

They can, mainly because they reach people who are not yet searching for a class. Search ads capture existing demand, while Stories ads create interest with an offer. [Pew Research Center's 2026 survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) found that 51% of US adults use Instagram, up from 50% in 2025. Facebook reached 70% of adults, down from 71%. Those are national figures, so the reach in your own postcode will be smaller.

Cost is the harder question. Benchmark data varies by year and source. A [WordStream industry table](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks) lists Health and Medical search cost per click at $2.62, but that page was first published in 2016 and only updated later, so treat it as directional. Run your own numbers from your first campaign before you set a budget you cannot sustain.

## Which platforms reach the most US adults?

The chart above shows the reach of four platforms. YouTube leads at 84%, and Instagram is ahead of TikTok at 51% against 37%. Nextdoor, a neighborhood-focused platform, grew from 13% in 2021 to 18% in 2026, which matters for a studio that draws members from a few streets.

For a Stories campaign, Instagram is the natural starting point because the ad format is built around vertical, full-screen video. Facebook can carry the same creative with a different audience age profile. Test one platform at a time so you know which one produced the members.

## Stories ads or Google search: which comes first?

The two channels do different jobs, so the order matters more than the choice between them.

| Question | Google search ads | Instagram Stories ads |
|---|---|---|
| Who sees it? | People typing "yoga near me" | People in your target radius, whether or not they are looking |
| Main job | Capture existing demand | Create demand with an offer |
| Benchmark cost | Health and Medical search CPC of $2.62 (WordStream, dated) | No platform benchmark used here; measure your own |
| What you need | Landing page, call tracking, Business Profile | Vertical video, one offer, booking link |

A practical order: if your Google Business Profile is incomplete or has few reviews, fix that first. Then run Stories ads for a first-class offer while search ads catch people ready to book. ## What does $600 a month produce?

This worked example uses assumed inputs, not results from a real studio. Every input is labelled.

- Monthly budget: $600 (assumption)
- Cost per lead: $30 (assumption, set this from your own test)
- Leads: $600 / $30 = 20
- Leads who attend a first class: 40%, about 8 (assumption)
- Attendees who join: 40%, about 3 (assumption)
- Membership price: $99 a month, kept for 6 months (assumption)
- Revenue from three new members: about $1,782 (assumption-based)
- Ad cost per new member: $600 / 3 = $200 (assumption-based)

If the join rate is half the assumed rate, the cost per member doubles to about $400 and the campaign may barely cover its costs. This is why attendance and joins matter more than leads.

Track these numbers from week one:

| Metric | Where to find it | Check frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per lead | Ads Manager | Weekly |
| Leads who book a class | Booking software | Weekly |
| Leads who attend | Front desk check-in | Weekly |
| New members from the ad | UTM link plus "how did you hear?" question | Weekly |
| Cost per new member | Spreadsheet: spend divided by members | End of test |

## What should the ad say?

Keep the ad focused on one decision. A single offer such as "free first class" is easier to act on than a general invitation to visit. Name the neighborhood in the first line so local viewers recognize it.

Use a short vertical clip of a real class, shot on a phone, with captions. Many viewers watch without sound. Stories are full-screen and vertical, so keep captions and the logo away from the edges where buttons and profile icons appear. Set the call-to-action button in Ads Manager to match the booking page. Make a second version only after the first has run long enough to read.

## How do you set up a Stories ad in five steps?

1. Connect your Instagram account and Facebook Page in Meta Business Suite, then install the Meta pixel on your booking page.
2. Choose a leads or sales objective. Avoid "boost post", because it gives less control over who sees the ad.
3. Target a radius around the studio. Start small, around 3 miles in a city, as a working assumption, and widen only if leads are thin.
4. Select Instagram Stories as the placement and upload one vertical video.
5. Set a daily budget and leave the ad alone for the test period. Changing it often makes results hard to read.

Meta's guidance is to test with one variable changed at a time and with an even budget split. Its help page warns that informal on-and-off testing creates overlapping audiences and unreliable results, so use Meta's A/B test tool when you want to compare two versions ([Meta, About A/B testing](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669)). ## FAQ

### What is the minimum budget for a Stories ad test?

No official minimum guarantees results. A practical test is a budget that produces enough leads to compare, so divide the number of leads you want by your expected cost per lead.

### How long should an ad run before you judge it?

Run it long enough to collect leads and attendance data. A working assumption is 7 to 14 days. Changing the ad every day makes the results hard to read.

### Should I use TikTok instead?

For a studio that needs members within a few miles, Instagram is simpler to target locally. Test TikTok after Instagram is producing members, not before.

### How do I know a member came from the ad?

Use UTM-tagged links on every ad and ask each new member how they heard about you. Record both in a spreadsheet next to the ad spend.
