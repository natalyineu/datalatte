## What can a booking and FAQ chatbot actually do?

A chatbot for a small business does three jobs well. It answers fixed-answer questions, collects details for a booking, and routes anything unusual to you.

- **FAQs:** opening hours, parking, price ranges, cancellation policy, services list.
- **Booking:** shows a link or collects name, service and preferred time, then passes it to your scheduling tool.
- **Handoff:** sends complex or upset customers to a human with the conversation attached.

It should not negotiate prices, give medical or legal advice, or promise anything that is not written in its knowledge base. Our [guide to AI agents vs chatbots](/blog/ai-agent-vs-chatbot-whats-the-difference-local-business) explains when you need more than a scripted bot.

## Worked example: does a $49 chatbot pay for itself?

This is an assumption, not a result. Say a nail studio gets 120 chat enquiries a month, answers 70% of them late or not at all, and each booked visit is worth $60.

1. If the bot recovers just 10 extra bookings a month, that is $600 in revenue.
2. A Tidio Growth plan at $49.17 plus a Lyro add-on at $32.50 costs about $82 a month.
3. Break-even is two extra bookings a month at $60 each, so the plan pays back if it recovers three or more.

Replace the three assumptions with your own numbers (enquiries, share missed, value per visit) before you buy.

## What do customers think of chatbots?

Do not assume customers love bots. Zendesk's 2019 Customer Experience Trends study, [as summarised on the Zendesk blog](https://www.zendesk.com/au/blog/ai/consumer-contradictions-mean-ai-opportunities-cx/), found nearly three-quarters of customers preferred to talk to a human agent. That is a 2019 figure from a company that sells support software, so treat it as a warning about handoffs, not a current benchmark.

The practical lesson is to put a visible "talk to a person" option in every flow and make sure someone answers during opening hours.

## How do I set up a chatbot in four steps?

1. **Write the 10 questions** customers ask most, with the exact answers (pull them from DMs, calls and Google reviews).
2. **Pick the channel** where your customers already message you: website chat, Instagram or Facebook Messenger.
3. **Connect booking.** Ask the vendor whether the bot books directly or only sends a link. Test with a real booking.
4. **Review transcripts weekly** for the first month and fix wrong answers, especially holiday hours and prices.

Pair the bot with the right follow-ups: appointment reminders cut no-shows, and a [hair salon chatbot setup](/blog/ai-powered-chatbots-for-hair-salons) shows the same steps for one niche.

## Which chatbot should I choose?

- **Fewer than 50 chats a month:** use a free plan or a plain FAQ page with a booking link.
- **Mostly Instagram or Facebook DMs:** choose a social-messaging tool such as Chatfuel or ManyChat.
- **Mostly website visitors:** choose a website chat tool such as Tidio.
- **Complex jobs (quotes, multi-step bookings):** you likely need an AI agent, not a scripted bot. See [AI agent for appointment booking](/blog/ai-agent-for-appointment-booking-local-business).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a chatbot make my business feel impersonal?

Only if it replaces people instead of supporting them. Use your own wording, keep answers short, and always offer a human handoff.

### What if the chatbot gives wrong information?

It can. Keep the knowledge base short, update it for holidays and price changes, and read transcripts weekly. Limit the bot to facts you have written down.

### Can it connect to my booking software?

Check each vendor's integration list. Some tools only send a booking link, while others can check availability. Test one real booking before paying for a year.

### Will it replace my staff?

For most small shops it removes repetitive questions rather than jobs. Staff time shifts to complex enquiries and in-person service.

### How do I know it is working?

Track bookings per week, average first-response time and revenue from online bookings for two weeks before and two weeks after launch.
