Take 100 new grooming clients. If 40 of them rebook and settle into a routine, and each routine client spends $75 five more times that year, that group is worth $15,000. If 70 rebook, it is worth $26,250. Same ads, same Google listing, same 100 people. Those inputs are my assumptions, not benchmarks, and we will lay them out in the math below. The point is the order of work: for a groomer, keeping the dog on your calendar is worth more than finding another dog.

This is the overview plan for groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It puts the pieces in order of payoff and points to the detailed guides on each, so you can start this week instead of reading ten articles first.

## How Pet Owners Pick a Groomer

Owners are protective, and the decision usually runs like this:

1. They search on a phone: "dog groomer near me", "mobile cat grooming", "puppy first groom".
2. They compare the top few Google Maps results by rating, review count, photos and distance.
3. They look for signs you handle nervous or older pets well.
4. They book with whoever answers first, by phone, message or form.

You are in a big market. The American Pet Products Association's [industry statistics page](https://www.americanpetproducts.org/industry-trends-and-stats) reports $158 billion spent on pets in the US in 2024 and 95 million US households owning a pet (APPA's 2025 National Pet Owners Survey). The page does not break out grooming alone; it falls inside a $14.3 billion "other services" category that also includes insurance, training, sitting and walking. So the demand is there, but treat any "grooming market size" figure with caution unless it names its source.

## The Order of Work

Do these in sequence. Each step feeds the next.

### 1. Make the booking path work first (week 1)

Before you spend a cent on marketing, answer one question honestly: if a new owner finds you at 9pm, what happens next? They need a working phone number or message option, a booking link or form, and a reply within hours. Everything else on this page sends people to that path, so fix it first. Our [AI scheduling guide for pet groomers](/blog/ai-scheduling-for-pet-groomers) covers tools that can book and remind without you at the desk.

### 2. Claim and complete your Google profile (weeks 1 to 2)

For most groomers the Google Business Profile is the single most valuable free asset, because it decides whether you appear in the map results. Complete it fully: the right primary category, each service with a short description, real photos of the salon and (with owner permission) before-and-after shots, accurate hours, a booking link. We walk through every field, with Google's own rules, in [Google Business Profile for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers). Do not duplicate that work here; do it once and carefully.

### 3. Build a small website that answers questions (weeks 2 to 4)

You need very few pages: home, services and prices, about, contact, a FAQ for vaccination rules, how long a groom takes and your cancellation policy. Keep your name, address and phone number identical to your Google profile. Our [pet groomer website pages and copy guide](/blog/pet-groomer-website-pages-and-copy-guide) gives the six pages and sample wording, and [local SEO for pet groomers](/blog/local-seo-for-pet-groomers) goes deeper on keywords and citations.

### 4. Ask for reviews every day (ongoing)

Reviews are the main trust signal in a pet service. Google's [help page on getting reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) tells businesses they can ask customers to review, share a review link or QR code, and reply to reviews, and says reviews must reflect a genuine experience. It also states that offering incentives such as free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews is "strictly prohibited". Google's [Maps user-generated content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) goes further: merchants may not discourage negative reviews or selectively solicit positive ones. So ask everyone, not just the happy owners, and no "10% off for a five-star review". Our guide on [how to ask pet grooming clients for Google reviews](/blog/how-to-ask-pet-grooming-clients-for-google-reviews) has wording and timing.

Reply to every review. For a bad one, Google's advice is to keep it professional, brief and personal, avoid sharing private details, and move complex issues offline.

### 5. Run a rebooking routine (from week 3)

This is where the money is, and it costs almost nothing:

- **Rebook at pick-up.** Suggest the next date before they leave, based on the coat. "Poppy's coat will want a trim around the 8th of next month. Shall I pencil in the Thursday?"
- **Remind 48 hours before** by text or email through your booking software.
- **Nudge lapsed clients.** If someone has not returned within their usual interval plus a couple of weeks, send one friendly message with a booking link.
- **Keep a profile per pet.** Temperament, preferred cut, allergies and health notes make visits personal and consistent.

Your own appointment book will tell you the typical gap between visits for each breed, which is a better guide than any national figure. Consent matters: in the US, the FTC's [CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) says opt-out requests must be honoured within 10 business days, messages must include a valid physical postal address, and each violating email can carry penalties of up to $53,088 (the page says that figure was updated in January 2024). That law covers commercial email; text-message rules are separate, and the UK, Australia and Canada have their own, so check your local regulator before you automate texts. Our [email marketing for pet groomers](/blog/email-marketing-for-pet-groomers) guide has templates and timings.

### 7. Use AI for admin, not for your reputation (anytime)

AI tools are good at first drafts and repetitive questions: social captions, email subject lines, review-reply drafts, an after-hours auto-reply with your hours and prices. Keep these rules: never let a tool invent prices, credentials or promises; never use generated images as stand-ins for real grooming results; and have a human read any message about a pet's health or behaviour. A free assistant is enough to begin.

## Track Six Numbers Monthly

Write these in a simple spreadsheet:

- New enquiries and where each came from (ask every new client).
- Bookings and no-shows.
- Percentage of first-time clients who rebook.
- Google profile calls, direction requests and website clicks.
- Review count and average rating.
- Ad spend per new client, if you run ads.

Trends over a few months are far more useful than any single week. The [pet groomer analytics tracking guide](/blog/pet-groomer-analytics-tracking) shows how to set up the counting.

## The Math

Everything here is an **assumption** for illustration. Replace with your own price, visit frequency and rebook rate from your booking system.

| Per 100 new clients | Weak rebooking | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Share who become routine clients (assumption) | 40% | 55% | 70% |
| Routine clients | 40 | 55 | 70 |
| Further visits per routine client in the year (assumption) | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Average ticket (assumption) | $75 | $75 | $75 |
| Revenue from routine clients over the year | $15,000 | $20,625 | $26,250 |
| Difference vs Weak | n/a | +$5,625 | +$11,250 |

This counts only revenue after the first visit, and ignores your costs and any pets that leave for reasons you cannot control. Two readings:

- **Fifteen more routine clients is worth about $5,625 a year here.** Buying 15 extra new clients through ads at an assumed $50 each would cost $750, but those clients also have to rebook, so the ads only work if the rebooking works. A text message costs far less.
- **Capacity caps the upside.** If your book is nearly full, extra clients mean a waiting list, not more revenue. Use the same table with your real open slots to see whether you need new clients at all or just fewer gaps.

## Do This Today

1. Pull the last 100 first-time clients from your booking system and count how many came back within twice their usual interval. That is your real rebook rate.
2. Test your booking path from a phone at night: can a stranger find a number, a form and a reply promise in 30 seconds?
3. Open your Google profile and fix the three most obviously incomplete things, using the profile guide.
4. Start saying the next appointment date out loud at every pick-up, and add a 48-hour reminder in your booking tool.
5. Put a QR code for your review link at the counter, and ask every client, not only the happy ones.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best marketing for a pet groomer?**
A: For most groomers the order is a working booking path, a complete Google profile, steady reviews and a rebooking routine, then paid ads. The Math table above shows why keeping clients can matter more than winning new ones.

**Q: How much should a pet groomer spend on marketing?**

**Q: Do I need a website?**
A: A simple one helps. It answers questions, supports local search and gives your profile a destination. See the website pages guide for the six pages that matter.

**Q: Can I give a discount for a Google review?**
A: No. Google's help page says incentives such as free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews are strictly prohibited.

**Q: Can AI answer customer messages for me?**
A: For simple, repeated questions, yes. Keep a clear path to a human for health, safety and complaints.
