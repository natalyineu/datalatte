A personal trainer website needs five things: a clear offer on the first screen, a short services page with prices or a starting price, proof from real clients, a booking or consultation form that works on a phone, and a page that loads fast. The tools to do it cost roughly $16 to $30 a month at the published 2026 prices below.

This guide is for solo trainers and small studios. Prices were checked on 31 October 2026 on the vendors' own pages. Where a number is an example, it is labelled as an assumption.

## Which Pages Does a Personal Trainer Website Need?

Start with a short list and build it well. A trainer with one location can cover everything on five pages.

| Page | What it must answer | Main button |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Who you train, where, and the result you aim for | Book a free consultation |
| Services | Formats (1:1, small group, online), session length, price or "from" price | Book or enquire |
| About | Qualifications, training style, who you work best with | Book a consultation |
| Results or reviews | Real client quotes with permission | Book a consultation |
| Contact and booking | Location, hours, form or calendar | Submit or book |

Keep the same button on every page. A visitor who has to hunt for the next step usually leaves. Our guide on [pricing pages for local businesses](/blog/website-pricing-page-local-business) covers how to show prices without scaring people off, and [testimonials pages](/blog/website-testimonials-page-local-business) covers proof.

## What Does Online Booking Cost?

Most website builders do not include appointment software in the base plan. On Squarespace, the pricing page lists Basic at $19 a month on annual billing and $25 monthly, with Core at $29 and $39. The page does not say scheduling is included in those plans; Acuity Scheduling is sold separately by the same company.

Wix lists scheduling and services on its Core plan at $29.77 a month on a yearly plan, and the page notes USD prices are for reference only. Acuity's own pricing page shows Starter at $16 a month billed annually ($20 monthly) for one calendar, with Standard at $27 for up to six calendars and text reminders.

| Option | Price (checked 31 Oct 2026) | Booking included? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squarespace Basic | $19/mo annual, $25/mo monthly | Not listed on the plan comparison |
| Wix Core | $29.77/mo yearly plan | Yes, listed as scheduling and services |
| Acuity Starter | $16/mo annual, $20/mo monthly | Yes, it is a scheduling tool |
| Acuity Standard | $27/mo annual, $34/mo monthly | Yes, adds text reminders and memberships |

Trainerize, a coaching app, shows different prices in its FAQ and in its comparison table, so this guide does not quote them. Check the current page before you decide.

## How Fast Should the Site Load?

Google publishes target thresholds for Core Web Vitals, measured at the 75th percentile of page loads: Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint of 200 milliseconds or less, and Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less. Photos are the usual cause of slow trainer sites, so resize before uploading.

Google also indexes the phone version of your site. Its documentation says the mobile site should contain the same content as the desktop site. Anything hidden on mobile may not count. For a deeper checklist, read our [mobile-first design guide](/blog/mobile-optimization-local-business) and [speed optimization guide](/blog/website-speed-optimization-small-business).

## What Should Your Services and Pricing Say?

Say what a session includes, how long it lasts, where it happens, and what the first step is. If you do not want to publish a full rate card, a "from" price still filters out people who cannot afford you and saves you intro calls.

Do not copy a competitor's package names. Describe the outcome in plain words: "strength training for beginners over 40" tells a visitor more than "Transformation Package." No source gives a reliable conversion lift for publishing prices, so treat this as a test: publish a range for a month and compare enquiries.

## Do You Need Email on Your Site?

If you collect emails for a newsletter or a free plan, US law applies. The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide requires accurate sender details, honest subject lines, a clear ad disclosure, a valid postal address and a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days. The FTC lists penalties of up to $53,088 per violating email. If you serve UK or EU clients, GDPR consent rules also apply; check the consent rules before you email them.

## Worked Example: What Is a Site Worth to You?

All inputs below are **assumptions**, not benchmarks. Replace them with your own numbers.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Site and booking tools | Squarespace Basic $19 + Acuity Starter $16 = $35 a month, $420 a year |
| Extra clients booked through the site | 1 a month |
| Client lifetime | 6 months |
| Revenue per client per month | $240 (one session a week at $60) |

One extra client a month adds $1,440 over six months of training per client, so twelve a year bring about $17,280 in gross revenue against $420 in tools. Even if only one in four of those clients is truly caused by the site, the tools pay back. The real constraint is your calendar, not the cost.

## FAQ

### Do I need a website if I already get clients from Instagram?

A site gives you a page that you control and that Google can index. Social profiles can be useful, but you do not control their rules or reach. No source gives a share of trainers who lose clients without a site, so treat it as risk reduction.

### How much does a trainer website cost to run?

Using the prices above, a Squarespace Basic site with Acuity Starter is $35 a month on annual billing. Wix Core at $29.77 a month includes scheduling. Domain, photos and your time are extra.

### Should I list my prices?

At minimum, show a starting price. It is a judgement call and there is no published benchmark, so test it.

### What matters most on the About page?

Qualifications, your approach, and the type of client you work with. Our [marketing strategy for personal trainers](/blog/marketing-strategy-for-personal-trainers) covers how this fits with your wider marketing.

### How do I get people to find the site?

Local search and a Business Profile do most of the work; ads can fill gaps. See our [Google Ads guide for personal trainers](/blog/google-ads-for-personal-trainers) for cost data.
