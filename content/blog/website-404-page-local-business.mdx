A 404 page for a local business should do three things: confirm the page is missing, send the visitor to a service page, hours, or a phone number, and keep the visit from ending there. Start with a plain message, add a short set of working links, place the main contact action near the top, and test the page on a phone. Most people who hit a broken URL were already looking for your business, so the page should make the next step obvious.

## Why does a 404 page matter for a local business?

A 404 page appears when someone requests a URL that does not exist. For a local business, the usual causes are old links from directory listings, retired service pages, typos in printed materials, and QR codes that point to pages you later moved. A generic error screen gives the visitor nowhere to go, and that visitor is often someone who was ready to call or visit.

Visitors spend most of their attention on the top of a screen. Nielsen Norman Group's eye-tracking research, as summarised on [Adwave's hero section guide](https://adwave.com/resources/website-hero-section-design), found that users spent 80.3% of their viewing time above the page fold (as of 2010). Put your message and your links in that top area, not in a footer. The same principle is covered in the [above-the-fold design guide for local businesses](/blog/above-the-fold-design-local-business).

## What should a local business 404 page say and link to?

Keep the headline short and plain. "This page has moved" or "We could not find that page" works better than a joke, because the visitor needs to act, not admire the copy. Then offer three to five links that match what local customers actually need: your core services, opening hours, your location, a phone number or contact form, and the booking page if you take bookings.

Avoid linking to a long list of blog posts or your whole sitemap. A short menu of high-intent pages is easier to scan on a phone. If you run a promotion, you can add one line about it, but only if the offer is current and the link works.

Write the copy in the same plain style you use on your main pages. The guide on [how to write website copy for local businesses](/blog/how-to-write-website-copy-local-business-examples) covers tone and clarity, and the same rules apply to error messages.

## How should a 404 page work on a phone?

Google uses the mobile version of a page's content, crawled with a smartphone user agent, for indexing and ranking, according to Google's mobile-first guidance as cited on [Adwave's mobile-first design page](https://adwave.com/resources/mobile-first-website-design) (secondary). Your 404 page is part of the site a phone user experiences, so build it for the phone first.

Use these checks for the mobile version:

1. Design the layout at 320 to 390 pixels wide first, then scale up.
2. Make every button at least 9 mm by 9 mm so it can be tapped without errors.
3. Keep the page light enough to render in under one second on a mobile network, which Google's guidance recommends evaluating.
4. If you add a search box or contact form, show each label above its input in portrait orientation.
5. Confirm the phone number is a tap-to-call link.
6. Check that the page loads without a horizontal scroll bar.

The [local business website checklist](/blog/local-business-website-checklist) covers the rest of the mobile and launch review.

## How much revenue can a 404 page recover?

A worked example helps you decide whether the time is worth spending. The inputs below are assumptions. Replace them with your own analytics numbers before you rely on the result.

1. Visits to broken URLs per month: 1,000 (assumption).
2. Share of those visitors who click a working link on the custom 404 page and reach a service page: 10% (assumption).
3. Share of those service-page visitors who book or buy: 2% (assumption).
4. Average sale value: $80 (assumption).

Calculation: 1,000 x 10% = 100 recovered visitors. 100 x 2% = 2 sales. 2 x $80 = $160 per month, or $1,920 per year. If your broken-link traffic is lower, the result is smaller, but the setup cost is usually a few hours of work on a template you already have. Track the same numbers in your analytics so you can replace the assumptions with real data.

## Which 404 page changes should you test first?

Test one change at a time. A common starting point is the headline and the first link, because those sit in the attention zone described above. Changing the copy, the button order, and the layout at once makes it impossible to tell which change helped.

HubSpot's 2024 marketing report, as cited by [Universal Mailworks](https://www.universalmailworks.com/blog/door-hanger-marketing-design-costs-distribution) (secondary), found companies that regularly A/B test their marketing materials see an average of 20-30% higher conversion rates than those that do not. Treat that as a reason to test, not as a promise for your page. The [A/B testing guide for local businesses](/blog/a-b-testing-for-local-business) explains how to run a simple test with a small site's traffic.

## Should a 404 page redirect to the homepage?

Redirect a broken URL to the closest matching page when one exists. A retired plumbing service page should point to the current plumbing page, not to the homepage, because the visitor wanted a specific answer. Google's SEO Starter Guide states that duplicate content is not a violation of its spam policies, but it can create a poor user experience and waste crawling resources, so avoid creating many near-identical pages just to catch errors. Use the custom 404 page for everything that has no good match.

## FAQ

### Do I need a custom 404 page if my business has only a few pages?

A small site still gets broken links from directories, social profiles, and printed QR codes. A custom page with links to your main services and contact details is a low-effort way to catch those visits, even if the site has only five or six pages.

### Should a 404 page show a search bar?

A search bar can help on larger sites with many product or service pages. For a small local business, direct links to your core services, hours, and contact options usually do more work. If you add a search box, keep its label above the input and make sure the button is large enough to tap.

### How do I find which URLs return 404 errors?

Check the current help pages for Google Search Console and your website platform, since the reporting screens change over time. Review the error list monthly, and again after any site redesign, page deletion, or URL change.

### Does a 404 page affect ad performance?

If your paid ads send people to a URL that no longer exists, those clicks are wasted. Check ad destination URLs whenever you change a page.
