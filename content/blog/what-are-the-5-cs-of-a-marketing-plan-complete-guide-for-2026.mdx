The 5 C's of a marketing plan are Company, Competitors, Customers, Collaborators and Climate. It is a situation analysis: you study your own business, the rivals, the buyers, the partners and the wider environment before choosing what to promote and where. Some guides swap Climate for Context or Capabilities, so check which version a template uses.

This guide is for owners of cafes, salons, studios and trades businesses who need a one-page plan. It explains each C with questions you can answer in an afternoon, then runs one worked example. Definitions come from the sources listed at the end. The example figures are assumptions.

## What Are the 5 C's of Marketing?

Wikipedia's entry on situation analysis describes the 5C analysis this way. Company covers your objectives, strategy and capabilities. Competitors covers rivals' goals, strategies and resources. Customers covers who buys and why, including demographics, needs and how often they buy. Collaborators covers outside partners such as agencies, suppliers and distributors. Climate covers the wider environment, also known as PEST analysis (political, economic, social, technological).

The framework describes the starting point. It does not give you a budget or a channel. The SBA says there is no single way to approach a marketing strategy, and that it should change over time to fit your needs.

## How Do You Analyse Your Company?

List what you sell, what you do better than nearby rivals and what you do badly. Be specific and verifiable: opening hours, prices, specialities, review score and number of reviews on Google. Add your constraints, such as staff hours and budget.

Look at your own data first: Google Business Profile insights, point-of-sale reports, booking records. These tell you which products and times carry the business. Then set a goal with a number and a date, such as new customers per month. For budgeting, see our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).

## Who Are Your Competitors?

The SBA says to identify competitors by product line or service and market segment, then assess market share, strengths and weaknesses, barriers to your entry, and indirect or secondary competitors. For a local shop, start with the three businesses that appear next to yours in a Google Maps search for your main service.

For each, note price range, hours, review count and score, how recently they post, and what their website offers. You do not need rival revenue. The gap you look for is simple: a service they lack, a time they are closed, a complaint that recurs in their reviews. ## What Do Your Customers Want?

The SBA lists questions to answer: is there demand, how many people might be interested, what is the income range and employment rate nearby, where customers live, how many similar options exist and what customers pay for them. It suggests free federal data for general numbers and surveys, interviews or focus groups for specific insight.

For a small business, a five-question survey at the till or in the booking confirmation is enough. Ask what brought them in, what they nearly chose instead, and how often they come. Write three short customer descriptions based on the answers, not on guesses.

## Who Are Your Collaborators, and What Is the Climate?

Collaborators are the outside parties that help you reach customers: a supplier who co-promotes, a neighbouring business, a delivery app, an agency. Note what each costs you and what each gives. A dependency on one platform is a risk, so write it down.

Climate is the outside environment. For a local business that usually means local economy and rent, rules that affect hours or signage, shifts in what people buy, and technology such as new search features. Pick the two or three items that could change your next six months and plan a response.

## A Worked Example: A Coffee Shop Plan on One Page

All details below are **assumptions** for illustration, not data from a real business.

| C | Finding (assumption) | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company | Strong espresso, 4.7 stars from 120 reviews, closes at 3 pm | Promote morning offers; test later hours |
| Competitors | Two nearby cafes open until 6 pm, one has no food | Highlight pastries made on site |
| Customers | Survey: most visit before work, few on weekends | Weekend brunch offer by email |
| Collaborators | Local bakery supplies pastries | Co-branded loyalty card |
| Climate | Office footfall changing, new rent review in spring | Build a delivery and pre-order option |

## FAQ

### What are the 5 C's of marketing?

Company, Competitors, Customers, Collaborators and Climate, according to Wikipedia's description of 5C analysis. Some sources use Context, Capabilities or Culture instead of Climate.

### Is the 5 C's framework the same as a marketing plan?

No. It is the analysis that feeds a plan. A plan adds goals, channels, budget and a schedule. The SBA places the marketing strategy inside the business plan and says to describe how you will attract and retain customers.

### How long should a small business spend on it?

An afternoon is enough for a first version if you use your own data and a short survey. Update it each quarter.

### Which C matters most?

None is fixed. Customers usually decide the channel, but a plan that skips Competitors can pay for ads in a market where it has no gap.
