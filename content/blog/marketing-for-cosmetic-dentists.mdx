## How Much Do Google Ads Cost a Cosmetic Dentist?

LocaliQ's 2026 row for Dentists and Dental Services shows $8.00 per click, 5.66% click-through rate, 10.67% conversion rate and $72.97 per lead. The all-industry averages are $5.42 per click and $66.69 per lead. Cosmetic terms such as veneers or smile makeovers may cost more than general dental terms, but LocaliQ does not publish a separate cosmetic row.

A lead here is a call or form, not a patient. Your front desk decides how many become consults. See [best Google Ads keywords for dentists](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-dentists-in-2026) for search terms to test, and set up call tracking so that you know which ads produce calls.

## What Do Cosmetic Patients Check Before They Call?

Reviews. BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found that 68% will only use a business rated 4 stars or higher, 47% will not use one with fewer than 20 reviews, and 74% only consider reviews from the last three months. 89% expect owners to reply.

Ask after treatment visits using a Google link or QR code. Google bans free or discounted services in exchange for reviews, and the FTC's 2024 rule bans buying or selling fake reviews. Reply without confirming that the reviewer is a patient or mentioning treatment, since that can disclose health information. Our [Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) has wording.

## Can You Use Before-and-After Photos in Ads?

Meta's health and wellness policy says cosmetic products, procedures and surgeries may depict general before-and-after transformation, but cosmetic procedure ads must be targeted to people 18 or older, and may not contain statements of inferiority about physical appearance. The policy page lists July 22, 2026 as its most recent dated change.

Photo use also needs patient consent, and HIPAA applies to patient information. Under 45 CFR 164.501, marketing is a communication that encourages people to buy a product or service, with exceptions for treatment communications. Ask your lawyer about consent forms before you publish a case. Our guide to [Facebook ads for dentists](/blog/facebook-ads-for-dentists) covers formats.

## How Should the Website and Profile Support Ads?

Google's guidelines say a Business Profile name should be the real-world name, without keywords or taglines. Descriptions cannot contain links. Use categories, services and photos of the practice, and make sure hours and phone match your website.

Send ad traffic to a page about the one treatment searched for, with a phone number, a form, financing information if you offer it, and a short list of what the consult includes. Our [website design for dentists](/blog/website-design-for-dentists) guide lists the elements. A generic home page loses the match between search and answer.

## Does Email Help Cosmetic Practices?

Email suits people who enquired but did not book, and past patients who may refer a friend. The CAN-SPAM Act requires accurate headers, no deceptive subject lines, a physical postal address, a clear opt-out, and honoring opt-outs within 10 business days. The FTC says each violating email can draw penalties of up to $53,088.

Ask a lawyer before using patient information for marketing. See [email marketing for dental practices](/blog/email-marketing-for-dental-practices) for sequences that fit.

## FAQ

### How much should a cosmetic dentist spend on marketing?

No verified percentage applies to all practices. Work from the value of an accepted case and your acceptance rate. At the $72.97 average cost per lead, $2,000 buys roughly 27 leads.

### Do Google Ads work for veneers?

They can, but test with a fixed budget. LocaliQ publishes a dental average, not a cosmetic one, so your cost per click may differ.

### Can I show patient testimonials?

Rules depend on state dental boards and privacy law. We did not verify specific state rules, so ask your lawyer. The FTC bans fake or bought reviews.

### Are before-and-after photos allowed on Meta?

Meta's policy allows general cosmetic before-and-after depictions with age and wording limits. Check the page for updates.
