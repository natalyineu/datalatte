Cosmetic dentists usually pay for enquiries through search ads, direct mail, local sponsorships and content, then win consults through clear offers and fast follow-up. One 2026 benchmark puts the median cost per lead for Dentists and Dental Services at $61.56 ([Adwave, citing WordStream](https://adwave.com/resources/facebook-ad-costs), secondary). That figure covers all dental work, not cosmetic treatment alone. Direct mail runs $0.50-1.50 per piece and local sponsorships $500-5,000. Paid advertising generates inquiries in days to weeks.

This guide is general information for practices in the US. It is not legal or advertising advice. Check your state dental board rules and patient privacy law before running any campaign that uses patient information.

## What Does a Cosmetic Dentist Pay per Lead?

The median cost per lead for Dentists and Dental Services is $61.56 in WordStream's 2026 data, as reported by [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/facebook-ad-costs) (secondary). No cosmetic-only figure is published in that benchmark, so treat it as a starting point for veneer or smile makeover campaigns, which may cost more or less.

A lead is a call or form, not a booked consult. Vendor results show what one campaign can produce. [Jarrett Digital](https://www.jarrettdigital.com/dental/google-ads-for-dentists/) reports that its campaign for one cosmetic dentistry client reliably generated 35-40 high-quality leads every day. That is one client's result, not a benchmark to plan around.

[MGE Management Experts](https://www.mgeonline.com/2024/the-right-way-to-market-cosmetic-dentistry-big-cases/) notes that Google ads, targeted social ads, implant funnels and direct mail "can get pretty pricey", but gives no figures. Ask your ad platform for the cost per click in your postcode before you set a budget.

## Which Channels Cost the Least per Contact?

Price per piece is not price per patient. The table lists the prices given in the sources, so compare them against the number of consults each channel can realistically return.

| Channel | Price point from sources | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct mail | $0.50-1.50 per piece, including design, printing and postage | Adwave (secondary) |
| Local sponsorship | $500-5,000 depending on scale and visibility | Adwave (secondary) |
| Outsourced blog post | $200-500 per post | Adwave (secondary) |
| Radio production | Free (station-produced) to $500+ for professional production | Adwave (secondary) |
| Paid search | $61.56 median cost per lead, all dental services | WordStream via Adwave (secondary) |

Direct mail suits a defined local area, where a mailer can reach households near the practice. A $1 mailer sent to the wrong area produces nothing, so pick the postcodes first. The [direct mail marketing guide for local businesses](/blog/direct-mail-marketing-local) covers targeting. Sponsorships suit a practice that can staff a table and follow up on every contact.

## How Fast Do Paid Ads Bring in Inquiries?

[Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-patients) states that paid advertising generates inquiries in days to weeks (secondary). That timeline suits a test, not a verdict. Judge a channel after several weeks of tracked calls and forms, not after the first few days.

Platform choice also depends on the market. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/dental-advertising-get-more-patients) says results from Yelp paid advertising vary widely by market: in some cities Yelp is a significant discovery platform for dentists, while in others Google and Healthgrades dominate (secondary). Check where patients in your city actually search before paying for a platform.

## Do Offers and Follow-Up Lift Consult Bookings?

[MGE](https://www.mgeonline.com/2024/the-right-way-to-market-cosmetic-dentistry-big-cases/) advises that promotional emails and social posts to patients should always include an attractive offer, such as a free consult or discount. The page was last updated on October 31, 2025. Test any offer against your fees and your state's rules on price claims before you run it.

The same article recommends repeating the cosmetic questionnaire given to every patient at each annual exam, because things can change over time. That gives you a refreshed list of patients who may want to revisit treatment. It also says dental offices may pay staff a commission when a hygienist or assistant gets a patient interested and the patient pays. Check any commission structure against state rules before adopting one.

Drafting offer emails and follow-up wording is faster with help from [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing). Have a clinician check every treatment claim before it goes out.

## Is a "2X Your Patients" Claim Proof of Results?

[Remedo's Google Ads page](https://www.remedo.io/google-ads-for-dentists) carries a banner reading "2X your dental patients now". Treat that as a marketing claim. The sources here do not give its method, sample size or time period, so it cannot serve as a benchmark for your practice.

Guides from vendors can help with ideas but date quickly. [Delmain's eight-strategy article](https://delmain.co/blog/cosmetic-dentist-marketing-ideas/) was last updated on February 14, 2025, and [Townsquare Interactive's guide](https://www.townsquareinteractive.com/blog/cosmetic-dentistry-marketing-ideas/) was published on May 30, 2023. Check each idea against current platform rules before you use it.

## What Could a Test Budget Return?

The only sourced figure in this example is the $61.56 median cost per lead. Every other input is an **assumption** you should replace with your own numbers.

| Line | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly search budget | $2,000 | Assumption |
| Cost per lead | $61.56 | Sourced median (Adwave, secondary) |
| Leads | about 32 | $2,000 divided by $61.56 |
| Leads who book a consult | 40% | Assumption |
| Consults booked | about 13 | 32 times 40% |
| Consults that accept treatment | 35% | Assumption |
| Accepted cases | about 4.5 | 13 times 35% |
| Average case value | $4,000 | Assumption |
| Revenue from accepted cases | about $18,000 | 4.5 times $4,000 |
| Ad cost per accepted case | about $444 | $2,000 divided by 4.5 |

Revenue is not profit. Materials, lab fees, staff time and the cost of treatment are not included. If acceptance falls to 15% (assumption), accepted cases drop to about 2 and ad cost per case rises to about $1,000. Run the table with your own fees and conversion rates before deciding on a monthly budget.
