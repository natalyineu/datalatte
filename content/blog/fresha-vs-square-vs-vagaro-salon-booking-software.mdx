There is no single winner, but there is a clear rule of thumb. Fresha is usually the easiest low-cost starting point for solo stylists and small salons, Square suits salons that also sell products and want one system for payments and retail, and Vagaro tends to fit multi-staff salons and studios that want deeper staff, class and marketing tools. Pricing and fees change often and differ by country, so confirm current numbers on each vendor's pricing page before you commit.

## The Short Comparison

| | Fresha | Square Appointments | Vagaro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical pricing model | Free core platform, with fees on payments and some add-ons | Free tier for individuals, paid tiers for teams | Monthly subscription that scales with staff |
| Best for | Solo and small salons | Salons with retail and a POS | Multi-staff salons, studios, classes |
| Strength | Low entry cost, marketplace exposure | Payments and inventory in one place | Staff, memberships and marketing tools |
| Watch out for | Processing and messaging fees add up | Booking features are simpler | Monthly fee plus add-ons |

Treat this table as a starting map. Features and plans change, so verify details before you decide.

## Fresha: The Low-Cost Starting Point

Fresha offers online booking, client records, staff scheduling and payment processing, and has traditionally charged little or nothing for the core software while making money on payment processing and certain add-ons such as messaging. It also runs a consumer marketplace, which can bring new clients but may come with extra fees on those bookings.

Good fit if you are a solo operator or a small team that wants to get online quickly.

## Square: The All-in-One Option

Square started as a payments company, so its strength is a point-of-sale system that handles card payments, retail inventory and appointments in the same place. If you sell shampoo, tools or gift cards, one system for both is convenient.

Good fit if retail is a meaningful part of your income. Its booking tools are generally simpler than those of salon-specific platforms, so check that your service menu (different durations per stylist, for example) works the way you need.

## Vagaro: The Industry-Specific Option

Vagaro is built around beauty, wellness and fitness businesses. It typically offers a monthly subscription that grows with staff numbers, along with staff management, commission handling, memberships, class scheduling and built-in marketing features.

Good fit for multi-stylist salons, spas and studios that want everything in one salon-specific system.

## How to Choose for Your Business Type

### Solo stylist or chair renter

You need low cost and simplicity. Fresha or Square's free tier are usually the first ones to test. Calculate your processing fees on a realistic month of sales, not just the headline monthly price.

### Small salon with 2 to 5 stylists

You will want staff schedules, per-stylist booking, commission tracking and client notes. Vagaro is often a strong match. If retail is large, compare it against Square.

### Busy nail bar or walk-in friendly business

You need quick check-in and fast checkout. Square's POS is a common choice here, but test how it handles a long service menu.

### Pet groomers

You need custom fields for pet details, vaccination dates and handling notes. Check each platform for custom client fields before you sign up, and read our guide for [pet groomers](/for/pet-groomers).

### Fitness and yoga studios

You need classes, memberships and attendance. Vagaro supports class-style booking; check whether the others fit your schedule model.

## The Hidden Costs to Compare

"Free" rarely means free once you add everything you actually use. Compare these on each platform:

- **Payment processing percentage and per-transaction fee** (these vary by country and change over time)
- **Text message reminders and marketing messages**, which are often charged per message or per bundle
- **Add-ons**, such as custom domain, online store or advanced reports
- **Extra staff or location fees**
- **Migration time:** exporting clients and rebuilding your service menu can take many hours, so choose carefully and plan to stay a year

The best way to compare is to take one typical month of your own sales and appointments and price it on each platform. Use the free trials where offered.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Choosing on headline price alone

A zero monthly fee can be offset by processing fees and message costs. Estimate your total cost for a typical month.

### Mistake 2: Not training your team

Many "software problems" are adoption problems. Block out a slow morning for hands-on training, write a one-page cheat sheet (add a client, reschedule, refund, apply a discount) and name one person as the go-to expert.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring the mobile booking experience

Most clients book from their phones. Open your booking link on a phone, try to book an appointment yourself, and remove any unnecessary fields. Simpler flows tend to convert better.

### Mistake 4: Not using reports to schedule staff

Your software can show you the busiest days and hours, revenue per staff member and the most profitable services. Review these every month or two and adjust the schedule to match demand.

### Mistake 5: Not automating follow-ups

All three tools can send reminders and some marketing messages. A simple flow is a thank-you with a review link the day after the visit, and a rebooking reminder around the time your client is due back. ## Ways to Increase Online Bookings

- **Reduce friction:** ask only for what you need to take the booking and collect extra details later.
- **Use deposits or card-on-file** to reduce no-shows. Platforms differ in how they handle cancellation fees, so set a clear policy and put it on your booking page.
- **Show real availability and any waitlist** so clients can grab open slots.
- **Link the booking page everywhere:** your Google Business Profile, Instagram bio and website. A well-optimised [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) often drives a large share of local bookings.

## FAQ

**Q: Which is best for a small hair salon, Fresha, Square or Vagaro?**
A: For a one-to-three-person salon, start by testing Fresha and Vagaro against your real monthly sales. Choose Square if you sell a lot of retail and want payments and inventory in one place.

**Q: Is Fresha really free?**
A: Fresha's core software has historically been free, but you will typically pay processing fees and charges for some extras such as messaging. Check the current pricing for your country.

**Q: Can I switch booking software without losing client data?**
A: Usually you can export clients as a CSV and import them elsewhere, but appointment history and notes may not transfer fully. Keep a backup and plan time to rebuild your service menu.

**Q: Which platform handles no-shows best?**
A: All three let you require a card or deposit and set a cancellation policy. Compare how each one handles fees and reminders in its settings.

**Q: Which software is best for multiple locations?**
A: Multi-location support varies by plan, so confirm it with each vendor before you decide.
