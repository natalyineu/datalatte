A small business can buy digital out-of-home through three routes: a pay-per-play marketplace, a self-serve programmatic platform, or a managed or direct buy. Entry points in the sources run from $0.01 per 8-second play on Blip, with no required minimum, to campaign minimums of $1,500 to $3,000 on AdQuick, to five-figure minimums for traditional managed buys. No platform wins for every budget. The right pick depends on spend, goal and how much setup work the owner will take on.

This comparison is independent of the vendors listed. Prices and minimums change, so confirm each figure on the vendor's own site before committing budget.

## What are the three ways to buy digital out-of-home screens?

The first route is a pay-per-play marketplace. Blip describes it this way: the advertiser sets a daily budget, and an algorithm uses that budget to bid for unsold billboard spots. The advertiser pays only when the ad plays ([Blip pricing](https://www.blipbillboards.com/pricing/), secondary). Blip says its platform shows live pricing for more than 3,500 digital billboards, filterable by city, state, traffic volume or board type.

The second route is a self-serve programmatic platform. Programmatic DOOH automates buying and delivery across digital billboards, transit displays and other public screens, and demand-side platforms purchase inventory in real time ([AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/answers/programmatic-dooh/what-is-the-best-affordable-programmatic-dooh-platform-for-small-businesses), secondary). Some self-serve networks sell a specific venue type instead, such as Take 10 Media's screens in convenience stores and on ATMs ([Take 10 Media](https://take10media.com/blog/self-serve-dooh-trends-2026-local-brands/), secondary).

The third route is a managed or direct buy, where a person or team plans and negotiates the placement. Adomni lists both self-service and managed-service models on its site ([Adomni](https://www.adomni.com/), secondary). AdQuick says locally negotiated direct campaigns can start below $10,000, and some small business campaigns begin at $1,000 to $5,000 per month ([AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/answers/dooh-campaigns/how-much-does-dooh-digital-out-of-home-advertising-cost), secondary).

For background on the channel itself, see the [complete DOOH guide](/blog/dooh-advertising-complete-guide-2026).

## What do the cost numbers mean for a small budget?

Two kinds of numbers appear in the sources, and mixing them causes bad plans. The first is the minimum a platform asks for. The second is the CPM, the cost per 1,000 impressions, which determines how far the money goes.

AdQuick reports delivered-impression CPMs from its own marketplace transactions between July 2025 and June 2026: roadside digital at an $8.00 median, retail or mall around $10.00, gyms around $8.00, and airports at a $14.00 median. These are computed from delivered ad plays and are not directly comparable to directly booked inventory ([AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/answers/programmatic-dooh/what-is-the-best-affordable-programmatic-dooh-platform-for-small-businesses), secondary).

The same vendor's cost page gives monthly flat-rate ranges: digital billboards from $1,200 in smaller markets, mall screens $750 to $2,500, supermarket screens $800 to $1,500. It also says programmatic campaigns generally require $10,000 to $50,000. Its small-business page says $1,500 to $3,000. The sources do not reconcile the two, which is another reason to ask each vendor for its current minimum in writing.

One more caution: AdQuick's median transacted multi-format campaign was $39,300 across 80 campaigns, far above a test budget. Creative production adds cost beyond media spend ([AdQuick cost page](https://www.adquick.com/answers/dooh-campaigns/how-much-does-dooh-digital-out-of-home-advertising-cost), secondary).

To estimate reach, divide budget by CPM and multiply by 1,000.

## Which option fits which budget and goal?

No source ranks these platforms for a given business, so this guide only maps the stated facts to common situations.

- A budget below any platform minimum, or a wish to test for a few dollars a day: Blip states no required minimum and no contract, and says a test can start with a few dollars per day in one market.
- One campaign of about $1,500 to $3,000: AdQuick states this range as its own minimum, and its page lists Adomni and Vistar Media at similar entry points (secondary). Ask Adomni and Vistar directly.
- A neighborhood goal, such as getting people near the shop through the door: screen-level or ZIP-level selection matters more than raw reach. Take 10 Media says highway boards offer short dwell times of 3 to 5 seconds and little geographic precision. That is a competitor-style argument from a vendor that sells retail screens, so weigh it accordingly.
- Several locations needing tight geographic control: AdQuick's page points to Simpli.fi and Vistar Media (secondary).
- DOOH as one channel among several, with budgets above $10,000 a month: AdQuick's page positions StackAdapt here (secondary). The [CTV and DOOH omnichannel guide](/blog/ctv-dooh-omnichannel-strategy-local-business) covers how the channels fit together.
- A wish to hand off the work: managed options exist at Adomni and Vistar, but pricing is not stated in the sources.

Time-limited promotions are a separate use case, covered in [DOOH for event marketing](/blog/dooh-event-marketing-local-business).

## What does a worked coffee shop test look like?

All inputs below are assumptions chosen for illustration, not benchmarks from any vendor.

Assumptions: a coffee shop sets a $1,500 four-week test budget on a self-serve programmatic platform. It assumes an $8 CPM, matching the roadside median above. It assumes $4.50 of gross profit per visit after ingredients and labor. Platform fees and creative costs are ignored for simplicity.

- Impressions: $1,500 divided by $8, times 1,000, equals 187,500 delivered impressions.
- Break-even visits: $1,500 divided by $4.50 equals about 334 visits.
- Per week: 334 divided by 4 is about 84 extra visits a week, or roughly 12 a day.
- Required response: 334 divided by 187,500 is about 0.18% of impressions turning into a tracked visit.

A smaller pay-per-play test works the same way. Assume $300 at an average of $0.05 per play (assumption; Blip says rates start at $0.01 and vary by location and time). That buys 6,000 plays. Break-even at $4.50 per visit is 67 visits. A play is not the same as a person, so the ratio is only a rough yardstick.

The same arithmetic works for a fitness studio. The assumption would change to gross profit per new member rather than per visit, and gym-venue CPMs of around $8 apply if the screens are in gyms.

## What does a 4-week first-test checklist look like?

### Week 1: set the goal and collect quotes

- Write one goal in a sentence, such as "new customers within a short drive of the shop."
- Pick one market or radius.
- Request current pricing and minimums from two or three platforms in the table, and save the answers.
- Decide the tracked action: a QR code, an offer code, or a "mention this screen" prompt.

### Week 2: build and submit creative

- Keep the design simple and readable. Blip advises artwork that is easy to read and stands out.
- Take 10 Media recommends silent-first video, bold typography and a clear QR code for busy retail environments (secondary).
- Submit for approval. Blip says designs are reviewed with its sign partners. Take 10 Media says review takes under 24 to 48 hours (vendor-stated).
- Confirm the schedule and daily budget cap.

### Week 3: launch and watch

- Check the dashboard for plays or impressions, spend and location mix.
- Log the tracked actions daily against the break-even number from the worked example.
- Change only one variable at a time, such as location or time of day.

### Week 4: decide

- Compare tracked visits to break-even.
- Keep, adjust or stop. The sources say pay-as-you-go setups let advertisers pause and adjust, so stopping should not carry a penalty.
- Read the [guide to measuring ads in local business](/blog/how-to-measure-ctv-ads-local-business) for measurement ideas that carry over.

If television or neighborhood-app budgets are also on the table, compare the [local TV advertising cost](/blog/local-tv-advertising-cost-small-business) and [Nextdoor ads cost](/blog/nextdoor-ads-cost-2026) articles before committing.

A DataLatte [free audit](/free-audit) can show whether screens belong in the mix at all, or whether search and local ads should come first.

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest way to try digital billboards?

Blip states that pricing starts at $0.01 per 8-second play, with no monthly minimum and no contract. Actual cost per play varies with location, traffic, time of day and demand, so the starting price is a floor and not a typical rate. Check live pricing on the platform before launch.

### Is a $1,500 budget enough to learn something?

AdQuick describes $1,500 to $3,000 as enough to run a single-market test and evaluate results before scaling (vendor-stated). Whether that is enough for one business depends on the market and offer, and the sources document no outcome benchmarks for small businesses.

### Will platform prices change?

Minimums and rates are set by each vendor and can change. The sources do not forecast price movement, so ask each vendor for current rates and minimums in writing before launch.

### Should a small business use a managed service instead?

Managed options exist, and AdQuick's page says they suit advertisers who want campaign execution handled for them (secondary). Pricing for managed services is not stated in the sources, except general ranges for traditional managed buys of $10,000 to $25,000 or more from Take 10 Media's comparison.
