## How much should a pet groomer spend on Meta ads?

Adwave puts a standalone Meta buy at [$300-$1,000 per month](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels). The same vendor notes that Meta [takes weeks to optimize](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-small-business-2026), so judging a campaign after three days tells you little. Plan for at least several weeks of data before deciding whether the budget is working.

The groomer-focused guide suggests [a daily budget of $10-$20](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers). Over 30 days, that is roughly $300-$600. Start at the lower end, and increase spending only once specific ads are producing bookings. Growing a budget before you know which creative works just multiplies the cost of weak ads.

## Which ad formats work for pet groomers?

Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, as the vendor describes it, saying [short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners) on Facebook and Instagram. Short clips of a finished groom are a natural fit for that format, and the same footage can be reused in Instagram Reels and Facebook feed placements.

Before-and-after photos remain a practical option for static ads. No sourced figure shows them beating video, so run one video ad and one photo ad against the same audience and compare cost per booking. Keep the call to action tied to a single action, such as booking a first appointment.

## Who should you target for pet grooming ads in Albuquerque?

The groomer-focused guide suggests [a 10-15 mile radius](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers) around the business and [pet owners aged 25-60 who are likely to have disposable income](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers). Those are general starting points. Adjust the radius to real drive times: a mobile groomer can reasonably cover more ground than a salon with a fixed location.

No sourced figure here describes Albuquerque's pet-owner population, so test the audience instead of assuming it. Meta's apps reach [an average of 3.56 billion daily active people](https://adwave.com/resources/facebook-advertising-for-small-business), which means the constraint is your targeting and offer, not the size of the audience pool. Check the detailed targeting list inside Ads Manager, because option names change over time.

Retargeting is a separate campaign. Adwave lists a typical Meta retargeting CPM of [$5-$15](https://adwave.com/resources/retargeting-ads-small-business). Build a custom audience from past clients and show them a booking reminder or a rebooking offer. This suits grooming because clients return on a schedule.

## What does a $600 monthly Meta budget return?

The example below uses assumption-labelled inputs. The cost ranges come from sources; the booking rate and ticket value do not, so replace them with your own numbers before relying on the result.

1. Monthly budget: $600 (assumption, equal to $20 a day for 30 days).
2. Cost per 1,000 impressions: $10 (assumption, midpoint of the $5-$15 range).
3. Cost per click: $2 (assumption, midpoint of the $1-$3 range).
4. Booking rate from click: 5% (assumption).
5. Average booking value: $75 (assumption).

Impressions equal $600 divided by $10, multiplied by 1,000, which gives 60,000. Clicks equal $600 divided by $2, which gives 300. Bookings equal 300 multiplied by 5%, which gives 15. Cost per booking equals $600 divided by 15, which gives $40. Booking revenue equals 15 multiplied by $75, which gives $1,125, so revenue is about 1.9 times spend under these assumptions. If your booking rate is 2%, the same budget produces 6 bookings and a cost of $100 per booking, so measure your own rate before scaling.

## How do you track Meta ad bookings?

Meta Ads Manager runs ads across Instagram and Facebook from one place, and [Instagram ads run through the same system because Instagram is owned by Meta](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business). The same tool also covers [Messenger](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media), which matters if clients message to book.

Set up tracking before you spend. Install the Meta pixel on your booking confirmation page, configure a lead event when someone submits the booking form, and add UTM parameters to every ad URL so your analytics can separate Meta traffic from other sources. Without that, the cost-per-booking figure in the example above cannot be measured, and you will be guessing. Check Meta's help center for the current setup steps, since interfaces change.

## Should pet groomers start with Facebook or Instagram ads?

Both are bought through the same system, so the choice is about where your clients look and which creative you have. A single campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook, or both. For a comparison of the two, see [Instagram ads vs Facebook ads](/blog/instagram-ads-vs-facebook-ads). Organic posting also matters, because paid ads perform better when the business profile looks active. The [pet groomers guide to Instagram marketing](/blog/pet-groomers-guide-to-instagram-marketing) covers that side of the work.
