## Is Your Customer on Instagram?

In Pew Research Center's 2025 survey of 5,022 US adults, fielded February to June 2025, 50% said they use Instagram. Use is far higher among younger adults: about 80% of 18 to 29-year-olds, against 19% of those 65 and older ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025)). Those are US figures. For the UK, Australia and Canada, look at your own follower and customer mix.

## How Instagram Decides Who Sees Your Reel

Instagram published an explainer on how it ranks Reels. It describes two steps: gather Reels a person might like, then order them by how interesting Instagram predicts each will be to that person. The predictions it calls most important are how likely someone is to reshare a Reel, watch it all the way through and like it ([Instagram: Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)). The post is older, so signals may have been updated, but the principle holds.

It lists the signals roughly in order of importance:

1. **The viewer's activity**: Reels they recently liked, saved, reshared or commented on.
2. **Their history with your account**: whether they have interacted with you before.
3. **Information about the Reel**: the audio track, the visuals and how popular it is.
4. **Information about your account**: follower count and engagement, which Instagram says is meant to "give everyone a chance to find their audience".

What this means in a café:

- **Finishing matters.** A 12-second Reel that people watch to the end beats a 40-second one they abandon. Cut every second that is not the pour, the reveal or the face.
- **Sharing matters.** Ask yourself "who would send this to a friend?" The best café shares are things like "this is where we should meet Saturday" or "you have to try this bun".
- **Past interaction helps.** The people who already engage with you are more likely to be shown your next Reel, so reply to comments and DMs.

## What Quietly Cuts Your Reach

Instagram applies Recommendation Guidelines to content shown to people who do not follow you. The same explainer says Reels with low resolution, watermarks, muted audio, borders, mostly text, or content already posted on Instagram are shown less often. Instagram's Creators page repeats the point: make work that is your own or new to Instagram, and reposts, "including content with noticeable watermarks", are less likely to be recommended. To build on a popular Reel, it points to the Remix tool ([Instagram Creators: Tips for improving your reach](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach)).

Turn that into a pre-post checklist:

- **Film on your phone's main camera in good light.** Blurry, dim footage is the easiest problem to fix.
- **Edit in-app, or export without a watermark.** If you also post to TikTok, save the clean version before uploading. Our [coffee shop TikTok guide](/blog/coffee-shop-tiktok-marketing-guide) covers that side.
- **Keep sound on.** A Reel with no audio at all is on Instagram's list of things shown less. The sound of the grinder or steam wand counts as audio.
- **Fill the frame.** No black borders, no slideshow of text cards.
- **Post fresh footage.** Do not recycle a clip you have already posted as a Reel or a post.
- **Keep it short.** Instagram says recommended Reels must be 3 minutes or less and advises you to keep them short. For a café, 15 to 30 seconds is my working rule, not an Instagram requirement.

Also check **Account Status** in your settings. Instagram's tips page says it shows whether your account or content can be recommended to non-followers, and what to do if it cannot.

## Use Trial Reels as Your Test Lab

Instagram's Trial Reels feature is the cleanest way to test without risk. Instagram describes it like this: create a Reel as usual, switch on the Trial toggle before sharing, and it is shown to non-followers first. It does not appear in followers' feeds or Reels tab or on your profile grid unless you later share it with everyone. About 24 hours after sharing you can see views, likes, comments and shares, and you can let Instagram auto-share the Reel to followers if it performs well based on views in the first 72 hours ([Instagram Creators: Trial reels](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels)). The page was written at the December 2024 rollout and does not list eligibility, so check whether the toggle appears in your app.

For a café, that gives you a four-week plan:

1. **Pick three formats.** For example: a close-up drink pour, a person on the team saying one thing about a drink, and a "what you get for $X" order.
2. **Make two versions of each** with different first seconds. Same drink, different hook.
3. **Post each as a Trial Reel** if available. If not, post one per day, same time of day, and compare.
4. **Look at the next day's numbers**, then share only the winners with everyone.
5. **Make more of the winning format** in week three and four, and drop what failed.

Judge each Reel by what Instagram says matters most: how many people watched to the end and how many shared it, not likes alone. Instagram's tips page also suggests comparing reach among followers and non-followers: if non-follower reach is tiny, your hook or visuals are the problem; if it is healthy but nobody visits, your call to action is.

## Point Every Reel at Your Door

Views are not visits. Four small habits connect the two:

- **Collabs.** Instagram says Collabs share one post to both accounts' audiences and that such posts can be recommended in the Reels tab, Explore and Feed. Invite the bakery that supplies your pastries or the bookshop next door to co-author a Reel. A neighbour's audience lives near you.
- **Search keywords.** Instagram says to put relevant keywords in your content, caption, bio and hashtags to get discovered through search. Write "flat white" and your neighbourhood in plain words rather than a joke only regulars understand.
- **One offer per Reel.** "Show this Reel at the counter for a free cookie with any drink this week" is countable. "Come visit us!" is not.
- **A complete Google profile.** Someone who sees a Reel in the evening will often search your name next morning. Make sure your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-coffee-shops) shows correct hours, photos and a menu link.

If you invite local creators to film in your café, disclosure rules apply; the US FTC guidance is summarised in our [Instagram guide for restaurants](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-restaurants), and the UK, Australia and Canada have their own regulators.

## Count Visits, Not Views

Pick two simple counters and run them for the whole test:

- **A "show this Reel" offer.** Staff tap a button on the till or add a tally mark on a card.
- **Ask every new customer** how they found you, and record the answer. People remember a Reel better than a coupon code.

Add a weekly look at Instagram Insights for views, non-follower reach and follows, and at your Business Profile for direction requests and calls. Treat those as supporting signals; the tally is your evidence.

## The Math

Take a four-week programme of 12 Reels. Every figure below is an **assumption** for illustration, since results vary by account and city. Replace them with your own counts after week four.

| Gross profit | $18 | $400 | $1,740 |
| Net after time cost | -$94 | +$288 | +$1,628 |

How to read it: break-even customers equal your time cost divided by gross profit per customer. In the Base case that is $112 / $20, about 6 people. The Strong column is a best case and not a plan. The number that surprises owners is the middle row: most viewers do not live nearby. A Reel that reaches 100,000 people in other countries brings you nothing, which is why Collabs with neighbours, local keywords and a local offer matter more than raw view counts.

## A Sustainable Weekly Routine

- **Monday:** film three clips in one 30-minute session, before the rush.
- **Tuesday and Wednesday:** edit and schedule. Our content calendar guide has a template.
- **Thursday to Sunday:** reply to comments within the hour, and check last week's results.
- **Every fourth week:** review the tally and decide which format to keep.

<Callout type="coffee" title="One Format, Not Fifteen">
Fifteen ideas are useful once. A format you repeat weekly ("What $6 gets you", "Meet the barista") builds a recognisable series, and a series is easier to make and to measure than a different idea every time.
</Callout>

## Do This Today

1. Open Instagram settings and check **Account Status** to see whether your content can be recommended to non-followers.
2. Write down your break-even: your weekly time cost divided by gross profit per new regular.
3. Film one 15 to 30-second clip on your phone's main camera in window light, with the real sounds of your bar, and no watermarks or borders.
4. Post it as a Trial Reel if you have the option, with one offer such as "show this Reel for a free cookie".
5. Put a tally card by the till, and check results 24 and 72 hours later.

## FAQ

**Q: How long should a coffee shop Reel be?**
A: Instagram says Reels must be 3 minutes or less to be recommended and advises keeping them short. For a café, 15 to 30 seconds is a practical starting point, but let your own watch-through data decide.

**Q: What are Trial Reels?**
A: A feature that shows a Reel to non-followers first, without putting it on your profile or in followers' feeds. After about 24 hours you can see views, likes, comments and shares, and you can choose to share it with everyone. Check that it is available on your account.

**Q: Can I post my TikTok videos as Reels?**
A: Not with the watermark. Instagram says Reels with watermarks and reposted content are less likely to be recommended. Export a clean copy. For video ideas on that platform, see our TikTok guide above.

**Q: Why do I get views but no customers?**
A: Often the viewers are not local. Check what share of your viewers live near you, add a local offer, collaborate with neighbouring businesses and use local keywords in captions.

**Q: How often should I post?**
A: Choose what you can keep up for 90 days. Three a week is a workable target for the test above; consistency beats volume. Instagram does not publish a required frequency in the pages we reviewed.
