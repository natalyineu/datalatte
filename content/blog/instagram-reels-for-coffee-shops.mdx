Instagram Reels can bring coffee shop customers, but only if you judge them by visits rather than views. Run a four-week test: post 12 short Reels, attach one local offer to each, and count which clips bring people through the door. In the worked example below, staff time costs about $112 a month (assumption), so the test pays off only if a few new regulars come from it. The steps below cover how to set up the test, what to check before posting, and how to count visits without guessing.

## Is Instagram Worth Your Time as a Coffee Shop?

Start with your own customers rather than national averages. Ask people at the counter which apps they use, and note which age groups buy drinks at your busiest hours. If your regulars are students, commuters and remote workers, Reels are a good fit. If most of your trade is older residents who come in for a daily cup, a different channel may earn more per hour.

Treat Reels as a cheap experiment, not a brand campaign. The question is narrow: which short video makes someone who lives within walking distance decide to visit this week?

Your time is the number that decides it. Three Reels a week at roughly 20 minutes each to film and edit, plus a few minutes of replies, comes to about 4.5 hours a month (assumption). At $25 an hour (assumption), that is about $112. Divide that cost by the gross profit from one new regular to get your break-even count.

## How Do You Test Reels Without Annoying Followers?

The cleanest test is a fixed format repeated for four weeks. Pick three formats that suit your bar. Examples include a close-up of a pour, a team member saying one useful thing about a drink, and a "what this price gets you" order walkthrough. Keep the same formats for the whole test so you can compare like with like.

Make two versions of each format with different opening seconds. The drink can stay identical; only the first three seconds change. Post one clip per day at the same time, so posting time does not explain the results.

Check each clip after 24 and 72 hours. Look at how many people watched to the end and how many shared it. Likes are the weakest signal for a café because they rarely turn into a visit. Share the strongest clip with your full following in week three, then make more of that format in week four.

Keep the tests inside your normal feed rhythm. Followers who see a clip about the espresso machine once a week will not mind. Followers who see five test clips in one day probably will.

## What Quietly Limits Reel Reach?

Several habits make it harder for a clip to find new viewers. Blurry or dim footage is the most common problem, so film near a window or in good light on your phone's main camera. Clips with a visible watermark from another app look like reposts and may get less attention, so export a clean copy before posting.

Sound matters too. A clip with no audio loses the grinder, the steam wand and the chatter that make a café feel real. Keep the frame full, with no black bars and no slideshows of text cards.

Avoid recycling old footage as a new Reel. If you want to build on a popular clip, film your own version of the idea instead. Check your account settings for any notice about content recommendations. ## How Do You Count Visits From Reels?

Views are not visits, so build a simple counter into the café. Two methods work well together.

First, attach one countable offer to every Reel. For example: "Show this Reel at the counter for a free cookie with any drink this week." Staff record each redemption on a tally card by the till. A vague line such as "come visit us" cannot be counted.

Second, ask every new customer how they found you, and log the answer. Customers often remember a Reel better than a discount code.

Use the platform's own insights as supporting data: views, the share of viewers who do not follow you, and follows gained. Compare these with your Google Business Profile activity, such as direction requests and calls. Keep your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips) accurate, because viewers who see a Reel in the evening often search your name the next morning. The tally is your main evidence; platform numbers help explain it.

## How Many Reels Should a Café Post Each Week?

Choose a schedule you can keep for 90 days. Three a week is a workable target for the test above, and consistency usually beats volume. A simple weekly routine works well:

- Monday: film three clips in one 30-minute session before the morning rush.
- Tuesday and Wednesday: edit, add captions with local keywords, and schedule.
- Thursday to Sunday: reply to comments promptly and update the tally.
- Every fourth week: review the results and keep the format that worked.

Collaborations help reach. Invite the bakery that supplies your pastries or the bookshop next door to appear in a Reel. Their audience lives near you, and that audience is the one most likely to turn into visits. ## FAQ

### How long should a coffee shop Reel be?

Long enough to show the drink or moment, and short enough that people watch to the end. A 15 to 30 second starting point (assumption) is common for cafés. Let your own watch-through data decide the final length.

### Should I use Trial Reels?

If your app offers them, use them for the test, since they reach non-followers first, which suits a test aimed at new people. Check whether the option appears on your account before building the plan around it.

### Can I post my TikTok videos as Reels?

You can, but export a clean copy without the other app's watermark. Watermarked reposts may get less attention. Reuse the idea with fresh footage where you can.

### Why do I get views but no customers?

Most viewers probably do not live near you. Check the share of viewers outside your following, add a countable local offer, use neighbourhood keywords in captions, and collaborate with nearby businesses.

### How often should I post?

Pick a frequency you can sustain for 90 days. Three a week is a practical target for the four-week test, and consistency matters more than volume.
