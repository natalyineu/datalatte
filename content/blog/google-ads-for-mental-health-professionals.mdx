Google Ads can bring a therapist new clients, but the cost per new client depends on three things: what each click costs, how many enquiries become booked intakes, and how many booked people attend. This guide does not quote a mental health cost benchmark, so check live auction data in your own account. Instead, it works through a $1,200 monthly budget with every input labelled as an assumption, sets a ceiling price per click, and covers the policy and ethics limits for practices in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It is not legal advice.

## Who Is Searching for Mental Health Care Online?

People research before they book. Pew Research, as reported by StackAdapt, found that 47% of internet users search online for information about doctors or other health professionals. That covers health professionals generally, not therapists specifically, but it shows search is a real first step.

A NIQ survey, reported by AdWave, found that 55% of consumers are willing to spend over $100 per month on better nutrition, self-care, physical and mental health, and related products or services. That is a wide wellness category, so it does not measure therapy demand directly.

A Tuff Growth mental health awareness campaign on StackAdapt, as the agency described it, produced 200,000 impressions, over 90% audio completion and 200 leads. That was an audio campaign run by one vendor, so treat it as an example of what is possible, not a forecast for a therapy practice.

## Which Ad Platforms Allow Mental Health Ads?

Platform rules differ, and some exclude mental health entirely. Optimum7 reports that ChatGPT ads will be kept out of sensitive categories such as health, mental health and politics (secondary). That is one platform's stated plan, so check the current policy on every platform before you build a campaign there.

For Google Ads, read the current health and personalized advertising policy pages in the Google Ads policy center before you build any audience. Plan on a keyword-led Search campaign first, because audience-based targeting is where health rules bite hardest. This article does not restate Google's wording, which changes over time.

## How Should a Therapist Set Up a Search Campaign?

Start with one Search campaign for one service that you are well placed to deliver. Target your office area and only the states or provinces where you are licensed to see clients. A click from a place where you cannot treat the person is wasted spend, and for telehealth this is the first check to make.

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anxiety | "anxiety therapist [town]", "therapy for anxiety near me" | Phrase + exact |
| Couples | "couples counseling [town]", "marriage therapist near me" | Phrase + exact |
| Trauma | "trauma therapist [town]", "EMDR therapy [town]" | Phrase + exact |
| Access | "therapist accepting new clients", "evening therapy appointments" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your practice name] | Exact |

Add negatives from the start: jobs, salary, internship, course, quiz, test, worksheet, pdf, book, free, hotline, suicide, crisis and emergency. Add "support group" if you do not run one. Review the search terms report every week.

Some people searching are in crisis and need immediate help, not an appointment next week. Exclude crisis terms, do not bid on them, and put a crisis line for your country on the landing page.

## What Ethics Rules Apply to Therapy Advertising?

Ad platform policy is only one layer. Your licensing board and professional body set rules on testimonials, specialty claims, fees and directory listings, and those rules apply to ads and websites as well as to therapy sessions. Rules differ by profession and by state or province, so read your own code before writing any copy.

AI tools are a common way to draft ad copy. A StackAdapt article on AI in connected TV advertising says brand safety, compliance and ethical considerations are among the most critical areas needing human oversight (secondary). The same logic applies to text ads: a licensed clinician should review every AI-drafted line before it goes live. In a StackAdapt and Ascend2 study, 18% of agencies cited ethical considerations as a concern about AI in advertising (secondary).

In practice, do not quote client testimonials, avoid cure or guaranteed-result claims, and keep credentials exact.

## What Tracking Is Safe Without Health Data?

Measure outcomes without putting health information into ad platforms. Three measures work well:

1. Calls from the ad that last longer than a minimum length you choose, matched to a real enquiry.
2. Form submissions that land on a thank-you page with no health details in the URL.
3. Booked intakes logged by hand, with a "how did you find us?" question on every intake.

Keep ad tags off intake, portal and booking pages. Do not upload client lists or booking records to any ad platform. Before any tag or pixel goes live on a page that involves health information, get advice from a qualified privacy adviser, because HIPAA in the US and privacy law in the UK, Australia and Canada each have their own rules.

## What Does a Worked Budget Look Like?

Every input below is an assumption. Replace them with your own account data after a quarter. The fee, session count and willingness to pay are your decisions, not benchmarks.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,200 | $1,200 | $1,200 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $6.00 | $4.00 | $3.00 |
| Clicks | 200 | 300 | 400 |
| Click to enquiry (assumption) | 2.0% | 2.0% | 3.0% |
| Enquiries | 4 | 6 | 12 |
| Enquiry to booked intake (assumption) | 50% | 50% | 50% |
| Booked to attended (assumption) | 80% | 80% | 80% |
| New clients | 1.6 | 2.4 | 4.8 |
| Ad cost per new client | $750 | $500 | $250 |

Volume is small in every column. In the Base case, one no-show changes the monthly result noticeably, so judge over a quarter, not a week.

Set a ceiling click price with this formula: most you will pay for one new client, multiplied by click-to-enquiry, enquiry-to-booked and booked-to-attended rates. If your limit is $400 per new client (assumption), the Base ceiling is $400 × 0.02 × 0.5 × 0.8 = $3.20 per click. The $4.00 Base click is above that, so the Base case fails your limit. The Strong case, at $250 per client, passes.

Revenue per client is a separate check. Assume a $140 session and 10 sessions (both assumptions): $1,400 in fees before your costs and time. All three scenarios sit under that figure, but the $400 limit is what separates them.

## What Should a Therapist Do First?

1. Read your licensing board's and professional body's advertising rules, and note what they say on testimonials, specialty claims and fees.
2. Decide the most you will pay for one new client and calculate your ceiling click price with the formula above.
3. Build one Search campaign for one service, limited to your office area and licensed states, with the negatives listed above and no remarketing or list uploads.
4. Put a crisis line for your country, your credentials, fees and a call button on one landing page, with no ad tags on intake or portal pages.
5. Set up call and form conversions with a chosen minimum call length, and log how every new client found you.

## FAQ

### Can therapists run Google Ads?

Yes, within limits. Check the current health policy pages in the Google Ads policy center and your licensing board's rules before you build any audience or upload client data.

### How much should a solo practice spend?

This guide gives no benchmark. Pick a monthly figure you can sustain for a quarter, then work backward from the most you will pay per new client using the ceiling formula above.

### Can I use client reviews in my ads?

Be careful. Professional codes and boards may restrict testimonials and requests for them. Check your own code before quoting any review.

### Can I upload client lists for remarketing?

Do not upload client data to ad platforms. Health information is sensitive, and any list-based targeting needs advice from a qualified privacy adviser and a check of current platform policy.
