LocaliQ's healthcare benchmarks put the median Mental Health click at $4.22 and the median conversion rate at just 1.85%. At those numbers, $1,200 buys about 284 clicks and roughly 5 enquiries. If half of enquirers book and 80% of those attend an intake, you pay about $570 for each new client. The click price and conversion rate are LocaliQ's; the booking and attendance rates are my assumptions. The takeaway is that therapy ads work in small numbers, so every point of your close rate and every wasted click matters more than it does for a coffee shop.

This guide is for solo therapists, counselors and small group practices in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers the benchmark, a tight Search setup, what Google's policy pages say about health advertisers (with my reading marked as mine), the professional-ethics side, privacy-aware tracking and the math. It is not legal advice. Check your licensing board's advertising rules, and take HIPAA or local privacy questions to a qualified adviser.

## What the Benchmarks Say

[LocaliQ's healthcare search advertising benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/healthcare-search-advertising-benchmarks/) cover 3,542 US search campaigns from October 2024 to September 2025, on Google and Microsoft. The page calls its averages medians, and each metric is reported separately.

Two cautions. First, a "lead" is whatever each advertiser counted, and because the medians are calculated separately, $4.22 divided by 1.85% does not equal $141.17. Second, the same page shows General Practice & Family Medicine at an 11.63% conversion rate, so therapy converts at a fraction of the rate of routine primary care. That is consistent with therapy being a more considered decision, and with many searchers being in research mode. Plan for a long, patient funnel and judge by booked intakes, not clicks.

## Choose Search, and Stay Narrow

Start with one Search campaign for your best-supported service. People type what they need: "anxiety therapist near me", "couples counseling [town]", "trauma therapy [state]". Search lets you target by keyword rather than audience, which matters for the policy reasons below.

Use location targeting around your office and any states where you are licensed to see clients, especially for telehealth. A client in a state where you hold no licence is a wasted click.

### Keywords and match types

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anxiety | "anxiety therapist [town]", "therapy for anxiety near me" | Phrase + exact |
| Couples | "couples counseling [town]", "marriage therapist near me" | Phrase + exact |
| Trauma | "trauma therapist [town]", "EMDR therapy [town]" | Phrase + exact |
| Access | "therapist accepting new clients", "therapist that takes [insurer]", "evening therapy appointments" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your practice name] | Exact |

Negatives from day one: `jobs`, `salary`, `internship`, `course`, `quiz`, `test`, `worksheet`, `pdf`, `book`, `free`, `support group` (if you do not run one), `hotline`, `suicide`, `crisis`, `emergency`. Review the search terms report weekly. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/negative-keywords-google-ads) explains campaign-level lists.

<Callout type="warning" title="Crisis searches are not therapy leads">
Some searchers are in crisis and need immediate help, not an appointment next week. The 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline says it is available 24/7/365 by call, text or chat in the US, and its [website](https://988lifeline.org/) describes it as free and confidential. Put a crisis line for your country on your landing page, exclude crisis terms from your keywords, and do not bid on them.
</Callout>

## The Policies That Apply, as Google's Pages Say Them

This section reports what Google's own pages say. My reading is marked as mine.

### 1. Counseling is named as a sensitive health category

Google's [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855) page names "counseling services for mental health issues like depression, anxiety, and addiction" among its included examples. For sensitive interest categories, advertiser-curated audiences cannot be used. The page lists Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion and lookalike segments as unsupported, and lists predefined Google audiences, in-market segments, affinity audiences and location targeting as supported. The [personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) says the same of sensitive interest categories generally, and adds that users under 18 are not eligible for personalized advertising of any kind. So plan a keyword-driven Search account with no remarketing and no email-list uploads.

### 2. Do not upload client conversions

The [customer data policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7475709) list health or medical information as a sensitive category and say you may not upload conversion information related to sensitive categories, which also cannot be used for enhanced conversions. Never push booking records from your practice management system into Google Ads.

### 3. Addiction services are restricted

The [Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) says Google restricts promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, and that advertisers can promote them in certain locations only if certified by Google. It treats behavioral addiction, nicotine and impulse control disorders as unrelated to that rule. Its telemedicine provisions sit under prescription drug services and, in the U.S., refer to LegitScript's certification for services that facilitate prescribing. My reading, not Google's statement: that wording is about prescribing, so a talk-therapy practice is not obviously covered, but a psychiatrist offering online prescribing should read the page in full first.

### 4. Claims

The [unreliable claims policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936857) bars claims of unproven cures and claims that entice with an improbable result, and says testimonials claiming specific results need a visible disclaimer. "Cure your anxiety" and "Stop panic attacks in two weeks" are the sort of lines to avoid. Describe the service instead: `Anxiety Therapy in Denver. Licensed Counselor`.

## The Professional-Ethics Layer

Google approval is not the same as professional approval. The American Psychological Association's [Ethics Code](https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf) includes Standard 5.05, which says psychologists "do not solicit testimonials from current therapy clients/patients or other persons who because of their particular circumstances are vulnerable to undue influence." Standard 5.01 covers false or deceptive public statements, and it extends to paid or unpaid advertising and directory listings. Counseling and social work codes have comparable provisions, according to a [secondary account from Person-Centered Tech](https://personcenteredtech.com/testimonials-reviews-in-mental-health-practice-ethics-hipaa-and-the-post%E2%80%91covid-marketing-landscape/), which also relays one clinician's view that therapists should not ask clients for Google reviews and that even a "thank you" reply can confirm someone is a client. Treat that as second-hand and check your own code and board.

Practical result: do not quote client testimonials in ads or landing pages, do not run review requests to current clients, and keep your credentials statement accurate.

## Landing Page That Builds Trust

Send ads to a page on your own site so you control the message and measurement. Include who you help and how in plain language, your license type and state, a photo, your approach in a few lines, fees, insurance or self-pay information, and availability. Offer a call button or a short form asking name, phone and preferred time, not symptoms. Add your privacy policy and the crisis line above. ## Tracking Without Health Data

Count:

1. **Calls from the ad**, above a minimum length you set. Google's [website call conversions help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) says a call counts only if it is longer than the minimum length you set. Pick a length matching a real enquiry; 30 to 60 seconds is my suggestion, not Google's.
2. **Contact form submissions** on a thank-you page that carries no health details in the URL.
3. **Booked intakes**, recorded by hand: ask each new client how they found you.

Google's [Analytics HIPAA page](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/13297105) says it does not offer Business Associate Agreements for Analytics, that HIPAA-regulated customers must refrain from exposing protected health information to Google, and that tags should not go on authenticated or HIPAA-covered health care pages. Keep ad tags off intake, portal and booking pages, and take advice before any pixel goes live. Our [conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) covers the mechanics.

## The Math

Take a $1,200 monthly budget. The Base click price and conversion rate are LocaliQ's Mental Health figures. Every other row is an assumption, including the fee and number of sessions. Replace them after a few months of data.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget (assumption) | $1,200 | $1,200 | $1,200 |
| Cost per click | $5.50 (assumption) | $4.22 (LocaliQ) | $3.50 (assumption) |
| Clicks | 218 | 284 | 343 |
| Click to enquiry | 1.0% (assumption) | 1.85% (LocaliQ) | 3.0% (assumption) |
| Enquiries | 2.2 | 5.3 | 10.3 |
| Enquiry to booked intake (assumption) | 40% | 50% | 60% |
| Booked to attended (assumption) | 70% | 80% | 85% |
| New clients | 0.6 | 2.1 | 5.3 |
| Ad cost per new client | $1,967 | $570 | $229 |

Read it three ways:

- **Volume is tiny.** Base gives about two new clients a month, so one extra no-show swings the month. Judge over a quarter, not a week.
- **Your ceiling click price.** Max CPC = most you will pay per new client x enquiry rate x booking rate x attendance rate. In Base that is 0.0185 x 0.50 x 0.80 = 0.0074. If a new client is worth enough that you will pay $400, the ceiling is $400 x 0.0074 = $2.96 a click, below the $4.22 median. That tells you to bid narrow, not wide.
- **Value of a client.** Suppose a session is $140 and a client stays 10 sessions (both assumptions): $1,400 in revenue. Against the Base cost of $570 that pays back, against the Tough $1,967 it does not. Use your own fee and retention, and count your time.

Your best levers are off-platform: answer or return calls the same day, offer an evening slot, and make the first call short and kind.

## Do This Today

1. Read your licensing board's and professional association's advertising rules, and write down what they say on testimonials, specialty claims and fees.
2. Decide the most you will pay for one new client and compute your ceiling CPC with the formula above.
3. Build one Search campaign for one service, in your office area and licensed states only, with the negatives above and no remarketing or list uploads.
4. Put the crisis line, credentials, fees and a call button on one landing page, with no ad tags on intake or portal pages.
5. Set up call and form conversions with a chosen minimum call length, and log "how did you find us" for every intake.

## FAQ

**Q: Can therapists run Google Ads at all?**
A: Yes, with limits. Google names counseling as a sensitive health category, which blocks audience tools like Customer Match and remarketing lists but not keyword-targeted Search. Your licensing board may add rules on testimonials and claims.

**Q: How much should a solo practice spend?**
A: There is no official figure. At LocaliQ's $4.22 click, $1,200 buys about 284 clicks and $600 about 142, which is thin for learning. Start with what you can sustain for three months and work backward from the value of a client.

**Q: Can I use client reviews in my ads?**
A: Be careful. The APA code bars soliciting testimonials from current therapy clients, and many boards limit them. Check your own code before quoting any review.

**Q: Should I send traffic to my Psychology Today profile?**
A: A page on your own site gives you control over the message and measurement. A directory profile can complement ads, but it is harder to track.

**Q: How long before ads bring in clients?**
A: Enquiries can arrive in days. Give it a quarter, my rule of thumb, because volumes are small and decisions are slow.
