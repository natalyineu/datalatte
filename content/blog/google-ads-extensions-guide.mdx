Google Ads extensions are now called assets. They add a phone number, location, links, extra text, prices or a form to a Search ad. Google's help lists eleven kinds, besides headlines and descriptions: location, call, sitelink, callout, structured snippet, text disclaimer, price, promotion, app, lead form and automated assets. Adding them is free, and you pay normal click charges when people interact.

Assets are not guaranteed to show. This guide explains each type, the rules Google publishes, and how a local business should prioritise them. Examples use made-up businesses and are labelled as such.

## What Are Google Ads Extensions Now?

Google renamed extensions to assets. Older articles still say "extensions", and the idea is the same: extra information attached to an ad, a campaign or the account.

Google's help says assets must be linked to an active ad group, campaign or account, are approved or disapproved like ads, and can be scheduled for certain dates, days or times. Removing an asset from one campaign does not remove it from others.

## Do Assets Cost Extra?

No. Google says adding assets is free. You pay your normal click charge, including for call, app download and directions clicks. Clicks on seller ratings and reviews are not charged, and no more than two clicks per impression are charged for an ad and its assets.

Assets show only when predicted to improve performance and when Ad Rank is high enough. Higher positions get priority for more assets, so a weak ad may show none.

## Which Asset Types Exist and What Do They Do?

| Asset | What it adds | Local use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Address, call button, link to hours, photos, directions | Walk-in shops |
| Call | Phone number or call button | Service and appointment businesses |
| Sitelink | Links to pages such as hours or booking | Every business |
| Callout | Short text such as "free delivery" | Offers and guarantees |
| Structured snippet | A set header with listed items | Menus and service lists |
| Price | Services or categories with prices | Fixed-price services |
| Promotion | Limited-time offers | Sales and events |
| Lead form | Form inside the ad | Quotes and bookings |
| Text disclaimer | Required terms | Regulated offers |
| App | App download links | Businesses with apps |
| Automated | Added by Google when predicted to help | Account-level |

## How Many Sitelinks Do You Need?

Google says Search and Performance Max need at least two sitelinks to show. Desktop can show up to six and mobile up to eight. Text is limited to 25 characters in most languages, and shorter links let more show.

Google recommends at least six sitelinks with descriptions for high-volume ad groups and campaigns, and at least four at account level, with at least two for mobile. Descriptions are not required to show sitelinks but unlock some formats. Link to pages people use, such as booking, prices, hours and the service page.

## How Do Call and Location Assets Work?

Clicks on a call asset cost the same as headline clicks. The number must be in E.164 format, and Google must see it in your site source code, or your domain must be verified. Vanity, premium and fax numbers are not accepted, and phone numbers should not appear elsewhere in the ad text.

Call reporting uses Google forwarding numbers, works on the Search Network and can count calls as conversions. Google warns this may change auto-bidding. Location assets show your address, a call button and a link to hours, photos and directions, and work well with call assets.

## What About Lead Forms?

Lead form assets let people submit contact details inside the ad. Search and Performance Max campaigns are supported. Google requires a good policy history, an eligible vertical and a privacy policy link.

The campaign must use conversion-focused bidding. Leads are downloadable for the last 30 days and stored for 60, so export often or connect email notifications, a webhook, Zapier or the API. Sensitive verticals may be ineligible, so do not assume a health or finance form will be allowed.

## What Does It Look Like for a Local Business?

This is an **assumption**-based example for a made-up café, built to show how billing works, not a forecast.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $600 | Assumption |
| Average CPC | $3.00 | Assumption |
| Total paid clicks | 200 | $600 / $3.00 |
| Of which on the headline | 150 | Assumption |
| Of which call button | 30 | Assumption |
| Of which direction taps | 20 | Assumption |

All 200 clicks are billed the same way, $3.00 each, per Google's rule that asset clicks cost the same as headline clicks. The benefit is not a lower price. It is that 50 of the 200 customers reached you without loading your site. Check the asset report to see which ones got used. ## Which Assets Should You Add First?

For most local businesses: sitelinks, a call asset, a location asset, callouts, then price or promotion if you have fixed offers. Add a lead form only if you can respond to leads the same day.

## FAQ

### Are Google Ads extensions the same as assets?
Yes. Google renamed extensions to assets. Help pages now use "assets".

### Do assets cost money?
Adding them is free. You pay normal click charges when people interact with them.

### Why are my assets not showing?
Google says they appear when predicted to help and when Ad Rank is high enough. They also need approval and an active link to the campaign.

### How many sitelinks can show?
At least two are needed. Up to six can show on desktop and eight on mobile in Search and Performance Max.

### Can I schedule assets?
Yes. Supported assets can be set to show only on certain dates, days or times.
