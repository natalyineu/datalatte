The best Google Ads keywords for a personal trainer are local, booking-intent searches: "personal trainer near me", "personal trainer [your city]" and specialty phrases such as "weight loss trainer [city]". Start with phrase match, add a negative list before launch, and judge each keyword by cost per booked consultation, not cost per click.

No public source lists a click price for each of these keywords, so this guide gives no per-keyword CPC. The only published benchmark that fits is the LocaliQ 2026 Health & Fitness category: $6.17 per click and $67.36 per lead. Use Google's Keyword Planner for your own city.

## Which Keyword Groups Should a Personal Trainer Bid On?

Group keywords by what the searcher wants, then give each group its own ad group and landing page section. The table lists six groups. The intent column is a judgement based on how the phrase is worded, not measured data.

| Keyword group | Example keywords | Intent | Suggested match type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local booking | personal trainer near me, personal trainer [city] | High | Phrase |
| Neighbourhood | personal trainer [suburb or district] | High | Phrase, then exact |
| Goal-based | weight loss trainer [city], strength coach [city] | High | Phrase |
| Audience-based | personal trainer for seniors, trainer for women | Medium to high | Phrase |
| Format-based | in home personal trainer, online personal trainer | Medium | Phrase, separate campaign |
| Price research | personal training cost [city], packages | Medium | Phrase, watch closely |

Local booking phrases come first because the searcher is already comparing nearby trainers. Online-only phrases draw people from anywhere, so keep them in a separate campaign with its own budget, or leave them out if you only train in person.

## How Much Do Personal Trainer Keywords Cost?

Google does not publish per-keyword prices outside your own account. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark puts Health & Fitness at $6.17 per click, against $5.42 for all industries, with a 6.94% conversion rate and $67.36 per lead. The category is broader than personal training, and the figures are medians from US search campaigns, so treat them as a rough range.

Google describes Keyword Planner as the tool for estimating monthly searches and the average cost to show on a keyword. You need to finish account setup with billing details to use it. Check your city there before you set a budget, and compare the result with the benchmark.

## Which Match Type Should You Use?

Google's help describes three match types. Broad match is the default and reaches the widest range of searches. Phrase match shows ads on searches that include the meaning of your keyword. Exact match shows ads on searches with the same meaning or intent, and gives the most control.

For a new local campaign, phrase match on your core terms is a reasonable start. Move proven terms to exact match once the search terms report shows they book clients. Google notes that broad match needs Smart Bidding to work well, so leave it until you have conversion data and a solid negative list.

## Which Negative Keywords Should You Add First?

Negative keywords stop your ads showing on searches that do not fit your business. Google says they do not match close variants, so a negative for "job" will not block "jobs". Add singular and plural forms yourself.

Starting negatives for a personal trainer:

- Training and careers: certification, course, become a personal trainer, jobs, salary, hiring
- Free intent: free, youtube, pdf, workout plan
- Other services: gym membership, group class, nutrition coach, app
- Places you do not serve: add location exclusions rather than relying on negatives

Review the search terms report every week for the first two months. Every irrelevant term you find is a click you stop paying for.

## How Do You Keep Ads Within Your Area?

Location settings matter as much as keywords for a trainer whose clients live within a short drive. Google allows radius targeting from 1 km upward. It recommends "Presence or interest" for most Search campaigns, and says "Presence" fits when you only want people who are physically in the target area.

If your report shows clicks from people far outside your service area, switch to "Presence" and check again after a few weeks. Then add the districts you actually serve as separate locations so you can see which ones book.

## How Do You Know Which Keywords Work?

Track calls and form submissions as conversions. Google lets you set a minimum call length, and only calls at least that long count. Pick a length that separates a real enquiry from a wrong number. Then check keyword and search term reports monthly.

Pause terms that spend without a lead after you have enough clicks to judge. Move budget to terms that book. ## FAQ

### What is the best Google Ads keyword for a personal trainer?

"Personal trainer near me" and "personal trainer [city]" are the usual starting points because they show a clear intent to hire locally. No public source ranks keywords by profitability, so test a small set and keep the ones that book consultations in your account.

### How much does a personal trainer keyword cost per click?

The LocaliQ 2026 Health & Fitness benchmark is $6.17 per click. The figure covers a broad category and varies by city. For your area, use Keyword Planner, which shows the average cost to show on a keyword.

### Should personal trainers use broad match?

Not at the start. Google says broad match is the default and the widest type, and that Smart Bidding is critical with it. Begin with phrase match, build a negative list, and consider broad match later with conversion data.

### How many keywords does a new campaign need?

There is no published minimum. A small campaign with 10 to 20 phrase match keywords across three or four ad groups is easier to manage and analyse than hundreds of terms. This is a practical suggestion, not a benchmark.

### Is Google Ads better than Instagram for finding clients?

For people ready to hire now, search captures intent that social media does not. Many trainers use both. See our [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads comparison](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins) and the [gym Google Ads guide](/blog/google-ads-for-gyms) for the trade-offs.
