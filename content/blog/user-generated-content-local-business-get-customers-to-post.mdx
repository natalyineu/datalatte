You get customers to post about your business by making your space and product easy to photograph, asking at the moment they are happiest, and giving them a simple way to tag you. Most people will not post on their own, but a large share will if you invite them, so the work is in the invitation, not in begging.

This kind of content is called user-generated content, or UGC: photos, videos and reviews your customers create. It is useful because people trust other customers more than they trust your own ads, and it fills your feed without a photo shoot. Here is a practical system for a coffee shop, salon, groomer or studio.

## Make your business worth sharing

Customers share what looks good and feels good. Before you ask for posts, look at your space through a phone camera.

- **Create one photo spot.** A wall, neon sign, mural, plant corner or branded backdrop with good natural light. Keep it clean and uncluttered.
- **Design for the photo.** Latte art, a plated dessert, a fresh blowout, a groomed dog with a bandana, a finished class selfie. The product itself should be the star.
- **Make it visibly tag-friendly.** Put your handle and location name on a small sign, cup sleeve, receipt or mirror sticker.

If you have no photo-worthy moment today, add one small touch. A salon can offer a ring light at the mirror; a groomer can add a branded bandana; a coffee shop can use a distinctive cup or pastry.

## Ask at the right moment

Timing matters more than wording. The best moment to ask is right after a good experience: when the drink arrives, when the client sees their new cut, when the dog trots out looking fluffy, or when a class ends.

Keep the ask short and specific:

- "If you love it, we would love a tag. We are @yourhandle."
- "Want a quick photo? You can tag us, and we will share it if you like."
- "Happy with the cut? Photos of it help us a lot."

Train your team to say it naturally, not to every customer. A genuine, occasional ask feels warmer than a script on repeat.

## Make sharing effortless

Every extra step loses people. Reduce the friction:

1. **One handle, one hashtag.** Choose a short branded hashtag and use it consistently. Check that it is not already used for something unrelated.
2. **QR code to your profile.** A small sign near the counter or mirror that opens your Instagram or TikTok page saves people searching.
3. **Location tag.** Make sure your business has a proper location page so people can tag it with one tap.
4. **A reason to post.** Seasonal drinks, new colours, a themed day, a "dog of the month" or a class challenge give people a prompt.

## Rewards without breaking rules

A small thank-you can encourage posting, but handle it with care.

**For social posts and tags**, you can run a monthly feature or small prize draw. State the rules clearly (how to enter, who can win, when it ends) and follow the platform's promotion guidelines and your local contest laws.

**For reviews, be careful.** Google's policies do not allow offering incentives in exchange for reviews, and the same caution applies on other review sites. You can ask for a review; you should not pay or reward people for one. Our guide to [Google Business Profile reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) explains how to ask in a compliant way.

Rewards that work well for social content include a feature on your page, a shout-out in Stories, a small perk for the monthly "best tag", or simply recognition. Many customers like being featured more than they like a discount.

## Get permission before you repost

Never repost someone's photo without asking. A tag is not the same as permission to reuse an image in ads or on your website.

A simple process:

1. Comment or message: "This is great, may we share it on our page? We will credit you."
2. Keep the reply (screenshot or saved DM) as a record.
3. Credit the creator in the caption or tag them in the image.
4. Remove it promptly if they change their mind.

For anything beyond a normal repost, such as a paid ad, a website banner or a printed menu, get clearer written consent. For photos of people, and especially children, be extra careful. Pet photos are usually simpler, but still ask the owner.

## Put the content to work

UGC is only useful if you reuse it. Here is where it earns its keep:

- **Instagram or Facebook feed and Stories.** Share a customer photo once a week, with credit.
- **Highlights.** Save your best tagged posts to a "Customers" Highlight so new visitors see social proof.

## Track what is working

You do not need complex analytics. Once a month, check:

- How many posts tagged you or used your hashtag.
- Which prompt or offer led to the most posts.
- Whether new customers mention seeing a post when you ask how they found you.

Keep a note of what to repeat. If tagged posts jump after a seasonal menu or a staff prompt, do more of that.

## FAQ

**Q: Why will customers post for a local business?**

A: People share when they feel proud, delighted or want to recommend something to friends. A photo-worthy product, a friendly invitation and an easy tag are usually enough.

**Q: Can I offer a discount for a tag or post?**

A: For social posts, small perks are common, but follow platform rules and local contest laws, and be transparent. For Google reviews, do not offer incentives.

**Q: Do I need permission to repost a customer's photo?**

A: Yes. Ask first, keep a record of the reply, and credit the creator. For ads or print, get clearer written consent.

**Q: How long until I see results?**

A: It varies by foot traffic and how consistently you ask. Many businesses notice more tags within a few weeks of adding a sign and a staff prompt, but treat it as a habit, not a one-off campaign.
