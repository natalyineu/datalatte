A home service business gets more reviews by asking every customer, the same day the job ends, with a direct Google review link sent by text. Do not offer anything in return. BrightLocal's 2026 survey says 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, so reviews are a minimum bar for being chosen, not a bonus.

This guide covers a simple request process for plumbers, electricians, HVAC and other trades, the review rules from Google and the FTC, and a worked example. The BrightLocal survey is a US sample of 1,002 adults and does not break out home services, so treat the numbers as general local-business behavior.

## How Many Reviews and Which Rating Do Customers Expect?

BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and 41% always do. Some 68% require at least four stars, and 31% only consider businesses rated 4.5 or higher. Only 9% would use a business with five reviews or fewer.

Recency matters as well. 74% only consider reviews from the last three months, and 32% look for reviews from the last two weeks. A business with 60 reviews that stopped a year ago can look weaker than one with 30 recent ones. That makes steady requests more useful than a one-time push.

## When Should You Ask for the Review?

Ask when the work is finished and the customer has seen the result, ideally before the technician leaves or within the same day. This is a practical recommendation, not a published statistic. The customer remembers the visit, and the technician can mention the text so it does not arrive as a surprise.

Make the request easy. Google's help says you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code, and customers need a Google Account to post. A text with the link, sent from the job-management tool you already use, avoids asking the technician to type anything. If your tool cannot send texts, a printed card with a QR code in the invoice envelope works.

## What Should the Request Say?

Keep it short and name the person who did the work. A sample message, written for you to adapt:

> Hi Dana, thanks for choosing us today. If the repair went well, would you leave a short Google review? It takes about a minute: [link]

Do not mention the star rating you hope for. Do not send the link only to customers you think are happy, and do not offer a discount or gift for posting. ## What Do Google and the FTC Not Allow?

Google's policy says offering incentives such as free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited, and so is offering incentives to change or remove negative reviews. Google's help page does not address review gating directly, but it says a mix of positive and negative feedback often feels more trustworthy.

The US FTC rule on consumer reviews took effect on October 21, 2024. It bars fake reviews and says incentives cannot depend on a particular sentiment, including incentivized five-star reviews. It also bars intimidation or unfounded legal threats to remove reviews. The page does not state a dollar penalty, so ask a lawyer about exposure in your state. Outside the US, rules differ, see our [Europe review strategy guide](/blog/online-reviews-strategy-europe).

## How Should You Reply to Reviews?

Reply to every review, short and specific. Thank the customer, use the first name and mention the job type. For a negative review, reply once, stay factual, and offer an offline route to fix it. Do not argue or accuse anyone without proof, since the FTC rule restricts accusations a business knows are false. Our guide to [dealing with fake Google reviews](/blog/how-to-deal-with-fake-google-reviews) covers what to do when a review is not genuine.

## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a home service business need?
BrightLocal's 2026 survey says 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, so 20 is a sensible first target, with recent ones counting most.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?
No. Google says offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. The FTC rule also restricts incentives tied to a sentiment.

### Is it okay to ask only happy customers?
Google's help page does not address this directly. Asking everyone is simpler, safer and gives a more credible review profile.

### What if a customer leaves a bad review?
Reply once, politely and factually, and offer to fix the problem offline. Do not threaten the reviewer.

### How often should I ask?
After every completed job. BrightLocal found 74% of consumers only consider reviews from the last three months, so steady requests beat a single campaign.
