At $5.50 a click and a 6.2% enquiry rate, a pool service lead costs about $89. If you close one in three, a new weekly customer costs roughly $269 in ads. That sounds steep until you remember that a weekly customer pays you for months. The real question for a pool company is not "is a click cheap" but "what is a route stop worth, and can I fit another one?"

This guide is for pool service owners in the US, Canada, the UK and Australia (the climate varies, the maths does not). It covers what clicks tend to cost, how to structure a campaign, which keywords and negatives to start with, how to track calls, and a payback calculation you can redo with your own numbers.

Notice a quirk. At a $4.20 to $6.80 click and a 6.2% conversion rate, simple arithmetic gives $68 to $110 per lead, not $35 to $55. The cost-per-lead figure probably comes from a different mix of accounts. Do not plan on the lower number; plan on your own cost per click divided by your own conversion rate.

One Phoenix agency (Valley Marketing Group, August 2026) says a search like "pool cleaning service Phoenix" might run $8 to $20 with no guarantee of a call, and cites no data for it. That is an estimate, not a measurement. The two sources already disagree by a wide margin, which tells you the honest answer: test small and find your own number.

## Why Search Ads Suit Route-Based Work

- **Intent is clear.** Someone typing "green pool cleaning" or "weekly pool service" needs help now.
- **Recurring revenue.** One weekly customer can be worth many months of work, so you can sometimes justify a higher cost to win one.
- **Local targeting.** You can show ads only in the postcodes or zip codes your trucks reach, which matches route-based work.
- **Competition is real.** Lead-generation sites and franchises also buy these keywords. Expect to test.

## Set Up the Campaign

### 1. Know your numbers first

Before any spend, write down: price per month for a recurring customer, how many months they typically stay, your margin after chemicals, labour and driving, and the share of quotes you close. Our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business) shows how to turn these into a ceiling for cost per customer.

### 2. Structure: one campaign, tight ad groups

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match | Send them to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly service | "pool cleaning service", "weekly pool maintenance", [pool service near me] | Phrase + exact | Recurring plans page |
| Green pool | "green pool cleaning", "algae removal pool" | Phrase | One-off cleanup page |
| Opening / closing | "pool opening service", "pool closing service" | Phrase | Seasonal page |
| Repair | "pool pump repair", "pool heater repair" | Phrase | Only if you repair |
| Leak detection | "pool leak detection" | Phrase | Only if you offer it |

Google's match type page says exact match "gives you the most control over who sees your ad but reaches the fewest searches", and that broad match, the default, needs Smart Bidding. With a small budget, avoid broad until you have conversion data.

### 3. Negative keywords

Add these on day one: `diy`, `how to`, `tips`, `robot`, `cleaner for sale`, `supplies`, `store`, `chemicals`, `jobs`, `hiring`, `course`, `certification`, `salary`, `above ground pool kit`, `builder`, `construction` (unless you offer them). Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) explains campaign-level lists. Check the search terms report every week.

### 4. Location: your route, not your region

Target the areas you actually serve and exclude those you will not drive to. Google's location targeting help says the default and recommended setting, "presence or interest", also covers people interested in a place, and that you can target a radius or exclude regions. For a route business I recommend presence only (my advice, not Google's rule), because a person researching homes in your town is not a lead. Then check the location report for pockets that cost too much. Dense routes are more profitable than scattered ones.

### 5. Ads that qualify the click

State the service, the area and a reason to call: `Weekly Pool Service in [Town]`, `Licensed and Insured` (only if true), `Free Quote Within 24 Hours`. Say what a visit includes. Avoid "cheapest". Google's responsive search ads take up to 15 headlines of 30 characters and 4 descriptions of 90, so use the space.

Add a call asset and a location asset. Google's help says location assets can show your address, a map to your location or the distance to your business. Our [extensions guide](/blog/google-ads-extensions-guide) covers sitelinks and callouts. If most enquiries are phone calls, consider [call-only campaigns](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns-service-businesses), which suit businesses that can answer during advertised hours.

## Tracking Calls and Forms

Pool customers phone. Without call tracking you cannot tell which keyword produced the job.

- **Calls from the ad:** Google counts a call as a conversion only when it lasts longer than a minimum length you set. Pick a length that matches a real enquiry; 60 seconds is my suggestion.
- **Calls from the website:** Google's phone snippet replaces the number on your page with a Google forwarding number. Per Google's help, the default tag tracks only one number per page, and Tag Manager is not recommended if you use dynamic number insertion or third-party call tracking.
- **Forms:** track the thank-you page.

Walk through it in our [conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) and see [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business). Then log which leads became customers; Google cannot see that for you.

## Landing Page and Speed

Do not send clicks to your home page. Build one page per main service with a headline matching the ad, a tap-to-call number, a short quote form (address, pool size, equipment), how pricing works, your service area, real photos and reviews, and licence or insurance details where relevant. Load speed matters on a phone; see [Core Web Vitals for local sites](/blog/core-web-vitals-local-business-website).

After the click, speed wins. Many homeowners ring two or three companies. Answer in business hours, return missed calls within minutes, and offer a clear next step such as a quick quote by pool size or a site visit.

## The Math

A $1,000 monthly budget, three scenarios. Every row is an **assumption** for illustration. The CPC range is chosen from the $4.20 to $6.80 spread above, and the conversion rate brackets the 6.2% figure.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $6.80 | $5.50 | $4.20 |
| Clicks | 147 | 182 | 238 |
| Click to lead rate (assumption) | 4% | 6.2% | 8% |
| Leads | 5.9 | 11.3 | 19 |
| Cost per lead | $170 | $89 | $52.50 |
| Lead to customer rate (assumption) | 25% | 33% | 40% |
| New weekly customers | 1.5 | 3.7 | 7.6 |
| Ad cost per customer | $680 | $269 | $131 |
| Monthly fee (assumption) | $150 | $150 | $150 |
| Gross margin on fee (assumption) | 40% | 40% | 40% |
| Months of margin to repay ad cost | 11.3 | 4.5 | 2.2 |

How to read it: months to repay equals ad cost per customer divided by monthly margin ($60). The Base case repays in about four and a half months. If customers typically stay a year, that is profitable; if half leave within three months, it is not. In the Tough case, you need customers to stay over eleven months just to break even, so shrink the area, tighten the keywords or fix your close rate before adding budget.

Your break-even click price: maximum CPC = the most you will pay per customer x lead rate x close rate. At $300 per customer, 6.2% and 33%, that is $300 x 0.062 x 0.33 = $6.14. Above that, you lose money on average.

Also check capacity. Seven new stops a month only helps if your route has room, and a customer two towns away may cost more in fuel than the margin.

## Optimise Weekly

1. Read the search terms report and add negatives.
2. Pause keywords with spend of about twice your target cost per lead and no leads.
3. Cut locations that cost much more than average.
4. Test one new headline every two weeks.
5. Raise the budget by about 20% only where cost per customer is on target.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a pool service company spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official figure. At an assumed $5.50 click, $1,000 buys about 180 clicks. Use the break-even formula above to see what your numbers support.

**Q: Are Local Services Ads better than search ads for pool companies?**
A: They can be cheaper per lead according to agency figures, but eligibility varies by category and country. Check Google's current list for your area first.

**Q: Which keywords work best?**
A: Service plus location terms such as "pool cleaning service [town]", "weekly pool service" and "green pool cleaning". Seasonal terms like opening and closing tend to convert well in Webtonic's data (8.1%), but test on your own account.

**Q: How do I stop wasted clicks?**
A: Negative keywords, a tight service area, a weekly look at search terms, and a focused landing page.

**Q: How long until I know if ads work?**
A: Calls can come within days. Give it four to six weeks, my rule of thumb, because a small route business needs enough leads to judge.
