Google Ads can book more pool maintenance jobs when you target high-intent searches such as "pool cleaning service near me", limit ads to the neighbourhoods you actually serve, track phone calls and form fills, and answer every lead fast. Pool service is well suited to search ads because customers are recurring and the lifetime value of each account is high.

The catch is that costs per click can be high in competitive markets, and a few wasted clicks add up quickly. This guide covers how to structure campaigns, what to bid on, how to avoid common waste, and how to work out whether ads pay for themselves.

## Why search ads suit pool service

- **Intent is clear.** Someone typing "weekly pool service" or "green pool cleanup" needs help now.
- **Recurring revenue.** A single weekly or fortnightly customer can be worth many months of work, so you can sometimes justify paying more to win one.
- **Seasonality is predictable.** Demand often builds before and during warm months, with spikes after storms and when pools turn green.
- **Local targeting.** You can show ads only in the postcodes you serve, which matches route-based work.

Be realistic: pool service is a competitive category in many markets, and national lead-generation sites also buy the same keywords. Expect to test and refine.

## Step 1: Know your numbers before you spend

Work out these figures first, even roughly:

1. **Average monthly price** of a recurring service and how long customers typically stay.
2. **Average one-off job** value (green-to-clean, opening or closing, equipment repair).
3. **Margin** after chemicals, labour and driving time.
4. **Close rate** from lead to customer. If you win about one in three quotes, for example, you can estimate what each lead is worth.

Then calculate the most you can pay for a customer. A cost per lead that looks high may still be profitable if the customer stays a year or more. Our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business) explains how to build this up.

## Step 2: Set up conversion tracking

Without tracking, you cannot tell which keywords produce jobs. Set up:

- **Call tracking:** Google forwarding numbers for ads, or a call-tracking tool such as CallRail. See [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).
- **Form submissions:** tracked via Google Tag Manager or your website platform.
- **Call extensions and call reporting** within Google Ads, with a minimum call length counted as a conversion.
- **Offline follow-up:** note which leads became customers and, if possible, import that back into Google Ads later.

Our [Google Ads conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) walks through the steps.

## Step 3: Build the campaign structure

Keep it simple. For a small pool company, one Search campaign with a few tightly themed ad groups is a good start.

| Ad group | Example keywords (adapt to your services) | Landing page focus |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly or regular pool service | pool cleaning service, weekly pool maintenance, pool service near me | Recurring service plans |
| Green pool cleanup | green pool cleaning, algae removal pool | One-off cleanup and options |
| Pool opening or closing | pool opening service, pool closing service | Seasonal service |
| Pool repair and equipment | pool pump repair, pool heater repair | Repair services, if you offer them |
| Pool inspection or leak detection | pool leak detection | Only if you offer it |

Use phrase and exact match for most keywords, then review the search terms report weekly. Broad match can work with strong conversion data and smart bidding, but it can burn money early on.

### Negative keywords

Add negatives right away to avoid irrelevant clicks. Examples:

- DIY intent: "how to", "diy", "pool cleaning tips", "pool cleaner robot", "pool chemicals buy"
- Job seekers: "jobs", "hiring", "salary", "training", "course", "certification"
- Retail: "pool supplies", "pool store", "above ground pool kit"
- Unwanted services you do not offer, such as pool building if you only do maintenance

Check the search terms report and keep adding negatives.

## Step 4: Target your real service area

- Use location targeting by postcode, city or radius around your base, and choose "people in or regularly in" your locations to avoid wasted impressions from people just researching.
- Exclude areas you will not travel to.
- Check location reports for pockets that convert poorly or cost too much, and adjust bids or exclude them.
- Consider the density of your route. Jobs close together are more profitable than isolated ones.

## Step 5: Write ads that qualify and convince

Good ads state the service, area and a reason to call. Include:

- Service and location in the headline: "Weekly Pool Service in [Town]".
- A trust cue: licensed and insured (if true), years in business, review rating.
- A clear next step: "Get a free quote" or "Call today".
- Specifics: what is included in a visit, how quickly you respond.

Avoid unrealistic promises like "cheapest" or guarantees you cannot keep. Use call extensions, location extensions linked to your Google Business Profile, and sitelinks to key pages. Our [Google Ads extensions guide](/blog/google-ads-extensions-guide) covers these.

For heavily phone-driven businesses, consider [call-only campaigns](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns-service-businesses), where ads show a call button only. They suit businesses that can reliably answer during advertised hours.

## Step 6: Make the landing page do its job

Clicks are expensive, so do not send them to a generic homepage. Create a landing page for each main service that includes:

- A headline that matches the ad.
- A phone number that is tappable on mobile and a short quote form.
- What is included in service plans, and how pricing works (for example, depending on pool size, equipment and frequency).
- Service-area information.
- Reviews and photos of real work.
- Licence and insurance details where relevant.

The page should load quickly on a phone. See [Core Web Vitals for local business websites](/blog/core-web-vitals-local-business-website).

## Step 7: Choose a sensible budget and bidding

There is no universal figure. Cost per click depends on your city, season and competition, so run a small test and learn your actual numbers. As a rule of thumb, set a daily budget that can generate enough clicks to learn from in a few weeks, then adjust based on cost per lead and close rate.

For bidding, many small accounts start with Maximise Clicks with a bid cap or manual CPC to collect data, then move to Maximise Conversions or a target CPA once there is reliable conversion tracking. Google's recommendations vary, and automated strategies need enough conversions to work well. Our [Google Ads bidding strategies guide](/blog/google-ads-bidding-strategies) explains each.

## Step 8: Speed matters after the click

Many pool customers contact several companies. To convert leads:

- Answer calls during advertised hours, or use a call-handling service or AI receptionist for after-hours. See our [AI agents service](/services/ai-agents) for options.
- Return missed calls and form leads quickly. Within minutes is ideal.
- Offer a clear next step, such as a quick quote based on pool size, or a scheduled site visit.
- Follow up on quotes after a day or two.

## Step 9: Combine ads with organic visibility

Ads switch off when you stop paying. Build lasting visibility too:

- Optimise your Google Business Profile with service areas, photos of real pools you maintain, and regular reviews. See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).
- Create service-area pages and helpful content like opening checklists or green-pool troubleshooting.
- Read [local SEO for service businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses) for a broader plan.

## Weekly and monthly routine

**Weekly:** check search terms, add negatives, look at calls and forms, and pause anything wasting money.
**Monthly:** compare cost per lead and cost per customer, review location and device performance, test a new ad or landing page headline, and adjust budget with the season.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a pool service company spend on Google Ads?**
A: It varies with market size, competition and goals. Start with a modest test budget, measure cost per lead and close rate, then scale what pays back.

**Q: Are Google Local Services Ads better than search ads for pool companies?**
A: Local Services Ads (pay per lead, with a Google guarantee badge) are available for some categories and countries, and may suit pool services where offered. Check eligibility in your area and compare lead quality and cost with search ads.

**Q: Which keywords work best for pool service?**
A: Service-plus-location terms such as "pool cleaning service [town]", "weekly pool service" and "green pool cleaning" tend to show strong intent. Test, then keep what produces calls and customers.

**Q: How do I stop wasted clicks?**
A: Use negative keywords, restrict locations to your service area, review search terms weekly and send traffic to a focused landing page.
