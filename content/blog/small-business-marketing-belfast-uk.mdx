## How big is the Belfast market?

Belfast is Northern Ireland's largest local government district by both population and business count. NISRA's 2025 register shows 11,815 VAT and/or PAYE registered businesses in Belfast, 14.6% of the 81,135 across Northern Ireland, and Belfast had the largest absolute increase of any council, 370 businesses or 3.2% ([Department for the Economy](https://www.economy-ni.gov.uk/news/northern-ireland-inter-departmental-business-register-statistics-2025)).

The register leaves out small businesses below the VAT threshold that do not run PAYE, so it understates the true number. NISRA's 2023 estimate put Belfast at 350,500 residents with the youngest median age of any district, 36.4 years ([NISRA](https://www.nisra.gov.uk/news/population-growth-continues-across-all-northern-ireland-local-government-districts)).

## How do you target Belfast neighbourhoods?

Google Ads lets you target regions, cities or postal codes depending on the country, and radius targeting needs a radius of at least 1 km ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722043)). Belfast postcodes start with BT, so a business in the south of the city can target the BT7, BT9 and BT6 areas it actually serves.

Google recommends broad "Presence or interest" targeting for Search, while "Presence" suits advertisers who only want people physically in the area. Pick Presence if you do not want to pay for clicks from people merely researching a visit.

Name your area in the ad and the landing page, such as Cathedral Quarter or Lisburn Road, so people can see you are close.

## Why start with Google Business Profile?

It costs nothing and appears in map results. Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your ranking ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

Follow Google's naming rule: use your real-world name, with no area keywords added. Choose the most specific primary category, add only categories that describe the business, and list your services accurately ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)).

Ask for reviews with a link or QR code, reply to them, and never offer a reward for a review ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) walks through every field.

## What are the UK rules for email and SMS?

The ICO's guidance says individuals can be sent marketing emails or texts only if they have agreed. An exception covers existing customers who bought a similar product or service, but not prospects or bought-in lists. You must offer a simple opt-out when you collect details and in every message ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/)).

In practice, tick-box consent at booking is the safest start. Appointment reminders that are purely service messages are a different category, so check your own case with the ICO. For salons, see [email and SMS marketing for UK salons](/blog/email-sms-marketing-uk-salons).
