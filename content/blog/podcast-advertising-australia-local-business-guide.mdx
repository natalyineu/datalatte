Here is the number that explains why podcast ads are worth a look in Australia. According to IAB Australia's figures, advertisers spent A$134 million on podcasts in 2025, up 13.5% on the year before and up from A$81 million in 2022 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/australian-digital-audio-ad-spend-hits-339m-but-measurement-gaps-threaten-growth/), reporting IAB Australia). Money follows attention, and Edison Research's 2026 survey found that 55% of Australians aged 10 and over consume podcasts every month.

For a café, salon, gym or tradie, the useful question is not "is podcasting big?" but "can I reach people near my business, and can I tell if it worked?" This guide answers both using Australian data only. Where we cite a figure, it is Australian; we have left US numbers out. ## What the Australian Audience Data Shows

The Infinite Dial Australia 2026 is Edison Research's tenth Australian edition. It surveyed 1,600 Australians aged 10 and over, fieldwork 12 to 28 April 2026, and is published through Commercial Radio & Audio (CRA) ([CRA](https://cra.au/infinite-dial-2026)). The headline: 55% of Australians consume podcasts monthly, up from 52% in 2025.

Mediaweek's report on the survey adds detail (Mediaweek is a second-hand source here; check the full report for exact wording) ([Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/radio/infinite-dial-australia-2026-radio-podcasting-milestone)):

- Audio-only monthly podcast listening reached 50% for the first time, and 55% including video.
- Weekly consumption was 45%, described as level with the US.
- An estimated 12 million Australians consumed a podcast monthly.
- Among monthly podcast consumers, 23% listen daily and 46% at least weekly.
- 40% of weekly listeners consumed podcasts in the car.
- 40% watched video podcasts monthly, and of those, 89% used YouTube and 47% Spotify.

What that means for you:

- **The audience is mainstream, not niche.** About one in two Australians. Your customers are in it.
- **Daily habits create repetition.** Nearly a quarter of monthly listeners listen daily, so a campaign can reach the same person several times in a week without extra effort.
- **The car matters.** If 40% of weekly listeners play podcasts in the car, a business near a commute route (café, bakery, mechanic, vet) is a natural fit for audio.
- **Video is now part of the picture.** If you sponsor a show, ask whether the host also publishes video on YouTube, because your read may be seen as well as heard.

None of this tells you how many of those people live in your suburb. The survey is national. Your job is to buy the part of the audience that can actually visit.

## Spotify Is the Easiest Place to Start Locally

Spotify's self-serve Ad Studio lets you run audio ads to free-tier listeners, and Spotify's Australian help page says Australia supports targeting by country, state, city and postal code ([Spotify Ads help](https://ads.spotify.com/en-AU/help-center/location-targeting/)). That corrects a common claim in older Australian guides, including an earlier version of this article, that postcode targeting is unavailable.

A few facts from Spotify's own pages:

- **Charging:** Spotify's help centre says that for advertisers in Australia you are charged only when a listener completes your ad, not when it plays ([Spotify Ads help](https://ads.spotify.com/en-GB/help-center/amount-spent-different-than-budget/)). Spend can come in slightly under budget because some people skip.
- **Length:** Audio and podcast ads are capped at 30 seconds, and Spotify recommends about 65 words for a 30-second script ([Spotify audio ad specs](https://ads.spotify.com/en-AU/ad-specs/audio-ad-specs/)).
- **Minimum budget:** Spotify's help pages describe a minimum of 250 in your local currency, which suggests A$250, but no Australian page we reviewed stated the dollar figure outright. Check the minimum in the tool before you plan around it.
- **Cost per thousand:** Spotify says there are no standardised costs and ad spots use a bidding system. We found no verified Australian CPM, so earlier guides' CPM ranges have been removed and the math below treats CPM as an assumption.

For creative tips, frequency caps and platform comparisons, see [digital audio advertising in 2026](/blog/digital-audio-advertising-2026-spotify-pandora-podcast-ads). It is written around US pricing, so use it for method, not for Australian costs.

### How to Set the Radius

Because you can choose postcodes, do what a gym or café owner would do with a map:

1. Pull your last 50 customers and list their postcodes.
2. Take the top three to five.
3. Add one adjoining postcode only if your catchment is large.

A very narrow audience can limit delivery. Spotify warns that spend can fall short when the audience is small, so if a campaign under-delivers, widen the postcode list before raising the bid.

## Local Shows and Radio-Network Audio

Spotify is not the only route.

**Independent and local shows.** City, suburb, sport and industry podcasts sell sponsorships directly. Ask for downloads per episode for the last 30 days, listener locations by city and the host's current sponsors. A host-read endorsement from someone the audience trusts works best when the host actually uses your service, so offer a free product.

**Commercial radio networks.** CRA's Infinite Dial summary says commercial radio reaches four times more Australians weekly than ad-supported Spotify ([SSRS](https://ssrs.com/news/key-findings-from-the-2026-infinite-dial-australia/)). Note who is saying this: the survey is published through the commercial radio industry body, so read it as one view, not neutral. Mediaweek estimates about 4 million Australians are weekly ad-supported Spotify listeners, based on 34% of Australians being on the free tier. We found no published small-business rate cards for network streaming, so ask each network for a quote and a postcode or market breakdown. Do not trust any guide that quotes a CPM without a source.

## Where the Industry Money Is Going (and What It Tells You)

IAB Australia's Audio State of the Nation report, published 3 March 2026, surveyed 128 mostly agency buyers in November and December 2025 (via [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/australian-digital-audio-ad-spend-hits-339m-but-measurement-gaps-threaten-growth/)). Among its findings:

- 69% plan to increase podcast investment in 2026.
- 59% bought sponsorships or custom integrations in 2025.
- 72% bought audio programmatically in 2025.
- Top measurement problems included fragmented formats (29%), lack of standardised audience data (27%) and last-touch attribution undervaluing audio (25%).

Two lessons for a small business. First, sponsorship is a mainstream format, so a host-read local deal is not an odd choice. Second, even big agencies struggle to measure audio. You have an advantage they lack: a counter, a phone and a booking form. Use a spoken offer, a short URL and a "how did you hear about us" question rather than waiting for a perfect dashboard.

## Rules: Disclosure and Claims

Under section 2.7 of the AANA Code of Ethics, advertising must be clearly distinguishable as such. The AANA practice note says there is no absolute requirement for a particular label, but content should not be disguised, and where someone is paid or gifted products, the relationship must be clear to the audience ([AANA](https://aana.com.au/code-of-ethics/)). The practice note puts responsibility on the advertiser, and complaints go to Ad Standards.

In practice, brief the host to say the show is sponsored by you, or that you provided a gift or fee. A free coffee still counts. This is general information and not legal advice; check the current code and any rules for your industry (for example, health claims for clinics).

## Measuring a Small Test

Audio does not produce clicks. Use signals you can count:

- **A spoken phrase or code** tied to the show ("tell us you heard us on the podcast").
- **A short URL** with a UTM tag, so your analytics separate the visits; see [our UTM guide](/blog/utm-parameters-track-every-marketing-campaign).
- **A tracked phone number** if you take calls, per [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).
- **A staff tally** at the counter or in the booking notes.
- **Branded searches** for your business name in Google Search Console, as a supporting signal.

## The Math

Take a hypothetical local service business with a A$500 Spotify test, targeted to a few postcodes. Every figure below is an **assumption** for illustration, because we have no verified Australian CPM or response rate. Replace them with your own.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Test budget | A$500 | A$500 | A$500 |
| CPM (assumption) | A$45 | A$30 | A$20 |
| Completed ads delivered | 11,111 | 16,667 | 25,000 |
| Customer response rate (assumption) | 0.02% | 0.05% | 0.10% |
| New customers | 2.2 | 8.3 | 25 |
| Gross profit per new customer (assumption) | A$80 | A$80 | A$80 |
| Gross profit | A$178 | A$667 | A$2,000 |
| Net after ad cost | -A$322 | +A$167 | +A$1,500 |

The Strong column is a best case, not a plan. The base case needs about 8 customers from roughly 16,700 completed ads.

Your break-even is simple: ad budget divided by gross profit per new customer. At A$500 and A$80, that is 6.25, so about 7 new customers. If a typical customer comes back several times, gross profit per customer is higher and the bar is lower; if it is a one-off job, the bar stands. Decide that number before you spend.

## Seasonality: Plan Around Your Calendar

Australian seasons run opposite to the northern hemisphere, so US advice about a "January fitness rush" or a "November holiday push" needs adjusting to your own calendar. Summer here is December to February, school holidays differ by state, and trade seasons vary by industry. We have no verified Australian listening-by-month data, so base timing on your own booking and sales history. Start a test 3 to 4 weeks before your busiest period so the campaign has run before demand peaks.

## Do This Today

1. Pull the postcodes of your last 50 customers and choose the three to five that matter most.
2. Work out your break-even: budget divided by gross profit per new customer.
3. Open Spotify Ad Studio, check the current minimum and postcode targeting, and draft a 65-word script with one offer.
4. Set up the counting: a spoken phrase, a short URL with a UTM tag and a tally at the till or booking form.
5. Email two local podcast hosts for last-30-day downloads, listener locations and sponsor lists, and ask what disclosure wording they use.

## FAQ

**Q: How many Australians listen to podcasts?**
A: Edison Research's Infinite Dial Australia 2026, fielded in April 2026 with 1,600 Australians aged 10 and over, found 55% consume podcasts monthly. Mediaweek's report on the survey puts audio-only monthly listening at 50% and weekly consumption at 45%.

**Q: Can I target a suburb on Spotify in Australia?**
A: Spotify's Australian help page says you can target by country, state, city and postal code. Use postcodes near your business, and widen them if delivery is low.

**Q: How much does podcast advertising cost in Australia?**
A: There is no verified public average. Spotify says it uses bidding rather than standard prices, and network and local-show prices are negotiated. Ask for a quote with audience size and location, convert it into a cost per thousand and use the break-even math above.

**Q: Do I need to disclose a sponsored podcast read?**
A: The AANA Code of Ethics requires advertising to be clearly distinguishable as advertising. Responsibility sits with the advertiser, so agree wording with the host. This is not legal advice.

**Q: Is podcast advertising worth it compared with Google Ads?**
A: They do different jobs. Google Ads catches people already searching; audio reaches people before they search. A small business should usually get search and its Google Business Profile in order first.
