Good website copy for a local business names what you do, where you do it and the next step on the first screen, then backs that up with short, plain sections. Keep each paragraph to one idea. Third-party benchmarks cited by [Adwave's website copy guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-write-website-copy-that-converts-visitors-into-customers) suggest first-person calls to action can outperform second-person ones by 25% to 90%, and that landing pages written at a 5th to 7th grade reading level convert at 11.1%, compared with 5.3% for college-level copy. These are benchmarks, not promises for your site, so change one thing at a time and measure the result.

## Which Words Should the Homepage Open With?

The first screen should state three things: what you do, the area you serve and the action you want. Adwave's guide cites a benchmark that 51% of consumers feel a brand relationship starts when the brand understands them and their desires, so open with your customer's need, not your history ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-write-website-copy-that-converts-visitors-into-customers), secondary).

A grooming business could write "Dog grooming in Maple Heights. Book online or call 555-0100." That line is an assumption for illustration, not a real business. Swap in your own town, service and action.

Outcome-led headlines also work. Adwave's sample subject line "Get Broadcast-Quality Ads Without the $100K Production Bill" pairs a benefit with the cost it avoids ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/email-subject-lines-that-actually-get-opened-formulas-and-examples), secondary). A café might adapt the pattern to "Espresso and pastries before your commute, open 6am to 2pm" (assumption). Attention is short: Adwave's advertising guide says the audience has about three seconds before someone scrolls past or changes the channel ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-write-advertising-copy), secondary).

## How Plain Should the Wording Be?

Plain wording is measurable here. The Genesys Growth roundup, cited by Adwave, reports that copy at a 5th to 7th grade reading level converts at 11.1%, against 5.3% for copy at a college reading level ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-write-website-copy-that-converts-visitors-into-customers), secondary). Plain means short sentences, common words and concrete nouns.

Practical edits:

- Replace "award-winning, best-in-class service" with what you offer, how long it takes and what it costs, if you can say.
- Cut intro sentences that repeat the heading.
- Use headings that match questions customers actually ask.
- Put prices, hours and your phone number where people can find them fast.

Only publish prices you will honour. A wrong price costs more trust than a vague one.

## What Does Before-and-After Copy Look Like?

All examples below are assumptions for illustration, not real businesses or results.

| Business | Weak copy | Better copy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Café | "We craft exceptional coffee experiences." | "Espresso, pastries and Wi-Fi, open 7am to 4pm at 12 Elm Street." |
| Hair salon | "Transform your look with our passionate stylists." | "Cuts from $45, colour consultations free. Book online in two minutes." |
| Pet groomer | "Premium pampering for your furry family." | "Full groom for dogs under 40 lb, about 2 hours. Pickup 8am to 5pm." |
| Yoga studio | "Find your inner peace." | "Beginner classes Monday and Thursday at 6pm. First class free." |

Each better version gives one fact a customer can act on. The weak versions describe the business; the better ones describe the visit.

## Does Customer Proof Improve Conversion?

Adwave's summary of WordStream's conversion research reports that user-generated content on 1,200 websites produced a 3.2% conversion rate, and that conversions rose by a further 3.8% when visitors engaged with that content ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-write-website-copy-that-converts-visitors-into-customers), secondary). Treat these as broad averages across sites, not a forecast for a local booking page.

Use only reviews that real customers wrote, and show them with the customer's permission. Adwave's blog guide lists four places original value usually comes from: customer work experience, a clearer process, stronger examples and a point of view shaped by business results ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-write-a-blog-post-that-ranks-on-google), secondary). A groomer can write about the process: bath, dry, trim, and the time each step takes.

## How Do You Handle Claims in Regulated Fields?

Adwave advises that in healthcare, legal, finance and real estate, a human reviewer should check the final copy and scrutinize any personal or outcome claim ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/website-microcopy-that-converts), secondary). The same caution helps any trade. Phrases such as "guaranteed results" or "cures anxiety" need evidence you can show, or they should come out.

If you draft copy with AI, treat the output as a first draft. The [AI vs human marketing comparison for local business](/blog/ai-vs-human-marketing-local-business-honest-comparison) explains where human review matters most, and [how to use AI to write ad copy](/blog/how-to-use-ai-to-write-ad-copy-local-business) covers the same checks for paid ads.

## What Should the Call to Action Say?

Name the action and the result: "Book a grooming slot," "Call for a quote," "Get directions." Place one main action at the top and repeat it at the bottom. Avoid vague labels such as "Submit" or "Click here," because they tell the visitor nothing about what happens next.

One reported experiment, cited by Adwave from invespcro.com, attributed a 48% increase in form fills to a copy change at a 98.5% confidence level ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/website-microcopy-that-converts), secondary). That is a single test on a different business, so use it as a reason to test, not as an expected result. For ideas to test, the [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) guide can help you draft variants.

## How Much Could a Copy Change Be Worth?

This worked example uses assumption inputs. Replace them with your own numbers before you decide anything.

Inputs:

- Visitors to your booking page per month: 300 (assumption)
- Conversion rate at college-level copy: 5.3% (benchmark, secondary)
- Conversion rate at plain copy: 11.1% (benchmark, secondary)
- Average booking value: $60 (assumption)

| Step | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bookings with current copy | 300 × 5.3% | 15.9 per month |
| Bookings with plain copy | 300 × 11.1% | 33.3 per month |
| Extra bookings | 33.3 − 15.9 | 17.4 per month |
| Extra revenue | 17.4 × $60 | $1,044 per month |
| If only half the gap closes | $1,044 ÷ 2 | about $522 per month |

The benchmark averages across many pages, so your result could be higher or lower. Measure bookings for four weeks before and after the change to see your own figure.

## What Should You Check Before Publishing?

1. Rewrite the headline so it names the service, the place and the action.
2. Delete every sentence that carries no fact.
3. Add prices or price ranges where you can honour them.
4. Put the address, phone number and hours in plain text.
5. Confirm that each page has a unique title and description.
6. Read the page aloud and remove anything you would not say to a customer.

## FAQ

### How long should website copy be?
No benchmark in these sources sets a universal length. Cut each page until it answers its question and stops, and make sure the first screen carries the offer.

### Should I write calls to action in the first person?
Benchmarks cited by Adwave say first-person CTAs can outperform second-person ones by 25% to 90% in tests. Try "Book my slot" against "Book your slot" and compare bookings over a few weeks.

### Do I need keywords in my copy?
Use the words customers actually type for your service and area, placed where they read naturally. Forcing them into every heading makes plain copy harder to read.

### Should I show customer reviews on my site?
Yes, if real customers wrote them and agreed to be quoted. Broad research cited by Adwave links user-generated content with higher conversion, but the effect varies by site.

### Who should check claims on my website?
In healthcare, legal, finance and real estate, a human reviewer should check the final copy and scrutinize any personal or outcome claim. Other trades benefit from the same check.
