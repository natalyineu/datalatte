Good website copy for a local business says what you do, where you do it and what to do next within the first screen, then backs it up with short, plain sections. Nielsen Norman Group found that 79% of test users always scanned new pages and only 16% read word by word, so write for scanning: one idea per paragraph, clear headings, no hype.

## What Should Your Homepage Say First?

State three things at the top: what you do, the area you serve and the action you want. A grooming business might write "Dog grooming in Maple Heights. Book online or call 555-0100." The main heading should also work as the page title in search, since Google says a good title is unique to the page, clear and concise and accurately describes the page.

That example is an **assumption**, not a real business. Replace the town, service and action with yours. If you serve a defined area, say so in plain words instead of leaving visitors to guess.

## How Do You Write for People Who Scan?

Nielsen Norman Group's guidance is to use meaningful subheadings, bulleted lists and highlighted keywords, keep one idea per paragraph, and start with the conclusion. It also says users disliked "marketese": promotional language imposes a cognitive burden. Its tested version with objective language scored 27% better on usability than the promotional control.

Practical edits:

- Replace "award-winning, best-in-class service" with a fact: what you offer, how long it takes, what it costs if you can say.
- Cut intro paragraphs that repeat the heading.
- Use headings that match questions customers ask.
- Put prices, hours and the phone number where people can find them fast.

## How Do You Make the Page Findable by Search?

Write for people first. Google's guidance defines people-first content as content created primarily for people, not to manipulate search rankings, and it asks you to show who wrote it and demonstrate first-hand expertise.

For search basics, Google's starter guide says to use a unique title and meta description per page, put useful words in URLs, write descriptive link text and add short, accurate alt text to images. It adds that some changes take effect in hours and others take months, so wait a few weeks before judging an edit.

Local pages should show your name, address, phone number and hours as text. Google's LocalBusiness markup treats the address as required, and lists a phone number and opening hours among the details to include. Keep these identical to your Google profile; see the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## What Should the Call to Action Say?

Name the action and the result: "Book a grooming slot," "Call for a quote," "Get directions." Place one main action at the top and repeat it at the bottom of the page. Avoid "Submit" and "Click here."

No source here ties a specific button wording to conversion lift, so test one change at a time and compare calls, bookings or direction requests before and after. Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) cover layout and tracking.

## A Checklist You Can Use Today

1. Rewrite your headline to include service, place and action.
2. Cut every sentence that has no fact in it.
3. Add prices or price ranges where you can.
4. Put address, phone and hours in text.
5. Check that each page has a unique title and description.
6. Read it aloud and remove anything you would not say to a customer.

Next, build content that answers customer questions; see [how to start a blog for your local business](/blog/how-to-start-blog-local-business-step-by-step).

## FAQ

### How long should website copy be?
No source gives a universal length. Nielsen Norman Group found that a version with about half the word count of conventional text was easier to use. Cut until each page answers its question and stops.

### Should I use my own voice or sound formal?
Use plain, objective wording. NN/g tested objective language against promotional language and found it more usable.

### Do I need keywords in my copy?
Use the words customers use for your service and town, naturally. Google warns against content made mainly to attract search visits.

### How often should I update my website text?
When hours, prices or services change. Wrong details cost customers, and Google's local markup treats address, hours and phone as core information.

### Should I hire a copywriter?
Only if you cannot state your offer plainly. Start with the checklist above.
