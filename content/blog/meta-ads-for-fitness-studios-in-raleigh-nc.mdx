At that price a $1,200 monthly budget buys about 44 leads and, with show-up and join rates we will label as assumptions, roughly six new members. If Raleigh costs twice the benchmark (also an assumption, because no published figure isolates the city), the same budget buys about three. Which of those you live in depends on your offer and your follow-up more than on the algorithm.

This guide is for studio and gym owners in Raleigh, North Carolina. ## The North Carolina Rules That Touch Your Offer

This is general information from the statute text, not legal advice. Ask a lawyer before you finalize contract wording.

North Carolina has a statute on prepaid entertainment contracts, G.S. 66-118 through 66-124, whose definition of a covered contract includes health or athletic club services and martial arts training where the buyer pays before receiving the service. Based on the statute text on the General Assembly website, a covered contract:

- must be in writing, fully completed, dated and signed, with a copy given to the buyer (G.S. 66-119);
- must carry, near the signature line in bold type of at least 10 points, a statement that the buyer may cancel until midnight of the third business day after the contract date (G.S. 66-119 and 66-121);
- must refund the buyer's payments within 30 days of a valid cancellation (G.S. 66-122);
- cannot be sold if the combined duration of the buyer's contracts with you exceeds three years, or if performance does not begin within 180 days (G.S. 66-123);
- must be backed by a surety bond (or an escrow arrangement) of at least $10,000 or the total of outstanding liabilities to buyers, whichever is greater (G.S. 66-124).

The statute also counts months described as "free" or "bonus" toward the contract duration (G.S. 66-118), and it requires refunds of at least 90% of the unused pro rata cost in certain cases such as death, disability, or a move of more than a set distance (G.S. 66-120). Whether a particular month-to-month membership with a free trial counts as "prepaid" depends on how it is structured, which is a question for your lawyer. For your ad, the practical rule is to describe the offer accurately, state what happens when the trial ends, and show contract terms before the person pays.

### Meta's advertising policy

Meta's Health and wellness policy page says ads for dietary, health or weight loss products and services must target people aged 18 or older, but it exempts fitness services and health clubs from that age restriction, as they are marketed for general well-being. It prohibits promises of specific outcomes within a set time frame without disclaimers in a health or weight loss context, and statements that attack a person's appearance. "Lose 10 pounds in 30 days" is a policy risk. "First class free, beginners welcome" is not. Check the live policy before you run any claim.

## Set Up the Campaign

These steps are recommendations, not Meta rules.

1. **Offer.** One clear, low-commitment offer: a free class, a first-week pass or an intro price. Put end-of-trial terms in writing on the form and landing page.
2. **Objective.** Start with a lead campaign using an instant form, or send traffic to a landing page with a short form. Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) cover the layout.
3. **Location.** Use the ZIP codes of your last 50 sign-ups, then set a radius or drive-time area around the studio. Because nearly everyone drives, think in minutes from your door.
4. **Creative.** Use real photos or short video of your coaches, your room and a class in progress. Avoid appearance-based messaging.
5. **Follow-up.** Call or text new leads within minutes. That is a recommendation, not a measured figure. Our [Facebook ads for gyms and fitness studios guide](/blog/facebook-ads-for-gyms-fitness-studios-complete-guide) goes through the funnel in detail.
6. **Tracking.** Install the Meta Pixel, set a Lead event for the form and log which leads become members.

## The Math

A $1,200 monthly lead campaign. Rows marked assumption are illustrations, not benchmarks.

| | LocaliQ benchmark | 1.5x (assumption) | 2x (assumption) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $1,200 | $1,200 | $1,200 |
| Cost per lead | $27.11 | $40.67 | $54.22 |
| Leads | 44 | 29.5 | 22 |
| Lead to attended trial (assumption) | 50% | 50% | 50% |
| Attended trial to member (assumption) | 30% | 30% | 30% |
| New members | 6.6 | 4.4 | 3.3 |
| Ad cost per new member | $181 | $271 | $361 |
| Monthly fee (assumption) | $120 | $120 | $120 |
| Months of fees to pay back the ad cost | 1.5 | 2.3 | 3.0 |

How to read it: the last row divides ad cost per member by the monthly fee. It uses revenue, not profit, so real payback is slower. The point is not these exact figures. The most you can pay per lead equals your allowable cost per member multiplied by your show-up rate and your join rate. At a $300 ceiling per member, 50% show-up and 30% joining, that is $300 x 0.5 x 0.3 = $45 per lead.

Check how long members stay before you scale. A member who leaves after two months loses money in the 1.5x and 2x columns, and you cannot lean on long contracts to cover it: North Carolina caps the combined duration of prepaid contracts with one buyer at three years.

## Do This Today

1. Pull your last 50 sign-ups and list their ZIP codes. Compare them with the table above and choose two or three for your first campaign.
2. Calculate your allowable cost per member: monthly fee times the months a typical member stays, times the share of that revenue you will spend on acquiring them.
3. Read G.S. 66-118 through 66-124 against your own contract and trial terms, and ask a lawyer to check the bond and cancellation wording.
4. Build one campaign, one offer and one form, with real photos and no weight-loss or appearance claims.
5. Decide who answers new leads within 15 minutes, and log each lead's outcome (attended, joined, not interested) for four weeks before judging.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do Meta ads cost for a fitness studio in Raleigh?**
A: There is no published Raleigh benchmark. LocaliQ's national figures for Health & Fitness are $0.64 per click on traffic campaigns and $27.11 per lead on lead campaigns. Treat your own first month as the real benchmark.

**Q: Do North Carolina contract rules apply to my free trial?**
A: The prepaid entertainment contract statute covers health and athletic club contracts where the buyer pays before receiving services, and includes a three-business-day cancellation right. Whether your trial and membership structure is covered depends on the details, so ask a lawyer.

**Q: Can I target a specific neighborhood in Raleigh?**
A: Yes, through location settings, though Meta offers radius and area options rather than exact neighborhood boundaries. Use your member ZIP codes to choose areas, then compare lead quality by area.

**Q: Facebook ads or Google Ads for a studio?**
A: They reach different moments. Google reaches people searching now; Meta can reach people who might join after seeing an offer. Many studios start with search and add Meta once their follow-up works.

**Q: How long before I know if it works?**
A: Leads can arrive within days, but plan on four to six weeks of outcomes before judging, so you can see attended trials and sign-ups. That is a rule of thumb, not a Meta figure.
