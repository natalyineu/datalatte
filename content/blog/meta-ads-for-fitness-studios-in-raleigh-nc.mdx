## Which Placements Fit a Studio Offer?

Start with the placement where your offer makes sense visually. A class in progress and a coach's introduction usually work well in short video, which suits Instagram Reels and Facebook feed placements. A promotion with a clear price and a booking button may suit a standard image with a short form.

Snapchat is worth testing if your members skew younger, since its share among US adults is lower than Facebook's. The [Snapchat ads guide for gyms and fitness studios](/blog/snapchat-ads-for-gyms-and-fitness-studios) explains where it fits and where it does not.

Spreading a small budget across every placement at once makes results hard to read. One placement with one offer gives a result you can interpret. Once you know which offer produces attended trials, you can widen the audience or add placements.

## How Do You Test an Offer Without Wasting Budget?

Meta's guidance is to use a proper A/B test rather than switching ad sets on and off by hand. Its help page says informal testing can lead to overlapping audiences and unreliable results, while an A/B test splits the audience so that nobody sees both versions ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669)).

Meta also says to use the same budget for both versions so the comparison is fair. Duplicating a campaign copies its budget, which helps keep the test even. Choose one variable to change, such as the offer, and keep the image, audience and budget the same.

1. Write a hypothesis, for example: a free first class will produce more attended trials than a discounted monthly price.
2. Duplicate one campaign in Ads Manager, or use the Experiments tool, and change only the offer.
3. Keep the budget identical for both versions.
4. Run the test for at least two weeks before judging it.
5. Compare cost per lead and, more importantly, attended trials and new members.

To set a ceiling, work backward. If you can afford $300 in acquisition cost per member (assumption), and 50% of leads attend a trial while 30% of those join, the most you can pay per lead is $300 x 0.5 x 0.3, which is $45. Anything above that loses money on this model.

Check how long members stay before raising the budget. A member who cancels after two months changes the payback sharply, so track retention alongside lead cost.

## How Should Google Fit Alongside Meta?

Google reaches people who are already searching, while Meta reaches people who may not have started looking yet. Google's help page describes Display campaigns as reaching websites, apps and Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)). That reach is broad, so it suits awareness more than direct booking.

For a studio, a sensible order is to set up search for people looking for classes, then add Meta to reach people who have not searched yet. ## What Should Follow-Up Look Like?

Much of the value from a lead campaign depends on what happens in the first hours. A lead who waits a day may book with another studio. Decide before launch who will answer new leads, how fast they will respond, and what they will say.

A simple process works well. Send an instant confirmation, call or text within a set window, and book a trial class directly. If lead volume exceeds what staff can handle, an automated first reply can hold the lead until a person follows up. The [AI chatbot guide for fitness studios](/blog/ai-chatbot-for-fitness-studios) covers that approach.

Log each lead's outcome as attended, joined, not interested or no answer. Without that log, you cannot tell whether a cheap lead is a good lead. Review the log weekly so that slow follow-up shows up in the numbers before it costs a month of budget.

## FAQ

### How much should a Raleigh fitness studio spend on Meta ads?

No source here gives a Raleigh figure. Start with a budget you can sustain for at least a month, then use your own cost per lead and conversion rates to decide whether to increase it. The worked example above shows how the numbers interact.

### Is Facebook or Instagram better for a studio?

It depends on the offer. Pew's 2026 figures show 70% of US adults use Facebook and 51% use Instagram, so both reach large audiences. Test the placement that matches your creative before assuming either one is better.

### How long should an A/B test run?

Meta's guidance is to use a proper A/B test with equal budgets and split audiences. This article suggests at least two weeks as a practical minimum, but the right length depends on how many leads each version generates.

### Do I need Google Ads as well as Meta?

Not necessarily. Google captures people already searching, while Meta reaches people who may not have started looking. Many studios start with search, then add Meta once their follow-up process works.

### What should I check before running a fitness ad?

Read the current advertising policies for your category, and make sure the offer, trial terms and cancellation rules are clear on the form and landing page. Have a lawyer review contract wording before selling prepaid memberships.
