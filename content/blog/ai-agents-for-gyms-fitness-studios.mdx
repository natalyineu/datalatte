PushPress lists its Grow add-on, which covers CRM, automated workflows, and email and text messaging, at $329 a month. At an assumed $55 monthly membership, that is six member-months of dues, or about one extra member who stays six months. That is the right way to judge "AI agents" in a gym: not as magic, but as a monthly cost that must be repaid by members you win or keep. This guide shows where an agent can plausibly do that, what your gym software may already include, and how to test one before you commit.

It is written for gym and studio owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Software prices come from vendor pages we opened in October 2026 and change often. We quote no no-show or retention percentages, because published, traceable figures for gyms are thin.

## Agent, Chatbot or Plain Automation?

Three different things get sold under one label:

- **Automation** follows fixed rules: "text 24 hours before the class". Most gym software does this already.
- **A chatbot** answers questions from a script or a knowledge base: hours, prices, the trial offer. See our [AI chatbot for fitness studios](/blog/ai-chatbot-for-fitness-studios) guide.
- **An agent** reads a message, decides what to do and does it using tools: it checks the schedule, offers real open trial slots and books one, or hands over to a person. We explain the difference in [what AI agents are and how they work](/blog/ai-agents-for-local-business-what-they-are-and-how-they-work).

An agent is the most flexible and the riskiest because it acts. Give it the permissions of a new front-desk hire on day one: look, then suggest, then book simple things, and never the right to promise anything you did not write down.

## What Your Gym Software Already Includes

Check this before buying anything. Here is what two vendors publish, from pages we opened.

**Mindbody.** The [pricing page](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/business/pricing) lists Starter from $79 a month per location, with unlimited email booking confirmations and reminders but no email or text marketing. Accelerate adds unlimited text and email confirmations and reminders. Ultimate adds automated email and text campaigns, a lead management dashboard and "24/7 AI front desk support". The page describes an **AI Concierge** as a 24/7 front desk assistant that answers client questions and responds to missed calls: it is not in Starter, is an add-on for Accelerate (no price listed), and is included in Ultimate. Accelerate and Ultimate prices are "let's talk".

**PushPress.** The [pricing page](https://www.pushpress.com/pricing) lists Free at $0, Pro at $159 a month and Max at $229, with unlimited members, leads and staff on all three. It says AI Support is unlimited on every plan and resolves 60% of conversations with a 95% satisfaction score (the vendor's own claim). An AI Assistant that answers questions about members, attendance, leads, revenue and retention is described, but the page does not say which plans include it. Automated emails and texts, lead capture forms and landing pages sit under Grow at $329 a month, and the page does not make clear which plan includes what, so confirm with sales.

**Glofox and Wodify.** Glofox shows a quote form rather than prices, and Wodify's current plans and prices should be confirmed with the vendor, so ask both for a written quote and a feature list.

The lesson is that reminders and basic follow-up are often in the plan, while the AI front desk and lead tools sit in higher tiers. Know which tier you are on before you pay for a separate tool.

## Five Jobs an Agent Can Do for a Gym

These are possibilities, not guarantees.

### 1. Answer new enquiries at any hour

A person types "any beginner classes this week?" at 10pm. An agent can read your timetable, offer two real trial slots and book one. The failure case is the same enquiry going unanswered until noon, when they have messaged two other gyms. Our [Google Ads guide for gyms](/blog/google-ads-for-gyms) makes the point that cost per member is mostly decided by how fast you reply.

### 2. Follow up trials and tours

Confirm instantly, remind before the visit, and ask afterwards whether they want to start. The wording should be yours, short and human. A phone or text system with a human handoff beats a fancy bot with no exit.

### 3. Fill cancelled spots

When a member cancels a small-group class, an agent can offer the spot to people on the waitlist. This works when your software exposes waitlists and bookings to the tool, so ask your vendor before assuming.

### 4. Nudge members who have gone quiet

Pull members who have not checked in for a threshold you choose, such as 14 days, and send a friendly message from a named coach. The threshold is your call, not an industry rule. See [email marketing for fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-for-fitness-studios-fill-classes-reduce-churn) for sequence ideas.

### 5. Ask for reviews the allowed way

Google's [contributed content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) says merchants may not offer incentives for reviews, such as payment, discounts or free goods or services, and may not selectively solicit positive reviews. It allows encouraging genuine reviews without incentives or influencing the rating. So the agent sends everyone the same link and offers nothing in return. Ask a free-trial member after their first month, not as a condition of a perk.

## What to Keep Human

- **Cancellations and freezes.** Often tied to injury, money or a move. An automated retention script in that moment costs more trust than it saves.
- **Injury, pregnancy and health questions.** Route these to a qualified coach.
- **Complaints and negative reviews.** Reply personally.
- **Prices outside your standard plans.** Corporate rates, family discounts and special deals need your sign-off.
- **Programming for rehab or competitive athletes.**

## Guardrails to Write Before You Switch It On

1. **A human exit.** Meta's developer documentation for Instagram messaging says an automated experience can escalate to a human agent using a fallback intent, a keyword or quick replies, with the thread handed to an inbox where a person replies. Whatever tool you use, make sure "talk to a person" works and someone actually sees it. See Meta's [human agent escalation page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/instagram-messaging/features/human-agent-escalation.md).
2. **Say it is automated.** Open with "I'm the studio's booking assistant". It is good practice, and you should check your local rules on disclosing automated chat.
3. **Only facts you gave it.** Write a one-page document with hours, prices, trial terms, cancellation policy and class descriptions. Re-check what the agent says every month.
4. **Consent for marketing messages.** The UK ICO's guidance says you must not send marketing emails or texts to individuals without specific consent, with a narrow "soft opt-in" for existing customers who bought something similar, provided you offered a simple opt-out when you collected their details and in every message. It does not cover prospects. US, Canadian and Australian rules differ and depend on the type of message, so ask your SMS provider or a lawyer; we have not verified them here. Keep reminders strictly about the booking, record consent when you take the number, and honour opt-outs.
5. **Small permissions first.** Start in draft mode, where the tool writes and you press send.

## A Safe Order of Operations

1. **Rules first.** Switch on confirmations, reminders and waitlist messages in your existing software.
2. **Draft mode second.** Let AI write replies to enquiries for two weeks and watch which questions repeat.
3. **Agent last.** Let it book trials only, only into slots you marked bookable, with a handoff for everything else.

## The Math

Will a tool repay a $329 monthly cost? The price is the only verified input, and it is for PushPress's Grow bundle, not a standalone agent. **Everything else is an assumption** for a single-location studio; replace it with your numbers.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New leads per month (assumption) | 40 | 40 | 40 |
| Share contacted too slowly today (assumption) | 40% | 40% | 40% |
| Of those, share the tool turns into a booked trial (assumption) | 5% | 15% | 25% |
| Extra trials | 0.8 | 2.4 | 4.0 |
| Trial to member rate (assumption) | 30% | 30% | 30% |
| Extra members won | 0.24 | 0.72 | 1.2 |
| Quiet members messaged per month (assumption) | 15 | 15 | 15 |
| Share who return and stay (assumption) | 5% | 10% | 20% |
| Members kept | 0.75 | 1.5 | 3.0 |
| Total members won or kept | 0.99 | 2.22 | 4.2 |
| Extra months of dues each (assumption) | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| Monthly fee (assumption) | $55 | $55 | $55 |
| Extra revenue | $327 | $733 | $1,386 |
| Tool cost (PushPress Grow listed price) | $329 | $329 | $329 |
| Extra revenue minus tool cost | -$2 | +$404 | +$1,057 |

Read it with care. It counts revenue, not profit, and the Strong column is a stretch. Rebooking nudges and reminders may already be in your software, so credit the tool only with what the plain version cannot do. The break-even is $329 divided by ($55 x 6 months), about 1.0 member won or kept. Run it for a month, count only the members who came through the tool, and keep it if the count clears that line.

## Do This Today

1. Count last month's enquiries that arrived outside opening hours or waited more than a few hours for a reply.
2. Open your gym software's pricing page and list what is already included: reminders, waitlists, texts, lead tools, AI features.
3. Write a one-page "truths" document: hours, plans and prices, trial terms, cancellation rules, class descriptions.
4. Write your never-automate list (cancellations, injuries, complaints, special prices) and name who gets handoff alerts and how fast they answer.
5. If you trial an agent, run it in draft mode for two weeks, then let it book trials only, and count agent-booked members against the break-even.

## FAQ

**Q: What can an AI agent do for a gym that my booking software cannot?**
A: Mostly hold a conversation. Your software sends fixed reminders; an agent can read a free-text question, check the schedule and offer a slot. If your enquiries are simple, rules may be enough.

**Q: How much does it cost?**
A: Mindbody lists Starter from $79 a month per location and includes its AI Concierge in Ultimate, with no price listed. PushPress lists Free, $159 and $229 plans, with Grow at $329. Standalone tools have their own pricing, so check them.

**Q: Will it make my gym feel impersonal?**
A: Only if it handles the wrong things. Let it do logistics such as slots, waitlists and confirmations. Keep cancellations, injuries and your longest-standing members with a person.

**Q: Can it ask for Google reviews automatically?**
A: Yes, if everyone gets the same genuine request and nothing is offered in return. Google's policy prohibits incentives and selective solicitation of positive reviews.

**Q: Where should I start?**
A: With your existing software's reminders and waitlist messages, then draft-mode replies, then a limited agent. See our [AI agents service page](/services/ai-agents) if you want help setting it up.
