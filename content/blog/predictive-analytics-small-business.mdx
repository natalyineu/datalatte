Predictive analytics for a small business means using past customer data to estimate who will buy, who will stop coming and what they may spend. Most local shops are too small for the built-in tools: Google Analytics 4 needs at least 1,000 returning users per outcome, and Klaviyo needs 500 customers who have ordered. Below those numbers, a simple "days since last visit" rule does the same job.

This guide shows what the real tools require, what to do when you are under the threshold, and a worked example. All figures come from the sources listed at the end.

## What Does Predictive Analytics Actually Predict?

It estimates the chance of a future action from patterns in past behaviour. Google Analytics 4 offers three: purchase probability (a user active in the last 28 days logging a key event in the next 7 days), churn probability (a user active in the last 7 days being inactive for the next 7), and predicted revenue over the next 28 days.

Klaviyo, an email platform for online stores, shows predicted customer lifetime value, churn risk as a percentage and an expected date of next order. These are estimates. They get worse when data is thin or when shop behaviour changes.

## Can a Small Business Use the Built-In Tools?

Often not yet. Google states that over a seven-day period within the last 28 days, at least 1,000 returning users must have triggered the predictive condition and at least 1,000 must not have. A purchase prediction also needs `purchase` events with `value` and `currency` sent to the property. Predictions can stop if model quality drops.

Klaviyo's customer lifetime value predictions need an ecommerce integration or API-sent orders, at least 500 customers who have placed an order, 180 days of order history with orders in the last 30 days, and some customers with three or more orders. A café selling over the counter will usually not meet either.

## What Should You Use If You Are Under the Threshold?

Use a rule built on your own records. Export customers from your POS, booking or loyalty system, and add one column: days since last visit. Compare it with each customer's usual gap between visits. When the current gap is about double the usual one, treat the person as at risk and contact them.

This is not machine learning, and it does not need to be. It answers the question that matters most to a local business: who is drifting away. ## How Much Data Do You Need First?

The sources set hard minimums only for their own tools. For a manual rule, the practical minimum is a customer list with a date for each visit or order, and enough history to see a normal gap between visits. Klaviyo asks for 180 days for its model, which is a reasonable yardstick for your own spreadsheet too.

Clean the list first. Duplicate profiles for the same person make a gap look longer than it is. No source quantifies how much cleaning improves accuracy, so treat that as a precaution, not a measured gain.

## The Math: What Could a Win-Back Rule Return?

All inputs below are **assumptions** for an illustrative café, not benchmarks.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Active customers on the list | 400 |
| Usual gap between visits | 14 days |
| Flag when gap exceeds | 28 days |
| Customers flagged this month | 60 |
| Share who return after a check-in message | 15% |
| Customers won back | 9 |
| Average ticket | $12 |
| First-visit revenue recovered | $108 |

Nine returning customers at $12 is $108 for the first visit. If those customers keep visiting, the value grows, but that depends on your shop. The cost is an hour of exporting the list and sending a message. Change each assumption to your own numbers before relying on the result.

## Which Data Should You Collect Going Forward?

Capture a visit or order date, a customer ID or email, the amount spent and the channel (walk-in, online, delivery). Record consent for email or SMS at the same time. ## FAQ

### Do I need a data scientist for predictive analytics?
No. Below the thresholds of GA4 and Klaviyo, a spreadsheet with a days-since-last-visit column gives a useful signal. The hard part is acting on the list, not building it.

### Does Google Analytics predict individual customers?
GA4 predicts at the level of users it can see on your website or app, using events you send. A shop that sells in person needs the POS data in a separate list.

### How accurate are predictions?
No source in this article publishes accuracy for local businesses. Google says predictions can become unavailable when model quality drops, so check results against what actually happened.

### How often should I update the list?
Monthly is enough for most shops with a visit cycle of a few weeks. Shorter cycles, such as daily coffee, justify a weekly check.
