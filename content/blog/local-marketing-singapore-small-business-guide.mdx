## What Can the Sources Support for Singapore?

The sources for this guide are three Singapore agency blogs, all secondary, plus a US federal page that returned only a promotional banner. They describe strategy and setup, but none gives a current Singapore reach figure or a Singapore price per click. Earlier versions relied on platform reach numbers and US benchmarks that are not in the source set, so those figures have been removed.

The one platform-scale figure is that Facebook had more than 1.5 billion users worldwide. That is a global count, not a Singapore audience size, so treat it as background only. For Singapore-specific audience size, check the platform's own ad tools with your location and targeting selected.

## Where Should a Singapore Business Start?

One agency blog argues that local firms have an edge in hyper-local targeting, using geolocation data and demographic insights to reach specific neighbourhoods ([S1, secondary](https://adguysco.com/blog/local-vs.-global-digital-marketing-strategies-explored)). That is a claim about agencies rather than a measured result, but the principle applies to any shop that serves a defined area.

Set up measurement before spending. The same agency guide recommends Google Analytics and Google Tag Manager, both free, and UTM codes so you can see which campaign sent each visit ([S2, secondary](https://www.impactbc.com.sg/ultimate-guide-digital-marketing-success/)). Without that, you cannot tell whether a campaign produced bookings. For a comparison of local search options, see [Google Local Service Ads: is it worth it for local businesses?](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business)

## How Do You Set Up a Facebook Ad?

A Singapore agency's setup guide splits Facebook advertising into three levels: campaigns (the main theme), ad sets (groups for different audiences) and individual ads ([S3, secondary](https://www.impossible.sg/a-guide-to-facebook-advertising-in-singapore-how-to-get-started/)). Choose the objective first, because the ads manager tailors the setup to it. The listed objectives are brand awareness, traffic, lead generation, conversions and sales.

Then pick a budget type. A daily budget suits ads meant to run all the time. A lifetime budget suits a campaign with a fixed end date, and the platform paces spending across that period. Monitor performance at the account level, then drill into each campaign, ad set and ad.

## Which Platform Fits, and What Should You Track?

The same agency guide says B2C businesses can build engagement through social media and blogging, provided they keep a steady content plan ([S2, secondary](https://www.impactbc.com.sg/ultimate-guide-digital-marketing-success/)). Pick the platform where your customers already look for your type of service, then let your results decide.

Track the basics first: click-through rate, cost per click, conversion rate and, once you have enough data, cost per acquisition. If you post across several platforms, a scheduling tool can save time. The [Hootsuite and Sprout Social comparison for local businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) covers the trade-offs.

## Which Checklist Should You Follow Before Launch?

Work through these steps in order before spending on paid ads:

1. Claim and verify your Google Business Profile, then complete hours, category and services.
2. Connect Google Analytics and Google Tag Manager, and tag every ad link with a UTM code.
3. Choose one platform and one objective, such as bookings or calls.
4. Set a daily or lifetime budget that you can run for at least a month.
5. Record cost per click, booking requests and customers each week.
6. Decide on a larger budget only after the real rates match or beat the assumptions above.

## What Should You Check Before Messaging Customers?

The sources for this guide do not cover Singapore's rules for marketing messages, so no specific requirement is stated here. Before sending marketing texts, emails or calls, check the current guidance from Singapore's Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) directly, including any registry checks and what counts as consent.

Whatever the rule, keep a simple record of when and how each customer agreed to hear from you, and make it easy to opt out. A short line in the booking confirmation is enough to start.

## FAQ

### What is the best first channel for a Singapore small business?

Start with the Google Business Profile and search, because customers looking for a nearby service use them. Add paid social ads once the profile, website and tracking are in order.

### How much should I spend on ads in Singapore?

No Singapore benchmark appears in the sources for this guide. Start with a budget you can run for a month, measure cost per booking, then adjust. The worked example shows how sensitive results are to click cost.

### Is a Facebook ad structure hard to set up?

The structure has three levels: campaign, ad set and ad. Choose the objective, set a daily or lifetime budget, then monitor results at each level.

### Can the 1.5 billion Facebook figure be used for Singapore?

No. It is a worldwide user count from a secondary source. Use the platform's own ad tools to size the audience for your location.

### Do marketing message rules apply to my business?

Possibly. Check the current PDPC guidance before sending marketing texts or calls, because this guide does not cover those rules.
