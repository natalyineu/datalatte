A small business should make an infographic only when it has one clear set of facts to show, such as a price comparison, a process or a local checklist. Publish it as a real image on a page with the same information in text, add descriptive alt text, and keep text contrast at 4.5:1 or higher. That makes it findable in Google Images and readable by everyone.

This guide is for owners and marketers without a design team. It uses Google Search Central, W3C and Meta documentation. Design tool prices could not be verified (two vendor pages returned 403), so none appear here. No source publishes how many shares or links a typical infographic earns, so none is quoted.

## When Is an Infographic Worth Making?

Make one when the content is a comparison, a sequence or a set of numbers that a paragraph explains badly. Good local examples are a price range with sources, a seasonal care calendar, or how a process works step by step. Skip it when the message is one sentence.

Each infographic needs a source for every number. If you cannot name the source, leave the number out. Our [guide to data analytics for small business](/blog/data-analytics-for-small-business) shows where to find your own first-party numbers that no competitor has.

## How Do You Make It Findable in Google?

Google Search Central says to use standard HTML image elements, because Google finds images in the src attribute of img and does not index CSS background images. It calls alt text "the most important attribute" for image metadata and says to avoid filling it with keywords. Use short descriptive file names such as spring-lawn-care-calendar.jpg.

Google supports BMP, GIF, JPEG, PNG, WebP, SVG and AVIF, recommends image sitemaps, and warns that large images slow pages. Put the infographic on a page with real text around it, not alone. ## How Do You Make It Accessible?

Complex images need two text parts, according to W3C. A short alt text identifies the image and points to the full details, and a long description gives the essential information in text. W3C lists four methods, including a figure with a figcaption that holds the full description.

Contrast matters too. WCAG 2.2 level AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text, where large means at least 18 point or 14 point bold. Pale grey text on a white background often fails. Check colours with a contrast checker before you export.

## How Should You Share It on Social?

Split the graphic into slides. Instagram carousels can hold up to 20 images or videos, according to Meta's developer documentation. One idea per slide reads better on a phone than a single tall image.

Post the full graphic on your website and link to it. For reach, add the topic in the caption in plain words. ## How Do You Measure Results?

Link to the infographic page with a UTM-tagged URL from each channel. Google says to always set utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, and that values are case-sensitive. Then watch visits to that page, clicks on your phone number and form submissions.

Our [local SEO KPI guide](/blog/how-to-measure-local-seo-success-kpis) lists the numbers worth tracking. Do not judge an infographic by likes alone, because a graphic can be shared widely and still bring no enquiries.

## FAQ

### Do infographics help SEO?

No source checked here gives a ranking effect. They can be indexed in Google Images when published as proper image elements with alt text, and they give your page more to share.

### What size should an infographic be?

No requirement comes from the sources used. Google advises sharp images and responsive techniques, because large files slow pages.

### Do I need alt text for an infographic?

Yes. W3C recommends a short alt text plus a long description for complex images. Google calls alt text the most important image attribute.

### Can I use free tools?

Possibly, but plan limits for Canva and Piktochart could not be verified here because their pricing pages returned 403. Check the vendor page before relying on a plan.

### How many slides can an Instagram carousel have?

Up to 20 images or videos, according to Meta's documentation.
