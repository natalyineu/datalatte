A local business cannot buy national March Madness spots, but it can reach the same viewers inside its own market. The workable plan has four parts: streaming ads timed to game windows in the home market, a watch-day offer in the shop, a bracket promotion, and search and maps ads for the days people look for a place to watch. Planning starts about two weeks before Selection Sunday. The 2027 dates are not covered in the sources used here, so check the NCAA schedule.

## What is changing for March Madness in 2027?

Two changes are documented, and one is reported by a single outlet.

The first is the bracket. Cord Cutters News [reports](https://cordcuttersnews.com/skydance-gains-full-tv-control-of-ncaa-mens-march-madness-rights-starting-in-2027/) (secondary) that the NCAA has already expanded the tournament field for the 2027 editions of both the men's and women's championships, generating additional rights revenue. A Sportcal [headline](https://www.sportcal.com/newsletters/ncaa-expands-march-madness-brackets-adds-50m-per-year-in-broadcast-income) (secondary) puts the added broadcast income at $50m per year. The exact bracket size is best confirmed on the NCAA site.

The second is the rights holder. The same Cord Cutters News article says Skydance takes exclusive television, digital and related rights from the 2027 edition through 2032, after Paramount's acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery combined CBS Sports and TNT Sports. It gives the combined annual rights fee as about $1.1 billion and says all games stay nationally available.

That rights change is reported by one source and is unconfirmed. Verify it before building a plan around it.

What it means for local buyers is not documented. Nothing in the sources says how local ad inventory or rates will change, so ask the platform for current rates and for the networks and apps carrying games in the market.

## Why does March Madness matter to a local business?

Attention is the reason. eMarketer's 2026 [coverage](https://www.emarketer.com/content/march-madness-viewership-soars-33-year-high-marketers-eye-sports-opportunities) (secondary) carries the headline that viewership hit a 33-year high.

The most detailed recent figures in the sources are from 2025. Front Office Sports [reported](https://frontofficesports.com/mens-march-madness-sets-opening-day-viewership-record) (secondary) preliminary Nielsen data showing the opening Thursday averaged 9.1 million viewers across CBS, TNT, TBS and truTV, up 6% from 2024. Primetime averaged 12.2 million. The Tuesday First Four drew a combined 3.6 million, up 21%.

One detail matters for cafes, bars and restaurants. Nielsen now tracks out-of-home viewing in 100% of the contiguous United States. In earlier years, at least one-third of the country, particularly rural areas, was not tracked. Year-over-year comparisons therefore carry an asterisk, but people watching in venues are now counted.

National brands also profit from the tournament. A report by Elevate Sports Ventures and Hive, [summarized](https://frontofficesports.com/article/march-madness-reportedly-generates-410m-of-in-game-brand-exposure/) (secondary) by Front Office Sports, valued 2022 in-game brand exposure at more than $410 million, over double the estimated $170 million for Super Bowl LVI. Five brands took 56% of that value. A local business is not in that group, so the goal is local foot traffic, not national awareness.

## Which local options fit a small budget?

National broadcast spots are priced for national brands. Front Office Sports notes that men's games command higher spot costs because viewership is higher. The realistic options are local and mostly flexible in size.

Cost levels below are relative descriptions, not quoted prices. Rates vary by market and platform, and the sources contain no local rate data.

| Option | What it does | Relative cost level | Main limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| National broadcast spot | Reaches the full tournament audience | Out of reach for most local businesses | Priced for national advertisers |
| Streaming ads in the home market during game windows | Reaches households in the delivery area while games run | Moderate, scales with budget and area | Needs creative and a clear offer |
| Watch-day offer in the shop | Converts viewers who want a place to watch | Low (the discount is the cost) | Capped by seating and staff |
| Bracket promotion | Builds repeat visits and email or social sign-ups | Low | Needs free entry and clear rules |
| Search ads on category and game terms | Captures people already looking nearby | Low to moderate, depends on competition | Demand rises and falls with game days |
| Maps and business profile updates | Shows hours, offers and watch details at the moment of search | Very low (time only) | Only helps people who search |

## How should game-window streaming ads be set up?

The aim is narrow: show ads to households near the business while games are on, with one offer and one action.

Practical settings to request from any provider:

- A delivery area built around the shop's real trade area (a radius or a ZIP list), not the whole metro.
- Scheduling concentrated on game days and the hours around tip-off, rather than an even spread across the month.
- A frequency cap, so the same household is not shown the ad repeatedly in one evening.
- A single call to action, such as a short web address or QR code tied to the offer.
- Reporting that shows which apps and devices delivered the ads.

Creative should match the promotion, not the tournament brand. National marks and tournament names belong to the NCAA and its partners, so generic wording such as "big game day" or "bracket season" is safer. Confirm usage rules with the NCAA or the ad platform before using any official name or logo.

Other leagues work the same way. The [NBA playbook](/blog/nba-ctv-advertising-local-business) and [ESPN+ advertising page](/blog/espn-plus-advertising-local-business) show how streaming inventory is bought around live events.

## What works for watch offers and bracket promotions?

A watch offer gives a reason to come in during a game. A cafe might offer a seat with a screen, a game-day drink-and-snack pairing, or a happy hour tied to tip-off. A fitness studio might run a short pre-game class and a post-game recovery session.

Confirm what the shop's TV or streaming subscription allows for public viewing. The sources do not cover licensing, so check with the provider.

A bracket promotion adds repeat visits. Customers fill out a free bracket in-store or online, and the best score wins a prize such as store credit. Keep entry free, write short rules, and check local requirements for contests before launching.

Both tactics generate email and social sign-ups, which can be reused after the tournament ends. Mention the offer in every channel: the streaming ad, the search ad, the business profile and the door sign.

Staffing matters as much as marketing. A watch offer that fills the room without extra hands creates slow service, so plan shifts around the busiest game windows before spending on media.

## How do search and maps pick up the demand?

Streaming ads create the awareness. Search and maps capture the action. People who see a game-day ad often look up the business, or search for a place to watch near them.

Three steps cover most of it:

1. Run search ads on the business category plus "near me" style terms, and add game-day terms during the tournament.
2. Update the business profile with watch-day hours, offers and photos before the first game.
3. Send ad clicks to a single page that states the offer, the address and the hours.

Search ads can also be scheduled to the same game windows as the streaming campaign. Review the search terms report weekly and exclude irrelevant queries.

## What does a 4-week calendar look like?

The calendar starts two weeks before Selection Sunday. The sources do not list 2027 dates, so check the NCAA schedule and count backward. For reference, the 2025 tournament opened with the First Four on a Tuesday and the Round of 64 on a Thursday, per Front Office Sports.

### Week 1: two weeks before Selection Sunday

- Choose the offer, the trade area and the total budget.
- Build the landing page and the QR code.
- Brief the staff on the watch-day offer and the bracket rules.

### Week 2: the week before Selection Sunday

- Update the business profile and launch search ads at low spend.
- Finish streaming creative and submit it for approval.
- Start collecting email addresses with a "bracket opens soon" sign-up.

### Week 3: Selection Sunday through the opening rounds

- Release the bracket and start the streaming ads in game windows.
- Raise search budgets for the opening days, when the sources show the heaviest viewing.
- Track redemptions daily.

### Week 4: later rounds

- Move budget to the windows and offers with the best redemption counts.
- Promote the final games with a last offer and a thank-you message to bracket entrants.
- Export the results and record what to repeat next year.

## What does a worked example look like for a coffee shop?

The inputs below are all assumptions, built to show the arithmetic rather than to predict results.

**Assumptions:** one coffee shop, total media budget of $1,200 over four weeks, average ticket of $9, gross margin of 65%, and an offer cost of $1.00 per redeemed visit (for example, a free cookie with a drink).

- Contribution per incremental visit: $9 × 0.65 = $5.85, minus $1.00 for the offer, leaves $4.85.
- Break-even visits: $1,200 ÷ $4.85 = about 248 incremental visits.
- Per day: 248 ÷ 28 days = about 9 extra visits a day.

A sensitivity check on the same example: if the average ticket is $12 (assumption), contribution per visit is $12 × 0.65 − $1.00 = $6.80, and break-even drops to about 177 visits, or roughly 6 a day.

A possible budget split, also an assumption: $600 for game-window streaming, $300 for search, $100 for bracket materials and $200 held back for the best-performing week. The numbers show what the campaign has to deliver. Whether a real shop reaches them depends on its market and offer.

## How should results be measured?

Redemptions are the first measure. Count offer codes, QR scans and "mention the game" claims at the register each day, and compare game-day traffic with the same weekdays before the campaign.

Add three platform checks. Look at search impressions and calls from the business profile, branded search volume, and the streaming report for delivered households and apps. Streaming reports show delivery, not visits, so tie them back to redemptions.

The guide on [measuring CTV ads for a local business](/blog/how-to-measure-ctv-ads-local-business) covers attribution options in detail. Use one report to compare weeks and decide what carries into next season.

## FAQ

### Can a small business advertise during March Madness games?

Not on the national broadcast, which is priced for national advertisers. A local business can run streaming ads in its own market during game windows, plus search and maps ads and in-store offers.

### Will the 2027 bracket expansion change local ad options?

The NCAA has expanded the field for 2027, according to Cord Cutters News (secondary). The sources do not say how that affects local inventory or rates, so ask the platform for current numbers.

### Is the Skydance rights change confirmed?

Not by the sources used here. The report that Skydance takes exclusive March Madness rights from 2027 comes from one outlet and is unconfirmed. Treat it as a possibility to verify.

### Do watch offers work for businesses without a bar?

Yes, in principle. A cafe, studio or groomer can tie an offer to game days without showing the game, such as a pre-game class or a bracket-themed special. Results depend on the local audience.

### When should the campaign start?

About two weeks before Selection Sunday, to leave time for creative, approvals and search testing. Check the NCAA schedule for the 2027 date.
