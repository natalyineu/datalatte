## What Do the Tournament Ad Figures Show?

TNS Media Intelligence reported that more than $2.73 billion was spent on network TV advertising during tournament games from 2000 to 2006, and it expected 2007 spending to top $500 million, a 65 to 70 percent increase for the decade. The same report says the tournament brings in more TV ad revenue than the post-season games of the NFL, NBA and MLB combined in its comparison period. These are the most detailed primary-style figures available here.

The figures are national and nearly two decades old. They tell you how the tournament's ad market is structured, not what a bar in one city will pay today. Treat them as context, not a planning rate.

## Why Do Returning Advertisers Matter for Local Buyers?

On average, 83 percent of the tournament's network TV ad revenue came from returning advertisers, according to the same TNS data. The top 10 advertisers spent almost $1.1 billion over seven years, about 40 percent of total network TV ad revenue. Core sponsors buy the same slots year after year.

For a local business, that means inventory is contested. Brands with large budgets claim the premium placements first, so a small advertiser should expect to buy later windows, smaller geographies or the streaming and general CTV inventory that national brands do not prioritize. Ask a vendor which of those routes they can guarantee in writing.

## How Can a Local Business Reach Tournament Viewers?

Local options fall into three routes. Each one has different controls and different proof requirements, so compare them before you sign anything.

| Route | What you buy | What to ask for before you commit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local cable or broadcast sales | Spots in a designated market, often tied to a network schedule | Written schedule, geographic coverage map, make-goods policy |
| CTV platform or DSP | Streaming impressions targeted by zip code, interest or household | Placement reports by app, frequency caps, viewability data |
| Search alongside CTV | Google Search ads for people already looking for a place to watch | Keyword list, daily budget cap, conversion tracking set up first |

Ask every vendor for placement reports by app and the games or time windows they can confirm. Broad claims about "March Madness inventory" are hard to verify. ## What Does a Local Test Campaign Cost?

No source reviewed for this page prints a local CPM for tournament inventory, so the figures below are assumptions. They show how to check whether a campaign can pay back before you spend the full budget.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test budget | Assumption | $3,000 |
| CPM | Assumption, not a quote | $35 |
| Impressions | $3,000 / $35 x 1,000 | about 85,700 |
| Households at 3 views each | 85,700 / 3 | about 28,600 |
| Guests needed to break even at $25 spend each | $3,000 / $25 | 120 |

Whether 28,600 households produce 120 paying guests depends on targeting, the offer and the creative. None of those rates is published, so the test should measure them. If the vendor's quote implies a CPM far above $35, rerun the table with their number before approving the flight.

## Where Does Search Fit Beside CTV?

Search and CTV do different jobs. Search captures people who already want to know where to watch the game. WordStream's industry benchmarks put the average Google Search click-through rate at 3.17 percent across industries and the average cost per click at $2.69, with display click-through at 0.46 percent. Those are secondary figures from an older benchmark update, so check your own account before relying on them.

Google's help documentation says the Display Network reaches people across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail, which makes it a useful companion for awareness. A sensible split for a first test is to run search for the people ready to book a table and use CTV or display to build recall for the broader area. Keep the two campaigns tracked separately so you can see which one drives visits.

## FAQ

### Can a local business advertise during March Madness games?

Yes, through local cable or broadcast sales, CTV platforms or DSPs. The network's national ad time is sold by the networks themselves, so local buyers usually target by geography and content category instead.

### Is there a published local CPM for March Madness ads?

Not in the sources reviewed for this update. Use a written vendor quote, and test with a small budget before committing to a full flight.

### Why are the tournament ad figures from 2000 to 2006?

Those are the most detailed published figures in the sources available for this update. Newer viewership and pricing data should be checked directly with the networks and vendors before you plan.

### Should a bar use CTV or Google Ads for March Madness?

They do different jobs. Search reaches people already looking for a place to watch, while CTV builds awareness across the local area. Run a small test of each and compare visits before expanding either one.

### How early should a local business plan a tournament campaign?

Agree on creative, a target area and a budget before the bracket is set, so the campaign can launch quickly once you know which games matter. Confirm the schedule with the NCAA before booking.
