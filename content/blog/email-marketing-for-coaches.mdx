A coach can start email marketing for $0: MailerLite's free plan allows up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, and Kit's free plan allows up to 10,000 subscribers but has no automations. Paid automation starts around $12 a month at MailerLite (Comfort, from its pricing FAQ) and $33 a month at Kit (Creator, 1,000 subscribers, monthly billing). The list itself matters more than the tool: people who chose to hear from you, a welcome sequence, and a clear next step.

This guide covers tool costs, the legal minimum, a simple list-building plan and a worked example with labelled assumptions. No source we opened publishes a conversion rate for coaching lists, so the example uses assumptions only.

## Which Email Tool Should a Coach Use?

Pick on automation limits, not on the headline price. A coach needs one thing from day one: a welcome sequence that runs without you. Check the plan you are buying includes it.

| | MailerLite | Kit |
|---|---|---|
| Free plan | Up to 250 subscribers, 2,500 emails a month, 3 active automations | Up to 10,000 subscribers, no automations, Kit branding stays |
| First paid plan | Comfort from $12 a month, 50 active automations | Creator, $33 a month at 1,000 subscribers (monthly billing) |
| Annual billing | MailerLite says 10% cheaper | $390 billed yearly at 1,000 subscribers |

MailerLite's page did not show the exact price for 500 or 1,000 subscribers, so look at the live calculator. Kit's own page states a yearly saving that does not match its listed prices ($33 times 12 is $396, against $390), so check the figure at checkout. Prices were read on 31 October 2026 and change.

## What Rules Apply to Coaching Emails?

If you email people in the United States, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide lists eight requirements. The main ones: do not use misleading headers or subject lines, say clearly that the message is an ad, include a valid physical postal address, and give a clear way to opt out. You must honour an opt-out within 10 business days, and the opt-out method must work for at least 30 days after sending. The FTC page states penalties of up to $53,088 per violating email.

Google asks for more from large senders. For senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail, it requires SPF, DKIM and DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, and a spam rate below 0.30%, recommending below 0.10%. A small coach is unlikely to hit 5,000 a day, but the same setup helps inbox placement. Rules outside the US, such as GDPR in Europe, are not covered here, so check them if you email there.

## How Does a Coach Build a List?

Offer something specific in exchange for an address, and ask on pages people already visit. Keep the promise narrow, such as a one-page worksheet for one problem, not a general newsletter.

- **Website:** a sign-up form on the home page and on every article.
- **Booking page:** a tick box to receive emails after a discovery call, never pre-ticked.
- **Talks and workshops:** a sign-up sheet or QR code at the end.
- **Social profiles:** one link in the bio to the sign-up page.

Only email people who agreed. Buying a list breaks the trust you are building and risks your sending reputation. Our guide on [email list building strategies](/blog/email-list-building-strategies) has more tactics.

## What Should You Send?

Start with a welcome sequence of three to five emails over about two weeks. The first delivers the promised resource. The next ones explain how you work, answer the question prospects ask most, and invite them to book a call. After that, send a regular email with one idea and one link.

Ideas for content come from client questions, not from a content calendar. Do not promise results in your emails you cannot document. ## What Open Rates Can You Expect?

Mailchimp's benchmark page, last updated in December 2023, lists a 31.35% unique open rate and a 2.78% click rate for Business and Finance, and 35.63% and 2.62% for all users. It has no coaching or consulting category. Open rates are also distorted by mail privacy features that preload messages, so look at clicks and replies first. Your own list will differ, and a small list moves a lot from week to week.

## The Math: What Does a Small List Earn?

All inputs below are **assumptions** for a one-to-one coach, not benchmarks.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Subscribers | 1,000 (assumption) |
| Tool cost | $33 a month (Kit Creator, monthly billing) |
| Emails inviting a call per month | 2 (assumption) |
| Clicks on the booking link per invitation | 2% of the list, 20 people (assumption) |
| Calls booked from those clicks | 25%, so 5 a month across two emails (assumption) |
| Calls that become clients | 20%, so 1 client a month (assumption) |
| Value of one client | $600 (assumption) |

At these inputs the list returns $600 a month for a $33 tool cost. If conversion is half, it is still above the tool cost. If you get no clients in three months, the problem is more likely the offer or the audience than the software. Do the same exercise with your own numbers before paying for a plan.

## FAQ

### How much does email marketing cost for a coach?

It can be free. MailerLite's free plan covers 250 subscribers and Kit's free plan covers 10,000, but Kit's free plan has no automations. Paid plans we read start at $12 (MailerLite Comfort) and $33 (Kit Creator, 1,000 subscribers).

### How many subscribers does a coach need?

No source we opened gives a number. A list of a few hundred engaged subscribers can produce enquiries. Track clicks and replies per email instead of list size.

### Do I need a physical address in every email?

CAN-SPAM requires a valid physical postal address in commercial emails. Many coaches use a PO box or a registered business address.

### Is it legal to add people from a networking event?

Not automatically. CAN-SPAM allows opt-out marketing in the US but other countries require consent first. Ask permission and record it.

### How often should a coach email the list?

No source we opened sets a figure. Pick a rhythm you can keep, such as one email a week, and watch unsubscribes.
