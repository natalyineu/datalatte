Local SEO for a pet groomer in New Orleans, LA mostly means appearing in the Google Maps local pack, which [Adwave defines as the top three business listings in local search results](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today). Most of that visibility comes from your Google Business Profile (GBP), a free listing that controls how your business appears across Search and Maps. This guide covers what to set up, how to build reviews and citations, and how to measure progress, with a worked review example you can adapt to your own booking volume.

## Why Does the Map Pack Matter So Much for a Pet Groomer?

Map pack placement matters because owners searching for grooming nearby tend to choose from a short list. [Adwave states that the top three businesses in the map pack are pulled directly from their GBP listings](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business), so the profile itself decides who gets seen first. Adwave also cites a 2025 multi-location SEO benchmark that puts GBP signals at [32% of local pack ranking weight](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit). Ahrefs reports a [BrightLocal survey in which 36% of SEOs named the GBP as the most important map pack factor in 2021](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo). Treat these as directional rather than exact, but they point the same way. Start with the profile, then check how competitors in New Orleans look in the results for your services.

## How Should You Set Up Your Google Business Profile?

Start with the basics, since the listing itself costs nothing. [Google states that its Business Profile is free to set up](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/). Choose your primary category by checking what competitors use. [Adwave recommends reviewing the top 3-5 businesses in local Google Maps results for a service search](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) and matching the category they choose. The same Adwave article claims the primary category is the top ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, though it does not name the analysis behind that claim.

Your description can run to 750 characters, so write it in plain language that names your service and neighborhood. Keep your hours, phone number, and address [100% accurate](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). If you work from home or travel to clients, you can [hide your street address and be designated a Service Area Business](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). For a fuller walkthrough of profile fields, see [GBP optimization for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers).

## Which Photos, Posts, and Q&A Keep the Profile Active?

Adwave recommends [at least 15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), and says a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls. Use real photos of finished grooms with owner consent, your grooming station, and your van if you are mobile.

Posts need an approved status to appear publicly. [Google's help page states that a post shows on Search and Maps only when its status is Live](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), and Pending or Not approved posts do not display. Adwave advises [at least one new post a week](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) to keep the profile fresh.

Q&A has been part of Google Maps since [2017](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a). Seed it with the questions customers ask most, such as which dog breeds you handle. Customer chats can also be managed [from the Business Profile dashboard on a desktop](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on), not only in the Maps app.

## How Many Reviews Do You Need, and How Fast Should They Arrive?

[Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), so each review is visible to searchers before they click. An analysis of two million GBP pages found that [businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business), a secondary finding that is useful as a benchmark rather than a guarantee.

Here is a worked example using assumed inputs. Assumption: 30 grooming appointments per week. Assumption: a review request is sent after every appointment. Assumption: 1 in 4 customers leaves a review. That gives 30 x 0.25 = 7.5 reviews per week, or about 32 per month. At that pace, reaching 200 reviews from zero takes roughly 27 weeks, about six months. Replace the assumed numbers with your own booking and review counts to build your real timeline.

## How Consistent Do Your Citations Need to Be?

Citations are mentions of your name, address, and phone number (NAP) on other websites. [Adwave states that the NAP must be exactly the same on your website, your GBP, and major directories like Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses). The detail matters: the [NAP on your website must match your GBP listing right down to punctuation and whether you write "St." or "Street"](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack).

Apple is worth checking too. Adwave argues that [Apple Business Connect has already captured a 7% share of a maps-only comparison and is growing](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring). That article's Google and Apple Maps usage figures, 88% and 12%, do not name a source, so use them only as context. Apple Business Connect reports Profile Views, which counts how many people saw your business card in Apple Maps.

## Should Your Website Repeat What Your Profile Says?

Your website should carry the same NAP as your profile, on the contact and location page especially. A [Google Maps embed on that page is a minor signal and easy to add](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website). Name your core services and New Orleans neighborhoods in plain text on your service pages, so the website reinforces what the GBP already says.

## Should You Add Google Ads Alongside Local SEO?

Organic local SEO and paid ads work from the same business data. [Google's help page states that location asset data used in Google Ads is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), and corrections made there flow into linked Ads accounts. If that option does not work for you, you can [select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link with your Ads account](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), as long as those locations are yours.

A [Marketing 360 case study of a mobile pet groomer](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/) reports that the business chose Facebook and Google as its advertising channels. If you are weighing paid options, see [Google Ads for pet groomers in New Orleans](/blog/google-ads-for-pet-groomers-in-new-orleans-la) and [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins).

## Which Metrics Show Your Local SEO Is Working?

Adwave names six useful local search metrics: [GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent). Record these before you start so you can compare later. Adwave also estimates that a business may begin [ranking for its own name in the Map Pack a few weeks after verification](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), which is a vendor estimate, not a fixed timeline.

## Where Should a New Orleans Pet Groomer Start?

1. Search your main service terms and note the categories used by the top three to five results.
2. Set your primary category, a 750-character description, and accurate hours, phone, and address.
3. Upload at least 15 high-quality photos with owner consent.
4. Schedule one approved GBP post per week and check its Live status.
5. Audit your citations on Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places for exact NAP matches.
6. Set a review request after every appointment and track your monthly count.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a pet groomer?

Yes. [Google states that its Business Profile is free to set up](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) and describes it as a free listing on Google Search and Maps.

### How many photos should a pet groomer upload to Google?

[Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) for a profile, with a gallery that its article says gets far more clicks and calls.

### Can a mobile pet groomer hide its home address?

Yes, if it works from home or serves customers at their location. [The business can hide its street address and is then designated a Service Area Business](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack).

### Do Google reviews appear outside the profile itself?

[Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), so they are visible to people browsing results before they open your listing.
