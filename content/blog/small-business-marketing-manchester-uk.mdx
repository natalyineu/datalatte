## What Should a Manchester Business Fix First?

Your Google Business Profile is the cheapest place to start. Use this order:

1. Check that the category, opening hours, services and photos match what you offer today.
2. Add a booking or contact link so a searcher can act straight away.
3. Ask each recent customer for a review at the point of sale, not by email a week later.
4. Reply to every review, good or bad, so future customers see that someone reads them.

Outdated hours are one of the most common reasons a listing loses a visit. Check them before any holiday period and after any change to your opening pattern.

Aim for a steady trickle of reviews rather than a burst. A sudden flood can look less natural, and a steady pattern is easier to keep going. Many competitors in Manchester districts are small, and many have not kept their listings current, so a complete profile with recent reviews can stand out without spending on ads.

## How Do You Keep Google Ads Local?

Location settings decide who sees your ads. Target a radius around your premises or a short list of districts you can reach in a normal trip. A blanket "Greater Manchester" setting wastes budget on people who will never travel to you, unless you genuinely serve the whole area. Google's ad platform is the place to set these rules, and a dedicated [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads) can help if the settings feel unfamiliar.

Negative keywords matter just as much. Exclude words such as "jobs", "courses", "free" or "DIY" if they do not match what you sell. Check the search terms report every week for the first month and add any irrelevant phrase as a negative. Plurals and close variants may need adding by hand, so review them rather than assuming the list is complete.

Write headlines that name the place only when it is true. If your shop sits beside a tram stop, say so. If you serve a wider area, name the districts you cover. Avoid generic claims that could apply to any city.

## What Does £600 a Month Buy?

The table below is a worked example. Every input is an assumption to replace with your own keyword estimates and first-month results. It is not a Manchester benchmark.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly Google Ads budget | £600 |
| Cost per click | £2.00 |
| Clicks from the budget | 300 |
| Clicks that become a call or booking | 5% (15 enquiries) |
| Cost per enquiry | £40 |
| Enquiries that become customers | 60% (9 customers) |
| Cost per new customer | about £67 |
| Average customer spend | £120 |
| Gross margin on that spend | 50%, so about £60 profit per customer |

On these inputs, the first purchase alone does not cover the £67 acquisition cost. The campaign pays off only if customers return, or if spend or margin is higher than assumed. Answer that question before adding budget. If customers return often, test a second district. If they do not, tighten the keywords and location targeting first.

Track calls and bookings, not clicks. A click shows interest, but it does not show whether a shop, café or trade business made money.

## Which Tools Help You Track Enquiries?

Once enquiries arrive from several sources, a spreadsheet stops being enough. For social posts that bring people into a shop or café, a scheduling tool keeps posting consistent. Pick the simplest tool that records where each enquiry came from. That link is what answers the question the worked example raises.
