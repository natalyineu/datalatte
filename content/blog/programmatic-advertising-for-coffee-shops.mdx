For a coffee shop, programmatic advertising means buying display or video ads by software, aimed at people near your shop or at those who already visited your site. It suits awareness and reminders. It is not where to start: Google Business Profile and Search should come first, because they reach people already looking for coffee nearby.

Demand is there. The National Coffee Association's Spring 2026 data shows 66% of US adults had coffee in the past day, and the SCA report says 47% had specialty coffee. This guide covers where programmatic fits, what platforms state about minimums, and a worked test budget.

## Does Programmatic Make Sense for a Coffee Shop?

Sometimes. The SCA report finds specialty drinkers are more likely to buy out of home: 36% got their coffee prepared away from home, against 23% of traditional drinkers. That is a useful audience for a nearby shop. The data is national, so it says nothing about your street.

Before ads, make sure people can find you. Our guide to [how coffee shops dominate Google Maps](/blog/coffee-shops-dominate-google-maps) and the [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) come first. Programmatic then supports a launch, a new menu or a seasonal drink.

## How Do You Keep Ads Close to the Shop?

Use location targeting. Google's radius targeting shows ads to customers within a set distance, with a minimum of 1 km. Very small radii may deliver intermittently or not at all. "Presence or interest" is Google's recommended broad setting for Search, while "Presence" restricts to people physically in the area.

Other platforms have their own settings. Check each platform's minimum audience size before you plan a few-block target.

## Can You Use Your Own Customer List?

Yes, in Google. Customer Match lets you use your online and offline data to reach customers on Search, YouTube, Gmail and Display. A list stays eligible when at least 100 members are added or updated within 540 days, and Customer Match will not be used with manual bidding strategies. Only upload data you have permission to use. ## The Math: What Does a $500 Test Show?

All inputs are **assumptions**. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark for Restaurants and Food, the closest category to cafés, lists $2.05 per click on Google Search ads, which is a different channel from display, so use it only as a rough guide.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget | $500 |
| Assumed display CPM | $8 |
| Impressions | 62,500 |
| Assumed click-through rate | 0.3% |
| Clicks | about 188 |
| Cost per click | about $2.67 |
| Visits to your menu or offer page counted | 188 |

Redeem a code only available in the ad to see real store visits. If 5% of clicks redeem at an average ticket of $7 (both assumptions), that is about 9 redemptions and $66 in sales against $500, which is a loss on day one. Programmatic pays only with repeat customers. See our [loyalty without an app](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app) ideas.

## What Should You Do First?

1. Complete your Business Profile.
2. Run a small Google Search campaign for nearby coffee searches.
3. Test one display or retargeting campaign for 4 to 6 weeks.
4. Compare redemptions and branded searches.

## FAQ

### How much should a coffee shop spend on programmatic ads?

There is no standard. A $500 test is enough to learn, not to scale.

### Is programmatic better than Instagram ads?

Different jobs. Instagram suits visuals; programmatic reaches across sites and apps. Test one at a time.

### Can I target only my neighbourhood?

You can set a radius, with Google's minimum at 1 km. Smaller areas may not deliver.

### How do I measure store visits?

Use a code, a menu page link or a QR code. Platform store-visit reporting has thresholds, so check availability.
