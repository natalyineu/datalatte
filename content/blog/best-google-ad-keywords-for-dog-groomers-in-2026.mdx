Dog groomer keywords fall into a few intent groups: ready-to-book searches like "dog groomer near me" and "dog grooming [city]", service searches like "deshedding" or "puppy first groom", and research searches like "how often should I groom my dog". Use the first two groups for Google Ads and service pages, and the third for blog content that earns organic traffic. Judge keywords by booked appointments, not clicks.

Here is where small budgets leak. Someone types "how to brush a matted dog", clicks your ad, reads for 20 seconds and leaves. You paid for that click. They were never going to book, because they wanted free instructions. One negative keyword ("how to") would have stopped it. Do that 50 times a month and you have paid for a lot of dog-owner curiosity.

Cost per click varies a lot by city and competition, and neighborhood-specific or long-tail searches are often cheaper than generic ones. Check Google Keyword Planner for your own market: it estimates monthly searches and shows what advertisers have historically bid, but Google notes these are estimates, not guarantees ([Google Ads Help: Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243?hl=en), checked Oct 2026).

## Dog groomer keywords by intent

Use this table for SEO (which page targets the phrase) and Google Ads (what to bid on). Replace [city] with your city or neighborhood.

| Intent | Example keywords | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Ready to book (local) | dog groomer near me, dog grooming [city], pet groomer [neighborhood], dog groomer open Saturday | Ads (exact/phrase), homepage, location page, Google Business Profile |
| Specific service | puppy first groom, deshedding treatment, mat removal, senior dog grooming, anxious dog grooming, poodle haircut [city] | Ads (phrase), dedicated service pages |
| Price research | dog grooming prices, how much does dog grooming cost | Blog or pricing page; test in ads carefully |
| Informational | how often to groom a dog, how to brush a matted dog, when to take a puppy to the groomer | Blog posts, internal links to services |
| Seasonal | summer haircut for dogs, spring deshedding, holiday dog grooming | Seasonal landing pages and short ad campaigns |

## Why Google Ads suit groomers

Most grooming customers search on a phone when they need an appointment: "dog groomer near me", "dog grooming open Saturday", "puppy grooming [city]". Ads put you in front of that search at the moment of intent, and a well-optimized Google Business Profile supports the map listings people tap on mobile. That profile matters. In the 50 Seattle pet groomers we pulled from Google Maps in Oct 2026, the median had 133 reviews and 8 had fewer than 50. A click that lands on a thin profile with 20 reviews is a weaker click.

## Core service keywords

Start with exact or phrase match for "dog groomer near me", "dog grooming [city]" and "pet grooming services [city]", and put each theme in its own ad group. Add city and neighborhood names as modifiers. Neighborhood searches are often cheaper and attract people who live close enough to become repeat customers. Test "dog grooming prices" in phrase match only, carefully.

## Long-tail keywords to test

Longer, more specific searches usually have lower competition and clearer intent. Test "deshedding treatment for golden retrievers", "mat removal for long-haired dogs", "senior dog gentle grooming", "anxious dog grooming", "hand scissoring dog grooming" and "flea bath for dogs".

Send each to a landing page or service section that actually covers it. A relevant landing page tends to improve Quality Score and conversion rate. For seasonal terms ("summer haircut for dogs", "holiday dog grooming"), switch the campaign on a few weeks before demand rises and pause it afterward.

## Three-step keyword research

1. **Start with Keyword Planner.** Enter your service and city, then review suggested keywords, average monthly searches and bid estimates. It shows average searches over a selected period (12 months by default) and a top-of-page bid range based on what advertisers have historically paid ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575), checked Oct 2026). Treat these as estimates.
2. **Check competitors.** Read their ads and landing pages for services you are missing.
3. **Read your search terms report every week.** Good terms become keywords, bad terms become negatives.

## Negative keywords to add before launch

| Category | Example negatives | Why block them |
|---|---|---|
| DIY / self-service | how to, diy, at home, tutorial | They want free instructions |
| Jobs and education | jobs, hiring, salary, course, school, certification | Job seekers and students will not book |
| Supplies | shampoo, clippers, brush, kit, wholesale | They want to buy products |
| Services you do not offer | cat (if dogs only), mobile (if salon only), boarding | Irrelevant clicks |
| Competitor brands | PetSmart, Petco, Wag, Rover | Usually low conversion unless you want to conquest |

Google lets you add negatives at the ad group, campaign and account level. Negatives do not match close variants, so list singular, plural and misspelled forms you want blocked ([Google Ads Help: negative keywords](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972?hl=en), checked Oct 2026). Use campaign-level negatives for terms you never want and ad group negatives to keep themes separate.

## The math: what junk clicks cost you

**Example only: assumptions are invented, swap in your own.** Budget $450 a month, an assumed $2.25 per click, 8% of relevant clicks book, $70 average appointment. The only difference between the two rows is how many clicks are junk.

| | No negatives | Negatives in place |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks ($450 / $2.25) | 200 | 200 |
| Junk share (assumed) | 25% = 50 clicks | 5% = 10 clicks |
| Money spent on junk | 50 x $2.25 = $112.50 | 10 x $2.25 = $22.50 |
| Relevant clicks | 150 | 190 |
| Bookings at 8% | 12 | 15.2 |
| Cost per booking | $450 / 12 = $37.50 | $450 / 15.2 = about $29.60 |
| Revenue at $70 | 12 x $70 = $840 | 15.2 x $70 = $1,064 |

Same budget, about 3 more bookings a month and roughly $224 more revenue. These are not market statistics or client results. The 25% and 5% junk shares are guesses. Your search terms report tells you your real share.

## Match types

Google describes them this way: broad match can show on searches related to your keyword, even without its direct meaning; phrase match shows on searches that include the meaning of your keyword; exact match shows on searches with the same meaning or intent ([Google Ads Help: match types](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en), checked Oct 2026). In practice:

- **Exact** for your best, highest-intent terms.
- **Phrase** for core service terms with a location.
- **Broad** only with solid conversion tracking and a strong negative list. Google says broad match should be paired with Smart Bidding, so it is a poor fit for a tiny budget with no conversion data.

## Ad copy that matches grooming searches

"Best in the Area" says nothing. Use specifics you can back up: "Same-day appointments available" (only if true), "Gentle handling for anxious dogs", "Book online in 60 seconds", "First-time client offer". Add call, location and sitelink assets (formerly called extensions) so mobile searchers can call or get directions in one tap.

## Budget, bidding and tracking

- **Budget:** pick a daily budget you can sustain for at least 60 to 90 days before judging. Use Keyword Planner's forecast for your city and our [budget guide for dog groomers](/blog/how-much-should-a-dog-groomer-spend-on-google-ads).
- **Bidding:** start with manual CPC or Maximize Clicks with a bid cap. Google says Target CPA can start without conversion history, but recommends judging performance over periods with at least 30 conversions ([Google Ads Help: Target CPA](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632?hl=en), checked Oct 2026). With steady data, test Maximize Conversions or Target CPA.
- **Schedule and radius:** weight budget toward the hours your bookings come in, and tighten the radius if far-away clicks do not convert.
- **Tracking:** count phone calls, booking form submissions and booking-software confirmations. Many grooming customers call rather than fill in forms. See [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).

## FAQ

**Q: What are the best Google Ads keywords for dog groomers?**
A: High-intent local keywords such as "dog groomer near me", "dog grooming [city]" and service-specific terms like "puppy first groom" or "deshedding treatment" usually work best. Use phrase and exact match, and add negatives for DIY, jobs and supplies.

**Q: How much does a click cost for dog grooming keywords?**
A: It varies widely by city and competition. Neighborhood and long-tail terms are often cheaper than generic ones. Check Keyword Planner for your market for current estimates.

**Q: How much should a dog groomer spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no fixed figure. Choose a budget you can sustain for at least 60 to 90 days, size it from Keyword Planner forecasts for your city, and see our [budget guide for dog groomers](/blog/how-much-should-a-dog-groomer-spend-on-google-ads).

**Q: Should I use broad match keywords?**
A: Usually not at the start. Phrase and exact match give more control on a small budget. Google recommends pairing broad match with Smart Bidding, so consider it later, with conversion tracking and a strong negative list in place.

**Q: Which negative keywords should dog groomers add first?**
A: Start with "diy", "how to", "at home", "jobs", "course", "school", "supplies", "shampoo", "clippers", plus any services you do not offer, such as "cat" or "mobile".
