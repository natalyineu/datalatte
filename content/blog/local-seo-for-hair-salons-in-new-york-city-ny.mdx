Local SEO for a New York City hair salon starts with the Google Business Profile, because that listing is what appears in the Maps pack. Google's guidelines set the basic rules: one profile per business, a name that matches your real-world signage, the fewest categories that describe your core service, and a description with no links. Fix those first, then build reviews, citations and service pages on top. The steps below follow those rules and include a worked budget you can adapt.

## What does a New York City hair salon need to fix first?

Start with the listing itself. Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say there should only be one profile per business, because duplicates can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search. If your salon has a second profile for a chair, a barber booth or an old address, merge or remove it before you optimise anything else.

Next, check that your data matches the real world. The guidelines ask that your business be represented as it is recognised across signage, stationery and other branding, and that your address or service area be accurate and precise. A mismatch between your storefront and your listing is one of the most common reasons a clean-looking profile still underperforms.

## How should a salon write its name, categories and description?

The name field is strict. Google's guidelines say the name should reflect the real-world name customers know, and they list what is not allowed: marketing taglines, store codes, business hours, phone numbers, URLs and fully capitalised words. Keyword-stuffed names such as "Style Studio | Balayage NYC" are the kind of addition the guidelines treat as unnecessary.

Categories follow the same logic. Google advises choosing the fewest number of categories that describe your overall core business. Common advice to add "Beauty salon" and "Hairdresser" as extra categories should be checked against this rule: pick the primary category that fits, and add a secondary only if it describes a core service you actually run.

| What you want to write | Allowed under Google's guidelines? | Safer version |
|---|---|---|
| "Style Studio \| Best Balayage NYC" | No, taglines and extra text are not allowed in the name | "Style Studio" |
| Prices and promotions in the description | No, the description should not focus on prices or offers | Describe services and history in plain language |
| A link to your booking page in the description | No, no links of any type are allowed | Put the link in the website field |
| Five categories for every service | No, choose the fewest that describe the core business | One primary, with a secondary only if it is core |

The description should explain services, mission and history in plain language. Put offers in posts instead, since the guidelines say the description should not focus on specials.

## How long before profile changes show in search?

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) states that changes can take effect in a few hours, while others may take several months. It recommends waiting a few weeks before assessing whether a change had a beneficial effect. Not every change produces a visible result, so test one change at a time and record what you did and when.

Keep your listing consistent while you wait. Re-editing the name or categories every few days makes it harder to tell which change mattered. A clean baseline also makes it easier to judge each item in a [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## Which reviews, photos and posts matter most?

Reviews and photos build credibility, but the content must follow Google's rules too. Published content should highlight what makes your business unique, and contact details for your own business are allowed. Reply to reviews with plain, accurate text that names the service the customer booked, since that reflects what you actually do.

Show your location clearly. A [Google Maps embed on your website](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) helps customers find the salon and reinforces the address on your listing. Keep the address identical everywhere you publish it, including the contact page and social profiles.

## FAQ

### How many Google Business Profiles should a hair salon have?

Google's guidelines say there should only be one profile per business. Duplicates can cause display problems on Maps and Search, so merge or remove extras and keep a single accurate listing.

### Can I put "NYC" and a service in my salon's business name?

The name should reflect your real-world name as customers know it. Google does not allow taglines, store codes or extra descriptive text in the name, so keep "NYC" and service terms in the description or service list instead.

### How many categories should my salon choose?

Choose the fewest number of categories that describe your overall core business. Add a secondary category only when it reflects a service you run as a core part of the business.

### How long should I wait before judging a profile change?

Google's SEO Starter Guide suggests waiting a few weeks before assessing a change, because some effects take months and others appear within hours. Test one change at a time to keep results readable.
