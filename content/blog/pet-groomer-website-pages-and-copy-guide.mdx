A pet groomer website needs six core pages: a home page, a services and pricing page, a booking page, an about page, a gallery or reviews page and a contact page with your location. Add an FAQ and a policies page, and each page should answer the questions pet owners ask before they trust you with their dog or cat.

Pet owners are choosing someone to look after a family member. They want to know what you do, what it costs, whether their pet will be safe and how to book. Your website's job is to answer those questions quickly, in plain language, on a phone.

## Page 1: Home page

Your home page should tell a visitor in a few seconds who you are, where you are and what to do next.

**What to include:**

- A headline with your service and area, such as "Gentle dog grooming in [Town]".
- One sentence on what makes you different (small-breed specialist, cat-friendly, mobile van, fear-free handling).
- A clear **Book now** button, visible without scrolling.
- Two or three short reviews from real customers.
- Your address, phone number and hours.
- Photos of real dogs and cats you have groomed.

Do not use a generic slogan. "Where every pet is family" appears on thousands of sites. Say something specific you actually do.

## Page 2: Services and pricing

This is typically the most visited page after the home page. Pet owners want a price range before they call.

**Layout idea:**

| Service | What is included | Price guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bath and brush | Bath, blow dry, brush out, ear clean, nail trim | From $X, depends on size and coat |
| Full groom | Bath plus haircut, face and feet tidy | From $X, depends on breed and condition |
| Nail trim only | Quick walk-in or short appointment | $X |
| Add-ons | De-shedding, teeth brushing, flea treatment | $X each |

Use "from" prices and explain what changes them: size, coat type, matting and temperament. If you do not publish prices, say how you quote, for example by photo or a short consult. Avoid inventing precise prices you do not use.

Write short, benefit-focused descriptions: "We trim nails with a grinder or clipper depending on your dog's comfort."

## Page 3: Booking page

Make booking as easy as possible. Many groomers use software with online booking such as MoeGo, Gingr, Square Appointments or Booksy. Pricing differs by tool and by plan, so compare current offers.

**What the booking page needs:**

- A short form or booking widget, not a long questionnaire.
- Fields for pet name, breed, size, last groom date and any health or behaviour notes.
- A clear note about **vaccination requirements,** if you have them.
- A line on how long appointments take.
- Confirmation text or email so owners know it worked.

If you prefer phone or message booking, put your number and a simple contact form here, and state your response times.

## Page 4: About and meet the groomers

Trust is the main product. Show who is handling the pets.

- A real photo of you and your team.
- Your qualifications, certifications or training, if you have them.
- How you handle anxious or senior pets.
- Your experience and a short personal note, such as why you became a groomer.
- Your safety practices: how you dry, how you keep pets supervised, how you clean between appointments.

Only mention certifications you actually hold. Keep it warm and honest. You do not need to sound corporate.

## Page 5: Gallery and reviews

Photos show your skills better than any description.

- Use **before-and-after photos** where owners agree. Ask permission before posting.
- Show a range of breeds and coat types.
- Add short captions with breed and service.
- Embed or quote recent **Google reviews** and link to your profile.

Fast-loading images matter. Compress photos before uploading. See [Core Web Vitals for local business websites](/blog/core-web-vitals-local-business-website) for practical speed tips.

## Page 6: Contact and location

Make it painfully easy to find and reach you.

- Full address, with a map. See how to [embed Google Maps](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).
- Parking and drop-off instructions.
- Phone, email and a contact form.
- Opening hours, with holiday changes.
- Service area, if you are mobile or offer pick-up.

Use the same name, address and phone number that appear on your Google Business Profile.

## Supporting pages worth adding

**FAQ page.** Answer questions you hear every week:

- Do you groom cats or only dogs?
- What vaccinations do you need?
- How long will my dog be here?
- What happens if my dog is matted?
- Can I stay during the groom?
- What is your cancellation policy?

**Policies page.** Cancellation, late arrivals, vaccination proof, aggressive-behaviour policy, matting fees and payment methods. Clear policies prevent awkward conversations.

**Service area pages.** If you cover several nearby towns, a short page for each can help local search, as long as each has unique, useful content. Avoid copying the same text with different place names.

**Blog or tips.** Short posts on coat care, shedding, nail trims and seasonal advice can help you appear in search and build trust.

## Copywriting tips for groomers

- **Speak to the owner,** not about "canines". Say "your dog".
- **Be specific.** "We use hypoallergenic shampoo on request" is better than "premium products".
- **Reassure.** Explain how you keep pets calm and what you do if one is stressed.
- **Use plain language.** Short sentences, no jargon.
- **Add a call to action to each page.** Book, call or message.

## Technical basics

- **Mobile first.** Most visitors will be on a phone.
- **Fast loading,** with compressed images.
- **Secure (HTTPS).**
- **Page titles** that include your service and town, such as "Dog Grooming in Austin | [Business Name]".
- **LocalBusiness schema** if your platform or developer can add it.

## Sample copy to adapt

**Home page headline:** "Calm, careful dog grooming in [Town]."

**Sub-headline:** "Small appointments, one groomer per dog, and no cage drying. Book online in two minutes."

**Services intro:** "Every groom starts with a quick chat about your dog's coat, comfort and any sore spots. Prices depend on size, coat and condition, so the figures below are starting points."

**Policy line:** "We need up-to-date rabies and core vaccination records before the first visit. Please bring or upload them when you book."

Replace these with what is true for your shop. Only promise practices you really follow, such as no cage drying or one groomer per dog.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a website if I already have Instagram and Facebook?**

A: Yes. Social profiles help, but a website gives you a stable place for pricing, policies, booking and search visibility that you control.

**Q: Should I list my prices?**

A: Showing "from" prices and what affects them helps visitors self-qualify and reduces time-wasting calls. If every groom is custom, explain how you quote.

**Q: How many pages is enough?**

A: Six to eight solid pages are enough to start. Add an FAQ and policies page, then expand with blog posts and location pages over time.

**Q: What matters most for getting booked?**

A: A fast mobile site, clear prices, real photos, visible reviews and an easy booking path.
