A solo mobile groomer can run online booking, payments and reminders for $49 a month, according to MoeGo's pricing page, and a website builder adds roughly another $19 to $39. That is about $70 to $90 a month for a front door that works at 10pm when your phone is off. The work is not the software; it is knowing which pages to build and what to put on them.

This guide is for groomers and mobile groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers six core pages, the extra pages worth adding, copy you can adapt, and the math on whether the site pays for itself. Prices are US dollars from vendor pages when this article was updated, and they change.

## Why the Website Still Matters

Pet owners are choosing someone to handle a family member. BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (1,002 US adults, from a vendor that sells review tools) found 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, 68% will only use a business rated four stars or higher, and 74% look for reviews from the last three months. So your site has to show recent proof, plain prices and an easy next step. Pair it with a strong [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers), because many people will see your profile before your site.

## Page 1: Home

A visitor should know within a few seconds who you are, where you are and what to do next.

- **Headline:** service plus place, such as "Calm, careful dog grooming in [Town]".
- **One specific differentiator:** small-breed specialist, cat-friendly, mobile van, one dog at a time. Only claim what you really do.
- **A Book button** visible without scrolling on a phone.
- **Two or three short reviews** from real clients with first names.
- **Address, phone and hours** as text, not just in an image.
- **Real photos** of dogs and cats you have groomed.

Skip generic slogans such as "where every pet is family". Say something a competitor could not copy.

## Page 2: Services and Prices

Pet owners want a price range before they call. Use "from" prices and say what moves them.

| Service | Includes | Price guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bath and brush | Bath, blow dry, brush out, ears, nails | From $X by size and coat |
| Full groom | Bath plus haircut, face and feet tidy | From $X by breed and condition |
| Nail trim | Short appointment or walk-in | $X |
| Add-ons | De-shedding, teeth brushing, flea treatment | $X each |

Replace $X with your real prices. Explain the factors in one line: size, coat type, matting and temperament. If every groom is custom, say how you quote, for example from a photo or a short consult. One benefit-led sentence per service beats a list of adjectives: "We trim nails with a grinder or clippers, whichever your dog finds calmer."

## Page 3: Booking

Make booking quick. Booking tools aimed at groomers include MoeGo, whose pricing page lists Basic at $49 a month for solo mobile groomers (online booking portal, payments, automated reminders and mass text), Growth at $99 per van and Ultimate at $159 per van. The page shows no free trial, but offers a demo and a product tour. Other pet-grooming and general booking tools exist; compare current plans yourself, because we did not verify their prices for this article.

The booking page needs:

- A short form or widget, not a questionnaire.
- Pet name, breed, size, last groom date, and health or behaviour notes.
- A clear line on vaccination requirements, if you have them. State the rule you actually enforce.
- How long an appointment takes.
- A confirmation message so owners know it worked.

If you book by phone or message, say so and state your reply time ("we answer within 2 hours, 9 to 5").

## Page 4: About and Meet the Groomers

Trust is the product. Show who handles the pets.

- A real photo of you and the team.
- Training or certifications you genuinely hold.
- How you handle nervous or senior pets.
- Your safety routine: drying, supervision, cleaning between dogs.
- A short personal note on why you became a groomer.

Keep it warm and factual. Do not list a certification you do not have.

## Page 5: Gallery and Reviews

Before-and-after photos show skill better than any paragraph. Ask owners for permission, show different breeds and coats, and caption each photo with breed and service. Quote recent Google reviews and link to your profile. Compress photos before upload and aim for Google's Core Web Vitals "good" targets, measured at the 75th percentile: Largest Contentful Paint 2.5 seconds or less, Interaction to Next Paint 200 milliseconds or less, Cumulative Layout Shift 0.1 or less (per web.dev). Our Core Web Vitals guide has practical fixes.

## Page 6: Contact and Location

- Full address and a map. See how to [embed Google Maps](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).
- Parking and drop-off instructions.
- Phone, email and a short form.
- Hours, including holiday changes.
- Service area, if you are mobile.

Use the same name, address and phone as your Business Profile. Google's guidance for service-area businesses is to hide your home address on the profile and list the areas you cover.

## Pages Worth Adding

**FAQ.** Answer what you hear every week: dogs only or cats too, vaccination proof, how long it takes, what happens with matting, can I stay, cancellation rules.

**Policies.** Cancellation, late arrivals, vaccination proof, aggressive-behaviour policy, matting fees and payment methods. A clear policy saves awkward conversations.

**Area pages.** Only if each one has something unique: the neighbourhoods you drive to, your travel fee, parking details. Google's spam policies name pages "targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as a doorway abuse example and flag blocks of text that just list cities. Three useful area pages beat fifteen copies with swapped town names.

**Tips posts.** Short posts on shedding, nail care and coat care build trust and can bring in search traffic.

## Copy Rules That Work

- **Say "your dog,"** not "canines".
- **Be specific.** "We use a hypoallergenic shampoo on request" beats "premium products".
- **Reassure.** Describe how you calm a stressed dog and what you do if one cannot finish.
- **Short sentences.** Plain words.
- **One call to action per page:** Book, Call or Message.

### Sample copy to adapt

**Home headline:** "Calm, careful dog grooming in [Town]."

**Sub-headline:** "One groomer per dog and a quiet room. Book online in two minutes."

**Services intro:** "Every groom starts with a quick chat about your dog's coat, comfort and any sore spots. Prices depend on size, coat and condition, so the figures below are starting points."

**Policy line:** "We need up-to-date vaccination records before the first visit. Upload them when you book, or bring them to drop-off."

Replace each with what is true in your shop. Only promise a practice, such as "no cage drying", if you follow it every day.

## Technical Basics

- **Mobile first.** Check every page on your own phone.
- **HTTPS** on every page.
- **Unique titles.** Google's title guidance says every page needs a descriptive, concise, unique title, for example "Dog Grooming in Austin | [Business Name]". Google may rewrite titles it judges inaccurate or repetitive.
- **LocalBusiness structured data.** Google's documentation requires `name` and `address` and recommends `telephone`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `geo`, `url` and `priceRange`. It also says features that use structured data are not guaranteed to appear. It is labelling, not a ranking lever.

On builders: Squarespace's pricing page lists Basic at $25 a month ($19 on annual billing) and Core at $39 ($29 annually), though the page does not clearly label which column is which. If you are weighing DIY against hiring, read [DIY website vs professional developer](/blog/diy-website-vs-professional-developer).

## The Math

Does the site pay for itself? Every row except the two software prices is an **assumption**; swap in your own numbers.

| | Low | Base |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Booking software (MoeGo Basic, from its pricing page) | $49/mo | $49/mo |
| Website builder (assumption, from Squarespace listing above) | $19/mo | $39/mo |
| Monthly cost | $68 | $88 |
| Average groom price (assumption) | $70 | $70 |
| Visits per client per year (assumption) | 4 | 6 |
| First-year value of a new client | $280 | $420 |
| New clients a year needed to cover 12 months of costs | 3 | 3 |

Annual cost is $816 in Low and $1,056 in Base. Divided by first-year value, you need about three new clients a year in either case (816 / 280 = 2.9; 1,056 / 420 = 2.5). That is before your time and any design cost, and it ignores the cost of the groomer's hours for each visit, so check your margin. If your site brings in three new regular clients a year, the software has covered itself.

## Do This Today

1. Write your real "from" prices for your four most-booked services, plus the factors that change them.
2. Write your vaccination, cancellation and matting policies in plain sentences you could say to a client's face.
3. Photograph five finished dogs or cats (with owner permission) and one photo of you and your setup.
4. Draft your home page headline, one differentiator and a Book button, using the sample copy above as a base.
5. Check every page on your phone, then confirm your address, phone and hours match your Google Business Profile exactly.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a website if I have Instagram and Facebook?**
A: Yes. Social profiles help, but a site gives you a stable place for prices, policies, booking and search visibility that you control.

**Q: Should I list my prices?**
A: Show "from" prices and what affects them. It lets visitors self-qualify and cuts time-wasting calls. If every groom is custom, explain how you quote.

**Q: How many pages do I need?**
A: Six solid pages are enough to launch. Add an FAQ and policies page, then tips posts and unique area pages as time allows.

**Q: Can I use my booking software's page instead of a website?**
A: It can work as a start, but a website of your own holds your reviews, policies and search presence. Link the two.

**Q: What matters most for getting booked?**
A: A fast mobile page, visible prices, real photos, recent reviews and a short path to book.
