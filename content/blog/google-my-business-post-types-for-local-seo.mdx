Google Business Profile has three post types: update, offer and event. An update needs only a message. An offer needs a title plus start and end dates, and Google adds a "View Offer" button automatically. An event also needs a title and dates, and an event with no start or end time shows as lasting 24 hours on its dates. Choose the type by the job the post has to do: a real deadline calls for an offer, a dated appointment calls for an event, and anything else is an update.

Most of the rules below come from [Google's help page on creating and managing posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en). Where a point comes from a third-party guide, the source is marked as secondary. Google's product used to be called Google My Business, so older articles may use that name.

## What are the three post types and what does each one need?

Each type carries a different set of required fields, so the choice changes how much work a post takes and what a customer sees. The table below uses only the requirements and features covered by the sources, plus a plain-language "best for" column that reflects how the types are usually used.

| Type | Required | Added by Google or recommended | Best for |
|------|----------|-------------------------------|----------|
| Update | A message only | Nothing beyond the message | News, a new menu item, a new stylist, changed hours |
| Offer | Title, start date and end date | "View Offer" button added automatically; a photo is recommended | Time-limited deals with a real deadline |
| Event | Title, start date and end date | A photo is recommended; without start or end times it shows as 24 hours | Classes, tastings, workshops, open days |

Offers and events need dates, so they only fit when something actually ends or starts at a set time. Updates need no dates, which makes them the default for anything that is not a deal or a dated event. Google will not publish an offer or event without the required dates, so a draft with blank dates is a wasted draft.

## How long does a post stay visible, and can you change it?

Google reviews each post after it is published. Only posts with the Live status are public and show on Search and Maps. Posts marked Pending or Not approved do not appear, so check the status after you publish rather than assuming the post is live.

Posts cannot be edited once they are published. A third-party guide to Google Business Profile automation states there is no edit, so changing a post means deleting it and publishing again (secondary). Deleting a live post removes it from the listing for good, so proofread the wording, dates and photo before you hit publish.

## What does Google not allow in posts?

Google's guidelines on representing a business allow you to post your own contact details, including social media handles, email and phone number, for your own business. They do not allow soliciting personal or confidential information from people through a post. Keep the post about your business and leave customer data out of it.

A third-party guide also states that businesses should not offer discounts, free products or other incentives in exchange for reviews (secondary). Keep offer posts separate from review requests, so a deal is never tied to a rating.

Read the guidelines before writing an offer for a regulated product, because content rules vary by category. If you are unsure, check Google's current help pages for your category.

## How do you build a 4-week posting plan?

The example below is a planning model, not a result. Assume a fitness studio with two class types and a trial offer. All numbers marked assumption are inputs you should replace with your own.

1. Week 1, offer (assumption: the trial runs Monday to Sunday): "First class free for new members". Set the start and end dates, add a photo, and let Google attach the View Offer button.
2. Week 2, event: "Saturday partner yoga workshop". Fill in both start and end times so the event does not default to 24 hours.
3. Week 3, update: a photo of the new timetable with one line pointing people to the booking page.
4. Week 4, update: a short note about a new coach, with a real photo of the coach.

Effort: assume four posts a month at 10 minutes each (assumption), so about 40 minutes a month. Track one number for 8 weeks (assumption): clicks to your booking page from the post, using a UTM tag on the link in your analytics. If a post type produces no clicks after 8 weeks, swap it for another type and keep the one that does. ## Which post type should you choose?

Use this order when you plan each post:

- Is there a real deadline? Use an offer, with start and end dates.
- Is there a date and time people must attend? Use an event, with start and end times.
- Neither? Use an update with a clear photo and one line of text.
- Is the product alcohol, tobacco, gambling or weapons? Check Google's current rules for your category before posting.
- Running a café? The [coffee shop profile guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization) covers the posts and details that suit that category.

<Callout type="tip">Do not repeat the same offer as an endless "limited time" post. An offer needs real start and end dates, and customers who see the same deadline twice stop believing it.</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many post types does Google Business Profile have?

Three: update, offer and event. A third-party guide lists these three types as the core options (secondary). Some older articles and dashboards may show other labels, so check the names on your own profile.

### Do offer posts need dates?

Yes. An offer needs a title, a start date and an end date, and Google will not publish it without them. Google also adds a View Offer button automatically.

### Can I edit a post after it is published?

No. To change a post, delete it and publish a new one. Deleting a live post removes it from the listing permanently, so check the wording before publishing.

### Do posts improve my local ranking?

The sources in this article do not say that posting changes a profile's position in the map results. Treat posts as current information for people who are already comparing you with other businesses.
