SEO and PPC solve different problems. PPC (pay-per-click ads) buys visibility today and stops when you stop paying. SEO builds free organic visibility that takes weeks to months to show. For most local businesses, the order is: complete your Google Business Profile, start a small PPC test for high-intent searches, and invest in SEO alongside it. Google says it never accepts money to rank sites in organic results.

This guide is for coffee shops, salons, groomers, studios and local service firms. Cost figures are LocaliQ's 2026 US benchmarks. No source checked publishes a price for SEO, so none is quoted. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Is the Difference Between SEO and PPC?

SEO is the work of making your pages and Business Profile easy for Google to find, understand and rank. PPC is paid advertising: you bid for placement and, on Google Ads, pay when someone clicks. Google's help describes a maximum CPC bid as the most you are willing to pay for a click, with the amount actually charged called the actual CPC.

Google's documentation says it costs nothing to appear in organic search results, and that advertising with Google will not affect your site's presence in them. So the two are separate systems. Buying ads does not improve your organic ranking, and good rankings do not remove the need for ads if you want guaranteed placement.

## How Do SEO and PPC Compare?

| | SEO (organic and Maps) | PPC (Google Ads) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How you pay | Time or a provider's fee; price not published in sources checked | Per click, set by auction |
| Speed | Google: changes take "a few hours" to "several months" | Ads can run once the account is approved and live |
| Control over position | None; Google says you cannot pay for ranking | Budget, bids, keywords and location are yours |
| When you stop | Rankings can persist, but are not guaranteed | Ads stop showing |
| Best for | Steady, lower-cost visibility over time | Testing offers and covering slow periods |

Neither is better in every case. Your choice depends on how fast you need customers, what a new customer is worth and how much competition you face.

Revenue of about $700 against $600 of ads leaves little for materials and labour, so this plumber should improve the landing page or raise the job value before scaling. The same arithmetic for a café, with a $2.05 click, looks very different, because the click is cheaper. Work out your own numbers before you spend.

## How Long Does SEO Take?

Google says some changes "might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months," and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether your work helped. It gives no standard timeline for rankings.

Treat any promise of a fixed number of months with caution. For a local business, the fastest free step is your Business Profile. Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and 15-step local SEO guide.

## Which Should You Start With?

Use these rules as a starting point, not a verified formula:

- **You need customers this month:** start a small PPC test on high-intent searches, and fix your Business Profile the same week.
- **You can wait several months:** spend time on your Business Profile, reviews and one useful page per service. [Local SEO costs](/blog/how-much-does-local-seo-cost-for-a-small-business-honest-pricing-guide) vary by provider.
- **Your customer is worth little per visit:** lean on SEO and repeat-visit tools such as email, since a $5 sale cannot carry a $2 click.
- **Your customer is worth a lot:** PPC is easier to justify, because one booking can cover several clicks.

Track which channel each lead came from with a call-tracking number or a form question, so you do not credit PPC for a call that came from the Map Pack.

## Can You Run SEO and PPC Together?

Yes, and many businesses do. PPC can test which keywords and offers bring bookings, then you build pages and reviews around those. Google does not link the two: ads do not boost organic ranking, so the benefit comes from your own testing, not from Google.

A sensible split depends on your budget. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows how to size the paid part from the value of a customer.

## FAQ

### What is the main difference between SEO and PPC?

SEO earns organic visibility without paying per click. PPC buys placement and charges per click on Google Ads. Google says paying for ads does not affect organic results.

### Which is cheaper, SEO or PPC?

No source checked publishes an SEO price. PPC costs are published: LocaliQ's 2026 average is $5.42 per click, from $2.05 for restaurants to $8.33 for home improvement.

### How long does SEO take to work?

Google says changes can take effect in hours or take months. It gives no fixed timeline, so be wary of promises.

### Can I use both SEO and PPC?

Yes. Ads and organic results are separate in Google's systems, so run both if your budget allows and track leads by source.

### What happens if I stop paying for PPC?

Your ads stop showing. Organic and Map results are not paid, so they stay, but they are not guaranteed to hold.

### Is Google Business Profile SEO or PPC?

It is part of local SEO. Google says you cannot pay for a better local ranking, but you can complete the profile and collect reviews.
