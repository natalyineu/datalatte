The best food delivery platform for advertising in Europe is the one with the most active customers in your own city, not the biggest brand overall. Deliveroo operates in the UK, Ireland, France, Italy and Belgium among other markets, Wolt covers the Nordics, Germany, the Baltics and Central and Eastern Europe, Glovo runs in 21 countries across Europe, Central Asia and Africa, and Uber Eats is present in many large cities. Check which apps your customers already use, then compare commission, ad pricing and order volume on that platform.

Friday, 7:45 pm. A 25-pound order lands on the tablet. On Uber Eats UK's published 30% rate for Uber delivery, 7.50 pounds of that never reaches you. Now someone offers to sell you a "sponsored" slot on top. Before you pay for visibility, know what an order is worth to you. The worked example below shows how.

Market presence changes as these companies expand, exit markets and merge. DoorDash, for example, completed its acquisition of Deliveroo on October 2, 2025 ([Deliveroo](https://deliveroo.co.uk/more/news-articles/doordash-deliveroo-acquisition)) and already owned Wolt. Confirm which platforms operate in your area, and verify fees and ad formats in each platform's merchant dashboard or with your account manager before committing.

## How to Choose the Right Platform

Use these factors to compare platforms in your city:

1. **Customer overlap.** Ask your regulars which delivery app they use, or check your own order history and Google reviews.
2. **Commission and fees.** Commissions differ by country, plan and whether the platform's couriers deliver. As one published example, Uber Eats UK lists a 30% fee for Uber delivery, 13% for self-delivery and 13% for pickup orders, plus a one-off activation fee ([Uber Eats UK](https://merchants.ubereats.com/gb/en/pricing/), checked Oct 2026). Wolt charges a percentage of the order subtotal, with a lower rate for pickup ([Wolt](https://merchant.wolt.com/learning-center/wolt-merchant-fees-and-commissions)). Confirm your exact rate in writing.
3. **Delivery area.** Make sure the platform covers the neighborhoods where your best customers live.
4. **Ad options and minimums.** Compare cost models, minimum spend and reporting.
5. **Contract terms.** Check exclusivity, notice periods and promotion obligations.

<Callout type="tip">Read the merchant terms carefully before signing up. Some platforms limit when and how you can promote prices elsewhere, or tie promotions to commission tiers.</Callout>

## Cost per Click vs. Cost per Order

Platforms sell visibility in a few ways, and the model changes your math:

- **Cost per click (CPC):** you pay when someone taps your ad, whether or not they order. Wolt uses this for sponsored products and collections.
- **Cost per order (CPO or CPA):** you pay only when an order comes through the promoted placement. Wolt's sponsored listings work this way.
- **Cost per thousand impressions (CPM):** you pay for views. Wolt uses this for sponsored banners.
- **Flat fee or percentage uplift:** you pay more commission or a fixed fee for featured placement.

Not every platform or market offers every model, and Wolt's own page does not state a minimum budget. To compare them, use one formula:

**Ad cost per order = ad spend divided by orders attributed to the ad.**

If that number is above your profit per order, the ad loses money on that sale, even if the customer comes back later. Run a two-week test with a small budget on one platform, record spend and orders, then decide.

## The Math: What an Order Is Worth

Example only. The commission rates are the Uber Eats UK figures quoted above (30% for Uber delivery, 13% for pickup). The 25-pound basket and the 35% food and packaging cost are assumptions. Swap in your own numbers and your own contract rate.

| Line | Uber delivery order | Pickup order |
|------|--------------------|--------------|
| Basket | 25.00 | 25.00 |
| Food and packaging (assume 35%) | 8.75 | 8.75 |
| Commission (30% / 13%) | 7.50 | 3.25 |
| Profit before ads | 8.75 | 13.00 |

Now the ad. Assume you spend 100 pounds on sponsored placement and the platform attributes 12 orders to it, all delivery orders.

- Ad cost per order: 100 / 12 = 8.33.
- Profit per ad order: 8.75 - 8.33 = 0.42.

You made 5 pounds over the whole test (12 x 0.42). That is a break-even result. It only pays if a decent share of those 12 customers order again at no ad cost. At 11 orders instead of 12, the ad cost per order becomes 9.09 and you lose money on every sale. Small changes decide it, which is why you should track the number weekly.

## Free Ways to Win More Delivery Orders

Before you pay for ads, fix what platforms already reward:

- **Photos:** bright, clear photos for your top 5 sellers.
- **Menu:** clear names, short descriptions, and hide items you cannot reliably prepare.
- **Ratings and reviews:** reply to every review and fix recurring complaints.
- **Prep time and availability:** keep opening hours accurate and avoid cancellations.
- **Offers:** a small discount or free item on a minimum order during quiet periods, not all day.

## Measuring Success

Track these numbers weekly for each platform:

| Metric | How to calculate | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Ad cost per order | Ad spend divided by ad-attributed orders | Whether ads are profitable |
| Profit per order | Order value minus food, packaging, commission and discounts | Your margin ceiling for ad spend |
| Conversion rate | Orders divided by listing visits or clicks, if the platform reports it | Whether your menu and photos convert |
| Repeat orders | Customers who order again within 30 to 60 days | Whether ads are building loyalty |
| Share of paid vs. organic orders | Compare weeks with and without ads | Whether you are paying for orders you would get anyway |

<Callout type="warning">Track customer acquisition cost against lifetime value. If paid delivery orders never repeat and your margin is thin, shift effort to direct ordering and local search instead.</Callout>

## Do This Today

1. Pull one week of delivery orders. Compute average basket and your real food and packaging cost.
2. Multiply by your contract commission. That is your profit per order.
3. Ask your account manager for ad pricing and the attribution window in writing.
4. Set a two-week test of one format on one platform, with a budget you can lose.
5. Ask your next 10 delivery customers whether they would order direct if you offered it.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best food delivery platform for a small restaurant in Europe?**
A: It depends on your city and customers. Check which apps dominate locally, compare commission rates, and run a short test on the platform with the most active customers in your area.

**Q: How much does food delivery advertising cost?**
A: It varies by platform, country and format. On top of ad spend you pay commission on orders; Uber Eats UK, for example, publishes 30% for Uber delivery and 13% for pickup. Wolt Ads can bill per order, per click or per thousand impressions depending on the format. Ask for your exact rate and ad pricing.

**Q: Can I use multiple food delivery platforms at once?**
A: Often, but check each contract for exclusivity clauses. Using several platforms can widen reach, but it adds menu management and fees, so track profit per platform separately.

**Q: Are sponsored placements on delivery apps worth it?**
A: They can be if your ad cost per order stays below your profit per order and customers come back. Test with a small budget for two weeks and compare to periods without ads.

**Q: How can I get more delivery orders without paying for ads?**
A: Improve photos, menu clarity, ratings and prep times, use targeted discounts in slow periods, and promote direct ordering to repeat customers.
