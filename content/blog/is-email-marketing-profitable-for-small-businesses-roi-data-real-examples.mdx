## Is Email Marketing Profitable for a Small Business?

Profit depends on whether emails bring back customers who would not have returned anyway. Winback Engine's guide reports that automated email and SMS campaigns typically recover 2–5% of lapsed customers. That is a useful planning range, not a guarantee, and it comes from a vendor, so treat it as directional.

Small lists can make averages misleading. Mailchimp's benchmark data only tracked campaigns sent to at least 1,000 subscribers, so open and click averages drawn from that data may not describe a list of 300 people. Single-number ROI multiples have the same weakness, because they rarely show the customer count, spend, or costs behind them.

The practical test is straightforward. Compare the revenue an email sequence brings in against the tool cost, staff time, and any discounts. Use a window you set in advance, such as 60 days (an assumption to adjust to your visit cycle). If the margin is positive over that window, the channel is paying. If not, the offer or the list is usually the problem.

## How Much Does Email Marketing Software Cost for Small Businesses?

Tool choice matters less than list quality, but price differences are real. The Stack Reviewer, a secondary review source, states that MailerLite is roughly half the price of Klaviyo for email campaigns plus basic automations. The right comparison is the set of features you will actually use each month, not the full feature list.

Builderall's roundup describes SendinBlue as combining email marketing, SMS campaigns, and marketing automation, and as suited to both small businesses and large enterprises. That mix helps if you want SMS and email in one workflow. Test which channel your customers respond to before paying for both.

Check current prices on each vendor's pricing page before you budget. Review-site figures go stale quickly, and plan tiers change with list size, so confirm the number that applies to your subscriber count.

## What Does a Worked Email ROI Example Look Like for a Coffee Shop?

This model uses one sourced input, the 2–5% recovery range, and labels every other figure as an assumption. Replace the assumptions with your own sales data before making any decision.

Inputs:

- Lapsed customers on the list (assumption): 400
- Recovery rate (sourced range): 2% low case, 5% high case
- Return visits per recovered customer in the campaign window (assumption): 2
- Average spend per visit (assumption): $12
- Monthly email tool cost (assumption): $30
- Staff time to write and schedule (assumption): 2 hours at $20/hour, so $40
- Total campaign cost (assumption-based): $70

| Scenario | Recovered customers | Revenue | Revenue per $1 of cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low (2%) | 8 | $192 | $2.74 |
| High (5%) | 20 | $480 | $6.86 |

Revenue is not profit. At an assumed 60% gross margin, the low case returns about $115 of gross profit, or $1.60 per $1 spent. The high case returns about $288, or $4.10 per $1. The low case barely clears cost on gross profit, which shows that list size and average spend decide whether a campaign pays. Rerun the table with your own counts before committing.

## Which Emails Should a Small Business Send First?

RingCentral's guide lists email follow-up sequences among the repetitive, rule-based marketing tasks that can be automated, alongside segmentation and campaign reporting. Start with flows triggered by a customer action, not by a calendar date. These run without weekly manual effort once built.

For most service and retail businesses, a sensible first set is a welcome message, a post-visit follow-up, and a win-back sequence for customers who have stopped returning. Keep the first version to two or three emails you can measure. A small set you can track beats a long calendar you cannot.

## How Do You Measure Email Profit Without Industry Benchmarks?

If your list has fewer than 1,000 subscribers, Mailchimp's published benchmarks were not built around your size. Measure your own trend instead. Compare each sequence against the same period before it started, using the same channel and offer.

Give each campaign a unique discount code or dedicated booking link, so revenue can be tied to it. A coffee shop can print a code on receipts or menu flyers for the same purpose. For coffee-specific setups, see the [coffee shop email marketing examples](/blog/coffee-shop-email-marketing-examples).

Track unsubscribes and complaints alongside revenue. A campaign that brings in sales while pushing people off the list is reducing its own future value.

## Do Customers Want Business Emails?

Square's Future of Commerce 2025 report, cited on its Canadian coffee shop marketing page, found that 65% of Canadians like businesses to communicate with them by email. That is a Canadian figure. Do not apply it directly to US or UK lists without local survey data.

Discovery usually happens online first. The Restaurant HQ cites Statista data showing that 98% of consumers use the internet to find new businesses. Email's job is to turn that first visit into a repeat one, and that only works with people who opted in.

Consent matters more than reach. Email only people who signed up, make unsubscribing easy, and check the consent rules that apply in your country before sending.

## FAQ

### Is email marketing still profitable for small businesses in 2026?

It can be, but profitability depends on your list, offers, and costs rather than on a universal ROI multiple. The worked example above shows how to test it with your own numbers.

### How many subscribers do I need before email marketing pays?

No sourced threshold for profitability exists. Mailchimp's benchmark data covers campaigns sent to at least 1,000 subscribers, so below that size, compare results against your own history rather than published averages.

### How often should a small business send email?

The sources here do not set a frequency. Choose a cadence you can sustain and watch unsubscribes. Sequences triggered by customer actions need less manual scheduling than a fixed calendar.

### Should I use email or SMS?

Some platforms offer both in one tool, such as SendinBlue according to Builderall's roundup. Test which channel your customers respond to, and check consent rules for each channel before sending.
