## What Are Nextdoor Neighborhood Sponsorships?

Nextdoor describes them as a local marketing solution for attracting new and loyal customers in your area. Sponsors can use Tips and Advice Ads to share expertise, Conversation Starter Ads to field neighbor questions one-on-one, and custom Business Posts, including events and polls ([Nextdoor for Business](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/become-a-sponsor)).

An Adweek report on the nationwide rollout says a business first sets up a free business page with its name, location, contact details and up to 10 service categories, then picks ZIP codes in a Sponsorships or Manage Plan tab that shows availability and pricing. You do not need to live in a ZIP code to sponsor it ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/?p=1228369), published before 2026, so details may have changed).

## How Much Do Neighborhood Sponsorships Cost?

No figure is published. Neither Nextdoor page we opened lists a price, and the Adweek report points to the Manage Plan tab. A search result mentioned a third-party price range, but we could not verify it on Nextdoor, so we do not repeat it.

Check price and availability by ZIP code in your account before you commit. Sponsorships appear to be sold by ZIP code, and availability may vary from one to the next. For paid ads on a budget basis, see our [Nextdoor ads cost guide](/blog/nextdoor-ads-cost-2026).

## Is It the Same as Nextdoor Ads?

No. Ads Manager is a self-serve platform where you set a budget and bids. Sponsorships are a separate offering sold by neighborhood. We could not open a Nextdoor page that compares billing terms, so confirm whether the plan renews monthly and how to cancel before you pay. Our [Nextdoor ads setup guide](/blog/nextdoor-ads-setup-guide-local-business) covers the self-serve route.

## Which Businesses Might Fit?

Businesses where trust and a short distance matter, such as home services, salons, cafes and pet care. Nextdoor's page features a home services business as its testimonial, but one story proves nothing about your results. The format asks you to answer neighbors' questions, so it suits owners who will reply fast.

## The Math: How Would You Judge a 60-Day Test?

All figures are **assumptions**, because Nextdoor does not publish prices here.

| Input | Value | Basis |

| Monthly cost for 2 ZIP codes | $300 | assumption |
| Test length | 2 months | assumption |

| New customers who say Nextdoor | 6 | assumption |
| Average first sale | $120 | assumption |
| First-sale revenue | $720 | 6 x $120 |

At these inputs the test pays back on first sales. If only 3 customers came, the cost per customer would be $200 and the test would lose money unless they return. Ask every new customer how they found you, and record "Nextdoor" separately from "Google".

## How Do You Make the Posts Work?

Use the formats as they are named. A Tips and Advice post can answer a question your customers ask, a Conversation Starter can invite questions, and an event post can promote an open day. Keep them specific to the neighborhood and reply promptly. Skip generic discounts as the first message.

## FAQ

### How much do Nextdoor Neighborhood Sponsorships cost?
Nextdoor's pages we opened do not state a price. Check the Manage Plan tab in your account for each ZIP code.

### Do I need a Nextdoor Business Page first?
Per the Adweek report, a free business page is the first step.

### Can I sponsor a ZIP code where I do not live?
Per the same report, account holders do not need to live in a ZIP code to sponsor it.

### How do I know it worked?
Ask new customers how they found you and compare against the months before. Do not rely on one testimonial.

### Is it better than Google Ads?
Not comparable on published data. Google Ads captures people searching now, while Nextdoor reaches neighbors browsing. Start with your free profile and reviews.
