Promote a pop-up shop in four weeks by splitting your spend 20% early, 50% in the middle and 30% in the final week. Announce the dates and address on every channel your customers already use, and collect email sign-ups at the counter. Sort out permits before anything goes public, because marketing cannot rescue a shop that cannot open. Paid ads are an optional test, not the core of the plan.

This guide suits owners opening a short-term shop in a market, mall unit, vacant store or shared space. Pop-up sales data is rarely published by neutral sources, so every dollar figure in the worked example is an assumption to replace with your own numbers.

## What Should You Sort Out Before You Promote?

Confirm that the pop-up can trade legally before any announcement goes out. Ask the city office, the landlord or the market organiser about permits, insurance, signage and sales tax for temporary sellers. Licensing and zoning rules differ by city, so the answers you need are local, not general.

Get the opening date in writing from the landlord or organiser, then build your calendar backwards from it. If the date moves after you have posted it, your audience hears about the change twice, and the second message is harder to get right than the first.

Keep a short file with the permit confirmations, the lease or market agreement and the insurance certificate. Staff and sales partners will ask for these details during the run, and having them ready keeps the opening week focused on customers.

## How Should the Four Weeks Be Timed?

Livestorm recommends splitting online event promotion as 20% early, 50% in the middle and 30% in the final week, rather than front-loading it. Applied to four weeks, the first week carries the smallest share, weeks two and three carry the middle half, and the final week carries 30%. Livestorm writes about online events, so treat the split as a planning pattern to test.

Timing also depends on how far buyers travel. Livestorm advises starting the promotion two to three months out for in-person events, because of travel and tickets. AdWave says smaller community events might need only 4-6 weeks, and it states that starting heavy promotion two weeks before the event rarely works. A pop-up in a busy high street can work on four weeks, but one that needs visitors to travel should start announcing earlier.

| Week | Focus | Share of promotion spend |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Announce dates, address and one reason to visit | 20% |
| Week 2 | Partner posts and a first round of reminders | 25% (assumption: half split evenly across weeks 2 and 3) |
| Week 3 | Product teasers and a second reminder to the list | 25% (assumption) |
| Week 4 | Final push, stock and hours updates | 30% |

## Where Should the Announcement Go?

Post the announcement on your existing Business Profile, in your email list and on the social channels your customers already use. Before creating a second listing for a short-term location, read Google's current Business Profile guidelines, since they set rules for showing a staffed, signed address. Keep each post to the dates, the address, the hours and one reason to visit.

For posting frequency, Bellwether Coffee's FAQ recommends three to five feed posts per week on Instagram plus daily Stories for a coffee shop. It estimates 5-7 hours per week for an active presence on one or two platforms. That guidance is for cafés, but it gives a realistic benchmark for a small team. If scheduling takes too long, the [AI tools for coffee shop marketing automation](/blog/ai-tools-for-coffee-shop-marketing-automation) guide covers low-effort options.

Local neighbourhood platforms can help. AdWave cites a Sparklight breakdown in which 88% of Nextdoor members shop at local businesses at least once a week. Check whether your area uses Nextdoor before relying on it. For event-style promotion ideas, the [event marketing for local business](/blog/event-marketing-for-local-business) guide gives more channel options.

## Should You Run Paid Ads for a Pop-Up?

Paid media is optional. StackAdapt advises advertisers to start increasing bids one week before a major sale or event, so they gain exposure before the rush of ad traffic begins. That article is dated 2021, so check the current bidding options in your ad platform before you set anything up.

Use a tight location radius and an end date that matches the closing day. The aim is to reach people near the shop during the run, not to keep the campaign live afterwards. Set a small daily budget and review it after the first few days, rather than committing the whole promotion budget up front.

Out-of-home options cost more. AdWave's budget table lists $400-$1,500 in total for a TV event promotion run 3-4 weeks before the event. That is a large commitment for most pop-ups, so compare it with the sales you expect. Screen-based options are covered in the [DOOH event marketing for local business](/blog/dooh-event-marketing-local-business) guide.

## What Does the Budget Need to Return?

This worked example uses assumptions throughout. Replace them with your own figures before you decide.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Promotion budget | $500 |
| Spend, week 1 (20%) | $100 |
| Spend, weeks 2 and 3 (50%) | $250 |
| Spend, week 4 (30%) | $150 |
| Average basket | $45 |
| Buyers from the pop-up | 25 |
| Gross margin | 50% |

| Step | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | 25 buyers × $45 | $1,125 |
| Gross profit | $1,125 × 50% | $562.50 |
| Result after promotion | $562.50 - $500 | $62.50 |
| Break-even buyers | $500 ÷ ($45 × 50%), rounded up | 23 buyers |

The example ignores rent, staffing, stock write-offs and card fees. Add those before deciding, because a pop-up that clears $62.50 after promotion may not cover its running costs. If your basket is larger or your margin is higher, break-even falls to fewer buyers.

## How Do You Capture Customers After It Closes?

Collect contact details at the counter, with clear consent for email or text, and send one follow-up within a week. Offer sign-up in exchange for early notice of the next pop-up, which gives people a specific reason to join. The [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) guide includes prompts you can adapt for that follow-up message.

Offers can drive visits during the run. AdWave's guide gives 20% off when customers shop in-store as an illustrative example, not a sourced statistic, so test it against your own margin before you use it. Keep the offer simple and time-limited.

If the pop-up runs on Small Business Saturday, Vision Monday reports that businesses do not need to pay or officially register to run a promotion. Listing on the official Shop Small map has its own rules, including a 25-location cap.

Track visitors, sales and email sign-ups for each week. Ask buyers how they heard about the shop and tally the answers, so the next pop-up repeats the channels that worked.

## FAQ

### How far ahead should I promote a pop-up shop?
Livestorm advises starting two to three months out for in-person events. AdWave says smaller community events might need only 4-6 weeks. A four-week plan suits a local pop-up, but start earlier if buyers need to travel.

### Do I need a permit for a pop-up shop?
Check with your city office, landlord or market organiser. Rules differ by city, and the answers decide your opening date, so confirm them before you announce.

### Can I run a promotion without registering?
Vision Monday reports that businesses do not need to pay or officially register just to run a promotion. Listing on the official Shop Small map has its own rules, including a 25-location cap.

### Should I run Google Ads for a pop-up?
Only as a small, time-limited test if people already search for what you sell. StackAdapt advises raising bids one week before a major event, but its article is dated 2021, so check your platform's current settings.

### How do I measure success?
Count visitors, sales and email sign-ups each week, and ask buyers how they heard about the shop. Repeat the channels that brought the most buyers.
