Social media listening means tracking where people mention your studio, your class names or your competitors, then acting on what they say. A fitness studio can do this at no cost with Google Alerts and Google review notifications. Paid AI tools exist: Brand24 lists plans from $249 a month ($199 on annual billing), with a 14-day free trial, which is a lot for a single studio.

This guide compares the free and paid options using vendor pages read on 31 October 2026, and shows a weekly routine for a studio owner. No independent data shows how much listening raises sign-ups for studios, so none is claimed.

## What should a fitness studio listen for?

Four things are worth watching, because each one leads to an action:

- Your studio name and common misspellings
- Your class names and instructors
- Neighbourhood plus a service, such as "pilates near" a street or district
- Competitor studio names

Each mention is either a review to answer, a question to answer publicly, a complaint to fix or a gap in what you offer. Everything else is noise. If you have no plan for an action, do not pay for a tool.

## What does the free route cover?

Google Alerts emails you when new Google Search results match a topic you follow. Per Google's help page, you can set how often you are notified, the types of sites, the language, the region and how many results you get. It does not cover posts inside Instagram or TikTok that Google does not show in search results.

Google Business Profile covers reviews. Google's help says you must verify your business to reply, that replies are checked against content policies, and that the reviewer is notified when you reply. Google also says helpful, positive replies can show you are responsive. You can share a review link or QR code to ask for reviews.

For Instagram and TikTok, check tagged posts and comments by hand in each app. See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) for review basics.

Mention's page showed no plan prices, only "pay yearly and get 2 months free", and said advanced Boolean alerts accept queries up to 2,000 characters. Confirm both on the vendor sites. For one studio with a few hundred followers, these plans are likely more than the volume of mentions justifies.

## Worked example: a 20-minute weekly routine

All figures are **assumptions**.

| Task | Time | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read the week's Google Alerts email | 5 min | $0 |
| Reply to new Google reviews | 8 min | $0 |
| Scan Instagram tags and TikTok comments | 7 min | $0 |
| Total | 20 min | $0 |

At 20 minutes a week, the owner spends about 87 minutes a month. At a staff cost of $20 an hour, that is about $29 a month, against $249 for Brand24. The paid tool would only make sense if it saved more than 12 hours a month or found mentions the free routine misses. Test this by running Brand24's free trial for 14 days and counting the extra mentions that led to an action.

## FAQ

### Is social listening worth it for one studio?

For most single studios the free routine is enough. A paid tool needs enough mention volume to justify $249 a month.

### What does Google Alerts monitor?

New Google Search results matching your topic. It will not see everything posted inside social apps.

### Is Brand24 free?

It offers a free trial of 14 days, with no card required per its FAQ. Plans start at $249 a month, or $199 on annual billing.

### Should I reply to every review?

Google says helpful and positive replies show you are responsive. Replies are reviewed against content policies before publishing.

### Can I monitor competitors?

Yes, by adding their names as alert keywords. Respect each platform's terms.
