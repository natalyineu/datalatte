Google Business Profile analytics sit in the Performance report. A user signs in with the Google Account linked to a verified profile, opens Performance, chooses a time period and clicks Apply. The report covers views, searches, calls, directions, website clicks and, where they apply, messages and bookings. Google counts a unique viewer at most once a day and filters out multiple taps and spam, so the figures will not match a phone log exactly. Each metric measures a different customer action, and reading them correctly matters more than collecting every number.

This guide explains where to find the data, what each metric counts, why it differs from other records, and how to keep a simple monthly record that supports decisions.

## Where Do You Find Business Profile Analytics?

There are two ways to open the report, and both show the same underlying data:

- **Google Search.** Go to your Business Profile and click Performance. Select a time period at the top and click Apply.
- **Google Maps app.** Open the app, tap Business at the bottom right, then tap See more next to Performance. Select a period and tap Apply.

Performance data is available only for verified Business Profiles, and you must be signed in to the Google Account linked to the profile. If the profile is not verified, the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers the setup steps first. Google also notes that the Insights suggestions may not appear for every profile, so treat them as optional.

## What Does Each Metric Count?

Google's definitions, simplified into what to watch for:

| Metric | What it counts | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Views | People who viewed the profile on Search and Maps | Unique visitors, counted once a day |
| Searches | Search terms people used to find the business | Updated at the start of each month, up to 5 days to appear |
| Calls | Clicks on the call button | Needs a phone number on the profile; counts taps, not answered calls |
| Directions | People who asked for directions | Multiple taps, cancelled requests and spam are filtered out |
| Website clicks | Clicks on the website link | Compare with the site's own analytics |
| Messages | Distinct message conversations | Shows only if messaging applies to the business |
| Bookings | Completed bookings | Needs bookings managed through a booking provider |

The report includes activity from both organic results and Google Ads, so it does not isolate one source. Only the metrics that apply to a business appear. The [performance and insights help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en) lists the full set before a report is built around one number.

## Why Don't the Numbers Match the Phone Log?

Business Profile metrics measure customer actions on Google, not the whole business. A caller who finds the number on a website is not a Performance call. A customer who views the profile twice in one day is counted once for Views. A tap on the call button is counted even if nobody answers.

That gap is normal. Use profile numbers to see trends in how people find and contact the business, and use the phone system, booking tool or point-of-sale records to count revenue. Mixing the two produces false conclusions, such as assuming every call becomes a customer.

Views can also look lower than the notifications or emails Google sends. The help page gives the reasons: unique visitors are counted, visits across devices and platforms can be merged, and repeat visits on the same day are not added. This is a measurement rule, not a sign that the profile is failing.

## How Does Local Ranking Affect These Numbers?

Views and searches depend partly on how Google ranks a profile. Google states that local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance comes from complete, detailed business information. Distance is how far the business is from the searcher. Prominence reflects how well known the business is, including how many websites link to it and how many reviews and ratings it has.

Google also states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Analytics therefore cannot be bought up. The practical response to weak numbers is to improve the profile: accurate categories, correct hours, a service area that matches where the business really works, and timely review replies. The [coffee shop profile tips](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips) show how these edits apply to a single location.

## What Does Google Analytics Add to Profile Data?

Google Analytics is a free tool, and it shows what people do on a website after they click through from a profile. Website clicks in Performance show how many people visited the site, but not what they did next. A GA4 property answers that question. The [Google Analytics 4 guide for small businesses](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business) covers the basic setup and the reports most owners need.

Google's own case studies show what the tool can do for organisations that track their sites closely. Lider reported an 85% decrease in cost per acquisition and an 18x conversion rate improvement after using Analytics to re-engage users, and McDonald's Hong Kong reported a 550% increase in in-app orders with GA4. A customer quoted on the same page, 412 Food Rescue, said it cut reporting time by 50%. These are large, app-focused organisations, so they are not a forecast for a local shop. They show the kind of website behaviour the tool can measure.

## How Do You Turn the Numbers Into Decisions?

Keep the record small so it stays up to date. Once a month, copy five figures into a spreadsheet: views, calls, directions, website clicks and the top five searches. Add a column for each change made that month, such as new photos, a category edit or updated hours. Compare each month with the one before.

A worked example for a mobile pet grooming business shows how to connect calls to revenue. Every input below is an assumption, so replace each one with real figures:

| Input or result | Month 1 | Month 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calls from profile (assumption) | 40 | 60 |
| Calls answered (assumption: 70%) | 28 | 42 |
| Bookings from answered calls (assumption: 50%) | 14 | 21 |
| Revenue per booking (assumption: $60) | $840 | $1,260 |

In this example, calls rise by 20, which adds 7 bookings and $420 in revenue for the month. The result depends entirely on the assumed answer and booking rates. Measure real rates for two or three months before relying on the figure. Google counts taps on the call button, so the booking step has to come from the business's own records. ## FAQ

### Is Google My Business the same as Google Business Profile?

Google renamed it. Its help pages now use Business Profile, and the report is called Performance.

### Why do the calls differ from phone records?

Google counts clicks on the call button, not completed or answered calls. Calls that start from a website or another directory are not counted in the profile figures.

### How often does the data update?

Google states the timing only for Searches, which update at the start of each month and can take up to 5 days to appear. The help pages give no schedule for the other metrics.

### Do I need a booking system to see booking data?

Yes. Bookings are counted only when they are managed through a booking provider, so profiles without one will not show this metric.
