Under UK PECR, prior consent is required before non-essential cookies are placed or read, and the rejection option must be as prominent as acceptance. Many sites fall short: a 2025 cross-country study found only 15% of websites were minimally GDPR-compliant. US-only sites have lighter rules but still need a privacy policy and honest disclosure. ## Do You Need a Cookie Banner?

Ask three questions. Do visitors from the UK or EU reach your site? Do you load analytics, advertising pixels or embedded tools that set cookies? Do you collect personal data such as emails, bookings or phone numbers? If the first two answers are yes, plan for consent before non-essential cookies load.

Under UK PECR, prior consent is needed before non-essential cookies are placed or read. The ICO says rejection must be as prominent as acceptance, a point the [GDPR guide for local business websites](/blog/local-business-website-gdpr-compliance) covers in more detail.

| Your situation | Likely need |
| --- | --- |
| Local shop, UK or EU visitors, uses analytics or ad pixels | Consent banner that blocks those tags until accepted |
| US-only audience, no ad pixels, few cookies | Privacy policy that lists your tools |
| US audience with ad pixels and state privacy rules | Check each state's rules and your opt-out options |
| Site loads only essential cookies | Disclose them in the policy |

## What Makes a Consent Choice Valid?

Where prior consent applies, non-essential categories must stay disabled until the visitor makes the matching affirmative choice. A banner that loads tags first and asks afterwards does not meet that test.

Reject should be as easy to choose as accept. A "Manage" button alone is weak if "Reject all" is buried. Keep both options on the first layer of the banner.

Global Privacy Control is not a general substitute for the consent process under EU GDPR, ePrivacy or UK PECR. Honouring the signal is useful, but it does not replace a banner for those visitors.

Rejecting does not always stop tracking. In a 2024 PrivacyTerms report, summarised by Adwave, 65.4% of websites offering a reject option still collected user data after refusal. Enforcement is also real: Hustler Marketing reports that over 500 small entities were fined by GDPR authorities in 2025 for inadequate consent in automated tools (secondary).

## Which Cookies Last How Long?

The table below lists maximum storage durations from third-party cookie pages. Use it as a reference when you audit your tags. Vendors update their cookies, so confirm each one on the vendor's current page.

| Cookie | What it does (per source) | Maximum storage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CookieConsentBulkSetting (Cookiebot) | Enables cookie consent across multiple websites | 1 year |
| OptanonConsent | Records whether the visitor accepted the consent box | 1 year |
| cookie-consent | Stores the consent state for the current domain | 1 day |
| cookieyes-consent (CookieYes) | Remembers consent preferences | 1 year |
| buid | Supports the website's login function | 30 days |
| pscd | Core functionality, visitor attribution, session continuity | 3 months |
| ajs_group_id (Segment) | Tracks visitor usage and events | 1 year |
| _calendly_session (Calendly) | Lets the meeting scheduler work on the site | 21 days |
| pagead/1p-user-list (Google) | Tracks interest in products; described as facilitating referral fees | 6 months |
| personalization_id (Twitter) | Lets visitors share content to their Twitter profile | 400 days |
| attribution_user_id (Typeform) | Used with pop-up questionnaires for statistical or marketing purposes | 1 year |

Durations show how long a choice or identifier persists on the visitor's device. Match each cookie to a banner category before you publish, and check the category with a qualified adviser where the purpose is unclear.

## How Do Retargeting Pixels and Forms Fit In?

Pixel-based retargeting places a pixel on your site that drops an anonymous cookie in the visitor's browser. Ad networks use that cookie to show ads later. Adwave describes the pixel as identifying visitors so they can see ads on other sites and social media until they convert or opt out (secondary). Treat it as a non-essential category.

Meta's pixel must be included in your website cookie policy if it tracks user activity under GDPR, according to WasteNot (secondary). Forms and pop-ups set cookies too, so list them in the same audit.

StackAdapt's cookie explainer states that sensitive information, such as an address or phone number, is not stored unless the user gives explicit consent (secondary). Keep that in mind when you design booking or quote forms.

## Does Consent Change Email and Text Marketing?

Consent rules also apply to messages. Textdrip's guidance says a business must obtain the recipient's explicit consent before sending messages, and sending without consent violates compliance rules and can lead to penalties (secondary). For Canadian recipients, Adwave's cold-email guide says CASL generally requires express consent or narrowly defined implied consent before a commercial electronic message is sent (secondary).

Stricter opt-in can help results too. Hustler Marketing reports that GDPR-style opt-in consent can improve email open rates by 20% to 30% (secondary). That is a vendor claim, so test it on your own list. The [A/B testing guide for small business emails](/blog/ab-testing-emails-small-business-guide) explains how to run that comparison.

## How Do You Audit Your Consent Setup?

Work through these steps once a quarter and after adding any new tool.

1. List every tag on the site: analytics, ad pixels, chat widgets, booking embeds and form plugins.
2. For each tag, note the cookies it sets and the maximum storage from the table above or the vendor's page.
3. Load the site in a private browser window and leave the banner untouched. Confirm that non-essential tags do not fire.
4. Click Reject and confirm the same tags stay off. Check again, because the 2024 study suggests some tags keep collecting after refusal.
5. Check that Reject is as prominent as Accept on both mobile and desktop.
6. Confirm the visitor's choice is stored and respected on the next page load.
7. Record the date and any tool versions, then repeat after every change.

Tag-scanning tools can speed this up. The [AI marketing tools guide for small business](/blog/ai-marketing-tools-for-small-business) compares options, but a manual pass is still the reliable check.

## What Does Consent Cost in Reporting?

Declined visitors drop out of analytics, so your reports undercount. The worked example below uses assumed inputs, which you should replace with your own figures.

| Input | Value (assumption) |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly site visits | 4,000 |
| Share of visitors who decline analytics | 30% |
| Conversion rate across all visits | 3% |
| Declined visits per month | 1,200 (4,000 × 30%) |
| Conversions from declined visits, if the rate is the same | 36 a month (1,200 × 3%) |

At these assumptions, about 36 enquiries a month would not appear in analytics. Check totals against booking, call and form logs before cutting spend based on analytics alone. Ongoing checks like these belong in regular site upkeep; the [website maintenance cost guide for small businesses](/blog/website-maintenance-cost-small-business) explains what to budget.

## What About US-Only Sites?

If every visitor is in the US, UK and EU consent rules may not apply. You still need a clear privacy policy that lists the tools and tags you run. State laws vary, so check the rules where your customers live.

California's amended Automatic Renewal Law took effect on 1 July 2025 and requires express affirmative consent for automatic renewals (ArentFox Schiff, secondary). If you sell subscriptions or memberships online, check whether it applies to your offer.

## FAQ

### Do I need a cookie banner if all my customers are in the US?

Not always. US rules vary by state and by how you use data, so review your tools against each state's law. Keep a privacy policy either way.

### Does Global Privacy Control replace a banner for EU or UK visitors?

No. Global Privacy Control is not a general substitute for the consent process under EU GDPR, ePrivacy or UK PECR.

### Will a consent banner reduce my conversions?

The sources used here do not quantify this. Compare form and call volumes before and after the banner goes live, using the same traffic sources.

### Does rejecting cookies stop all tracking?

Not by itself. The 2024 PrivacyTerms report, summarised by Adwave, found 65.4% of sites with a reject option still collected data after refusal. Verify your tags in practice.

### Do I need consent to send texts or emails?

Usually yes. Text messages need explicit consent before sending, and Canadian commercial messages generally need express or narrowly implied consent under CASL.
