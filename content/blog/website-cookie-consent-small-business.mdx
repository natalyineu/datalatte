Whether you need a cookie banner depends on who visits your site and what runs on it. If you serve visitors in the EU or UK and use analytics or advertising cookies, you need consent before they load. If you only serve US visitors, California's law has size thresholds that many small shops fall under, but you still need a privacy policy and honest disclosure. ## Do You Need a Cookie Banner?

Ask three questions. First, do visitors from the EU or UK reach your site? Second, do you load analytics, advertising pixels or embeds that set cookies? Third, do you sell or share personal data in California?

| Your situation | Likely need |
| --- | --- |
| Local shop, EU or UK visitors, uses Google Analytics or Meta Pixel | Consent banner that blocks those tags until accepted |
| US-only audience, no ad pixels, few cookies | Privacy policy; banner may not be legally required, but check your state |
| US audience, ad pixels, California visitors, meets a CCPA threshold | Opt-out link and honouring Global Privacy Control |
| Site loads only essential cookies | Disclose them in the policy |

The EU rules on device storage are in the ePrivacy rules and GDPR; the UK has its own version. An attempt to load the ICO's cookie guidance timed out when checked, so confirm UK details on ico.org.uk.

## What Does GDPR Say About Consent?

Article 7(1) says the controller must be able to demonstrate that the person consented. Article 7(3) says withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it. In practice that means a "Reject" or "Manage" choice should be as easy to reach as "Accept", and you should keep a record of choices.

Fines in Article 83 reach up to EUR 10 million or 2% of worldwide annual turnover for some breaches, and up to EUR 20 million or 4% for breaches of core principles and data subjects' rights, whichever is higher. These are maximums, not typical amounts. The Article text was read on gdpr-info.eu, an unofficial copy, so treat it as secondary and confirm on EUR-Lex.

## Does CCPA Apply to a Small Business?

The California Attorney General's page says CCPA applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California and meet any one of three tests: gross annual revenue over $25 million; buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more California residents or households; or deriving 50% or more of annual revenue from selling residents' personal information.

Covered businesses must honour a user-enabled global privacy control as an opt-out request. The page lists no civil penalty amounts for enforcement. Under CCPA the usual approach is opt-out for sale or sharing, not an opt-in banner for every cookie. Other US states have their own laws; check each.

## How Does Google Consent Mode Fit In?

Consent Mode shares the visitor's choice with Google; it does not provide a banner. Google's documentation says that when visitors deny consent, tags send cookieless pings instead of storing cookies, no Analytics cookies are set or read, and Google uses modelling to fill gaps in Analytics. When ad storage is denied, no new advertising cookies are written. Expect less precise reporting from visitors who decline.

## FAQ

### Do I need a cookie banner if I only have US customers?

Not necessarily. CCPA has thresholds and uses opt-out, not a universal banner. Many states differ. Get advice if you advertise with pixels.

### Will a banner hurt conversions?

No source verified for this article quantifies the effect, so test it on your own traffic. Keep the banner small and do not block page content.

### Is a free plugin enough?

For a small site, a free tier can work if it blocks tags before consent and fits your traffic. Check the limits above.

### Do I need a privacy policy too?

If you collect personal data such as emails or bookings, yes. See our [GDPR-compliant email marketing guide](/blog/gdpr-compliant-email-marketing).

### Does SSL matter for privacy?
