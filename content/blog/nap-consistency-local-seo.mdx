## What Does NAP Mean in Local SEO?

NAP stands for name, address and phone number. BrightLocal calls it shorthand for three pieces of local business information. A mention that leaves out one of the three is a "partial NAP".

Google's own page on local ranking does not use the term. It says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that "businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results." Google does not publish how the three factors are weighted, and it says there is no way to pay for a better local ranking.

So NAP is an industry term for a real requirement: accurate, matching details wherever a customer or a search engine might look.

## Does NAP Consistency Affect Rankings?

BrightLocal says it does, citing the Local SEO Ranking Factors Survey, which lists citations as a top-five ranking factor. That survey polls industry practitioners. It is an opinion poll, not a measured weighting, so read it as "experts consider this important", not as a percentage of your ranking.

The reasoning is trust. When Google finds the same details on many sources, it can be more confident they are right. When details differ, BrightLocal says Google can doubt the data.

Google's prominence factor is also tied to links and reviews, which a clean NAP does not replace (see our [Google Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews)). ## Which NAP Differences Actually Matter?

Small factual errors matter. BrightLocal gives the example of a missing digit in a zip code or phone number, a reporter misspelling a zip code, or an event page showing an old phone number.

Formatting differences matter much less. According to BrightLocal, Google reads "St" and "Street" the same, and "No." and "#" the same. Its advice is to focus on the essentials instead.

| Difference | Priority |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong phone digit | High |
| Old address after a move | High |
| Missing suite number | Medium (BrightLocal does not address suites) |
| Street vs St | Low |
| Different phone number format, such as dashes vs brackets | Not covered by BrightLocal, so keep one format where you control it |

If you use a tracking number on one listing, keep the main number on your Google Business Profile and website, and note the exception in your audit sheet.

## What Name Should Your Business Use?

Google says the name on your Business Profile should reflect your real-world name as used on your storefront, website and stationery. Extra details do not belong in it. Google lists hours, locations, service descriptions and store codes as things to keep out, and warns that unnecessary information in the name is not permitted and can lead to suspension.

Legal suffixes such as LLC or INC are generally excluded unless you can show signage, cards or invoices that use them as part of the name.

This also settles the consistency question. Pick the name customers know, write it once in a reference sheet, and copy it to every listing. A keyword-stuffed variant on one directory is both a consistency problem and a policy risk.

## What If You Work From Home or Travel to Customers?

Google's guidelines say a service-area business, which serves customers at their locations, should have one profile for its central location with a defined service area, and should hide its address from customers. The example Google gives is a plumber working from home, who should clear the address from the profile.

A virtual office, where you rent a mailing address but do not operate from it, is not eligible for a Business Profile.

For NAP, this means the address you show publicly must be one you are allowed to show. If you hide your address on Google, do not publish a home address on directories that you then have to keep in sync.

## How Do You Audit NAP in Five Steps?

1. **Write the reference.** Record the exact name, address and main phone number you want everywhere, plus your website URL.
2. **List every place you appear.** BrightLocal says to check review sites, directory listings, social media profiles and your Google Business Profile.
3. **Search for old details.** Search your old phone number and old address in quotes, because old details are the usual source of errors.
4. **Fix at the source.** Edit each listing you control. For ones you do not control, use the directory's correction form.
5. **Add structured data.** Google's LocalBusiness markup requires name and address, and recommends telephone, opening hours and URL. Put it on a page about your business and use the most specific business type available.

Then recheck every few months, and after any move, rebrand or phone change. A tool can monitor listings, but a manual audit is enough for one location.

## What Does a NAP Audit Cost in Time? A Worked Example

All numbers below are **assumptions** for a single-location business.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Listings found | 12 |
| Listings with a wrong detail | 3 |
| Minutes to check one listing | 10 |
| Minutes to correct one listing | 15 |

Checking takes 12 x 10 = 120 minutes. Correcting takes 3 x 15 = 45 minutes. The total is 165 minutes, about 2.75 hours, which rounds to the 3 hours in the stat row above.

The point of the example is scale: for one location this is a half-day job, not a project. Your listing count will differ, and some directories take days to apply a change. Neither Google nor BrightLocal publishes how long updates take.

## FAQ

### What does NAP stand for?
Name, address and phone number. BrightLocal calls it shorthand for three key pieces of local business information.

### Is NAP consistency a Google ranking factor?
Google does not name it. Its local ranking page lists relevance, distance and prominence, and says complete, accurate information makes a business more likely to appear. BrightLocal cites an industry survey that ranks citations in the top five factors.

### Do I need to write "Street" the same way everywhere?
Not for Google. BrightLocal says Google treats "Street" and "St" as the same. Wrong or missing digits matter more.

### Can I use a tracking phone number?
BrightLocal's NAP page does not address tracking numbers. A safe approach is to keep your main number on your Profile and website, and record any exception in your audit sheet.

### Should I hide my address?
If you serve customers at their location and have no storefront, Google says to hide the address on your Business Profile.
