CTV (connected TV) means video ads on streaming services watched on a television. For a single restaurant it is a test, not a foundation: at an assumed $30 CPM, $1,000 buys about 33,000 ad views, roughly 8,300 households at four views each. If an extra party leaves you $18 in contribution, you need about 56 extra parties to break even, or 0.67% of the households reached. Everything in that sentence except the arithmetic is an assumption to replace with your own numbers.

This guide is for independent restaurants, cafes and bars in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Self-serve tools are US-centred, so outside the US ask each platform what is available in your country. It covers targeting, a 15-second spot, break-even maths and measuring walk-ins when nobody clicks anything.

## What Does the Evidence Say About CTV for Restaurants?

Independent proof that streaming TV fills small restaurants is thin. Most published results are vendor case studies about big chains.

Amazon's [case study of 17 quick-service brands](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/case-studies/amazon-dsp-qsr-streaming-results) across 30+ streaming campaigns reports an incremental return of $1.74 per $1 spent and a 0.79% incremental sales lift, measured with Attain (February 2026, US). Campaigns combining live sports and streaming returned $2.08 per $1, against $1.17 for single-platform campaigns.

Read it with care:

- It is Amazon's own marketing page and it does not publish the full method.
- The return is on **sales, not profit**. At restaurant margins, $1.74 of extra sales per dollar of ads may not cover the ad.
- The brands are national chains with large budgets and existing awareness.

CTV can create incremental visits, but the effect on any one restaurant is small relative to the spend unless the offer, targeting and tracking are tight.

## How Does Local Targeting Work on CTV?

Many guides say you can target a radius around your restaurant. On Roku's self-serve tool you cannot. Roku's [location targeting help page](https://help.ads.roku.com/en/articles/7191978-location-targeting) lists three types: states, postal or ZIP codes, and designated market areas (DMAs). It adds that ZIP codes should be residential only, that you can include or exclude locations using one geographic type at a time, and that ZIP targeting is not selectable if you declare a credit, housing or employment special ad category.

1. Pull 50 recent reservations, delivery orders or loyalty sign-ups and note each ZIP code.
2. Rank the ZIPs by share of customers and pick the top three to six within a short drive.
3. If the restaurant sits in a commercial ZIP with few homes, add the residential ZIPs around it.
4. Check the forecaster before launch. Roku's [audience targeting page](https://help.ads.roku.com/en/articles/7062001-audience-targeting) warns that limiting your audience may impact performance, so a very small ZIP set can cost more per view.

Tubi announced a self-serve platform powered by Universal Ads on 6 May 2025 ([Fox Corporation](https://www.foxcorporation.com/?p=6482)); the release lists no targeting options or minimums, so ask before planning around it. The [CTV platform comparison](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026) goes through the self-serve routes.

**Is a tight ZIP set always right?** Not always. Roku's [car dealer case study](https://advertising.roku.com/learn/resources/hyperlocal-ctv-how-a-car-dealer-network-efficiently-reached-audiences-at-scale) describes a dealer group moving from narrow ZIP targeting to a whole DMA, buying 45.5M+ impressions at a $7.36 effective CPM and reaching 1.7M+ households. Its stated view is "reach over repetition". That is a large group with cheap inventory, not a single restaurant, and $7.36 is far below the vendor estimates below.

## What Goes Into a 15-Second Restaurant Ad?

Food is visual, so spend the first seconds on it.

| Seconds | Show | Say or write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 | Your best dish, close up | Dish name |
| 3 to 8 | Room, patio or the team plating | One line on what makes it yours |
| 8 to 12 | The offer | "Tuesday: free dessert with any main" |
| 12 to 15 | Logo, street or neighbourhood, short URL or QR code | Phone number |

- **One offer, one action.** "Mention TUESDAY for a free dessert" can be counted. "Come see us" cannot.
- **Name the neighbourhood** so viewers know it is nearby.
- **Use sound.** CTV is usually watched on a TV, often with audio on.
- **Check the spec sheet** of the platform before you film; a phone in good window light can be enough.
- **Make a second version** after four weeks so the same homes do not see an identical ad all quarter.

## How Much Does CTV Cost?

CTV is bought by CPM, the cost per 1,000 ad views. No platform publishes a price list. Vibe's [Roku advertising cost guide](https://www.vibe.co/blog/roku-advertising-cost), a vendor source, puts Roku CPMs at $20 to $60 and says campaigns typically start around $500 total, while noting that Roku does not publicly disclose a minimum. Confirm in the tool. The [CTV cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data) covers market-wide ranges.

## What Does a $1,000 Test Need to Achieve? A Worked Example

Every input is an **assumption**: the CPMs sit inside the vendor range above; frequency, ticket and margin are placeholders. A month of $1,000, four views per household, and an extra party of four spending $60 that leaves $18 in contribution after food cost and variable labour.

| | Cheaper | Middle | Pricier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CPM (assumption) | $20 | $30 | $45 |
| Ad views | 50,000 | 33,333 | 22,222 |
| Households at 4 views each | 12,500 | 8,333 | 5,556 |
| Extra parties to break even ($1,000 / $18) | 56 | 56 | 56 |
| Share of reached households that must visit | 0.44% | 0.67% | 1.00% |
| Cost per extra party at 1% response | $8 | $12 | $18 |

<ColumnChart title="Households reached by $1,000 at four views each (assumption)" labels="CPM $20|CPM $30|CPM $45" values="12500|8333|5556" highlights="CPM $30" caption="Assumed CPMs inside the vendor range; four views per household is also an assumption." />

Break-even parties equal the budget divided by contribution per party. At $30 contribution you need 33 parties; at $10, 100. The 1% response row is an optimistic guess, not a benchmark.

- **Frequency is a dial.** Cutting from four views to two doubles households reached, but fewer people see it enough times to remember it.
- **Lifetime value changes the answer.** If a new regular visits 10 times a year, the first party need not pay for the ad.
- **Compare with search.** If Google Ads for "restaurant near me" cost less per extra party, spend there first: see the [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads).

## How Do You Run a 60-Day Test?

| Phase | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Before launch | Record baselines: covers on your two slowest nights, weekly branded searches, Business Profile calls and direction requests |
| Weeks 1 and 2 | Launch one 15-second ad with one offer in your ZIPs; check delivery and completion rate |
| Weeks 3 to 6 | Compare slow-night covers and redemptions against baseline; swap creative if completion is low |
| Weeks 7 and 8 | Scale, change the offer or ZIPs, or stop |

Sixty days is an assumption, not an industry standard: it gives roughly eight weekly cycles, enough to see a pattern past one rainy Friday. Run the test off-peak, when you have empty seats to fill.

## How Do You Measure Walk-Ins When Nobody Clicks?

CTV viewers rarely type a URL into a television. Set up several weak signals and read them together:

- **Offer code.** "Say TUESDAY for a free dessert", logged by servers.
- **Source question.** Add "How did you hear about us?" to online booking and the phone script.
- **Branded search.** Compare weekly searches for your name in Google Search Console before and after launch.
- **Business Profile.** Watch calls, direction requests and website clicks; first fix hours, menu and photos with the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).
- **Call tracking number** in the ad, if you use one.

None of these proves cause. A lift in all of them in the weeks the ad ran, after a flat baseline, is a decent signal. A lift in none is a clear one.

## Which Restaurants Should Skip CTV?

- **Under $500 a month to spend.** Too few homes reached to see an effect.
- **No slow nights to fill.** If you are full every evening, extra awareness has no seat to sell.
- **Weak basics.** Old photos, unanswered reviews or a broken menu link come first. [Instagram marketing for restaurants](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-restaurants) builds visual awareness more cheaply.
- **Sports bars.** Live sport is bought differently from general streaming inventory.

## FAQ

### Can I target a radius around my restaurant on CTV?

On Roku's self-serve tool the location options are states, ZIP codes and DMAs, and ZIP codes should be residential. Other platforms differ, so ask before you buy.

### What does CTV cost for a small restaurant?

There is no price list. A vendor guide puts Roku CPMs at $20 to $60 with campaigns often starting around $500. Use the worked example with your own quote.

### Is CTV better than Google Ads for a restaurant?

They do different jobs. Search catches people already looking for dinner; CTV tries to reach them before they look. Most single-location restaurants should prove search and their profile first.

### How long before I see results?

Plan about 60 days (an assumption). Offer redemptions and branded searches usually move before covers do.
