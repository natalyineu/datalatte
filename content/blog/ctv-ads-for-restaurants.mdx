CTV advertising for restaurants means running short video ads on streaming TV (Hulu, Peacock, Tubi, YouTube TV and others) shown only to households near your restaurant. It works best as an awareness channel for slow nights, new neighborhood residents and promotions, and it is measured by walk-ins, reservations and branded searches rather than clicks.

Many restaurants start with a modest monthly test, run it for at least 60 days, and track results with a promo code or a "how did you hear about us?" question. Pricing varies by platform, market and season, so treat the figures below as planning ranges, not quotes.

## Why Restaurants Use CTV

Flyers, newspaper and radio are local but imprecise. Google and Meta ads are precise but competitive. CTV sits in between: full-screen video that can show your food, with geographic targeting down to ZIP code or radius.

For restaurants it can help with three recurring problems:

- **Slow nights:** schedule ads for the evenings before your quiet days and promote a midweek special you can track.
- **New neighborhood residents:** some platforms offer "new mover" audience segments, so you can introduce yourself before competitors do.
- **Standing out:** if nearby restaurants rely on search and social ads, CTV can give you more visibility in a different channel.

## Targeting Setup

**Geography:** match the radius to how far people realistically travel. Full-service restaurants often use a few miles, neighborhood cafes and bars a smaller radius, and dense cities tighter still. Start with the area that already supplies most of your customers.

**Daypart:** consider lunch (late morning to early afternoon), the dinner decision window (roughly 5 to 7pm) and late evening for bars. Test and compare rather than assuming one window wins.

**Audiences:** options vary by platform and may include dining-out interests, date-night behavior, household income ranges or parents with young children. Availability depends on the DSP, so ask what is offered before you buy.

**Platforms:** Hulu offers broad reach, Peacock suits sports bars, Tubi offers free ad-supported streaming, and YouTube TV reaches live and local news viewers. See our [CTV platform comparison](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026).

## What to Show in a Restaurant CTV Ad

Food is visual, so lead with your best shot. A simple 15-second structure:

- **First seconds:** a hero shot of your signature dish.
- **Middle:** a glimpse of the atmosphere, or guests having a good time.
- **Then:** one specific reason to visit, such as live music Friday, brunch Saturday or a Tuesday special.
- **End:** your name, neighborhood and a short URL or QR code.

Use sound: sizzle, glasses and a warm voiceover help, since CTV is usually watched with audio on. Rotate seasonal versions (patio, winter interior, holidays) to avoid ad fatigue.

## Budget

CTV costs depend on the platform, targeting, season and market, and minimum spends vary. Many local advertisers test with roughly a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars per month. See our guide to [real CTV CPM data](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data) for how CPM pricing translates into impressions, and confirm current minimums with each platform.

A simple way to judge whether it is worth it: take your average check, estimate how many extra tables you would need each month to cover the ad spend, and compare that to what a promo code or reservation tracking shows after 60 days. Use your own numbers rather than industry averages.

## Measuring Results

CTV does not drive clicks; it drives visits and searches. Track:

- **Promo codes:** "Mention STREAMDEAL for a free appetizer."
- **Reservation source:** add "how did you hear about us?" to your booking flow or phone script.
- **Branded search:** watch Google Search Console for increases in searches for your restaurant name in the weeks after launch.
- **Google Business Profile insights:** compare profile views and direction requests before and after launch.

## FAQ

**Q: Is CTV advertising right for a small restaurant on a tight budget?**
A: It can be, but a very small budget limits frequency. Consider concentrating spend on the days you most need traffic, running for around 60 days before judging, or running CTV only during slow seasons or promotions.

**Q: Can I target people who order delivery?**
A: Delivery apps do not share their customer lists. Some ad platforms offer delivery-app user or dining-interest segments built from other signals. Layer them with tight geography and ask the platform what data sits behind each segment.

**Q: How do I produce a CTV ad on a small budget?**
A: Smartphone footage with good natural light is often acceptable. Film several clips of your best dishes, edit in a free tool such as CapCut or DaVinci Resolve, and export to the platform's required specs. A local videographer or the platform's creative team may be options if you want a more polished spot.

**Q: How long before I see results from CTV?**
A: Awareness takes time to build. Plan on at least 60 days before evaluating, and watch leading signs such as branded searches and promo code redemptions first.

**Q: Should I run CTV myself or hire an agency?**
A: One location with a simple campaign can be run through a self-serve platform. Multiple locations or complex targeting may justify an agency. Ask about fees and minimums up front.
