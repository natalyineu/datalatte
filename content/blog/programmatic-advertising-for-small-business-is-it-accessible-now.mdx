Yes, a small business can buy programmatic ads in 2026, but it should treat them as a second channel after Search and the Google Business Profile. The vendor ad:personam states that its Self Serve DSP has no minimum budget, while recommending at least $1,000 to reach a meaningful audience. Programmatic means ads are bought through automated systems, often in real time. For a local business the main risk is paying for impressions from people outside your service area. This guide covers access, cost and a 30-day test built on assumed figures.

## Can a small business buy programmatic ads without a minimum spend?

Access depends on the platform. ad:personam says its Self Serve DSP lets you start with a low budget because it does not require a minimum. [StackAdapt's plans page](https://www.stackadapt.com/plans-and-packages) and [AdRoll's pricing page](https://www.adroll.com/pricing) also state no minimum spend, though StackAdapt self-serve access is subject to application and approval. Check each platform's current terms before you sign up.

No minimum does not mean the platform will perform at any size. ad:personam describes $1,000 as the minimum recommended budget to ensure substantial reach and impact. Treat that as vendor guidance. A smaller budget can still run, but it produces fewer impressions and clicks, so the test teaches you less.

## Which platforms can a local business actually use?

Options differ by access as much as by price.

| Option | Who it suits | What the vendor states |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Display campaigns | Most small businesses | Part of Google Ads; ads are responsive by default |
| AdRoll | Retargeting and small tests | No minimum spend for Ads campaigns; exact prices not listed |
| StackAdapt Basic | Self-serve advertisers | Self-serve by credit card; subject to application and approval |
| ad:personam Self Serve DSP | Low-budget self-serve buying | No minimum budget; $1,000 recommended for reach |
| Display & Video 360 | Agencies and large advertisers | Partner accounts are for agencies, trading desks and large advertisers |

Display & Video 360 is not a tool most local owners can sign up for alone. Google's partner rules require certified partners with at least five local, full-time experts, so an agency is the usual route there. For a first test, a self-serve platform with a low entry point is the simpler path.

## What does programmatic cost a small business?

Costs have two parts: the media you buy and any platform or agency fee. Neither ad:personam's blog nor the StackAdapt and AdRoll pages list full rate cards, so no verified price per thousand impressions is available from the vendors themselves.

A secondary source, [OwlClaw's display benchmarks](https://owlclaw.com/benchmarks/display-ads-benchmarks/), gives rough CPM bands of $2 to $5 for Google's Display network and $1 to $4 for open exchange. It attributes its data to DoubleVerify and IAB 2025, and its own FAQ gives a slightly different Display range, so treat these as bands, not quotes.

CPM is cost per 1,000 impressions, not cost per visit. A low CPM on poorly viewed placements can produce fewer useful visits than a higher CPM on better ones. Judge spend by cost per enquiry or cost per booking, not impressions alone.

## How much budget do you need before a test means anything?

A test needs enough clicks and enquiries to show a pattern. At very small budgets, one or two conversions can swing the result sharply, which tells you little. StackAdapt suggests most teams need around $5,000 a month to see good results, which is a recommendation rather than a rule. The $1,000 figure from ad:personam is a sensible floor for a short, controlled test, not a promise of performance.

Set the test up before spending. Define one goal, such as booking requests, and one control, such as your current Search cost per enquiry. Track conversions with a tag or a unique landing page so you can see which visits came from display.

## Which programmatic setups fit a local business?

Retargeting is the most practical use for a local business. It shows ads to people who already visited your website, booking page or product pages. Those people already know you, so the ad reminds them rather than introducing you.

Prospecting, meaning reaching people who have never visited, is harder to justify at a small budget. The audience is larger, click rates are usually lower and enquiries per visit tend to be low for a new audience. Keep prospecting out of the first test unless you already know your conversion rate from other channels.

Creative matters too. ad:personam accepts GIF, JPEG, PNG, HTML5, video and audio files. For a local shop, a simple banner with your suburb, opening hours and one call to action is enough to test. ## What does a $1,000 test look like? A worked example

Every input below is an assumption except the $1,000 budget, which comes from ad:personam's recommended minimum. Replace the assumptions with your own figures before deciding.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget for 30 days | ad:personam recommended minimum | $1,000 |
| CPM (assumption) | Inside the $2 to $5 band | $4 |
| Impressions | $1,000 / $4 x 1,000 | 250,000 |
| Click-through rate (assumption) | Chosen for illustration | 0.2% |
| Clicks | 250,000 x 0.2% | 500 |
| Enquiry rate from visits (assumption) | Chosen for illustration | 2% |
| Enquiries | 500 x 2% | 10 |
| Cost per enquiry | $1,000 / 10 | $100 |

On these assumptions each enquiry costs $100 before any becomes a paying customer. If the click-through rate falls to 0.1%, the cost per enquiry doubles to $200. Compare that with your Search cost per enquiry for the same period. If display costs more per enquiry than Search, keep the budget for retargeting only.

After two weeks, check where visits and enquiries come from. If most enquiries come from outside your service area, tighten location targeting before extending the test.

## When should a local business skip programmatic?

Skip programmatic if you do not yet have a working Search campaign or a Business Profile that brings in enquiries. Display works as a reminder channel, and reminders only help once someone already knows what you sell.

Skip it also if you cannot track conversions. Without tracking you cannot tell whether the budget produced enquiries or only impressions. ## FAQ

### Is programmatic advertising too expensive for a small business?

Not necessarily. ad:personam states it has no minimum budget, so you can start small. It recommends $1,000 for substantial reach, which is the figure to test against your own costs.

### Can a small business buy programmatic without an agency?

Yes, on self-serve platforms such as ad:personam's Self Serve DSP. Enterprise platforms like Display & Video 360 are aimed at agencies and larger advertisers, so an agency is the usual route there.

### What should I start with, programmatic or Search?

Start with Search and your Business Profile, where people already look for what you sell. Add programmatic retargeting once you have a baseline cost per enquiry to compare against.

### How long should a programmatic test run?

The sources reviewed do not set a standard duration. A 30-day test at $1,000 gives a workable number of clicks and enquiries for many local businesses, but check your own volume before deciding.
