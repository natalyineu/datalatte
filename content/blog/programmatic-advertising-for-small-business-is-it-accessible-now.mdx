Yes, a small business can buy programmatic ads in 2026, but it should treat them as a second-step channel after Google Search and the Business Profile. StackAdapt's plans page says it has no minimum advertising spend, and AdRoll's pricing page says it has no minimum spend for its Ads campaigns. Both also say results need enough budget: StackAdapt notes most teams "hit their stride" around $5,000 a month, which is a recommendation, not a rule.

Programmatic means ads are bought through automated systems, often in auctions. For a local shop the risk is paying to reach people nowhere near you. This guide covers what you can buy, what it costs and a test with assumed numbers.

## What Is Programmatic Advertising?

The IAB says programmatic buys are distinguished by whether prices are fixed and whether inventory is reserved, and that many transactions finish in milliseconds. The main types are open auction, invitation-only auction (private marketplace), automated guaranteed and unreserved fixed rate. Real-time bidding is the mechanism behind both auction types.

In plain terms, a platform decides in an instant whether to show your ad to a person on a site or app, based on your targeting and bid. You do not negotiate with each website. Google Display campaigns work this way within Google's network, which Google says reaches 35 million websites and apps.

## Which Platforms Can a Small Business Actually Use?

Options differ by access, not just price.

| Option | Who it suits | What the vendor states |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Display campaigns | Most small businesses | Part of Google Ads; optimized targeting is the default; ads are responsive by default |
| AdRoll | Retargeting and small tests | No minimum spend for Ads campaigns; dynamic CPM pricing; exact prices not listed |
| StackAdapt Basic | Self-serve advertisers | Self-serve by credit card; access is subject to application and approval; no minimum spend |
| Display & Video 360 | Agencies and large advertisers | Partner status requires certification, including at least five local, full-time experts |

Display & Video 360 is not a tool a local owner signs up for alone. Google's help page says partners represent agencies, trading desks or large advertisers. Go through an agency if you want it. For reviews of one self-serve option, see our [StackAdapt review for local business](/blog/stackadapt-review-local-business-programmatic).

## What Does Programmatic Cost?

Prices vary by inventory type. OwlClaw's display benchmarks, which it attributes to DoubleVerify and IAB 2025 data, list CPMs of $1 to $4 for open exchange, $2 to $5 for Google's Display network, $5 to $15 for private marketplaces and $10 to $30 for programmatic guaranteed. The page is a secondary source, and its own FAQ gives a slightly different Display range, so use these as rough bands.

CPM is the cost per 1,000 impressions, not per visit. A cheap CPM on poorly viewed inventory can be worse than a higher one on better placements. Platform fees come on top, and neither StackAdapt nor AdRoll lists prices on the pages we opened. Our [programmatic CPM cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-programmatic-advertising-cost-real-cpm-benchmarks-2026) explains the maths.

## When Does Programmatic Make Sense for a Local Business?

It makes sense when you already convert visitors and want more reach, for example retargeting people who visited your booking page, or reminding past customers. It makes less sense as a first channel. People who have never heard of you rarely book from a banner, and there is no published, reliable local conversion benchmark for programmatic display that we could verify.

Start with Search, where people already want what you sell, then add display for reminders. The [beginner's guide to programmatic for local business](/blog/programmatic-advertising-for-local-business-beginners-guide) and [how programmatic buying works](/blog/how-programmatic-buying-works-local-business) cover the mechanics.

## What Does a $500 Test Look Like? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions**. The CPM sits inside OwlClaw's Google Display range.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget for 30 days (assumption) | Chosen | $500 |
| CPM (assumption) | Within a $2-5 band | $4 |
| Impressions | $500 / $4 x 1,000 | 125,000 |
| Click-through rate (assumption) | Chosen | 0.3% |
| Clicks | 125,000 x 0.3% | 375 |
| Visit-to-enquiry rate (assumption) | Chosen | 2% |
| Enquiries | 375 x 2% | about 8 |
| Cost per enquiry | $500 / 7.5 | about $67 |

On these assumptions each enquiry costs about $67 before any of them become customers. If clicks fell to 0.15%, the cost per enquiry would double to about $133. Compare that with your Search cost per enquiry. If programmatic is more than that, keep it small and use it for retargeting only.

Match the geography. Programmatic targets by area, but audiences can spill over. Check where visits and enquiries come from after two weeks.

## FAQ

### Is programmatic advertising too expensive for a small business?

Not necessarily. Both StackAdapt and AdRoll state no minimum spend, so you can start small. Results need enough volume to learn from, and StackAdapt suggests around $5,000 a month for good results.

### Can I use Display & Video 360?

Unlikely on your own. Google says partner accounts are for agencies, trading desks and large advertisers, and certified partners need at least five local, full-time experts.

### What is the difference between programmatic and Google Display?

Google Display is programmatic buying inside Google's network. Other platforms buy across many exchanges. See [programmatic versus Google display ads](/blog/programmatic-vs-google-display-ads-whats-the-difference).

### Should I start with programmatic?

No. Start with Search and your Business Profile, then add display or retargeting.

### How long should I run a test?

No published standard exists in the sources we opened. Run long enough to gather clicks and enquiries, and compare against Search.
