How much can a coffee shop afford to spend on ads? In Intuit QuickBooks industry benchmarking data, the average coffee shop keeps between 0.0% and 7.1% of its revenue as profit, depending on the state. That leaves little room to treat ad spend as a share of profit: in New York, 2% of average revenue spent on ads equals 104.1% of average profit. Ad budgets have to be sized against what an ad can earn back, not against what is left at year end.

This article uses the dataset DataLatte pulled from QuickBooks on 2026-11-03 for coffee shops, hair salons, fitness studios and pet care in 12 US states. All dollar amounts below come from that dataset or are computed from it.

## What does the QuickBooks data say about coffee shop profit?

The dataset gives, for each state and niche, average yearly revenue, average profit and a margin percentage. Coffee shops use NAICS 722515 (Snack and Nonalcoholic Beverage Bars). The other niches are salons (812112), fitness (713940) and pet care (812910).

Main findings:

- Coffee shop margins range from 0.0% (Illinois) to 7.1% (Pennsylvania). The median of the 12 state margins is 3.2%.
- Salons, in 11 states, have a median margin of 11.9%. Pet care (12 states) has 9.45% and fitness (12 states) 5.45%.
- Coffee shops have the highest average revenue of the four niches in all 12 states, and the lowest margin in every state but Pennsylvania, where fitness (4.3%) is lower.
- New York has the highest coffee revenue ($980,419) and one of the lowest margins (1.9%).
- Florida and Illinois coffee shops averaged $3,070 and $272 of profit.

The dataset reports net profit margin only. It has no gross margin (sales minus cost of goods) figure, so nothing below claims a gross margin for any state.

## Why size ads against sales earned, not against profit?

Profit is the number left after every cost, including rent, labor and ingredients. A coffee shop at a 2% margin has little left to spend on anything new. But an ad is judged by the sales it brings, so the useful question is how much extra profit each ad dollar produces.

That is why the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) sizes a budget from the other end: profit per new customer times the share of leads that become customers gives the most you can pay per lead. The dataset adds one fact to that logic: with margins this thin, a month of ads that brings in no customers is a visible share of the year.

## How does a $500 test month look for a coffee shop?

**Worked example (inputs marked assumption are not from any dataset).** A Texas coffee shop at the QuickBooks state average: revenue $678,796.47, profit $15,920.07.

| Input or step | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly test budget | $500 | Assumption |
| Yearly spend if repeated 12 months | $6,000 | $500 x 12 |
| Yearly spend as share of revenue | 0.88% | $6,000 / $678,796 |
| Yearly spend as share of profit | 37.7% | $6,000 / $15,920 |
| Google cost per click, Restaurants and Food | $2.05 | LocaliQ, via the budget guide |
| Google conversion rate, Restaurants and Food | 8.05% | LocaliQ, via the budget guide |
| Clicks from $500 | about 244 | $500 / $2.05 |
| Leads | about 20 | 244 x 8.05% |
| Share of leads who become customers | 25% | Assumption |
| New customers | about 5 | 20 x 25% |
| Profit per new customer over six months | $40 | Assumption |
| Profit from new customers | about $200 | 5 x $40 |

Result under these assumptions: $500 of ads returns about $200 of profit in the period, a loss of about $300. The maximum cost per lead is $40 x 25% = $10, below the $30.57 benchmark cost per lead for Restaurants and Food in the same guide. Change the assumptions to your own numbers; a shop with a higher value per customer or a higher lead-to-customer rate gets a different result.

## What are practical rules for sizing a test budget?

Each rule below rests on the QuickBooks figures or on the budget guide's cited sources.

1. **Start from the cost per lead you can afford.** Profit per new customer times lead-to-customer rate is the ceiling, as in the [budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).
2. **Pick a test amount you can lose.** The guide suggests $250 to $500 a month as a test range; the QuickBooks table shows why: $500 a month is $6,000 a year, which is 37.7% of Texas average profit.
3. **Treat the first month as a measurement.** The guide says local competition changes every benchmark figure.
4. **Count calls, forms and bookings by source** before adding money, so cost per customer can be compared. See coffee shop analytics and tracking.
5. **Use the free channel first.** Google states that a Business Profile is free; see [marketing with zero budget](/blog/local-business-marketing-when-you-have-zero-budget).
6. **Pick the channel by the buying moment.** Search ads reach people looking now; see [Google Ads for coffee shops](/blog/google-ads-for-coffee-shops) and [Facebook ads budget for local business](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend).

No source used here gives a safe percentage of revenue or an expected return for coffee shop ads, so none is stated.

## What does this dataset not show?

- **Who is in it.** The figures are averages of businesses that keep books in QuickBooks, not of all coffee shops in a state. Shops that use other software, or none, are not counted.
- **Missing values.** A niche missing for a state means no value was returned. Hair salons in Pennsylvania are the only gap in the 12 states.
- **Date.** Fetched through the QuickBooks MCP connector (benchmarking against industry) on 2026-11-03.
- **No gross margin, no ad spend line.** The dataset does not say how much of the revenue goes to ingredients or how much existing shops spend on marketing.
- **No sample sizes.** The number of businesses per state is not given, so small differences between states are not reliable. Treat state ranks as indications.
- **Averages hide spread.** One state average, such as Illinois at 0.0%, can combine profitable and loss-making shops.

## FAQ

### What profit margin does a coffee shop make according to QuickBooks data?

In the QuickBooks dataset fetched on 2026-11-03, average coffee shop margins by state range from 0.0% (Illinois) to 7.1% (Pennsylvania), with a median of 3.2% across 12 states. These are averages of businesses that keep books in QuickBooks.

### Is 2% of revenue a good coffee shop ad budget?

No source used here supports a fixed percentage. In the dataset, 2% of average revenue equals between 28.2% (Pennsylvania) and 104.1% (New York) of average profit in states with margins above 1%, so the amount only makes sense if the ads bring in more profit than they cost.

### How much should a coffee shop spend to test Google Ads?

The budget guide suggests testing with $250 to $500 a month. At LocaliQ's Restaurants and Food average of $2.05 per click, $500 buys about 244 clicks. Track calls, forms and orders to see whether the cost per customer fits.

### Does the data cover every state?

No. It covers 12 states: CA, TX, NY, FL, IL, PA, OH, GA, NC, WA, AZ and CO. Other states were not returned, and Pennsylvania has no hair salon value.
