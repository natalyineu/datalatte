Squarespace is worth it for most local businesses that want a polished, easy-to-manage website without hiring a developer, particularly cafés, salons, studios and groomers with simple needs. It is less suitable if you need heavy customisation, complex integrations or the lowest possible monthly cost.

This review covers what you get, what it typically costs, where it falls short and how it compares with the main alternatives, so you can decide with confidence.

## What Squarespace is

Squarespace is an all-in-one website builder: hosting, templates, a drag-and-drop editor, a domain option, basic SEO tools, e-commerce and scheduling, all under one subscription. You do not install software or manage security updates yourself. For a small team without technical staff, that bundle is the main attraction.

## Typical pricing

Squarespace changes its plans and prices from time to time and they differ by region and billing period, so check its pricing page before buying. As a general guide:

- Plans are billed monthly or annually, with annual billing usually cheaper per month.
- Entry-level plans for a basic site have typically been in the range of roughly $16 to $25 a month on annual billing.
- Plans with more e-commerce features cost more, often around $30 to $50 a month.
- A custom domain is often free for the first year with an annual plan, then renews at the normal price.
- Add-ons, such as Acuity Scheduling for advanced booking, extra email accounts through Google Workspace or paid extensions, cost extra.

Compare that with the price of your time. A site you can edit yourself in minutes often saves money compared with paying a freelancer for every small change.

## What works well for local businesses

**Design out of the box.** Squarespace templates are among the more attractive and consistent on the market. Even a first draft tends to look professional on phones, where most local searches happen.

**Simplicity.** Editing text, swapping photos and updating opening hours is straightforward. This matters because a stale website with wrong hours costs you customers.

**Built-in essentials.** Contact forms, maps, galleries, social links, email campaigns, basic analytics and SSL (the secure padlock) come included.

**Scheduling.** Acuity Scheduling, which Squarespace owns, can handle appointments for salons, studios and groomers, with reminders and online payments on suitable plans.

**Reliability and security.** Hosting, backups and updates are handled for you, so there is less to maintain than with a self-hosted WordPress site.

## Where it falls short

- **Limited flexibility.** You work within the template system. Unusual layouts or custom features can be difficult or impossible.
- **Integrations.** Squarespace connects to many tools through Zapier, Make and built-in options, but it has fewer plugins than WordPress.
- **Platform lock-in.** Moving a site to another platform means rebuilding it. You can export some content, but the design does not transfer.
- **Speed tuning.** You have less control over performance than on a custom site. Squarespace sites are generally acceptable, but heavy images and scripts can still slow them down.
- **SEO depth.** The basics are covered well: page titles, descriptions, sitemaps, clean URLs and redirects. Advanced technical control is more limited than on WordPress or Webflow.
- **Cost for bigger shops.** Selling many products or needing advanced e-commerce can push you toward Shopify.

## Squarespace for local SEO

A website builder does not make you rank by itself, but Squarespace can support a solid local presence if you use it well:

1. Write unique page titles and descriptions with your service and town.
2. Create separate pages for each main service.
3. Add your name, address and phone number in text, matching your Google Business Profile exactly.
4. Compress images before uploading and add descriptive alt text.
5. Embed a Google Map and add reviews or testimonials you have permission to use.
6. Connect Google Search Console and submit your sitemap.

## How it compares

| Platform | Best for | Strengths | Trade-offs |

## Examples by business type

- **Coffee shop:** an image-led homepage, menu page, opening hours, location map and an email signup for specials. Squarespace suits this well.
- **Hair or beauty salon:** service list with prices, team profiles, online booking through Acuity or your existing booking app, and an Instagram feed.
- **Pet groomer:** service and breed guidance, before-and-after photos with owners' permission, booking and vaccination policy.
- **Fitness studio:** class timetable, pricing, trial offers and a booking link. If your timetable is complex, link to a dedicated platform such as Mindbody, Mariana Tek or Glofox.

## Who should not choose Squarespace

- Businesses that need a complex membership system or custom booking logic
- Teams with a developer who wants full code control
- Sites planning hundreds of landing pages for programmatic SEO
- Owners who want to pay the lowest possible monthly price and do not mind some technical work (a basic WordPress host can cost less, though your time cost rises)

## A quick decision checklist

Choose Squarespace if you can answer yes to most of these:

- I want a good-looking site live within a week or two.
- I will update it myself.
- I only need a handful of pages plus bookings or a small shop.
- I prefer predictable monthly pricing to managing plugins.
- I do not need unusual functionality.

## Before you commit

Use the free trial to build a draft with your real content. Check it on your phone, test the contact form and booking flow, and run it through Google's PageSpeed Insights. If it feels right, buy an annual plan. If you decide to switch later, keep a copy of your text and images.

## Practical tips for your first month

- Write your core pages first: home, services with prices, about, contact. Add extras later.
- Use your own photos wherever possible, since real images build trust and set you apart from competitors using the same templates.
- Put your phone number, address and opening hours where visitors can see them without scrolling on a phone.
- Add a clear button such as "Book now" or "Call us" at the top of every page.
- Connect your Google Business Profile link to the site and check that both show the same details.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Squarespace good for SEO?**
A: It covers the basics well and is fine for most local businesses. Rankings depend more on content quality, your Google Business Profile, reviews and links than on the platform.

**Q: Can I take bookings on Squarespace?**
A: Yes, through Acuity Scheduling or by embedding third-party tools such as Fresha, Vagaro, Calendly or Mindbody. Features depend on your plan.

**Q: Can I move my site off Squarespace later?**
A: You can export some content, but you will need to rebuild the design on a new platform. Keep your texts and images backed up.

**Q: Is Squarespace better than Wix?**
A: Squarespace is usually more design-consistent and simpler. Wix offers more flexibility and apps. The right choice depends on your needs.
