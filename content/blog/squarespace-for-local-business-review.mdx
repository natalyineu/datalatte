Squarespace's Basic plan is listed at $19 a month on annual billing and $25 month to month. The next plan, Core, is $29 on annual billing. That $10 gap decides whether you can add custom code, whether you pay a 2% fee on online store sales, and whether professional email comes with the plan. For a café, salon, groomer or studio, picking the right tier matters more than choosing between Squarespace and its rivals.

This review is built on Squarespace's own pricing and help pages, read in October 2026, and it names what could not be checked. Prices are in dollars as shown on the page, which does not name a currency code. Outside the US, expect different figures and taxes. ## The Four Plans, Straight From the Pricing Page

According to [Squarespace's pricing page](https://www.squarespace.com/pricing), plans are:

| Plan | Monthly billing | Annual billing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | $25 | $19 |
| Core | $39 | $29 |
| Plus | $65 | $49 |
| Advanced | $139 | $99 |

The page lists a 20% annual saving on each. It also states that every site starts with a 14-day free trial and that no credit card is required. The page does not label which of the two figures is monthly and which annual in its raw text, so the table reads the higher number as monthly billing; confirm on the live page.

What changes between the plans, per the same page:

- **Domain:** all plans include a free custom domain for one year on annual terms. The page does not give the renewal price.
- **Email:** Basic does not include Google Workspace email. Core, Plus and Advanced include one account free for the first year.
- **Online store fee:** Basic carries a 2% fee on online store sales. Core, Plus and Advanced list 0%.
- **Card rate through Squarespace Payments:** 2.9% + $0.30 on Basic and Core, 2.7% + $0.30 on Plus, 2.5% + $0.30 on Advanced.
- **Selling:** all plans can sell products and services, send invoices and sell gift cards. Basic lacks shipping labels, discounts, payment plans and ecommerce analytics.

Separately, Squarespace's help page on [code-based customizations](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/216107348-Code-based-customizations) says code injection and code blocks are available on Core, Plus, Advanced and its older Business and Commerce plans, and that Squarespace support cannot help set up or troubleshoot that code. Read this as: Basic is the no-code plan.

## Basic or Core: A Decision in Three Questions

1. **Will you sell online (gift cards, merch, class packs)?** On Basic, the 2% fee on online store sales is the cost. The Core upgrade costs $10 more a month on annual billing, which is $120 a year. Two percent of $6,000 is $120, so if you expect more than about $500 a month in online store sales, Core pays for itself on the fee alone. This is my arithmetic from the page's figures, and it ignores the card rate, which is the same on both plans.
2. **Do you need custom code?** Reasons include a tracking pixel, extra structured data or a booking widget that wants a script. If yes, you need Core or higher.
3. **Do you want a branded email address?** You can add Google Workspace to Basic yourself, but you pay for it from day one.

If you answered no, no and "I'll use my domain email forwarding", Basic is enough for a brochure site with hours, a menu or service list, a map, a contact form and a booking link.

## Bookings: Scheduling Is a Separate Product

Squarespace owns Acuity Scheduling, and its [scheduling page](https://www.squarespace.com/scheduling) shows three tiers: one bookable calendar, up to six, and up to 36. Text message reminders appear from the second tier up, and the first tier includes confirmation, reminder and follow-up emails. The page lists no dollar prices and no trial length in the text that could be read, so the scheduling price is unverified here. Check it before you commit.

Practical meaning: a solo stylist or groomer fits the one-calendar tier. A four-chair salon needs a plan with at least four bookable calendars, which means the second tier. If you already pay for Fresha, Vagaro or Square, you may not need Acuity at all; embed your existing booking page instead. ## What It Does for Local SEO

According to Squarespace's [search optimization help](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206744067-How-does-Squarespace-optimize-my-site-for-search-results), every site gets:

- An automatically created and updated sitemap
- Clean, stable URLs
- Automatic redirects from connected domains to your primary domain
- Free SSL certificates on connected domains, which you must enable in domain settings
- Mobile-optimized styling by default
- Structured data for local businesses, blog posts, events, organizations, products and websites, which you cannot edit or remove

Two consequences. The local business schema is already there, so adding a second LocalBusiness block through code injection risks duplicate markup; test with Google's Rich Results Test before adding anything. And what Squarespace does not do for you is write: page titles, SEO descriptions and image descriptions need your input. Its SEO report tool in version 7.1 flags missing alt text and page descriptions.

Platform choice does not rank a local business by itself. Your Google Business Profile, reviews and consistent name, address and phone number do more. Use our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and make sure your site details match it exactly.

## Speed: Squarespace Gives Numbers You Can Follow

Squarespace's [page size guidance](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/360022529371-Reducing-your-page-size-for-faster-loading) is specific:

- Keep each page to 5 MB or less; on cellular connections, 1 MB is the safest size.
- Keep images below 500 KB with a width of 1500px to 2500px.
- Leave the .webp setting on, limit galleries to 50 images and pages to 60 blocks, and spread embedded videos across pages.

It also warns that some speed tools may return false negatives on its sites. For the broader picture, read our core web vitals guide for local business websites. Most local slowness comes from huge photos of food, haircuts and dogs, which you can fix in an afternoon.

## The Exit Cost: What Does Not Export

Squarespace's [exporting help](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206566687-Exporting-your-site) says the export file includes layout pages, one blog page with its posts and up to 1,000 comments per post, text blocks, image blocks and gallery pages. It does not include album, cover, index, info, calendar, portfolio and store pages, page-specific headers and footers, dropdowns, audio, video and product blocks, drafts, style settings or custom CSS. You cannot export a trial site. Images may not carry over into a self-hosted WordPress import, and the article warns the image links stop working after you cancel unless you copy the images.

So the design, store and booking setup are rebuilt, not moved. That is acceptable if you plan to stay for years. It is a reason to keep a folder of your original photos and text from day one.

## The Math

Three-year cash cost for a simple local site. Prices come from the pricing page. Items marked as assumptions are mine; replace them with real quotes.

| | Basic, annual | Basic, monthly | Core, annual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plan price per month | $19 | $25 | $29 |
| Plan cost, 36 months | $684 | $900 | $1,044 |
| Domain renewal in years 2 and 3 (assumption, $20 a year) | $40 | $40 | $40 |
| Business email, Google Workspace (assumption, $84 a year per user) | $252 (years 1 to 3) | $252 | $168 (free in year 1) |
| Three-year total | $976 | $1,192 | $1,252 |
| Scheduling add-on | Unverified | Unverified | Unverified |

How to read it:

- **Billing monthly instead of annually costs $216 more** over three years on Basic, because $25 versus $19 is $6 a month.
- **Core costs $276 more than Basic over three years** in this scenario, even after its free first-year email. You are buying code injection and 0% store fees, not just email.
- **Compare with other platforms.** The [Webflow vs WordPress analysis](/blog/webflow-vs-wordpress-small-business) put a three-year Webflow Basic site at $585 and a WordPress.com Business plan at $945, using its own assumptions. Those figures leave out email and hands-on time, so compare like with like.
- **Your hours matter more than $200.** If you can launch in two weekends and edit it yourself, a site that costs $976 over three years is cheap against a freelancer's fee for every change.

## Who Should Use It, by Business Type

- **Coffee shop:** Basic is enough for hours, menu, map and an email signup. Add Core only if you sell gift cards or merch above the break-even sales level.
- **Salon or barber:** use your existing booking app link, or Acuity's second tier if you need text reminders and several calendars.
- **Pet groomer:** a service page, booking link and vaccine policy page. Keep customer pet details in your booking software, not on the site.
- **Fitness or yoga studio:** link to your class platform. Squarespace is the shop window.

## Who Should Skip It

- You plan hundreds of programmatic location pages. A platform with a CMS built for scale will serve better.
- You need custom booking logic or a membership system built into the site.
- Your developer wants full code control and local hosting.

## Do This Today

1. Use the 14-day free trial to build the home page, one service page and the contact page with your real photos and text.
2. Resize every photo to under 500 KB and 1500 to 2500 pixels wide before uploading.
3. Decide Basic or Core using the three questions above, and write down your expected monthly online sales.
4. Fill in every page's SEO title and description, and make your name, address and phone number match your Google Business Profile.
5. Save your original photos and text in a folder outside Squarespace, then test the site on your phone before you pay.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does Squarespace cost for a local business?**
A: According to its pricing page, Basic is $19 a month billed annually or $25 monthly, and Core is $29 annually or $39 monthly. Add the domain after the first year, email on Basic and any scheduling plan.

**Q: Is Squarespace good for SEO?**
A: It covers the technical basics: sitemap, clean URLs, SSL and built-in local business markup. Rankings depend more on your content, reviews and Google Business Profile than on the platform.

**Q: Can I take bookings on Squarespace?**
A: Yes, through Acuity Scheduling or by embedding another booking tool. Squarespace's scheduling page shows tiers of one, six and 36 calendars, but its prices could not be confirmed here.

**Q: Can I move my site off Squarespace later?**
A: Partly. Pages, one blog and some blocks export as a WordPress-format file, but store pages, style settings and custom CSS do not, and you must handle images yourself.

**Q: Is Squarespace better than Wix?**
A: Neither is universally better. Compare your actual needs, using our [Wix vs Squarespace guide](/blog/wix-vs-squarespace-small-business) as a starting point, and trial both with your own content.
