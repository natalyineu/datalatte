CTV (connected TV) advertising in Minneapolis, MN lets a local business run video ads on streaming services and smart TVs, targeted to households in the ZIP codes you choose. Many self-serve and programmatic platforms let you start with a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month, though minimums, CPMs and available inventory change, so confirm current numbers with each platform before you commit.

This guide covers how CTV works in the Minneapolis-St. Paul market, which platforms to consider, how to target, what to spend, and how to tell whether it worked.

## The Minneapolis CTV Market

The Twin Cities is a large Midwest market with an engaged streaming audience. Households here watch across Hulu, Peacock, Max, Prime Video, Tubi, Pluto TV and other services, plus live sports streams. Cold winters mean more indoor viewing time, which many local advertisers use to plan seasonal pushes, though you should check your own results rather than assume.

The metro has cafe culture, strong fitness and outdoor communities, and a diverse population, so creative that reflects your actual neighborhood tends to land better than generic stock footage. Niches that often suit CTV include fitness studios, coffee shops, salons, pet groomers and independent restaurants.

## Why CTV Can Work for Local Businesses

CTV reaches households rather than individual phones, and you can often limit delivery by ZIP code or radius. Ads usually play full-screen, with sound on, in a lean-back setting. That makes it a good fit for building familiarity with people who live near you. It is less suited to immediate direct-response than search, so treat it as a brand and awareness channel that supports your other marketing. Our overview of [what CTV advertising is](/blog/what-is-ctv-advertising-guide-for-local-business-owners-2026) explains the basics.

Three practical advantages for Minneapolis businesses:

1. **ZIP-code or radius targeting** around your location or service area.
2. **Household reach** across the family or roommates in a home.
3. **TV-quality format** that can make a small brand look established.

## Targeting Setup

- **Geography:** Start with a radius around your location or a list of ZIP codes where your best customers live. In a metro with long commutes, base it on where customers actually come from.
- **Audience:** Layer in age, household income or interests only if they help. Over-narrow targeting inflates cost and may stop a campaign from delivering.
- **Daypart:** Evening is the busiest viewing window, but mornings or weekends can be cheaper and relevant for breakfast or weekend-focused businesses.
- **Frequency cap:** A modest weekly cap per household helps avoid fatigue. Start low and adjust after seeing reach.

## Seasonality in Minneapolis

Plan around local rhythms: January fitness resolutions, winter indoor viewing, spring grooming and outdoor activity, summer events and the State Fair, and holiday shopping. Time heavier flights to your own busy seasons, and check CPMs, which tend to rise when many advertisers compete (for example, around the holidays and big sports events).

## Creative Specifications

Check each platform's current specs, but common requirements are:

- File format: MP4 or MOV
- Resolution: 1920x1080 (16:9)
- Length: 15 or 30 seconds
- Audio: stereo, with loudness limits
- File size: platform dependent

A simple 15-second structure works well: show the brand and location in the first seconds, then the main benefit, then a clear call to action such as your name and neighborhood or a short web address. Keep it simple and use real footage of your shop where you can. See our [CTV creative specs guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-creative-specs-guide-2026).

## Budget Guide

Budgets vary widely by platform, targeting and season, so treat these as starting points rather than quotes.

| Plan | Idea | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Trial (about 4 weeks) | Small test in a tight area | Whether the ad delivers and whether branded searches or inquiries move |
| Core (about 3 months) | Steady presence in your core ZIPs | Builds frequency and recall |
| Seasonal burst (6 to 8 weeks) | Concentrate around your peak season | Align with promotions |

Ask each platform for a delivery estimate (impressions and reach) for your exact targeting before paying. Impression counts at a given budget depend on CPM, which is set by the auction and the audience you choose.

## Measuring Results

CTV rarely drives immediate clicks, so measure leading indicators:

- Branded search volume and Google Business Profile views of your name
- Direction requests, calls and website visits during and after the campaign
- A "How did you hear about us?" question at the register or on booking forms
- Direct traffic to your website

Compare against a similar period before the campaign and give it several weeks. Some platforms and partners offer footfall or lift studies, but those usually need larger budgets. For setting up measurement, see our [analytics service](/services/analytics).

## FAQ

**Q: How much does CTV advertising cost in Minneapolis?**
A: It depends on the platform and targeting. CTV is typically sold on CPM, and local campaigns can start at a few hundred dollars, but premium platforms and programmatic partners may have higher minimums. Ask for a delivery estimate first.

**Q: Can I target specific ZIP codes in Minneapolis?**
A: Yes, most CTV platforms allow ZIP-code or radius targeting, though precision varies by platform.

**Q: Do I need a professionally produced video?**
A: Not necessarily. A clear 15-second ad with real footage and a strong call to action can be enough to start. Make sure it meets technical specs.

**Q: How long until I see results?**
A: Give it at least several weeks, and look at branded searches and inquiries rather than clicks.

**Q: Is CTV better than Google Ads for a local business?**
A: They do different jobs. Search captures existing demand, CTV builds awareness. Many local businesses start with search and add CTV once the basics are working.
