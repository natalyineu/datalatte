## How Much Does Google Ads Cost for an Optometrist?

LocaliQ does not publish an eye care row, so use nearby health categories. Health and Fitness: $6.17 per click, 5.81% click-through, 6.94% conversion, $67.36 per lead. Dentists: $8.00, 5.66%, 10.67%, $72.97. Physicians and Surgeons: $4.76, 6.61%, 12.43%, $40.04. The all-industry average is $5.42 per click.

The spread between $4.76 and $8.00 matters. At 100 clicks a month it is a difference of $324 in spend. Run your own campaign for 4 to 6 weeks before trusting any benchmark.

## Are There Google Ads Rules for Eye Care?

Yes, check them before you build. Google's healthcare and medicines policy restricts prescription drug sales and services, speculative treatments and several other categories. The page does not mention contact lenses or eyeglasses in the text I could read, so I cannot say how it treats them. Read the policy for your country, and check the status of each ad in your account.

Google's personalized advertising policy lists Health as a sensitive interest category. For those categories, advertisers cannot use advertiser-curated audiences, and Customer Match is not supported. Whether routine eye exams fall inside that, the page does not say. If remarketing lists will not attach, this is the likely reason.

## Which Keywords Bring Patients, Not Browsers?

Group by service so each ad matches the search:

- **Eye exams:** "eye exam near me", "optometrist" plus your town, "eye test for driving" where relevant
- **Glasses:** "prescription glasses" plus your town, "same day glasses"
- **Specific care:** "dry eye treatment", "children's eye exam", only if you offer it

Add negatives so you do not pay for the wrong click. Google explains that negative keywords exclude search terms and do not match close variants, so add plurals yourself. Typical negatives: "jobs", "school", "course", "free", "online glasses" and "reviews" if your cost per click is high.

## How Do You Target a Local Catchment?

Patients usually choose an optometrist close to home or work. Google's help says radius targeting needs a radius of at least 1 km, and recommends broad geo targeting ("Presence or interest") as best practice on Search. Presence alone is for cases such as a sensitive vertical with strict targeting limits.

Start with a radius around the practice, then read the Search terms report and the location report. Add cities or postcodes where bookings come from, and exclude places you do not serve.

## What Should You Track?

Book-an-exam is the conversion. Google says clicks on a call asset on mobile cost the same as headline clicks. For call conversions, you set a minimum call length and every call that lasts at least that long counts. Set it to match a real booking enquiry.

Also track the online booking form. Ask reception to log the source of every new patient ("Google ad", "Maps", "referral"). Compare those notes with the ads report each month, and with our [chiropractors guide](/blog/google-ads-for-chiropractors), which has the same booking-led structure, and [reputation management for dentists](/blog/reputation-management-for-dentists).

## FAQ

### How much should an optometrist spend on Google Ads?
There is no fixed amount. At $6.17 per click, $800 a month buys roughly 130 clicks. Start small and scale once you know your cost per attended exam.

### Is there a published CPC for optometrists?
No. LocaliQ publishes Health and Fitness, Dentists, and Physicians and Surgeons rows. Use them as a range.

### Can I run remarketing for eye care?
Health is listed as a sensitive interest category in Google's personalized advertising policy. Check your account for audience restrictions before you plan on remarketing.

### Do I need Google certification?
Google requires certification for prescription drug services, pharmaceutical manufacturers and health insurance in the US. The policy text I read did not mention optometry practices or eyeglasses.

### Do calls count as conversions?
Yes, if they last at least the minimum call length you set.
