Houzz ads can be worth it for contractors, remodelers and designers who sell higher-value projects, because Houzz attracts homeowners who are already planning renovations and browsing portfolios. Houzz says its sponsored placements are prioritized by category, relevance (distance and style match) and the price paid for the advertising ([Houzz Pro Help](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-help/r/pros-shown-on-houzz), checked Oct 2026). Houzz does not publish a rate card we could find, so get a written quote for your category and area before committing. It pays off only if the jobs it brings cover the fee, and only if your photos, reviews and response time are strong.

We have not quoted prices because we found no official Houzz figures, and third-party numbers vary widely.

## What is Houzz Pro and how does Houzz advertising work?

Houzz is a home design and renovation marketplace, and Houzz Pro is the business side, where contractors, designers and other home service pros manage a profile, respond to leads and can buy advertising or lead products. Packages, naming and pricing have changed over time, so look at the current options in your Houzz Pro account.

Houzz says its organic listings consider category, location, distance, how well your description matches the search, style similarity between your project photos and photos the homeowner saved, and quality signals such as review count ([Houzz Pro Help](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-help/r/pros-shown-on-houzz), checked Oct 2026). Paid placement is ranked on top of those relevance factors, and the matching tool also weighs advertising status and responsiveness. In practice that means a strong profile helps even if you also pay.

**Why it can be different from Google Ads:** Google Ads catches homeowners who are searching for a service, sometimes long before they are ready. Houzz visitors are often further along in planning or comparing, although lead quality still varies. Test it against your other channels instead of assuming it will outperform them.

## How much does Houzz advertising cost?

Houzz does not publish a single price for advertising, and the product, naming and pricing differ by market and over time. Get the options for your own category and location from your Houzz Pro account or a Houzz representative.

Houzz states that price paid influences placement, so expect a competitive category in a busy metro to cost more than a niche one in a small market, though you should confirm that with your quote.

The best way to get a real number is to look at the advertising options in your Houzz Pro account for your category and location, or to ask a Houzz representative for a written quote.

**Is it worth it?** Do the break-even maths. If you sell projects worth several thousand dollars or more, closing one extra job in a few months may cover a lot of advertising. If your jobs are small, the same fee may not pay back. Use the ROI table below before you commit.

## Build a Houzz profile that converts

Even the best placement will waste money if your profile is weak.

**1. Photos are everything.** Houzz is visual, so upload plenty of high-quality photos from real completed projects, organised by project type such as kitchens, bathrooms and exteriors. Add captions that mention the city, scope and materials. Real project photos generally persuade better than stock images.

**2. Reviews build trust.** A profile with a solid number of recent, specific reviews tends to get more enquiries than one with very few. After each project, send clients a direct review link and a personal ask, for example: "Could you share what the bathroom renovation was like for your family?"

**3. Complete every section.** Add your business description, service areas, licence numbers, years in business and typical project ranges. Incomplete profiles look less serious.

**4. Respond fast.** Fast replies often win projects, because homeowners usually contact several pros. Aim to reply within a couple of hours on working days, and set notifications on your phone.

<Callout type="tip">Before you start paying for placement, ask a handful of recent happy clients to leave a Houzz review. A profile with some reviews almost always looks more credible than one with none.</Callout>

## Targeting and geographic options

Houzz considers location and distance when choosing which pros to show, so set a service area. Pick an area you can realistically serve. Too wide wastes time on estimates you will not win, and too narrow limits lead volume.

If you work in multiple markets, check whether Houzz lets you set separate service areas, and make sure your profile content mentions each location.

Be specific about your category. A general category sounds comprehensive but can be the most competitive and expensive. If most of your work is kitchen remodeling, a kitchen and bath category may bring more relevant leads. Confirm what is available in your account.

## How to measure ROI on Houzz

Track these numbers every month:

| Metric | How to track |
|---|---|
| Houzz spend | Invoice or dashboard |
| Leads received | Houzz Pro dashboard |
| Leads contacted | Your CRM or spreadsheet |
| Estimates given | Your project log |
| Jobs closed | Your project log |
| Revenue from Houzz clients | Invoice records |

**ROI formula:** (revenue from Houzz clients minus Houzz spend) divided by Houzz spend.

Here is an illustration with simple made-up numbers, not a prediction. If you spend $500 in a month, give 5 estimates and close 1 project worth $9,000, you earned $9,000 against $500 of spend. Count your costs of doing the work too, because revenue is not profit. If you close nothing for three months, the same spend is a loss, so review profit over a quarter rather than a single month.

Give any new channel a fair trial of several months, then compare cost per closed job with Google Ads, Angi, Thumbtack and referrals. Our guide to [tracking which marketing is bringing customers](/blog/how-to-track-which-marketing-is-bringing-customers) shows a simple method.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do Houzz ads cost?**
A: It depends on your category, market and the product you choose. Check the advertising options in your Houzz Pro account or ask for a written quote, and calculate the break-even number of closed jobs before you commit.

**Q: Can I cancel Houzz advertising at any time?**
A: Check the terms when you sign up. Ask whether there is a minimum term, how cancellation works, and what happens to your placement if you stop.

**Q: What if I get no leads from Houzz?**
A: Review your profile first: photos, reviews, categories, service area and response time. If you still see little traction after a fair trial period, Houzz may not have enough search volume for your category in your area, and you may do better putting the budget into Google Ads or local SEO.

**Q: Do I need a website to use Houzz?**
A: Your Houzz profile can act as a portfolio, but a simple website still helps you look credible and gives you a backup channel. Many contractors use both.

**Q: How is Houzz different from Thumbtack or Angi?**
A: Houzz is a design and renovation platform where visitors browse portfolios and plan projects. Thumbtack and Angi are lead marketplaces that connect homeowners with pros for specific jobs. The pricing models and lead quality differ, so test and compare cost per closed job.

**Q: Can I run Houzz and Google Ads together?**
A: Yes. They reach homeowners at different points in the journey. Track leads from each source separately so you can see what works.
