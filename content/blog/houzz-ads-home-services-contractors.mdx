Houzz ads can be worth it for contractors, remodelers and designers who sell higher-value projects, because Houzz attracts homeowners who are already planning renovations and browsing portfolios. Houzz says its sponsored placements are prioritized by category, relevance (distance and style match) and the price paid for the advertising ([Houzz Pro Help](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-help/r/pros-shown-on-houzz), checked Oct 2026). Houzz does not publish a rate card we could find, so get a written quote for your category and area before committing. It pays off only if the jobs it brings cover the fee, and only if your photos, reviews and response time are strong.

Picture a homeowner with a saved folder of kitchen photos. She is not typing "plumber near me" in a panic. She is comparing portfolios on a Sunday night, and she picks three pros to message. If your profile has six blurry photos and no reviews, you are not in the three, however much you pay for placement. That is why this article spends more time on your profile and your break-even math than on the ad itself.

We have not quoted prices because we found no official Houzz figures, and third-party numbers vary widely.

## How Houzz Advertising Works

Houzz is a home design and renovation marketplace. Houzz Pro is the business side, where contractors, designers and other pros manage a profile, respond to leads and can buy advertising or lead products. Packages, naming and pricing have changed over time, so look at the current options in your Houzz Pro account.

Houzz says its organic listings consider category, location, distance, how well your description matches the search, style similarity between your project photos and photos the homeowner saved, and quality signals such as review count ([Houzz Pro Help](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-help/r/pros-shown-on-houzz), checked Oct 2026). Paid placement is ranked on top of those relevance factors, and the matching tool also weighs advertising status and responsiveness. A strong profile helps even if you also pay, and a weak one wastes the payment.

**How it differs from Google Ads:** Google Ads catches homeowners searching for a service, sometimes long before they are ready. Houzz visitors are often further along in planning or comparing, although lead quality still varies. Test it against your other channels. Do not assume it wins.

## The Math: How Many Jobs Pay for It?

Houzz states that price paid influences placement, so a competitive category in a busy metro will likely cost more than a niche one in a small market. Confirm with your quote. Until you have one, work the break-even.

**Example with made-up inputs. The $500 is a placeholder, not a Houzz price. Swap in your own numbers.** Assume you pay $500 a month, so $1,500 over a 3-month trial, and your gross margin on a job is 20% (what is left after materials, labor and subs, before overhead).

| Average job | Profit per job (20%) | Jobs needed to cover $1,500 |
|---|---|---|
| $4,000 | $800 | 1,500 / 800 = 1.9, so 2 jobs |
| $9,000 | $1,800 | 1,500 / 1,800 = 0.83, so 1 job |
| $25,000 | $5,000 | 1,500 / 5,000 = 0.3, so 1 job |

On a $4,000 job you need two closed in 3 months just to break even. On a $25,000 remodel a single job's profit covers 3.3 quarters of the same spend ($5,000 / $1,500), about 10 months. That is the whole case for Houzz: the bigger your average job, the easier the math. If your jobs are small, the same fee may never pay back.

Now the ROI formula: (revenue from Houzz clients minus Houzz spend) divided by Houzz spend. Illustration: you spend $500, give 5 estimates and close 1 project worth $9,000. Revenue is $9,000 against $500 of spend. Revenue is not profit, so count your cost of doing the work. If you close nothing for three months, the same spend is a loss. Review a quarter, not a month.

## Build a Profile That Converts

Even the best placement wastes money on a weak profile.

**1. Photos.** Houzz is visual. Upload plenty of high-quality photos from real completed projects, organized by type: kitchens, bathrooms, exteriors. Caption them with the city, scope and materials. Real project photos persuade better than stock.

**2. Reviews.** Houzz lists review count as a quality signal. After each project, send the client a direct review link and a personal ask: "Could you share what the bathroom renovation was like for your family?"

**3. Every section.** Business description, service areas, licence numbers, years in business and typical project ranges. Incomplete profiles look less serious.

**4. Speed.** Homeowners usually contact several pros. Aim to reply within 2 hours on working days and turn on phone notifications.

<Callout type="tip">Before you pay for placement, ask 5 recent happy clients to leave a Houzz review. A profile with some reviews looks more credible than one with none.</Callout>

## Service Area and Category

Houzz considers location and distance, so set a service area you can really serve. Too wide wastes time on estimates you will not win. Too narrow limits volume. If you work in several markets, check whether Houzz lets you set separate service areas, and mention each location in your profile.

Be specific about category. A general one sounds comprehensive but can be the most competitive and expensive. If most of your work is kitchens, a kitchen and bath category may bring more relevant leads. Confirm what your account offers.

## Do This Before You Pay

1. Open your Houzz Pro account and screenshot the advertising options for your category and area.
2. Ask for the price, the minimum term and the cancellation terms in writing.
3. Work out your average job value and gross margin, then fill in the break-even table.
4. Upload photos from your last 5 finished projects, with captions that name the city, scope and materials.
5. Message 5 recent clients with a direct review link this week.
6. Set a phone notification for new Houzz leads and a target reply time of 2 hours.
7. Put a review date 90 days out in your calendar, and compare cost per closed job with your other channels.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do Houzz ads cost?**
A: It depends on your category, market and the product you choose. Check the advertising options in your Houzz Pro account or ask for a written quote, and calculate the break-even number of closed jobs before you commit.

**Q: Can I cancel Houzz advertising at any time?**
A: Check the terms when you sign up. Ask whether there is a minimum term, how cancellation works, and what happens to your placement if you stop.

**Q: What if I get no leads from Houzz?**
A: Review your profile first: photos, reviews, categories, service area and response time. If you still see little after a fair trial, Houzz may not have enough search volume for your category in your area, and Google Ads or local SEO may deserve the budget.

**Q: Do I need a website to use Houzz?**
A: Your Houzz profile can act as a portfolio, but a simple website still helps you look credible and gives you a backup channel. Many contractors use both.

**Q: How is Houzz different from Thumbtack or Angi?**
A: Houzz is a design and renovation platform where visitors browse portfolios and plan projects. Thumbtack and Angi are lead marketplaces that connect homeowners with pros for specific jobs. Pricing models and lead quality differ, so compare cost per closed job.

**Q: Can I run Houzz and Google Ads together?**
A: Yes. They reach homeowners at different points in the journey. Track leads from each source separately.
