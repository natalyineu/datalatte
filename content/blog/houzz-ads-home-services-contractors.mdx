## How does Houzz advertising work?

Houzz is a home design and renovation marketplace. Houzz Pro is the business side, where contractors, designers and other pros manage a profile, respond to leads and can buy advertising or lead products. Packages, naming and pricing have changed over time, so look at the current options in your Houzz Pro account.

Houzz says its organic directory listings are ordered by category (service type, professional type, location), relevancy (for example physical distance and how well the text matches the search) and content quality (for example the number of reviews and the number of photos saved to ideabooks) ([Houzz Pro Help](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-help/r/pros-shown-on-houzz), read 3 Nov 2026).

Paid placements are labelled "sponsored". The matching tool uses services provided, budget and location, plus a pro's paid advertising status, responsiveness and other factors. Houzz does not explain how responsiveness is measured or weighted. A strong profile helps even if you also pay, and a weak one wastes the payment.

**How it differs from Google Ads:** Google Ads catches homeowners searching for a service, sometimes long before they are ready. Houzz visitors are often further along in planning or comparing, although lead quality still varies. Test it against your other channels. Do not assume it wins.

## How many jobs does it take to pay for Houzz?

Houzz states that the price paid influences placement, so a competitive category in a busy metro will likely cost more than a niche one in a small market. Confirm with your quote. Until you have one, work the break-even.

**Assumption, not a Houzz price:** the $500 a month is a placeholder with made-up inputs. Assume you pay $500 a month, so $1,500 over a 3-month trial, and your gross margin on a job is 20% (what is left after materials, labor and subs, before overhead). Swap in your own numbers.

| Average job | Profit per job (20%) | Jobs needed to cover $1,500 |
|---|---|---|
| $4,000 | $800 | 1,500 / 800 = 1.9, so 2 jobs |
| $9,000 | $1,800 | 1,500 / 1,800 = 0.83, so 1 job |
| $25,000 | $5,000 | 1,500 / 5,000 = 0.3, so 1 job |

On a $4,000 job you need two closed in 3 months just to break even. On a $25,000 remodel a single job's profit covers 3.3 trial periods of the same spend ($5,000 / $1,500), about 10 months at $500 a month. That is the whole case for Houzz: the bigger your average job, the easier the math. If your jobs are small, the same fee may never pay back.

The ROI formula is (revenue from Houzz clients minus Houzz spend) divided by Houzz spend. Illustration (assumption): you spend $500, give 5 estimates and close 1 project worth $9,000. Revenue is $9,000 against $500 of spend. Revenue is not profit, so count your cost of doing the work. If you close nothing for three months, the same spend is a loss. Review a quarter, not a month.

## How do you build a Houzz profile that converts?

Even the best placement wastes money on a weak profile.

**1. Photos.** Houzz is visual, and Houzz lists photos saved to ideabooks as a content quality signal. Upload high-quality photos from real completed projects, organized by type: kitchens, bathrooms, exteriors. Caption them with the city, scope and materials.

**2. Reviews.** Houzz lists review count as a content quality signal. After each project, send the client a direct review link and a personal ask: "Could you share what the bathroom renovation was like for your family?"

**3. Every section.** Business description, service areas, licence numbers, years in business and typical project ranges. Incomplete profiles look less serious.

**4. Speed.** Homeowners usually contact several pros, and Houzz names responsiveness in its matching factors. Aim to reply within 2 hours on working days and turn on phone notifications.

<Callout type="tip">Before you pay for placement, ask 5 recent happy clients to leave a Houzz review. A profile with some reviews looks more credible than one with none.</Callout>

## How should you set the service area and category?

Houzz considers location and distance, so set a service area you can really serve. Too wide wastes time on estimates you will not win. Too narrow limits volume. If you work in several markets, check whether Houzz lets you set separate service areas, and mention each location in your profile.

Be specific about category. A general one sounds comprehensive but can be the most competitive and expensive. If most of your work is kitchens, a kitchen and bath category may bring more relevant leads. Confirm what your account offers.

## What should you do before you pay?

1. Open your Houzz Pro account and screenshot the advertising options for your category and area.
2. Ask for the price, the minimum term and the cancellation terms in writing.
3. Work out your average job value and gross margin, then fill in the break-even table.
4. Upload photos from your last 5 finished projects, with captions that name the city, scope and materials.
5. Message 5 recent clients with a direct review link this week.
6. Set a phone notification for new Houzz leads and a target reply time of 2 hours.
7. Put a review date 90 days out in your calendar, and compare cost per closed job with your other channels.

## FAQ

### How much do Houzz ads cost?

Houzz does not publish advertising prices on the help page read for this article. It depends on your category, market and the product you choose. Check the options in your Houzz Pro account or ask for a written quote, and calculate the break-even number of closed jobs before you commit.

### Can I cancel Houzz advertising at any time?

Check the terms when you sign up. Ask whether there is a minimum term, how cancellation works, and what happens to your placement if you stop.

### What if I get no leads from Houzz?

Review your profile first: photos, reviews, categories, service area and response time. If you still see little after a fair trial, Houzz may not have enough search volume for your category in your area, and Google Ads or local SEO may deserve the budget.

### Do I need a website to use Houzz?

Your Houzz profile can act as a portfolio, but a simple website still helps you look credible and gives you a backup channel. Many contractors use both.

### How is Houzz different from Thumbtack or Angi?

Houzz is a design and renovation platform where visitors browse portfolios and plan projects. Thumbtack and Angi are lead marketplaces that connect homeowners with pros for specific jobs. Pricing models and lead quality differ, so compare cost per closed job.

### Can I run Houzz and Google Ads together?

Yes. They reach homeowners at different points in the journey. Track leads from each source separately.
