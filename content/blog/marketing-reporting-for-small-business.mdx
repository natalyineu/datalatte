Marketing reporting for a small business should fit on one page and answer one question: what did we spend, and what came back? Pick about six numbers, pull them on the same day each month, and compare them with the last month. A report nobody reads is worse than none, so keep yours short enough to review in ten minutes.

This guide shows which numbers belong on the page, where each one comes from, what Google's tools can and cannot keep, and a worked example with every figure labelled as an assumption.

## What Should a Small Business Marketing Report Include?

Choose numbers that tie to money. A useful one-page report has six lines:

1. **Spend:** total ad spend and any tool or agency fees.
2. **Enquiries:** calls, form fills, bookings and messages, counted the same way every month.
3. **Cost per enquiry:** spend divided by enquiries.
4. **Revenue from new customers:** from your till or booking system, not from the ad platform.
5. **Google Business Profile actions:** calls, direction requests and website clicks.
6. **One note:** what changed this month and what you will do next.

Reach, impressions and follower counts can sit in an appendix. They explain why something moved. They do not show whether the business gained.

## Where Does Each Number Come From?

Each source has quirks, so know them before you trust a trend.

**Google Business Profile.** According to Google's [Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094), the performance metrics include views, calls, directions, website clicks, messages and bookings. Calls count clicks on the call button, not completed calls. Views count unique visitors and a person is counted once a day, so the total can be lower than your notification emails. Performance data is only available for verified profiles.

**Google Analytics 4.** The [data retention page](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/7667196) says standard properties can keep event data for 2 months or 14 months, and the setting affects Explorations and funnel reports but not standard aggregated reports. Check Admin, then Data Settings, then Data Retention, and choose 14 months. Reducing retention later deletes older data in the next monthly process.

**Ad platforms.** Export spend and conversions each month and save the file. Platforms can restate numbers later, so your own copy matters.

**Your till or booking system.** This is the number you trust most. Record how each new customer heard about you.

## How Do You Build the Report for Free?

Use a spreadsheet first. A Google Sheet with one row per month and one column per metric is enough for most single-location shops. Our [dashboard guide](/blog/how-to-create-marketing-dashboard-small-business) walks through the layout.

When you want charts that update, Google's free dashboard tool helps. Google's [documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/looker/docs/studio) says it is now called Data Studio (formerly Looker Studio) and states it is a no-cost tool, with a paid Pro tier for collaboration and administration. The page names no specific connectors, so check which of your data sources it can connect to before you commit. For a walk-through, see [local SEO reports with Google Data Studio](/blog/ai-driven-local-seo-reports-with-google-data-studio).

Automation can also fill your sheet each month, but only after you trust the manual version.

## How Do You Read the Report? A Worked Example

All figures are **assumptions** for a small café or salon.

| Metric | Last month | This month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad spend | $400 | $400 |
| Enquiries (calls, forms, bookings) | 40 | 50 |
| Cost per enquiry | $10.00 | $8.00 |
| New customers (from intake question) | 12 | 15 |
| Average first-visit spend | $45 | $45 |
| Revenue from new customers | $540 | $675 |

Cost per enquiry fell from $10 to $8, and new-customer revenue rose by $135. That is not proof the ads caused the change. It is a signal to check. If direction requests also rose on your profile, local visibility may have helped. If they fell while ads rose, ask which part of the change you can explain. Note it in the report, then test one change next month.

## What Reporting Mistakes Should You Avoid?

- Changing how you count enquiries from month to month.
- Treating ad-platform conversions as sales.
- Reporting too many metrics.
- Forgetting to set GA4 retention and losing history.
- Comparing a quiet month with a holiday month without saying so.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should I report?**
A: Monthly for most local businesses, with a quick weekly check if you run ads.

**Q: Which tool should I start with?**
A: A spreadsheet. Add a dashboard tool when the manual process is stable.

**Q: What if my numbers disagree between tools?**
A: Expect it. Each tool counts differently. Pick one source of truth per metric and stick to it.

**Q: Do I need to keep old data?**
A: Yes. Export key numbers monthly, because platform retention limits can remove detail.

**Q: Who should read the report?**
A: You, plus anyone who spends your marketing budget.
