Pause an ad campaign when tracking is broken, when it misses its cost-per-lead target after enough data has accumulated, or when the season it was built for ends. Do not pause after day one. StackAdapt says results after the first day are [not a true indicator of long-term performance](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/pausing-digital-campaigns) (secondary) and suggests a first check on day three. Google notes a new campaign can show "Learning" for [up to 7 days](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190?hl=en).

## Why do campaigns get paused too early?

Most early pauses come from checking a dashboard too often. StackAdapt's blog (published 2020, secondary) warns that marketers can pause a programmatic campaign [after only one day](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/pausing-digital-campaigns) when expected results do not appear immediately. Its guidance is to check on the third day after launch in most cases.

The same post advises one change at a time. After an adjustment, it recommends allowing two to three days for the change to take effect before tweaking something else. Its example: remove a supply source on a Monday, leave the campaign undisturbed for a full day, and check back on Wednesday.

For a coffee shop or salon owner, the practical takeaway is a rule rather than a feeling. Write down the check dates before launch, change one setting per check, and leave the campaign alone between checks. The guidance is aimed at programmatic buying, which includes programmatic CTV, since that format uses [real-time bidding](https://adwave.com/resources/ctv-advertising-examples) (secondary).

## What does the learning phase mean for a pause decision?

A learning phase is the period when a platform's bidding system is still gathering data. Pausing or restructuring during it can throw that data away. Google Ads Help says major structural changes, such as creating a new campaign or bid strategy, may show a temporary "Learning" status for up to 7 days. It also says reactivating a Display campaign that was paused for an extended period restarts the learning phase.

Learning thresholds differ by platform and by source:

- Meta: Bons and Frazer (UK guide, secondary) says a lead campaign needs about 50 conversions a week to exit learning. At an assumed £20 cost per lead, that is roughly £1,000 a week.
- Performance Max: a vendor roundup cited by [Biznex](https://biznex.app/blogs/blogs-performance-max-for-local-business/) (secondary) lists learning estimates from 6 days to 6 weeks, and 4 to 6 weeks from another author.
- Smart bidding: Orbis (Ireland, secondary) says Target CPA and Target ROAS typically need a few weeks of consistent data.

Biznex also cites an observation that most accounts with poor Performance Max results made structural changes in weeks two to three, which reset learning. That is a third-party observation, not a Google rule, but it supports the one-change-at-a-time habit.

## Which signals say pause and which say keep running?

The table below turns the sourced guidance into a decision aid. Each row pairs a signal with an action and the reason behind it. Thresholds such as a target cost per lead belong to the advertiser and should be set before launch.

| Signal | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weak or zero results on day 1 | Keep running | Day-one results are not a true indicator of long-term performance (StackAdapt, secondary) |
| Day 3 after launch | First review; make one change at most | StackAdapt suggests a day-three check (secondary) |
| A setting was changed under 3 days ago | Wait before changing anything else | Allow two to three days for an optimization to take effect (StackAdapt, secondary) |
| Display bid, budget or targeting edit made today | Wait 12–24 hours before judging | Google says such changes can take 12–24 hours to apply |
| "Learning" status after a new campaign or bid strategy | Hold; do not restructure | Learning can last up to 7 days (Google Ads Help) |
| Daily budget below the platform's working floor | Raise the budget or cut the number of ad sets | Bons and Frazer (secondary): below about £10 a day, costs swing week to week |
| Weekly review shows an ad missing its cost-per-lead KPI | Pause that ad, keep the campaign | AdWave (secondary) advises pausing ads that miss KPIs |
| Conversion tracking not working | Pause or fix before spending more | AdWave (secondary) says to set up tracking before launch |
| The season the offer was built for has ended | Pause on purpose and note the date | Timing should follow peak demand (AdWave, secondary) |

One caution applies to the weekly KPI row: a "miss" only counts once the campaign has gathered enough data. AdWave suggests a test budget of around $500 to $2,000 to collect enough impression data before making decisions.

## How much budget does a campaign need before judging it?

A campaign that spends too little cannot produce enough results to judge. Bons and Frazer (secondary) says Meta has no minimum monthly spend, only a small daily floor per ad set. It puts the technical minimum at about $1 a day per ad set for impression billing and about $5 for clicks, likes or video views. The working floor for lead generation is nearer £10-£12 a day.

Its rule of thumb is to spend at least half the expected cost per lead each day, which averages a lead every couple of days. The minimum applies to each ad set separately, so three ad sets triple the daily floor. A £5 daily budget, the guide says, yields about 1-2 leads a week at a £20 cost per lead. Its suggested starting test is £12-£25 a day for at least two weeks.

On Google, Orbis (secondary) says an Irish account should usually budget at least a few hundred euro a month per active campaign to exit learning. For CTV, AdWave says campaigns can launch with [as little as $50](https://adwave.com/resources/ctv-advertising-examples), though it suggests $500 to $2,000 for a real test. Real CPM figures are in the [CTV cost breakdown](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data), and the [small-budget CTV worth-it guide](/blog/is-ctv-advertising-worth-it-small-budget-2026) covers when smaller spends make sense.

## When does a seasonal pause make sense?

A seasonal pause is a planned decision, not a reaction to a bad week. AdWave (secondary) recommends aligning campaign timing with peak seasonal demand, such as HVAC services in spring or tax services in the first quarter. It also advises planning a CTV calendar 3 to 6 months ahead by mapping the industry's peak seasons.

Orbis (secondary) suggests holding a seasonal reserve, often 20 to 30 percent of annual budget, for peak weeks when competition and returns spike. It describes Christmas as a long peak that rewards advertisers who start in November and sustain through last-order dates. That guidance comes from an Irish retail context, so a studio or salon should check its own booking data before copying it.

The restart penalty matters for any pause. Google says reactivating a Display campaign paused for an extended period restarts learning, but the page does not define "extended." Whether ad prices fall after a peak is not documented in these sources, so ask the platform or agency for current rates before planning a restart.

## What does a week-by-week review calendar look like?

The calendar below combines the sourced timing rules into one schedule. Dates are counted from launch day. Platform-specific windows, such as 4 to 6 weeks for Performance Max, can lengthen it.

1. Before launch: set the goal as a number, for example 50 new appointment bookings. Set up conversion tracking and campaign-tagged links, and add a dedicated tracking number for phone inquiries (AdWave, secondary). A [GA4 setup guide](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business) covers the analytics side.
2. Day 1: confirm ads are delivering. Make no judgment on results.
3. Day 3: first review. Check tracking and spend pacing, and change one thing at most.
4. Day 5-6: if a change was made on day 3, review it only after the two-to-three-day wait.
5. End of week 1: weekly review against the cost-per-lead KPI. Pause any single ad that clearly misses it, and keep two or three creative variations in rotation.
6. Week 2: no structural changes. Bons and Frazer suggests running a test for at least two weeks.
7. End of week 2: first real keep-or-pause decision, using the signal table above.
8. Weeks 3-6: for Performance Max or smart bidding, keep funding the campaign. Biznex's own checklist says a business should be able to fund 4 to 6 weeks without cutting the budget.
9. Season end: pause deliberately, record the date, and schedule the restart review.

## What does a worked example look like for a fitness studio?

This example uses assumptions throughout, applied to a local studio running Meta lead ads for trial-class signups. Every input below is an assumption, not a benchmark.

Assumptions: daily budget $30, one ad set, target cost per lead $40, pause threshold $80 per lead (twice the target), goal of 10 trial signups in two weeks.

- Budget floor check: half the target cost per lead is $20 a day. The $30 budget clears it.
- Expected pace: $30 × 7 = $210 a week, or about 5 leads a week at $40.
- Day 1: $30 spent, 0 leads. Action: keep running.
- Day 3: $90 spent, about 2 leads expected. Action: confirm tracking fires, change one thing at most.
- Day 14: $420 spent. With 10 leads, cost per lead is $42, so keep running. With 4 leads, it is $105, above the $80 threshold, so pause the weakest ad first. With 0 leads and verified tracking, pause the campaign.
- Learning check: 50 leads a week at $40 would need about $2,000 a week, roughly $286 a day. At $30 a day the campaign is not expected to leave learning on a leads objective (inference built on the roughly 50-a-week figure from Bons and Frazer), so judgment should rest on cost-per-lead trend, not the learning label.

## FAQ

### How long should a new ad campaign run before it is paused?

Sources suggest a first check on day three (StackAdapt, secondary), allowing up to 7 days for Google's Learning status, and running a Meta test for at least two weeks (Bons and Frazer, secondary). Performance Max learning estimates run from 6 days to 6 weeks, so the right window depends on the platform.

### Does pausing and restarting a campaign hurt performance?

For Google Display, Google says reactivating a campaign paused for an extended period restarts the learning phase. The page does not say how long counts as extended. Short pauses are not documented in the sources, so ask the platform or agency before pausing a campaign that is performing well.

### What if a campaign gets no leads in the first week?

Check tracking first. A small budget also produces few leads: Bons and Frazer (secondary) estimates a £5 daily budget returns 1-2 leads a week at a £20 cost per lead. Weekly, compare results against the cost-per-lead KPI and pause underperforming ads, as AdWave (secondary) advises.

### Is it better to pause one ad or the whole campaign?

AdWave (secondary) advises monitoring weekly and pausing ads that miss KPIs such as cost-per-lead, and running two or three creative variations before raising spend. Pausing a single weak ad keeps the campaign's learning intact, while a whole-campaign pause suits broken tracking or the end of a season.
