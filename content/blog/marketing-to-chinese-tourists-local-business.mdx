To win them as a local business, you need Mandarin information they can read, a presence on the Chinese platforms they use, and a campaign sized to what you can measure. Google, Facebook and Instagram are blocked in China, so the search and social channels you already use will not reach them. Most small businesses do not need a national campaign. They need a Mandarin listing, a small Xiaohongshu or Douyin presence, and a way to count where bookings come from.

## How Many Chinese Travellers Go Abroad Each Year?

Chinese outbound travel is large and recovering. [Shanghai Jungle](https://www.shanghaijungle.com/blog/tourism-marketing-china-guide) reports 145 million outbound trips in 2024, spending of 5.75 trillion RMB with a 17% year-on-year increase, and a projection of 170 million trips in 2026.

Earlier figures show the scale of the rebound. [HI-COM](https://www.hicom-asia.com/marketing-to-chinese-tourists-in-2023-where-are-they-going/) cites 170 million trips in 2019 worth 254 billion USD to the global economy, and says 2022 arrivals to most Asia-Pacific destinations were about a tenth of 2019 levels.

Forecasts depend on visa and flight policy, which can change. Treat them as direction, not a budget guarantee.

## Which Destinations and Visitors Should You Target?

Southeast Asia takes 37% of outbound overnight visitors, Northeast Asia 29% and Europe 21%, according to [Shanghai Jungle](https://www.shanghaijungle.com/blog/tourism-marketing-china-guide). The same source lists a 45-country visa-waiver list that includes Australia, France, Germany and Italy, and says visa-free policy is the strongest driver of destination choice for independent travellers.

[HI-COM](https://www.hicom-asia.com/marketing-to-chinese-tourists-in-2023-where-are-they-going/) reports, from Trip.com booking data, that the US, UK and Australia are the leading long-haul destinations. For a local business in those countries, that means Chinese visitors are most likely to be independent, planning online and arriving with little prior contact.

Visitor types differ. Shanghai Jungle describes younger travellers as booking last-minute from viral content, families wanting Chinese-language service and child-friendly options, and travellers over 60 as increasingly active on Douyin.

## Which Platforms Reach Chinese Travellers?

Google, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube and Twitter are blocked in China, according to [Shanghai Jungle](https://www.shanghaijungle.com/blog/tourism-marketing-china-guide). Chinese travellers research on domestic apps instead.

[Charlesworth Group](https://www.charlesworth-group.com/blog/how-to-attract-chinese-tourists-through-little-red-book/) describes Xiaohongshu (also called RED or Little Red Book) as having over 300 million registered users and about 350 million monthly active users. Nearly 40% of active users are under 24, traditionally about 70% are female, and over 85% are in urban areas. The same source cites research that 85% of Chinese tourists consult social platforms before booking, though it does not name the study.

Douyin suits the older and video-first audience. Read the [Douyin and TikTok guide for local business](/blog/douyin-tiktok-local-business-chinese-market) before choosing between them.

## What Should Your Mandarin Content Say?

Content should be written for Chinese readers, not translated word for word. [Charlesworth Group](https://www.charlesworth-group.com/blog/how-to-attract-chinese-tourists-through-little-red-book/) recommends Mandarin, acknowledging cultural norms, and matching local holidays and travel seasons. It also finds that users trust authentic experiences and peer reviews over polished promotion.

Responsiveness matters. The same source notes that Chinese users expect quick replies to questions. Plan who answers messages in Mandarin before you post.

[HI-COM](https://www.hicom-asia.com/marketing-to-chinese-tourists-in-2023-where-are-they-going/) notes that Chinese-speaking guides make guests feel more at ease in an unfamiliar country, and that they also give you collateral. If you draft copy with an AI tool, follow the [ChatGPT prompts guide for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) and have a fluent speaker check every line before it goes live. Time your posts around peak travel dates, as the [Chinese New Year marketing guide](/blog/chinese-new-year-marketing-local-business) explains.

## How Do You Check Whether a Chinese-Market Campaign Pays?

The example below uses assumptions, not sourced figures. Replace them with your own numbers before deciding.

Assume a walking-tour company runs 30 tours a month at an average booking of $60, and spends $400 a month on a Mandarin listing and Xiaohongshu posting. The table shows the break-even point.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly tours (assumption) | Input | 30 |
| Average booking (assumption) | Input | $60 |
| Campaign cost (assumption) | Input per month | $400 |
| Extra bookings at a 10% lift (assumption) | 30 x 10% | 3 |
| Extra revenue | 3 x $60 | $180 |
| Net monthly result | $180 - $400 | -$220 |
| Break-even extra bookings | $400 / $60, rounded up | 7 |
| Break-even lift | 7 / 30 | 23% |

At a 10% lift the campaign loses money, so either the cost must fall or the lift must reach about 23%. This example ignores margin. If your margin is 50%, break-even moves to roughly 14 extra bookings a month. Run the same sum with your own figures before committing.

## What Does a 90-Day Plan Look Like?

Work through these steps in order:

1. Write a one-page Simplified Chinese version of your site or listing with address, opening hours, prices, payment options and nearest transit.
2. Choose one platform, Xiaohongshu or Douyin, based on which visitor group you see most.
3. Post with three to five relevant hashtags, including location tags, as [Charlesworth Group](https://www.charlesworth-group.com/blog/how-to-attract-chinese-tourists-through-little-red-book/) suggests.
4. Invite a few local creators whose audience matches your visitors, and offer a clear, honest experience rather than a scripted promotion.
5. Name one person who answers Mandarin messages within a short, agreed time.
6. On day 90, ask every new customer how they found you, and compare the count with your break-even figure.

## FAQ

### Do I need to speak Mandarin to attract Chinese tourists?

No, but Mandarin written content makes it easier to be found and trusted. Shanghai Jungle and Charlesworth Group both point to Mandarin content as a basic requirement for Chinese-market reach.

### Which platform should a small business start with?

Choose based on your visitors. Charlesworth Group describes Xiaohongshu as a strong travel-research platform, while Shanghai Jungle notes older travellers are active on Douyin.

### Can I use Google Ads to reach Chinese travellers?

Google is blocked in China, so Google Ads will not reach people using Chinese apps. Reaching them requires Chinese-language platforms.

### How many Chinese travellers visit my city?

The sources here give national and global figures, not city-level counts. Use your own booking data and ask new customers where they are from.
