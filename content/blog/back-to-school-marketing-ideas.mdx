The US National Retail Federation (NRF) says families with children in elementary through high school plan to spend an average of $863.86 on school items in 2026, and 62% of shoppers had already started buying by early July. That is retail spending on electronics, clothes, shoes and supplies, so a salon or studio does not get a slice of it automatically. What it does show is the pattern your customers follow: they decide early, they hunt for value, and the rush is over before the first bell.

This guide gives local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada a six-week plan built around real term dates, plus the math to check whether a back-to-school offer is worth running. The ideas work for cafes, salons, groomers and fitness studios, but the calendar matters more than the idea. Dollar figures below are US benchmarks or labelled assumptions; use your own prices.

## What the Data Says (and What It Does Not)

The NRF's [July 2026 survey release](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/majority-of-back-to-school-shoppers-get-a-head-start-on-the-season) is the cleanest public source for US back-to-school behaviour. It was fielded July 1 to 8, 2026 with 7,677 consumers.

Three points matter for a local business:

- **Early planning is normal.** Most shoppers had started before July. If your promotion only appears in the last week, you are late.
- **Value sits at the front of the buyer's mind.** The NRF release says shoppers are keeping value front and center, and over half used June sales events. A local business can compete on convenience and certainty rather than the lowest price.
- **It measures goods, not services.** The NRF's four categories are electronics, clothing, shoes and school supplies. There is no public figure here for haircuts, coffee or classes, so any claim that "back to school lifts salon bookings by X%" is not backed by this data. Measure your own.

Two things follow. First, if you are in Australia, your biggest window is not behind you. It is roughly sixteen weeks away from October 2026, so planning starts now. Second, in the UK and the US the next natural "reset" moments are the October half-term, the January return and the spring term, which suit smaller versions of the same campaign.

## The Six-Week Plan

Count back from your local first day of term. In Queensland that is 27 January, so Week 6 begins in mid-December; for a US district starting in late August, Week 6 is mid-July.

**Week 6 to 5: Decide the offer and fix the basics.**
- Pick one offer per business, with dates, eligibility and a capacity cap.
- Update your Google Business Profile with term-time opening hours, new photos and a link that opens your booking page. Our [GBP optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers the fields.
- Open the extra slots you will need. A salon that is fully booked the week before term cannot sell a back-to-school cut.

**Week 4: Tell your own list first.**
Send one email or text to past customers before you spend anything on ads. Parents who already know you convert more cheaply than strangers. Use opt-in contacts only. See [email marketing for small business](/blog/email-marketing-for-small-business) for the structure of a single-offer email.

**Week 3 to 2: Go public.**
- Post the offer on your Business Profile using the [Google Business Profile posts guide](/blog/google-business-profile-posts).
- Run a small local ad if you have a booking page that works on a phone: a Search campaign for "kids haircut near me" style terms, or a short Meta campaign within a few miles. Our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business) shows how to size it.
- Ask nearby schools, parent associations and university unions how they accept local offers. Follow each one's advertising policy.

**Week 1: Reminders and last-minute slots.**
Send a short "we have slots this week" message to your list. People remember the day before term more than the month before.

**Weeks 1 to 3 after term starts: Convert one visit into the habit.**
Ask for a review within a day of the visit, and give new customers a reason to rebook within 30 to 60 days (a loyalty stamp, a pre-booked next slot, a class pack).

These are options, not proven winners. Test one.

- **Coffee shops:** a pre-order for the school-run window, a staff-room coffee box for teachers, or a study-hour price in quiet periods. See [email marketing for coffee shops](/blog/email-marketing-for-coffee-shops).
- **Hair salons and barbers:** early family slots, a small sibling perk, a pre-term text saying "book before the rush". See [hair salon Instagram bookings](/blog/hair-salon-instagram-bookings) and [email marketing for hair salons](/blog/email-marketing-for-hair-salons).

- **Fitness and yoga studios:** a one- or two-week intro pass aimed at parents who gain daytime hours, or a student rate with a clear price. ## The Math

Take a salon or studio running a back-to-school offer with a $300 ad budget. Every input is an **assumption** for illustration; replace it with your numbers.

| | Assumption | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad budget | $300 | |
| Cost per click | $2.50 (assumed) | 120 clicks |
| Click to booking rate | 4% (assumed) | about 5 bookings |
| Free email/text bookings from your existing list | 5% of 300 contacts (assumed) | 15 bookings |
| Average first-visit value after offer | $45 (assumed) | |
| Margin on that visit | 60% (assumed) | $27 per visit |
| Total new bookings | 5 paid + 15 list | 20 |
| Gross margin from first visits | 20 x $27 | $540 |
| Ad cost per paid booking | $300 / 5 | $60 |
| Break-even repeat rate | Needed to cover $60 on ad bookings | about 1.2 extra visits per ad customer |

How to read it: the email and text bookings cost almost nothing, while the paid bookings cost more than their first visit earns. That is normal, and the profit comes from the rebooking. If you cannot get 1 to 2 repeat visits from a new customer, cut the ad budget and put the effort into the list. The break-even row is simply $60 divided by $27, about 2.2 total visits, so 1.2 beyond the first.

## Measure It

Use one discount code per channel, or ask "how did you hear about us?" on every first booking. Count new customers, not clicks, and check repeat visits at 30 and 60 days. If you use ad conversion tracking, our [conversion tracking setup guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) walks through it, and the [negative keywords guide](/blog/negative-keywords-google-ads) keeps the spend clean. For a short, high-conversion event, Google's help page says seasonality adjustments suit events of about 1 to 7 days and may be less effective beyond 14 days, but only when you use Smart Bidding with Target CPA or Target ROAS and expect a major change in conversion rate.

## FAQ

**Q: When should I start back-to-school marketing?**
A: Six weeks before your local term starts is a workable plan: offer and basics first, your own list at four weeks, public promotion at two to three weeks. The NRF found 62% of US shoppers had started buying by early July, so earlier is safer for retail-style offers.

**Q: Do I need to discount?**
A: No. Priority slots, add-ons and rebooking perks protect margin better. Use the math above to see what a first visit earns before you cut price.

**Q: Is back to school relevant in Australia?**
A: Yes, on a different calendar. Queensland's Term 1 2027 starts on 27 January and NSW's Eastern division on 3 February, so the plan starts in December.

**Q: Can I promote at local schools?**
A: Often through sponsorships, fundraisers or parent associations, but follow each school's advertising rules and always ask first.

**Q: How much should I spend on ads for this?**
A: Start with a small test, around the $300 in the example, only if your booking page works on mobile. Judge it on cost per new customer and repeat visits, not clicks.
