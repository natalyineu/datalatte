Back to school is a short, early buying window, so local businesses should start planning about six weeks before their own term begins. US estimates put 2026 K-12 and college back-to-school spending at $146.8 billion, and 32% of shoppers began buying in early June, the highest level since the NRF started asking in 2018. Most of that money goes on retail goods, so a salon or studio should copy the timing and offer logic rather than the spending. This guide gives a six-week plan, vendor budget benchmarks and a worked example with assumed inputs.

## What Does the Back-to-School Data Show for Local Business?

StackAdapt describes back to school as one of the peak seasons, alongside the holidays and Prime Day, when consumer spending tends to increase. It also defines seasonal marketing as marketing built around events such as the back to school season, so the calendar is the starting point for the campaign, not an afterthought.

Three cautions apply. These figures measure retail goods, not services, so there is no public number here for haircuts, coffee or classes. The figures are retold by StackAdapt from NRF and eMarketer data, so treat them as secondary. Measure your own bookings before you assume any seasonal lift.

## When Should a Local Business Start?

AdWave advises starting back-to-school TV advertising in mid-July. Its 2026 summer trends page goes further and says early August is too late, recommending that awareness and consideration creative start in mid-July. A separate AdWave guide for daycares recommends advertising four to six weeks before back-to-school, because August is the biggest enrollment month.

Your local calendar decides the final date. Check your state, provincial or council site for the first day of term, then count back six weeks. Dates vary by district and school board, so do not copy a neighbouring area's calendar. The six-week count applies to your own local start, not to a national date.

AdWave sells TV advertising, so read these as a vendor's benchmarks, not neutral market rates. The smaller-service-area line is the most useful for a single neighbourhood business, because it shows that a tight local radius can work on a far smaller budget than a national brand.

## How Do You Build a Six-Week Plan?

Count back from your local first day of term and work through these steps.

1. Week 6: Choose one offer with an end date, an eligibility rule and a capacity cap. Update your Google Business Profile hours and photos, and open extra booking slots so the offer can actually be fulfilled.
2. Week 4: Email or text past customers who have opted in. Ask them to refer a friend. AdWave cites Wharton School research finding that referred customers have 16% higher lifetime value than non-referred customers.
3. Weeks 3 to 2: Post the offer on your Google Business Profile. If your booking page works well on a phone, run a small local search or social campaign aimed at your service area.
4. Week 1: Send a short last-slot reminder to your list.
5. After term starts: Ask each new customer for a review and offer a reason to rebook within 30 to 60 days.

## Which Businesses Need a Different Calendar?

Some sectors have their own seasonal peaks. AdWave says dental new patient acquisition peaks with back-to-school check-ups in August, alongside January insurance plan changes and a year-end benefits rush in November and December. For dental practices, August is the window to prepare for, not the only one to plan around.

Tutoring follows a staged pattern. AdWave recommends awareness video and social ads in early to mid August, adding search from late August through September as the peak capture window, and a lighter presence from October for fall and winter exam dates. It also describes connected TV and AI-generated ads as options, though these are vendor claims to test rather than proven results. Similar calendar questions come up in other markets, so the [New Zealand guide](/blog/local-marketing-new-zealand-small-business-guide), the [Singapore guide](/blog/local-marketing-singapore-small-business-guide) and the [Malaysia guide](/blog/local-marketing-malaysia-small-business-guide) cover local context for those countries.

## Is a $300 Back-to-School Test Worth Running?

Every input below is an assumption for a salon or studio. Replace them with your own figures before you decide.

| Line | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad budget | $300 | Assumption |
| Cost per click | $2.50 | Assumption |
| Clicks | 120 | $300 divided by $2.50 |
| Click-to-booking rate | 4% | Assumption |
| Paid bookings | about 5 | 120 x 4%, rounded from 4.8 |
| Email or text bookings | 15 | Assumption: 5% of 300 opted-in contacts |
| Total new bookings | 20 | 5 + 15 |
| First-visit value | $45 | Assumption |
| Margin on a first visit | $27 | Assumption: 60% of $45 |
| Gross margin from 20 first visits | $540 | 20 x $27 |
| Net after ad budget | $240 | $540 minus $300 |
| Ad cost per paid booking | $60 | $300 divided by 5 |
| Total visits needed to cover $60 | about 2.2 | $60 divided by $27 |

Each paid booking costs $60 but earns $27 on the first visit, so the test pays only if those customers come back. They need about 1.2 extra visits each to break even. The list bookings assume email costs nothing, which is generous, so count your staff time too. If repeat visits fall short, move the budget into your list.

## How Do You Measure Results and Referrals?

Use one discount code or booking question per channel, and count new customers rather than clicks. Check repeat visits at 30 and 60 days, because a first visit alone rarely covers the cost of paid acquisition. This is the measure that matters in the worked example above.

Community support can also bring in referrals at low cost. AdWave lists $15 to $25 for a prize in a school, church or youth team raffle among its customer appreciation ideas under $50. Check each organisation's rules on sponsorship and fundraising before you offer anything.

## What Should You Do Before Term Starts?

Start with the offer, not the advert. A single clear offer with a capacity limit is easier to fulfil and easier to measure than a set of discounts. Add-ons, priority booking and rebooking packs protect margin better than a flat price cut, and the worked example shows what a first visit is worth before you decide.

Set up tracking before anything goes live. A booking question such as "how did you hear about us?" costs nothing and shows which channel brought each new customer.

## FAQ

### When should a small business start back-to-school marketing?

Start planning about six weeks before your local first day of term. AdWave says early August is too late for awareness creative in the US, and recommends starting in mid-July.

### Do you need to discount to compete?

Not necessarily. Priority slots, add-ons and rebooking perks protect margin. Use the worked example to see what a first visit earns before cutting your price.

### Is back to school relevant outside the US?

Yes, but the calendar differs by country, state, province and district. Check your own council, state, provincial or school board site before counting back six weeks.

### How much should a salon spend on a test?

The worked example uses $300 as an assumption. Start only if your booking page works on a phone, and judge the test on cost per new customer and repeat visits, not clicks.
