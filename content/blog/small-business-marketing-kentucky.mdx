A Kentucky small business should start with three free or cheap basics: a complete Google Business Profile, a small Google Search campaign aimed at a tight radius, and a seasonal plan built around the visitors who actually reach your town. No source we could open publishes a Kentucky-only ad price. The national benchmark from WordStream by LocaliQ (June 2026) is $5.42 per click and $66.69 per lead across industries.

Kentucky has one unusual traffic source. The Kentucky Distillers' Association says the Kentucky Bourbon Trail drew about 2.7 million visits in 2025, roughly 80 percent from outside the state. That matters for cafes, salons and gyms near distilleries, and much less for businesses in towns the trail does not reach.

## What Makes Kentucky Marketing Different?

Demand is uneven across the state. Louisville, Lexington and the distillery towns see tourists and event crowds. Smaller towns depend on repeat local customers. So decide first whether you sell to visitors, residents, or both, because the keywords, the hours you advertise and the offer all change.

The Bourbon Trail figures are self-reported by member distilleries and compiled by IQS Research of Louisville, according to the association. Treat them as a sign of scale, not a count of people who will walk into your shop. We did not find a published source for how many of those visitors spend money outside the distillery.

Local rankings matter for both groups. Google's help says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that you cannot pay for a better local ranking. That is why the profile comes before ads.

## Where Should a Kentucky Business Spend First?

Fix free things before paid ones. Claim and complete the profile, add real photos, set holiday hours, and ask satisfied customers for reviews. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) lists the fields to complete.

Then add a small Search campaign. ## How Do You Keep Ads Inside Your Town?

Google says radius targeting needs a radius of at least 1 km, and it recommends the broader "Presence or Interest" setting for Search. "Presence" only reaches people physically in the area, so it suits a business that cannot serve anyone else.

For a distillery-adjacent shop, the broader setting can be right, because visitors planning a trip search from other states before they arrive. For a barber or groomer whose customers live nearby, test "Presence" and check the Search terms report. Add negative keywords for jobs, courses and free-advice searches. Google notes that negatives do not match close variants, so add plurals yourself.

## What Is a Realistic First Budget? A Worked Example

Every input below is an **assumption** that borrows the national LocaliQ averages. It is not a Kentucky price.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | Chosen | $500 |
| Cost per click | LocaliQ national average | $5.42 |
| Clicks | $500 / $5.42 | about 92 |
| Conversion rate | LocaliQ, Google Ads, all industries | 8.18% |
| Leads (calls, bookings) | 92 x 8.18% | about 7.5 |
| Share who become customers (assumption) | Chosen | 40% |
| New customers | 7.5 x 40% | about 3 |
| Cost per new customer | $500 / 3 | about $167 |

Whether $167 pays back depends on what a customer is worth over a year. A salon client who returns six times at $60 is worth about $360 on these assumptions. A single $4 coffee is not. Replace the assumptions with your own average sale and repeat rate before you commit.

## Is There Free Help for Kentucky Owners?

Yes, some. University of Kentucky pages describe the Kentucky Small Business Development Center as offering no-cost, confidential coaching, planning and marketing assistance, funded in part through the U.S. Small Business Administration. Those descriptions come from university and news pages of varying age, so confirm current services and your nearest office at ksbdc.org.

Food and farm businesses can also look at Kentucky Proud, the state agriculture department's brand. Reports describe cost-share programs for advertising and signage, but terms have changed over the years. Check kyproud.com for current rules and eligibility.

## FAQ

### How much does Google Ads cost in Kentucky?

No Kentucky-specific average is published in the sources we opened. LocaliQ's 2026 national average is $5.42 per click, and individual categories range widely, from $2.05 for restaurants to $9.87 for legal services.

### Do I need a different plan for Louisville than for a small town?

Usually yes. A city has more competitors and more search volume, so a tight radius and specific keywords matter. In a small town, the Business Profile and reviews often do most of the work, and ads can be small.

### Is the Bourbon Trail relevant to my business?

Only if visitors pass your door. About 2.7 million visits were reported in 2025, but they are spread across nearly 70 distilleries. Check where your customers come from before building a tourist campaign.

### When should I advertise around big events?

Start before the event, since visitors search while planning. We did not find a published Kentucky Derby attendance figure we could verify for 2026, so size your budget from your own sales on past event days.

### Should I use Meta ads as well?

Test them after Search. Our [Instagram and Facebook comparison](/blog/instagram-ads-vs-facebook-ads) explains the costs and when each helps a local shop.
