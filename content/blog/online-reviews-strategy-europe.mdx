A European small business should ask every customer for an honest review, never pay or reward for one, and never pick only happy customers to ask. Google, Trustpilot and, in the UK, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 all point the same way. Google's Maps policy bans incentivised reviews and selective solicitation, and Trustpilot says it does not allow incentivised reviews. Build a simple routine: one neutral request after every visit, a reply to every review, and a record of what you did.

This guide covers the rules in the sources that could be verified, a worked plan and what could not be confirmed. Tripadvisor's guidelines and EU national laws were not verified, so check them for your country.

## What Do Google's Review Rules Say?

Google's Maps contributed content policy bans reviews posted because of an incentive from a business, such as payment, discounts, or free goods or services. It also says merchants may not selectively solicit positive reviews, discourage negative ones, pressure users to review on the premises, or ask staff to collect a certain number of reviews.

What is allowed: soliciting content that represents a genuine experience, without offering incentives or trying to influence the rating or text. Google also bans reviews posted from multiple accounts by or at the request of one person. See our [Google reviews strategy](/blog/google-business-reviews-strategy) for the full routine.

## What Does Trustpilot Require?

Trustpilot's June 2026 guidelines state it does not allow incentivised reviews, including discounts, prize draws, refunds or freebies. It forbids inviting only people you know had a positive experience and timing invitations so only happy customers get them. It also bars sending happy customers to review while sending unhappy ones to contact you instead.

Invite everyone with a genuine experience, once per experience, the same way and at the same point in their journey. Allow time to experience the product first. Do not invite family, staff or shareholders. Use neutral wording and never suggest reviewers should only leave positive reviews.

## What Do the Law and Regulators Say?

In the UK, the CMA published guidance on 4 April 2025 on a new banned practice under the DMCC Act. It covers fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews, and says traders publishing reviews must take steps to prevent fake or hidden-incentive reviews. The guidance says commissioning is not limited to money: asking a member of staff to write a review counts as an example.

For the EU, the European Commission lists Directive (EU) 2019/2161 as amending the unfair commercial practices rules, applying from 28 May 2022, and lists consumer reviews among topics in its guidance. The page checked gives no detail on penalties, which differ by country. This is not legal advice.

## How Should You Ask for Reviews?

Send the same neutral message to every customer after the visit. Link straight to your Google review page. Do not offer a discount for reviewing, and do not offer a reward for removing a bad one. Google's policy bans both.

Reply to every review. Google Business Profile help says replies show customers you value their feedback. For a negative review, answer politely, offer to continue offline and avoid arguing. Our [fake review guide](/blog/how-to-deal-with-fake-google-reviews) explains what to flag, and [managing online reviews](/blog/managing-online-reviews-small-business) covers the routine.

## How Do You Handle Different Countries?

Rules and favored platforms vary across Europe, so check each country you serve. Google works in all of them, which makes it the base. Add a second platform only if your customers use it, and read that platform's guidelines first. Tripadvisor's guidelines could not be reached when this article was written, so confirm them before you ask guests for reviews there.

## What Does a 30-Day Plan Look Like? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions** for a salon with 120 appointments a month.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Appointments per month | 120 |
| Customers who receive the request | 120 (all, not a selection) |
| Share who leave a review | 5% |
| New reviews | 6 |
| Replies written | 6 |
| Time per reply | 5 minutes |
| Weekly time | about 10 minutes |

Under these assumptions the salon gains six reviews a month and spends 30 minutes replying. Ratings may drop if you ask everyone; that is the point of the rule. A request sent only to customers who seemed happy is exactly what Google and Trustpilot forbid.

## FAQ

### Can I give a discount for a Google review?
No. Google's policy bans reviews posted due to an incentive from a business, such as payment, discounts or free goods or services.

### Can I only ask customers who were happy?
No. Google bans selectively soliciting positive reviews, and Trustpilot prohibits inviting only people who had a positive experience.

### Does UK law apply to small businesses?
The CMA guidance covers traders that submit, commission or publish reviews. Read it for your situation or ask a lawyer.

### Do EU rules differ by country?
The Directive applies EU-wide from 28 May 2022, but national laws implement it. Check your country's consumer authority.

### What about Tripadvisor?
Its guidelines were not reachable when this article was written, so no claim is made. Read them before you ask for reviews there.
