Bakeries have an unusual advantage on short video: the product is visual, the process is satisfying, and you can sell it in a way that avoids waste, by taking pre-orders before you bake. This guide is the organic playbook for bakeries in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: what to film, how to turn a video into a pre-order, how to run a sell-out drop, and the allergen rules that matter when your videos and posts talk about ingredients. Dollar figures are illustrative; run the same sums in your own currency.

## What to Film: Four Formats That Fit a Bakery

**Process shots.** Laminating croissant dough, scoring sourdough, piping buttercream, a cake going from bare sponge to finished design. Keep the real sound and cut anything that is not the satisfying part. There is no magic length.

**Reveals.** The oven door opening, a tray coming out, a cake box opened at pickup (with the customer's permission). These double as proof that real people collect real orders.

**Pre-order prompts.** A single item, its price, the cut-off date and how to order. "Christmas pies: 40 available, order by Dec 15" works because it creates a deadline and a limit at once.

**Local hooks.** The 5am start, the neighbourhood school's birthday order, the nearby cafe you supply. Viewers in other cities can enjoy these, but only your neighbours can act on them. ## Make the Video Point at an Order

Views do not pay the rent. A video can reach 100,000 people and bring none through your door if most of them live somewhere else. Give each video one job and one next step:

- **Custom cakes:** show the finished design, then say the lead time and how to enquire ("Order two weeks ahead, form in our bio").
- **Pre-orders:** name the pickup day and the cut-off.
- **Walk-ins:** name the street or neighbourhood out loud and put it on screen.

Keep the order path short. A form that asks for date, flavour, size and allergies beats a long menu PDF. And reply to comments fast: price and "do you have gluten-free?" questions are buying signals.

## The Sell-Out Drop

A drop is a small, announced batch, such as 24 cruffins on Saturday at 8am. It suits TikTok because it gives a video a date and a reason to act now. Used well, it also protects your margin, because you bake to demand.

How to run one:

1. **Announce early.** Post the item, the quantity and the pickup window two or three days ahead.
2. **Cap it.** A real limit is what makes "sold out" believable. Only say "limited" when it is.
3. **Take pre-orders if you can.** You know exactly what to bake, and no one stands in a queue for nothing.
4. **Film the sell-out.** An empty tray at 8:40 is your next video, and it is honest.

## Allergens: What Your Posts Can and Cannot Say

Bakeries sit at the centre of food allergy rules, and TikTok content can drift into claims without anyone noticing. No TikTok policy specific to bakery allergen or "gluten-free" claims turned up in research for this article, so the rules that matter are food-labelling law in your country. Points confirmed from official pages:

- **US:** The FDA lists nine major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame. Its [food allergies page](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies) says retail and food-service businesses that package and label products for sale must declare them, but food wrapped after a customer orders it at the point of purchase is exempt, and most unpackaged foods sold at retail or food service fall outside the FDA labelling rules. So a boxed pre-order labelled in advance is treated differently from a loaf wrapped at the counter. State and local health rules can differ, so ask your local health department.
- **"Gluten-free":** The FDA's [gluten-free labelling page](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/gluten-free-labeling-foods) describes it as a voluntary claim that has a regulatory definition. Do not use the phrase casually in a video caption if your kitchen also handles wheat flour. Say what is true, such as "made without wheat ingredients, baked in a kitchen that also handles wheat."
- **UK:** The government's [PPDS introduction](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/introduction-to-allergen-labelling-for-ppds-food) says allergen labelling requirements for food prepacked for direct sale, also called Natasha's Law, apply in England, Northern Ireland and Wales. From summaries of FSA announcements (second-hand; not checked against the FSA page itself), the rule requires a full ingredients list with allergens emphasised on food packed on the premises before the customer orders it. That covers a pre-boxed pastry selection, for example. Scotland is separate, so check Food Standards Scotland.
- **Australia and Canada:** These markets were not verified for this article, so check your national food regulator before making allergen claims.

One more reason to be careful: a CIEH report on a BBC investigation found food listings on TikTok Shop that lacked allergen information, and reported that UK businesses must state whether products contain any of the 14 main allergens ([CIEH](https://www.cieh.org/ehn/food-safety-integrity/2025/july/foods-listed-on-tiktok-shop-exclude-key-allergen-information/)). The lesson for your own account: if a video leads to a sale, the allergen information has to be easy to find at the point of order.

## Set Up the Account: Music and Profile

**Use a business account and cleared music.** TikTok's ads blog on the [Commercial Music Library](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/audio-library-royalty-free-music) says the general library is not cleared for any brand or business and that promoting a business through a personal account puts you at risk of copyright infringement. It tells users to confirm they have a Business Account before using the Commercial Music Library. TikTok's [help page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library) describes the library as a pre-cleared catalogue of 1 million songs, free for businesses and usable for organic content. A trending sound that is not in the library may not be safe for your bakery's account.

**Profile:** bakery name, neighbourhood, hours, and the one thing you want people to do (order, pre-order, visit). Link to your order form. Availability of links depends on your account and region, so check the profile editor.

## Count Orders, Not Views

TikTok shows views, not sales. Use three cheap checks:

1. **A code word.** "Say CROISSANT at the counter for a free coffee" gives a count without software. Only offer what you can honour.
2. **A field on the order form:** "How did you hear about us?" with TikTok as an option.
3. **Your Google Business Profile.** Check directions and website clicks after a strong video. This shows correlation, not proof, so combine it with the first two. The Strong column is a rare outcome, so do not plan around it. The Tough column is common, since many accounts post for months before one video travels.

The cake sum is separate and simpler. Break-even orders = time cost divided by gross profit per order = $250 / ($85 x 0.55) = 5.3, so about 6 orders. A custom cake is the best product to promote because one order covers a large share of your monthly effort. A $3 cookie needs 139 sales to do the same ($250 / ($3 x 0.60)). That is why custom cakes, boxes and pre-orders lead the content plan, and single cookies ride along.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should a bakery post on TikTok?**
A: TikTok does not publish a minimum. Two or three posts a week is a sustainable starting point (my suggestion); consistency over two months matters more than daily bursts.

**Q: Can I say my bakes are gluten-free or nut-free in a video?**
A: Only if it is true for how you actually bake. The FDA describes "gluten-free" as a voluntary claim with a regulatory definition, and allergen rules vary by country. If your kitchen handles wheat or nuts, say so plainly.

**Q: Do I need a business account?**
A: To use music legally for a business, yes. TikTok says the general library is not cleared for brands and tells users to confirm they have a Business Account before using the Commercial Music Library.

**Q: Which products should I promote?**
A: Custom cakes, boxes and pre-orders, because one sale covers more of your time. Use single low-priced items as filler in process videos.

**Q: What if nobody in my town uses TikTok?**
A: Check your customer ages and track where orders come from. If your buyers skew older, put more effort into Google Business Profile and Google search.
