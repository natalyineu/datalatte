To set up Google Ads for a small business, create a Search campaign in five steps: choose a goal, set campaign settings (bidding, locations, language), build an ad group with keywords, write the ads and choose a daily budget. If you want less control, a Smart Search campaign asks only for your business, website, goal, keyword themes, location and budget. Median search click costs were $5.42 across industries in LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark, so start with a small daily budget.

## Should You Use a Smart Campaign or a Search Campaign?

Google describes a Smart Search campaign as a campaign that shows your ad on Google Search and Google Maps when people look for businesses like yours. You pay when someone clicks, Google manages the campaign after setup and you can cancel anytime. It needs a Google Business Profile linked to your account, or a business name if you have none.

A standard Search campaign gives control over keywords, match types and locations. Use Smart if you have no time and a simple offer. Use Search if you want to see which searches trigger your ads and block waste. Smart campaigns are only available in Smart Mode.

## What Do You Need Before You Start a Google Ads Account?

Have these ready:

- A Google account and a website or landing page that works on a phone
- A clear goal: calls, bookings, form fills or visits
- A verified phone number in international format if you want call ads
- A payment method
- A Google Business Profile, if you serve customers locally. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) shows how to prepare it.

Google says at least one conversion goal is required to create a Search campaign, even if you skip goal guidance, so decide what counts as a result first.

## How Do You Create the Campaign in Steps 1 and 2?

**Step 1: goal and type.** Go to Campaigns, click the plus icon and choose New campaign. Pick an objective (Sales, Leads or Website traffic) or create a campaign without goal guidance, then choose Search as the campaign type and add your conversion goals.

**Step 2: settings.** Choose a bidding focus (Conversions, Clicks, Conversion value or Impression Share). Under Networks, decide whether to include Google search partners and the Display Network. Set Locations and Languages. Add audiences if you want them, and add assets such as sitelinks, calls and location.

Two settings save money for a local business. First, use location options: Google says "Presence or interest" is recommended as a best practice on Search, and "Presence" fits when you only want people actually in your area. Radius targets must be at least 1 km. Second, turn off the Display Network on a first Search campaign unless you mean to use it.

## How Do You Build Ad Groups and Ads in Steps 3 and 4?

**Step 3: ad group and keywords.** Google recommends standard ad groups for new advertisers. Type keywords on separate lines. Match types: broad is the default, phrase uses quotation marks and exact uses brackets. Add negative keywords with a minus sign. Google notes negatives do not match close variants, so add plurals and synonyms yourself. Our guide on [negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) lists what to block.

**Step 4: ads.** Enter your final URL and write headlines and descriptions that match the keywords in the ad group. Google recommends at least three ads per ad group. Review the summary and use Fix it on any billing, keyword or URL issue, then Publish.

To bring in phone calls, add a call asset. Google says clicks on a call asset on mobile cost the same as headline clicks. The number must be in strict international (E.164) format and visible in your site code or verified through Search Console. Vanity and premium numbers are not accepted.

## What Budget Should You Set in Step 5?

You can enter an average daily budget or a total campaign budget with start and end dates. Google says daily spend can vary by day but averages out over the month.

Use this as a sizing method, with every input an **assumption**:

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Daily budget | $20 |
| Days in month | 30 |
| Monthly spend | $600 |
| Cost per click (all-industry median) | $5.42 |
| Clicks | about 111 |
| Conversion rate | 8% (round figure; LocaliQ's all-industry median is reported as 8.18%) |
| Conversions | about 9 |
| Cost per conversion | about $67 |

That cost per conversion lines up with LocaliQ's all-industry cost per lead of $66.69, which is a median across many trades. Your industry will differ. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows how to split spend across channels.

## How Do You Track Whether It Worked?

Google lists four conversion types: website, app, phone call and offline. Website actions generally rely on a tag or code snippet on your site, and Google recommends the Google tag if you do not use Google Analytics. Call assets use a Google forwarding number and need call reporting turned on in account settings. For calls to a number on your website, you add a phone snippet, and a call counts only if it lasts longer than a minimum length you set.

Check the Search terms report weekly for the first month, add negatives, and judge results after at least two weeks of data. If you serve one town, review location reports too. Our [responsive search ads guide](/blog/google-responsive-search-ads-best-practices-10-tips-to-improve-your-ad-strength) covers ad copy once the campaign is live.

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to start Google Ads?

Google sets no fixed price; you choose a daily or total budget. The 2026 median click cost across industries was $5.42 in LocaliQ's benchmark, so a $20 daily budget buys roughly 3 to 4 clicks a day on that median.

### How long does setup take?

The five steps can be completed in one sitting if your website, phone number and conversion goals are ready. Allow extra time for ad approval and phone number verification.

### Is a Smart campaign enough for a small business?

For a simple local service with a linked Business Profile, it can be. You lose keyword and search term control, which matters when budget is tight.

### Can I set up Google Ads without a website?

Smart campaigns ask for a website, and Search campaigns need a final URL. A single mobile-friendly page with your phone number and offer is enough to begin.
