Here is a number to hold onto: 25. If a campaign costs you about $80 a month in tool fees and your own time, and each extra visit it brings in leaves you $3.30 after drink cost and the offer itself, you need roughly 25 extra visits a month to break even. That is under one extra customer a day. Those inputs are my assumptions, and we will work through them below, but the point stands: email for a cafe is a small-numbers game, and AI matters because it cuts the time cost, not because it makes emails magic.

This guide is for coffee shop owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers which jobs AI does well, which plan limits will trip you up, the three automations to build first, the legal minimum for sending, and the math on whether it pays.

## Where AI Actually Helps (and Where It Does Not)

AI is good at the blank-page problem. Give it three bullet points and it drafts a newsletter. Ask for ten subject lines and you get ten. It is not good at knowing that your oat milk delivery is late or that Tuesday is dead. That part is yours.

| Task | What AI does | What you still do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject lines | Offers 5 to 10 options in your tone | Pick one, cut any clickbait |
| Newsletter draft | Turns bullets into 100 to 150 words | Add real details, prices, the photo |
| Welcome series | Drafts three short emails | Check the offer and links work |
| Win-back email | Drafts a short "we miss you" note | Choose who gets it and what it costs you |
| Reports | Summarises clicks and trends | Decide what to change |

## Pick a Tool: What the Free Plans Actually Allow

Plan limits are where cafe owners get caught, and they change often. These were on the vendors' own pages when this article was updated:

| Tool | Free plan limits | AI note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/) | 250 contacts; max 500 emails a month or 250 a day | Generative AI features are listed for Standard and Premium, not Free or Essentials |
| [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/pricing) | Up to 250 subscribers; 2,500 emails a month | Free has a limited subject line generator; the AI writing assistant is listed on the Comfort plan |
| [Brevo](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208580669-FAQs-What-are-the-limits-of-the-Free-plan) | 300 emails a day, no time limit | Not covered on the page checked |

What that means in practice, using simple arithmetic on those limits:

- On Mailchimp's free plan, 250 contacts and 500 emails a month means two emails a month to a full list. A weekly newsletter needs a paid plan or a smaller list.
- On MailerLite's free plan, 2,500 emails divided across 250 subscribers allows about 10 emails per person per month, which covers a weekly newsletter plus automations.
- Brevo's daily cap does not limit how many contacts you can hold. Per its help page, a campaign to more than 300 people must be re-sent over several days using the Requeue option, and you cannot schedule ahead once the day's limit is hit.

Mailchimp's page also shows Essentials from $13 a month and Standard from $20 a month, billed at those prices for the first 12 months under a promotion, with 14-day trials. The ongoing rate after the promotion was not shown, so check before you commit. Prices and tier names move, so use this table as a prompt to read the live page.

## Set Up the List Before You Touch AI

AI cannot fix a list that does not exist. Aim to collect addresses at the counter:

- A QR code on the till and on receipts that opens a one-field sign-up.
- A reason to join: a free pastry with the first drink, early access to seasonal menus, or loyalty points.
- Only what you need: email, first name, maybe birthday month.

## The Legal Minimum

This is not legal advice, but the official sources are short and worth reading.

- **US (CAN-SPAM):** the FTC's [compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) lists requirements including accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, clear identification as an ad, a valid physical postal address, an opt-out explanation and honouring opt-outs within 10 business days. It states penalties of up to $53,088 per violating email, and that hiring another company to send your email does not transfer your responsibility.
- **UK (PECR):** the ICO's [electronic mail marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/) says you need specific consent, or a qualifying "soft opt-in" for existing customers who were offered a simple opt-out when you collected their details. It does not cover prospects or bought-in lists, and every message needs an opt-out.
- **Australia and Canada** have their own anti-spam laws that were not reviewed for this article. Check your regulator's guidance before you email.
- **Gmail delivery:** Google's [sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) ask all senders to set up SPF or DKIM and keep spam complaint rates below 0.3% (it recommends staying under 0.10%). Bulk senders, meaning more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail accounts, also need DMARC and one-click unsubscribe. A cafe will not reach 5,000 a day, but the habits are good ones, and your email tool usually handles them if you connect your domain.

## Build Three Automations First

Automations run while you are on the machine, so they beat a newsletter for effort-to-return.

### 1. Welcome series

Three short emails over about a week: thanks plus the promised offer; your best-sellers and where to find you; a reminder to follow on Instagram or join the loyalty scheme. Ask AI to draft from bullets, then rewrite anything generic. See our welcome series guide.

### 2. Win-back

Send a "we saved your usual" note to people who have not opened or clicked in a long while. Keep it to three lines and one offer that costs you little, like a free pastry with a drink.
### 3. Birthday or loyalty milestone

A small treat in someone's birthday month is cheap and feels personal. It only needs the birth month on the sign-up form.

## A Prompt That Gets Usable Drafts

Give the AI four things and nothing more:

- **Who you are:** "a neighbourhood coffee shop, friendly and slightly cheeky."
- **What's new:** two or three bullets, such as a seasonal drink or changed opening hours.
- **Goal:** "get people in on Tuesday and Wednesday afternoons."
- **Rules:** "under 150 words, one call to action, no exaggerated claims, no emojis in the subject."

Ask for three subject lines and two bodies. Pick, edit, add a real photo.

## Segment Simply

You do not need dozens of segments. Four will do: new subscribers (welcome flow), regulars who open and click, lapsed contacts (win-back), and an interest group if you collect one, such as oat milk drinkers or event attendees. Our segmentation guide for coffee shops shows the setup.

## Measure the Right Things

Mailchimp's [benchmark page](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-marketing-benchmarks/) lists an all-users average of 35.63% opens and 2.62% clicks, with data last updated in December 2023. Its table does not include a restaurant or cafe category, and it warns that Apple's Mail Privacy Protection may affect the accuracy of open rates. So do not chase an industry open rate. Track instead:

- Clicks on your offer link.
- Redemptions at the till, with a code or a "show this email" rule.
- Unsubscribes and complaints, which tell you if you are sending too often.

## The Math

Take a list of 1,000 subscribers and four campaigns a month. Every row below is an **assumption** for illustration. Replace them with your own numbers after a month of data.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subscribers (assumption) | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| Campaigns per month (assumption) | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Extra visits per campaign, as a share of the list (assumption) | 0.5% | 1.5% | 3% |
| Extra visits per month | 20 | 60 | 120 |
| Spend per extra visit (assumption) | $8 | $8 | $8 |
| Gross margin on that spend (assumption) | 60% | 60% | 60% |
| Gross profit | $96 | $288 | $576 |
| Offer cost, $1.50 per visit (assumption) | $30 | $90 | $180 |
| Tool fee, roughly Mailchimp Standard's $20 promotional price | $20 | $20 | $20 |
| Your time: 3 hours at $20 (assumption) | $60 | $60 | $60 |
| **Net monthly result** | **-$14** | **+$118** | **+$316** |

Two things follow:

- **Break-even is about 25 extra visits a month.** Cost of $80 divided by $3.30 left per visit ($8 x 60% = $4.80, less the $1.50 offer) is about 24.2. If you cannot honestly say email brings in 25 visits who would not have come anyway, fix the offer before the tool.
- **The "extra" matters.** Many redeemers would have come anyway. Count only visits you can tie to the email, for example by asking "did the email bring you in?" at the till for a month.

Time is the hidden cost, and that is where AI helps: cutting a weekly newsletter from two hours to 45 minutes is worth more than any feature on the pricing page.

## Guardrails for Using AI

- **Never let it invent offers, prices or ingredients.** Check allergen details yourself.
- **Protect customer data.** Do not paste your subscriber list into a general chat tool. Use AI features inside your email platform or give only anonymous context.
- **Keep your voice.** Read it aloud before sending.
- **Do not overclaim.** "Best coffee in town" is a claim you cannot prove. Describe what is true: roast date, origin, today's special.

## Do This Today

1. Put a sign-up QR code at the till and offer one clear reason to join.
2. Pick a tool by checking its live free-plan limits against your list size and send plan. Write down how many emails a month you really need.
3. Build the welcome series first: three emails drafted with AI, edited by you, with the legal footer (physical address and unsubscribe) in place.
4. Add a redemption method, such as a code or "show this email", so you can count extra visits.
5. Block 45 minutes a week for the newsletter, and review clicks and redemptions at the end of the first month.

## FAQ

**Q: Is AI worth it for a cafe with 200 subscribers?**
A: Mostly for automation and drafting speed. A welcome series and a win-back email run in the background. At 200 contacts you can stay on a free plan, though Mailchimp's free plan only allows 500 emails a month, so check limits against how often you send.

**Q: How often should a coffee shop email?**
A: There is no official figure. A reasonable starting point, my rule of thumb, is two to four a month, and weekly only when there is something new. Watch unsubscribes and adjust.

**Q: Will AI-written emails land in spam?**
A: Mailbox providers publish rules about authentication, unsubscribe options and complaint rates, as Google's sender guidelines show. Those are about sending behaviour, not about whether AI wrote the text. Authenticate your domain and email only people who opted in.

**Q: Can I add SMS?**
A: Yes, for short, time-sensitive offers like a quiet-hour deal, but text marketing has its own consent rules in each country. Get explicit opt-in and check local law first.
