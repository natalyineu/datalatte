## What Are Bark and Thumbtack?

### Bark

Bark is a UK-founded lead marketplace that now operates in the US, Canada, Australia, Ireland, and other English-speaking markets. The model: customers post a job request, Bark sends it to multiple service providers in that category, and pros buy "credits" to respond.

**Key Bark characteristics:**
- Credits-based system — you pay to respond to requests, not per enquiry received
- Very wide category coverage: 1,000+ categories from cleaning and tutoring to plumbing and wedding photography
- Available in UK and international markets where Thumbtack doesn't operate
- Pro doesn't know job value before spending credits to respond

**Bark's biggest strength:** Breadth of categories and geographic coverage. If you're a pet groomer in Manchester, UK or a personal trainer in Toronto, Bark is likely your only option of the two.

**Bark's biggest weakness:** You pay before you know whether the job is worth pursuing. A request listed as "house cleaning" could be a studio flat or a 7-bedroom manor. Responding to both costs the same credits.

### Thumbtack

Thumbtack is a US-focused platform where customers describe their job and get matched with nearby pros. Unlike Bark's credits model, Thumbtack uses a direct pay-per-lead or subscription model, and the platform shows pros a cost estimate before they accept the lead.

**Key Thumbtack characteristics:**
- US-only (no UK or international coverage)
- Pay-per-lead or monthly subscription depending on your category and market
- Thumbtack shows estimated job value before you pay for the lead
- Stronger in home services: cleaning, plumbing, handyman, landscaping
- Thumbtack Pro subscription available for broader exposure

**Thumbtack's biggest strength:** Lead intent and transparency — customers come with a specific job in mind, and Thumbtack shows you the job value before you accept a lead.

**Thumbtack's biggest weakness:** Rising lead costs in competitive markets. In a saturated category like home cleaning or photography in a major US city, lead prices can creep toward $50–80 per lead.

## Pricing Models: How Each Platform Actually Charges You

This is where most comparisons oversimplify. Bark and Thumbtack don't charge the same way.

### How Bark Charges

Bark uses a **credits system**. You purchase a bundle of credits, and each response to a customer request costs a set number of credits depending on the category and job size.

**Bark credit pricing (approximate):**
- Credit bundles: $150–400 for 50–200 credits
- Cost per response: 2–15 credits depending on job type
- Effective cost per response: $3–30+ per request contact

**What you're paying for:** The right to send the customer a quote. You don't know if the customer will read it, respond, or book.

**Bark Pro subscription:** Bark also offers a monthly subscription ($50–100+/month) that gives you credits plus enhanced profile visibility. Worth it only if you're consistently profitable on the platform.

### How Thumbtack Charges

Thumbtack uses **direct lead pricing** — you see the price before you decide to contact the customer.

**Thumbtack lead pricing (typical ranges):**
- Home cleaning: $15–35 per lead
- Handyman/home repair: $20–50 per lead
- Personal training: $15–40 per lead
- Event photography: $25–60 per lead
- Lawn care: $10–30 per lead

**Thumbtack Pro:** A monthly subscription ($40–100+/month depending on market) that gives you broader profile exposure and the ability to have customers contact you directly (vs. you contacting them).

<Callout type="warning">
Hidden fees on Thumbtack: Thumbtack charges a "service fee" (10–15%) on top of the lead price when a job is booked through the platform. A $30 lead that converts to a $500 job means an additional $50–75 Thumbtack takes at booking. Factor this into your cost-per-job calculation.
</Callout>

## Lead Quality: What the Numbers Don't Show

Cost per lead is the obvious metric. Lead quality is harder to measure but matters more.

### What Affects Lead Quality on Bark

**The shared lead problem:** When a customer posts on Bark, their request typically goes to 5–6 pros simultaneously. You're competing on the phone before you've even had a conversation. Some customers post on Bark with no real intent — just gathering quotes they'll never act on.

**Response speed is everything:** Leads on Bark contacted within 5 minutes convert at 2–3x the rate of leads contacted after 30 minutes. The customer hasn't yet heard from the first responder — you can set the reference point for price and quality.

**Category matters:** Bark performs well in niche or specialist categories (dog grooming, music lessons, wedding hair and makeup) where the customer can't easily find a provider through Google. It underperforms in commoditised services (house cleaning, handyman) where price competition is brutal.

### What Affects Lead Quality on Thumbtack

**Intent is clearer:** Thumbtack customers have typically gone further in the decision process before posting. They've described their job in more detail, which tends to filter out "just browsing" contacts.

**Cancellation risk:** Thumbtack leads can cancel after you've paid for them, and while Thumbtack offers credits for certain cancellation types, the process isn't always smooth.

**Review ecosystem matters:** Thumbtack profiles with strong reviews convert at significantly higher rates. A pro with 50 reviews at 4.8 stars is being chosen before price in most categories. A new Thumbtack profile with no reviews starts with a meaningful disadvantage.

## Geography: The Biggest Deciding Factor

This is often the question that answers itself:

- **In the UK, Australia, Canada, or Ireland:** Use Bark. Thumbtack doesn't operate in these markets.
- **In the US (home services):** Thumbtack is typically stronger for plumbing, handyman, cleaning, landscaping.
- **In the US (specialist services — photography, tutoring, pet care, music):** Test both. Bark has broader category coverage even in US markets.
- **Multi-location service businesses:** Bark if you operate across international markets; Thumbtack if you're US-only.

## Setting Up Your Test: How to Get Useful Data

Running both platforms poorly tells you nothing. Here's how to run a test that produces real data:

**Step 1: Allocate equal budget**
Give each platform an equal budget for 60 days minimum — at least $200–300 each, ideally $500+. Seasonality can distort a 30-day test.

**Step 2: Create a tracking system**
Before you start spending, set up a simple spreadsheet:
- Date | Platform | Lead cost | Response time (mins) | Connected? | Quoted? | Booked? | Job value | Job completed?

**Step 3: Respond identically**
Use the same response template, the same qualification questions, the same pricing structure. You're testing the platform, not your sales process.

**Step 4: Calculate cost per closed job, not cost per lead**
At 60 days, divide total platform spend by number of completed jobs from that platform. That's your real metric.

**Step 5: Factor in service fees and credit wastage**
For Thumbtack: add the service fees from booked jobs to your platform cost. For Bark: note how many credits were spent on requests that didn't respond — this is your wastage rate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Bark or Thumbtack cheaper?

On a per-lead basis, Bark typically runs $10–40 versus Thumbtack's $15–50. But Thumbtack adds a 10–15% service fee at booking, which significantly raises the true cost for higher-ticket jobs. For jobs under $200, platforms are roughly comparable. For jobs over $500, Bark's no-service-fee model often wins on total cost per client acquired.

### Which platform has better lead quality?

"Quality" depends on your category and geography. Thumbtack leads show clearer intent (customers have described the job in more detail). Bark leads are more variable — some are highly serious, others are quote-gathering with no real intent. Response speed matters more than platform choice: a Bark lead contacted in under 5 minutes converts at similar rates to a Thumbtack lead.

### Can I use Bark and Thumbtack simultaneously?

Yes, and many successful service businesses do. Using both protects against one platform having a slow period and diversifies your lead sources. The key is tracking separately so you know which platform is performing.

### How many reviews do I need before Thumbtack works?

Getting 10+ reviews on Thumbtack (ideally 20+) before you expect consistent conversion is realistic. The platform's algorithm and customer behaviour both favour established profiles. If you're starting fresh, expect the first few months to be above-average cost while you accumulate reviews.

### Does Bark work in the US or is it mainly a UK platform?

Bark operates in both markets. Its US presence is growing but still weaker than Thumbtack in home services specifically. For specialist categories (photography, tutoring, music, pet care) Bark can perform well in the US. For home services (cleaning, plumbing, handyman) Thumbtack is typically stronger in the US.

## The Bottom Line

**Choose Bark if:** You're in the UK, Australia, or Canada; you're in a specialist or niche category; or you have high-ticket work where Thumbtack's service fees become painful.

**Choose Thumbtack if:** You're in the US in a home services category; you value lead transparency (seeing job value before paying); or you want a platform with a strong US home services review ecosystem.

**Choose both if:** Your budget allows it and you want real data rather than general advice.

Neither platform is a substitute for owning your own customer acquisition — which means Google Ads, a strong Google Business Profile, and SEO that brings customers to you without paying a platform per lead. Lead marketplaces fill gaps; they shouldn't be your only channel.
