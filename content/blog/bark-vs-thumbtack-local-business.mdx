Thumbtack is usually the better fit for US home services, while Bark is usually the better fit if you are in the UK, Canada, Australia or another market where Thumbtack does not operate, or if you sell a specialist service such as tutoring, photography or pet care. Both charge you for leads rather than for results, so the platform that is cheaper per lead is not always cheaper per customer. The only reliable way to choose is to track your cost per closed job on each for 60 days.

Pricing changes by category, location and demand, so treat any figure in this guide as a starting point and check current pricing inside each platform.

## What are Bark and Thumbtack?

### Bark

Bark is a UK-founded marketplace now operating in several English-speaking countries. Customers post a request, Bark sends it to relevant pros, and pros spend credits to respond.

- Credit-based: you pay to contact a customer, not per enquiry received
- Very broad category coverage, from cleaning and tutoring to photography
- Available in markets where Thumbtack is not
- You often decide to spend credits before knowing the exact job size

Strength: category and geographic breadth. Weakness: a request that looks attractive may be small, and responding costs the same credits either way. Several pros usually receive the same request, so speed matters.

### Thumbtack

Thumbtack is a US-focused marketplace. Customers describe a job and get matched with nearby pros, who typically pay per lead or per contact, with pricing that depends on category and area.

- Focused on the US
- Strong in home services such as cleaning, handyman, landscaping and repair
- Lead price is shown before you accept in many cases, which helps you decide
- Reviews and response time strongly influence who gets chosen

Strength: clear job descriptions and a large US customer base. Weakness: lead prices can rise in competitive categories and cities.

## How each platform charges

| | Bark | Thumbtack |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Credits you buy and spend to respond | Pay per lead or contact, depending on the category |
| What you pay for | The right to contact the customer | The lead or the introduction |
| Subscriptions | Optional paid plans exist | Optional paid features exist |
| Price visibility | Varies by request | Often shown up front |

Both platforms change their pricing and rules often, so confirm the current model, any minimums and any extra fees in your own account. Our deeper breakdown of [Thumbtack lead costs](/blog/thumbtack-leads-cost-what-you-pay-per-job-2026) covers what to look for.

## Cost comparison: use your own numbers

The cost that matters is **cost per closed job**, not cost per lead. Calculate it like this:

**Cost per closed job = total platform spend divided by jobs you actually completed from that platform**

To compare, record these for each platform:

| Metric | How to calculate |
|---|---|
| Cost per lead | Total spend divided by leads or responses |
| Contact rate | Leads where the customer replied divided by leads bought |
| Booking rate | Jobs booked divided by leads bought |
| Cost per booked job | Total spend divided by jobs booked |
| Revenue per job | Average invoice value |
| Return | Revenue divided by spend |

Here is an illustration with made-up round numbers, to show the logic and not to predict results. If you spend $300 on a platform and book 4 jobs, your cost per job is $75. If your average job is worth $250, you spend about 30 cents to earn each dollar of revenue. If your average job is worth $60, the same platform loses money.

That is why job value is the first thing to check. Low-ticket services need cheap leads and quick responses. High-ticket services can tolerate a higher cost per lead.

## Lead quality: what the numbers do not show

**Shared leads.** On both platforms, the same request often goes to several pros, so you compete before the conversation begins. Some customers are only gathering prices.

**Response speed.** Replying within minutes is widely considered one of the biggest levers on marketplace platforms, because customers often hire one of the first pros who answers.

**Category matters.** Specialist categories where customers cannot easily find a provider through Google can work well on Bark. Price-driven categories such as basic cleaning tend to be more competitive on both platforms.

**Reviews matter.** A profile with many strong reviews generally gets chosen more often than a new profile. If you are starting from scratch, expect early results to be costlier while you build reviews.

## How to run a fair 60-day test

1. **Set an equal budget** for each platform, enough to buy a meaningful number of leads. Avoid judging after a week or two, because seasonality distorts short tests.
2. **Track everything** in a simple sheet: date, platform, lead cost, response time, replied?, quoted?, booked?, job value, completed?
3. **Respond the same way** on both, with the same template and the same price structure, so you compare the platform and not your sales process.
4. **Judge by cost per closed job** after 60 days, including any fees, credits that went nowhere and refunds.
5. **Keep the winner, trim the loser,** and review again after a quarter.

## FAQ

**Is Bark or Thumbtack cheaper?**
It depends on your category, location and job value. Compare cost per closed job rather than cost per lead, and include any extra fees. Check current pricing in each platform before you spend.

**Which platform has better leads?**
Thumbtack leads often include clearer job descriptions in home services, while Bark leads vary more by category. In both cases, fast replies and strong reviews improve your results more than the platform choice alone.

**Can I use Bark and Thumbtack at the same time?**
Yes, many service businesses do. Track results separately so you know which platform is worth the spend.

**Does Thumbtack work in the UK?**
Thumbtack is focused on the US, so UK businesses typically use Bark, Checkatrade, Rated People or similar local platforms. Check each platform's current coverage.

**How many reviews do I need before these platforms work?**
There is no fixed number, but profiles with a solid base of recent, positive reviews usually convert better. Ask happy customers to leave a review straight after each job.

**Is it better to rely on Google than lead platforms?**
Owning your own traffic through Google Ads, a strong Google Business Profile and local SEO means you are not paying a platform for every lead. Marketplaces can fill gaps, but they should rarely be your only channel.
