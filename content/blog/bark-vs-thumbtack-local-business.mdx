Thumbtack is usually the better fit for US home services, while Bark is usually the better fit if you are in the UK, Canada, Australia or another market outside the US, or if you sell a specialist service such as tutoring, photography or pet care. Both charge you for leads rather than results, so the platform that is cheaper per lead is not always cheaper per customer. The only reliable way to choose is to track your cost per closed job on each for 60 days.

Here is how a bad week looks. A request pings your phone at 8:40 pm. You spend credits or pay for the lead. You send a quote. By morning the customer has picked someone else, or never answers at all. You paid either way. Nothing on either platform will tell you whether that week was normal. Only your own spreadsheet will.

Prices change by category, location and demand, so check current prices inside each platform before you spend.

## What Bark and Thumbtack are

### Bark

Bark is a services marketplace headquartered in London and founded in 2014. Wikipedia lists eleven markets: the UK, US, Australia, Canada, France, Germany, India, Ireland, New Zealand, Singapore and South Africa ([Wikipedia, Bark.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bark.com), checked Oct 2026). Customers post a request, Bark sends it to relevant pros, and pros spend credits to respond.

- Credit-based: you pay to contact a customer, not per enquiry received. Bark says each lead's credit cost is shown before you commit ([Bark Help Centre](https://help.bark.com/hc/en-us/articles/13346288068892-What-is-a-credit-and-how-much-does-it-cost), checked Oct 2026).
- Very broad categories, from cleaning and tutoring to photography.
- Credit prices vary by country and pack size, and credits are valid for 3 months from purchase.
- Several pros usually receive the same request, so speed matters.

Strength: breadth of categories and countries. Weakness: a request that looks attractive may be small, and responding costs the same credits either way.

### Thumbtack

Thumbtack is a US marketplace that describes its coverage in terms of professionals in every US county ([Thumbtack](https://www.thumbtack.com/)). Customers describe a job and get matched with pros. You pay for leads, meaning new customers who reach out to you, and prices vary with the type and size of the job, how many pros are available and your market ([Thumbtack Help](https://help.thumbtack.com/article/pay-for-leads), via search result, checked Oct 2026). You can set maximum lead prices and a weekly budget for direct leads.

- Strong in home services such as cleaning, handyman, landscaping and repair.
- No fixed published rate, so expect a small repair to price differently from a large project.
- Reviews and response time strongly influence who gets chosen.

Strength: clear job descriptions and a large US customer base. Weakness: lead prices can rise in competitive categories and cities, and a paid lead does not guarantee a job.

## How each platform charges

| | Bark | Thumbtack |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Credits you buy and spend to respond | Pay per lead, depending on category and market |
| What you pay for | The right to contact the customer | A new customer reaching out to you |
| Cost control | Credit cost shown per lead | Maximum lead price and weekly budget settings |
| Refunds | Credit returns for leads that do not meet quality standards, requested within 14 days; cash refunds only in limited cases | Check the refund rules in the Thumbtack Help Center before relying on them |

Both change pricing and rules often, so confirm the current model, minimums and fees in your own account. Our [Thumbtack lead costs guide](/blog/thumbtack-leads-cost-what-you-pay-per-job-2026) covers what to look for.

<Callout type="warning">Ask what happens when a lead is fake, a duplicate or cancelled. Bark lists a 14-day window for credit return requests, and Thumbtack's rules are in its help center. Keep screenshots and ask promptly.</Callout>

## The math: cost per closed job

Cost per closed job = total platform spend / jobs you completed from that platform. Cost per lead is a trap, because it ignores what happens after you pay.

**Example only: every figure is invented to show the arithmetic, not a real result from either platform.** Assume you spend $300 on each for 60 days, and the average job is worth $250.

| | Platform A | Platform B |
|---|---|---|
| Spend | $300 | $300 |
| Leads bought | 20 (so $15 each) | 8 (so $37.50 each) |
| Customers who replied | 8 | 5 |
| Jobs closed | 2 | 3 |
| Cost per closed job | $300 / 2 = $150 | $300 / 3 = $100 |
| Revenue (jobs x $250) | $500 | $750 |
| Revenue per $1 spent | $1.67 | $2.50 |

Platform A has the cheaper lead and the worse result. That is the whole point. Now change the job value. If your average job is $60, Platform B earns $180 on $300 of spend and loses money. Low-ticket services need cheap leads and quick replies. High-ticket services can afford a pricier lead.

## Lead quality: what the numbers hide

- **Shared leads.** The same request can go to several pros on both platforms, so you compete before the conversation begins. Some customers are only gathering prices.
- **Response speed.** You control this one. Aim to reply within minutes, then compare your close rate on fast replies against slow ones in your own sheet.
- **Category.** Specialist categories where customers cannot easily find a provider through Google can work well on Bark. Price-driven categories such as basic cleaning tend to be competitive on both.
- **Reviews.** A profile with many strong reviews generally gets chosen more often than a new one, so expect early results to cost more while you build reviews.

## Do this today: a 60-day test

1. Pick a budget you can lose, and give each platform the same amount.
2. Open a sheet with these columns: date, platform, lead cost, minutes to reply, replied, quoted, booked, job value, completed.
3. Use the same reply template and pricing on both, so you compare platforms, not your sales skills.
4. Reply to every lead within 15 minutes for the first two weeks. Note the ones you could not.
5. On day 60, divide spend by closed jobs for each platform, including credits that went nowhere and refunds you got back.
6. Keep the winner, trim the loser, and review again after a quarter.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Bark or Thumbtack cheaper?**
A: It depends on your category, location and job value. Bark credit prices vary by country and pack size, and Thumbtack lead prices vary by job, market and competition. Compare cost per closed job rather than cost per lead.

**Q: Which platform has better leads?**
A: It varies by category. In both cases, fast replies and strong reviews improve your results more than the platform choice alone, and the same request may go to several pros.

**Q: Can I use Bark and Thumbtack at the same time?**
A: Yes, many service businesses do. Track results separately so you know which platform is worth the spend.

**Q: Does Thumbtack work in the UK?**
A: Thumbtack describes its coverage in terms of US counties, so UK businesses typically look at Bark, Checkatrade, Rated People or similar local platforms. Check each platform's current coverage before you register.

**Q: Can I get my money back for a bad Bark lead?**
A: Bark lets you request a credit return for leads that do not meet its quality standards, within 14 days of purchase. Cash refunds are only given in limited cases at Bark's discretion.

**Q: Is it better to rely on Google than lead platforms?**
A: Owning your traffic through Google Ads, a strong Google Business Profile and local SEO means you are not paying a platform for every lead. Marketplaces can fill gaps, but they should rarely be your only channel.
