To measure local SEO success, track customer actions as well as rankings. Count calls, direction requests, website clicks and bookings from your Google Business Profile, plus review count and average rating. Connect those profile actions to website results in Google Analytics and Search Console, then compare a short monthly scorecard with the month before. No public source gives a standard target for these numbers, so your own trend is the benchmark. The worked example below uses assumed salon figures to show how to read one month.

## Which local SEO KPIs should a small business track?

Start with four groups of measures. Profile actions include calls, direction requests, website clicks and bookings, and they show whether people who found your listing did something next. Visibility covers how often your profile appears and how many people view it. Reputation covers review count, average rating and new reviews each month. Website outcomes cover form submissions and taps on your phone number.

Keep the list short. Four or five KPIs are easier to read each month than fifteen. Only track a metric if you can name the action you would take when it moves, because a number that cannot change a decision adds noise to the scorecard.

| KPI group | Example measure | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profile actions | Calls, directions, bookings | Shows customer intent from the listing |
| Visibility | Profile views and searches | Shows how often you are found |
| Reputation | New reviews, average rating | Shows trust signals customers see |
| Website | Form submissions, phone taps | Shows what visitors do on your site |

Choose one measure from each group to start. Add more only after the first month has been tracked consistently.

## How do you connect profile actions to website results?

Google Analytics 4 records actions on your site as events. Google's guidance lists recommended events, including generate_lead, which covers a user who submits a form or a request for information. These events are not sent automatically, so you must add them to your website before they appear in reports. [S4]

For a small business, the simplest setup is to mark two events as key events: a contact form submission and a tap on your phone number. You can then see how many visitors completed each one and which channel brought them. Offline bookings, such as a phone call that becomes a job, still need a manual count at the point of sale.

## How long should you wait before judging a change?

Changes to a website or profile do not show results at once. Google's SEO guidance says some changes can take effect within hours, while others can take several months. It suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change had a beneficial effect. [S1]

This matters for the scorecard. Log each new photo set or category fix in the note column, then read it against the next two or three months rather than the next week. Not every change will show up in search results, so judge each one against the actions it was meant to improve.

## Do reviews belong in the scorecard?

Yes, because reviews are a measurable part of how your business appears to customers. Google's guidelines for business profiles allow owners to post their own contact details in responses to reviews, but they do not allow soliciting personal or confidential information. [S3] Keep that rule in mind when you write replies or ask customers for feedback.

Track the count of new reviews each month, the average rating and the number of replies you have posted. Those three numbers show whether reputation is growing. They do not prove a ranking effect on their own, so read them alongside profile actions.

## Should you track rankings?

Rankings are a diagnostic, not the goal. Google's guidance says no site is guaranteed a place in search results, but sites that follow its Search Essentials are more likely to appear. [S1] Improving the basics of your listing and website is the practical lever, and rankings are one way to check whether that work is being seen.

A single rank check from your office can mislead, because results differ by where the searcher is. Use two or three checks from nearby streets, or rely on profile views and searches as a proxy. If rankings improve while calls stay flat, the problem is usually the profile content, the reviews or the offer rather than visibility.

## How do you build a monthly scorecard?

Use one row per month with these columns: profile views, searches, calls, directions, website clicks, bookings, new reviews, average rating and new customers who said they found you on Google. Ask that last question at booking or checkout, and record the answer in the same row.

Add a note column for the changes you made that month. Businesses that book through software should take the booking count from the scheduling tool, not from memory. ## Worked example: reading one month of numbers

All figures below are **assumptions** for a hypothetical salon. They are not benchmarks, and your own baseline should replace them.

| Metric (assumption) | Last month | This month | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Profile views | 1,200 | 1,350 | +12.5% |
| Calls | 40 | 52 | +30% |
| Directions | 25 | 27 | +8% |
| Website clicks | 90 | 85 | -5.6% |
| New reviews | 4 | 9 | +5 |

Profile views rose 12.5% and calls rose 30%, so more people who saw the listing acted on it. Website clicks dipped by five, which may be normal variation at that volume. The next step is to check what changed, such as the nine new reviews, and repeat it if the pattern holds. Do not draw a conclusion from one month.

## What steps should you follow each month?

Work through the same steps each month so the numbers stay comparable:

1. Record the five core numbers in the scorecard row.
2. Note every profile, website and review change made during the month.
3. Compare each metric with the previous month and look closer at any move you consider large.
4. Reply to new reviews and log the reply count.
5. Pick one change to test, then judge it after two or three months.

## FAQ

### What is a good local SEO KPI?

Calls, directions and bookings from your profile are the most useful because they are customer actions. Google's guidance does not give a target number, so compare each month with your own baseline. [S1]

### How often should I check these numbers?

Review the scorecard monthly, since changes take time to show results. Check new reviews weekly so you can reply promptly, which Google's guidelines allow for your own business contact details. [S3]

### Do reviews affect rankings?

This guide does not claim a ranking effect from reviews. Treat review count and rating as trust and reputation measures, and read them alongside profile actions.

### Can I measure local SEO without Google Analytics?

Yes, to a degree. Profile actions and Search Console data cover the main search activity. Google Analytics adds what visitors do on your site, through recommended events you set up yourself. [S4]
