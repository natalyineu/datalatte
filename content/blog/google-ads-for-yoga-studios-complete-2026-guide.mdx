Google Ads can fill yoga classes by showing your studio to people searching "yoga near me", "beginner yoga classes [city]" or "hot yoga [neighborhood]" at the moment they are ready to book. A sensible first test is one Search campaign, a radius around your studio sized to how far students will travel, an intro-offer landing page, and conversion tracking for calls and bookings. Fund it for at least two months, set the budget from what a new student is worth, and judge it by cost per sign-up.

Here is why a basic profile is not enough. In the 113 fitness studios we pulled from Google Maps in Denver in October 2026, the median studio had 72 reviews, and 44 of them had fewer than 50. Most of the competition is thin. A new student who searches on her phone at lunch picks from the first few results, and a studio with a sharp ad and a clear intro offer beats a studio with a nice logo.

There is no universal yoga click price. Use Keyword Planner for estimates in your area, then your own account data. Test small, track sign-ups, scale what works.

## The Math: What $600 a Month Can Buy

**Example with made-up inputs. These are not market benchmarks. Swap in your own numbers.** Assume a $600 monthly budget, a click price, the share of clicks that sign up for the intro offer, 40% of intro students joining, and a member paying $120 a month who stays 6 months (lifetime value $720).

| | A: base case | B: clicks cost double | C: weak landing page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per click (assumed) | $3 | $6 | $3 |
| Clicks ($600 / cost) | 200 | 100 | 200 |
| Click-to-intro sign-up rate (assumed) | 5% | 5% | 2% |
| Intro sign-ups | 10 | 5 | 4 |
| New members (40%) | 4 | 2 | 1.6 |
| Cost per new member | $150 | $300 | $375 |
| Lifetime value of those members | $2,880 | $1,440 | $1,152 |

All three still beat $600 of spend in this example, which is the point: the break-even is about one member (600 / 720 = 0.83). The danger is not click price. It is the sign-up rate. Scenario C costs the same media money as A and returns 60% fewer members. Fix the page before you raise the budget. If your own lifetime value is $240, even scenario A (4 x $240 = $960) is thin, so do your own multiplication first.

## Why Yoga Studios Use Google Ads

Ads work best alongside:

- A complete Google Business Profile (hours, photos, class types, reviews).
- A landing page that makes booking an intro class easy.
- Tracking that counts calls, form fills and booking-software sign-ups.

Ads send people to your profile and your website, so run our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) first.

## How to Set Up the Campaign

### Step 1: Choose Search

Start with a **Search campaign**, which shows your ad when someone types a relevant query. Consider Performance Max later, once you have conversion data and good landing pages.

### Step 2: Define your area and schedule

Google Ads lets you target a radius around a location ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038), checked Oct 2026). Tighter in a dense city, wider in a rural area. Google's default setting reaches people in, regularly in, or showing interest in your target area; the "Presence" option limits this to people in or regularly in it, which suits a studio that serves only local residents. Check when calls and bookings arrive and weight budget toward those hours.

### Step 3: Build keyword groups

Group by theme: beginner classes, hot yoga, prenatal yoga, yoga for back pain. Examples:

- "beginner yoga classes [city]"
- "yoga near me"
- "hot yoga [neighborhood]"
- "prenatal yoga [city]"
- "yoga for back pain [city]"
- "yoga studio intro offer"

Start with phrase and exact match. More ideas in [best Google Ads keywords for yoga studios](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-yoga-studios-in-2026).

### Step 4: Add negative keywords

Negative keywords exclude searches you do not want to pay for ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972), checked Oct 2026). Starters: "yoga pants", "yoga mat", "yoga teacher training" (unless you offer it), "yoga jobs", "free yoga videos", "yoga poses pdf", and "yoga retreat" (if you do not run them). Read the Search terms report weekly to find more ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708), checked Oct 2026).

## Write Ads a First-Timer Answers

Lead with an offer, a location and easy booking.

- "Beginner Yoga in [City] | First Class Free" (only if you offer it)
- "Book an Intro Class Online"
- "All Levels Welcome. Small Classes."
- "Yoga for Back Pain and Stress Relief"

Answer the hesitations: "No experience needed", "Mats provided", "Cancel anytime". Add a call asset so mobile searchers can phone from the ad ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453991), checked Oct 2026). Claim only the reviews and awards you can show.

## Budget: Work Backward

I could not find a reliable benchmark for yoga-studio ad budgets, so use your numbers:

1. Lifetime value of a new student = monthly price x months they typically stay.
2. The most you can pay to win one student and stay profitable.
3. Monthly budget divided by that number = the new students you need to break even.

Check Keyword Planner for click costs, set a daily budget that buys enough clicks to learn from, and run it at least 60 days. If the numbers fail at your current sign-up rate, fix the page and offer first. Our [yoga studio budget guide](/blog/how-much-should-a-yoga-studio-spend-on-google-ads) goes deeper.

## Landing Page Basics

Send clicks to a page that matches the search, not the homepage. Include:

- A headline for the offer (intro class, trial week, beginner series).
- Schedule, price, address and phone number.
- A one-tap booking button above the fold on mobile.
- A few real photos and reviews.
- A fast load on a phone.

## Track Results

Track phone calls, booking-software sign-ups or intro-class purchases, and contact forms. Watch cost per sign-up, click-through rate, and which keywords and hours produce students. Use call tracking to separate ad leads from organic ones; see [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).

## Seasons

Demand for classes often rises around New Year and in spring and can dip in summer in some markets. Check Google Trends and your own booking history, then raise budget a few weeks before the peak with an offer such as a January beginner series.

## Local Services Ads: A Possible Extra

In the United States, Google lists "Yoga studio" among the business types supported by Local Services Ads, which are pay-per-lead ads on Google Search and Maps. Availability depends on your country and area, and Google is moving Local Services Ads into Performance Max in stages through 2027, so check eligibility in Google Ads before planning around them ([Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841), [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585), checked Oct 2026).

## Common Mistakes

1. **Sending traffic to the homepage.** Use a focused landing page.
2. **No negative keywords.** Wasted clicks add up fast on a small budget.
3. **Ignoring mobile.** Test your ad and page on a phone.
4. **No call tracking.** Many students call before they book.
5. **Changing too much too soon.** Wait a few weeks of data and change one thing at a time.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a yoga studio spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no fixed figure. Work backward from what a new student is worth, check Keyword Planner for click costs in your area, and run a test you can sustain for about 60 days before adjusting.

**Q: Should a yoga studio bid on its own name?**
A: Often yes, because brand clicks are usually cheap and stop competitors capturing people who already know you. If you already rank first organically and no competitor bids on your name, you can test pausing it.

**Q: Are Google Ads or Facebook and Instagram ads better for yoga studios?**
A: Google Ads capture people already searching for a class, which suits sign-ups. Meta Ads suit awareness, workshops and retargeting. Many studios start with Google and add Meta once tracking is in place. See our [Meta Ads service](/services/meta-ads).

**Q: How long until Google Ads start working for a yoga studio?**
A: You may see enquiries in the first couple of weeks, but expect 30 to 60 days of data before optimizing and 60 to 90 days before judging results.

**Q: Do I need a website or can I use a booking link?**
A: A simple landing page beats a raw booking link or homepage. It should show schedule, price, location, phone and a booking button. A [studio-friendly website](/services/website) does not have to be elaborate.
