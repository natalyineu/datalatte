Google Ads can fill yoga classes by showing your studio to people searching "yoga near me", "beginner yoga classes [city]" or "hot yoga [neighborhood]" at the moment they are ready to book. A sensible first test is one Search campaign, a radius around your studio sized to how far students will travel, an intro-offer landing page, and conversion tracking for calls and bookings. Set a budget you can sustain for at least two months, based on what a new student is worth to you, and judge it by cost per sign-up.

There is no universal budget or click price for yoga. Costs vary by city, season and competition, so use Keyword Planner for estimated click costs in your area and your own account data once ads run. The goal is to test small, track sign-ups and scale what works.

## Why yoga studios use Google Ads

People looking for a class usually search on their phone and choose among the first few options. If your studio is not there, the searcher books with a competitor. Ads work best alongside:

- A complete Google Business Profile (hours, photos, class types, reviews).
- A landing page that makes booking an intro class easy.
- Tracking that counts calls, form fills and booking-software sign-ups.

See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) before you spend on ads, since ads send people to that profile and your website.

## How to set up your campaign

### Step 1: Choose the campaign type

Start with a **Search campaign**, which shows your ad when someone types a relevant query. Consider Performance Max later, once you have conversion data and good landing pages.

### Step 2: Define your area and schedule

Google Ads lets you target a radius around a location ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038), checked Oct 2026). Pick a radius that matches how far students will realistically travel: tighter in a dense city, wider in a rural area. Google's default setting reaches people in, regularly in, or showing interest in your target area; the "Presence" option limits this to people in or regularly in it, which can suit a studio that serves only local residents. Look at when calls and bookings arrive and weight your budget toward those hours.

### Step 3: Build keyword groups

Group keywords by theme: beginner classes, hot yoga, prenatal yoga, yoga for back pain. Examples:

- "beginner yoga classes [city]"
- "yoga near me"
- "hot yoga [neighborhood]"
- "prenatal yoga [city]"
- "yoga for back pain [city]"
- "yoga studio intro offer"

Start with phrase and exact match. Our list of [best Google Ads keywords for yoga studios](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-yoga-studios-in-2026) has more ideas.

### Step 4: Add negative keywords

Negative keywords exclude searches you do not want to pay for ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972), checked Oct 2026). Good starters: "yoga pants", "yoga mat", "yoga teacher training" (unless you offer it), "yoga jobs", "free yoga videos", "yoga poses pdf", and "yoga retreat" (if you do not run them). Review the Search terms report weekly to find more ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708), checked Oct 2026).

## Write ads that convert

Lead with what a first-time student cares about: a clear offer, location and easy booking.

- "Beginner Yoga in [City] | First Class Free" (only if you offer it)
- "Book an Intro Class Online"
- "All Levels Welcome. Small Classes."
- "Yoga for Back Pain and Stress Relief"

Answer common hesitations in the copy: "No experience needed", "Mats provided", "Cancel anytime". Add a call asset so mobile searchers can phone you straight from the ad ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453991), checked Oct 2026). Only claim reviews or awards you can show.

## Budget: how much should you spend?

We could not find a reliable benchmark for yoga-studio ad budgets, so work from your own numbers instead:

1. Estimate the lifetime value of a new student (monthly price multiplied by the months they typically stay).
2. Decide the most you can pay to acquire one student and stay profitable.
3. Divide your monthly budget by that number to see how many new students you need to break even.

Check Keyword Planner for estimated click costs in your area and set a daily budget that can buy enough clicks to learn from. Run the test for at least 60 days before deciding. If the numbers do not work at your current conversion rate, fix the landing page and offer before increasing spend. Our [yoga studio budget guide](/blog/how-much-should-a-yoga-studio-spend-on-google-ads) goes deeper.

## Landing page basics

Send ad clicks to a page that matches the search, not a generic homepage. A good yoga landing page includes:

- A clear headline for the offer (intro class, trial week, beginner series).
- Class schedule, price, address and phone number.
- A one-tap booking button above the fold on mobile.
- A few real photos and reviews.
- Fast load time on a phone.

## Track results

Set up conversion tracking for phone calls, booking-software sign-ups or intro-class purchases, and contact forms. Watch cost per sign-up, click-through rate, and which keywords and times produce students. Use call tracking so you can separate ad leads from organic ones; see our guide to [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).

## Seasonal opportunities

Demand for classes often rises around New Year and in spring, and can dip in summer or holidays in some markets. Check Google Trends and your own booking history to confirm your pattern, then raise budget a few weeks before expected peaks with an offer such as a January beginner series.

## Local Services Ads: a possible extra channel

In the United States, Google lists "Yoga studio" among the business types supported by Local Services Ads, which are pay-per-lead ads on Google Search and Maps. Availability depends on your country and area, and Google is moving Local Services Ads into Performance Max in stages through 2027, so check your eligibility in Google Ads before planning around them ([Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841), [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585), checked Oct 2026).

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Sending traffic to the homepage.** Use a focused landing page.
2. **No negative keywords.** Wasted clicks add up quickly on a small budget.
3. **Ignoring mobile.** Test your ad and page on a phone.
4. **No call tracking.** Many students call before they book.
5. **Changing too much too soon.** Wait for a few weeks of data and change one thing at a time.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a yoga studio spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no fixed figure. Work backward from what a new student is worth, check Keyword Planner for click costs in your area, and run a test you can sustain for about 60 days before adjusting.

**Q: Should a yoga studio bid on its own name?**
A: Often yes, because brand clicks are usually cheap and stop competitors from capturing people who already know you. If you already rank first organically and no competitor bids on your name, you can test pausing it.

**Q: Are Google Ads or Facebook and Instagram ads better for yoga studios?**
A: Google Ads capture people already searching for a class, which suits sign-ups. Meta Ads suit awareness, workshops and retargeting. Many studios start with Google and add Meta once tracking is in place. See our [Meta Ads service](/services/meta-ads).

**Q: How long until Google Ads start working for a yoga studio?**
A: You may see enquiries within the first couple of weeks, but expect to gather about 30 to 60 days of data before optimizing and 60 to 90 days before judging results.

**Q: Do I need a website or can I use a booking link?**
A: A simple landing page works better than a raw booking link or homepage. It should show schedule, price, location, phone and a booking button. A [studio-friendly website](/services/website) does not have to be elaborate.
