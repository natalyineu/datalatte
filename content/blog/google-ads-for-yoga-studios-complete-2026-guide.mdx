Google Ads can fill yoga classes by showing your studio to people searching "yoga near me", "beginner yoga classes [city]" or "hot yoga [neighborhood]" right when they are ready to book. A typical single-location studio can test with roughly $500 to $800 a month, a tight 3 to 10 mile radius, a free-trial or intro-offer landing page, and conversion tracking for calls and bookings.

Costs, click prices and results vary by city and season, so treat the figures in this guide as starting points and check Google Keyword Planner for your own market. The goal is to test small, track sign-ups and scale what works.

## Why Yoga Studios Use Google Ads

People looking for a yoga class usually search on their phone and pick from the first few options they see. Ads and the local map listings sit at the top of those results. If your studio is not there, the searcher books with a competitor.

Google Ads work best for yoga studios when you combine them with:

- A complete Google Business Profile (hours, photos, class types, reviews).
- A landing page that makes it easy to book an intro class.
- Tracking that counts calls, form fills and booking-software sign-ups.

See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) before you spend on ads, since ads send people to that profile and your website.

## How to Set Up Your Campaign (Step by Step)

### Step 1: Choose the right campaign type

Start with **Search campaigns**. They show your ad when someone types a relevant query. Consider Performance Max later, once you have conversion data and quality landing pages.

### Step 2: Define your area and schedule

Target a radius around your studio that matches how far students will realistically travel, often 3 to 10 miles in a city and larger in rural areas. Look at when your phone rings and bookings arrive, then weight your budget toward those hours.

### Step 3: Build keyword groups

Group keywords by theme, such as beginner classes, hot yoga, prenatal yoga or yoga for back pain. Examples:

- "beginner yoga classes [city]"
- "yoga near me"
- "hot yoga [neighborhood]"
- "prenatal yoga [city]"
- "yoga for back pain [city]"
- "yoga studio intro offer"

Use phrase and exact match to start. Our list of [best Google Ads keywords for yoga studios](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-yoga-studios-in-2026) has more ideas.

### Step 4: Add negative keywords

Block searches that will not become students: "yoga pants", "yoga mat", "yoga teacher training" (unless you offer it), "yoga jobs", "free yoga videos", "yoga poses pdf", "yoga retreat" (if you do not run them).

## Write Ads That Convert

Lead with what a first-time student cares about: a clear offer, location and easy booking. Ideas:

- "Beginner Yoga in [City] | First Class Free" (only if you offer it)
- "Book an Intro Class Online"
- "All Levels Welcome. Small Classes."
- "Yoga for Back Pain and Stress Relief"

Answer common hesitations in the copy: "No experience needed", "Mats provided", "Cancel anytime". Use call, location and sitelink extensions so mobile searchers can call or get directions in one tap. Only claim reviews or awards you can show.

## Budget: How Much Should You Spend?

Many single-location studios test with about $500 to $800 per month, which is roughly $15 to $25 per day. Large, competitive cities may need more to show up consistently, while small towns may do fine with less. Run the test for at least 60 days before deciding.

A quick way to sanity-check your budget:

1. Estimate the lifetime value of a new student (monthly price multiplied by the number of months they typically stay).
2. Decide the most you can pay to acquire one student while staying profitable.
3. Divide your monthly budget by that number to see how many new students you need to break even.

If numbers do not work at your current conversion rate, fix the landing page and offer before increasing spend.

## Landing Page Basics

Send ad clicks to a page that matches the search, not a generic homepage. A good yoga landing page includes:

- A clear headline for the offer (intro class, trial week, beginner series).
- Class schedule, price, address and a phone number.
- A one-tap booking button above the fold on mobile.
- A few real photos and reviews.
- Fast load time on a phone.

## Track Results

Set up conversion tracking for:

- Phone calls from ads and from your website.
- Booking-software sign-ups or intro-class purchases.
- Contact form submissions.

Useful metrics to watch: cost per booking or sign-up, click-through rate, and which keywords and times produce students. Use UTM parameters or call tracking so you can separate ad leads from organic ones. See our guide to [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).

## Seasonal Opportunities

Demand often rises around New Year and in late spring, and can dip in summer or holidays in some markets. Increase budget a few weeks before expected peaks, and prepare offers such as a January beginner series. Check your own search trends in Google Trends and your booking history to confirm your pattern.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Sending traffic to the homepage.** Use a focused landing page.
2. **No negative keywords.** Wasted clicks add up quickly on a small budget.
3. **Ignoring mobile.** Most local searches happen on phones, so test your ad and page there.
4. **No call tracking.** Many students call before they book.
5. **Changing too much too soon.** Wait for a few weeks of data and change one thing at a time.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a yoga studio spend on Google Ads?**
A: Many single-location studios test with about $500 to $800 a month and adjust after 60 days. Higher-cost cities may need more. Our [yoga studio budget guide](/blog/how-much-should-a-yoga-studio-spend-on-google-ads) goes deeper.

**Q: Should a yoga studio bid on its own name?**
A: Often yes, because brand clicks are usually cheap and stop competitors from capturing people who already know you. If you already rank first organically and no competitors bid on your name, you can test pausing it.

**Q: Are Google Ads or Facebook and Instagram ads better for yoga studios?**
A: Google Ads capture people who are already searching for a class, which suits sign-ups. Meta Ads are better for awareness, workshops and retargeting. Many studios start with Google and add Meta once tracking is in place. See our [Meta Ads service](/services/meta-ads).

**Q: How long until Google Ads start working for a yoga studio?**
A: You may see inquiries within the first couple of weeks, but expect about 30 to 60 days of data before optimizing, and 60 to 90 days to judge results.

**Q: Do I need a website or can I use a booking link?**
A: A simple landing page works much better than a raw booking link or homepage. It should show schedule, price, location, phone and a booking button. A [studio-friendly website](/services/website) does not have to be elaborate.
