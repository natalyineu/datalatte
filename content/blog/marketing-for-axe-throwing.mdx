The best way to market an axe throwing venue is to sell the experience as a group event: build strong Google Business Profile and local search presence for "things to do" and party searches, make booking effortless, and use short videos of real customers landing their first bullseye to drive social shares. Social content helps, but virality is never guaranteed, so a steady plan beats chasing one hit.

Axe throwing is naturally visual and social. People come in groups for birthdays, team events, bachelor and bachelorette parties and date nights, and they love filming it. That gives you plenty of raw material. Below is how to turn it into bookings, without gimmicks or promises of overnight fame.

## Understand who actually books

Think in terms of the decision-maker, not just the thrower. Typical bookers include:

- **Friends organising a night out or birthday**, who compare "things to do" options and read reviews.
- **Corporate team organisers**, who care about capacity, safety, food and drink options and invoicing.
- **Stag, hen, bachelor and bachelorette organisers**, who want packages.
- **Couples and small groups**, who often book last minute.
- **Locals looking for league nights or repeat visits.**

Each group searches and decides differently, so your content and offers should reflect that.

## Step 1: Win the local search basics

Before posting a single reel, make sure people can find and trust you.

1. **Google Business Profile:** choose the most accurate primary category available (for example, an amusement or recreation category that fits your region). List your services, opening hours, booking link and attributes. Add plenty of real photos and short video clips. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) walks through each field.
2. **Reviews:** ask every group, ideally the organiser, for a review soon after the visit while the mood is high. Reply to every review.
3. **Website:** one clear page for each main offer: walk-ins, private parties, corporate events, leagues, gift vouchers. State capacity, session length, age limits, safety briefing and pricing approach.
4. **Directories:** keep details consistent on Google, Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook, TripAdvisor and local event listings.

Make sure prices and policies are accurate everywhere, including cancellation and age rules.

## Step 2: Make booking effortless

Group bookings die at friction points. Check:

- Can someone book for 8 people at 7 pm on Saturday in under two minutes on a phone?
- Is availability live, or do they have to wait for an email?
- Can they pay a deposit online?
- Are cancellation and waiver processes clear?
- Is there an obvious route for corporate or large-group enquiries?

Use a booking system that fits your venue, such as Bookeo, Checkfront, FareHarbor, Peek or Square Appointments (features and pricing vary). Whatever you choose, link directly to it from your profile, site and social bios.

## Step 3: Create social content that is worth watching

Social platforms reward content people actually watch and share. For an axe throwing venue, strong ideas include:

- **First-throw reactions:** short clips of beginners landing (or missing) their first throw, with the group's permission.
- **Coach tips:** a 20-second "how to hold the axe" or "why it keeps bouncing off" video.
- **Trick-shot and challenge clips:** staff trying tough throws or friendly competitions.
- **Behind the scenes:** target maintenance, lane set-up, how you keep things safe.
- **Event spotlights:** a birthday setup or corporate group (with consent).
- **Leaderboard and league updates:** encourages repeat visits.
- **Local collaborations:** a nearby brewery, food truck or hotel.

Keep clips vertical, short and captioned, since many people watch with sound off. Make the first second count. ### Consent and safety matter

- Get clear permission before posting identifiable people, and be especially careful with minors.
- Show safe behaviour, staff supervision and proper technique, because your content also signals how seriously you take safety.
- Do not encourage alcohol-related stunts or unsafe throwing. If you serve alcohol, follow your licence conditions and local regulations on promotion.

## Step 4: Encourage customers to make content for you

User-generated content is often more convincing than a polished ad.

- Create a photo-friendly spot: a branded wall, neon sign or "wall of fame" with names or scores.
- Put a simple hashtag and your handle on signs and scoreboards.
- Ask groups to tag you and share their clips.
- Repost with permission, crediting the creator.
- Offer a small, optional perk for tagging, such as entry into a monthly prize draw, while following each platform's promotion rules.

## Step 5: Use paid ads selectively

Organic reach on social platforms is unpredictable, so some venues add modest paid support.

- **Google Ads:** search ads for high-intent terms such as "axe throwing near me", "team building activity [city]" and "birthday party ideas [city]". Track bookings, not just clicks. See our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business).
- **Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram):** target people within a sensible radius with video of real sessions. Promote private-event packages and gift vouchers, particularly ahead of holidays. Our [Facebook ads for event promotion](/blog/facebook-ads-for-event-promotion) article gives a framework.
- **Retargeting:** show reminders to people who visited your booking page but did not complete it.

Start small, test two or three creatives, and judge results over a few weeks. Costs vary widely by city and season.

## Step 6: Sell packages, not just throws

Packages make decisions easier for organisers. Typical structures include:

| Package type | Who it suits | What to include |
|---|---|---|
| Walk-in or hourly lane | Casual groups, date nights | Time, coaching, number of throwers per lane |
| Birthday or party | Friends and families | Private lane, host or coach, optional food or drink |
| Corporate team event | Offices, clubs | Tournament format, catering options, invoicing |
| Bachelor or bachelorette | Social groups | Group tournament, photo options, extras |
| Gift voucher | Gift buyers | Flexible value, easy online purchase |
| League or membership | Regulars | Weekly schedule, scoring, standings |

Keep pricing transparent and show what is included. A "request a quote" form works for large groups, but avoid hiding all pricing.

## Step 7: Work the corporate and community angle

Team-building is a solid revenue channel for many venues.

- Create a dedicated corporate page with capacity, timings, catering and an enquiry form.
- Reach out to local HR managers, office managers, coworking spaces and event planners with a short, personal message.
- Partner with local hotels, breweries and tour operators.
- Offer a "bring your team midweek" option when lanes are quieter.

## Step 8: Bring people back

Repeat visits come from relationships, not luck.

- Collect emails or phone numbers at booking (with consent).
- Send a thank-you message with a review link.
- Promote leagues, themed nights and seasonal events.
- Offer a simple loyalty or referral perk.
- Email past organisers a few months later ("planning another night out?"). See [email marketing for small business](/blog/email-marketing-for-small-business).

## What to measure

Track weekly: bookings by source (ask "how did you hear about us?"), private-event enquiries, review count and rating, social saves and shares, and cost per booking for any paid ads. Compare weekday and weekend fill rates to see where promotions are needed. If you can only track one thing, track bookings per source.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I market an axe throwing business on a small budget?**
A: Start with a well-built Google Business Profile, regular reviews, a simple booking page and short videos shot on a phone. Add small paid tests once your basics convert.

**Q: What social platform is best for axe throwing?**
A: Instagram and TikTok suit short, visual clips, while Facebook is useful for events and local groups. Choose one or two you can keep consistent.

**Q: Can I film customers for social media?**
A: Only with clear consent, ideally collected as part of your waiver or signage, and be especially careful with minors. Check local privacy rules.

**Q: How do I attract corporate bookings?**
A: Create a corporate page, show team-event packages, and contact local office managers and event planners directly. Offer quieter weekday slots to make it easy to say yes.
