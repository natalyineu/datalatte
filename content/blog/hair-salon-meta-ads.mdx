Meta ads fill salon chairs when three things line up: a radius tight enough to match real travel distance, creative showing actual work from the chair, and a booking objective instead of a likes objective. Google Ads has no daily minimum, and Meta tests commonly start at $5 to $20 a day, on platform terms checked in November 2026.

Salons sell trust at short distance. A balayage specialist may draw clients across a city; a walk-in barber draws from a few streets. The radius setting should copy that reality, not the platform default, which often covers a whole metro.

## Which Meta objective should a salon use?

Use the booking or leads objective connected to the real booking system. Traffic and engagement objectives buy cheap taps from people who like hair content, including other stylists. Optimising for completed bookings costs more per result and reports the number that pays rent.

Connect the pixel or Conversions API before spending. Without it, Meta cannot tell a booking from a bounce, and the auction drifts toward whoever clicks prettiest. [Instagram retargeting](/blog/instagram-retargeting-how-to-re-engage-website-visitors) covers that setup for website visitors.

The follow-up channel carries the rest; [AI-powered email tools](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-tools) draft the reminder and win-back sends. Retargeting carries much of the return. People who viewed the price list or a stylist's page and did not book are the warmest audience a salon owns. [Salon rebooking strategy](/blog/salon-rebooking-strategy-that-gets-clients-back) covers the existing-client version of the same audience.

## What creative actually books appointments?

Before-and-after video from the chair outperforms designed graphics because it proves the stylist, not the template. Film the consultation question, the process in two cuts, and the finish in natural light. Captions carry the service name and starting price.

Offer creative needs a deadline and a limit. "Five colour appointments this week" outperforms "20% off" because it names scarcity the salon actually has. Rotating the same discount for months trains clients to wait for sales.

**Assumption example.** Assume a salon spends $15 a day for 30 days, or $450. At an assumed $30 cost per booking, that buys 15 bookings. If the average first ticket is $90 and half rebook once, expected revenue is about $2,025 against $450 of ads. Every rate is an assumption; replace cost per booking after the first $100 of live spend.

## How tight should salon targeting be?

Start with the distance clients already travel. Pull the last 100 bookings and map their postcodes; most salons find the true trade area is far smaller than their ambition. Advertise to that area plus one ring, not to the whole city.

Layer interests lightly, if at all. Inside a tight radius, broad targeting with a booking objective usually beats narrow interest stacks, because Meta's auction finds bookers from behaviour. Age and gender adjustments should follow the service: colour, extensions and barbering have different buyers.

Split campaigns by service value. Balayage and extensions justify a wider radius and higher cost per booking than a fringe trim. One blended campaign hides which service makes money, and the budget drifts to the cheapest click.

## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Meta ads?

A readable test runs $5 to $20 a day for at least two weeks inside the real trade area, on November 2026 platform practice. Judge it on cost per completed booking. A salon that cannot track bookings should fix tracking first; untracked spend reports likes, not revenue.

### Facebook or Instagram for salon ads?

Run both under one Meta campaign and let the auction allocate, unless the clientele clearly sits on one platform. Instagram usually carries the visual proof for colour and styling; Facebook often converts older, higher-spend clients. Splitting budgets in half starves both of learning data at local spend levels.

### Do salon ads need a discount to work?

No. A specific service, a named stylist, a real before-and-after and a limited number of slots convert without training clients to expect sales. Discounts help launch a new stylist or fill a genuinely empty weekday. Permanent discounting usually signals that targeting or creative, not price, is the problem.

### Why do salon ads get clicks but no bookings?

Usually three causes: the radius includes people who will never travel, the objective optimises for taps instead of bookings, or the booking page is slow or confusing on mobile. Check in that order. Most salons find the fault in the objective or the page, not in the creative.
