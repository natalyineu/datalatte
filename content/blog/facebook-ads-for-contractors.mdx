Before-and-after Facebook ads work for contractors because they show the problem being solved, not only the finished job. A usable ad pairs a visible transformation with a specific offer, one piece of proof, and one clear action. Benchmarks give a starting point: a [PipelineOn analysis](https://pipelineon.com/blog/high-converting-facebook-ad-examples/) reports Facebook lead campaigns averaged $27.66 per lead in 2025, a 21% rise, still well below Google's $70.11 lead average. The rest of this guide covers structure, budget, and measurement.

## Why does a before-and-after ad work better than a finished photo?

A finished photo shows a result without a reason to act. A before-and-after ad shows the problem first, so the viewer recognises their own situation. The structure most published contractor examples follow has five parts: a hook that names the homeowner, an agitation that names the cost of waiting, an offer that makes the next step cheap, proof such as a number or a name, and a call to action that states what tapping does. [PipelineOn](https://pipelineon.com/blog/high-converting-facebook-ad-examples/) and [AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-for-construction-companies) both describe this pattern.

The transformation is only one part of the ad. A before photo of a cracked patio does not help if the ad gives no price, no timeline, and no next step. Pair the visual with a line that says what the homeowner gets and what it costs them to ask.

Facebook also works as a discovery channel rather than an intent channel. [Lead Nurture Close](https://leadnurtureclose.com/facebook-ads-vs-google-ads-for-contractors/) describes Google as connecting with people already searching, and Facebook as building awareness before a buyer searches. Before-and-after creative fits that role because it builds familiarity with the work before the homeowner needs it.

## How do you build a before-and-after campaign step by step?

Start with one trade and one service, such as an AC tune-up or a deck repair. Running several services in one ad set makes it hard to tell which creative drove the leads. Set the campaign objective to Leads in Meta Ads Manager and choose Instant Forms as the conversion location, so the form opens inside Facebook. The [Leads-objective setup](https://leadsync.me/blog/facebook-ads-for-contractors/) keeps completion inside the app.

Start with $20 to $50 per day, according to the same guide. Set a radius of 15 to 30 miles around the base, and aim for an audience of 100,000 to 500,000 in the service area. Narrower audiences can raise costs, and wider ones pay for leads the business cannot service.

1. Choose one trade and one service to advertise.
2. Create a Leads campaign with Instant Forms.
3. Set a starting budget of $20 to $50 per day.
4. Target a 15 to 30 mile radius and an audience of 100,000 to 500,000.
5. Build the creative: hook, before-and-after visual, one number of proof, one call to action.
6. Route every lead to a phone call or message quickly.

Speed matters after the lead arrives. [LeadSync](https://leadsync.me/blog/facebook-ads-for-contractors/) reports leads contacted within 5 minutes are 21 times more likely to convert. Automated replies and follow-up tools can help; the [AI tools guide for local marketing](/blog/ai-tools-replace-marketing-assistant-local-business) covers the options. For ad copy variants, the [ChatGPT prompts for local marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) article offers a starting set.

## What does a worked budget look like for an HVAC contractor?

The example below uses assumption-labelled inputs. Replace them with the business's own figures before making a decision. The cost-per-lead input sits inside the HVAC range from the table above.

- Monthly test budget: $1,000 (assumption)
- Cost per lead: $35 (assumption, within the $30–$75 HVAC range)
- Close rate from lead to booked job: 20% (assumption)
- Average job value: $1,500 (assumption)

Leads: $1,000 ÷ $35 ≈ 28 leads. Jobs: 28 × 20% = 5.6 jobs. Revenue: 5.6 × $1,500 = $8,400. Return on ad spend: $8,400 ÷ $1,000 = 8.4. Cost per booked job: $1,000 ÷ 5.6 ≈ $179.

If the close rate fell to 10%, revenue would halve to about $4,200 and return would drop to 4.2. The example shows why close rate matters as much as cost per lead. A cheap lead that never books is not a profitable lead.

## Which mistakes stop before-and-after ads from converting?

The most common problem is a form that asks for too much. Lead form ads convert 25 to 35% higher than landing page ads for home service contractors, according to [LeadsBridge 2025 data cited by PipelineOn](https://pipelineon.com/blog/high-converting-facebook-ad-examples/). Keep the form short: name, phone, and project type cover most needs.

The second mistake is slow follow-up. An ad can generate leads on schedule and still lose them if nobody calls within minutes. The third is running one creative for weeks. When cost per lead rises steadily, swap the image or headline and compare results over a fresh test window.

Check the ad against the five-part structure before launch. If any part is missing, such as the offer or the call to action, the ad is incomplete.

## How should you measure before-and-after ads?

Track three numbers: cost per lead, lead-to-booked-job rate, and return on ad spend. Cost per lead is ad spend divided by leads. Lead-to-booked-job rate is booked jobs divided by leads. Return on ad spend is revenue from ad-sourced jobs divided by ad spend. Compare these across creative versions, not just across the whole account.

Tie leads to jobs in a CRM or booking tool so each lead has an outcome. Without that link, a cheap lead count looks like success even when nothing books. The [free audit](/free-audit) can review a tracking setup if the numbers are hard to match.

## FAQ

### How much should a contractor spend on Facebook ads to start?

Start with $20 to $50 per day, as recommended in the [LeadSync guide](https://leadsync.me/blog/facebook-ads-for-contractors/). Run long enough to gather enough leads to judge cost per lead and close rate. The [budget guide for local businesses](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) explains how to scale once results are clear.

### Should a contractor use Facebook ads or Google Ads?

Both serve different buyers. Google reaches people already searching for a service, while Facebook builds awareness and demand, as [Lead Nurture Close](https://leadnurtureclose.com/facebook-ads-vs-google-ads-for-contractors/) describes. The [comparison for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) covers when each makes sense. For search-driven demand, see [Local Service Ads](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business).

### Do before-and-after ads need a video?

The sources reviewed do not prove video beats static images for every trade. Start with the format that matches the project photos, and test a second format once there are enough leads to compare.

### How fast should a contractor respond to a Facebook lead?

Respond within 5 minutes where possible. [LeadSync](https://leadsync.me/blog/facebook-ads-for-contractors/) reports leads contacted within that window are 21 times more likely to convert.

### Is a lead form better than a landing page for contractors?

For home service contractors, lead forms convert 25 to 35% higher than landing pages in [LeadsBridge 2025 data](https://pipelineon.com/blog/high-converting-facebook-ad-examples/) reported by PipelineOn. Test both if the website already converts well.
