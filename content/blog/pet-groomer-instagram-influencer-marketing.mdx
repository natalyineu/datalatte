A pet groomer can work with local pet owners who post on Instagram by offering a free or discounted groom in exchange for posts, as long as the posts are labelled. The FTC says to disclose any relationship with a brand, including free products or services, and that the disclosure must be hard to miss. Start with two to four small local creators, agree on the posts in writing, give each a unique code, and judge the test by new bookings, not likes.

## Does Influencer Marketing Work for a Pet Groomer?

It can work if the audience is local. A groomer serves a few ZIP codes, so a creator with 100,000 followers across the country adds little. A local dog owner with a few thousand nearby followers is more useful. The Influencer Marketing Hub 2026 benchmark report, based on 600 or more respondents, shows that brands mostly expect budgets to rise, but it gives no groomer data.

Treat the first campaign as a test of whether local creators bring bookings. Our [pet groomer Instagram strategy guide](/blog/pet-groomer-instagram-marketing-strategy) covers your own account first, which matters because creators send people to your profile.

## What Does It Cost?

The IMH report does not state typical per-post rates. It shows how respondents' cost selections were distributed: about 55% of selections for nano creators and 45.5% for micro creators fell under $500, and the rest were higher or uncertain. It is a survey of brands, not a rate card.

For a groomer, the common currency is a service. A groom has a real cost to you, mainly time and supplies, so price it at cost, not at menu price. Agree the number of posts, the Stories, the date and the disclosure wording before the appointment, in writing.

## What Must the Posts Disclose?

The FTC says to disclose any financial, employment, personal or family relationship with a brand, and that free or discounted products count. It says likes, tags and pins can count as endorsements. The disclosure should sit with the endorsement itself, not in a profile page, a hashtag pile or behind a "more" link.

Wording matters. FTC examples include "ad," "advertisement," "sponsored" and "Thanks to Acme brand for the free product." It advises avoiding vague terms such as "collab" or a stand-alone "thanks." In videos, include it in the video, and in Stories superimpose it on the picture long enough to read.

The FTC also says advertisers are responsible for what their networks post, so approve paid posts in advance and check them afterward. This is not legal advice.

## How Do You Find and Brief Local Creators?

Search Instagram for local dog hashtags and the tagged posts at nearby parks, shops and your own profile. Look at whether comments come from people nearby and whether the account posts regularly. Message the owner with a short offer: the service, the dates and what you ask in return.

Give each creator a one-page brief: your address, a few facts about your services, the offer code and the disclosure line. Do not script their voice. Pet owners respond to their own dog, not to your copy. ## Is It Worth It? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions**.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Creators | 3 local dog owners |
| Free groom cost to you (time and supplies) | $30 each |
| Total cost | $90 |
| Posts agreed | 1 feed post and 2 Stories each |
| New bookings using the code | 6 |
| First-visit price | $65 |
| Return rate within a year | 50% |
| Visits per returning client per year | 6 |

Six bookings at $65 is $390 in first-visit revenue against $90 in cost. If three return six times, that adds $1,170 more. These are assumptions, and a different code count could change the result. If no one uses a code after the posts run, the test failed and you only lost $90.

## FAQ

### Do I have to label free-service posts?
Yes. The FTC says to disclose when you gave anything of value, including free products, and the disclosure must be clear and placed with the post.

### Who is responsible for a missing label?
The FTC says influencers must disclose, and that advertisers are responsible for what their networks say. Both should act.

### How much should I pay a local creator?
No source checked gives a rate for groomers. The IMH report shows only a distribution of cost selections. Use the service cost and agree the figure before you start.

### How do I track results?
Give each creator a unique code and ask new clients how they found you at booking.

### Is a micro creator better than a big one?
For a local shop, a nearby audience matters more than size. The sources checked do not compare results by size.
