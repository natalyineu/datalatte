Smart campaigns are worth testing if you have no time to manage an ad account and want a local business to appear on Google Search and Maps. They are a poor fit if you need control over keywords, bids and reporting. You write one ad, pick keyword themes and set a monthly budget, and Google handles placement, ad variations and targeting. You pay only for clicks.

This guide covers what Google's own help pages say Smart campaigns do, what they leave out, and how a small shop can decide. It makes no claim about average results, because Google does not publish Smart campaign benchmarks for local businesses.

## What Is a Google Smart Campaign?

Google describes Smart campaigns as a simplified campaign type. You write an ad about your business, choose keyword themes and set a budget. Google then shows the ad across Google Search, Maps, YouTube, partner websites and the Display Network. It also builds and tests alternate ads from your business information and website, and runs the better ones more often.

You can only create a Smart campaign while your account is in Smart Mode. Switching the account to Expert Mode opens other campaign types, but Google states that Expert Mode cannot be reverted to Smart Mode. Existing Smart campaigns can still be edited from Expert Mode.

## What Do You Control in a Smart Campaign?

You control four things: the ad text, the keyword themes, the location and the budget. Everything else, including bidding, placement and which ad variation shows, is automated.

| Setting | What you can do | Limit or note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyword themes | Choose themes such as "bakery" | Aim for 7 to 10, no punctuation or URLs, 20 characters maximum |
| Negative keyword themes | Block themes that do not fit | Too many can reduce reach; close variants are not matched |
| Location | Pick the area to serve | Ads can also reach people outside it who include your location in a search |
| Budget | Set a monthly or daily budget | Daily figure is multiplied by 30.4 for the monthly cap |
| Ad content | Write headlines and descriptions | Can add a phone number, business name or address |

Google says a theme such as "bakery" can make the ad eligible for searches like "local bakery" and "cake shop". The matching is broader than exact keywords, so check the search terms report after launch.

## How Much Do Smart Campaigns Cost?

You pay for clicks, not for the budget. Google's example: a $50 maximum monthly budget that produces $13 of clicks results in a $13 charge. Spend per day can swing, but total spend in a month does not exceed your maximum monthly budget.

Google does not publish a fixed click price for Smart campaigns. It suggests a budget based on average click volume for your business type and location. For a reference range, WordStream by LocaliQ lists an all-industry average of $5.42 per click, a 6.64% click-through rate and $66.69 per lead for 2026 search ads.

Local category averages differ, so see [our Google Ads vs Facebook Ads comparison](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) for how to compare channels.

## Smart Campaign vs Manual Campaign: Which Fits?

Choose by how much time you have and how much control you need.

| | Smart campaign | Expert Mode Search campaign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup time | Short, guided | Longer, more decisions |
| Keywords | Themes only | Exact, phrase and broad keywords |
| Negatives | Negative keyword themes | Full negative lists |
| Reporting | Keyword themes and search terms report | Full account reporting |
| Reversible | Account switch to Expert is permanent | Not applicable |

Smart campaigns suit a business with one clear service, such as a single-location pet groomer or café, and a budget small enough that a wasted click matters less than the time saved. See our [pet groomer Google Ads guide](/blog/google-ads-for-pet-groomers-guide) for the manual route, which gives more control over search terms.

## How Do You Stop Wasted Clicks?

Open the keyword themes and search terms card and review it weekly for the first month. Google advises that if many searches do not fit your business, make your themes more accurate; if only a few do not fit, add them as negative keyword themes.

Keep themes narrow and tied to what you sell. Google warns that adding too many themes can show your ad for less relevant searches. Also keep your Business Profile current, since Maps placement depends on it: use our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## Worked Example: A $300 Monthly Budget

All inputs are **assumptions**, not Smart campaign data. They use the all-industry CPC and conversion benchmarks as a rough guide.

- Monthly budget: $300 (assumption)
- Cost per click: $5.42 (all-industry average; yours will differ)
- Clicks: about 55
- Conversion rate: 8.18% (all-industry average, LocaliQ)
- Conversions (calls, bookings, direction taps): about 4.5
- Cost per conversion: about $67

If a booking is worth $60 and half of conversions become customers, the campaign loses money on the first visit and pays back only with repeat business. Run it for a month, compare against the real count of new customers, and stop if the number does not move.

## When Should You Move Off Smart Campaigns?

Move when you need exact keyword control, location exclusions beyond the basics, or reporting by search term and device. Google says Expert Mode unlocks other campaign types, but it is a permanent switch for the account, so decide before you click. ## FAQ

### Do Smart campaigns charge me for the whole budget?
No. Google says you are charged only for clicks received. If clicks cost less than your maximum monthly budget, you pay the lower amount.

### How many keyword themes should I use?
Google recommends 7 to 10. Each must be 20 characters or fewer and have no punctuation, URLs or phone numbers.

### Can I add negative keywords to a Smart campaign?
Yes, as negative keyword themes. Google notes they do not match close variants, and too many can limit reach.

### Can I switch back to Smart Mode after using Expert Mode?
No. Google states that Expert Mode cannot be reverted to Smart Mode.

### Are Smart campaigns cheaper than regular Search campaigns?
Google publishes no comparison. Cost depends on your category, area and competition, so test with a small budget.
