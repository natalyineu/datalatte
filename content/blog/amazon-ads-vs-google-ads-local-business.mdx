A local business that sells services or wants customers to visit should use Google Ads. A local business that sells a shippable product it can list on Amazon, and keeps a margin after Amazon's fees, can test Amazon Ads. The two are not substitutes: Google ads reach people searching for a solution, often near them, while Amazon ads reach people already shopping for a product in the Amazon store.

Click costs are not directly comparable. Google's $5.42 average is a published all-industry benchmark, while the $0.82 Amazon figure comes from a small vendor sample.

## What Is the Core Difference?

Google Search shows your ad when someone types a query, so it can send a person to a shop, a booking page or a phone call. Amazon Sponsored Products show your product listing in shopping results and on product pages, and the sale happens on Amazon.

If the thing you sell cannot go in a shopping cart, Amazon has little to offer you. A hair salon, a gym or a plumber needs bookings, which is a Google job. A bakery that ships tins of biscuits, or a groomer with a shampoo line, can make a case for Amazon. ## How Do Costs and Fees Compare?

| | Google Ads | Amazon Sponsored Products |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pricing model | Pay per click on Search | Pay per click, no monthly or upfront campaign fee |
| Published benchmark | $5.42 CPC, 6.64% CTR, $66.69 per lead (all industries, LocaliQ 2026) | $0.82 median US CPC, 5.85% median conversion rate (69-account vendor sample) |
| Budget | You set a daily budget | You set a daily budget; it is not paced through the day, so a small budget can run out early |
| Who can advertise | Any advertiser in a supported country | Professional sellers, vendors and agencies with eligible products |
| Extra costs | None required for ads | $39.99 a month Professional plan, plus referral fees |

Amazon lists referral fees of 15% for Pet Supplies and Home and Kitchen, and 8% up to $10 then 15% for Beauty, Health and Personal Care, with a $0.30 minimum. Fulfilment fees are extra.

WisePPC says its accounts all use an optimisation tool, are not a random sample, and that the company publishes the data to promote its product. Treat the Amazon numbers as a rough guide.

## Which Local Businesses Should Use Which?

| Business | Better first channel | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salon, barber, groomer, gym | Google Ads | People book near them |
| Cafe or restaurant | Google Ads and Maps | Visits, not shipments |
| Local shop that ships branded goods | Test Amazon after Google | Product-shopping intent |
| Business with its own haircare or pet-care line | Both, small tests | Services on Google, products on Amazon |

## Does the Math Work? A Worked Example

Both examples use **assumptions**.

Amazon product: a $30 item with a 15% referral fee ($4.50) and $12 of product and shipping cost leaves $13.50, a 45% margin before ads. Break-even ACoS is therefore 45%. The median in the vendor sample was 28.2%, which would leave about 17 points of margin, but your account may differ.

Google service: a first appointment worth $60 and the all-industry average cost per lead of $66.69 do not pay back on the first visit. They can pay back if the client returns, so estimate lifetime value first.

Amazon's own daily budget logic matters too. Its help page says a $100 daily budget could allow up to $3,100 in clicks in a 31-day month. Set the cap deliberately.

## Should You Run Both?

Start with the channel that matches how customers buy from you. Run the second only after the first is tracked and profitable. Two channels at once split a small budget and make results hard to read.

Track each separately: Google with calls, forms and direction requests; Amazon with ACoS and units sold. See [Amazon Sponsored Products for local business](/blog/amazon-sponsored-products-for-local-business) for setup basics.

## FAQ

### Is Amazon Ads cheaper than Google Ads?
Per click, one vendor sample shows a lower figure ($0.82 against $5.42), but the datasets differ and Amazon adds seller fees. Compare cost per sale, not cost per click.

### Can a service business advertise on Amazon?
Amazon Sponsored Products are for eligible products sold on Amazon, so a service business cannot use them.

### Do I need an Amazon seller account to advertise?
Yes. Sponsored Products are available to professional sellers, vendors, book vendors, KDP authors and agencies.

### Which gives faster results?
No source here compares speed. Search ads can show immediately for matching queries, but learning takes weeks on either platform.
