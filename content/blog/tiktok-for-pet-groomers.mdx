If a new client is worth roughly $63 in gross profit over their first 90 days, you need about 3 new clients a month from it to break even. This guide is for salon and mobile groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. For comparison, Pew reports 71% of US adults use Facebook and 50% use Instagram. TikTok is not the biggest platform, but it is strong among under-50s, which is a lot of the dog-owning, first-puppy crowd. "Ever use" is not "uses daily", so this shows reach, not attention. It is US data; check your own client list for age before you commit.

The sensible reading: if most of your regulars are over 50, Facebook and Google reach them more reliably, and TikTok is an experiment. If your books are full of 25 to 45-year-old doodle owners, TikTok is worth testing. Look at your last 30 bookings and guess ages honestly.

## Why Groomers Are Naturally Good at This

A groom is a transformation with a start, a middle and an end, which is the shape short video likes. The tangle becomes a fluffy teddy cut in 20 seconds. The dog looks proud at the end. You do not need acting, a studio or a script. You need the job you already do, filmed well.

The catch is that views are not bookings. A video that reaches 100,000 people in other countries brings you nobody. What matters is the share of viewers who live within a short drive, and whether they can tell where you are and how to book.

## What to Film

### 1. The before-and-after reveal

Film the dog on arrival and the finished dog, from the same angle, in the same light. Cut between them. Use a plain backdrop or a towel so the coat is the focus. Keep each one short enough that the reveal arrives early.

### 2. The process clip

Clippers gliding through a coat, a brush finishing a doodle, scissoring a face. Keep real sound where you can. Satisfying process clips are easy to batch because you film them while working.

### 3. Breed-and-coat explainers

"What a Cavapoo needs every 6 weeks" or "Why we do not shave double coats". They show expertise and answer questions people search for. These get fewer views than a reveal but attract more serious owners.

### 4. First-groom and nervous-dog stories

A puppy's first visit, filmed gently with the owner's permission, builds trust. This is where your calm approach is the selling point.

### 5. Local hooks

"Grooming spots near [neighbourhood]", "Summer coat care in [your town]", a clip of the street outside your salon. These bring neighbours.

## Rules of Good Taste (and Good Business)

These are recommendations, not platform rules. TikTok's Community Guidelines have their own rules on animal content that change over time, so read the current version before you post.

- **Never film a dog in distress** to make a more dramatic video. A frightened dog on camera costs you trust faster than a boring video costs you views.
- **Get the owner's permission** before posting any client's pet, and keep a short note of it. A simple line on your booking form covering photos and video is enough.
- **Be honest about matting.** A severely matted dog may need a shave-down for its comfort. Explain it kindly, not as a "transformation fail".
- **Do not post health or behaviour details** about a pet or owner without permission.
- **Keep the dog safe first.** If you are filming, you are not watching the dog. Use a tripod or a second pair of hands around clippers and dryers.

## Set the Account Up to Convert

**Use a business account, and use cleared music.** TikTok's ads blog on the [Commercial Music Library](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/audio-library-royalty-free-music) says that using the General Music Library through a personal account to promote a business puts you "at risk of copyright infringement", and that its songs are not cleared for brand content. A Business Account is needed to see the in-app Commercial Music Library. TikTok's [help page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library) says it holds about 1 million songs and covers organic content as well as ads. It also says that if you use other users' original sounds, you should confirm you have a proper licence. So a trending sound that is not in the library may not be safe for your salon's account.

**Write a bio that names a place.** "Dog groomer in [neighbourhood]. Doodles and anxious dogs welcome. Book below." Add your booking link if your account allows one (availability varies, so check the profile editor in your app).

**Say the location in every clip.** Put your neighbourhood in the first seconds, on screen or out loud, and in the caption. This is how a viewer 12 miles away scrolls on and one 2 miles away stops.

**Link TikTok to your search presence.** People who like a video often Google you before they book. A complete [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers) with recent reviews is what they find, and your [website pages](/blog/pet-groomer-website-pages-and-copy-guide) should answer price and booking questions without a phone call.

## A Four-Week Starter Plan

Film in one batch a week, during your normal day.

| Week | Batch | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 reveals, 2 process clips | Learn your angles and lighting |
| 2 | 2 reveals, 1 breed explainer, 2 local hooks | Test what draws neighbours |
| 3 | 1 first-groom story, 2 reveals, 1 FAQ answer | Build trust |
| 4 | Repeat the best format from weeks 1 to 3 | Double down on data |

Post three or four times a week (my suggestion, not a platform rule). Judge on bookings, not views, after at least 8 to 12 weeks.

## Count Bookings, Not Views

TikTok shows views, not appointments. Use three cheap checks:

1. Add "How did you find us?" to your booking form, with TikTok as an option.
2. Offer a video-only perk you can honour, such as a free blueberry facial add-on if they mention a specific video. Check your local rules on promotions first.
3. Watch your Business Profile for a bump in searches and directions after a strong video. Google's [performance page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094) explains those metrics. It shows correlation, so pair it with the form.

## Should You Pay for TikTok Ads?

Not first. TikTok's [help page on budgets](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/budget), last updated July 2026, says a daily budget must exceed $50 at the campaign level and $20 at the ad group level. For a lifetime budget it gives the formula: total days (including past days) times the $20 ad group daily minimum, with a 31-day example needing at least $620. The page does not name the currency, though amounts use the dollar sign. So even a 14-day test is about $280 or more.

That is the price of a trial, and it only makes sense after you know which organic formats get booked. ## The Math

Time cost is 6 hours a month: two filming sessions of 1 hour each, plus about 3 hours of editing and 1 hour of replies. All rows are **assumptions** except where noted; replace them after a couple of months of tracking.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly views (assumption) | 5,000 | 20,000 | 40,000 |
| Share from your area (assumption) | 10% | 15% | 20% |
| Local viewers | 500 | 3,000 | 8,000 |
| Local viewers who book within 30 days (assumption) | 0.2% | 0.3% | 0.4% |
| New clients | 1 | 9 | 32 |
| Visits in first 90 days (assumption) | 1.5 | 1.5 | 1.5 |
| Average groom price (assumption) | $70 | $70 | $70 |
| Revenue per new client in 90 days | $105 | $105 | $105 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 60% | 60% | 60% |
| Gross profit per new client | $63 | $63 | $63 |
| Gross profit total | $63 | $567 | $2,016 |
| Time cost (6 hours at $30, assumption) | $180 | $180 | $180 |
| Net | -$117 | +$387 | +$1,836 |

Break-even is $180 divided by $63, about 2.9 new clients a month. The Strong column is a rare outcome, so do not plan around it; many accounts post for months before a clip travels. A 14-day paid test at about $280 would need roughly 4.4 new clients just to cover itself.

Two levers matter more than views:

- **Repeat visits.** A dog groomed every 6 to 8 weeks makes a new client worth far more than the first booking. That is assumption-level, so use your own rebooking rate.
- **Local share.** A video with half the views but twice the local viewers is worth the same. That is why the neighbourhood hook beats chasing a trend.

For market size context, APPA reports US pet owners are in 95 million households, and its "other services" category (boarding, grooming, insurance, training, sitting, walking and similar) was $14.3 billion in 2024, projected at $14.9 billion for 2026. That category does not break out grooming, so it shows that people spend on services, not what you will earn.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to be on camera?**
A: No. Hands, tools and the dog are the content. A voiceover or on-screen text can carry the story. Show your face if you want to, but it is not required.

**Q: How often should I post?**
A: TikTok publishes no minimum. Three or four posts a week is a sustainable starting point (my suggestion), and consistency over months matters more than bursts.

**Q: Can I use trending sounds?**
A: Only if they are in the Commercial Music Library or you have a proper licence. TikTok says the general library is not cleared for businesses.

**Q: What if my videos get views but no bookings?**
A: Usually the viewers are not local, or the clip gives no reason or way to book. Add the neighbourhood, a booking link and a clear next step, then track with your form.

**Q: How much does TikTok advertising cost?**
A: TikTok's help page says a daily ad group budget must exceed $20, and a campaign budget must exceed $50, so a short test costs hundreds of dollars. Test organic first.
