Ten dollars a day. That is the number Google's own budget rules turn into roughly $304 a month, because Google lets a campaign spend up to 30.4 times its average daily budget in a month.

At an assumed $2 per click, that is about 150 clicks, enough to learn whether your neighbourhood searches for coffee the way you hope. This page is the short version: one campaign, one afternoon, one budget floor.

If you want the full playbook (seasonal pushes, catering, audiences, Performance Max), read the [complete Google Ads for coffee shops guide](/blog/google-ads-for-coffee-shops-complete-2026-guide) after you have this first campaign running. Written for café owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Dollar figures are US benchmarks; run the same math in your own currency.

## What Should You Check Before You Spend a Dollar?

Ads send people to your listing and your website. If those are weak, you pay for clicks that leave. Check three things first:

1. Your Google Business Profile has correct hours, a real menu link, photos from this year and recent replies to reviews. Our [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) takes about an hour.
2. You rank at least reasonably in Maps already. See how [coffee shops win on Google Maps](/blog/coffee-shops-dominate-google-maps), since free map results often cost less than paid clicks.
3. Your website loads on a phone and shows hours, address and a directions button without scrolling.

## What Does a Click Cost for a Coffee Shop?

Public benchmarks do not break out coffee shops. The closest category is "Restaurants & Food". WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks list $2.05 per click, a 6.83% click-through rate, an 8.05% conversion rate and $30.57 per lead for it, against $5.42 per click across all industries.

A secondary roundup (Webtonic, October 2026) quotes a much lower $0.79 CPC from a 2025 data set of tracked campaigns, so the spread is wide. We use only the LocaliQ figures as the reference and treat the rest as context.

Plan around $2 a click, and expect your own city to differ. Dense downtown areas with many cafés tend to cost more than suburbs; that is a general pattern, not a published number.

## How Do You Build the Campaign in Two Hours?

### 1. One Search campaign, one small radius

Create a Search campaign with the goal of website traffic, phone calls or local actions, depending on what you can track. Set the location to a radius around your shop that matches how far people really walk or drive for a coffee, not the whole city. Use presence targeting (people physically in the area) so you do not pay for someone merely curious about your town.

### 2. The starter keyword list

Google defines three match types. Exact match, in square brackets, "gives you the most control over who sees your ad but reaches the fewest searches"; phrase match, in quotes, includes more specific versions of your keyword; broad is the default and, per Google, needs Smart Bidding. At this budget, start with phrase and exact only.

| Ad group | Keywords | Match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nearby | "coffee near me", [coffee shop near me], "coffee shop [neighbourhood]" | Phrase + exact |
| Drinks | "iced latte near me", "cold brew [town]", "oat milk latte near me" | Phrase |
| Work and study | "cafe with wifi near me", "quiet coffee shop [town]" | Phrase (only if true) |
| Open now | "coffee open now", "coffee open late near me" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your shop name] | Exact |

Keep each group to three to five related keywords. For more ideas by city and season, see our [coffee shop keyword list for 2026](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-coffee-shops-in-2026).

### 3. Negative keywords on day one

Add `beans`, `machine`, `grinder`, `wholesale`, `jobs`, `hiring`, `franchise`, `recipe`, `pods`, `instant`, `starbucks`, `dunkin` and `free`. Competitor-brand negatives are optional; you may prefer to bid on them deliberately. Our negative keywords guide shows where to add them.

### 4. One ad that gives one reason to visit

Responsive search ads accept up to 15 headlines of 30 characters and 4 descriptions of 90, per Google. Fill several slots with: your neighbourhood, a distinctive drink or roast, your hours, and an action.

- Headlines: `Specialty Coffee in [Area]`, `Open 7am Daily`, `Oat Milk Lattes`, `Get Directions`
- Description: `Small-batch roasts and fresh pastries. Walk in, no wait. Find us on [Street].`

Only promise what the counter can honour.

### 5. Assets: location and call

Link your Business Profile so location assets show your address and distance, and add a call asset so a tap rings the shop. Google's help says location assets can display your address, a map to your location or the distance to your business.

### 6. Schedule and bid

Run ads for your open hours only. Choose Maximize clicks with a sensible cap on cost per click at first, since a brand new campaign has no conversion history for smarter bidding. Move to conversion-based bidding after you have a steady count of calls and direction requests. The point about waiting for data is a general rule of thumb, not a Google threshold.

## What Is the Minimum Daily Budget?

Google explains that your average daily budget is a monthly average, not a hard daily cap: spend can exceed it on a given day, up to twice the daily budget for most campaigns, and the monthly limit is 30.4 times the daily figure. So $10 a day means up to about $304 a month, and a single day may be $20.

Below roughly $10 a day, a one-location café campaign gets so few clicks that a week tells you almost nothing. That floor is a practical starting point, not a Google minimum. Above about $20 a day, you are better off fixing tracking and the landing page before spending more.

What is a regular worth, and how many do you need? All numbers below are **assumptions** except the 30.4 budget rule (Google).

If your margin on a coffee is, say, 60% (assumption), that is about $130 of gross profit against $47.50 of ad cost. The lever you control most is the last block: loyalty cards, a second-visit offer and a friendly counter change visits per month and months retained.

## What Should You Track?

You do not need a perfect setup on day one. You need:

- **Calls from the ad and clicks on directions.** Follow the steps in our conversion tracking setup guide.
- **A promo code or "mention this ad" offer,** with staff trained to ask. A free flat white for a new face, redeemed at the counter, is a crude but honest tracker.

Check your Business Profile insights weekly for direction requests and calls.

## What Should You Do Today?

1. Open your Business Profile and fix hours, menu link and photos before anything else.
2. Create one Search campaign, one radius, presence-only targeting, $10 a day.
3. Paste in the five ad groups above with phrase and exact match, plus the negative list.
4. Write one responsive search ad with your neighbourhood, hours and a drink name, and link your profile for location and call assets.
5. Set up call and directions conversions, add a promo code, and put a 20-minute weekly review in your calendar.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does a coffee shop need to spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official minimum. A budget of about $10 a day (up to roughly $304 a month under Google's 30.4 rule) is a practical starting floor, enough to collect around 150 clicks at an assumed $2 each.

**Q: Is "coffee near me" worth bidding on?**
A: Yes as a test, because intent is high. But Google Maps already shows nearby cafés for that search, so compare ad clicks with your free Maps traffic and keep the keyword only if it brings visits.

**Q: Should I use Performance Max or Search?**
A: Search first. Search lets you see exactly which terms trigger your ad. The [complete guide](/blog/google-ads-for-coffee-shops-complete-2026-guide) covers when to test other campaign types.

**Q: How fast will I see results?**
A: Clicks start within hours of approval. Judging results takes about four weeks, a rule of thumb rather than a Google figure, because a small campaign needs time to gather enough data.

**Q: Do ads work better than just improving my Maps listing?**
A: Not usually as a first step. Fix the free listing first, then add ads for a slow daypart, a new opening or a seasonal drink.
