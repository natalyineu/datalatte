To set up Google Ads remarketing, you add Google's tag to your website, create a "your data" segment of past visitors, attach it to a campaign and cap how often people see the ads.

A segment needs at least 100 active users in the last 30 days before Google will serve it on Display, Search or YouTube ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472738)). The steps below follow Google's own help pages, plus a break-even test so you know whether it pays.

## What is Google Ads remarketing, and is it right for my shop?

Remarketing shows ads to people who already visited your website or app. Google describes it as a way to "re-engage people who have previously interacted with your brand or services" as they browse Google or partner sites ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453998)). Google now calls these audiences "audience segments", and the remarketing type is a "your data" segment.

It fits you if people look at your menu, prices or booking page and then do not act. That is common for salons, groomers and studios where a booking is a considered choice.

It does not fit yet if your site gets very few visits. A segment needs 100 active users in 30 days to serve, and a small segment gives you little to learn from. Build traffic first with [local search and your Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist), then add remarketing.

## How do I set up remarketing in Google Ads step by step?

Follow these six steps in order.

1. **Add the Google tag.** Google's setup page says you first "set up an audience source for your website or apps" using the tag from Audience sources ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10569156)). If you use a site builder, paste the tag in the header or use its Google Ads integration.
2. **Check the tag fires.** Visit your own site and confirm the tag is detected before building segments.
3. **Create segments by page.** Rules can use a page address, for example a URL that contains a path such as /pricing. Make one for your booking or pricing page and one for the whole site.
4. **Set the membership duration.** The default is 30 days and the maximum is 540 days. For a haircut or a coffee, 30 days is a fair start.
5. **Add the segment to a campaign.** Each campaign and ad group can include one audience, and an audience can contain several segments.
6. **Add a conversion action.** Count bookings, calls or form submissions so you can judge results. Our [GA4 for small business guide](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business) covers the counting side.

## How big does my audience need to be?

For website visitors on Display, Search and YouTube, Google requires a minimum of 100 active users within the last 30 days. Customer lists have the same 100-user eligibility, but only for lists uploaded or refreshed after February 1, 2024. Older lists still need 1,000 matched users ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472738)).

"Active users" means "the number of people who engaged with your site or app". That count can differ from the total users shown in Analytics. If your segment sits below the minimum, it will show but not serve.

Many guides still quote 1,000 users for Search. Google's current page says 100, so use that.

## How do I stop ads from following people around?

Use a frequency cap on Display and Video campaigns. Google says you can use frequency capping in those two campaign types, choosing a limit per day, week or month. For Display, only viewable impressions count toward the cap ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6034106)).

Two details trip people up:

- You set it from the Frequency management section in campaign settings after the campaign exists.
- Google states that frequency capping isn't supported in Demand Gen campaigns.

Google does not publish a perfect number. A starting cap such as 3 impressions per person per day is an assumption to test, not a rule. Watch your frequency and conversion reports and loosen or tighten it. For wider rules on staying welcome, read our [retargeting best practices](/blog/retargeting-best-practices-10-rules-to-avoid-annoying-your-audience).

## Should I use targeting or observation on Search campaigns?

On Search, you can add a segment as **targeting**, which narrows who sees your ads, or **observation**, which does not narrow reach. Google's API documentation describes it the same way: observation leaves reach unchanged and still allows bid adjustments ([Google Ads API docs](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/targeting/targeting-settings)).

Practical rule for a small account:

- Use **observation** first. You see how past visitors convert without cutting your traffic.
- Use **targeting** only when you want the ad shown to that group alone, for example a special offer for people who viewed your pricing page.

## What does a remarketing campaign cost, and when does it pay?

Google publishes no standard cost, so test your own numbers. Every figure here is an **assumption** for a hair salon.

| Assumption | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly remarketing budget | $200 |
| Cost per click | $1.00 |
| Clicks | 200 |
| Click-to-booking rate | 3% |
| Bookings | 6 |
| Cost per booking | about $33 |
| Average ticket | $85 |
| Gross margin | 60% |
| Margin from 6 bookings | $306 |
| Result after the $200 spend | about $106 |

At those inputs the campaign breaks even at about 4 bookings (200 divided by $51 margin each). If your booking rate is 1%, you get 2 bookings and lose money. Run for 4 to 6 weeks before judging, and compare against your costs from the [Google Ads pricing guide](/blog/how-much-does-google-ads-cost-for-small-businesses-real-2026-pricing-breakdown).

Check policy before you start. Google's personalized advertising policy lists Health among sensitive categories where advertisers are unable to use advertiser-curated audiences ([Google Ads Policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465)). If you run a clinic or similar, confirm eligibility first, and update your privacy policy to describe data segments.

## FAQ

**Q: How many visitors do I need for Google Ads remarketing?**
A: At least 100 active users in the last 30 days for Display, Search or YouTube. Customer lists uploaded after February 1, 2024 have the same 100 minimum.

**Q: How long do visitors stay in my remarketing list?**
A: The default is 30 days for Display and Search. You can extend it up to 540 days.

**Q: Can I cap how often people see my remarketing ads?**
A: Yes, in Display and Video campaigns, per day, week or month. It is not supported in Demand Gen campaigns.

**Q: Does remarketing work without a website?**
A: You need an audience source, which is a website or app with the tag, or a customer list. A booking page that lets you add the tag can serve as the source, but its audience may be small.

**Q: What is the difference between remarketing and retargeting?**
A: In practice they mean the same thing. Google uses remarketing and "your data segments". For the wider channel picture, see our [retargeting guide for small business](/blog/retargeting-ads-for-small-business-complete-guide-2026).
