Local SEO in France starts with Google. StatCounter puts Google at 88.16% of search traffic in France for September 2026, far ahead of Bing at 5.47%. A French small business gets found by completing a Google Business Profile, writing pages in French for each town and service, collecting genuine reviews, and following French rules on reviews and cookies.

This guide is for independent shops, salons, cafés and trades in France. Every figure comes from a source listed at the end, and the worked example is labelled as an assumption.

## How big is the online audience in France?

DataReportal's Digital 2026 report counts 63.4 million internet users in France in October 2025, or 95.2% of the 66.7 million population. Only about 3.23 million people were offline. It also counts 51.5 million social media user identities (77.2% of the population) and 77.3 million mobile connections.

For a local business this means nearly every customer can search on a phone. The question is whether your listing appears when they do.

## Which search engine matters most?

Google. StatCounter's September 2026 figures for all devices are Google 88.16%, Bing 5.47%, Yahoo 2.75%, Ecosia 1.34%, Qwant 0.86% and DuckDuckGo 0.84%. Privacy-focused French engines exist, but together they are a small slice.

Optimise for Google Search and Google Maps first. Bing Places is a cheap second step, because Bing is the next largest engine.

## What ranks a business in the local results?

Google states that local results rely mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that you cannot request or pay for a better local ranking. You control relevance and prominence, not distance.

Google's help page also says an inaccurate profile may not show for relevant searches in your area. Set the right primary category, add regular and special hours (for example holidays), and give a full address if customers visit you. Keep name, address and phone number identical everywhere. For a checklist, see our [Google Business Profile guide for Europe](/blog/google-business-profile-optimisation-europe).

## Is PagesJaunes still worth a listing?

It can be a useful extra citation, but treat its numbers with care. Solocal, the owner of PagesJaunes, reported in its 2024 results about 13% more visits and 2.2 million more unique visitors. A Solocal sales page from June 2026 claims one French person in three uses PagesJaunes monthly. That is a vendor claim, not an audited figure.

A free basic listing with consistent details costs little. Paid packages need a clear return: ask what leads you receive before you sign. Solocal's own AGM slides show falling renewal sales in 2024, so negotiate and compare.

## What should the website say in French?

Write for the town and the service together, in natural French. A page for each service and each town you actually serve beats one generic page. Use the words customers type, such as the trade plus the town name, and show your address, hours, prices where possible, and a phone number that works on mobile.

Add `LocalBusiness` structured data and make sure the page loads fast on a phone. Avoid copying one page and swapping the town name, because thin duplicates add nothing.

## How do you handle reviews under French rules?

Ask every customer, never pay for positive reviews, and reply to all of them. According to a French lawyer's 2026 article (secondary source), fake reviews fall under the consumer code as a deceptive practice following the transposition of the EU Omnibus directive in 2022, with fines that can reach 10% of average annual turnover.

The same source says DGCCRF has checked more than 1,200 businesses since July 2023. Check the current text on Légifrance before relying on these details.

In practice: do not write your own reviews, do not offer gifts only for five stars. Our [online reviews strategy for Europe](/blog/online-reviews-strategy-europe) covers the process.

## Do French cookie rules affect local SEO?

Yes, if you measure traffic. CNIL says non-essential cookies cannot be placed or read before the visitor consents, that refusing must be as easy as accepting, and that you must be able to prove consent. Analytics and ad tags are included unless exempt.

A badly built banner can break your measurement, so test that tracking fires only after consent. For ads, see our [Google Ads guide for European businesses](/blog/google-ads-for-european-businesses).

## What does a first 30 days plan cost? (worked example)

All inputs are **assumptions** for a one-location hairdresser in a mid-sized French town, not measured results.

- Assumption: 3 hours to claim and complete the profile and fix the main category and hours.
- Assumption: 4 hours to write two service pages in French.
- Assumption: 15 minutes per week to ask recent customers for reviews by SMS or a card at the till.
- Assumption: no paid listing in the first month.

Time is about 9 hours in month one, then roughly one hour a month. The aim is to measure calls, direction requests and website clicks in the profile after 30 days and decide on any paid step from that data. For ranking tactics inside Maps, read [Google Maps ranking tips for Europe](/blog/google-maps-ranking-tips-europe).

## How many French businesses compete with you?

Insee counted 1,111,238 business creations in France in 2024, up 5.7% on 2023. Micro-entrepreneurs made up 716,194 of them, companies 284,564 and classic sole traders 110,480. These are new registrations, not the total number of businesses, so do not read them as market size.

Many new local competitors are one-person micro-entreprises with little web presence. A complete profile and a few good pages can already set you apart.

## FAQ

### Can I pay Google to rank higher in local results?

Google's help page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Check Google's current terms for any fees on related products.

### Do I need a French-language website?

If your customers are in France, yes. Write in French for the towns and services you serve. The DataReportal and StatCounter figures here describe the whole French market, so check your own search terms in Google Search Console.

### Should I use Bing Places too?

It is a low-cost extra. Bing has 5.47% of French searches in StatCounter's September 2026 data, so it is secondary to Google.

### Can I buy reviews or offer a discount for a good rating?

Do not. French consumer law treats fake reviews as deceptive practices (secondary source, verify on Légifrance).

### How long until local SEO shows results?

No source gives a reliable timeline. Track calls and direction requests monthly in your profile and compare with the month before.
