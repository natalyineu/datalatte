Instagram marketing for hotels means showing what a stay feels like, sending interested people to a direct booking page, and measuring that page separately from other traffic. Instagram has about 1.7 billion users, according to figures Adobe Express cites from DataReportal, and a vendor guide reports that about 81% of people use Instagram to research a service. Hotel-specific Instagram benchmarks are scarce, so treat the numbers below as a starting point. Test each idea, and decide on paid ads only after you have tracked bookings, not likes.

## Is Instagram Worth Testing for a Hotel?

Start with your guest mix. Adobe Express, citing DataReportal, reports that the largest age group active on Instagram is 25 to 34, followed by 18 to 24. It also reports users as 48% female and 52% male, with data for other genders not available. Its claim that a business can reach over 20% of the world's population describes a reach ceiling, not a booking forecast.

If most of your guests fall in those age ranges, Instagram deserves a test. If most arrive through travel agents, corporate accounts or group contracts, put the first effort into your Google listing and your direct booking page, then add Instagram later. Check your booking system for guest age and origin, or ask at check-in for a month.

## What Should a Hotel Post on Instagram?

Post what helps a stranger imagine the stay: rooms in daylight, the breakfast, the street outside, the team, and things to do nearby. Use real photos of your own property, because guests compare what they see online with what they find on arrival. Ask guests for permission before reposting their photos, and credit them when you do.

Frequency matters less than consistency. Hootsuite's research, as cited by a salon software blog, recommends posting to Instagram two to seven times per week (secondary). Stories are temporary, which suits same-day offers and last-minute availability. For a comparison point, the [coffee shop social media strategy](/blog/coffee-shop-social-media-strategy) guide recommends three to five feed posts per week plus daily Stories for a different type of business.

## How Do You Turn Followers Into Bookings?

Each post should lead to one clear next step. Use this checklist before you publish:

1. Add a Book Now button to your Instagram and Facebook profile, which both platforms support.
2. Add your Instagram link to your Google Business Profile. Google allows one social media link per platform.
3. Create a tagged booking link used only for Instagram, so you can attribute bookings to it.
4. Put the offer, the dates and the booking link in the caption, and make sure the landing page shows the same offer.
5. Retarget visitors who did not book. Benchmark Email describes Expedia targeting people who searched for flights but did not book, offering airfare and hotel discounts, which the page says significantly increased bookings (secondary).

## Should a Hotel Run Instagram Ads?

Test ads with a small budget after your booking page and offer are ready. Meta Ads Manager, formerly Facebook Ads Manager, works as a central place to run ads across Instagram and Facebook, and Turrboo notes that it also covers Messenger (secondary). Per-click prices are a rough guide only. AdRoll's 2021 figures put Instagram ads between $0.20 and $2 per click (secondary), and current prices appear in Ads Manager.

Clicks are not bookings. Judge ads by cost per booking, and compare results with the [AI-driven social media management tools](/blog/ai-driven-social-media-management-tools) you already use for scheduling and reporting, so the numbers are tracked the same way each month.

## What Does a Test Budget Return Per Booking?

All inputs below are **assumptions**. The per-click figure sits inside the $0.20 to $2 range cited above.

| Line | Assumption | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $300 | $300 spent |
| Cost per click | $1.00 | 300 clicks |
| Visitors who start a booking | 2% | 6 booking starts |
| Booking starts that complete | 25% | 1.5 bookings |
| Cost per booking | derived | $200 |
| Average booking value | $350 | $525 gross booking value |

On these inputs the gross booking value is $525 against $300 of spend, before room costs, commissions and repeat stays, which are not counted. Change only the completion rate to 10% and the result is 0.6 bookings, a cost per booking of $500, and a booking value of $210, which is less than the spend. The test is worth running only if your real completion rate is close to the first scenario.

## How Do You Track Instagram Bookings?

Use the tagged link for every Instagram post and ad, then compare bookings from that link each month. Add a question to check-in or the booking confirmation asking how the guest heard about you, and keep the answers. Organic posts can build reach without paying the platform, as the Bookeo guide notes, but the revenue effect still needs a record.

Run the test for two months before you decide. If cost per booking is above your average booking value, pause the ads and keep organic posting. The guide on [AI-powered social media analytics for local businesses](/blog/ai-powered-social-media-analytics-for-local-businesses) explains how to set up reporting that does this without manual spreadsheets.

## FAQ

### Does Instagram help hotels get direct bookings?

Sources show Instagram reaches people and that many people use it to research services. They do not measure hotel bookings directly, so use a tagged booking link and compare results.

### How often should a hotel post on Instagram?

Hootsuite's research, as cited on a salon software blog, recommends two to seven posts per week (secondary). Pick a rhythm you can keep for several months.

### What does an Instagram ad click cost?

AdRoll's 2021 figures, cited by Adobe Express, put Instagram ads between $0.20 and $2 per click (secondary). Check current costs in Meta Ads Manager, since prices change.

### Can I repost guest photos?

Only with the guest's permission, and always with credit. Ask in a message or comment and keep a record of the reply.
