A hotel should use Instagram to show what a stay feels like, then send interested people to a direct booking page and measure that link. Pew's 2026 survey found that 51% of US adults use Instagram, rising to 79% of those aged 18 to 29 and falling to 21% of those 65 and over. So it fits a hotel whose guests skew younger, and less so one whose guests are mostly older.

## Is Instagram the Right Channel for Your Guests?

Check your guest mix first. Pew's 2026 figures show Instagram use by age: 79% of 18 to 29s, 64% of 30 to 49s, 41% of 50 to 64s and 21% of 65 and over. Facebook is used by 70% of US adults overall, so a hotel with older guests may reach more of them there. These are US numbers, so look for local data if you are elsewhere.

Open your booking system and look at the age and origin of guests, or ask at check-in. If most guests are under 50, Instagram deserves a test. If not, spend the time on Google and reviews first. ## What Should a Hotel Post?

Post what helps a stranger picture the stay: rooms in daylight, the breakfast, the street outside, the team, local things to do, seasonal offers. Use real photos of your hotel, since guests compare them with what they find on arrival. Ask guests for permission before reposting their photos, and credit them.

A simple weekly rhythm is enough to start: two feed posts or Reels, a few Stories, and a reply to every comment and message within a day. ## How Do You Fill Rooms in the Off-Season?

Tie content to a reason to come in the quiet months: a local event, a midweek package, a spa offer, a food festival. Put the offer and the dates in the caption, and add a link to a direct booking page with that offer visible. Avoid claiming savings you cannot show.

No verified study shows how much a given post raises direct bookings, so measure it. Use a separate link for Instagram, such as a tagged URL, and compare bookings from that link month by month.

## Should a Hotel Run Instagram Ads?

Test them with a small budget after the page and offer work. WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook ads benchmarks give all-industry traffic campaigns a $0.60 average click and 1.93% click-through rate, and list Travel at a $0.42 click and 2.28% click-through rate. Those are traffic clicks on Meta platforms, not bookings, and not specific to hotels.

Clicks are cheap compared with search ads, but they come from people who were not looking for a room. Judge ads by cost per booking, not cost per click. ## The Math: What Does a Test Cost per Booking?

All inputs are **assumptions** that use the Travel click price as a starting point.

| | Assumption |

| Monthly ad budget | $300 |
| Cost per click | $0.42 |
| Clicks | about 714 |
| Visitors who start a booking | 2% |
| Booking starts | about 14 |
| Booking starts that complete | 10% |
| Bookings | about 1.4 |
| Cost per booking | about $214 |
| Average booking value (assumption) | $320 |

On these inputs one booking is worth more than the cost per booking, but only just, and no repeat stay is counted. If the booking-start rate is 1% instead, cost per booking doubles. Test for two months and record how many guests mention Instagram. If the numbers do not work, stay with organic posts and Google.

## FAQ

### Does Instagram help hotels get direct bookings?

It can send traffic and build awareness, but no verified study quantifies the effect for hotels. Use a tagged booking link and compare bookings from it.

### How often should a hotel post?

Choose a rhythm you can keep, such as two posts or Reels and a few Stories a week. No source reviewed here proves an ideal frequency.

### What do Instagram ads cost for a hotel?

LocaliQ's 2026 Meta traffic benchmarks list $0.42 per click for Travel and $0.60 across all industries. Booking costs depend on your site and offer.

### Is Instagram or Facebook better for hotels?

It depends on your guests. Pew's 2026 data shows Instagram use of 79% among 18 to 29s and 21% among those 65 and over, while 70% of US adults use Facebook overall.

### Can I repost guest photos?

Only with permission from the guest, and credit them. Ask in a comment or message and keep a record.
