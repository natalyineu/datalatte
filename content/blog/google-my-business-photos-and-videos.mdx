Upload sharp, well-lit JPG or PNG photos of your real premises, products and team to your Google Business Profile, plus short videos. Google accepts photos from 10 KB to 5 MB, recommends 720 by 720 pixels, and takes videos up to 30 seconds and 75 MB at 720p or higher. Google says photos and videos help customers see what you offer. It does not publish a ranking boost for them.

"Google My Business" is the old name. Google's help pages now say Business Profile, and this guide uses that term. The rules below come from Google's help pages. Examples marked as assumptions are illustrations.

## What Are Google's Photo and Video Requirements?

Google's photo guidelines set the technical limits. Photos must be JPG or PNG, between 10 KB and 5 MB. The minimum resolution is 250 by 250 pixels and the recommended one is 720 by 720. Videos can run up to 30 seconds, up to 75 MB, at 720p or higher.

Google also asks for photos that are in focus and well lit, with no significant alterations or heavy filters, showing the business as it really is. Media that breaks the content policy is marked "Not approved" and does not appear on Search or Maps.

## Do Photos Help You Rank in Google Maps?

Google lists adding photos and videos among its tips to improve local ranking, next to verifying, keeping details current and replying to reviews. It does not say how much weight photos carry, and no published Google figure ties a number of photos to a ranking.

Treat them as a conversion tool. Google says its local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Photos do not change distance, but they help a searcher choose between you and the business next door. ## Which Photos Should a Local Business Upload?

Google's photo categories include a logo, a cover photo and business photos. The logo only appears if the profile has a name, valid street address, categories, phone number and opening hours. The cover photo shows at the top, but Google does not guarantee it is the first image shown.

Beyond those, upload what a customer wants to check before visiting:

- The exterior and the entrance, so people recognise the place from the street
- The inside, including seating or waiting areas
- Products or finished work, such as a drink, a haircut or a groomed dog
- The team at work, if staff agree

Hair and beauty businesses should read our guide to [before-and-after photos](/blog/hair-salon-before-after-photos-legal-and-converting) for consent rules.

## Why Do Customer Photos Matter Too?

Customers can add their own. Google says low-quality photos, or photos that do not best represent the business, may be replaced by a user-submitted photo. So a thin gallery gives the public control of your first impression.

Photos also sit beside reviews. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults, 36% said an appealing photo or video accompanying a review matters to them. Ask happy customers to add a photo when they leave a review, but do not offer rewards for reviews, which Google prohibits. See our [review strategy](/blog/google-business-reviews-strategy).

## What Does a Photo Plan Look Like? Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions**. A café wants to refresh its profile in one afternoon and keep it fresh afterwards.

| Item | Assumed plan | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exterior and entrance | 3 photos, day and evening | 15 minutes |
| Interior | 4 photos, empty and busy | 20 minutes |
| Products | 6 photos of top sellers | 30 minutes |
| Video | One 20-second walk-through, under 75 MB | 15 minutes |
| Monthly refresh | 2 new photos | 10 minutes |

That is 14 photos and one video in under 90 minutes, then ten minutes a month. Check the size and format before uploading, and look at your profile a day later to confirm each file shows as approved.

## FAQ

### What size should Google Business Profile photos be?

Use JPG or PNG between 10 KB and 5 MB. Google recommends 720 by 720 pixels and sets a minimum of 250 by 250.

### How long can a Google Business Profile video be?

Up to 30 seconds and 75 MB, at 720p or higher.

### Do more photos improve my local ranking?

Google lists photos as a tip for improving local ranking but does not state how much they matter or how many to upload. Do not expect a fixed number to guarantee results.

### Why was my photo marked Not approved?

Google says media that violates its content policy is marked "Not approved" and does not appear on Search or Maps. Retake it without heavy edits and upload it again.

### Can I use stock photos?

Google asks for photos that show the business as it really is. Stock images of your premises or products are a poor fit for that rule.
