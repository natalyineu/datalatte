For most small businesses, the best marketing automation tool is the cheapest one that sends a welcome email, an appointment or booking reminder, and a lapsed-customer follow-up without manual work. On vendor pricing checked in November 2026, that shortlist is Mailchimp, ActiveCampaign, HubSpot, Brevo, MailerLite and Constant Contact. They differ mainly in free-plan limits, automation depth and how fast the price rises with list size.

## What should a small business automate first?

Start with three automations. A welcome email for new leads sets expectations and carries one clear next step, such as booking or visiting. A reminder before an appointment or class reduces no-shows. A lapsed-customer message after 60 to 90 days gives past buyers a reason to return.

Add more only after those three run cleanly for a month. Extra sequences built on a messy list usually amplify the mess: wrong names, stale offers and messages sent to people who already booked. One well-kept automation is worth more than ten forgotten ones.

## What does marketing automation cost in practice?

**Assumption example.** Assume a salon owner spends 3 hours a week sending reminders and follow-ups by hand, and values that time at $30 an hour. That manual work costs about $360 a month in time. An entry plan at $13 to $20 a month only needs to save roughly half an hour a week to cover its subscription. The inputs here are assumptions for the arithmetic, not measured results; substitute the real hours and rate before deciding.

The same arithmetic explains why premium tiers disappoint at small scale. A $49 to $800 monthly jump only makes sense when the extra features replace paid work or recover bookings that entry tools cannot. List the paid task first, then buy the tier that removes it.

## When does a free plan stop being enough?

Free plans end in one of three ways. The list outgrows the contact cap, which on Mailchimp's free plan is 250 contacts and 500 sends a month. The business needs a multi-step sequence that the free tier limits or excludes. Or reporting gets too thin to tell whether messages produce bookings.

Upgrade when one of those three is measurably true, not when a vendor banner says so. A list of 200 contacts with one welcome email rarely needs a paid tier. A list of 900 contacts with reminders, lapsed-customer flows and A/B testing usually does.

## Which mistakes waste the most money?

The first mistake is automating before the customer journey is written down. If the steps from first contact to booking are unclear, sequences send the same message to new leads, regulars and lapsed customers at once. Write the three stages on paper first, then build one automation per stage.

The second mistake is buying depth that never gets used. Lead scoring, multi-channel journeys and custom reporting only pay when someone reviews them every month. Schedule a 30-minute monthly check: pause stale offers, test one sequence end to end, and confirm lapsed customers who rebooked stop receiving win-back messages.

The third mistake is tracking opens instead of bookings. Apple Mail Privacy Protection pre-opens messages in the background, so open rates overstate reading. Clicks, replies and completed bookings are slower numbers, but they are the ones that pay rent. Retention plays a larger role here; [AI-driven customer retention strategies](/blog/ai-driven-customer-retention-strategies) covers that side in detail.

## FAQ

### What is the best free marketing automation tool for a small business?

For a very small list, Brevo's free plan allows 300 emails a day, and Mailchimp's free plan covers 250 contacts with 500 sends a month, on vendor pricing checked in November 2026. Either runs a welcome email and a simple reminder. Paid tiers matter once list size or sequence depth passes those caps.

### How much should a small business pay for marketing automation?

Entry paid plans from the vendors compared here start between $9 and $20 a month at small list sizes, billed by contacts and period. A sensible ceiling is the value of the manual work replaced. The assumption example above shows the arithmetic: half an hour saved a week roughly covers an entry subscription at a $30 hourly rate.

### Is HubSpot worth it for a very small business?

HubSpot's free CRM suits a business that wants contact records and basic email in one place. Paid Marketing Hub tiers are per-seat and rise steeply at Professional level, which is hard to justify below a few thousand contacts. Most single-site businesses get the same three starter automations from Mailchimp or ActiveCampaign for less.

### What should be automated before anything else?

A welcome email, an appointment or booking reminder, and a lapsed-customer follow-up after 60 to 90 days. Those three cover acquisition, attendance and retention. Add birthday offers, review requests and abandoned-booking flows only after the first three have run cleanly for a month.
