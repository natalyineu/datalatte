A restaurant can retarget with programmatic ads only if enough people visit its website. Google requires at least 100 active visitors in the last 30 days before a website data segment can serve ads on Display or Search, and Meta advises waiting for a few hundred people in a website audience. AdRoll states it has no minimum spend, so budget is rarely the barrier. Traffic is. If your site gets fewer visitors than that, fix Search, your Business Profile and reviews first.

Retargeting means showing ads to people who already visited your site, menu or booking page. This guide is for single-location restaurants and small groups. No source we opened publishes restaurant-specific programmatic returns, so the example below uses labelled assumptions.

## What Does Retargeting Mean for a Restaurant?

It means ads reach people who looked at your menu, reservation or catering page and left without acting. Google's website-visitor segments let you build those lists, and AdRoll lists website segments, contact-list uploads from CRM or CSV files, and others as audience sources. Meta offers website Custom Audiences too, with a default 30-day window that can be extended.

The rule that matters: the list must exist before the ads do. Install the tag, wait for traffic, then launch. A restaurant that gets most of its visits from Google Maps and never loads its own website will build a list slowly.

## How Big Does the Audience Have to Be?

Google's help page says website visitor segments need at least 100 active visitors or users within 30 days on both the Display and Search networks. Membership defaults to 30 days and can run up to 540 days. Meta recommends waiting until you have several hundred people in a website audience before targeting it, per its help centre.

A quick check: look at last month's website users in your analytics. If it is under 100, retargeting will not serve on Google, so spend on getting found first. ## What Should the Ads Say?

One job per ad: reserve, order, or book an event. Show a current offer or the menu item people looked at, your address, and a single button. Exclude people who already ordered or booked, if your platform lets you upload a customer list. Local targeting matters because someone 40 miles away cannot come to dinner, so set a radius around the restaurant.

Keep the retargeting window short for fast decisions, such as tonight's dinner (days), and longer for private dining and catering. Test a 7-day and a 30-day window and compare.

## Worked Example: Is a $300 Test Worth It?

All inputs are **assumptions**, with a CPM inside OwlClaw's published $2 to $5 band for Google's Display network (a secondary source).

| Item | Assumption or calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | Chosen | $300 |
| CPM | Assumption | $4 |
| Impressions | $300 / $4 x 1,000 | 75,000 |
| Click-through rate | Assumption | 0.5% |
| Clicks | 75,000 x 0.5% | 375 |
| Click-to-booking or order | Assumption | 3% |
| Bookings or orders | 375 x 3% | about 11 |
| Cost per booking | $300 / 11.25 | about $27 |

If an average booking brings $60 of margin, the test pays. At a 1% conversion rate the cost per booking would be about $80, and it would not. The list size also caps impressions: a small audience will see the same ad repeatedly, so set a frequency cap. Judge after four weeks, against what Google Search brings per booking.

## FAQ

### Do programmatic ads work for a single restaurant?

They can, for retargeting, if the site has enough traffic. Google needs 100 active visitors within 30 days. No source we opened gives restaurant-specific results.

### How much should a restaurant spend on programmatic?

AdRoll states no minimum spend, so you can start small. Begin with a test like the one above and expand only if cost per booking beats Search.

### Is retargeting creepy?

It depends on frequency and relevance. Cap how often one person sees the ad and exclude people who already converted.

### Should I use delivery-app ads instead?

They are a different channel with commission. See our [delivery budget guide](/blog/restaurant-delivery-marketing-budget-how-to-split).

### What if my website has little traffic?

Build it first through Search, Maps and email. Retargeting needs an audience to retarget.
