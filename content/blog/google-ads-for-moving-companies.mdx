To get leads from Google Ads as a moving company, target only the areas you serve, split local moves and long-distance moves into separate campaigns, send each to a matching landing page, and track phone calls as conversions. Moving is a call-heavy, high-intent category, so call tracking and fast follow-up usually decide whether the campaign is profitable.

If your ads bring in more clicks than customers, the usual causes are loose location settings, broad keywords and no way to tell which clicks became booked jobs.

### Why Location Targeting Matters for Moving Companies

Moving companies live or die by geography. A search like "movers in Austin TX" signals a ready buyer in your area, while a vague national search often brings people you cannot serve. In Google Ads:

1. Open Campaign settings and then Location targeting.
2. Target the cities, counties or radius you actually serve.
3. Choose the option that targets people *in or regularly in* your locations, not just those showing interest, to cut out-of-area clicks.
4. Review the Locations report and exclude areas that spend without converting.

<Callout type="tip">Use city and neighborhood names in your keywords ("movers in Austin TX," "apartment movers downtown"). Local modifiers tend to attract more relevant clicks than generic "moving companies."</Callout>

For long-distance moves, create a separate campaign with keywords such as "long distance movers from Texas to Florida" or "cross country moving company." Send those clicks to a landing page that asks for origin and destination upfront, so you can qualify leads quickly. Check which licenses and registrations your region requires for interstate moves, and display them.

<Callout type="warning">Don't automatically exclude nearby-city searches. Customers just outside your core area may still be profitable if you serve them. Look at your own data before cutting them.</Callout>

### Google Ads Budget for Moving Companies

Cost per click varies widely by city, season and competition, and moving keywords are often among the pricier local-service terms. Use Keyword Planner for your market rather than relying on a national average, then plan like this:

* Start with a budget that buys enough clicks to learn from, and give it several weeks.
* Begin with local moves, where intent is clearest, and add long-distance once you have a baseline.
* Lower bids on low-value segments (for example, small apartment moves outside prime hours) once you have conversion data.
* Pause keywords that spend steadily without producing calls after enough clicks to judge.

<Callout type="coffee">I recommend starting with a small, local-only test before scaling. Many moving companies find that only certain types of leads are truly profitable, and you want to know which before you spend more.</Callout>

If Google Local Services Ads are available for movers in your region, they are worth checking because they charge per lead and can sit above standard search results. Eligibility and rules differ by country and change over time, so confirm in your Google account.

### Ad Copy That Moves

Your ad text should answer the searcher's question fast. Examples to adapt (only claim what is true for your business):

1. **Urgent move:** "Last-minute movers in [City]. Call for same-week availability."
2. **Apartment move:** "Apartment movers in [City]. Licensed and insured. Free quote."
3. **Long distance:** "[State] to [State] movers. Written estimate. Call or request a quote online."

Test two or three variations with different callouts, such as "licensed and insured," "upfront estimates" or "local crews," then keep the winner. Add call, location and review extensions.

### Keywords and Negative Keywords

Start with phrase and exact match around core terms: "movers near me," "local moving company [city]," "apartment movers," "office movers," "long distance movers." Add negatives to filter out do-it-yourself and job-seeker traffic: "jobs," "hiring," "truck rental," "DIY," "boxes for sale," "free," "storage only" if you do not offer it. See our guide to [negative keywords](/blog/negative-keywords-google-ads).

### Track the Right Metrics

Moving ad success is not about clicks. Track these weekly:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Cost per lead | How much each call or quote request costs |
| Lead-to-booking rate | Reveals lead quality and phone follow-up |
| Cost per booked job | The number that determines profit |
| Call duration and source | Shows which keywords bring real customers |

Set a target cost per lead from your own average job value and booking rate rather than a national benchmark. Set up [call tracking](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) and make sure your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) is complete, since reviews influence both ad clicks and map results.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should I budget for moving company Google Ads as a startup?**
A: Enough to collect a meaningful number of clicks in your market over at least a month or two. Competitive metros require more than smaller cities. Start with one metro area and local moves, and expand only after you know your cost per booked job.

**Q: Do I need a separate landing page for each service?**
A: It helps. Local moves, long-distance moves and commercial moves attract different customers, so give each its own page with matching copy and a clear quote form or phone number.

**Q: How do I compete with companies spending far more than me?**
A: Don't try to outspend them. Focus on specific local keywords, strong extensions, honest ad copy, a fast response to every lead, and good reviews.

**Q: Should I use broad match or phrase match keywords?**
A: Start with phrase and exact match. Broad match can attract searches like "moving boxes for sale" unless you maintain strong negatives and review search terms weekly.

**Q: How long does it take to see results from Google Ads?**
A: Clicks and calls can begin within days. The full picture, including booked jobs and revenue, takes longer, especially for long-distance moves with a longer sales cycle, so judge over several weeks.

**Q: What is the biggest mistake moving companies make with ads?**
A: Not tracking phone calls. Without call tracking you cannot tell which keywords produce booked jobs, so you may cut the wrong ones.
