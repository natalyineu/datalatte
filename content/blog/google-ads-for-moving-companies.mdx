Take an $8.50 click, an 8% chance that a click becomes a quote request, and a 30% chance that a quote request becomes a booked move. Every booked move then costs about $354 in ads ($8.50 divided by 0.08 divided by 0.30).

Whether that is a bargain or a disaster depends on one number only you know: what a job earns you after crew, fuel and truck time. This guide shows how to set up the account, which levers move that $354, and how to work out your own break-even click price.

It is written for owner-operators and small fleets in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Dollar figures are US-based and come from agency data, so treat them as orientation and run your own numbers in Keyword Planner.

## What the Benchmarks Say (and How Shaky They Are)

There is no official Google benchmark for movers. What exists comes from agencies and software vendors that sell to movers, so read it as directional.

An MB Adv Agency [moving and storage dataset](https://www.mbadv.agency/ppc-industry-statistics/moving-storage-ppc-statistics) reports:

The same page shows CPC ranging from $3.00 to $45.00 by city. It also states that only 6 city benchmarks were observed directly from campaigns and the other 121 were estimates calibrated to WordStream's per-metro distributions, and the conversion-rate figure rests on just 16 cities. Use it as a ballpark, not a quote.

SmartMoving, a moving-software vendor, published [data from 500 movers](https://www.smartmoving.com/blog/how-much-should-you-spend-on-google-ads-for-your-moving-business) (August 2025). It says the average mover spends $6,300 a month on ads, and by fleet size: $2,780 for 0 to 2 trucks, $2,967 for 3 to 4, and $6,137 for 5 to 10.

It also gives undated, unmethodised estimates of $66 to $200 cost per lead for 2 to 4 trucks. Its worked example uses a $15 click, 8% website conversion and 30% booking rate; the article labels these illustrative.

What to take from it: expect clicks in the high single digits to low teens in many metros, a cost per lead somewhere around $60 to $200, and expect it to be worse in peak season. Your own account will tell you within a few weeks.

## Structure: Three Campaigns, Not One

**1. Local moves.** The simplest and highest-intent campaign. Small area, specific keywords, phone-first.

**2. Long-distance moves.** Different customer, different sales cycle, higher job value, fewer searches. Keep it separate so a few expensive clicks do not distort your local numbers.

**3. Brand and Business Profile terms.** Cheap, defends your name. Keep it tiny.

### Location settings

Pick the cities, counties or radius you actually serve, and exclude the places you do not. Then choose the location option carefully. Google's [location options help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038) says "presence or interest" is the default and recommended option for Search, reaching people in or regularly in your area plus people who have shown interest in it. "Presence only" shows ads only to people likely to be in the area.

For movers this has a real consequence. Someone in another state who searches "movers in Austin" may be exactly the customer you want, because they are relocating into your area. Presence-only would hide your ad from them.

For a local-move campaign, test presence only if out-of-area clicks are wasting money; for a campaign aimed at people moving into your city, keep presence or interest. That is my judgment, not Google's rule, so check your search terms and locations reports after two weeks.

For long-distance work, state your origin and destination in the ad and the landing page. The licence display rules are covered in the long-distance section below.

### Keywords and negatives

Start with phrase and exact match. Build ad groups by intent:

| Ad group | Example keywords |
| --- | --- |
| Local movers | "movers near me", "local movers [city]", "moving company [city]" |
| Property type | "apartment movers [city]", "2 bedroom movers", "office movers [city]" |
| Urgent | "last minute movers", "same day movers [city]" |
| Long distance | "long distance movers [state] to [state]", "cross country movers" |
| Specialty | "piano movers [city]", "packing and moving services" |

Negatives on day one: `jobs`, `hiring`, `careers`, `salary`, `driver jobs`, `truck rental`, `van rental`, `u-haul`, `diy`, `boxes for sale`, `free boxes`, `storage only` (if you do not offer it), `donation`, `cheap` (optional; judge it against your pricing). Add more weekly from the search terms report. Our negative keywords guide shows how.

### Ads that answer the question

Responsive search ads take up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions. Only claim what is true:

- `Movers in [City]. Free Written Quote`
- `Licensed and Insured Local Crew`
- `Apartment and House Moves`
- `Same-Week Availability. Call Now`

Add call and location assets so a mobile searcher can ring you with one tap.

### Local Services Ads: check, don't assume

Google's [help page on how leads work](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435) says Local Services Ads charge per valid lead, that leads judged invalid or low quality are not charged, and that missed calls ending before 20 seconds are not charged. You set a weekly average budget and a monthly maximum follows from it.

That structure suits movers because you pay for contact, not browsing. The Google pages we opened do not list which trades qualify in which country; third-party guides list movers as eligible in the US and Canada, so confirm eligibility in your own account. Our Local Services Ads guide compares them to standard search.

## What Changes for Long-Distance and Interstate Moves?

Keep the campaign separate (see above) and add the legal display rules to your checklist. In the US, the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration rules for interstate household goods moves are in 49 CFR Part 375, titled "Transportation of Household Goods in Interstate Commerce; Consumer Protection Regulations". Under [section 375.101](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/375.101), the part applies to household goods carriers moving goods for individual shippers across state lines.

**What an interstate carrier's ad must show.** [Section 375.207](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/375.207) says advertisements must be "truthful, straightforward, and honest". Every advertisement must include the carrier's name or trade name, matching its DOT registration or the carrier whose authority it uses, and its U.S. DOT number in the form "U.S. DOT No. (assigned number)". The section text does not say how this applies to a short search ad, so confirm with FMCSA or a transport lawyer whether the number belongs in headlines, in descriptions or only on the landing page. The safe route is to put it on the landing page and in the ad text where it fits.

**What a broker's ad must show.** If you sell moves and hand the job to another carrier, [section 371.107](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/371.107) applies. It requires you to prominently display in ads and on your website your physical location, your U.S. DOT registration number and MC licence number, your status as a household goods broker, and the statement that you will not transport an individual shipper's household goods. It also limits which carrier names and logos you may show to those with a written agreement.

**Local moves inside one state.** These are not covered by Part 375. State rules differ and no state's advertising requirements are verified here, so check your state regulator.

**Outside the US.** UK, Canadian and Australian moving licence rules are not verified here. Check your regulator before running long-distance ads there.

**Local Services Ads.** Google's [category list](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841) includes "Moving services" and shows eleven countries in the country selector: Austria, Belgium, Canada, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Spain, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States. The page does not break categories down by country, so check eligibility in your account. Charging works as described above: valid leads only, with a 20-second rule for missed calls.

**What to put in the ad and landing page.**

- Origin and destination: "Austin to Denver Movers".
- Your name as registered and your U.S. DOT number, formatted as above.
- A quote form that asks for origin and destination ZIP codes, move date and home size first.
- A phone number, because many long-distance buyers want to talk first.

**How to judge it.** Long-distance clicks cost more, but a job is also worth more. No long-distance CPC is published by Google or FMCSA. Run the break-even formula from the math section using your own long-distance gross profit per job, and keep the numbers apart from the local campaign.

## Landing Pages: Quote in One Screen

Send local searchers to a page that repeats the search ("Local Movers in [City]"), shows your phone number as a tap-to-call button, and has a short quote form: name, phone, move date, from, to, size. Add real photos of your crew and trucks, your licence or registration details, and two or three reviews.

Keep long-distance on a separate page that asks for origin and destination first. See [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices).

## Track Calls Like Revenue

Movers get most of their leads by phone, so a campaign without call tracking is guesswork. Set up three conversions: quote form submit, calls from ad assets, and calls from the website.

Google's [website call conversions help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) explains that a phone snippet swaps the number on your page for a Google forwarding number, and that a call counts only if it lasts longer than a minimum length you set. Pick a threshold that matches a real enquiry.

See call tracking for local business and the conversion tracking setup guide.

Then close the loop by hand: log which leads booked and what each job earned. The ad platform counts leads; only your booking sheet knows revenue.

## The Math

Assume a $3,000 monthly budget, which sits inside the $2,000 to $3,000 SmartMoving suggests for 2 to 4 trucks. Every input below is an **assumption** to replace with your own data. The Base CPC and lead rate are the MB Adv medians, and the 30% booking rate is SmartMoving's illustrative figure.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $3,000 | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $12.00 | $8.50 | $6.00 |
| Clicks | 250 | 353 | 500 |
| Click to lead (assumption) | 5% | 8% | 10% |
| Leads | 12.5 | 28 | 50 |
| Cost per lead | $240 | $106 | $60 |
| Lead to booked move (assumption) | 20% | 30% | 40% |
| Booked moves | 2.5 | 8.5 | 20 |
| Ad cost per booked move | $1,200 | $354 | $150 |
| Gross profit per move (assumption) | $450 | $450 | $450 |
| Monthly gross profit minus ad spend | -$1,875 | +$812 | +$6,000 |

Gross profit per move is what is left after crew wages, fuel and truck costs, before the ads. $450 is a placeholder, for example 30% on a $1,500 job. Use your real figure.

Your **break-even click price** is gross profit per move x lead rate x booking rate. In the Base case that is $450 x 0.08 x 0.30 = $10.80. Pay more than that on average and each click loses money. In the Tough case your ceiling is $450 x 0.05 x 0.20 = $4.50, far below the $12 click, which is why that column loses money.

Two takeaways. First, the booking rate swings results as hard as the click price: going from 20% to 40% halves your cost per move without touching the ad account. That is a phone-answering and estimate-follow-up problem. Second, if your gross profit per move is $200, the Base case loses money, so check your margins before you scale spend.

## Weekly Routine

1. Read the search terms report; add negatives.
2. Check the locations report; exclude areas that spend without leads.
3. Pause keywords that spent about twice your target cost per lead with no leads.
4. Compare local and long-distance cost per booked move separately.
5. Match ad hours to when someone answers the phone.

Seasonality matters. Several vendor sources report that moving demand and CPCs rise in the summer months; compare your months against your own last year rather than a national number. Similar logic applies in related home-and-property trades; see [Google Ads for property management](/blog/google-ads-for-property-management) and [Google Ads for window companies](/blog/google-ads-for-window-companies).

## Do This Today

1. Work out your gross profit per move and your average job value for local and long-distance separately.
2. Calculate your break-even click price with the formula above, then compare it with Keyword Planner estimates for your service area.
3. Build one local-move Search campaign with five tight ad groups, phrase and exact match, and the negative list above.
4. Create a tap-to-call quote page and set up form, ad-call and website-call conversions before switching ads on.
5. Start a booking log (lead source, quoted price, booked yes/no, final job profit) and set a rule to answer every lead within 15 minutes.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a moving company spend on Google Ads per month?**
A: SmartMoving's 2025 data reports an average of $6,300 a month across movers, and $2,780 to $2,967 for companies with up to four trucks. Treat that as what others spend, not what you should. Start with what your break-even math supports and scale only campaigns that show a profitable cost per booked move.

**Q: What is a good cost per lead for movers?**
A: MB Adv reports a median of $82.50 across 93 cities, and SmartMoving estimates $66 to $200 for small fleets. Both are vendor figures. A good cost per lead is one below your break-even: gross profit per move x booking rate.

**Q: Should I use Local Services Ads or regular Google Ads?**
A: Try both if movers are eligible in your country. Local Services Ads charge per valid lead, while search ads charge per click. Compare by cost per booked move, not cost per lead.

**Q: Do I need separate landing pages for local and long-distance moves?**
A: Yes. They attract different searchers and need different questions on the form.

**Q: How long until Google Ads brings bookings?**
A: Calls can start within days. Judge cost per booked move after four to six weeks, my rule of thumb, longer for long-distance moves with slower decisions.
