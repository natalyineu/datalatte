A cleaning company website should let a visitor request a quote or book a clean in under a minute on a phone, explain what each service includes, and make recurring service the easy choice. Solo and new cleaning businesses with under 25 clients are advised by [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-cleaning-janitorial-services) to plan a total monthly ad budget of $1,000-$2,300, aiming for 15-35 quote requests. The site has to convert those requests, so its booking step, service pages and mobile speed matter as much as the ads.

## What Should a Cleaning Website Show First?

State the service, the area and the next step in one line, such as "Home cleaning in your city. Get a price in 60 seconds." Then show the single button that starts a quote or booking, with your phone number beside it for people who prefer to call. Keep the top of the page focused on that one action; the guide to [above-the-fold design for local businesses](/blog/above-the-fold-design-local-business) explains how to order these elements.

Add the details visitors check before they let someone into their home: what each clean includes, what to prepare, whether you bring supplies, your service area and your cancellation terms. Do not publish insurance, bonding or background-check claims unless you hold them and can show proof.

Mobile tracking matters too. Adwave's mobile-first guidance states that each tap-to-call and booking link should log an event in analytics, so you can see which buttons people actually use.

## Should You Use a Quote Form or Instant Booking?

It depends on how standard your jobs are. Instant booking works for routine home cleans priced by size, while a quote form suits deep cleans, move-outs and commercial offices where the price depends on the site. Call-first pages suit urgent or complex work, but they lose visitors who will not phone a stranger.

| Approach | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instant online booking with fixed prices | Standard home cleans by size | Needs clear pricing rules and add-ons |
| Quote request form | Deep cleans, move-outs, offices | Slower; you must reply fast |
| Call-first | Urgent or complex jobs | Misses visitors who will not call |

Online booking has strong demand behind it. A 2025 Eliciting Insights survey, cited by [hibu](https://hibu.com/blog/industries/8-essential-dog-grooming-marketing-strategies) (secondary), reports that 90% of customers prefer booking appointments online, though that figure comes from a grooming-focused article and should be tested against your own enquiries. Change one thing at a time and track whether requests or completed bookings rise.

## How Much Should You Spend on Ads?

Budgets scale with how many active clients you already serve. Adwave's tiers give a starting point for planning, not a guarantee of results.

| Business stage (Adwave) | Recommended monthly spend | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo or new, under 25 active clients | Google Ads (Search + LSA) $400-$1,000; total $1,000-$2,300 | 15-35 quote requests |
| Growing residential, 25-100 clients | CTV $1,500-$2,500 as a starting point; total $3,300-$7,100 | 40-90 quote requests |
| Established residential or commercial, 100+ clients | Total $7,500-$17,000 | Not stated |

Adwave also gives a hypothetical that frames the value of recurring work: a channel producing 30 quotes that convert to 5 one-time jobs is worse than one producing 12 quotes that convert to 6 recurring weekly clients. Fewer, better-qualified enquiries can outperform volume. For search terms to bid on, see the [keyword guide for cleaning services](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-cleaning-services-in-2026).

## Do Local Service Ads Work for Cleaners?

Local Services Ads (LSA) are one route to leads. Get-Ryze reports a typical cost per lead of $8-$25 for cleaning services. Carpet cleaning appears among the categories that [ContractorMarketingPros](https://contractormarketingpros.net/blog/best-digital-advertising-platforms-for-contractors/) (secondary) lists as eligible for LSAs, so check Google's live help page for house cleaning eligibility in your area before you plan around it.

Your site still matters after the click. A lead who lands on a slow page with no clear price or booking step is wasted spend, so the page needs to be ready before the ads start.

## How Fast Should a Cleaning Site Load?

Speed affects whether people stay. Adwave cites Google's mobile site speed playbook, which reports that mobile pages loading one second faster achieved up to a 27% increase in conversion rates, based on the SOASTA study (secondary, as retold by Adwave). The exact lift varies by site, but the direction is clear for mobile visitors booking a clean from a phone.

Before-and-after photos are often the heaviest part of a cleaning site. Compress them, use descriptive file names, and keep the booking form on the first screen. Google indexes the mobile version of your site, so the mobile page should carry the same service information as the desktop one.

## How Do You Make Recurring Clients the Default?

Offer weekly, every-two-weeks and monthly options in the booking form, and show the one-off clean second. If you discount recurring service, state the discount plainly and check that your margin still holds after labour and supplies. Ask for a card or payment method at booking so repeat visits do not depend on chasing invoices.

Recurring work also changes the site's job. The homepage should explain the benefit of a regular schedule, not only the price of a single deep clean. ## Worked Example: What One Month of Ads Could Return

All inputs below are **assumptions** for illustration, not benchmarks.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend | $450 |
| Cost per lead | $15 (within the $8-$25 LSA range cited by Get-Ryze) |
| Leads per month | 30 ($450 / $15) |
| Share of leads that book | 20%, so 6 new clients |
| Share of new clients who go recurring | 50%, so 3 recurring and 3 one-time |
| Recurring clean | $150, every two weeks, 26 cleans a year, kept 12 months |
| Recurring value per client | 26 x $150 = $3,900 |
| Total first-year revenue from the month's clients | 3 x $3,900 + 3 x $150 = $12,150 |

This is gross revenue. It excludes labour, supplies, software and any churn beyond the 12-month assumption, so the real return will be lower. The useful check is whether your own close rate and recurring share match these inputs. Replace the assumptions with your actual numbers before deciding on a budget.

## Should You Build the Site Yourself or Hire a Developer?

A booking-first cleaning site with a few service pages is within reach of a DIY builder, but the choice depends on how much custom logic you need, such as zone-based pricing or multi-step quote forms. The comparison in [DIY website vs professional developer](/blog/diy-website-vs-professional-developer) sets out the trade-offs. Whichever route you take, test the form on a phone before you pay for ads to send traffic to it.

## FAQ

### How much should a cleaning company spend on ads each month?

Adwave recommends a total monthly budget of $1,000-$2,300 for solo or new cleaning businesses with under 25 active clients, aiming for 15-35 quote requests. Growing residential businesses with 25-100 clients are advised to plan $3,300-$7,100 a month.

### Should I use a quote form or instant booking?

Use instant booking for standard home cleans priced by size, and a quote form for deep cleans, move-outs and commercial work. No independent data was found showing which converts better for cleaners, so test one change at a time.

### What does a Local Services Ad cost for a cleaning business?

Get-Ryze reports a typical cost per lead of $8-$25 for cleaning services. Check Google's live help page for eligibility in your area before you rely on LSAs.

### Does site speed affect bookings?

Adwave cites a SOASTA study in Google's playbook showing up to a 27% conversion increase for mobile pages that load one second faster. Treat this as a directional benchmark, and measure your own pages.

### Should I list insurance on my website?

Only if you hold it. Show proof on request and do not publish bonding or background-check claims you cannot back up.
