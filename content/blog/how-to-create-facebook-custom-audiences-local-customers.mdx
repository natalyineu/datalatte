To use Facebook Custom Audiences to reach past customers, upload your customer list (emails and phone numbers) to Meta Ads Manager, let Meta match it to Facebook and Instagram accounts, and then run ads only to those people. It is one of the cheapest ways to bring lapsed customers back, because you are speaking to people who already know you.

For a local business, this matters more than most owners realise. Your past customers have already driven to your door, paid you, and (hopefully) liked the experience. Reminding them you exist usually costs far less than convincing a stranger. Below is how the setup works, what to upload, which messages tend to work, and the mistakes that waste budget.

## What a Custom Audience actually is

A Custom Audience is a saved group of people that you define using your own data instead of Meta's interest or demographic targeting. Meta supports several sources:

- **Customer list:** a CSV of emails, phone numbers, names and postcodes from your booking system, POS or email platform.
- **Website activity:** people who visited your site, based on the Meta Pixel or Conversions API.
- **Engagement:** people who interacted with your Facebook Page, Instagram profile, videos, or lead forms.
- **App activity and offline events:** less relevant for most small local businesses.

For past customers, the customer list is the starting point. Engagement and website audiences are a useful second layer for warm prospects who never booked.

## Step 1: Build a clean customer list

Your audience is only as good as the file you upload. Export from wherever your customers actually live: Square, Fresha, Vagaro, Mindbody, Shopify, Mailchimp, or even a spreadsheet of booking confirmations.

1. Include as many identifiers as you have: email, phone number, first name, last name, city, postcode and country. More fields generally means a better match rate.
2. Use international phone formats (with country code) and remove obvious junk like test entries.
3. Add a "value" column if you want to build value-based lookalikes later (for example, total spend).
4. Keep separate files for separate groups: all customers, customers in the last 90 days, customers who have not visited in six months or more, and your top spenders.

Match rates vary a lot. Email-only lists often match at a lower rate than lists that also contain phone numbers and names, so do not be alarmed if Meta matches only a portion of your file.

A note on permission: Meta requires that you have the right to use the data for advertising, and privacy laws differ across the US, UK, Australia and Canada. If you collected the details for a booking, check that your privacy policy mentions marketing use, and honour opt-outs. When in doubt, ask a local legal adviser.

## Step 2: Upload the list in Ads Manager

1. Open Meta Ads Manager, then Audiences.
2. Click Create audience, then Custom audience, then Customer list.
3. Choose whether to include customer value, upload your CSV, and map each column to the right identifier.
4. Name the audience clearly, for example "Customers - lapsed 6m+ - May 2026". You will thank yourself later.
5. Meta hashes the data in your browser before upload and then matches it. Processing usually takes a short while.

Meta generally needs a minimum number of matched people (commonly around 100) before an audience can be used for ads. A single-location shop with a small list may fall short, in which case combine groups or add engagement audiences.

## Step 3: Decide what each audience is for

Different groups need different messages. Here is a simple framework.

| Audience | Goal | Example offer |
|---|---|---|
| Active customers (last 90 days) | Increase frequency, upsell | New menu item, membership, add-on service |
| Lapsed customers (6+ months) | Win back | "We have missed you" offer, new service announcement |
| Top spenders | Retain and reward | Early access, VIP event, referral ask |
| Instagram and Facebook engagers | Convert warm interest | First-visit offer |
| Website visitors who did not book | Close the gap | Reminder with a clear booking button |

For acquisition campaigns aimed at new people, add your customer list as an **exclusion** so you do not pay to show a first-visit discount to people who already visit you.

## Step 4: Create ads that fit past customers

Past customers do not need your whole origin story. They need a reason to come back and a frictionless way to do it.

- **Coffee shops:** a seasonal drink, a loyalty bonus, or a reminder about a new location or hours. See our guide on [coffee shop Facebook ads](/blog/facebook-ads-for-coffee-shops) for creative ideas.
- **Hair salons and barbershops:** "It has been a while" rebooking prompts, new stylists, or slow-day availability.
- **Pet groomers:** coat-type reminders and seasonal shedding or summer cut messaging, timed roughly to your usual grooming cycle.
- **Fitness studios:** reactivation offers for lapsed members, a new class launch, or a challenge.

Keep the copy short, name the offer in the first line, and use a real photo of your space or team. Use a call-to-action button that matches the goal, such as Book Now, Learn More, or Send Message. Make sure the landing page works well on a phone, because most of this traffic will come from mobile.

## Step 5: Set a sensible local budget

You do not need a large budget for small lists. Many local businesses start with something in the range of $5 to $15 per day (or the equivalent in GBP, AUD or CAD) and judge results after a couple of weeks rather than a couple of days. Small audiences can saturate quickly, meaning the same people see the ad too often, so watch the frequency figure in your reports and refresh the creative if it climbs. ## Step 6: Layer in a Pixel and lookalikes

Once your customer list is working, two upgrades are worth considering:

- **Install the Meta Pixel** (and ideally the Conversions API) so website visitors become an audience too. Our [Facebook Pixel setup guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-setup-guide) covers it step by step.
- **Build a lookalike audience** from your best customers, which tells Meta to find people with similar characteristics in your area. Read our [Facebook lookalike audiences guide](/blog/facebook-lookalike-audiences-guide) for how to seed one properly.

If you want a non-ad way to reach the same people, a short email win-back sequence often pairs well. See [customer reactivation campaigns](/blog/customer-reactivation-campaigns).

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Uploading an old, unclean list full of duplicates and typos.
- Using one message for everyone instead of segmenting.
- Forgetting to exclude current customers from new-customer offers.
- Judging results after two days.
- Sending people to a slow or confusing booking page.
- Not refreshing the list. Customer lists do not update themselves, so re-upload every month or two or connect a data source if your tools allow it.

## Measuring whether it worked

The best measure is simple: did past customers come back? Use a unique offer code, a dedicated booking link with a UTM tag, or ask at the counter "how did you hear about this?" Compare against what you would normally expect from that group. Reach and click-through rate are helpful signals, but bookings and revenue are what pay the bills.

## FAQ

**Q: How many customers do I need for a Custom Audience?**
A: Meta typically needs a minimum of around 100 matched people to use an audience, so a list of a few hundred contacts is a safer starting point. Smaller lists can be combined with engagement audiences.

**Q: Is it legal to upload my customers' emails to Facebook?**
A: It depends on how you collected the data and on local privacy laws. You generally need a lawful basis and a privacy policy that covers marketing use, and Meta requires you to confirm you have permission. Check with a local adviser if you are unsure.

**Q: How often should I update my customer list?**
A: Every month or two is a good rhythm for most local businesses, and sooner after a big promotion or event. Stale lists lead to wasted impressions on people who have already moved or unsubscribed.

**Q: Will Custom Audiences work if I only have emails?**
A: Yes, but match rates are often lower than with phone numbers and names added. Add whatever fields you legitimately hold to improve matching.
