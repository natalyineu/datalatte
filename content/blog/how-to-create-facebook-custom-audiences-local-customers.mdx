To build a Facebook Custom Audience from past customers, create the audience in Ads Manager, not from your Facebook Page, then target ads at the people on your list. Facebook's Custom Audiences tool lets you retarget anyone who has interacted with your business, and a lookalike audience needs a seed of at least 100 users. KlientBoost cites an ideal custom audience of 1,000 to 5,000 users, so a small local list will reach fewer people. Check upload formats and matching rules on Meta's live help page, because the steps change.

## What Can a Customer-List Audience Do for a Local Business?

Custom audiences must be created through Ads Manager rather than from a Facebook Page, according to [AdEspresso](https://adespresso.com/guides/facebook-ads-beginner/facebook-ads-types/) (secondary). The tool lets you retarget people who have interacted with your business, both on your Facebook Page and elsewhere, as [Benchmark Email](https://www.benchmarkemail.com/blog/tools-for-retargeting-ads/) (secondary) describes it.

For a local shop or clinic, the practical uses are win-back offers for lapsed clients, announcements for new services, and exclusions so existing clients do not see first-visit promotions. Brolik describes a related option: Customer Value Audiences, where you add lifetime spend or another ranking to a list so Meta can weight higher-value customers, [as its Maximize Your Ad Spend article explains](https://brolik.com/blog/maximize-spend-facebook-lookalike-audiences) (secondary).

If you want to compare platforms before committing budget, [Google Ads versus Facebook Ads for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) sets out where each channel tends to fit.

## How Many Customers Do You Need?

A lookalike audience needs a custom audience of at least 100 users, according to [KlientBoost](https://www.klientboost.com/facebook/facebook-lookalike-audiences/) (secondary). The same page says the ideal custom audience ranges from 1,000 to 5,000 users, citing a Facebook help article. [Leadsync](https://leadsync.me/blog/facebook-ads-for-contractors/) (secondary) puts the practical floor lower, saying a list of even 100 past customers works.

Those two figures describe different things. The 100-user minimum is the threshold for building a lookalike at all. The 1,000 to 5,000 range is the size KlientBoost calls ideal. A list under 1,000 can still be used, but expect a narrower reach than the ideal range suggests.

KlientBoost also notes that a 1% lookalike most closely matches your custom audience, and larger percentages match less closely but reach more people. Lookalikes work best when you already have good customer data, so a clean list matters more than a long one.

## Which Lists Should a Local Business Build?

Match each list to one goal, and keep the windows short enough to be useful. The table below is a starting checklist, not a Meta rule.

| List | Goal | Check before uploading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clients seen in the last 90 days | Repeat booking | Last visit date is filled in for every row |
| Clients not seen for 6+ months | Win-back | Consent to contact is recorded |
| High-spend clients | Retention | Spend column is accurate |
| All clients | Exclusion | Duplicates are removed |

UpSwell's fitness marketing page describes a similar split into new customers, win-backs, loyal customers and at-risk customers, [as a segmentation approach](https://upswellmarketing.com/industry/fitness) (secondary). Adstellar advises retargeting people who engaged with your Page or Instagram profile in the past 30 days, which can be a second audience alongside your client list ([Adstellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/meta-ads-for-local-businesses), secondary).

## What Does a Worked Budget Look Like?

Every input below is an assumption, so swap in your own numbers before deciding anything. The goal is to test whether a win-back campaign can pay for itself.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Past clients in your export | 800 (assumption) |
| Share with usable contact details | 60% (assumption) |
| Matched contacts in the audience | 480 (assumption) |
| Monthly budget | $600 (assumption) |
| Share held for testing | 20% = $120 (per Adstellar guidance) |
| Share for proven audiences | 80% = $480 |
| Bookings from the campaign | 15 (assumption) |
| Cost per booking | $40 ($600 divided by 15) |
| Average booking value | $90 (assumption) |
| Revenue from 15 bookings | $1,350 (assumption) |
| Return on spend | 2.25x (assumption-based) |

The 480 contacts clear the 100-user minimum for a lookalike, but they sit below the 1,000 to 5,000 range KlientBoost calls ideal, so start with retargeting and a small lookalike test. The 20/80 split follows the testing guidance in Adstellar's local Meta Ads guide.

If your real booking value is $40 or less, this campaign loses money at these inputs. Run it for two weeks, measure bookings against spend, and only scale if cost per booking sits well below your margin. A useful test design is to split the audience evenly. Meta's A/B testing help page says even splits keep audiences [statistically comparable, while informal testing can lead to overlapping audiences](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669) (primary).

## What Consent and Data Rules Apply?

Google's Customer Match policy requires first-party consent for custom audiences built from its own lead data, as [Google's small business marketing article](https://business.google.com/us/resources/articles/small-business-marketing/) states (primary). The same caution is sensible for a Facebook list: only upload people who gave you their details for marketing or whose use your local law allows.

WasteNot notes that Meta pixel retargeting only reaches users who visited your properties in the last 180 days, and that the pixel must appear in your cookie policy under GDPR when it tracks user activity ([WasteNot](https://www.wastenot.io/blog/retargeting-facebook-ads), secondary). UK businesses should confirm the current UK GDPR wording with a local adviser rather than rely on the older reference.

Remove anyone who has asked not to be contacted before every upload. Keeping a clean suppression list protects the account and your sender reputation.

## How Do You Keep the Audience Fresh?

Adstellar recommends refreshing lookalike audiences quarterly as the customer list grows ([Adstellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/meta-ads-for-local-businesses), secondary). Refresh the seed list too, so people who have since left are dropped and new clients are added.

Watch frequency. Adstellar's ecommerce guide says frequency above 3 to 4 in retargeting signals audience fatigue, and that most ecommerce ads fatigue after 2 to 4 weeks of consistent delivery, especially in retargeting ([Adstellar ecommerce guide](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/meta-advertising-for-ecommerce-brands), secondary). Those thresholds come from ecommerce, so treat them as a prompt to check, not a local rule.

Rotate creative every few weeks. A small local list has fewer people to show ads to, so the same faces see the same message quickly.

## Do Email Subscribers Work as a Source List?

Email lists often make good seeds, but only when the engagement is healthy. Brolik says a list with 30% or more open rates or 20% or more click rates is strong ([Brolik](https://brolik.com/blog/maximize-spend-facebook-lookalike-audiences), secondary). Cleverly gives reactivation benchmarks: a 12 to 15% open rate is healthy for win-back campaigns, and below 8% signals a subject line or list quality problem ([Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/customer-reactivation-campaign-strategies), secondary).

Unsubscribe rate is the other check. Cleverly says an unsubscribe rate above 0.5% means your message does not match the list ([Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/customer-reactivation-campaign-strategies), secondary). If your email list fails those tests, clean it before using it as an audience source.

## What Results Have Local Businesses Reported?

ARC's case study for a New York salon recorded 53 appointments from Meta retargeting within 30 days, for the period April 18 to May 17, 2023 ([ARC](https://arccreativeco.com/what-a-salon-booking-costs-on-google-ads/), secondary). That is one business in one period, so use it as a sense of scale, not a forecast.

Brolik reported a 5.05% click-through rate at $0.21 per click and a 1.16% conversion rate for a lookalike built from a client's customer list and prior leads ([Brolik](https://brolik.com/blog/maximize-spend-facebook-lookalike-audiences), secondary). Those are agency results, not guarantees. Compare any figure against your own bookings.

## FAQ

### Do I need a Facebook Page to create a custom audience?

You create custom audiences in Ads Manager. AdEspresso says custom audiences and catalogs must be made through Ads Manager rather than from a Facebook Page ([AdEspresso](https://adespresso.com/guides/facebook-ads-beginner/facebook-ads-types/), secondary).

### How many customers do I need for a lookalike?

KlientBoost says the custom audience must contain at least 100 users, and it cites an ideal range of 1,000 to 5,000 users ([KlientBoost](https://www.klientboost.com/facebook/facebook-lookalike-audiences/), secondary).

### How far back can pixel retargeting reach?

WasteNot says the Meta pixel only lets you retarget users who visited your properties within the last 180 days ([WasteNot](https://www.wastenot.io/blog/retargeting-facebook-ads), secondary).

### How often should I refresh a lookalike audience?

Adstellar recommends refreshing lookalike audiences quarterly as your customer list grows ([Adstellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/meta-ads-for-local-businesses), secondary).
