Neither Glovo nor Wolt is better for every restaurant. The better choice is the app your local customers already open, at the lower total cost per order in your contract. Neither platform publishes one fixed commission: Wolt says its fees depend on your market and your agreement ([Wolt merchant help](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/aut/learning-center/wolt-merchant-fees-and-commissions), checked Oct 2026), so compare the written offers you are actually quoted.

It is 7:40 on a Saturday night. Two tablets chirp on the pass, one orange, one blue. Your best cook is boxing a pizza for an app that takes a cut, and a walk-in waits at the counter. Nobody asks the real question at signup: after the cut, what did that box earn you? In the example below, a 10-point gap in commission on a EUR 28 order costs EUR 2.80 per order, or EUR 560 a month at 200 orders. That is more than many owners spend on marketing in a month.

## Glovo vs Wolt at a Glance

Commission rates vary by country, city, delivery method and contract, and neither company publishes a single rate card for restaurants. Wolt describes several commission types: a standard rate for orders delivered by its couriers, a slightly higher rate (or fixed fee, depending on the market) for Wolt+ subscriber orders, and a reduced rate for takeaway orders. It also lists a platform fee and penalties for delayed orders or missing items ([Wolt merchant help](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/aut/learning-center/wolt-merchant-fees-and-commissions), checked Oct 2026). Glovo offers paid advertising for partners; its Partner Ads product for small and medium restaurants is sold on a pay-per-click basis ([CIO Africa](https://cioafrica.co/glovo-launches-glovo-ads-for-its-partners/), checked Oct 2026).

| Factor | Glovo | Wolt |
|--------|-------|------|
| Commission | Set in your contract; ask for a written quote | Set in your agreement; standard, Wolt+ and takeaway rates differ |
| Extra services | In-app advertising (Glovo Ads) | Wolt+ subscriber orders, takeaway orders |
| Contract flexibility | Check for exclusivity clauses and notice periods | Check for exclusivity clauses and notice periods |
| Best for | Cities where Glovo is the habit app | Cities where Wolt is the habit app |

These are directional descriptions, not quotes. The only number that matters is the one in your contract, and a headline percentage means nothing until you know what it is charged on.

## The Math: What One Order Really Earns

Example only. These are assumed numbers, not Glovo or Wolt rates and not a market average. Swap in your own.

Assumptions: average basket EUR 28, food and packaging cost 32% of the basket (EUR 8.96), 200 delivery orders a month.

| Commission you are quoted (assumed) | Commission per order | Left after food cost | Per month (200 orders) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | EUR 5.60 | EUR 13.44 | EUR 2,688 |
| 25% | EUR 7.00 | EUR 12.04 | EUR 2,408 |
| 30% | EUR 8.40 | EUR 10.64 | EUR 2,128 |

The 20% and 30% offers differ by EUR 2.80 per order, so EUR 560 a month. That is before rent, wages, courier costs or a single penalty.

Now the promotion trap. You fund a 20% discount on that EUR 28 order, so the customer pays EUR 22.40. If commission is charged at 25% on the discounted price, you pay EUR 5.60 and keep EUR 7.84 after food cost. If it is charged on the pre-discount EUR 28, you pay EUR 7.00 and keep EUR 6.44. Same order, EUR 1.40 apart, 200 times a month: EUR 280.

## Which Platform Has Lower Fees?

Neither is cheaper in every market. Cost depends on whether you use platform couriers or your own, how much you spend on promotions, and how hard you negotiate. Two restaurants on the same street can hold different agreements.

Line up the same items in every offer:

- Commission percentage and what it applies to (food only, or food plus tax and fees), including any separate Wolt+ or takeaway rate
- Payment processing fee, if itemized separately
- Costs of promoted listings, discounts and banners
- Penalty rules for late preparation, cancellations and missing items
- Minimum contract length and exclusivity terms

Ask each sales rep for a written proposal. Then calculate cost per order with the table above.

## How to Choose Based on Your Restaurant Type

### High-volume, low-margin (pizza, fast food)

Margins are thin, so every point of commission matters. Request a volume-based rate and ask whether a lower rate applies to orders your own couriers deliver. Run each platform for a few weeks and compare net profit per order, not order counts.

### Premium or specialty (sushi, steakhouse, vegan)

Higher baskets soften the commission, but delivery quality matters more. Look at packaging standards, courier quality and reviews in your city. Ask each rep how a specialty restaurant gets featured in search and collections.

### Cafes and bakeries

Speed is everything for hot drinks and pastries. Place test orders from a competitor's listing on each app and time them in your own neighborhood. If you sell packaged goods such as beans or gift boxes, ask whether the platform supports retail-style listings. For more on this niche, see our guide to [coffee shop marketing ideas](/blog/coffee-shop-marketing-ideas-30-strategies-to-fill-every-seat-in-2026) and the [coffee shop marketing page](/for/coffee-shops).

### Single location vs small chain

A single location needs a responsive account manager, so ask who yours is and how fast support replies. Small chains should negotiate one rate across all locations and check that menus can be managed centrally.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Accepting the default commission.** Terms differ between restaurants. Show your order volume, strong ratings or a competing offer, and ask for a written proposal and a trial period.

**Setting the delivery radius too wide.** Far orders arrive slower, which means colder food and worse reviews. Track where orders come from for two weeks and shrink the radius to the area that supplies most of them.

**Copying the dine-in menu.** Long menus slow ordering. Build a shorter delivery menu with dishes that travel well, clear photos and a bestsellers section on top. Our guide to [menu optimisation for delivery platforms](/blog/restaurant-menu-optimisation-delivery-platforms) covers this step by step.

**Running five promotions at once.** Run one offer at a time, such as a first-order discount, and compare new customers acquired against what it cost you. If you cannot tell which offer worked, none of them did.

**Not reading reviews and refund data.** Ratings influence placement and trust. Review item-level feedback weekly and fix any dish that is criticized twice.

<Callout type="warning">Watch out for commission being charged on the pre-discount order value. If you fund a promotion, check on your invoice whether you pay commission on the full price or the discounted price.</Callout>

## Hidden Costs Beyond the Commission

Check your contract for these:

- **Payment processing or service fees**, which may be itemized separately.
- **Promotion costs.** Featured placement and discounts come out of your margin.
- **Late-preparation or cancellation penalties.** Set realistic preparation times in your profile.
- **Refunds and missing-item claims.** Platforms may charge these back to you, so good packaging and a pickup quality check matter.
- **Tax treatment.** Ask whether commission is calculated on the order value including or excluding tax.

## Can You Use Both Platforms?

Yes, many restaurants list on several apps. You gain reach and you pay in complexity: duplicate tablets, menu mismatches, missed orders. If you go multi-platform, use a POS integration or order aggregator, and read your contract for exclusivity clauses that trade a lower rate for being listed only there. ## Reduce Your Dependence on Delivery Apps

Whichever you pick, move repeat customers to your own channels: a good Google Business Profile, a simple ordering page, and an email or SMS list. Every order that arrives that way carries no commission. See [delivery app marketing strategy for restaurants](/blog/delivery-app-marketing-strategy-restaurants) for practical ideas.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Glovo or Wolt cheaper for restaurants?**
A: It depends on your country, your volume and your contract. Both charge a commission set in your contract, plus optional paid promotion. Request written proposals from both and compare total cost per order.

**Q: Can I use Glovo and Wolt at the same time?**
A: Usually yes, unless your contract includes an exclusivity clause in exchange for a lower rate. Running both increases reach but needs a clear process for menus, prices and order handling.

**Q: Which platform brings more orders to a new restaurant?**
A: It depends on which app your local customers already use. Check which platform is dominant in your city, look at how many competing restaurants are listed in your category, and run a short trial on each.

**Q: How much should I spend on in-app promotions?**
A: Start small and measure. Test a single offer for a few weeks, track new customers and profit after commission, and only scale what pays back. Glovo sells its partner ads on a pay-per-click basis, so set a daily cap; check current promotion pricing in your dashboard.

**Q: Can I negotiate the commission rate?**
A: Ask. Terms are set in your agreement, so decent order volume, good ratings or a competing offer gives you a case for a trial period or volume tiers. Get anything agreed in writing.
