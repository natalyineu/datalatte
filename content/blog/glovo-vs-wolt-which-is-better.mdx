Neither Glovo nor Wolt is better for every restaurant. Wolt tends to suit restaurants in cities where it has strong local brand awareness and a loyal user base, while Glovo often suits owners who want wider market coverage or extra services such as grocery-style listings. Commission rates are negotiable on both platforms and differ by country, so the right answer comes from comparing the offers you are actually quoted.

## Glovo vs Wolt at a Glance

Published commission rates change often and vary by country, city, delivery method and contract. As a typical range, delivery platforms in Europe charge restaurants somewhere in the region of 15% to 35% of the order value, with lower rates for pick-up orders and higher rates for orders using the platform's couriers and promotion tools. Always check current pricing with each platform's sales team before you decide.

| Factor | Glovo | Wolt |
|--------|-------|------|
| Typical commission | Often in the mid-20s to 30s % range for platform delivery; negotiable | Often in the 20s to low 30s % range for platform delivery; negotiable |
| Coverage | Strong across Southern and Eastern Europe, parts of Africa and Central Asia | Strong in the Nordics, Central and Eastern Europe, Germany and Israel |
| Extra services | Grocery and retail listings, in-app ads | In-app promotions, Wolt+ subscription, promoted placement |
| Contract flexibility | Check for exclusivity clauses and notice periods | Check for exclusivity clauses and notice periods |
| Best for | Owners who want broad reach and retail-style add-ons | Owners in markets where Wolt is the habit app |

These are directional descriptions, not quotes. The only number that matters is the one in your contract.

## Which Platform Has Lower Fees?

Neither platform is cheaper in every market. Rates depend on whether you use platform couriers or your own, how much you spend on promotions, and how hard you negotiate. Two restaurants on the same street can pay different rates.

When you compare offers, line up the same items:

- Commission percentage and what it applies to (food only, or food plus tax and fees)
- Payment processing fee, if itemized separately
- Costs of promoted listings, discounts and banners
- Penalty rules for late preparation, cancellations and missing items
- Minimum contract length and exclusivity terms

Ask each sales rep for a written proposal and calculate your cost per order, not just the headline percentage.

## How to Choose Based on Your Restaurant Type

### High-volume, low-margin (pizza, fast food)

Margins are thin, so every point of commission matters. Request a volume-based rate and ask whether the lower rate applies to orders delivered by your own couriers. Test both platforms for a few weeks and compare net profit per order, not just order counts.

### Premium or specialty (sushi, steakhouse, vegan)

Higher order values soften the impact of commission, but delivery quality matters more. Look at how each platform handles packaging standards, courier quality and customer reviews in your city. Both platforms use curated collections and search placement, so ask how a specialty restaurant gets featured.

### Cafes and bakeries

Speed is everything for hot drinks and pastries. Compare real delivery times in your own neighborhood by placing test orders from a competitor's listing on each app. If you sell packaged goods such as beans or gift boxes, ask whether the platform supports retail-style listings. ### Single location vs small chain

A single-location owner benefits from responsive account management; ask who your account manager is and how fast support replies. Small chains should negotiate one rate across all locations and check whether menus can be managed centrally.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Accepting the default commission.** Rates are often negotiable, particularly if you can show order volume, strong ratings or a competing offer. Ask for a written proposal and a trial period.

**Setting the delivery radius too wide.** Orders from far away arrive slower, which usually means colder food and worse reviews. Track where your orders come from for a couple of weeks and keep your radius close to the area that supplies most of them.

**Copying the dine-in menu.** Long menus slow down ordering. Build a shorter delivery menu with dishes that travel well, clear photos and a bestsellers section at the top. Our guide to [menu optimisation for delivery platforms](/blog/restaurant-menu-optimisation-delivery-platforms) covers this step by step.

**Ignoring promotional tools.** Platform promotions can lift visibility, but only if you track the result. Run one offer at a time, such as a first-order discount, and compare new customers acquired against the cost.

**Not reading reviews and refund data.** Ratings influence placement and trust. Review item-level feedback regularly, fix dishes that are repeatedly criticized, and respond to complaints.

<Callout type="warning">Watch out for commission being charged on the pre-discount order value. If you fund a promotion, check on your invoice whether you pay commission on the full price or the discounted price.</Callout>

## Hidden Costs Beyond the Commission

Many owners compare only the headline percentage. Check your contract for these items:

- **Payment processing or service fees** that may be itemized separately.
- **Promotion costs.** Featured placement and discounts can come out of your margin.
- **Late-preparation or cancellation penalties.** Set realistic preparation times in your profile.
- **Refunds and missing-item claims.** Platforms may charge these back to you, so good packaging and a pickup quality check matter.
- **Tax treatment.** Ask whether commission is calculated on the order value including or excluding tax.

## Can You Use Both Platforms?

Yes, many restaurants list on several delivery apps. The upside is reach; the downside is operational complexity: duplicate tablets, menu mismatches and missed orders. If you go multi-platform, use a POS integration or order aggregator, and read your contract for exclusivity clauses that trade a lower rate for being listed only there. ## Reduce Your Dependence on Delivery Apps

Whichever platform you pick, aim to move repeat customers to your own channels: a good Google Business Profile, a simple ordering page, and an email or SMS list. ## FAQ

**Q: Is Glovo or Wolt cheaper for restaurants?**
A: It depends on your country, your volume and your contract. Both platforms typically charge a percentage commission that is negotiable, plus optional paid promotion. Request written proposals from both and compare total cost per order.

**Q: Can I use Glovo and Wolt at the same time?**
A: Usually yes, unless your contract includes an exclusivity clause in exchange for a lower rate. Running both increases reach but needs a clear process for menus, prices and order handling.

**Q: Which platform brings more orders to a new restaurant?**
A: It depends on which app your local customers already use. Check which platform is dominant in your city, look at how many competing restaurants are listed in your category, and run a short trial on each.

**Q: How much should I spend on in-app promotions?**
A: Start small and measure. Test a single offer for a few weeks, track new customers and profit after commission, and only scale what pays back. Check current promotion pricing in your restaurant dashboard.

**Q: Can I negotiate the commission rate?**
A: Often yes, particularly with decent order volume, good ratings or a competing offer. Ask for a trial period or volume tiers in writing.
