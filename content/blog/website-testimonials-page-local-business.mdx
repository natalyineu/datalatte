A good testimonials page shows real customer words, with a name or initials, the service they bought and a date, next to a clear way to book. Real, recent and specific beats polished. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found that 97% of consumers read online reviews for local businesses, and 74% only look at reviews from the last three months.

That means your testimonials page should feed on the reviews you already earn on Google, and it must follow the FTC's rules on fake reviews. This guide covers what to include, what is banned, and how to place testimonials where they help.

## What do customers expect from reviews?

BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (1,002 US adults, published February 2026) gives the baseline. 85% are more likely to use a business after positive reviews, and 77% are less likely after negative ones. 49% trust strangers' reviews about as much as personal recommendations.

Recency and volume matter too. 32% look for reviews from the last two weeks, and 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher. The survey does not measure website testimonials separately, so treat it as evidence about reviews in general.

## What can you legally publish?

The FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials took effect on October 21, 2024. It bars writing or selling fake reviews, requires clear disclosure for reviews from insiders such as staff or relatives, and prohibits suppressing reviews through false accusations, intimidation or groundless legal threats.

Incentives are allowed unless the review must express a particular sentiment. Courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations; the FTC page does not give a dollar amount. This is a summary, not legal advice. In the UK, Australia and Canada, check your own consumer-protection regulator.

## What belongs on a testimonials page?

Each entry should let a stranger judge it:

- The customer's words, unedited except for typos, with their permission to publish.
- A first name and last initial, or a business name for trade clients.
- The service they used and a month and year.
- A link to the original review on Google when it came from there.

Lead with the strongest three, add a short list of the next ones, and finish with a booking button. Our [guide to Google reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) shows how to collect more.

## Should you use star markup on your own site?

Not for your own business. Google's review snippet guidelines say pages that use LocalBusiness or other Organization structured data are ineligible for the star review feature when the business controls the reviews about itself, even through an embedded widget. Ratings for local businesses must be sourced directly from users.

You can still show testimonials as plain page content. Just do not expect star ratings in search results from them. Your Google Business Profile reviews are what appear in Maps and local results. See also [schema markup for local business](/blog/schema-markup-for-local-business).

## Where should testimonials go besides one page?

BrightLocal found 54% of consumers visit a business's website after reading positive reviews. They arrive already curious, so put a short quote near the booking button on your home page and on each service page. A dedicated testimonials page works as a destination to link from Google posts, emails and your [about page](/blog/website-about-page-local-business).

Do not build the page from a screenshot gallery only. Text is easier to read on a phone and can be indexed.

## How do you ask for testimonials without breaking rules?

Ask every customer the same way. Google's guidelines say merchants may not selectively solicit positive reviews, offer payment, discounts or free goods for reviews, or ask staff to hit a review quota. Encouraging genuine reviews is fine.

For website testimonials, ask permission separately: "May we quote your review on our website with your first name?" Keep the reply. For review automation, see [our automation guide](/blog/best-marketing-automation-tools-small-business).

## FAQ

### How many testimonials should I show?

There is no fixed number. Three to six strong, specific ones beat twenty vague ones. BrightLocal shows buyers want volume on Google (47% want 20 or more reviews), so keep collecting there.

### Can I write testimonials myself?

No. The FTC rule prohibits fake or false reviews and testimonials. Use only words from real customers who agreed to be quoted.

### Should I include negative feedback?

You do not have to publish criticism on your testimonials page, but you cannot threaten or intimidate people to remove reviews elsewhere. Reply politely to public reviews instead.

### Do video testimonials work better?

No source reviewed here compares formats, so we make no claim. A video needs written text beside it for search and for people on silent phones.

### Can I use Google reviews on my site?

You can quote them and link to them. Embedding them does not earn star ratings in search results for your own LocalBusiness pages.
