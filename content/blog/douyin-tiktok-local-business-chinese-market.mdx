For most local businesses outside China, TikTok is the platform to run yourself, and Douyin is the platform you influence indirectly. Douyin and TikTok come from the same company (ByteDance) but run as separate apps, and content is not shared between them: Douyin is made for the Chinese market, while TikTok is the international version, which is not directly available in mainland China ([WhatsOnWeibo](https://www.whatsonweibo.com/are-douyin-and-tiktok-the-same/), checked Oct 2026). Use TikTok to reach Chinese diaspora residents and students in your city, and use Douyin mainly through tourists and travel creators who post about your business.

Picture a visitor from Shanghai at your counter on a Saturday. She films your latte for 30 seconds, adds a Chinese caption and posts it to Douyin for her followers back home. You will never see it on TikTok, and your TikTok videos will never reach her followers. That wall is the whole strategy. Tourists keep using Douyin, sometimes via VPN. Diaspora communities (people who moved from China to Australia, the UK, the US or Canada) often use both. So you do two jobs: make your shop easy to film for Douyin, and run your own TikTok in Chinese for residents.

## Douyin vs TikTok at a glance

| | Douyin | TikTok |
|---|---|---|
| Main audience | Mainland China, Chinese tourists | Everyone outside mainland China, including diaspora |
| Business account | Requirements vary; foreign businesses often use a Chinese entity or local partner | Standard business account, available in many countries |
| Language | Simplified Chinese | Any, including Mandarin/Cantonese captions |
| Best use for a local business | Tourist-generated content, travel creator partnerships | Organic video and paid ads to Chinese-speaking residents |
| Ads from outside China | Difficult for most small businesses | Available via TikTok Ads Manager |

Account and advertising rules change, so check each platform's current requirements before you commit.

## Why short video works for Chinese audiences

Video is the native format on both platforms, and both lean on discovery rather than follower counts. A Chinese customer with a great experience at your cafe is more likely to film 30 seconds than write a review. **The experience you deliver shapes your organic reach more than any content calendar.**

## Douyin strategy for businesses targeting Chinese tourists

Running your own Douyin business account is harder for a foreign business. Foreign companies typically sell or advertise there through a Chinese entity, a cross-border route or a local partner, and verification rules differ by account type ([AppInChina](https://appinchina.co/blog/the-complete-guide-to-douyin-tiktok-marketing-in-china/), checked Oct 2026), so check the current requirements before you commit. You can still benefit without an account.

**Strategy 1: Encourage tourist-generated Douyin content**

Chinese visitors will film anyway. Help them film better:

- Create a good filming spot with strong lighting and your signature product in view
- Add a small sign in Chinese such as "适合拍视频的角度" (best angle for filming)
- Make your business name in Chinese visible somewhere in the frame

**Strategy 2: Partner with Chinese travel creators**

Chinese travel creators (旅游博主) post extensively about overseas trips. A single post from a creator with a relevant audience can send tourists your way for weeks, though results vary widely.

To find them:
- Search Douyin for "[your city] 旅游" and note who posts regularly about your area
- Look at micro-creators (roughly 10,000 to 200,000 followers), who are usually easier to reach and often more engaged than big names
- Offer a complimentary visit in exchange for an honest post, not a guaranteed positive review. Authenticity matters on Douyin.

<Callout type="tip">Reach out before major Chinese holiday periods such as Golden Week and Chinese New Year, when creators look for overseas content. See our guide to [Chinese New Year marketing for local business](/blog/chinese-new-year-marketing-local-business) and [marketing to Chinese tourists](/blog/marketing-to-chinese-tourists-local-business).</Callout>

## TikTok strategy for reaching Chinese diaspora communities

For diaspora communities in Australia, the UK and North America, TikTok is the more practical platform. What tends to resonate:

**Bilingual content.** Even a short "欢迎来到我们的咖啡厅!" (Welcome to our cafe!) at the start shows effort, and diaspora viewers often respond warmly to it.

**Familiar flavours with a twist.** Chinese and Asian ingredients such as matcha, taro, pandan, osmanthus or red bean are naturally shareable in food communities. A pandan latte video with Chinese subtitles speaks directly to that audience.

**Local "best spots" content.** If you are near a university with many Chinese international students, content that welcomes them specifically can work well.

For general TikTok fundamentals, see [how to target local customers on TikTok](/blog/how-to-target-local-customers-on-tiktok).

## A short video format that works on both platforms

If someone on your team speaks Mandarin, even conversationally, you can adapt one video for both platforms.

- **Seconds 0 to 3:** a visual hook, such as the product being plated. No logo, no talking.
- **Seconds 3 to 25:** context. Where you are, what the product is called in English and Chinese, and what makes it special. Keep it conversational.
- **Final seconds:** a soft call to action, for example "地址在 [address]" or "关注我们" with your handle on screen.

Douyin expects Chinese captions and Chinese-language posting times. TikTok usually works best with English captions (Chinese optional) posted in your local time zone.

## Running paid TikTok ads to Chinese-speaking residents

TikTok Ads Manager lets you target by language, based on the language a user has selected in the app and/or their predicted language, alongside age, gender and location ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/ad-targeting?lang=en), checked Oct 2026). That lets you reach people who use the app in Chinese within your city. Menu names change, so check the current interface.

1. Create a campaign with an objective such as traffic or conversions.
2. In the audience settings, choose the Chinese language option(s) available to you.
3. Set the location to your city or catchment area.
4. Use a Mandarin or bilingual video.
5. Start with a modest daily budget and run it for at least a week before judging results.

Check current pricing in your own account. Our [TikTok ads cost guide](/blog/tiktok-ads-cost-guide) covers budgeting.

Good creative: a "day in the life" video with Mandarin subtitles, or a limited-time offer with a Chinese text overlay (本周特惠, "this week's special").

<Callout type="warning">Do not run the same creative on TikTok and Douyin. The platforms have different algorithms, community standards and norms. Reuse the idea, but adapt the format, pacing and language.</Callout>

## The Math: A Small TikTok Test

Example only. The click cost and claim rates are assumptions for illustration, not TikTok averages and not a client result. Swap in your own numbers from Ads Manager.

Assumptions: $15 a day for 14 days = $210, $0.50 per click (assumed), so $210 / $0.50 = 420 clicks. Some of those people claim your Mandarin-captioned offer (a coupon code or "show this video") and visit. Average ticket $9.

| Clicks that become customers (assumed) | Customers | Cost per customer | First-visit revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2% | 8.4 | $25.00 | about $76 |
| 4% | 16.8 | $12.50 | about $151 |
| 8% | 33.6 | about $6.25 | about $302 |

On the first visit alone, only the 8% row comes close to paying for the ads. The rest depends on repeat visits: at three visits a year ($27), the 4% row covers its $12.50 and the 2% row does not. Do not scale a test that sits in the 2% row. Fix the offer or the video first. The same logic applies to a creator you host: count the redeemed codes against what the free visit cost you.

## Optimising a Douyin presence for foot traffic

If you do have access to a Douyin business presence, or work with a partner who does, these basics help:

- **Set your location.** Tag your physical address and keep the profile in Mandarin with your address, phone number and hours.
- **Create content around what people search.** Think of queries like "best egg tarts in Sydney" (悉尼最好吃的蛋挞) or "Chinese-friendly barber in Melbourne" and make short videos that answer them.
- **Use series.** Episodic content, such as a weekly "Coffee Knowledge Class" (咖啡知识小课堂), gives people a reason to follow.
- **Try live streams** only if someone can answer comments in Mandarin in real time.

## Measuring results on Chinese video platforms

Standard Western analytics tools will not capture most Douyin-driven visits, so use simple attribution:

- Train staff to ask "How did you hear about us?" and prompt with "小红书? 抖音? 微信?"
- Use a unique offer or code for creator partnerships, such as "Show this video for 10% off"
- Add the TikTok Pixel and UTM links for paid TikTok traffic. See [what a pixel tag is](/blog/what-is-a-pixel-tag-tracking-local-business).
- Track engagement rate (likes, comments and shares divided by views) on your own posts to see which hooks work

Each month compare four numbers: new followers, offer redemptions, walk-ins mentioning video, and cost per new customer.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a Douyin account separate from TikTok?**
A: Yes. They are separate apps and networks, and a Douyin business presence has its own registration and verification process. Many local businesses focus on TikTok for owned channels and rely on tourist-generated content for Douyin visibility.

**Q: Can my TikTok videos be seen in China?**
A: No. TikTok is not directly available in mainland China, where people use Douyin. Visitors abroad may open TikTok, but Douyin often remains their main app.

**Q: What is the fastest way to reach Chinese diaspora customers on TikTok?**
A: Paid TikTok ads targeted by language, with a bilingual creative, usually give you data fastest, because you do not have to wait for organic reach. Organic growth takes longer but builds a more durable audience.

**Q: Is it worth hiring a Chinese-speaking content creator?**
A: If your business sits in or near a large Chinese community, a part-time bilingual creator can be worth testing. Agree on clear goals first, such as a number of videos per month and a tracked offer, and review results after two months.

**Q: How should I respond to a negative Douyin video about my business?**
A: Reply calmly and publicly in Chinese, acknowledge the issue, and move the conversation to direct messages, for example "请私信我们解决此问题" (please DM us so we can fix this). A graceful reply is often seen positively.
