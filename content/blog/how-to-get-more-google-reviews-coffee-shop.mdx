To get more Google reviews, a coffee shop needs three things: a direct review link or QR code from its Business Profile, a short spoken ask at the moment a customer is happy, and a rule that every customer is asked the same way. Do not offer free drinks for reviews. Google bans it. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found that 78% of US consumers were asked to leave a review in the past year, so being asked is normal.

This guide covers the link, the ask, timing, replies and the rules that can get reviews removed. Every figure comes from a source at the end. Examples marked as assumptions are illustrations, not results.

## Why Do Google Reviews Matter for a Coffee Shop?

Three reasons, each backed by a source. First, shoppers read them: BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (1,002 US adults) found 97% read reviews for local businesses, and 41% say they always do. Second, count and recency matter: 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and 74% look for reviews written in the last three months.

Third, Google lists reviews among the signals for prominence, one of its three local ranking factors alongside relevance and distance. Google does not publish how much weight reviews carry, and it says you cannot pay for a better ranking. So treat any claim that a certain number of reviews will rank you first as unproven.

## How Do You Create a Google Review Link and QR Code?

Google's help page gives the steps. Sign in at business.google.com, open your Business Profile, choose Read reviews, then select Get more reviews (the share icon). You can copy the link or download the QR code. Google notes that QR codes can currently be generated only from a computer browser, not from a phone.

Customers need a Google Account to post a review, and a non-Gmail address works. Print the QR code on a table card, the counter or the cup sleeve, and test it with your own phone before printing. Put the short link in your order-ready text or email too.

## When and How Should You Ask?

Ask right after a positive moment: a compliment on the drink, a regular chatting at pickup, a mobile order collected without issues. Keep it short, tell people where to scan, and ask everyone the same way. Two example scripts:

- At the counter: "If you enjoyed it, a Google review helps us a lot. The QR code is on the card by the till."
- By text after a pickup, where the customer agreed to messages: "Thanks for visiting today. If you have a minute, here is our review link."

Do not ask only the customers you expect to be happy. Google's review policy lists selectively soliciting positive reviews and discouraging negative ones as prohibited. Do not ask staff, relatives or business partners to post reviews either, since the policy covers conflicts of interest, including current or former employment.

## Can You Give a Free Coffee for a Review?

No. Google's contribution policy says reviews posted due to an incentive from a business, such as payment, discounts or free goods, are not allowed and will be removed. The help page repeats that rewards for posting, changing or removing a review are prohibited. Thank people in your reply instead.

This also applies to staff targets. The policy names merchants requesting that staff solicit a certain number of reviews as a violation. You can set a goal for how often staff mention the QR code. Do not set a quota of reviews per person.

## How Should You Reply to Reviews?

BrightLocal reports that 89% of consumers expect owners to reply, 80% are likely to use a business that replies to all its reviews, and 50% are unlikely to choose a business that uses templated or generic replies. Google's advice matches: keep replies short, specific and professional, and do not use them to promote deals.

For a negative review, acknowledge what went wrong, say what you can and cannot do, apologise where fair, and move complex cases to phone or email. Never share a reviewer's private details. If a review breaks Google's content policy, you can flag it. For a reply template that is not generic, see our [review auto-reply script](/blog/ai-agent-google-reviews-auto-reply-script).

## What Tools Can Automate Review Requests?

Some point-of-sale and review tools can send a review link after a purchase. Features and prices change, so check the vendor's page before you buy and confirm customers have agreed to receive messages. Automation does not replace the spoken ask at the counter. It helps with customers you cannot talk to, such as delivery and mobile-order customers.

## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a coffee shop need?

There is no official number. BrightLocal found 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, so 20 is a reasonable first target. Recent reviews matter too, since 74% look for reviews from the last three months.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?

No. Google's policy says incentivized reviews are not allowed and will be removed. This includes discounts, free goods and payment.

### Can employees leave reviews for the shop?

No. Google's policy lists conflict of interest, including current or former employment, as a violation. Ask real customers.

### Where do I find my review link?

In your Business Profile, choose Read reviews, then Get more reviews. Copy the link or download the QR code from a computer.

### What if I get a fake review?

Flag it through your profile and reply professionally in the meantime. See our guide on [fake Google reviews](/blog/how-to-deal-with-fake-google-reviews). Google's help page links to its reporting steps.
