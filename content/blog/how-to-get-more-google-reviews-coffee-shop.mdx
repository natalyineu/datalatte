Square's 2025 report found 65% of Canadians like businesses to contact them by email, while 39% appreciate text updates. ## Why Ask for Google Reviews at All?

Reviews are one of the few trust signals a coffee shop can build without paying for ads. The Digital Resource page states that featuring reviews or testimonials from real-life customers can help build trust and increase conversion rates by 270%. That figure comes from a 2021 marketing blog, so treat it as a directional claim rather than a forecast for your shop.

Use it to justify the effort, not to promise results. Reviews also give your listing fresh, customer-written text. Pair them with the profile basics in our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization).

## Is Your Google Business Profile Ready to Collect Reviews?

Per Diem explains that Google asks you to verify a coffee shop before the listing goes live. This usually means receiving a postcard with a code at the business address, though some businesses can verify by phone or email. Per Diem also warns that without verification, your updates will not show publicly and customers may not find you.

Finish verification before you print any QR code. Per Diem recommends choosing the "Coffee shop" category or a related term rather than something broad such as "Restaurant". Google also lets people message businesses directly from the listing, so set a routine for messages as well as reviews.

## How Do You Set Up a Review Link and QR Code?

Open your Business Profile dashboard and look for the option to get more reviews. Menu names change, so follow the current steps on Google's Business Profile Help pages rather than an older screenshot. Copy the short link and generate a QR code, then test the code with your own phone before printing.

Per Diem reports that Think Coffee, an independent brand, placed its app download link in its Google profile and now has thousands of downloads. Seven in 10 Canadians use smartphones to access the internet, according to Square's citation of Statista, so a printed QR code will reach most phone users.

## What Should the Ask Sound Like?

Keep it short, name the place to scan, and use the same words for every customer. Two example scripts:

- At the counter: "If you enjoyed the coffee today, a quick Google review helps a local shop. The code is on the card by the till."
- By text, after the customer has opted in: "Thanks for coming in today. If you have a minute, here is our review link."

Do not ask only the customers you expect to be happy. A filtered list makes the profile less representative of the people who actually visit. Skip rewards too. A free drink or discount in exchange for a review changes what people write and makes the review less trustworthy to the next reader.

## How Many Reviews Can a Shop Expect?

Every input below is an **assumption** for a neighbourhood cafe. The 65% email share is borrowed from Square's Canadian figure, so it applies only if your customers are in Canada.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Customers per week | 120 |
| Share who gave an email and agreed to messages | 65% |
| Emails sent per week (120 x 65%) | 78 |
| Share of emailed customers who post a review | 5% |
| Reviews per week (78 x 5%) | about 4 |
| Reviews per month (about 4 x 4.3 weeks) | about 16 |

Time is the second input. Bellwether lists Google Business at about 5 minutes per day for a coffee shop, so an assumed 35 minutes a week covers replies and checks. Track your own response rate for a month, then replace the 5% with your real figure. The 270% claim is not part of this calculation.

## How Should You Reply to Reviews?

Reply to every review with a short line that refers to something specific, such as a drink the customer named or a detail they mentioned. Thank the reviewer in the same tone whether the review is five stars or two.

For a complaint, acknowledge what went wrong, say what you can do, and move the detail to phone or email. Never share private details about a customer in a public reply. Use the same calm tone when a customer messages through the listing.

## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a coffee shop need?

This guide does not give a sourced target. Track your monthly count, and use the worked example above to set a goal that fits your customer volume.

### Can I offer a free coffee for a review?

No, this guide advises against it. Rewards change what people write and make the review less trustworthy to other customers.

### Do I need to verify my profile before asking for reviews?

Yes. Per Diem explains that without verification, updates will not show publicly and customers may not find the business.

### Should I text customers for reviews?

Only customers who opted in. Square's 2025 report found 39% of Canadians appreciate text updates from businesses, so consent matters more than the percentage.

### Which category should my coffee shop choose?

Per Diem recommends "Coffee shop" or a related term rather than a broad category such as "Restaurant".
