Customer lifetime value (CLV) is the total value a customer brings over the whole relationship with your business. The simplest way to work it out is average order value x purchases per year x years as a customer. Multiply that by gross margin to get a profit figure, then compare it with what it costs to win one customer. Shopify's guide shows a coffee retailer with a $30 order, 12 purchases a year and a 3-year relationship: $30 x 12 x 3 = $1,080.

This guide gives the three standard formulas, a worked example with labelled assumptions, and ways to raise the number. No industry-average CLV for coffee shops, salons or studios is published in the sources reviewed, so the article does not quote one.

## How Do You Calculate CLV?

Use one time period throughout. Per Shopify's guide, the revenue-based formula is:

CLV = average order value x annual purchase frequency x average customer lifespan in years

- **Average order value:** total revenue divided by number of orders.
- **Purchase frequency:** orders in the year divided by unique customers who bought that year.
- **Lifespan:** the average length of customer relationships. For a business with little history, estimate it from how many customers from two years ago still buy.

A margin version multiplies the result by gross margin. Shopify's example: $300 CLV at a hypothetical 60% margin gives $180 of gross profit, before acquisition and operating costs. Use that profit figure for decisions.

## What Is a Subscription or Membership Version?

For recurring fees, such as gym memberships or grooming plans, Shopify gives: average monthly recurring revenue per customer x gross margin, divided by monthly churn rate. It assumes revenue, margin and churn stay stable and does not discount future earnings.

If a studio keeps 4% of members leaving each month, the average membership lasts roughly 25 months under that formula. Our [membership retention tactics](/blog/fitness-studio-membership-retention-tactics) cover how to move that churn number.

## What Is the CAC Test?

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is what you spend to win one new customer. Shopify's guide suggests comparing margin-adjusted CLV with CAC for the same customer group, and notes that 3:1 is a common benchmark that depends on your margins and costs. If CLV in profit is below CAC, each new customer loses money.

CAC comes from your own spend: ad cost plus any offer or discount, divided by new customers won. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows how to split spend across channels.

## The Math: A Salon Example

All inputs are **assumptions**, not benchmarks.

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Average order value | $85 |
| Visits per year | 6 |
| Average lifespan | 2 years |
| Revenue CLV | $85 x 6 x 2 = $1,020 |
| Gross margin | 60% |
| Gross-profit CLV | $612 |
| Acquisition cost (assumption) | $90 |
| CLV to CAC | about 6.8 to 1 |

Now test a change. If the salon adds one extra visit a year through rebooking reminders, CLV becomes $85 x 7 x 2 = $1,190, or $714 at 60% margin, so $102 more profit per customer. If you pay $20 a month for reminders and keep 40 customers more engaged, you can compare the cost directly with the gain.

## How Do You Raise CLV?

Each lever matches one of the three inputs.

- **Order value:** offer a natural add-on at the point of sale. Measure whether average order value rose.
- **Frequency:** rebooking prompts at checkout, reminders and a loyalty scheme. See our [loyalty program ideas](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app) and [AI customer retention](/blog/ai-driven-customer-retention-strategies) guides.
- **Lifespan:** follow up with customers who stop coming and ask why.

HBR's article says acquiring a new customer is "anywhere from five to 25 times more expensive than retaining an existing one" and cites Bain research that a 5% retention increase raises profits 25% to 95%. HBR gives no method for either claim and says the first depends on which study you believe, so treat both as direction, not a forecast.

## What Mistakes Distort CLV?

- Using revenue when you need profit.
- Mixing time periods, such as monthly orders with a yearly lifespan.
- Averaging all customers together. Compare regulars and one-time visitors separately.
- Leaving out discounts and acquisition costs.
- Using a lifespan you have not checked against real customers.

## FAQ

### What is customer lifetime value in simple terms?

It is the total value a customer brings across all their purchases with you.

### What is a good CLV?

There is no universal number. Compare your profit CLV with your acquisition cost. Shopify notes 3:1 as a common benchmark, depending on margins.

### Should I use revenue or profit?

Use gross profit for decisions. Revenue CLV is useful as a first step.

### How do I estimate lifespan with little data?

Check what share of customers from a year or two ago still buy, then estimate how long a typical relationship lasts. Revise when you have more data.

### Is retention cheaper than acquisition?

Often, but the ratio varies. HBR cites a range of 5 to 25 times without a named study.
