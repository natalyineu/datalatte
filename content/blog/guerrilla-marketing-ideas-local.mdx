Guerrilla marketing means promoting a business with surprise, creativity and low spend instead of a big media budget. For a local business the workable versions are partnerships with neighbouring shops, tracked street-level offers, events, creator collaborations and well-timed Google Business Profile posts. Each has rules: public-space permission, contest terms, review policies and ad labelling. Skipping them can cost more than the idea saves.

This guide gives seven ideas, the rule that applies to each, and an illustrative cost example. The example inputs are labelled as assumptions.

## What Counts as Guerrilla Marketing?

Wikipedia describes it as an unconventional approach that uses surprise or unusual interactions to promote a product, aiming at a memorable experience that people discuss. It says Jay Conrad Levinson popularised the term in his 1984 book. Types include ambient, street, viral, buzz and grassroots promotion.

For a small shop the grassroots and street types are the practical ones. The same article describes astroturfing, meaning fake hype or fake endorsements, as one of the most controversial approaches with a high risk of backfiring. Avoid it. Wikipedia is a secondary source, so treat the history as background only.

## Which Seven Ideas Fit a Local Business?

| Idea | What you do | Rule to check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neighbour partnership | Swap offers or cards with a non-competing shop | Agree terms in writing |
| Tracked street offer | Hand out cards with a unique code | Public-space permission |
| Community event | Host a class, tasting or open day | Local permits |
| Contest or giveaway | Prize for entries | Platform and local promotion rules |
| Creator collaboration | Free service for a local creator's post | Ad labelling |
| Profile post or offer | Post a timed offer on your Business Profile | Google post policies |
| Surprise gesture | Small free item for people in the queue | Food and safety rules |

Start with the two cheapest to track: the neighbour partnership and the coded card. Our guide on [event marketing with DOOH](/blog/dooh-event-marketing-local-business) shows a paid way to promote events.

## What Rules Apply to Street Promotions?

Wikipedia notes that street campaigns generally need government permission, and that some agencies proceed without it and risk fines. The rules depend on your city: flyers, chalk, stickers and posters on public property are often restricted. Ask your local council or city office before you start, and use private noticeboards and partner shops where you have permission.

For gifts or incentives to creators who post about you, the FTC says a material connection, including free products, must be disclosed clearly, close to the endorsement. ## Can You Run a Giveaway Safely?

Yes, within limits. Meta's Pages policy says promotions must comply with applicable laws, give entrants official rules, release Meta from liability, and must not require or incentivise people to share, repost or tag others. Meta also says it will not help administer a promotion.

Do not tie a prize to a Google review. Google's Maps policy prohibits reviews posted because a business offered an incentive, such as payment, discounts or free goods. Merchants may encourage genuine reviews but not reward them. Our [review strategy guide](/blog/google-business-reviews-strategy) explains how to ask in a compliant way.

## How Do You Use Your Google Business Profile?

Google Business Profile posts come as updates, offers and events. An offer needs a title and dates and shows a "View Offer" button, with an optional coupon code and terms. Posts older than six months are archived unless you set a date range.

A timed offer for a quiet weekday is a low-cost test that needs no permit. Pair it with a code so you can count redemptions. See also [how to get more Google reviews for a coffee shop](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop).

## FAQ

### Is guerrilla marketing legal?
Many forms are, but public-space promotion usually needs permission, and contests and creator posts have rules. Check local law and platform policies.

### Can I offer a prize for leaving a review?
No. Google's policy prohibits incentivised reviews and removes them.

### How do I measure guerrilla marketing?
Use unique codes, short links or a "how did you hear about us" question, and count redemptions per idea.

### What is the cheapest idea to start with?
A partnership with a neighbouring business, because it needs only printed cards or a counter display.
