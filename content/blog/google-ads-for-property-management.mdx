Yes. Google Ads can help property management companies fill vacancies faster because they put your listings in front of people actively searching for a rental in your area, and you pay only when someone clicks. The ads work best for specific searches like "2 bedroom apartment [neighborhood]" or "pet-friendly rentals [city]", sent to the page for the exact unit, with calls and tour requests tracked.

Every vacant unit costs you rent for each day it stays empty, so even a modest, well-targeted budget can pay for itself. Your results depend on your market, the unit, the price and your landing page.

## Why Google Ads Suit Property Managers

Renters search with specific intent: a neighborhood, a bedroom count, a pet policy, a budget. Search ads let you appear at that moment, and you can limit them to the towns and ZIP codes you manage. Compared with social ads, which interrupt people, search ads capture people who are already looking.

Three starting steps:

- Pick a daily budget you can sustain for a few weeks, based on how many vacancies you have and how urgent they are
- Start with Search campaigns focused on specific unit and neighborhood terms
- Add location targeting and exclusions so you do not pay for clicks outside your service area

[Google Ads management](/services/google-ads) can take over setup and optimisation while you focus on showings and tenant screening.

<Callout type="tip">**Use negative keywords** like "buy", "for sale", "investment", "commercial" and "jobs" to avoid irrelevant clicks from buyers, investors and job seekers. If you do not manage short-term rentals, exclude those terms as well.</Callout>

## Building High-Converting Campaigns

Mirror the way tenants search. Create ad groups by unit type or area, for example "1BR downtown", "2BR family-friendly", "studio near campus" or "pet-friendly". Each ad group should have its own keywords, ad text and landing page.

| Campaign type | Best use |
|---|---|
| Search | Capturing active renters searching for specific units and areas |
| Performance Max / Display | Remarketing to people who viewed a listing, once Search is working |
| Call-focused ads | Prospects who prefer to phone, particularly on mobile |

Search is the right starting point for most property managers. Display and other formats are better added later for remarketing, using a small share of budget. See our guide to [call-only campaigns](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns) if most of your leads come by phone.

<Callout type="warning">**Be careful with broad match keywords** like "housing" or "rentals". They often attract irrelevant clicks. Use phrase and exact match for terms like "apartment for rent [city]" and watch the search terms report weekly.</Callout>

### Fair Housing and Ad Policy

Rental advertising is regulated. Do not use ad copy or audience choices that discriminate or imply a preference based on protected characteristics. Google also restricts targeting options for housing ads in some countries, so check the current Google Ads housing policy for your location before you set audiences.

## Budgeting for Vacancies

You do not need a huge budget, but you need enough for a steady flow of clicks. Click costs vary widely: competitive city markets will cost more per click than smaller towns. Use Google's Keyword Planner to estimate costs for your area before launching, then set a daily budget that can produce a handful of clicks per day for your priority keywords.

A simple way to think about it: if an empty unit costs you its monthly rent, each week of reduced vacancy is worth roughly a quarter of the rent. Compare that with your cost per tour request to see whether the numbers work.

A practical split for a small account:

- Most of the budget on Search for your priority vacancies
- A small share on remarketing to people who viewed a listing but did not enquire
- Pause or reduce spend on units with pending applications

<Callout type="example">Illustrative example: if you have three vacancies and a rent of about $1,800, one extra week of vacancy per unit is already worth several thousand dollars of lost income. That is the lens to use when deciding how much to invest in ads.</Callout>

## Ad Copy and Landing Pages

Lead with details renters care about: the neighborhood, bedrooms, rent, pet policy and the next step ("Book a tour today"). Use extensions for calls, locations and extra links.

Send each ad to the page for that exact unit, not a general "all listings" page. Make sure the page loads quickly on mobile, shows photos, price and availability, and has a clear way to book a tour or call.

## Keywords to Start With

Build your keyword list from how renters actually search:

- "[bedrooms] apartment for rent [neighborhood or city]"
- "pet-friendly apartments [city]"
- "apartments near [university, hospital or employer]"
- "house for rent [city]" and "townhouse for rent [city]"
- Your brand name, so people who hear about you elsewhere can find you

Add negative keywords for "for sale", "buy", "mortgage", "jobs", "property manager salary", "section 8 application" (if irrelevant), "login" and "pay rent" (current tenants searching for your portal will otherwise click your ads). Review the search terms report weekly for the first month.

## Landing Page Checklist for Rental Listings

- Current photos, price, availability date and lease terms above the fold
- Pet policy, parking and deposit details so people self-qualify
- A short tour request form and a click-to-call button
- Application link or clear next step
- Fast load time on mobile, because many renters search from their phones

If your listings live on a third-party platform, send ads to the page that lists the specific unit rather than a general search page. For a better listing site, see our [website service](/services/website).

## Tracking What Matters

Track both calls and tour requests as conversions. Many rental enquiries come by phone, so use call assets or [call tracking](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business). Review the search terms report weekly with [analytics and reporting](/services/analytics): pause keywords that spend without leading to enquiries, and add new ones that show up in good searches.

## Avoiding Common Mistakes

1. **Outdated listing photos or availability.** If renters see a unit that is already taken, they bounce.
2. **Ignoring ad schedule and device data.** Look at when enquiries actually come in and adjust bids.
3. **Skipping call tracking.** Without it you may undercount your best campaigns.
4. **Leaving ads running on rented units.** Pause campaigns or ad groups as soon as a unit has an application.
5. **Sending traffic to the home page** instead of the specific unit.

## FAQ

**Q: I manage around 30 units and have a $500 monthly ad budget. Is that enough?**
A: It can be, if you focus it on a few priority vacancies and a tight location. Spreading a small budget across too many ad groups is the main risk. Check Keyword Planner for your area to see how many clicks that budget buys.

**Q: Will Google Ads show my rentals to people who are only browsing?**
A: Search ads show when someone types a specific query, so they tend to be more intentful than broad display. Use specific keywords, negatives and a unit-specific landing page to filter out casual traffic.

**Q: Should I use manual CPC or automated bidding?**
A: Many accounts start with manual CPC or Maximize Clicks while they learn what keywords cost, then move to a conversion-based strategy once there is steady conversion data. Follow Google's current recommendations for how much data is needed.

**Q: Will Google Ads work in a small town?**
A: It depends on search volume. If very few people search for rentals in your town each month, ads may not be efficient and local classifieds, Facebook Marketplace and your Google Business Profile may work better. Check search volume in Keyword Planner first.

**Q: How long before I see leads?**
A: Clicks and calls can begin as soon as ads are approved and running. Give campaigns a few weeks to tune, and track tours booked and leases signed, not just clicks.
