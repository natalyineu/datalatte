An empty unit that rents for $1,800 a month costs you about $60 a day to leave vacant ($1,800 divided by 30). That number is the real budget for a vacancy campaign. If $600 of ads fills a unit even 10 days sooner than it would have filled anyway, the ads paid for themselves. If it fills nothing, you lost $600. This guide shows how to tell which one you are getting.

It is written for property managers and small landlords in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Property management ads do two different jobs, and mixing them up is the most common way to waste money: filling vacancies for renters, and winning management contracts from owners. We cover both, plus the housing ad rules that quietly change what you can target.

## Two Jobs, Two Campaigns

| | Fill a vacancy | Win an owner contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Searcher | Renter | Landlord or investor |
| Example search | "2 bedroom apartment [neighborhood]" | "property management [city]" |
| Landing page | The page for that exact unit | A page about your management service and fees |
| Conversion | Tour request, call, application | Call or quote request |
| Value of one conversion | One lease (a year of rent) | Months or years of management fees |
| Keep running | Only while the unit is empty | All year |

Run them as separate campaigns with separate budgets. Renter keywords are seasonal and tied to specific units. Owner keywords are steady and tied to your service area.

## What the Benchmarks Say

Public data for property management is thin, and most of it comes from one marketing agency that sells to property managers. That makes it useful for orientation and wrong to treat as neutral. A [Fourandhalf analysis published in November 2021](https://fourandhalf.com/property-management-cpc-since-2018/) compared six US cities between 2018 and 2020 and reports an average cost-per-click increase of 117.2%, ranging from about 9% in Tampa to about 373% in Honolulu, with leads per month roughly flat (6 in 2018, 5.88 in 2020). It gives percentages, not dollar prices. Another [Fourandhalf article](https://fourandhalf.com/grow-property-management-business-google-adwords/) estimates that a mature campaign costs about $300 to win a new management client, within a $200 to $500 range, set against an average contract value of about $3,100 (two to three years of management and lease-up fees, per the agency).

Fourandhalf also says typical CTR for property management ads is 3% to 4% in a [2020 post](https://fourandhalf.com/property-management-google-ads-campaign-maintenance/), and says CPL depends on how competitive your market is, without giving a dollar figure. All of this is vendor data from 2020 and 2021, so what to take from it:

- Click prices rose sharply in competitive cities, and the spread between cities is huge. Your market decides your cost, not an average.
- Owner leads are scarce: about six a month per account in that agency's six-city sample.
- Do your own check: Google's [Keyword Planner help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243) says it shows monthly search estimates and the average cost for your ad to appear on a keyword. Run "property management [your city]" and "apartments for rent [your city]" through it before you budget.

## Check the Housing Ad Rules First

This is where property managers get caught. Google's [housing, employment and credit policy FAQ](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9997418) covers housing ads served in the United States and Canada, based on the location the ad targets. It says in-scope housing includes promotion of housing for sale or rental, such as apartments, and services whose purpose is to enable sale or rental of homes. Property management services are listed among the examples that are not restricted, but individual unit rentals are, and Google reviews both the ad and the landing page, so a landing page with significant housing content can bring an ad into scope.

If your ads are in scope, Google says:

- Age, parental status and gender must all be set to "Enable" (excluding only the Unknown category is allowed for age and parental status).
- Demographic bid adjustments revert to 0% or are removed.
- ZIP or postal-code targeting is restricted. Google says to use city, state or radius instead. Full Canadian postal codes are restricted, and the three-character FSA is not.
- Remarketing and Customer Match lists are restricted only if they are built on demographic, marital, parental or ZIP signals.

In practice: build your service area from cities and radius circles, not ZIP lists, for rental ads in the US and Canada. Check the current policy page in your account, since Google updates it.

Separately, US federal rules apply to the words you write. [24 CFR 100.75](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.75) makes it unlawful to publish any advertisement that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin. It specifically covers using words, phrases, photographs or illustrations that convey a dwelling is available or not available to a particular group. Describe the unit (bedrooms, rent, pet policy, lease terms), never the ideal tenant. This guide is not legal advice; if you are unsure about a line of copy, ask a fair housing professional. UK, Australian and Canadian equivalents differ, so check local discrimination rules.

## Build the Vacancy Campaign

### Campaign type and match types

Start with Search, one campaign for vacancies. Google's [match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) says broad match is the default and can show on related searches without the exact words, phrase match reaches searches with the keyword's meaning, and exact match gives the most control but reaches the fewest searches. Google also says it is critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match. With a small budget and no conversion history, start with phrase and exact.

### Ad groups that mirror how renters search

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| By bedrooms and area | "2 bedroom apartment [neighborhood]", [studio for rent in your city] | Phrase + exact |
| Pet-friendly | "pet friendly apartments [city]", "dog friendly rentals [city]" | Phrase |
| Near a landmark | "apartments near [university or hospital]" | Phrase |
| Property type | "house for rent [city]", "townhouse for rent [city]" | Phrase + exact |
| Brand | [your company name] | Exact |

One ad group per unit type or area, each with its own ads and its own landing page.

### Negative keywords

Add on day one: `for sale`, `buy`, `mortgage`, `investment`, `jobs`, `salary`, `login`, `pay rent`, `portal`, `maintenance request`, `complaint`, `lawsuit`. The last four catch current tenants and unhappy ones, who click your ads when they should use your portal. Our negative keywords guide explains lists and levels. Review the search terms report weekly for the first month.

### Ads and landing page

Fill in the headlines with specifics: bedrooms, area, rent, pet policy, move-in date, "Book a tour". Use every headline slot the ad editor offers. Add a call asset: Google's [call asset help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453991) says call reporting uses Google forwarding numbers and is available only on the Search Network, and you can choose whether calls count as conversions.

Send each ad to the page for that exact unit with current photos, price, availability date, pet policy, deposit, a short tour form, click-to-call and a visible application link. Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) cover layout. If the unit is already taken, pause its ad group the same day; paying for clicks to a leased unit is the most common leak.

## Build the Owner-Acquisition Campaign

Landlords search with different words: "property management [city]", "property manager near me", "rental property management fees [city]", "how much does property management cost". Send them to a page that answers the fee question honestly and offers a quote or call. Negatives: `jobs`, `salary`, `courses`, `software`, `app`, `tenant`, `apply`, `login`. Owner conversions are rare and valuable, so a modest steady budget and tight tracking matter more than volume. If most leads phone, see our call-only campaigns guide.

## Tracking: Count Tours, Calls and Signed Contracts

Create conversions for tour requests, call-asset calls and form submissions. Our conversion tracking setup guide covers the steps, and call tracking for local business covers phone numbers. Then do the manual part: in your CRM or property software, mark which enquiries became leases or signed management agreements and what source they came from. Ad platforms count enquiries; only your own records know which became revenue.

## The Math

Two calculations. The first is a vacancy campaign: $600 a month, chasing one or more units at $1,800 rent. Every row marked "assumption" is illustrative, not a benchmark: no verifiable public CPC exists for rental searches, so test your own with Keyword Planner.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $2.50 | $1.50 | $1.00 |
| Clicks | 240 | 400 | 600 |
| Click to tour-request rate (assumption) | 2% | 4% | 6% |
| Tour requests | 4.8 | 16 | 36 |
| Tour request to lease rate (assumption) | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| Leases | 0.48 | 1.6 | 3.6 |
| Ad cost per lease | $1,250 | $375 | $167 |
| Daily vacancy cost (rent / 30) | $60 | $60 | $60 |
| Days of vacancy saved to break even | 21 | 6.3 | 2.8 |

Read the last row as: the ads must fill a unit that many days sooner than it would have filled anyway to pay for themselves. Nobody can measure the "anyway" precisely, which is why you compare against your average days-vacant before ads. If your units already fill in a week through listing sites, Tough will lose money; if they sit for six weeks, even Tough may pay.

The second is owner acquisition using Fourandhalf's figures as the reference point. If one management contract is worth about $3,100 over its life and a mature campaign costs about $300 per signed client (both agency estimates), the ratio is roughly 10 to 1. Substitute your own numbers: your monthly management fee x expected months an owner stays, divided by what you spend per signed owner. If your owners stay 14 months at $120 a month in fees (assumptions), a contract is worth $1,680, and your ceiling per signed owner is whatever leaves you the margin you need. Pull your retention from your own books.

## Optimise Weekly

1. Read the search terms report and add negatives.
2. Pause ad groups for units with a pending application.
3. Compare calls and tour requests by day and hour, and align ad hours with when someone answers.
4. Move budget from areas with no tours to areas with tours.
5. Test one new headline every two weeks.

Pair this with a complete [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and our real estate agent ads guide if you also list properties for sale.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do Not Describe the Tenant">
"Perfect for young professionals" or "adults only" describes who should live in the unit, not the unit. Under the federal rule above, that kind of language can create liability. Write bedrooms, rent, pet rules and lease length instead.
</Callout>

## Do This Today

1. Write down your average days-vacant and monthly rent per unit. Multiply daily rent (rent / 30) by days to see what a vacancy actually costs you.
2. Look up your two main keywords in Keyword Planner to see a real cost-per-click range for your city.
3. If you advertise rentals in the US or Canada, remove ZIP-code targeting and replace it with city or radius, and set age, gender and parental status to Enable.
4. Create one Search campaign for vacancies, with ad groups by unit type, phrase and exact match, the negative list above and a landing page per unit. Set up tour and call conversions before launch.
5. Add a weekly 20-minute slot to pause leased units and read the search terms report.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a property manager spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official number. Fourandhalf describes spending of "a few hundred dollars" as a short boost and says some clients spend thousands a month. Work backwards from the cost of a vacancy and your tour-to-lease rate.

**Q: Do the housing ad restrictions apply to me?**
A: Google's FAQ lists property management services as an example that is not restricted, but ads for individual units for rent are in scope in the US and Canada. Because Google reviews the landing page too, assume rental ads are in scope.

**Q: Should I bid on "apartments for rent"?**
A: Only with a tight location and phrase or exact match. Broad terms bring browsers from outside your area. Judge each keyword by tours and leases, not clicks.

**Q: Will Google Ads work in a small town?**
A: It depends on search volume. Check Keyword Planner; Google says keywords with very low search volume may not return forecasts. If almost nobody searches, listing sites and your Business Profile may serve you better.

**Q: How long before I see leads?**
A: Calls and tour requests can arrive within days once ads are approved. Give owner campaigns longer, because the sales cycle is longer.
