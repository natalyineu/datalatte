A white-label DSP lets a small agency sell programmatic display, video, audio or CTV under its own brand without building ad tech. Most vendors publish no minimum spend, so the real comparison is entry terms, reporting quality and support. On pages checked in November 2026, StackAdapt, Choozle, Basis and Epom cover the realistic small-agency routes.

Unpublished minimums are not zero minimums. Sales-led platforms often expect five figures a month once the demo ends. Any shortlist should start with one question in writing: what monthly media spend keeps this account open?

## Which white-label DSP fits a small agency?

StackAdapt is the common self-serve start. It publishes no minimum advertising spend and covers display, native, video, CTV and audio, on its site checked in November 2026. Branding controls and client reporting suit agencies testing programmatic with two or three local clients.

Choozle positions for smaller buyers with a simpler interface. Secondary 2026 reports put its platform fee near $99 a month plus media, with some services quoting a $15,000 spend over 90 days for self-serve tiers. Treat those figures as negotiation starting points, not a rate card.

Basis suits agencies wanting planning, buying and billing in one workflow. Secondary reports put typical spend near $10,000 a month, which prices out most local-only rosters. Epom's white-label route advertises entry from $250 in its platform comparison, aimed at networks building their own front end.

## What should an agency check before reselling?

Reporting decides renewals. A client who sees postcode-level delivery, placement lists and cost per call renews; a client who sees one blended CPM does not. Export a real report during the trial and show it to a friendly client before signing anything.

Margin decides survival. **Assumption example.** Assume an agency resells $2,000 of monthly media at a 20% margin, keeping $400. If platform fees and management take 6 hours at $50 an hour, the account loses money before creative costs. The inputs are assumptions; price the management hours honestly, per client, before quoting.

Brand safety decides reputation. Ask which inventory sources, blocklists and CTV apps are included, and whether ads can appear beside unsuitable content. A local client's screenshot of a bad placement travels further than a good results deck.

## FAQ

### What is a white-label DSP?

A demand-side platform an agency rebrands as its own. Clients log into the agency's domain, see the agency's logo, and view reports under the agency's name, while the vendor runs the buying technology. The agency keeps the client relationship and margin; the vendor keeps a platform fee or a share of media.

### How much does a white-label DSP cost?

StackAdapt publishes no minimum spend; Choozle is reported near a $99 monthly platform fee plus media; Epom advertises white-label entry from $250; Basis is reported near $10,000 a month of spend, on secondary sources checked in November 2026. Only the first figure is vendor-published, so confirm every other number in writing.

### Can a small agency make money reselling programmatic?

Yes, when management is priced as its own line. Media margin alone rarely covers reporting, creative tweaks and client calls at local budgets. Agencies that charge a setup fee plus monthly management, and report cost per call or booking rather than impressions, keep programmatic clients longer than agencies selling cheap CPMs.

### White-label DSP or managed service for a first client?

Managed service, almost always. One local client does not justify platform fees, learning time and brand-safety setup. Prove the channel produces calls with a managed buy, then move to self-serve white-label once three or more clients spend steadily and reporting demands a branded dashboard.
