The best yoga studio keywords pair a class style, life stage or offer with your town: "hot yoga [city]", "prenatal yoga [city]", "beginner yoga classes [neighborhood]" and "first yoga class free [city]". Group them by intent, start with phrase and exact match, and keep teacher training in its own campaign. Judge each keyword by what a new student is worth to the studio, not by cost per click. The click price a studio can afford depends on its own numbers. With an assumed $200 maximum cost per new member, a generic "yoga near me" click is worth about $2.50, while an intro-offer click can justify about $5.60.

## Which Yoga Studio Keywords Should You Bid On?

Give each intent group its own ad group and its own landing page. Swap [city] and [neighborhood] for the place names students actually use. Search volumes and per-keyword click prices are not included here because they vary by city and season, so pull them in Keyword Planner.

| Intent | Example keywords | Suggested match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready to visit | "yoga studio [city]", "yoga classes [neighborhood]", "yoga classes near me" | Phrase and exact |
| Style | "hot yoga [city]", "vinyasa yoga [city]", "yin yoga classes [city]" | Phrase and exact |
| Life stage | "prenatal yoga [city]", "postnatal yoga classes [city]", "chair yoga near me" | Phrase and exact |
| Goal | "gentle yoga for stiff backs [city]", "yoga for stress [city]" | Phrase |
| Beginner and offer | "beginner yoga classes [city]", "yoga intro offer [city]" | Phrase |
| Membership | "yoga membership [city]", "unlimited yoga [city]" | Phrase |
| Private and group | "private yoga lessons [city]", "kids yoga classes [city]" | Phrase |
| Teacher training | "200 hour yoga teacher training [region]" | Phrase and exact, own campaign |
| Brand | your studio name, your studio name schedule | Exact |

Only list styles and life-stage classes that appear on the timetable this month. A "prenatal yoga" ad that lands on a page with no prenatal class wastes the click. Goal keywords need careful wording: "gentle classes for stiff backs" describes what is offered, while "cure back pain" promises a result that cannot be backed up. A practice that treats back complaints can compare how it words these terms in the [chiropractor keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-chiropractors-in-2026).

Online yoga is a different product. A studio that streams classes should run it as a separate campaign with its own page, or it will pull local ad groups toward people who will never visit.

## Which Match Types and Negatives Should You Start With?

Start with phrase and exact match. A studio with a few sign-ups a month gives automated bidding little to learn from, so broad match is hard to control at this stage. Phrase match shows how people really word their searches, and exact match locks in the terms already known to fill classes.

Add negatives to class campaigns to block searches that will never convert. Common groups for a yoga studio include:

- Free and do-it-yourself terms: free, DIY, tutorial, YouTube, video, app, at home
- Wrong format: online, virtual, Zoom, livestream
- Training and jobs: teacher training, certification, instructor, jobs, salary, hiring
- Travel: retreat, holiday
- Products: mat, pants, leggings, blocks, straps, equipment
- Poses and theory: poses, sequence, benefits, history, philosophy

Two rules matter most. First, keep "teacher training" as a keyword in its own campaign and as a negative in class campaigns, never both in one campaign. Second, if a studio offers a free first class, keep "first yoga class free" as a keyword and block only plain "free yoga" terms. Remove any negatives for things the studio sells.

Review the search terms report weekly and add irrelevant searches as negatives. The [personal trainer keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-personal-trainers-in-2026) shows a similar review routine for one-to-one training.

## How Should You Structure the Campaigns?

Keep the structure small enough that each ad group collects data.

1. Campaign one covers classes: ready-to-visit, style and life-stage ad groups. Most of the budget starts here.
2. Campaign two covers the intro offer and membership: beginner, offer and price terms, landing on a page that repeats the offer. Judging it by cost per new member is easier when it stands alone.
3. Campaign three covers teacher training: a different geography, budget and ceiling, with a page listing dates, price and curriculum.
4. Campaign four covers brand: the studio name in exact match, so a competitor cannot outrank it on its own name.

Headlines should carry the style, the town and the offer, for example "Hot Yoga in [Town]", "First Class Free" or "Book Online in 30 Seconds". Use only reviews and awards that can be shown.

## What Can One Keyword Afford to Pay?

The same click is worth different amounts depending on what it sells. The example below uses assumptions throughout, so replace each one with the studio's own figures after 30 days of data. The ceiling is the most a studio can pay per click while keeping its target cost per sale.

| | Generic near me | Style class | Intro offer | Teacher training | Brand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Max ad cost per sale (assumption) | $200 | $200 | $200 | $450 | $200 |
| Click to lead rate (assumption) | 5% | 7% | 8% | 5% | 15% |
| Lead to sale rate (assumption) | 25% | 30% | 35% | 10% | 50% |
| Ceiling cost per click | $2.50 | $4.20 | $5.60 | $2.25 | $15.00 |

Formula: ceiling cost per click equals maximum cost per sale times click-to-lead rate times lead-to-sale rate. For the intro offer, that is $200 x 0.08 x 0.35 = $5.60. The $200 limit assumes a $100 monthly fee, so it covers about two months of membership revenue, not profit. The teacher training figure assumes a $3,000 course and allows 15% of that, or $450, to enrol one trainee.

Compare each ceiling with the average cost in Keyword Planner for the studio's city. If the average sits above the ceiling, change an input rather than only the bid. Tightening the page, answering enquiries quickly or raising the price can help. Cutting the bid alone may remove the studio from the auction. If members leave after two or three months, lower the $200 limit, because the break-even point moves with retention.

## How Do You Track Which Keywords Bring Students?

Set up conversion tracking for intro-offer sign-ups, class bookings and phone calls before the campaign goes live. Ask every new student how they found the studio and record the answer next to the keyword group, so offline sign-ups still count. Send each ad group to a landing page that matches its wording. A "prenatal yoga" click should land on prenatal information, not the homepage.

Read the numbers by cost per new member. A cheap click on "yoga poses for beginners" is no bargain if the people who search it do not live near the studio.

## FAQ

### What are the best Google Ads keywords for a yoga studio?
Local phrases that name your style or offer and your place, such as "hot yoga [city]", "prenatal yoga [neighborhood]" or "beginner yoga classes [city]". The best ones depend on the market, so test them and judge by cost per new member.

### How many keywords should a yoga studio start with?
Start with about a dozen phrase and exact keywords across the class, style and offer groups, plus the brand. Add more only when each ad group is collecting data.

### Should I bid on "yoga classes near me"?
Test it in its own ad group. Under the assumptions in the table above, its ceiling is the lowest of the class groups, so check the cost per new member before scaling it.

### Should I use broad match?
Not at the start. Automated bidding needs conversion data to learn from, so tracking and a solid negative list should come first.
