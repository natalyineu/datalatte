A plumber's marketing strategy comes down to three steps in order: fix the Google Business Profile and collect reviews, add pay-per-lead Local Services Ads or Google Search ads for urgent jobs, then track calls and booked jobs. WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks show Home and Home Improvement at $8.33 per click and $90.92 per lead, so every wasted click is expensive. Expect to budget for leads, not clicks.

This is a 90-day plan for owner-operators and small crews in the US. Every figure has a source at the end, and examples are marked as assumptions.

## What Should a Plumber Do in the First 30 Days?

Start with the basics because plumbing is a search-driven trade: people look for help when something is leaking.

Google says local results rely on relevance, distance and prominence. It advises verifying the business, filling in every field, keeping hours current and replying to reviews.

Plumbers usually visit customers rather than host them, which makes them a service-area business in Google's terms. Google says to remove your address if customers do not come to you, and you can list up to 20 service areas, kept within roughly 2 hours of driving.

Then build review volume. BrightLocal found 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and 31% want 4.5 stars or more.

## Should a Plumber Use Local Services Ads or Google Search Ads?

Many plumbers test both. Local Services Ads charge for leads. Google's help says your bid is the most you will pay for a lead, and you may pay less. It also says higher-quality profiles may rank higher and pay lower costs per lead.

Plumbing is an "urgent" category in Google's screening rules. Advertisers face extra checks, and the help page lists business and owner background checks, general liability insurance, state-level licences where local law requires them, and a public, verified Business Profile. Allow time for approval before you plan around the ads.

Google is also moving Local Services campaigns into Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals. Its migration page names plumbing in the August 2026 first phase for selected US providers.

| Channel | You pay for | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Business Profile | No ad cost; ranking cannot be bought | Reviews, categories, service areas |
| Local Services Ads | Leads (bid is a maximum) | Screening; lead quality |
| Search ads | Clicks; calls on a call asset cost the same as headline clicks | Negative keywords, call tracking |
| Website | Your hosting and design costs | Phone number visible, mobile speed |

## How Much Does a Plumbing Lead Cost?

The only published benchmark used here is the closest category, Home and Home Improvement, not plumbers alone. It lists $8.33 per click, an 8.05% conversion rate and $90.92 per lead. Treat it as a ceiling check, not a promise. Your own account data after one month beats any benchmark.

Worked example. Inputs are **assumptions** except the benchmark:

- Monthly ad budget: $1,500.
- Cost per lead: $90.92 (benchmark), giving about 16 leads.
- Leads that become booked jobs: 50% (assumption), about 8 jobs.
- Average job value: $400 (assumption), about $3,200 revenue.
- Cost per booked job: about $188.

If your close rate is 25%, the cost per job doubles to about $375. That is why answering the phone fast matters more than the ad setup. Use call assets and track calls as conversions, and read our [plumber keyword list](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-plumbers-in-2026) to avoid junk searches.

## What Else Should Plumbers Track After Day 60?

Count three numbers each month: calls, booked jobs and cost per booked job. Ask callers how they found you and compare with the ad report.

Check that your website works on a phone and shows the number at the top. Our [plumber website guide](/blog/website-design-for-plumbers) lists what to include. Review replies should be short and specific. See [reviews strategy for home services](/blog/reviews-strategy-for-home-services).

The FTC's rule on fake reviews took effect on 21 October 2024. It bars buying reviews and offering incentives that require a positive review, so ask every customer, not only the happy ones.

## FAQ

### How much should a plumber spend on marketing?
No published figure fits every plumber. Use the benchmark of about $90.92 per lead for home services, then work backwards from the number of jobs you can handle. See the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).

### Are Local Services Ads worth it for plumbers?
They can be, because you pay for leads and not clicks. Plumbing is an urgent category, so expect background checks and insurance proof before ads go live.

### Do I need a website if I have a Business Profile?
Yes, as a place to send calls and show proof. Google's profile ranking also rewards complete information.

### How long until marketing works?
Ads can produce calls in days. Reviews and profile strength build over weeks. No source used here gives a fixed timeline for organic results.

### Should I hide my address?
If customers never visit your premises, Google says to remove your address and list service areas instead.
