The main marketing lessons for local businesses in 2026 are about proof and visibility: have enough recent reviews, answer them, keep your Google profile complete, and pay for ads only where intent is clear. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20. Each lesson below rests on published data.

This article draws on research, platform rules and government figures, not on named business stories. Illustrative numbers are labelled as assumptions.

## Which Lessons Hold Up Across Local Businesses?

| Lesson | Evidence | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reviews decide shortlists | 47% skip businesses with under 20 reviews; 31% want 4.5 stars or more | Ask every customer, every week |
| Replies count | 89% expect a response; 42% unlikely to use a business that never replies | Answer within a week |
| A complete profile ranks better | Google: complete, verified profiles are more likely to appear | Fill every field, keep hours current |
| Buy intent, not awareness | Ad costs vary by category | Test small, track cost per lead |
| Play fair with reviews | FTC rule effective 21 Oct 2024 | Never pay for or fake reviews |

## Why Do Reviews Come First?

Reviews are the cheapest proof a local business has. BrightLocal found 41% of consumers always read reviews, up from 29% the year before, and 32% look for reviews from the last two weeks. Recency matters as much as count.

Where people look is shifting too. Google was used by 71% of consumers, down from 83% in 2025, while AI tools such as ChatGPT rose to 45% from 6%. The source gives these as survey shares, not search volume.

Keep your name, address, hours and services consistent everywhere, so both maps and AI tools find the same facts. See our [guide to Google reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews).

## What Should You Do With Negative Reviews?

Reply politely and offer to fix the problem offline. Google's own advice is that replying to reviews shows you value feedback. BrightLocal found 42% of consumers are unlikely to use a business that never responds.

Do not try to suppress reviews. The FTC's rule bars intimidation, unfounded legal threats and knowingly false accusations aimed at stopping or changing a review. For suspect reviews, read how to [deal with fake Google reviews](/blog/how-to-deal-with-fake-google-reviews).

## How Much Does Size Matter?

Small is normal. UK government figures show 5,690,265 private sector businesses at the start of 2025, of which 5,643,495, or 99.18%, had 0 to 49 employees. You compete with many peers, so a specific, well-reviewed profile counts.

## The Math: How Fast Can a Shop Reach 20 Reviews?

| Step | Assumption | Result |

| Current reviews | 8 | needs 12 more |

Real response rates vary, so measure yours. If only 5% respond, the same goal takes about 12 weeks. Make the ask part of checkout, not an afterthought, and stay within the FTC's rules.

## FAQ

### What is the most important marketing lesson for a local business?
Reviews. They influence both who finds you and whether people choose you.

### How many reviews do I need?
BrightLocal found 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and 74% only consider reviews from the last three months.

### Are ads worth it for a local business?
Sometimes. Cost per lead ranges from $29.96 to $90.92 in the categories above. Test with a small budget and track results.

### Do consumers use AI to find local businesses?
BrightLocal says 45% used AI tools such as ChatGPT, up from 6%. That is a survey share, not search volume.

### Can I pay customers for reviews?
Not for a positive review. The FTC's rule bars incentives that require one.
