Is native advertising worth it for a small local business? Usually not as a first channel. Outbrain's self-serve minimum is 20 USD a day, about 600 USD a month. Taboola's Realize help page says tight local targeting may need a bid 30% to 50% higher than your run-of-network campaigns.

And the FTC expects native ads to be clearly labelled as ads. It can still work when you have a useful page to promote, such as a guide or an offer, and when search and your Google Business Profile are already doing their job.

## What Is Native Advertising?

Native ads are paid placements designed to match the look of the content around them, such as a recommended-article box at the bottom of a news page. Content discovery networks such as Outbrain and Taboola sell this format on publisher websites.

The risk is that readers may not realise it is an ad. The FTC's guide for businesses states the core rule: "An advertisement or promotional message shouldn't suggest or imply to consumers that it's anything other than an ad" ([FTC: Native advertising, a guide for businesses](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/native-advertising-guide-businesses)).

For a small business this means two jobs, not one: make the ad good enough to earn a click, and make the label clear enough to stay compliant.

## What Does Native Advertising Cost?

Outbrain's help center says the minimum daily budget to create a campaign is 20 USD, 20 EUR or 20 GBP, and that you pay only for the clicks you accrue, up to the daily budget ([Outbrain: Budget and duration](https://www.outbrain.com/help/advertisers/budget-duration/)). Cost per click is set by you.

Taboola's advertiser platform, now called Realize, does not publish a minimum spend in the pages used for this guide, and listing sites show pricing on request. Ask a sales contact for the current minimum before planning around it.

For context, LocaliQ's 2026 search benchmarks show a median cost per click of 5.42 USD and cost per lead of 66.69 USD across industries, and 4.06 USD per click for Animals and Pets ([LocaliQ search advertising benchmarks](https://www.localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/)). Those are search figures. Native readers were not searching, so their intent is lower than a searcher's. LocaliQ does not publish native benchmarks on that page, so none are quoted here.

## Can Native Ads Target a Single City?

Yes, with a cost. Taboola Realize lists location levels of countries, regions, cities (after you choose a country or region), DMAs and postal codes. It warns that when targeting regions, DMAs or postal codes you "may need to set your CPC to be approximately 30% to 50% higher" than your run-of-network campaigns.

Regions, DMAs and postal codes can be targeted for only one country per campaign ([Taboola Realize: Campaign location targeting options](https://realize.com/help/en/articles/3878037-campaign-location-targeting-options)).

The reason is simple: a small area means fewer eligible readers, so you bid more to win them. Local reach is possible, but you pay for the precision.

## Worked Example: What Does 600 Dollars Buy?

All numbers are **assumptions**. Replace them with your own.

- Budget: 600 USD a month (20 USD a day).
- Run-of-network cost per click: 0.50 USD.
- Local targeting uplift: 40% (the midpoint of Taboola's 30% to 50%), so 0.70 USD per click.
- Clicks: 600 divided by 0.70, about 857.

How to read it: at a 1% conversion rate the cost per lead of 70 USD sits above LocaliQ's cross-industry search median of 66.69 USD, so search wins. At 4% native looks cheap. The deciding number is what a visitor does after the click, not the click price.

A reader who has never heard of you may convert less often than a searcher. Treat 1% as the prudent case until your own data says otherwise.

## When Should a Small Business Choose Native Ads?

Choose native when all of these are true:

1. **Search and your profile are in place.** Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) is the cheaper first step.
2. **You have a page worth reading.** A guide, a menu story, a before-and-after, or an offer with a clear next step.
3. **You can afford the test.** At 20 USD a day, a one-month test is about 600 USD.
4. **You can track leads.** Without a booking or form count, you cannot read the table above.

Skip it if you need customers this week, run a single small location with a thin budget or sell something people only buy when they search for it. ## How Do You Label a Native Ad Properly?

The FTC guide gives specific advice ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/native-advertising-guide-businesses)):

- Use terms such as "Ad", "Advertisement", "Paid Advertisement" or "Sponsored Advertising Content".
- Avoid "Promoted" or "Promoted Stories", which it calls at best ambiguous.
- "Presented by" or "Sponsored by" may be read as funding rather than creating the content.
- A logo or brand name alone is generally not enough.
- Place the disclosure where people look, most often immediately in front of or above the headline.
- If the ad can also be reached directly, disclose on both the main page and the click-through page.

The guide is informal staff guidance and not legal advice. If you are unsure, ask a lawyer who knows advertising law in your country. Rules outside the US differ.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do I need to start with Outbrain?**
A: Outbrain's help center sets a minimum of 20 USD a day per campaign, charged only for clicks received. That is the technical minimum, not a recommendation for a good test.

**Q: Does local targeting cost more?**
A: Taboola Realize says region, DMA or postal code targeting may need a CPC around 30% to 50% higher than run-of-network campaigns because the audience is smaller.

**Q: Do native ads need to say they are ads?**
A: Under the FTC's guidance, an ad should not imply it is anything other than an ad, and any necessary disclosure must be clear and prominent. Clear wording such as "Ad" or "Advertisement" is among the terms it lists.

**Q: Is native better than Google Ads for a local shop?**
A: They do different jobs. Search reaches people already looking. Native reaches readers who were not. For most local shops, search comes first.

**Q: How long should I test?**
A: The sources used here give no required duration. Set a spend limit instead, such as one month at your daily budget, and a target cost per lead before you start.
