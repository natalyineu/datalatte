A local business should run a short Facebook video ad with a clear offer in the first seconds, captions on screen, a local audience and one action such as a call or a booking. Start with traffic or lead campaigns on a small budget. LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmarks (updated September 23, 2026) give an all-industry average of $0.60 per click for traffic campaigns and $27.39 per lead for lead campaigns.

This guide covers format, structure, budget and measurement. Figures come from named sources at the end. Advice that is not backed by a source is marked as a suggestion, and examples are marked as assumptions.

## What Format Should a Video Ad Use?

Meta's Ads Guide lists the following for Facebook Feed video: 4:5 aspect ratio, 1440 x 1800 pixels, MP4, MOV or GIF files up to 4 GB, H.264 compression, stereo AAC audio at 128 kbps or higher. Captions and sound are optional but recommended. Meta's page also notes that with the Awareness objective, or certain reach and engagement goals, the footer with headline, description and button does not show on mobile Feed.

Specs for Reels and Stories could not be read from Meta's pages during this check. Third-party guides agree on 9:16 at 1080 x 1920 but disagree on safe zones and length, so confirm in Ads Manager's preview before launch.

## How Should You Structure a 15-Second Video?

The structure below is a suggestion, not a Meta rule. No source reviewed gives an ideal length.

1. **Seconds 0 to 3:** show the thing or place the viewer cares about and say who it is for ("Dog groomer in your town").
2. **Seconds 3 to 10:** one proof point you can show, such as a before and after, a menu item or a class in progress.
3. **Seconds 10 to 15:** one action. Name it ("Tap to book") and match the button.

Because Meta recommends captions, put the main message on screen as text so the ad works without sound. Film on a phone in natural light, and use your real staff and space.

## How Do You Make Several Ads from One Video?

Record one master video of 30 to 60 seconds. Cut three or four versions that differ only in the opening line, the first shot or the offer. Then run them in one ad set and compare cost per result. This tests one variable at a time without new filming. Keep the file formats above, and export a 4:5 version for Feed.

## What Should You Spend?

Meta does not publish a minimum that guarantees results, and the learning-phase rules could not be read on Meta's help pages during this check, so no minimum is stated here. A workable approach is to set a test budget you can afford to lose and run it for at least two to four weeks before judging. ## FAQ

### How long should a Facebook video ad be?

Meta's spec for Feed allows from 1 second to 241 minutes, so length is your choice. Fifteen seconds is a sensible starting point, but test it against your own results.

### Do I need a Facebook Page?

Yes for Facebook ads. Check Meta's current account requirements in Ads Manager, as they were not reviewed here.

### Should captions be on?

Meta recommends captions. Treat sound as a bonus.

### How often should I change the video?

No source gives a number. Watch frequency and cost per result in Ads Manager and swap the video when results worsen.

### Can a small budget work?

It can produce data, but slowly. A test needs enough budget to produce a handful of leads each week. Watch cost per lead against the benchmark above.
