LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put the average Google Search lead in Beauty & Personal Care at $39.25. A lead is not yet a customer: if 3 in 10 of those leads book (our assumption), a new customer costs about $131 in ads. A $20 salon credit paid only after a referred friend has paid for a first visit costs you nothing until the sale happens. That is the appeal of affiliate marketing for a local business: you pay for results, not for attention.

It also has a catch that most guides bury. The moment a customer, a neighbouring business or a local creator is rewarded for recommending you, advertising rules apply to what they say. This guide covers how a pay-on-result partner programme works for salons, cafés, studios and groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada, how to set rewards, how to track them, and what the FTC, the UK ASA and Canada's Ad Standards say about disclosure. It is general information, not legal or tax advice.

## What "Affiliate" Means for a Local Business

An affiliate is anyone who promotes you using a unique code, link or form and gets rewarded when someone becomes a paying customer. Locally, that comes in four shapes:

| Model | Who promotes | Typical reward | Disclosure risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer referral | Your regulars | Credit or free service | Low if private; higher if they post publicly |
| Local partner | A wedding planner, physio, vet, pet shop | Reciprocal referrals or a flat fee | Medium |
| Creator or community admin | Local bloggers, Instagram creators, group admins | Free product plus code or fee | High, public endorsements |
| Online affiliate | Coupon and review sites, networks | Percentage per sale | High |

Customer referrals and local partners are the best starting point. For creator partnerships specifically (free products, content briefs, contracts) see our [influencer marketing guide for fitness studios](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-fitness), which applies to any niche. For the customer-to-customer side, our [referral marketing guide](/blog/referral-marketing-guide) and [salon referral programme without discounts](/blog/salon-referral-program-without-discounts) go deeper.

## Do Referred Customers Actually Pay Off?

There is one rigorous study worth knowing. Schmitt, Skiera and Van den Bulte tracked about 10,000 customers of a leading German bank for almost three years and published the results in the Journal of Marketing in January 2011. They found referred customers had a higher contribution margin (though the gap eroded over time) and higher retention (which persisted), and that a referred customer was on average at least 16% more valuable than a similar non-referred customer. They also found the size of the gap varied across customer segments and advised a selective approach.

Treat this carefully. It is one bank in one country, not a salon or a café. Use it as a reason to test and to track retention of referred customers separately, not as a promise of a 16% bonus.

## Step 1: Work Out What You Can Pay

Start from what a customer is worth and what you pay now.

1. **Gross profit per new customer in the first year** (revenue minus direct costs). This is your ceiling.
2. **What you pay today.** Divide your ad spend by new customers, or use the benchmark: LocaliQ's search figures show Restaurants & Food at $30.57 per lead, Animals & Pets at $31.50, Beauty & Personal Care at $39.25, Personal Services at $54.60 and Health & Fitness at $67.36. Divide by your lead-to-customer rate to get cost per customer.
3. **Set the reward well below the ceiling** and below what ads cost you, so referral stays your cheapest channel even after admin time.

Credit and free services usually cost you less than their face value and bring the customer back. Cash works better for partner businesses. Pay only after a first paid visit, not at sign-up.

## Step 2: Write Rules That Fit on a Card

- Who counts as new: never booked or bought before.
- When the reward is earned: after the first paid visit clears.
- How and when it is paid, and any limit per person or per month.
- What counts as abuse: self-referral, fake bookings, buying codes.
- Whether the new customer also gets a welcome offer.

Short rules prevent disputes and mean staff can explain the programme in 20 seconds.

## Step 3: Track Every Referral

From simplest to most advanced:

1. **Referral by name.** The new customer says who sent them. Free, but depends on memory.
2. **Unique codes.** ANNA10 for each partner, entered at booking or checkout.
3. **Unique links.** A partner-specific booking page or tagged link. Google's [UTM guide for Analytics](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952) says to always use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, and that values are case sensitive, so keep everything lowercase (for example `utm_source=anna` and `utm_medium=referral`).
4. **Software.** Referral and affiliate tools exist, and booking and point-of-sale systems often include basic versions. Pricing varies, and you should check it before committing.

Most local businesses start with codes and a spreadsheet with six columns: date, partner, customer, first-visit paid (yes or no), reward owed and reward paid.

## Step 4: Recruit Five Partners, Not Fifty

- **Best customers.** Ask in person, at checkout and in your confirmation emails.
- **Complementary businesses.** A groomer and a vet, a gym and a physio, a florist and a photographer. Agree reciprocal referrals where possible.
- **Local creators and group admins.** Check each group's rules before posting.
- **Staff.** A small bonus for referrals, with the disclosure point below.

Give each partner a code, a ready-made message and a free trial, so they speak from experience. A partner who has tried your service is also much safer from a disclosure and honesty standpoint.

## Step 5: Disclosure Rules You Cannot Skip

This is the part that turns an informal favour into an advertising matter. The rules below are what each regulator's pages say; check your own country's current rules before launch.

### United States: FTC

The FTC's [Endorsement Guides Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) says a connection that a significant minority of consumers would not expect should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Free products given with the expectation of promotion count, and affiliate commissions require disclosure; the page's example wording is "I get commissions for purchases made through links in this post." It adds that:

- "Affiliate link" on its own may not be understood by consumers. "Paid link" placed right next to the affiliate link is considered adequate, and "commissionable link" is probably not clear enough.
- The closer the disclosure is to the recommendation, the better.
- Advertisers need reasonable programmes to train and monitor the members of their network, including periodically searching for what they are saying and acting on questionable practices. Delegating to an outside firm does not remove responsibility, and the page states that your company is ultimately responsible for what others do on its behalf.
- Employees endorsing their employer should disclose the relationship, and listing the employer on a profile is not enough.

The FTC's [Disclosures 101 for influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) covers wording and placement. Disclosures should be hard to miss and placed with the endorsement, not buried in a profile, behind a "more" click or in a block of hashtags. In a video, the disclosure should be in the video itself. "Ad," "advertisement" and "sponsored" are acceptable, while "sp," "collab" and a stand-alone "ambassador" are not. The page puts the responsibility to disclose on the influencer, but the Q&A above puts a monitoring duty on you.

**Incentives for reviews are a separate trap.** The FTC's [Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) says the rule, effective October 21, 2024, prohibits implying a review has to be positive to earn an incentive; its example is "tell us how much you loved your visit... and get a $5 coupon." So reward referrals, never reviews, and never word an offer so that a good opinion is required. Our guide to getting more Google reviews shows how to ask without incentives.

### United Kingdom: ASA

The ASA's [affiliate marketing guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html) says marketing must be obviously identifiable as such. Labels that work include "Ad" or "#Ad" at the start of a post, title or description. Labels the ASA has found to fail include "*affiliate" on an Instagram Story, a spaced-out "a d", "#collab", bottom-of-post disclaimers and vague wording such as "may earn an affiliate commission." The ASA also says both the business and the affiliate are responsible under the Code, so a brand cannot hand responsibility to its affiliates.

### Canada and Australia

Ad Standards Canada's [influencer marketing page](https://adstandards.ca/programs/influencer-marketing/) says disclosures of material connections must be clear, prominent and in close proximity to the representation being made. It lists managing affiliate disclosures in its 2025 guidelines but the page itself does not set out the details. For Australia, check the ACCC's current influencer and testimonial guidance; this guide does not summarise it.

### What this means in practice

- Put one sentence in every partner agreement: "You must label any post or message that promotes us for a reward as an ad, at the start, in plain words."
- Give partners approved wording, and ask to see paid posts before they go live.
- Search for your brand name and your codes monthly, and ask partners to fix or delete anything unclear.
- Do not let partners claim results you cannot back up. Your own claims rules still apply.

## Step 6: Money, Tax and Regulated Fields

- **Tax (US).** The IRS instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC say you generally file a 1099-NEC for each person you paid at least $2,000 in nonemployee compensation, a threshold that rose to $2,000 for tax years beginning after 2025. Filing is due on January 31. Whether your referral payments count as nonemployee compensation depends on the facts; ask an accountant before you pay anyone regularly. Other countries have different rules.
- **Regulated professions.** Healthcare, legal, financial and some other services may restrict paying for referrals. We have not verified the rules by country, so check with your professional body before launching.
- **Privacy.** Collect referral data lawfully and get consent before contacting people the partner names.

## The Math

Take a salon with an assumed $300 of first-year revenue per new customer and $180 of gross profit. All cost figures other than the LocaliQ lead cost are **assumptions** to replace with your own.

| | Search ads | Customer referral | Local partner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source of cost figure | LocaliQ 2026, Beauty & Personal Care | Assumption | Assumption |
| Cost per lead | $39.25 | n/a | n/a |
| Lead to customer rate (assumption) | 30% | 100% (reward paid only on a sale) | 100% (paid only on a sale) |
| Reward | n/a | $20 credit for referrer | 15% of an $85 first order = $12.75 |
| Welcome offer for new customer | n/a | $10 | $10 |
| Admin time (assumption: 15 minutes at $25 an hour) | n/a | $6.25 | $6.25 |
| Cost per new customer | $131 | $36 | $29 |
| First-year gross profit left per customer | $49 | $144 | $151 |

Search ads cost $39.25 / 0.30 = $131. The referral rows add the reward, welcome offer and admin time. Three conclusions:

- **Pay-on-result is cheaper per customer, but it does not scale on demand.** If 40 regulars each refer 0.15 friends a month (assumption), that is six customers a month. Ads can be turned up; referrals have to be earned.
- **Credit costs less than face value.** If your margin on a $20 service credit is 60% (assumption), its real cost is closer to $8, which would lower the referral column further.
- **Limit by your ceiling.** If gross profit per customer is $180, a reward of $20 plus $10 is under a fifth of that. A reward of $90 would not be.

Revenue is not profit, and the German bank result may not carry over to your business. Track the retention of referred customers at 3 and 6 months.

## Do This Today

1. Calculate gross profit on a new customer in year one, and what you pay per customer today. Use the LocaliQ line for your industry if you have no ad data.
2. Choose a reward at well under a fifth of gross profit, payable only after the first paid visit, and write the rules on one card.
3. Set up a code per partner and a six-column tracking sheet. Add a "referred by" question to your booking form.
4. Write a one-sentence disclosure rule ("label it as an ad, at the start, in plain words") into your partner message.
5. Invite five partners this week: three regulars and two neighbouring businesses, each with a free trial.

## FAQ

**Q: Is affiliate marketing the same as a referral programme?**
A: They overlap. Referral programmes reward customers for introducing friends, usually with credit. Affiliate programmes reward partners or creators who promote you more widely, often with a commission. The disclosure rules matter most when the promotion is public.

**Q: Do I have to disclose that someone is rewarded for recommending me?**
A: In the US, the FTC says affiliate commissions and free products should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, close to the recommendation. The UK ASA expects an upfront "Ad" label. Rules differ by country; check your own regulator.

**Q: Am I responsible if a partner posts something misleading?**
A: The FTC's Q&A says advertisers need reasonable programmes to train and monitor their network and that your company is ultimately responsible for what others do on its behalf. The ASA says both brand and affiliate are responsible. Brief partners in writing and check their posts.

**Q: Can I reward customers for Google reviews?**
A: Avoid it. The FTC says you cannot imply that a review must be positive to earn an incentive. Keep referral rewards for sales, not reviews.

**Q: Do I need software?**
A: Not at first. Codes and a spreadsheet are enough for a small programme. Move to software when you have too many partners to track by hand.
