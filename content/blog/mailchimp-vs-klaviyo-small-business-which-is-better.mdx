For most local service businesses, such as cafes, salons, groomers and studios, Mailchimp is the easier and usually better fit. Klaviyo is worth it if you run an online shop (especially on Shopify or WooCommerce) and want advanced automations based on what customers buy and browse.

Both tools are established, and both change plans and pricing regularly, so check current details on their websites before you decide. The comparison below focuses on how each works in practice, and which kind of business benefits from which.

## The short version

| | Mailchimp | Klaviyo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built for | General small business marketing | Ecommerce and online retail |
| Ease of use | Beginner friendly | More of a learning curve |
| Strongest at | Newsletters, simple automations, landing pages, all-in-one tools | Behaviour-based flows, product data, segmentation |

## How Mailchimp works for small businesses

Mailchimp started as a newsletter tool and has grown into a broader marketing platform. For a small local business, the useful features are:

- **Drag-and-drop email builder** with templates, so you can send a good-looking newsletter without a designer.
- **Sign-up forms and landing pages** to collect subscribers.
- **Basic automations,** such as a welcome email or a birthday message.
- **Audience tags and segments** to group contacts by behaviour or details you capture.
- **Integrations** with booking and sales systems, including Square and Shopify, plus many others via connectors.
- **Reports** showing opens, clicks and unsubscribes.

**Drawbacks:** The pricing tiers can feel confusing, costs rise as your contact list grows, and some advanced features sit on higher plans. Mailchimp counts contacts including those who unsubscribed in some cases, so keep your list tidy. Always check the current plan page.

## How Klaviyo works for small businesses

Klaviyo is designed around customer data, mainly from online stores. It watches what people browse, add to a basket and buy, and uses that to trigger messages automatically.

Useful features include:

- **Pre-built flows** for welcome series, abandoned carts, browse abandonment and post-purchase.
- **Detailed segmentation** based on purchase history, spend and product interest.
- **Product feeds** that pull items into emails.
- **Email and SMS together** in the same system.
- **Predictive and reporting tools** that show revenue linked to campaigns.

**Drawbacks:** It is more complex to set up, and its value depends on having ecommerce data flowing in. If you only sell appointments or walk-in coffee, much of its strength goes unused. Pricing scales with the number of profiles and sends, and it can become more expensive than Mailchimp for businesses that do not use the advanced features.

## Pricing: what to expect

Both tools offer entry-level plans that are free or low-cost for small lists, and both increase in price as your list grows. As a rough guide, small lists typically cost somewhere between free and a few tens of dollars a month, and larger lists or advanced features cost more. These tools revise tiers often, so look at each pricing calculator with your real list size and check what is included, including SMS, automations and support.

Ask these questions before you buy:

1. What does my plan cost at my current list size, and at double the size?
2. Do I pay for unsubscribed or inactive contacts?
3. Are automations and segmentation included?
4. How much do SMS messages cost, and are they available in my country?
5. What does it cost to cancel or downgrade?

## Which business should choose which?

**Choose Mailchimp if you are:**

- A coffee shop sending a monthly newsletter and offers.
- A salon, barbershop or groomer sending reminders and promotions.
- A fitness studio sending class news and trial offers.
- A business that wants simplicity and has no online store.

**Choose Klaviyo if you are:**

- A coffee roaster selling beans online through Shopify or WooCommerce.
- A salon or pet business with a sizeable online retail store.
- A business that wants abandoned-cart and browse-based automations.
- Someone willing to invest time in setup and testing.

## When neither is the right answer

There are other tools worth a look, depending on your needs:

- **MailerLite, Brevo or Constant Contact** for simple, often cheaper email.
- **ActiveCampaign** for deeper automation. See our [ActiveCampaign vs Mailchimp comparison](/blog/activecampaign-vs-mailchimp-local-business).
- **Square Marketing, Vagaro, Fresha or similar** if your booking or POS system already includes email and SMS to your customers.
- **Your booking system's built-in tools** for appointment reminders and rebooking prompts.

If your customers are already in a POS or booking app, using its built-in messages may save time and avoid syncing issues.

## How to choose in an afternoon

1. List what you want to send: newsletter, welcome email, reminders, abandoned carts, SMS.
2. Check where your customer data lives: booking app, POS, online store or spreadsheet.
3. Check integration options for your key system.
4. Test the free plan or trial for a week with a small list.
5. Compare the price for your real list size.
6. Choose the simplest tool that does what you need.

Switching later is possible, but exporting and rebuilding automations takes time, so pick with a view of the next two years.

## What to set up first, whichever you choose

Starting with a few simple automations does more for most small businesses than a long list of features:

1. **Welcome email** sent as soon as someone joins, introducing your business and any sign-up offer.
2. **Birthday or anniversary message,** if you collect those dates.
3. **Win-back email** for customers who have not visited in a while.
4. **Review request** after a purchase or appointment.
5. **Monthly newsletter** with one clear offer and a link to book or order.

Both tools can handle these, so the real difference is how comfortable you are with the interface and whether your data syncs from your other systems.

## A note on deliverability

Whichever tool you pick, emails only work if they reach the inbox. Authenticate your sending domain (the tool will guide you through SPF and DKIM), send only to people who opted in, avoid spammy subject lines and prune inactive contacts. These habits matter more than the logo on the dashboard.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Klaviyo better than Mailchimp?**

A: For ecommerce, often yes, thanks to its data and automation depth. For most local service businesses, Mailchimp is simpler and enough.

**Q: Can I use Klaviyo without an online store?**

A: You can, but you would use only a fraction of what it offers, and it may cost more than a simpler tool.

**Q: Which is cheaper?**

A: It depends on your list size, sending volume and features. Compare both calculators with your real numbers, and check current plans.

**Q: Can I switch later?**

A: Yes. You can export your contacts, but you will need to rebuild automations and templates, so choose carefully.
