At 500 contacts, Mailchimp Standard and Klaviyo's Email plan both cost roughly $20 a month. At 5,000 they drift apart to about $75 and $100. That gap is small enough that for most local businesses price is not the deciding factor. What decides it is where your customer data lives. If your customers book through an app, pay at a till and walk in, you are mostly sending newsletters, reminders and offers, and the simpler tool wins. If your customers browse and buy on a website, and you want to message them about what they looked at, Klaviyo earns its keep.

This guide is for owners of cafés, salons, groomers, studios and small online shops in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. A companion piece, Klaviyo vs Mailchimp, covers a broader feature comparison. Here the focus is narrower: what each tool costs at the list sizes local businesses actually have, which one fits which kind of data, and how many extra customer visits you need to pay for it. Prices are US dollars and change often, so check both pricing pages before you buy.

## What the Pricing Pages Say

Both vendors offer a free plan with the same size cap. According to [Mailchimp's pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/), Free covers up to 250 contacts, with sends capped at 500 a month or 250 a day, one user and one audience. Paid plans move up through Essentials (adds scheduling, branding removal and up to four automation flow steps), Standard (up to 6,000 emails a month, up to 200 automation flows, dynamic content) and Premium (up to 150,000 emails a month, A/B testing, predictive segmentation). Pricing scales by contact tier, and the page says SMS is an add-on to paid plans in select countries, sold as monthly credit blocks.

[Klaviyo's pricing page](https://www.klaviyo.com/pricing) lists a free plan with up to 250 active profiles, 500 email sends a month, and $5 of mobile messages and $5 of Composer usage per month. It did not show paid prices in the text that loaded, because they sit behind a slider.

The same sources put Mailchimp Essentials from $13 a month, Klaviyo Email+SMS from about $35 with US texts around $0.01 each (and picture messages around $0.03), and note that Klaviyo counts any contactable profile toward your tier, not just those you email. That site's Mailchimp page also reports a free tier of 1,000 sends a month, while Mailchimp's own page says 500, so use Mailchimp's number and treat the tracker's figures as estimates.

One more billing point, from third-party guides rather than Mailchimp's own help pages (which would not load): several 2026 guides say Mailchimp counts unsubscribed contacts toward your plan unless you archive or delete them, and sources disagree on which action lowers the bill. Check your own account's contact count before you assume you are on the cheaper tier.

## The Real Difference: Data

Pricing is similar, so look at what each tool sees.

**Mailchimp is a general-purpose marketing platform.** Its [integrations page](https://mailchimp.com/integrations/) lists Shopify, WooCommerce and Square under "Most Popular", and has a booking and scheduling category. It does newsletters, sign-up forms, landing pages and basic automation. For a café with a loyalty list or a salon with an appointment book, that is the job.

**Klaviyo is built around purchase and browse behaviour.** Its strength, as the market consistently describes it, is e-commerce flows: abandoned cart, browse abandonment and post-purchase sequences triggered by what a shopper did. Klaviyo's integrations page did not load for this article, so confirm the integration for your own store or POS inside the app. The practical rule: Klaviyo is only as smart as the data you feed it.

| Your business | Where customer data lives | Better fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Café, no online ordering | POS, loyalty app, sign-up form | Mailchimp |
| Salon or groomer | Booking app | Mailchimp, or your booking tool's built-in messaging |
| Studio with online class packs | Booking app plus a small web shop | Mailchimp first, Klaviyo if web sales grow |
| Coffee roaster selling beans online | Shopify or WooCommerce | Klaviyo |
| Small retail shop with an online store | Shopify or WooCommerce | Klaviyo |

The table is judgement based on how the tools are designed, not a measured outcome. If your booking system has no direct integration with either tool, you will be uploading CSV exports, and then a simpler tool is a gift.

## Where Mailchimp Wins for Local Businesses

- **Familiar, forgiving interface.** A first newsletter takes an afternoon.
- **Sensible free start.** 250 contacts at no cost covers a new café's list while you learn.
- **Square, Shopify and WooCommerce connections** for a small shop selling at a till and online.
- **Enough automation for the five emails that matter** (below).

Weaknesses worth knowing: plan features are split across tiers (the Customer Journey Builder, which Automation Atlas calls its core automation feature, is not on Free or Essentials), costs rise with list size, and a messy list costs money.

## Where Klaviyo Wins

- **Segmenting by what people bought or viewed**, rather than just by tags you add by hand.
- **Email and SMS in one place**, billed per message on the SMS plan.
- **Revenue attribution** tied to campaigns and flows, which matters when a campaign has a measurable sales page.

Weaknesses: it assumes data you may not have, and its value shrinks if you only sell appointments or walk-in coffee. Our guide to [using Klaviyo for email and SMS in local business](/blog/using-klaviyo-for-email-and-sms-marketing-in-local-business) shows where a local business can still use it well.

## The Five Emails to Build, Whichever You Choose

Start with a handful of flows. The tool matters less than whether these exist:

1. **Welcome email**, sent when someone joins, with your offer and your best link.
2. **Birthday or anniversary**, if you collect the date.
3. **Win-back**, to customers who have not visited in 60 to 90 days (a threshold you should tune to your own visit pattern).
4. **Review request** after a visit.
5. **Monthly newsletter** with one offer and one button.

For a café version, see Mailchimp for coffee shops and [AI-assisted email marketing for coffee shops](/blog/ai-assisted-email-marketing-for-coffee-shops).

## Deliverability Basics

Whatever you choose, the email has to arrive. Google's [email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) say all senders should set up SPF or DKIM for their sending domain, use TLS, and keep the spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.30%, with a recommended rate under 0.10%. Senders of more than 5,000 messages a day also need SPF, DKIM and DMARC together, and one-click unsubscribe on marketing mail. A local list will usually sit far below 5,000 a day, but authenticating your domain is still the right habit. Both tools walk you through it.

## The Math

How many extra visits does the tool need to generate? Take a café with a $7 average ticket and 65% margin, so $4.55 of gross profit per visit. Every row is an **assumption** for illustration, except the plan prices, which are the approximate Automation Atlas figures above.

| | 500 contacts: Mailchimp | 500 profiles: Klaviyo | 5,000 contacts: Mailchimp | 5,000 profiles: Klaviyo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plan per month (approximate) | $20 | $20 | $75 | $100 |
| Margin per extra visit (assumption) | $4.55 | $4.55 | $4.55 | $4.55 |
| Extra visits to cover the plan | 4.4 | 4.4 | 16.5 | 22 |
| Share of list that must visit once more per month | 0.9% | 0.9% | 0.3% | 0.4% |
| Optional: US text blast to full list at $0.01 each | n/a | $5 | n/a | $50 |

How to read it: at 500 contacts, you pay for the tool with about 4 extra visits a month, which one decent newsletter should manage. At 5,000, you need roughly 17 to 22 extra visits, still only a fraction of a percent of the list. The tool price is not the risk. The risk is a list that never opens anything, or a flow that never gets built. A plan only returns its cost if you send, so the break-even logic matters more than the logo.

Your own break-even: monthly plan cost divided by gross profit per visit. If Klaviyo costs $25 more than Mailchimp at your size, that is about 5.5 more visits a month, and it is worth it only if Klaviyo flows (cart, browse, post-purchase) actually produce them. If your customers do not shop online, they will not.

## How to Choose in an Afternoon

1. Write down where your customer data lives: till, booking app, website or spreadsheet.
2. List the emails you really will send in the next 90 days.
3. Open both pricing pages and compare at your current list size and at double.
4. Test the free plan with 50 real contacts and send yourself a campaign.
5. Pick the simplest tool that covers your list. If you are still unsure, compare the other side of the market in ActiveCampaign vs Mailchimp.

## Do This Today

1. Count your contacts, and delete or archive bounces and long-dead addresses so you do not pay for them.
2. Note where each customer record lives and whether either tool lists an integration for it.
3. Pull up both pricing pages and write down the cost at your size and at double.
4. Sign up for one free plan and import 50 contacts; send a welcome email to yourself.
5. Set up SPF and DKIM through the tool's guided domain authentication before your first real send.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Klaviyo better than Mailchimp?**
A: For online stores with purchase and browse data, usually. For businesses selling appointments or walk-in coffee, Mailchimp is simpler and enough. The approximate prices are close at small list sizes.

**Q: Which is cheaper?**
A: Per Automation Atlas, about $20 each at 500 contacts, and about $75 for Mailchimp Standard versus $100 for Klaviyo Email at 5,000. Check each pricing calculator, since both change tiers often.

**Q: Can I use Klaviyo without an online store?**
A: Yes, but you will use a fraction of it. Klaviyo's free plan covers 250 profiles and 500 sends a month if you want to test.

**Q: What are the free plan limits?**
A: Mailchimp Free: 250 contacts, 500 sends a month. Klaviyo Free: 250 active profiles, 500 email sends a month.

**Q: Can I switch later?**
A: Yes. You can export contacts, but you will need to rebuild templates and automations, so choose with the next two years in view.
