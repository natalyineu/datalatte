A nail salon gets bookings from Instagram when every post shows finished work and every profile visit ends in one clear booking step. Use carousels for before and after sets (up to 20 photos or videos), reels under 3 minutes for reach beyond followers, and a booking button or link.

This guide is for independent nail salons and nail technicians. Platform limits come from Instagram and Meta documentation. No vendor publishes a typical booking rate from Instagram, so none is quoted, and the example is labelled as an assumption.

## What Should a Nail Salon Post on Instagram?

Post proof that you do the work, in formats Instagram supports. A carousel can hold up to 20 photos and videos, so one post can show a full set from bare nails to final shot. Reels suit time-lapses of a design being painted.

Useful repeatable post types:

- Finished sets, with the style or shade named in the caption
- Short time-lapse reels of one design
- Nail care tips tied to a service you sell
- A monthly "open slots" post with the booking link

## How Do Reels and Hashtags Fit?

Instagram's creator FAQ says it recommends videos to unconnected audiences that are 3 minutes or less, so keep reels short. It also says relevant, trending hashtags can help reach more people and warns that irrelevant ones may hurt engagement and distribution. The FAQ gives no number of hashtags to use.

Instagram also advises removing third-party watermarks, which may affect reach, and encourages original content. If you reuse a clip from another app, export it without the watermark. ## How Do You Turn Profile Visits into Bookings?

Instagram professional accounts can add one action button, such as Book, through a partner. Meta's Help Center says setting up these buttons depends on working with a partner, and only one button can be active at a time. Check the current partner list in the app, because it changes.

If your booking tool is not supported, put its link in your bio and in Stories. Keep the link on the same domain as your website so the path from post to booking is short. ## Can You Pay Local Creators or Models?

Yes, but disclose it. The FTC's endorsement guides say a material connection between the endorser and the business should be disclosed "clearly and conspicuously", and that payment or anything of value usually counts, including a free service. The disclosure belongs in the post itself, not only in a comment or a link.

Get written permission before posting a client's hands or face. Our guide on [before and after photos](/blog/hair-salon-before-after-photos-legal-and-converting) explains consent, and the same logic applies to nail work.

## Should a Nail Salon Run Instagram Ads?

Only after the organic basics work. Paid reach does not fix a profile with no booking path. If you test ads, set a small budget and track cost per booking. ## FAQ

### How often should a nail salon post on Instagram?

No platform source gives a required frequency. Pick a rate you can keep, such as the five posts a week in the example, and review results monthly.

### How many hashtags should I use?

Instagram's creator FAQ gives no number. It says relevant hashtags can help and irrelevant ones can hurt.

### Can I add a Book button to my Instagram profile?

Yes, if you use a supported booking partner. A profile can show only one action button at a time.

### Do I need to tell followers when a creator is paid?

Yes. The FTC says a material connection, including payment or a free service, should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously.

### How long should reels be?

Instagram recommends videos of 3 minutes or less to reach people who do not follow you. Short time-lapses fit well.
