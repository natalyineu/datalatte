A local business hero section should answer three things in the first screen: what you do, where you do it, and what the visitor should do next. Add one real photo, one proof point and one button. Nielsen Norman Group's 2018 eye-tracking study found people spent 57% of their viewing time above the fold, so the top of your home page is where visitors decide whether to stay.

## What must a hero section include?

NN/g's homepage principles are a good checklist: state who you are and what you do, add a tagline if your name does not make it obvious, show what makes you different in your customers' language, put the most important content above the fold, and use images that show your real offer rather than generic stock photos.

NN/g also says to avoid obstacles in the first view, such as autoplaying video, animated taglines and popups. In practice the hero needs a headline, one supporting line, one main button and one proof point such as your Google rating. If you quote it, make sure the number is current and links to the source.

## What do good hero patterns look like?

Each pattern below follows the same formula: service + place + proof + action. The bracketed text is a placeholder.

| Business | Sample headline pattern | Button |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coffee shop | "[Specialty] coffee and pastries on [street], open from [time]" | Order ahead |
| Hair salon | "[Service] for [hair type] in [neighbourhood]" | Book online |
| Pet groomer | "Gentle grooming for dogs of every size in [town]" | Book a groom |
| Fitness studio | "Small-group [class type] classes in [town]" | Try a class |
| Trades | "[Trade] in [area]. Call for same-day help" | Call now |

Pair each with a photo of your real space or team and a line of proof, for example "[X] Google reviews, [Y] average rating".

## How long should the headline be?

Short enough to read on a phone in one glance, which usually means under about eight to ten words. NN/g notes people scan rather than read, so lead with the service and the place, not a slogan. Our guide to [call-to-action examples](/blog/website-conversion-rate-optimization) on the same topic covers button wording.

## Should the hero use a big image or video?

A large image is fine if it is fast. The hero image is usually the Largest Contentful Paint element, which Google rates as good at 2.5 seconds or less. web.dev says never to lazy-load the LCP image, and to put it in the page HTML with high fetch priority. Skip autoplaying video, in line with NN/g. See our [speed guide](/blog/website-speed-optimization-small-business).

## How do you keep text readable on a photo?

WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.3 requires a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (18 point or 14 point bold and up). Put text on a solid or darkened overlay and test with a contrast checker. ## Worked example: testing two headlines

All inputs are **assumptions**.

| Input (assumption) | Headline A (generic) | Headline B (service + place) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitors in the test | 500 | 500 |
| Clicks on the main button | 20 | 30 |
| Click rate | 4% | 6% |

B has a 2-point higher click rate in this made-up test. With only 500 visitors per version, a gap this size can be chance. Run it longer, or until each version has several hundred button clicks, before deciding. For small sites a simpler approach is to change the headline and compare the 4 weeks before with the 4 weeks after.

## FAQ

### What is a hero section?

It is the first block visitors see on a page, usually a headline, supporting text, an image and a button.

### How many buttons should a hero have?

One main action. Add a secondary link only if you really have two goals, such as call and book.

### Should I use a slider?

No source reviewed here supports sliders. NN/g's advice to avoid animated taglines in the first view points the other way.

### Should the hero show prices?

A "from" price can help; NN/g reports prospects want price early. See our [pricing page guide](/blog/website-pricing-page-local-business).

### What photo should I use?

A real photo of your space, product or team. NN/g advises images that reflect your actual offerings over generic stock.
