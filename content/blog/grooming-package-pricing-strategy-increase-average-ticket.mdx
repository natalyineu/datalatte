To raise your average ticket, offer three named tiers, price each one by dog size and coat, and bundle the small add-ons customers already request into the middle and top tiers. No published study gives a set percentage lift from packages, so measure any gain in your own bookings. Local grooming price lists vary widely, so check competitor menus in your area before you set numbers. The worked example below uses labelled assumptions, not benchmarks.

Agency pricing is a useful structure reference, even though it is not a grooming price list. Marketing agencies for groomers tier their own plans, and the way they name and gate those tiers can guide how you name yours.

## Where Do Published Package Prices Come From?

Grooming businesses can look at how marketing agencies structure their tiers. [TailWerks](https://tailwerks.com/industries/pet-grooming-marketing-agency/) lists its Foundation package at $997 per month per location and its Growth package at $1,497 per month per location. Its Scale package is quoted privately, and it requires six months on Growth first, followed by a six-month rolling contract with pricing confirmed in writing.

TailWerks also says its Essentials option needs no advertising package or extra appointment capacity. [Perform Pets](https://performpets.com/how-much-does-grooming-salon-marketing-cost/) publishes an ongoing multi-channel package from $2,000 a month for one location. These are marketing fees, not grooming service prices. Use them to see how tiers are named and gated, not as a price to charge clients.

## How Should You Structure Three Tiers?

Name tiers by what the client receives, not by a list of tasks, and keep the difference easy to explain at the front desk. Essential, Full groom and Premium work as a simple pattern. Essential covers the wash, dry and basic tidy. Full groom adds the cut to the owner's style. Premium adds the extras customers ask for most often.

Price each tier by dog size and coat type. Matting work should sit on its own line rather than inside a tier, so a heavy coat does not quietly erode the margin on the price you quoted.

Front-desk flow matters as well. [Esperaly](https://www.esperaly.com/blog/pet-grooming-queue-management/) describes a Ticket as a pet parent joining by phone QR code at arrival or by tablet at the desk. It defines a Counter as a front desk or groomer station that can take the next pet for the services it covers. If each tier maps to specific stations, staff can see at a glance which pets can be taken at which counter.

## Which Add-Ons Belong in Which Tier?

Bundle small, frequent add-ons into Premium, and keep time-consuming work as separate paid lines. A customer who would have bought two extras separately now pays one bundle price, which is easy to explain. A long de-matting job should be quoted on its own, so the bundle does not absorb the extra time.

Decide with data. Pull the add-ons you sold most often over the past two months and sort them by how long each takes. As an assumption, treat the top five by count as your bundle candidates. Anything that adds significant time stays outside the bundle, even if it sells often.

## How Many Dogs Can One Groomer Handle?

Capacity limits how many packages you can sell. [Vanspeed PRO](https://www.vanspeedpro.com/blogs/news/what-is-the-cost-to-start-a-mobile-dog-grooming-business-vs-a-franchise) states the industry standard is 5 to 8 dogs per day for one groomer. It also says a typical mobile groomer works 20 to 22 days per month on a Monday to Friday schedule.

Those two figures give a planning range. At the low end, one groomer handles about 100 appointments a month (5 × 20). At the high end, the calculated figure is about 176 (8 × 22). If a higher tier takes longer, it also takes a slot. Price Premium so it does not crowd out the base bookings you need to fill the week.

## Worked Example: Does a Tier Mix Lift the Ticket?

All inputs below are **assumptions**. They illustrate the arithmetic and do not measure any real business. The 100 appointments come from the low end of the capacity range above.

| | Before (one price per size) | After (three tiers) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appointments per month (assumption) | 100 | 100 |
| Price (assumption) | $70 base | $65 Essential, $85 Full, $105 Premium |
| Mix (assumption) | 100% at $70 | 40% Essential, 40% Full, 20% Premium |
| Average ticket | $70 | $81 |
| Monthly revenue | $7,000 | $8,100 |

The after average is 0.40 × $65 + 0.40 × $85 + 0.20 × $105 = $26 + $34 + $21 = $81. Revenue is 100 × $81 = $8,100, which is $1,100 above the before scenario under these assumptions.

If most customers pick Essential, the average falls below the old $70, so the mix matters more than the price list itself. Track the real mix for two months before you treat the result as a gain.

## How Should Packages Appear Online and in Rebooking?

Show tier names, what each one includes and a starting price for each dog size on your website and Business Profile. Keep prices current, since outdated figures create awkward conversations at the desk.

## FAQ

### How many package tiers should a groomer offer?

Three is a practical start because it is easy to explain at the front desk. Add a fourth tier only after the current mix has settled.

### Should a mobile groomer price each van separately?

TailWerks states that each extra van is not assumed to be a separate package location. That is a useful question to ask any agency quoting a mobile business. For your own rates, price by service and by how many dogs each van can realistically handle.

### Should matting be included in a package?

Keep it as a separate line. Matting adds time that a fixed tier price may not cover, and a separate line makes that cost visible to the client.
