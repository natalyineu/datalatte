To raise your average ticket, offer three clearly named packages by dog size and coat, and move the add-ons people usually request into the middle and top tiers. No published study proves a percentage lift from packages, so test it on your own bookings. Published price lists give you the range to price against: basic grooming runs about $30 to $200 and comprehensive grooming $50 to $250, depending on size.

This guide shows the published ranges, a three-tier structure, and a worked example with assumptions marked. The price data comes from content sites and software vendors, so it is secondary and varies by region.

## What Do Dog Groomers Charge in the US?

Whole Dog Journal lists basic grooming (bath, brush-out, nails, ears and a simple trim) at $30 to $50 for small dogs, $50 to $80 medium, $80 to $120 large and $120 to $200 giant. Its comprehensive level, with a deep-cleaning bath, detailed cut and extras, is $50 to $80 small, $80 to $110 medium, $110 to $150 large and $150 to $250 giant. The article is dated November 2024.

MoeGo, a grooming software vendor, published higher ranges in February 2026, for example $75 to $125 for a small dog full groom. It cites no data source. ThePricer, citing a June 2026 Find My Kennel dataset of 1,354 shops, reports a $65 median for each shop's lowest advertised groom, with state medians from $35 in Arkansas to $80 in Massachusetts. Your local market matters more than any national figure.

## How Should You Structure Three Package Tiers?

Name tiers by outcome, not by list of tasks, and keep the difference between them easy to explain at the front desk. A simple pattern:

- **Essential:** bath, dry, brush-out, nails, ears.
- **Full groom:** Essential plus the cut or trim to the owner's style.
- **Premium:** Full groom plus the add-ons customers most often request, such as teeth brushing or a conditioning treatment.

Price each tier by size and by coat type, since Whole Dog Journal notes that breed, coat and condition move the price, and double-coated or high-maintenance coats cost more to groom. Keep matting charges outside the tiers; list them as a separate line so you are not discounting hard work.

## Which Add-Ons Should You Bundle?

Published add-on prices are scattered. Whole Dog Journal lists de-matting at $10 to $100, flea baths $10 to $45, medicated baths $15 to $45 and stand-alone nail trims at about $15. ThePricer, citing retailer menus, reports nail grinding at $8, extra brushing at $10 and deshedding at $18 to $27, and a $100 severe-matting surcharge from one mobile service.

Bundle the small, high-uptake extras into Premium and keep time-consuming ones, such as de-matting, as separate paid lines. Review your own booking data for the five add-ons you add most often before you decide.

## Do Packages Actually Raise the Average Ticket?

There is no independent source to cite for a percentage. MoeGo says only that bundled packages often offer better value than separate add-ons. The mechanism is plain: a customer who picks the middle or top tier pays more than the base price, and a customer who would have bought two extras separately pays a bundle price.

The risk is the opposite: if the middle tier is priced too close to the base, nobody upgrades. Change one thing at a time and compare average ticket over 4 to 8 weeks.

## FAQ

### How much should I charge for dog grooming?

Start from your local competitors and your time per dog. National published ranges are wide: $30 to $200 for basic grooming by size, per Whole Dog Journal. A $65 median lowest advertised groom comes from a 2026 dataset reported by ThePricer.

### How many package tiers should a groomer have?

Three is a common starting point because it is easy to explain. Test before adding more.

### Should I charge extra for matting?

Many groomers list it separately because it adds time. Published add-on prices run from $10 to $100 for de-matting.

### Can packages work for mobile groomers?

Yes. Mobile prices are higher; Whole Dog Journal notes mobile groomers cost more than salon groomers. Apply the same tier logic to your own rates.

### How do I get new customers to book a package?

Show tiers on your profile and website, then use [Google Ads for local pet groomers](/blog/how-much-should-a-pet-groomer-spend-on-google-ads) if you need more demand.
