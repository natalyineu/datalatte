A coffee shop should run Instagram ads as a small traffic or reach campaign aimed at a few miles around the shop, with one offer, one vertical video or image, and a way to count redemptions at the till. LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook and Instagram benchmarks give Restaurants and Food traffic campaigns an average click cost of $0.45 and a 2.68% click-through rate. A first-visit offer rarely pays back alone, so the test below is built around repeat visits.

This playbook is for independent coffee shops in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Meta does not publish a coffee-shop benchmark, so Restaurants and Food is the nearest category. Examples are marked as assumptions.

## Step 1: What Should the Ad Achieve?

Pick one goal: new customers through the door, preorders, or loyalty sign-ups. Instagram ads are managed in Meta Ads Manager, so the objective list there decides what you can optimize for. For a first test, choose traffic to a page with your offer, hours and map, or an objective that fits a local reach goal if your account offers one.

Do not choose several goals at once. A coffee shop cannot tell whether a campaign worked unless one number, such as redeemed offers, is agreed in advance.

## Step 2: Who Is the Customer?

Describe the customer in terms Meta can target: where they live or work, their age range, and interests such as coffee or local events. Meta lets you choose location options that include people living in an area or recently in it. The allowed radius depends on the location type, so check what Ads Manager offers.

Keep the audience local. A shop with 15 minutes of walking traffic gains little from clicks 30 miles away. Our [coffee shop Meta ads targeting guide](/blog/coffee-shop-meta-ads-targeting) goes deeper on audiences.

## Step 3: Which Ad Format Fits a Coffee Shop?

Short vertical video and a single bright image are enough. Meta's ads guide lists 9:16 as the aspect ratio for Reels image ads at 1440 x 2560 pixels, and for Instagram Feed video at 1080 x 1920. It tells advertisers to keep about 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% of each side free of text and logos on Reels, so the interface does not cover them.

Instagram Feed video primary text can run to 125 characters, while the Reels image ad primary text limit is 44. Specs change, so check the Ads Manager preview before publishing. For content ideas, see our [Reels ideas for coffee shops](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-reels-ideas-that-get-views).

## Step 4: What Should the Ad Say?

Name the place and the offer in the first line: a neighborhood, a drink and a reason to come now. Show the product, a person making it, or the room. Avoid stock photos.

Write for one action, such as "show this ad for a free upgrade". Avoid claims you cannot support, such as a guaranteed number of new customers. A short offer with a real end date works better than a vague promise.

## Step 5: What Budget and Schedule Should You Use?

Start small and cap it. LocaliQ's all-industry Facebook traffic average of $0.60 per click and the Restaurants and Food average of $0.45 mean a $10 daily budget buys roughly 17 to 22 clicks a day at those averages. Your own price will differ by city and creative.

Run ads on the days you want more footfall, not all week. If mornings are your peak and afternoons are slow, aim the offer at afternoon traffic. Give the test at least two weeks before you judge it.

## Step 6: How Do You Measure Results?

Clicks and views are not sales. Use a code, a card or a phrase that staff can ring up at the till. Track redemptions, new versus returning guests, and the weekday split. Add the Meta Pixel to your landing page if you take orders online.

Click-through rate is a diagnostic, not a goal. LocaliQ's 2.68% is an average across Restaurants and Food traffic campaigns, not a target you must hit. Compare your ads with each other first.

## Step 7: How Do You Test and Scale?

Change one thing at a time: the first line, the picture, or the offer. Keep the winner and test a new challenger. Raise the budget in small steps only when cost per redeemed offer stays below your break-even number. ## Does a Two-Week Test Pay Back?

All inputs are **assumptions** except the LocaliQ click price.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Daily budget | $10 |
| Days | 14 |
| Total spend | $140 |
| Click price | $0.45 (LocaliQ Restaurants and Food traffic average) |
| Clicks | about 311 |
| Clicks that become a redeemed offer | 2% (assumption) |
| New customers | about 6 |
| Cost per new customer | about $23 |
| Gross profit per visit | $4.55 (assumption: $6.50 ticket at 70%) |
| Visits needed to pay back one new customer | about 5 |

On these assumptions a new customer must visit about five times for the ad to pay back, which is plausible for a regular but not for a tourist. If your redemption rate is higher or your ticket is larger, the picture improves. If fewer than 6 customers come, fix the offer or landing page before raising the budget.

Work out your gross profit per visit, then divide your planned test spend by it. That is the number of visits the test must create.

## FAQ

### How much should a coffee shop spend on Instagram ads?

There is no standard figure. At LocaliQ's $0.45 average click price for Restaurants and Food traffic, $10 a day buys roughly 22 clicks. Test for two weeks and count redemptions before scaling.

### Are Instagram ads better than organic posts?

They serve different jobs. Organic posts build habit with existing followers, and ads reach nearby people who do not follow you. Our [Instagram marketing guide](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-marketing-strategy) covers the organic side.

### What size should the image or video be?

Meta lists 9:16 for Reels and Instagram Feed video. Reels image ads are 1440 x 2560 pixels, and Feed video is 1080 x 1920. Keep text out of the top and bottom zones.

### Can I target only people near the shop?

Yes. Ads Manager offers location targeting, and the allowed radius depends on the location type.

### How do I know if the ads worked?

Count redeemed codes or offers at the till and compare them with ad spend. Do not judge by likes.
