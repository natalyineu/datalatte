To get more bookings from Google Business Profile (GBP) as a pet groomer, verify your profile, fill in every section, choose "Pet groomer" as your primary category, add real photos, collect genuine reviews and reply to them, and link to online booking. Google says local results are ranked on relevance, distance and prominence, and that businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), checked Oct 2026). Results build over weeks and months, not overnight.

Picture a pet owner in the driveway with a muddy dog and a 9 a.m. appointment somewhere. She types "dog groomer near me" and picks from the map listings at the top. She does not scroll. She compares photos, stars and review counts in about ten seconds.

Here is what she is comparing. In the 50 Seattle pet groomers we pulled from Google Maps in October 2026, the median groomer had 133 reviews, 17 had more than 200, and 8 had fewer than 50. In the 50 we pulled in Chicago, 13 had fewer than 50 and the median was 114. If you are under 50, you are not invisible, but you are behind. The good news is that the gap closes with a system, and the math is below.

## Why GBP matters for local search

Many groomers have a half-finished GBP. That is an opening: a well-maintained profile can compete with bigger websites. Google describes its three local ranking factors this way:

1. **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what someone is searching for. Detailed, accurate information helps.
2. **Distance:** how far you are from the searcher.
3. **Prominence:** how well known your business is, based on things like links from the web and review volume. Google notes that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

You cannot change distance, but you can improve the other two.

## Step 1: Verify and complete your profile

Verification gives you ownership of the profile, and the methods offered depend on your business type, region and hours. Google's review of verification details can take up to 5 business days ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242), checked Oct 2026). Then check your profile against this list:

- **Business name** exactly as on your signage. Google's guidelines do not allow marketing taglines, service information or location details in the name ([Guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), checked Oct 2026).
- **Address, or a service area** if you are a mobile groomer
- **A phone number** consistent with your other listings
- **Website URL** and **business hours**, including holiday hours
- **A description** of up to 750 characters. Focus on what you offer and what sets you apart; Google says to leave out URLs and not to focus on promotions or prices ([Edit your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617), checked Oct 2026).
- **Primary and secondary categories**
- **Services** such as full groom, bath and brush, nail trim, de-shedding and breed-specific cuts
- **Attributes** that apply, photos and a booking link

## Step 2: Choose the right categories

Pick the most specific primary category Google offers; for a grooming business, look for "Pet groomer". Google advises using the fewest categories needed to describe your core business, and adding extra categories only for special departments or services ([Manage your business category](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669), checked Oct 2026). If you also offer boarding, daycare or sell pet supplies, add a secondary category only if it truly applies.

Check the categories top-ranking competitors use, but never copy one that does not describe you.

## Step 3: Add photos regularly

Photos make your business look active and help pet owners picture the experience. Post:

- Before and after photos (with the owner's permission)
- Your grooming space, clean and well lit
- Your exterior and entrance, so first-time visitors can find you

Add new photos on a schedule, not in one burst.

## Step 4: Build a steady flow of reviews

Google lists review volume and ratings among the signals that support local ranking, and reviews influence customer trust too. Build a simple system:

- Send a text or email with your direct Google review link after each appointment
- Ask when the owner sees their freshly groomed pet and is happiest
- Put a QR code to your review page on receipts and reminders
- Reply to reviews, positive and negative, ideally within a day or two

Follow Google's review policy. It prohibits offering payment, discounts or free goods or services in exchange for reviews, and it bars selectively asking only happy customers for reviews or discouraging negative ones ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2622994), checked Oct 2026). Breaking these rules can get reviews removed or your profile restricted.

For a negative review, keep the reply short and professional: "Hi [Name], we are sorry to hear this. Please contact us at [email/phone] so we can make it right."

## The Math: How Long to Close the Review Gap

This is a worked example. Every input is an assumption, not a result from a real groomer and not a market statistic. The only real number is the 133-review Seattle median above. Swap in your own figures.

Assumptions: you have 20 reviews today, you want to reach 133, you groom 40 dogs a week, and you ask every owner. The share who leave a review is the number that decides everything.

| Share of owners who review | Reviews per week (40 x share) | Weeks to add 113 (133 - 20) |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 4 | 28.3 |
| 15% | 6 | 18.8 |
| 25% | 10 | 11.3 |

At 10% you need roughly 28 weeks. At 25% it is about 11. A text with a direct link, sent while the dog is still fluffy, is the difference between those rows. 

Ratings move too. If 20 reviews average 4.5 (90 stars), adding 10 five-star reviews gives 140 stars over 30 reviews: 4.67.

What a better profile is worth: if a regular client grooms every 6 weeks at $75 (an example), that is about 8.7 visits a year (52 / 6), or roughly $650 a year. Five extra regular clients from better visibility would be about $3,250 a year. These are illustrative numbers. Use your own prices and rebooking cycle.

## Step 5: Post updates

Posts let you share an update, offer or event directly on your profile, and you can schedule them, including on a recurring basis ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169), checked Oct 2026). Ideas:

- **Seasonal offers:** "Summer de-shedding special this month"
- **New services:** "Now offering mobile grooming in [neighborhood] on Thursdays"
- **Tips:** "Signs your dog is overdue for a groom"

Posts take a few minutes. Treat any ranking benefit as a bonus.

## Step 6: Keep your name, address and phone consistent

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address and phone (NAP). Check yours on Yelp, Facebook, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Nextdoor and local pet directories. Small differences such as "Suite 4" versus "Ste. 4" can cause confusion.

## Step 7: Add booking links

If you use an online booking tool, add the booking link to your GBP so people can book from the listing. Check Google's current options, as available buttons and integrations change.

Do not plan around profile chat: Google shut it down on 31 July 2024 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-business-profile-chat-feature-to-sunset-on-july-31-2024)). Show your phone number and booking link instead.

## Step 8: Use the services section

Add each service with a short description and, where you are comfortable, a starting price. Google lets you manage services directly on your profile ([Manage your services](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9455399), checked Oct 2026). List "full groom" and "nail trim" separately so customers see what you offer. Keep descriptions honest and consistent with your website.

## Step 9: Check your performance

Review your profile performance regularly to see calls, direction requests and website visits, and note which search terms bring people to you. Google has replaced the Questions and Answers feature in Maps with an AI-powered Ask button ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-quietly-kills-q-a-for-an-ai-button-most-wont-use/)), so put key facts such as breeds you groom, prices and policies in your description, services and website instead.

## Mobile groomers

Mobile groomers can use a service area instead of showing a street address. Google tells service-area businesses to hide their address, and expects a service area of no more than about two hours' drive from your base ([Guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), checked Oct 2026).

## What to expect and when

Expect more impressions within a few weeks of completing your profile. Rankings move more slowly, over a few months of steady reviews, photos and posts. Nobody can guarantee a position.

## Do This Today

1. Count your reviews and write down the number. Compare it with the three groomers ranking above you.
2. Check your description: is it under 750 characters, with no URL and no prices? Rewrite it if not.
3. Upload 10 real photos: 3 before-and-after pairs, 2 of the grooming space, 2 of the exterior.
4. Save your direct Google review link as a text shortcut. Send it to your next 10 owners, the same day as the appointment.
5. Confirm your name, address and phone match on Yelp, Facebook and Apple Maps.
6. Add your booking link and check it works from a phone.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I get my pet grooming business on Google Maps?**
A: Create or claim your Google Business Profile, verify it, then complete every section: categories, hours, services, photos and contact details. Verification gives you ownership of the profile.

**Q: What is the best primary category for a pet groomer?**
A: The most specific one Google offers, usually "Pet groomer". Add secondary categories only if they describe what you really do.

**Q: How many reviews do I need to rank in the map pack?**
A: There is no fixed number. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your ranking, so compare yourself with the groomers currently ranking near you and aim for a steady flow of genuine new reviews.

**Q: Can I list my business if I am a mobile groomer with no shop?**
A: Yes, if you follow Google's service-area business guidelines: you can hide your address and list a service area.
