Google says local results are ranked mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), read 3 Nov 2026). ## How do groomers compare on reviews?

A pet owner searching "dog groomer near me" compares photos, stars and review counts on the map listings in seconds. DataLatte's October 2026 Google Maps pulls give a benchmark for two cities, 50 pet groomers each: Seattle has a median of 133 reviews, 17 groomers above 200 and 8 below 50. Chicago has a median of 114, 12 above 200 and 13 below 50.

## Why does Google Business Profile matter for local search?

Many groomers have a half-finished profile. That is an opening: a well-maintained profile can compete with bigger websites. Google describes its three local ranking factors this way:

1. **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what someone is searching for. Detailed, accurate information helps.
2. **Distance:** how far you are from the searcher.
3. **Prominence:** how well known your business is, based on things like links from the web and review volume. Google notes that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

You cannot change distance, but you can improve the other two. Google also states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

## Step 1: How do you verify and complete your profile?

Verification gives you ownership of the profile. Google says verification reviews take up to 5 business days, and the methods offered depend on your business type, region and hours, for example phone or text, email, video call or postcard ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242), read 3 Nov 2026). Then check your profile against this list:

- **Business name** exactly as on your signage. Google does not permit marketing taglines or service descriptions in the name ([Guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), read 3 Nov 2026).
- **Address, or a service area** if you are a mobile groomer
- **A phone number** consistent with your other listings
- **Website URL** and **business hours**, including holiday hours
- **A description** of up to 750 characters. Focus on details about your business rather than promotions, prices or sales, and leave out URLs ([Edit your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617), read 3 Nov 2026).
- **Primary and secondary categories**
- **Services** such as full groom, bath and brush, nail trim, de-shedding and breed-specific cuts
- **Attributes** that apply, photos and a booking link

## Step 2: Which categories should a pet groomer pick?

Pick the most specific primary category Google offers; for a grooming business, look for "Pet groomer". Google advises using a few categories from its list and adding extras only for special departments or services customers would look for ([Manage your business category](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669), read 3 Nov 2026). It also says not to use categories as keywords. If you also offer boarding or daycare, add a secondary category only if it truly applies.

Check the categories top-ranking competitors use, but never copy one that does not describe you.

## Step 3: What photos should you add?

Photos make your business look active and help pet owners picture the experience. Post:

- Before and after photos (with the owner's permission)
- Your grooming space, clean and well lit
- Your exterior and entrance, so first-time visitors can find you

Add new photos on a schedule, not in one burst.

## Step 4: How do you build a steady flow of reviews?

Google lists review volume and ratings among the signals behind prominence, and reviews influence customer trust too. Build a simple system:

- Send a text or email with your direct Google review link after each appointment
- Ask when the owner sees their freshly groomed pet and is happiest
- Put a QR code to your review page on receipts and reminders
- Reply to reviews, positive and negative, ideally within a day or two

Follow Google's review policy. It removes content posted because of an incentive offered by a business, and bars businesses from discouraging negative reviews or selectively soliciting positive ones ([Review policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2622994), read 3 Nov 2026). Breaking these rules can get reviews removed or your profile restricted.

For a negative review, keep the reply short and professional: "Hi [Name], we are sorry to hear this. Please contact us at [email/phone] so we can make it right."

## How long does it take to close the review gap?

**Assumption, not data:** you have 20 reviews today, you want to reach 133 (the Seattle median above), you groom 40 dogs a week, and you ask every owner. The share who leave a review is invented here and decides everything. Swap in your own figures.

| Share of owners who review (assumption) | Reviews per week (40 x share) | Weeks to add 113 (133 - 20) |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 4 | 28.3 |
| 15% | 6 | 18.8 |
| 25% | 10 | 11.3 |

At 10% you need roughly 28 weeks. At 25% it is about 11. A text with a direct link, sent while the dog is still fluffy, is the difference between those rows.

Ratings move too. If 20 reviews average 4.5 (90 stars), adding 10 five-star reviews gives 140 stars over 30 reviews: 4.67.

What a better profile is worth, as an assumption: a regular client grooming every 6 weeks at $75 is about 8.7 visits a year (52 / 6), or roughly $650 a year. Five extra regular clients would be about $3,250 a year. Use your own prices and rebooking cycle.

## Step 5: Should you post updates?

Posts let you share announcements, offers, updates and event details directly on your profile. You can schedule them, including on a repeating basis, and Google archives posts older than 6 months unless a date range is set ([Create and manage posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169), read 3 Nov 2026). Ideas:

- **Seasonal offers:** "Summer de-shedding special this month"
- **New services:** "Now offering mobile grooming in [neighborhood] on Thursdays"
- **Tips:** "Signs your dog is overdue for a groom"

Posts take a few minutes. Google does not list posts as a ranking factor on its ranking page, so treat any benefit as a bonus.

## Step 6: How do you keep name, address and phone consistent?

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address and phone (NAP). Check yours on Yelp, Facebook, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Nextdoor and local pet directories. Small differences such as "Suite 4" versus "Ste. 4" can cause confusion.

## Step 7: How do you add booking links?

If you use an online booking tool, add the booking link to your profile so people can book from the listing. Available buttons and integrations change, so check the options in your own profile. Show your phone number and booking link prominently instead of relying on messaging features.

## Step 8: How do you use the services section?

Add each service with a short description and, where you are comfortable, a starting price. Google's editor lets you keep services and prices accurate, though availability of the feature is limited ([Edit your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617)). List "full groom" and "nail trim" separately so customers see what you offer. Keep descriptions honest and consistent with your website.

## Step 9: How do you check performance?

Review your profile performance regularly to see calls, direction requests and website visits, and note which search terms bring people to you. Put key facts such as breeds you groom, prices and policies in your description, services and website, so customers find them without asking.

## What about mobile groomers?

Google says service-area businesses should hide their business address from customers, and that the overall service area should not extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time from the base, though larger areas may suit some businesses ([Guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), read 3 Nov 2026).

## What results can you expect, and when?

Expect more impressions within a few weeks of completing your profile. Rankings move more slowly, over a few months of steady reviews, photos and posts. Nobody can guarantee a position.

## What should you do today?

1. Count your reviews and write down the number. Compare it with the three groomers ranking above you.
2. Check your description: is it under 750 characters, with no URL? Rewrite it if not.
3. Upload 10 real photos: 3 before-and-after pairs, 2 of the grooming space, 2 of the exterior.
4. Save your direct Google review link as a text shortcut. Send it to your next 10 owners, the same day as the appointment.
5. Confirm your name, address and phone match on Yelp, Facebook and Apple Maps.
6. Add your booking link and check it works from a phone.

## FAQ

### How do I get my pet grooming business on Google Maps?

Create or claim your Google Business Profile, verify it, then complete every section: categories, hours, services, photos and contact details. Verification gives you ownership of the profile.

### What is the best primary category for a pet groomer?

The most specific one Google offers, usually "Pet groomer". Add secondary categories only if they describe what you really do.

### How many reviews do I need to rank in the map pack?

There is no fixed number. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your ranking. In DataLatte's October 2026 data the median was 133 reviews in Seattle and 114 in Chicago, so compare yourself with the groomers ranking near you.

### Can I list my business if I am a mobile groomer with no shop?

Yes, if you follow Google's service-area business guidelines: you hide your address and list a service area.
