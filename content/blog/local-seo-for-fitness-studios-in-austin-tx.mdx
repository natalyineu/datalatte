## How does the Google Map Pack choose which Austin studios appear?

The Map Pack shows relevant local businesses for queries like "personal training studios near me," according to [How to Make Your Fitness Business Stand Out on Google](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google), a secondary article from the Association of Fitness Studios. The same article describes Google Business Profile as a free tool that displays hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews, and photos directly in search results.

Your profile is therefore the asset that search results draw from. A survey cited by [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo) found that 36% of SEOs in 2021 considered the Google Business Profile the most important ranking factor for the local pack, up from 23% in 2013. That is a survey of practitioners, not a formal Google rule, so use it as direction rather than certainty.

For an Austin studio, the practical takeaway is to treat the profile as the front door. Every field should match what your website and citations say.

## What should an Austin studio fix first on its Google Business Profile?

Start with the fields you control directly. Choose your primary category carefully, then add modality categories that describe what you actually teach, such as yoga, Pilates, or HIIT. Set a service area only if you serve clients beyond your street address, and list each class type as a service.

Photos come next. [Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), a secondary guide, recommends uploading at least 15 authentic photos of your facility, classes, and staff. Use real images from your own studio rather than stock.

Posts are the third lever. Google states that a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, and Pending or Not approved posts stay hidden, so check the status after publishing. For deeper profile work, see the [fitness studio Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization).

## Which searches should an Austin studio target?

Brand and near-me searches are often the most valuable, but you need real volume data before prioritising. A [Brandrocket](https://www.brandrocket.net/resources/gym-google-ads-settings-location) page, a secondary source, reports that "planet fitness austin" gets about 1,900 searches a month nationally and about 1,000 locally, per Keyword Planner. That is a brand search for a competitor, so it shows demand for gyms in Austin rather than a keyword you should bid on.

Build your list from themes you can verify in Keyword Planner or Search Console:

1. Class-type plus city, such as "yoga studio Austin" or "Pilates Austin."
2. Service-plus-city, such as "personal trainer Austin."
3. Neighbourhood names where your members live.
4. Near-me variants that match the modality you teach.

## How do reviews and citations support map pack visibility?

Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search, which makes reviews a visible trust signal for every searcher. Ask members to describe specific classes or coaches in their feedback, since real descriptions are more informative than generic praise.

Some industry pages claim that businesses with 50+ reviews and a 4.7+ average dominate the top spots. That claim is unsourced, so test it against your own competitors rather than treating it as a target.

Citations are listings of your name, address, and phone on other sites. Keep the format identical everywhere. "St." and "Street" are different strings to a directory, so pick one and apply it across all listings, including booking platforms your members already use.

## What does a worked ROI example look like for a studio?

The example below uses assumption-labelled inputs. Replace them with your own GBP Insights figures before making decisions.

Assumption: 120 profile actions per month (calls, direction requests, and website clicks).
Assumption: 15% of those actions become trial bookings, which gives 18 trials.
Assumption: 40% of trials join at $150 per month, which gives 7.2 new members.
Result: about $1,080 in new monthly recurring revenue.
Assumption: an average member stays 6 months, which gives roughly $6,480 in revenue per member cohort.

If your actions are lower, the model shows how sensitive the outcome is to each input. Improving the trial conversion rate often matters as much as raising profile actions.

## How does Google Ads connect to your profile data?

Google Ads uses location assets sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, so corrections you make to your profile can flow into linked ads. If your chain or profile options do not work, you can select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link, as long as those locations are yours.

This matters for studios running paid search alongside local SEO. Keep profile data consistent before you launch campaigns. Our [Google Ads for fitness studios complete guide](/blog/google-ads-for-fitness-studios-complete-2026-guide) explains the campaign side.

## How should an Austin studio track progress?

Set up measurement before changing anything. Google Search Console shows impressions and clicks by page, and GBP Insights shows searches, views, and actions such as calls and direction requests. Record these monthly so you can compare changes against a baseline.

For map pack position, check your top searches in a private browser window every week, and log the results in a simple spreadsheet. Note any new listings you gain or lose. Google does not publish a timeline for ranking changes, so judge progress over several months rather than days.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for an Austin studio?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as a free tool that lets businesses manage their presence across Google Search and Maps. Paid options, such as Google Ads, are separate.

### How many photos should I upload to my profile?

A secondary guide from Vertical Impression recommends at least 15 authentic photos of your facility, classes, and staff. Use real images and refresh them as your space changes.

### Do posts on my profile always appear to searchers?

No. Google shows a post publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not appear, so check the status after publishing.

### Should I link my Google Ads account to my profile locations?

If your profile and chain options work, Google Ads sources location data directly from your Business Profile. If they do not, you can select up to 10 locations from Google Maps, provided the locations belong to you.
