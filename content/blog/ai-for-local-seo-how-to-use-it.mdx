Ninety minutes a week is enough to run the routine parts of local SEO with an AI assistant, as long as the assistant drafts and you decide. That is the whole idea of this guide: AI is a fast first-draft machine for the chores (audits, review replies, page drafts, markup, reports), and a person stays responsible for every fact a customer will read.

This is the operations companion to our keyword piece. For finding what to target, read [AI local keyword research](/blog/ai-boost-local-seo). Here we cover what to do each week once you know your keywords. It is written for single-location businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: cafes, salons, groomers, studios, clinics.

## What Google Actually Says About AI and Your Site

Two Google statements shape everything below.

**On AI-written content.** Google's guidance on generative AI says it "can be particularly useful when researching a topic, and to add structure to original content". It also warns that models predict likely word sequences rather than retrieve facts, calls it "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content", and says that generating many pages without adding value for users may violate its spam policy on scaled content abuse. Its spam policies add that "multiple pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" and near-identical pages count as doorway abuse. For a local business that means: do not publish 30 suburb pages with the suburb name swapped.

**On AI search features.** Google's page on AI features in Search says to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. It adds there are "no additional requirements" and no "special optimizations necessary", and that no special schema.org structured data is needed. Traffic from these features is included in the Search Console Performance report under the Web search type. In other words, ignore anyone selling a secret AI-ranking trick. Good basics are the plan.

## The Weekly Routine (90 Minutes)

| Slot | Task | AI's job | Your job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 min | Consistency check | Spot mismatches in name, address, phone, hours | Fix them at the source |
| 20 min | Review replies | Draft replies | Edit, add detail, post |
| 25 min | One page or post | Outline and first draft | Add real facts, photos, prices |
| 15 min | Schema check | Draft markup | Validate against Google's docs |
| 15 min | Reporting | Summarise the numbers | Read the numbers yourself |

### 1. Consistency Check (15 minutes)

Paste your Business Profile details, your site's footer and contact page, and two or three directory listings into an assistant. Ask it to list every difference in business name, address, phone number, categories and opening hours, in a table. This is where AI shines: comparing messy text is dull for humans. Then fix the differences yourself in the actual listings. Our [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) lists the fields to check.

Why bother? Google says local results rely on relevance, distance and prominence, and that complete, detailed information helps Google match a profile to a search. Mismatched data undermines the first one.

### 2. Review Replies (20 minutes)

Google's help page says only verified businesses can reply, replies are public under the customer's review, and the customer is notified. AI can produce a decent first draft in seconds. The edit is the work:

- Mention one concrete thing the customer said ("glad the cold brew hit the spot").
- Thank by first name only if the reviewer shows one.
- Never reuse the same sentence across replies. Customers and Google can both spot repeats.
- For negative reviews, a person writes the reply. Our scripts are in [how to respond to negative Google reviews](/blog/how-to-respond-negative-google-reviews-scripts).

Two hard lines. Google's contribution policy says fake engagement is not allowed and will be removed, and that merchants may not offer incentives in exchange for reviews, which includes discounts or free goods. So do not have AI write reviews, and do not trade freebies for them. Google's help page on asking customers for reviews says to ask people to visit a link or scan a QR code, and to remind customers to leave reviews rather than selecting who gets asked. For automation options see [AI agents for Google review management](/blog/ai-agent-for-google-reviews-management).

### 3. One Page or Post a Week (25 minutes)

Pick one real question customers ask (from your inbox, phone calls or Search Console queries) and write one useful page or post. Use AI for:

- an outline built from your answers;
- a first draft of a service page or FAQ;
- tightening a long paragraph.

Then add what only you can: your prices, your process, your team, your photos, your neighbourhood. Delete any claim you cannot prove (awards, years in business, certifications). One page that answers a real question beats ten generic ones. For profile posts and Q&A drafts, see [AI for Google Business Profile management](/blog/ai-for-local-google-business-profile-management).

### 4. Schema Check (15 minutes, monthly is fine)

AI can draft LocalBusiness structured data in a minute. Check it against Google's own documentation, because assistants often use properties from memory. Google's LocalBusiness page lists `address` and `name` as required, `openingHoursSpecification` as the recommended way to give opening hours, and other recommended properties such as `telephone`, `url`, `geo` and `priceRange`. It also says Google does not guarantee that features using structured data will appear. The markup must match what is visible on the page. If your AI draft says you open at 9 and your page says 10, the markup is wrong. Our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-for-local-business) goes through it.

### 5. Reporting (15 minutes)

Export last month's Search Console queries and Business Profile figures (calls, direction requests, website clicks). Ask the assistant to summarise changes and flag the three biggest movers, then open the numbers and check. AI summaries of spreadsheets occasionally invent a trend, so never act on one you have not seen in the data.

<Callout type="coffee" title="The One Rule">
If a customer could act on a claim (a price, an opening time, a guarantee, a medical or legal statement), a person checks it before it goes live. Everything else can be fast.
</Callout>

## Why Your Website Still Matters

BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (1,002 US adults) found 54% of consumers visit a business's website after reading positive reviews, and 24% visit its social channels. The same report says 45% now use ChatGPT or similar tools for local recommendations, and that Google's share of review reading fell from 83% to 71%. BrightLocal sells review software and this is a US panel, so it is a signal, not a forecast. The lesson: wherever people first meet you, many of them end up on your site, so keep it accurate and fast to scan.

Break-even is $20 divided by $30, which is 40 minutes saved a month. That is easy to hit. The risk runs the other way. Suppose one unchecked page lists the wrong opening time and costs you 3 customers at $40 each (an assumption): that is a $120 loss, enough to wipe out the Base month. The saving is real, but only if the checking step stays in your routine.

AI also does not create rankings. The rankings come from relevance, distance and prominence, and AI only helps with the first and third. It cannot move your shop closer to the searcher.

## What to Avoid

- Publishing raw AI output across your site in one go.
- Near-identical suburb pages with only the place name changed.
- AI-written or incentivised reviews.
- Trusting AI-reported rankings or traffic numbers without checking Search Console and Business Profile insights.
- Stuffing keywords into your business name or description.

## Do This Today

1. Paste your Business Profile and site contact details into an AI assistant and ask for a table of every mismatch in name, address, phone and hours. Fix them at the source.
2. Draft replies to your last five unanswered reviews with AI, edit each with one concrete detail, and post them.
3. Write down five real questions customers ask. Pick one and draft a single page with real prices and photos.
4. Check your LocalBusiness markup against Google's documentation, and make sure hours in the markup match your page.
5. Put a recurring 90-minute weekly block in your calendar and add a rule on the first line: "A person checks every fact."

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need AI for local SEO?**
A: No. You can do all of it by hand. AI saves time on drafting, comparing and summarising, which matters when you run the business yourself.

**Q: Will Google penalise AI-written content?**
A: Google's guidance says the problem is generating many pages without adding value for users, which may violate its scaled content abuse policy. Original, accurate, human-checked content is fine. Fact-checking is the part Google calls critical.

**Q: Do I need special markup to appear in AI Overviews?**
A: Google's AI features page says no additional requirements or special schema are needed beyond being indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. Keep your structured data accurate and matching the page anyway.

**Q: Can AI reply to my Google reviews automatically?**
A: It can draft them, and some tools post them. A person should still check each reply, and negative reviews deserve a human-written one. Never trade discounts for reviews, which Google prohibits.

**Q: How long until I see results?**
A: There is no reliable number. Fixing inconsistent data can show up sooner than building reviews and content. Judge by trends in calls, direction requests and Search Console clicks over a few months.
