Here is the number that decides whether Local Services Ads (LSAs) work for you: $53. That is the home-services average cost per lead in one 2026 benchmark. If your average job earns you $160 in profit and you close 40% of leads, a lead is worth $64 to you, so $53 leaves you $11 of room. If you close 25%, a lead is worth $40 and you lose money on every one. LSAs are not "good" or "bad". They are a close-rate bet, and you can check that bet before you spend much.

This guide is for local service businesses in the US, UK, Canada and Australia. It covers who can use LSAs, how Google bills you, what leads tend to cost, and a worked break-even calculation. Dollar figures are US benchmarks; run the same maths with your own local prices.

## What LSAs Are

LSAs are Google's pay-per-lead ads for local businesses. Unlike Search ads, you do not pick keywords or write copy. You choose your job types and service area, set a budget, and Google shows your profile with rating, reviews and a call or message option. You pay when a valid lead contacts you, not when someone clicks.

## Are You Eligible? Check Before You Plan Anything

Eligibility is the first filter, and it differs by country. Google says on its help pages that LSAs are "available only for certain service categories", and that the category list is per country. Here is what Google's country pages listed when we checked in October 2026:

- **United States:** a long list. Beyond the trades (plumbers, electricians, HVAC, roofers, cleaning, pest control, landscaping, locksmiths and more), the page also lists professional services (lawyers, financial planning, tax), health care (dentists, chiropractors, physical therapists), and categories many owners assume are excluded: hair salon, barber shop, nail salon, massage therapist, personal trainer, yoga studio, pet grooming, pet training, veterinarian, and even coffee shop and bakery. Some categories (tattoo studio, insurance agency, interior designer and others) are limited to California and Florida. A category appearing on the list does not guarantee you can run ads in your city, so use Google's eligibility tool.
- **United Kingdom:** home and trade services across the UK (plumbers, electricians, cleaning, landscaping and lawn care, roofers, handyman and similar). Lawyers, personal injury and estate agents are Greater London only.
- **Canada:** a shorter list: appliance repair, carpet cleaning, cleaning, electricians, HVAC, junk removal, lawn care, locksmiths, movers, pest control, plumbers, roofers, tree services, water damage and window cleaning or repair.
- **Australia:** the pages we reviewed did not list Australia. Check the eligibility tool in your Google account before building a plan around LSAs.

The practical takeaway: if you are a salon, studio, groomer or café in the US, do not assume LSAs are closed to you. Check. If you are in the UK or Canada, the list is mostly trades.

## How Google Bills You

Read these rules from Google's [Local Services Ads billing page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435) before you set a budget:

- **What is chargeable:** a call you answer, or a call you return that reaches the customer; a missed call during business hours where the caller stays on for more than 20 seconds; and texts, emails, voicemails or booking requests. Booking requests are US and Canada only.
- **Repeat contact:** follow-up from the same customer within 15 days is not charged again.
- **Budget:** you set an average weekly budget. Some weeks can run over, but Google says you will never spend more than your monthly max, which is the weekly budget multiplied by the average number of weeks in a month.
- **Price:** varies by location, job type, lead type and bidding mode.
- **Credits:** Google reviews charged leads over a 30-day period and automatically credits poor-quality, duplicate or invalid ones. Credits typically reach your balance within 30 days, and the original charge still shows on your invoice. Set your job types and service area carefully, because leads outside them are the most common source of wasted spend.

Some third-party sites describe an older manual dispute button. Google's current pages describe automatic credits, so look at what your own account shows.

<Callout type="warning" title="Missed calls can cost you">
A missed call in business hours that lasts more than 20 seconds is chargeable. If nobody answers your LSA number, you can pay for a lead you never spoke to. Fix your phone coverage before you turn the ads on.
</Callout>

## The Badge Has Changed

Older guides talk about "Google Guaranteed" and "Google Screened". Google's [Verified badge page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/16498018) says it is moving to a single badge, Google Verified, and that it is discontinuing the Money Back Guarantee tied to the old badge. Advertisers who already completed verification get the badge automatically; new advertisers must complete screening. Google also states the badge does not affect ranking. Its [getting started page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841) says the Verified badge is unavailable for auto, beauty and dining categories.

If you have not finished screening, "pre-badge" ads may let you receive leads while you complete onboarding. Google says those listings appear below fully verified providers, and need a licence or business registration, reviews (depending on category), billing set-up and a budget. They are not available for garage door services, health care or locksmiths.

## The Math

Take $1,000 of monthly spend. Every input below is an **assumption** for illustration, except that the $53 middle case is the benchmark above. The $400 job value and 40% close rate echo the article's own worked example. Replace all of them with your data.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly spend (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Cost per lead (assumption; Base = $53 benchmark) | $90 | $53 | $35 |
| Leads | 11 | 19 | 29 |
| Lead to booked job rate (assumption) | 25% | 40% | 50% |
| Booked jobs | 2.8 | 7.5 | 14.3 |
| Ad cost per booked job | $360 | $133 | $70 |
| Average job value (assumption) | $400 | $400 | $400 |
| Profit margin on a job (assumption) | 40% | 40% | 40% |
| Profit from those jobs | $444 | $1,208 | $2,286 |
| Profit after ad spend | -$556 | +$208 | +$1,286 |

Two formulas to keep:

- **Break-even cost per lead** = close rate x profit per job. At 40% and $160 profit: 0.40 x $160 = **$64**. If Google quotes you more than that for your area, either raise your close rate or skip LSAs.
- **Break-even close rate** = cost per lead / profit per job. At $53 and $160 profit: 33%.

Notice the Tough column is not a bad market. It is a business that closes one in four leads at $90 a lead. Most of the swing comes from your phone handling and quoting, not from Google. Judge LSAs by cost per booked job, never by cost per lead. For a one-off job where customers return, count lifetime value; for a one-time repair, do not.

## LSAs vs Search Ads

| | Local Services Ads | Search ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pricing | Per lead | Per click |
| Control | Job types, area, hours, budget | Keywords, copy, bids, targeting |
| Setup | Screening first, then simple | More work, more options |
| Best for | Eligible, call-driven services | Any business, more complex funnels |

Many service businesses run both: LSAs for ready-to-hire callers, Search for specific services and keywords. ## Set-Up Checklist

1. **Check eligibility** for your category and location in Google's sign-up flow.
2. **Tidy your Google Business Profile** so name, address, phone, hours and categories are accurate. Use our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).
3. **Complete screening** (licence or business registration, and any category-specific checks).
4. **Select only the job types you want** and a service area you will actually travel to. Remember Google no longer credits job-type or area mismatches.
5. **Set a modest weekly budget** and, if your bidding mode allows it, a maximum price per lead below your break-even figure.
6. **Mark booked jobs and rate bad leads** in the lead inbox, so you have clean numbers after a month.

For a wider walkthrough, see our [complete guide to Local Services Ads](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide). To count which calls become jobs outside Google's own inbox, read [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).

## Do This Today

1. Calculate your profit per job (price minus labour, materials and travel), then your lead close rate from the last 50 enquiries.
2. Work out your break-even cost per lead with the formula above.
3. Open Google's eligibility tool and check your category and ZIP, postcode or city.
4. Decide who answers the LSA number in business hours, and what happens when they cannot. A 5-minute call-back rule is a good start.
5. If it is a yes, launch with a small weekly budget, review leads every week, and compare cost per booked job with your Search ads after a month.

## FAQ

**Q: Are Local Services Ads worth it for a small business?**
A: They are worth testing when your category is eligible, you can answer calls fast, and your break-even cost per lead (close rate x profit per job) is above what Google quotes you. Otherwise Search ads and a strong Business Profile are the better first spend.

**Q: Can salons, groomers or coffee shops use LSAs?**
A: In the US, Google's category list includes hair salons, barbers, nail salons, personal trainers, yoga studios, pet grooming and coffee shops, though some categories are limited by location. The UK and Canada lists we saw were trades-focused. Confirm with the eligibility tool.

**Q: Do I pay for missed calls?**
A: Possibly. Google's billing page says a missed call during business hours where the caller stays on for more than 20 seconds is chargeable.

**Q: Can I get a refund for bad leads?**
A: Google says it automatically credits poor-quality, duplicate or invalid leads after reviewing them over 30 days, with credits usually arriving within 30 days. It no longer credits job-type or geography mismatches, and credits are not available for health care, tax specialists or EMEA advertisers.

**Q: How much should I budget?**
A: Google shows estimates in setup. A sensible start is enough for 10 to 20 leads a month at your quoted price, then adjust using cost per booked job. That range is our rule of thumb, not a Google figure.
