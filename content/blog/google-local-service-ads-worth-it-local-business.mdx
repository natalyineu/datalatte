Google Local Services Ads (LSAs) are often worth testing if your business is in an eligible service category and you can answer calls quickly, because you pay per lead rather than per click and the ads appear at the top of local results. They are not worth it if your category is not supported, if you cannot respond fast, or if you have not first measured what a lead is worth to you.

LSAs are a different product from standard Google Search ads. They are tied to your Google Business Profile and, in most categories, a verification process. Here is how they work, what to watch out for and how to decide.

## What Local Services Ads Are

LSAs are Google's pay-per-lead ads for local service businesses. They typically appear above or alongside regular search ads and the map results, and show your business name, rating, reviews, hours and a call or message option. Many categories also display a "Google Guaranteed" or "Google Screened" badge once you pass Google's checks.

Because availability changes by country, state and category, check Google's current list rather than assuming. The page where you sign up will tell you whether you can advertise in your location.

## Benefits

- **Top-of-page visibility** for high-intent searches such as "plumber near me".
- **Pay per lead** instead of per click. You pay for valid leads that call or message you, so you do not pay for every curiosity click.
- **Trust badges** where available, which can help new businesses stand out.
- **Simple setup** compared with a full Search campaign, since much of the information comes from your profile.
- **Lead management tools** to mark leads as booked and to dispute invalid ones.

## Drawbacks and Risks

- **Eligibility is limited.** Many categories, and many regions, are not supported. Retail and food service businesses such as coffee shops and bakeries are generally not eligible, so those businesses should look to Search ads and their Business Profile instead.
- **Verification takes time.** Background checks, licensing, insurance and review steps vary by category and location.
- **You may pay for leads that never convert.** Wrong-service, out-of-area and spam leads happen. Use the dispute process, and review your lead log.
- **Missed leads cost money.** If you do not answer, the lead may still be billed unless it is credited. Answer quickly or have a backup.
- **Less control** than standard Google Ads over keywords, ad text and targeting.
- **Competitive markets** can drive lead prices up, especially for legal, home services and other high-value categories.

<Callout type="warning" title="Respond fast">
Speed matters. Customers often contact several providers, and slow responses lose jobs while the lead still counts against your budget. Set up call forwarding and notifications before you launch.
</Callout>

## How to Get Started

1. **Check eligibility** for your business type and location in Google's LSA sign-up flow.
2. **Prepare your Google Business Profile.** Make sure your name, address, phone, hours, categories and photos are accurate and match what you submit. See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).
3. **Complete verification** (business, licenses, insurance and any required checks).
4. **Choose service types and areas** that you actually want jobs in.
5. **Set a weekly budget** and, where offered, a bid or lead cap, and start modestly.
6. **Answer, track and dispute.** Mark booked jobs, dispute invalid leads and review weekly.

## Budget and Costs

Lead prices vary widely by industry, city and competition. Google shows estimates for your category and area during setup. Rather than guessing, test:

| Question | How to answer it |
|---|---|
| What can I afford per booked job? | Average job value times profit margin, times your close rate |
| What does a lead cost here? | Use Google's estimate, then check your first few weeks of data |
| How many leads become jobs? | Track calls answered, quotes given and jobs booked |
| Is it better than my Search campaign? | Compare cost per booked job, not cost per lead |

A common approach is to run LSAs and Search side by side for at least a month and compare cost per booked appointment. If you want to go deeper into bidding, read [Google Ads bid strategies for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-bid-strategy-which-to-use-local-business).

## LSAs vs. Standard Google Ads

| | Local Services Ads | Search ads |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing | Per lead | Per click |
| Control | Limited | High (keywords, copy, bids) |
| Setup | Verification, then simple | More work, more options |
| Best for | Eligible, call-driven services | Any business, more complex funnels |
| Badge | Google Guaranteed or Screened, where available | None |

Many local service businesses run both. LSAs capture ready-to-hire callers, while Search covers specific services and keywords LSAs do not. ## Tips to Get More From LSAs

- Keep reviews coming and respond to them. Rating and review count are prominent in LSAs.
- Keep your hours accurate and your phone answered during them.
- Use the lead log to mark which leads booked, so Google can learn.
- Check the job types and service areas regularly to avoid unwanted leads.
- Review the dispute options for wrong-service and spam leads.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a Google Business Profile to run LSAs?**
A: Yes. LSAs are linked to your profile, so it should be complete and accurate, and the details should match your LSA application.

**Q: Can I run LSAs from a home-based business?**
A: Sometimes. Requirements depend on the category and country, and service-area businesses may be treated differently from those with a public storefront. Check Google's current rules in the sign-up flow.

**Q: How much should I budget?**
A: Use Google's estimates for your category and area, start with a modest weekly budget and adjust based on cost per booked job.

**Q: What happens if I miss a lead?**
A: Missed or unanswered leads can still count against your budget in some cases, so answer promptly and use the dispute tools for invalid leads.

**Q: Are LSAs available for every business?**
A: No. Eligible categories vary by location and change over time, so check Google's current list. Retail and food businesses are generally not supported.
