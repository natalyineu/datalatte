The email campaigns most small businesses should set up first are a welcome series, a thank-you after the first purchase, a cart or browse reminder if you sell online, a winback for lapsed customers, and a segmented newsletter. This guide lists 12 campaigns, shows which ones run automatically, and gives the one public benchmark that could be verified: Klaviyo's revenue per recipient for three flows.

## What counts as an email campaign for a small business?

Two kinds of email matter. A flow (also called an automation) starts by itself when a customer does something, such as joining the list or abandoning a cart. A one-off campaign is sent by you on a date, such as a newsletter or a holiday offer.

Klaviyo's help center describes metric-triggered flows: abandoned cart (Started Checkout or Added to Cart), post-purchase (Placed Order), browse abandonment (Viewed Product) and winback (Placed Order). These need a connected store or integration to send the event data.

## Which campaigns welcome and thank new customers?

Klaviyo lists "Welcome series" under "Nurture subscribers" and "First purchase thank you & bounceback" under "Build customer loyalty". A welcome series starts when someone joins the list, so it reaches people at the moment they chose to hear from you.

A thank-you email after the first order does a different job: it confirms the purchase and points to a second one. For list-building tactics that feed the welcome series, see the guide to email list building strategies for local businesses.

## Which campaigns recover lost sales?

Three Klaviyo templates sit under "Prevent lost sales": browse abandonment, abandoned cart reminder (one variant for carts, one for checkout) and back in stock.

Klaviyo's help article separates two cart flows. The default flow starts at Checkout Started. The Added to Cart flow "targets more casual shoppers who have yet to start checkout" and drops anyone who begins checkout or orders after entering. Both need event tracking switched on. A full walkthrough, including the rules, is in [the abandoned cart email strategy](/blog/abandoned-cart-email-strategy).

## Which campaigns bring customers back?

Klaviyo groups "Customer winback", "Price drop notification" and "Low inventory reminder" under "Remind customers to purchase". The winback flow starts from the Placed Order metric, so it measures time since the last order.

Under "Encourage repeat purchases" the library lists post-purchase follow-up, post-purchase bounce back, repeat purchase nurture series, replenishment reminder and product review or cross-sell. A "First purchase anniversary" template sits under loyalty. Replenishment suits products that run out, such as coffee beans or dog shampoo. Service businesses can use the same logic with a date since the last visit.

For review requests, the rules from Google matter; they are covered in the post-purchase email strategy.

## How do you segment a list without a big budget?

Mailchimp defines it this way: "To segment is to filter and divide similar contacts." Its help page names engagement with your last email, purchase history, sign-up date and location as example conditions. Contacts not subscribed to the channel are removed automatically.

Plan limits differ. Regular segments on Free or Essentials allow up to 10 conditions with one and/or operator; the advanced builder with nested logic is listed from the Standard plan up. Tools that handle segmentation:

- [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/): Segments by engagement, purchase history, sign-up date and location, with the plan limits described above.
- [Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/): Flow templates and metric-triggered flows for abandoned cart, post-purchase, browse abandonment and winback.
- [Flodesk](https://flodesk.com/): Built around design, with advanced segmentation and multi-trigger workflows. Signup forms, including popups, inline forms and landing pages, can be tied to a segment as people join.

A segmented newsletter (campaign 11) then needs only one decision: which segment gets which message. Start with new subscribers, recent buyers and people who have not opened for a long time.

Klaviyo publishes benchmarks for revenue per recipient (flow revenue divided by delivered emails) across thousands of its customers, last updated October 8, 2026. The table shows the 25th to 75th percentile for stores with $1M or less in annual revenue and an average order value of $44 to $83.

## What do the sender rules and the law require?

Google's guidelines say all senders need SPF or DKIM, while bulk senders (more than 5,000 messages a day) need SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and marketing messages must support one-click unsubscribe. A small local list usually stays under that volume, but the 0.3% spam ceiling still applies to everyone.

The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide adds eight requirements: accurate headers, honest subject lines, an ad disclosure, a physical postal address, a clear opt-out, and opt-outs honored within 10 business days. Each violating email can cost up to $53,088. EU and UK rules on consent are in the GDPR email guide, and holiday timing is in the holiday email plan.

## What could a cart-recovery flow be worth? A worked example

Take an online shop with an average order value near $60. Assumption: the abandoned cart flow delivers 1,000 emails in a quarter. Using Klaviyo's range for stores under $1M in revenue, multiply 1,000 by $0.86 and by $2.91.

Result: roughly $860 to $2,910 in flow-attributed revenue for the quarter. The volume is an assumption, the per-email range is Klaviyo's 25th to 75th percentile. Your own number can fall outside it, so track flow revenue in your platform for a full quarter before judging.

## FAQ

### How many email campaigns does a small business need?

Start with three: a welcome series, a winback and a segmented newsletter. Add cart and browse reminders if you sell online. The other campaigns in the table can wait until the first three run reliably.

### Are automated flows better than newsletters?

Klaviyo's published benchmarks cover flows only, so they cannot be compared with newsletters from this source. Flows run on a customer action, which is why they fit lists that are too small to send often.

### Do these campaigns work for cafes and salons?

The Klaviyo templates and benchmarks come from online stores. A cafe or salon can use the welcome series, winback by date since the last visit, segmented newsletters and holiday sends. Cart and back-in-stock campaigns apply only with online ordering.
