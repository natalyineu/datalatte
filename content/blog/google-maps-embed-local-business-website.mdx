To embed Google Maps on your business website, open Google Maps on a computer, search for your business, click Share, choose the Embed a map tab, copy the HTML code and paste it into a custom HTML or embed block on your site. It takes a few minutes, and it helps customers find you, get directions and trust that you are a real, local business.

## Why Embed a Map at All?

As a local business owner, you invest a lot in making your storefront welcoming and visible. An embedded map is the digital equivalent of a clear sign on your door. It lets visitors see where you are, check how far away you are and tap for directions without leaving your page.

An embed does not magically boost your rankings. Google does not publish a ranking boost for having a map on your site. The value comes from convenience for customers and from reinforcing a consistent, real-world location alongside your name, address and phone number. The map you really need to get right for visibility is your Google Business Profile.

## Step 1: Get Your Google Business Profile in Order

The map you embed pulls from Google's listing for your business, so make sure that listing is accurate first. (Google Business Profile was formerly called Google My Business.)

- Claim your profile at business.google.com and verify it.
- Check that your name, address, phone number and hours are correct and consistent with your website.
- Confirm that the map pin sits exactly on your entrance or building.
- Add photos, categories and a description.

## Step 2: Get the Embed Code

You can get the code from Google Maps itself:

1. Open Google Maps in a desktop browser (the embed option is not shown in the mobile app).
2. Search for your business name so your listing appears.
3. Click **Share**.
4. Choose the **Embed a map** tab.
5. Pick a size, then click **Copy HTML**.

There are two common approaches. The simple iframe embed above is free, quick and good enough for most small businesses. A custom map built with the Google Maps Platform lets you style markers or show multiple locations, but it needs a developer, an API key and billing set up on a Google Cloud account, so only choose it if you need those features. Check Google's current pricing and terms before you do.

## Step 3: Add the Embed Code to Your Website

How you add the code depends on your platform.

- **WordPress:** Add a Custom HTML block on your contact or location page and paste the code.
- **Squarespace, Wix and Shopify:** Use an Embed, HTML or Custom code block and paste the code, or use the platform's built-in map block, which usually works fine.
- **Custom site:** Paste the iframe where you want the map to appear.

Save, then view the live page on a phone and a computer to check that the map loads and the pin is correct.

## Step 4: Make Sure the Map Works on Mobile

Most local searches happen on phones, so check the mobile experience carefully.

- Make the iframe width flexible (for example 100 percent) so it fits small screens.
- Give the map a sensible height so it does not dominate the page.
- Put your address, phone number and a "Get directions" link near the map as text. Do not rely on the map alone.
- Test on a real phone, not only a desktop preview.

## Step 5: Keep It Fast

The default embed can add weight to your page, because it loads Google's map code. To keep the page fast:

- Add `loading="lazy"` to the iframe so it loads only when visitors scroll near it.
- Put the map on your contact or location page rather than on every page.
- If you have several locations, give each one its own page with its own map instead of cramming several maps onto one page.

## Step 6: Measure Whether It Helps

You cannot tell what a map is doing without tracking.

- **Google Analytics:** Track visits to your contact or location page, and set up an event for clicks on your phone number or directions link.
- **Google Business Profile performance:** Review how many people request directions, call or visit your website from your profile.
- **Google Search Console:** Monitor how your location pages perform in search.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Embedding a map that points to the wrong place because your profile pin is off.
- Hiding your address in the map image only, instead of also writing it as text.
- Adding maps to every page, which slows your site without helping visitors.
- Using a different business name or phone number on your site than on your profile.
- Forgetting to check the map after you move, rebrand or change your hours.

## FAQ

**Q: Will embedding a Google Map slow down my website?**
A: It can. The map loads extra resources from Google. Use lazy loading so the map only loads when visitors scroll to it, and avoid placing several maps on one page.

**Q: What if I have multiple locations?**
A: Give each location its own page with its own embedded map, name, address, phone and hours. This is clearer for visitors and for search engines than putting every map on a single page.

**Q: Can I use Apple Maps instead of Google Maps?**
A: You can, but Google Maps is the more common choice for most local audiences in many markets. You can add an "Open in Apple Maps" link alongside the Google embed so iPhone users have both options.

**Q: Does embedding a map help with SEO?**
A: Not directly. Google does not give a documented ranking bonus for it. It helps indirectly by improving user experience and reinforcing a consistent location, especially when your name, address and phone number match your Google Business Profile. For ranking, focus on your profile, reviews and [local SEO](/services/local-seo).

**Q: My map embed shows the wrong location. How do I fix it?**
A: Start with your Google Business Profile. If the pin is off, correct it there, or suggest an edit in Google Maps. If the embed shows a different listing, search again and copy a fresh embed code for the correct place.

**Q: Can I track how many people use the map to find my business?**
A: You can track clicks on your directions link and phone number with Google Analytics or Google Tag Manager. A map embedded in an iframe does not report clicks inside it, so use a separate "Get directions" link you can measure. Your Google Business Profile also reports direction requests.
