A good dental website builds trust fast and makes booking effortless: clear services, real photos of your team and practice, visible reviews, an obvious "Book an appointment" button, and pages that load quickly on a phone. If a nervous patient cannot find your hours, insurance information and a way to book within a few seconds, they will usually try the next practice.

For dentists, a website is more than a brochure. Patients are often anxious, comparing several practices, and looking for signals that you are safe, friendly and easy to deal with. This guide covers what to include, how to structure the site, and the compliance and technical details that matter.

## What patients look for on a dental website

Patients tend to arrive with one of a few goals: find an emergency dentist, check whether you accept their insurance or plan, learn about a specific treatment, or compare practices. Design the site around those questions.

Common priorities include:

- Are you nearby, and are you open when I can come?
- Are you taking new patients?
- Do you accept my insurance, or what are the self-pay and payment options?
- Who will treat me, and do they seem kind?
- Can I book online, or call quickly?
- Do you treat the thing I need, such as implants, braces, children or emergencies?

## Step 1: Get the homepage right

The top of your homepage (the "above the fold" area on a phone) should include:

1. A clear headline stating what you do and where: for example, "Family and cosmetic dentistry in [town]".
2. A prominent "Book online" button and a tap-to-call number.
3. A line about new patients, opening hours or emergency availability.
4. A friendly, real photo of your team or practice, not a stock image of a smiling model.
5. Trust cues: review rating, professional memberships and years serving the community. Use these only if accurate.

## Step 2: Plan a clear page structure

| Page | What to include |
|---|---|
| Home | Core services, booking, location, reviews, insurance note |
| Services overview | Links to each treatment |
| One page per major service | Check-ups and cleaning, fillings, crowns, implants, whitening, orthodontics, emergency care, children's dentistry |
| New patients | What to expect, forms, insurance and payment options, accessibility |
| Meet the team | Photos, short bios, qualifications, personal touches |
| Contact and location | Map, address, parking, public transport, hours, booking form |
| Reviews | Genuine patient feedback, with permission and compliance in mind |
| Blog or FAQ | Answers to common questions |

Each service page should explain what the treatment is, who it is for, what to expect, how long it takes, aftercare, and how to book. Write in plain language and avoid jargon. If you mention prices, make clear that they vary with the case, or explain what influences cost.

## Step 3: Make booking effortless

- Offer online booking through your practice management system or a compatible tool, if available.
- Keep forms short. Name, phone, email, reason for visit and preferred time is often enough.
- Show a tap-to-call number on every page on mobile.
- Confirm requests quickly. A promise like "we reply within one working day" is helpful if you can keep it.
- Make emergency instructions clear: what to do and who to call.

Patients booking from a phone at lunchtime will not fill out a long form. See our guide to [booking form optimisation](/blog/booking-form-optimization).

## Step 4: Build trust the right way

Trust is the main currency in dentistry. Ways to build it on your site:

- **Real photos** of the reception, treatment rooms and team.
- **Team bios** with qualifications and a personal line about why they became a dentist.
- **Reviews** from Google or other platforms, displayed with permission and in line with local rules.
- **Clear information** about infection control and safety, in language patients understand.
- **Accessibility details**: step-free entry, parking, and support for anxious patients.

Be careful with before-and-after photos and testimonials. Advertising rules for healthcare differ by country and profession, and dental boards in the US, the UK (such as the General Dental Council), Canada and Australia set their own guidance. Check your regulator's current standards, and obtain written consent for any patient images. Do not make guarantees or exaggerated claims.

## Step 5: Optimise for local search

Most patients search locally ("dentist near me", "emergency dentist [town]"). The website supports your Google Business Profile.

- Use a unique title and heading for each page, including the service and area where natural.
- Keep your name, address and phone number consistent with your Google profile.
- Add LocalBusiness or Dentist structured data so search engines can understand your practice details.
- Embed a map and write directions in text.
- Link service pages together and to the booking page.

## Step 6: Technical basics that affect patients

- **Mobile-first design.** Most local searches happen on phones. Test on a few devices.
- **Speed.** Compress images, avoid heavy sliders and videos that auto-play. Check Core Web Vitals in Google Search Console. Our article on [Core Web Vitals for local business websites](/blog/core-web-vitals-local-business-website) explains what to look for.
- **HTTPS and security.** Required for forms that collect personal details.
- **Accessibility.** Good contrast, readable font sizes, alt text and keyboard-friendly navigation help all users and may be a legal expectation in some regions.
- **Privacy.** If your site collects health-related information or uses tracking tools, review privacy law and consent requirements in your region (for example HIPAA in the US for protected health information, and UK GDPR or Canadian and Australian privacy law). Take advice before adding tracking pixels to pages involving patient data.

## Step 7: Decide who builds it

You have three broad options:

| Option | Typical fit | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|

## Step 8: Track what matters

Set up tracking for calls, booking clicks and form submissions. Review monthly:

- How many visitors become booking requests?
- Which pages lead to bookings?
- Which sources (Google search, profile, ads) bring them?
- Where do visitors drop off?

Be mindful of privacy when tracking. See our [Google Tag Manager guide](/blog/google-tag-manager-for-small-business) for no-code tracking basics.

## FAQ

**Q: What pages does a dental website need?**
A: At minimum: home, services (ideally one page per main treatment), new patient information, team, contact and location, and a booking option. Add reviews and an FAQ or blog to answer common questions.

**Q: Should I list prices on my dental website?**
A: It depends on your practice and regulations. Many practices explain what affects cost and give "from" prices for routine services, which can reduce unnecessary calls, while noting that complex cases vary.

**Q: Can I use patient testimonials and before-and-after photos?**
A: Often yes, but only with written consent and within your dental regulator's advertising rules. Standards vary by country, so check current guidance.

**Q: How often should I update my dental website?**
A: Review hours, team details and insurance information at least quarterly, and update whenever something changes. Fresh, accurate content helps patients and supports your local search presence.
