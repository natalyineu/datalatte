## What is the difference between HomeAdvisor and Angi?

Both are marketplaces where homeowners describe a project or browse pros, and the platform charges service pros for the connection. The company changed its name to Angi Inc. in March 2021 and keeps several brands running at once (Angi Inc. 10-K, FY2025).

Naming is messy. Jobber and several vendor guides say HomeAdvisor is now sold as Angi Leads and Angie's List as Angi Ads. Angi's 10-K does not use those two names and still lists HomeAdvisor as a brand, so ask the rep which product and contract you are actually signing.

- **Angi Ads** combines a business profile, reviews and paid promotion. Homeowners can browse pros directly, so your profile and reviews matter even when you are not buying a lead.
- **HomeAdvisor (called Angi Leads in some guides)** is the pay-per-lead product. Angi's annual report says pros pay fees for consumer matches regardless of whether they end up doing the work, and that in 2025 pros used either full-priced leads within a monthly budget or discounted leads in a subscription package ([Angi Inc. Form 10-K, FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000011/angi-20251231.htm)).

## How much do HomeAdvisor and Angi leads cost?

Angi's annual report says lead revenue varies with the service requested, the product experience and the geographic location ([Angi Inc. Form 10-K, FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000011/angi-20251231.htm)), so any single published price is unreliable.

Jobber, a secondary source, puts Angi Ads leads at about $15 to $85 and HomeAdvisor (Angi Leads) leads at $15 to $100, depending on service, location and competition ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/homeadvisor-vs-angi/), checked Oct 2026). These figures are not from Angi, so ask for your own trade and ZIP codes. What matters is the structure.

| Factor | Angi Ads | HomeAdvisor (Angi Leads) |
|--------|----------|--------------------------|
| Typical model | Paid promotion of your profile; terms depend on the plan | Pay per lead, plus a subscription or monthly budget depending on the offer |
| Cost per lead | Ask for your category and ZIP | Ask for your category and ZIP |
| Lead sharing | Confirm in writing | A single service request can produce multiple leads, so assume shared |
| Best fit | Profile and review-driven pros, higher-ticket jobs | Contractors who can respond in minutes and screen leads |
| Watch out for | Contract length and cancellation terms | Paying for leads that are not a fit for your service or area |

## How do you calculate cost per signed job?

The number to calculate is total platform spend divided by jobs won. Every figure below is an assumption to show the arithmetic, not Angi pricing and not a market average. Swap in your own numbers.

| Assumption | Platform A | Platform B |
|--------------------|-----------|-----------|
| Monthly budget | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Price per lead | $90 | $50 |
| Leads bought | 11.1 | 20 |
| Close rate | 25% (1 in 4) | 10% (1 in 10) |
| Jobs won | 2.8 | 2.0 |
| Cost per signed job | $360 | $500 |

Now add the job size. Assume a 20% profit margin on each job.

| Average job | Profit per job at 20% | Platform A ($360) | Platform B ($500) |
|-------------|-----------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| $5,000 remodel | $1,000 | +$640 | +$500 |
| $1,500 repair | $300 | -$60 | -$200 |
| $400 service call | $80 | -$280 | -$420 |

The lesson: the cheaper lead (B) earned less on every row, and a $400 job loses money on both. A $90 lead can beat a $50 lead. Small jobs cannot carry a $360 acquisition cost at all.

<Callout type="warning">Ask about every fee before signing: subscription or membership fees, per-lead charges, credits for bad leads, cancellation terms and any extra fees on completed jobs. Get the answers in writing.</Callout>

## How is a lead different from a job request?

Angi's annual report counts both. In 2025 it recorded 15.5 million service requests and 20.2 million leads, so one request produced about 1.3 leads on average (20,195 divided by 15,543, thousands, company-wide including international). The average hides the range: some requests go to one pro, others to several.

For you it means a share of leads are contested. The report also says pros pay for matches whether or not they do the work. The active-pro base fell from 152 thousand to 122 thousand monthly on average between 2024 and 2025, so competition per request may be lower than before, but the filing does not say that directly.

<Callout type="stat" title="What the 10-K does not say">Angi does not publish revenue per lead, win rates or refund rates. Any "average close rate" you read elsewhere is an estimate.</Callout>

## What lead quality should you expect?

Lead quality depends on your trade, your service area and how well your profile and filters are set up.

- **Speed matters.** If a lead is sent to several contractors, the homeowner can talk to whoever answers first. Reply within 5 minutes, not hours.
- **Job size filters help.** Ask your rep which filters you can set, such as service type and area. Set a minimum job size if the platform allows it, so you do not pay for small repairs you would decline anyway.
- **Price-shoppers exist on both.** Expect some leads that are only collecting quotes. Screening protects your time.

For high-ticket work such as remodels or HVAC installs, paying more per lead for better intent can make sense. For low-ticket work, shared leads rarely pay back, as the table above shows.

<Callout type="tip">Start with a small test budget on each platform, such as $300 over 30 to 60 days, and track every lead in a spreadsheet. Your own close rate will tell you more than any published average.</Callout>

## How do you set up and manage your account?

1. **Complete your profile.** Add licenses, insurance, a clear service list and photos of finished work. A thin profile loses leads before the first call.
2. **Set a small budget.** Adjust after two to four weeks of data.
3. **Define filters.** Limit service types, service area and, where available, project size.
4. **Respond fast.** Turn on app and text alerts. A "got your request, calling in 15 minutes" text keeps you in the running.
5. **Track every lead.** Record date, source, cost, response time, estimate booked and job value if won.
6. **Review monthly.** Calculate cost per lead, per estimate and per signed job. Cut whichever is not paying back.

## What should you do today?

1. Pull your last 20 leads from any platform. Count how many you answered within 5 minutes.
2. Write down your average job value and your profit margin. Multiply them.
3. Divide your last 30 days of platform spend by jobs won. Compare it with the profit per job.
4. If cost per job is above profit per job, pause that platform or cut the budget in half.
5. Ask the rep for the credit and refund policy on bad leads, in writing.

## How to qualify leads before you spend time on them

A simple two-step screen saves hours:

- **Step 1: automated text or email** within minutes asking project size, whether they have other quotes, and their timeline.
- **Step 2: a short call** for leads that answer sensibly. Confirm budget range, property ownership and any constraints before booking a site visit.

If a prospect will not discuss budget or timing, move on. This protects your schedule and improves your cost per signed job.

## When should you choose one over the other?

- **High-ticket, low-volume work (remodels, decks, installations):** a profile-and-review product such as Angi Ads may suit you, since homeowners compare pros before contacting them.
- **Lower-ticket, higher-volume work (repairs, maintenance):** pay-per-lead products like HomeAdvisor can work if you can respond instantly and your per-lead cost is small compared with the job value.

## FAQ

**Q: Is HomeAdvisor the same as Angi?**
A: They share a parent, Angi Inc., but they are separate brands with different products. HomeAdvisor is the pay-per-lead service, while Angi (formerly Angie's List) centers on profiles, reviews and promotion. Some guides call HomeAdvisor "Angi Leads", though Angi's 10-K still lists HomeAdvisor by name.

**Q: Is Angi better than HomeAdvisor for contractors?**
A: Neither wins across the board. Angi suits pros who rely on reviews and higher-ticket jobs, and HomeAdvisor suits those who can answer in minutes. Compare cost per signed job from a small test on each.

**Q: How much does HomeAdvisor charge contractors per lead?**
A: Jobber, a secondary source, gives $15 to $100 per lead depending on service, location and competition. HomeAdvisor does not publish a public rate card, so request a quote for your trade and ZIP codes.

**Q: What is the HomeAdvisor business model?**
A: Homeowners search for free, and pros pay. Angi's 10-K says pros pay fees for consumer matches whether or not they win the work, and some buy subscription packages with discounted leads.

**Q: What is the difference between Angi leads and Angi ads?**
A: Leads are homeowner requests you pay for per match. Ads are paid promotion of your profile, priced as a plan or per click depending on the offer. See our guide to Angi Ads vs Angi Leads.

**Q: Are leads exclusive?**
A: Assume not. The 10-K says a single service request can result in multiple leads, so speed and screening matter. Confirm exclusivity terms in writing.

**Q: Can I get a refund for bad leads?**
A: Some products offer credits or disputes for invalid leads, but policies change. Ask for the current policy in writing and document each problem lead.

**Q: What is a cheaper alternative?**
A: Google Local Services Ads, a well-optimized Google Business Profile, referrals and your own Google Ads campaigns. A mix is safer than a single marketplace.
