Angi Ads and HomeAdvisor are not rival companies. They are two products from the same parent, Angi Inc. HomeAdvisor's lead-selling business now operates under the name Angi Leads, while Angi Ads is the directory-promotion product that grew out of Angie's List ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/homeadvisor-vs-angi/), checked Oct 2026). Angi Leads sells homeowner requests you pay for whether or not you win the job. Angi Ads is built around promoting your business profile. Which one is worth it depends on your job size and how fast you answer, and the number that settles it is cost per signed job, not cost per lead.

Your phone buzzes at 7:12 on a Saturday morning. A homeowner wants a quote on a bathroom remodel. You call at 7:40. She already spoke to someone else at 7:18. Angi's annual report says a single service request can result in multiple leads, so assume that scene is normal, and that you paid for the call you lost.

Angi does not publish a public price list for pros. Ask each sales rep for current pricing in writing and check your own results before spending more.

## What are Angi Ads and HomeAdvisor?

Both are marketplaces where homeowners describe a project or browse pros, and the platform charges service pros for the connection.

- **Angi Ads** combines a business profile, reviews and paid promotion. Homeowners can browse pros directly, so your profile and reviews matter even when you are not buying a lead.
- **HomeAdvisor (now Angi Leads)** is the pay-per-lead product. Angi's annual report says pros pay fees for consumer matches regardless of whether they end up doing the work, and that in 2025 pros used either full-priced leads within a monthly budget or discounted leads in a subscription package ([Angi Inc. Form 10-K, FY2025](https://ir.angi.com/static-files/d3031b4a-745e-42cc-8b21-d749dd0ed2f2)).

If you want the details on how Angi's own products differ, read our guide to [Angi Ads vs Angi Leads](/blog/angi-ads-vs-angi-leads-difference).

## How do the costs compare?

Angi's annual report says lead revenue varies with the service requested, the product experience and the geographic location ([Angi Inc. Form 10-K, FY2025](https://ir.angi.com/static-files/d3031b4a-745e-42cc-8b21-d749dd0ed2f2)), so any single published price is unreliable. Third-party guides quote wide per-lead ranges, but we could not verify one against Angi, so ask for your own trade and ZIP codes. What matters is the structure.

| Factor | Angi Ads | HomeAdvisor (Angi Leads) |
|--------|----------|--------------------------|
| Typical model | Paid promotion of your profile; terms depend on the plan | Pay per lead, plus a subscription or monthly budget depending on the offer |
| Cost per lead | Ask for your category and ZIP | Ask for your category and ZIP |
| Lead sharing | Confirm in writing | A single service request can produce multiple leads, so assume shared |
| Best fit | Profile and review-driven pros, higher-ticket jobs | Contractors who can respond in minutes and screen leads |
| Watch out for | Contract length and cancellation terms | Paying for leads that are not a fit for your service or area |

## The math: cost per signed job

The number to calculate is total platform spend divided by jobs won. Every figure below is an invented example to show the arithmetic, not Angi pricing and not a market average. Swap in your own numbers.

| Example assumption | Platform A | Platform B |
|--------------------|-----------|-----------|
| Monthly budget | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Price per lead | $90 | $50 |
| Leads bought | 11.1 | 20 |
| Close rate | 25% (1 in 4) | 10% (1 in 10) |
| Jobs won | 2.8 | 2.0 |
| Cost per signed job | $360 | $500 |

Now add the job size. Assume a 20% profit margin on each job.

| Average job | Profit per job at 20% | Platform A ($360) | Platform B ($500) |
|-------------|-----------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| $5,000 remodel | $1,000 | +$640 | +$500 |
| $1,500 repair | $300 | -$60 | -$200 |
| $400 service call | $80 | -$280 | -$420 |

The lesson: the cheaper lead (B) earned less on every row, and a $400 job loses money on both. A $90 lead can beat a $50 lead. Small jobs cannot carry a $360 acquisition cost at all.

<Callout type="warning">Ask about every fee before signing: subscription or membership fees, per-lead charges, credits for bad leads, cancellation terms and any extra fees on completed jobs. Get the answers in writing.</Callout>

## Lead quality: what to expect

Lead quality depends on your trade, your service area and how well your profile and filters are set up.

- **Speed matters.** If a lead is sent to several contractors, the homeowner can talk to whoever answers first. Reply within 5 minutes, not hours.
- **Job size filters help.** Ask your rep which filters you can set, such as service type and area. Set a minimum job size if the platform allows it, so you do not pay for small repairs you would decline anyway.
- **Price-shoppers exist on both.** Expect some leads that are only collecting quotes. Screening protects your time.

For high-ticket work such as remodels or HVAC installs, paying more per lead for better intent can make sense. For low-ticket work, I would not buy shared leads at all.

<Callout type="tip">Start with a small test budget on each platform, such as $300 over 30 to 60 days, and track every lead in a spreadsheet. Your own close rate will tell you more than any published average.</Callout>

## Setting up and managing your account

1. **Complete your profile.** Add licenses, insurance, a clear service list and photos of finished work. A thin profile loses leads before the first call.
2. **Set a small budget.** Adjust after two to four weeks of data.
3. **Define filters.** Limit service types, service area and, where available, project size.
4. **Respond fast.** Turn on app and text alerts. A "got your request, calling in 15 minutes" text keeps you in the running.
5. **Track every lead.** Record date, source, cost, response time, estimate booked and job value if won.
6. **Review monthly.** Calculate cost per lead, per estimate and per signed job. Cut whichever is not paying back.

## Do this today

1. Pull your last 20 leads from any platform. Count how many you answered within 5 minutes.
2. Write down your average job value and your profit margin. Multiply them.
3. Divide your last 30 days of platform spend by jobs won. Compare it with the profit per job.
4. If cost per job is above profit per job, pause that platform or cut the budget in half.
5. Ask the rep for the credit and refund policy on bad leads, in writing.

## How to qualify leads before you spend time on them

A simple two-step screen saves hours:

- **Step 1: automated text or email** within minutes asking project size, whether they have other quotes, and their timeline.
- **Step 2: a short call** for leads that answer sensibly. Confirm budget range, property ownership and any constraints before booking a site visit.

If a prospect will not discuss budget or timing, move on. This protects your schedule and improves your cost per signed job.

## When to choose one over the other

- **High-ticket, low-volume work (remodels, decks, installations):** a profile-and-review product such as Angi Ads may suit you, since homeowners compare pros before contacting them.
- **Lower-ticket, higher-volume work (repairs, maintenance):** pay-per-lead products like HomeAdvisor can work if you can respond instantly and your per-lead cost is small compared with the job value.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Angi better than HomeAdvisor for contractors?**
A: They are sister products from the same company, not rivals, and neither is better for everyone. Angi Ads leans on your profile and reviews, while HomeAdvisor (now Angi Leads) is the pay-per-lead option. Compare cost per signed job from a small test on each.

**Q: How much do Angi and HomeAdvisor leads cost?**
A: It varies by trade, location and product, and Angi does not publish a public price list. Ask each platform for pricing in your category and ZIP codes, and calculate cost per signed job instead of looking only at cost per lead.

**Q: Are Angi and HomeAdvisor leads exclusive?**
A: Assume not. Angi's annual report notes that a single service request can result in multiple leads, so response speed and screening matter. Confirm exclusivity terms for your plan before you buy.

**Q: Can I get a refund for bad leads?**
A: Some products offer credits or disputes for invalid leads, but policies change. Ask for the current policy in writing and document each problem lead.

**Q: What is a cheaper alternative to Angi and HomeAdvisor?**
A: Google Local Services Ads, a well-optimized Google Business Profile, referrals and your own Google Ads campaigns are common alternatives. Use a mix rather than relying on a single marketplace.
