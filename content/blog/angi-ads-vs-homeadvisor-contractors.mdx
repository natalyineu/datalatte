Neither platform wins for every contractor. Angi tends to suit higher-ticket jobs where you want fewer, better-qualified leads, while HomeAdvisor is typically a volume play that works only if you respond fast and screen hard. Both sell leads that are often shared with other contractors, so the real question is which one gives you the lowest cost per signed job, not the lowest cost per lead.

Angi and HomeAdvisor are closely related (they share a parent company and a large part of their lead network), and their products and pricing change regularly. Treat every number below as a typical range, ask each sales rep for current pricing in writing, and check your own results before spending more.

## What are Angi Ads and HomeAdvisor?

Both are marketplaces where homeowners describe a project and the platform sells that request to one or more home-service pros.

- **Angi (including Angi Ads and Angi Leads)** combines a business profile, reviews and paid lead or advertising products. Homeowners can browse pros directly, so your profile and reviews matter even when you are not buying a lead.
- **HomeAdvisor** is better known for pay-per-lead pricing, where you are charged when a matched homeowner request is sent to you, whether or not you win the job.

## How do the costs compare?

Pricing varies by trade, region and season, so published averages are unreliable. What matters is the structure.

| Factor | Angi Ads / Angi Leads | HomeAdvisor |
|--------|----------------------|-------------|
| Typical model | Subscription or ad spend plus per-lead fees, depending on product | Mostly pay-per-lead |
| Cost per lead | Varies widely by trade and market; ask for your category and ZIP | Varies widely by trade and market; ask for your category and ZIP |
| Lead sharing | Often shared with multiple pros | Often shared with multiple pros |
| Best fit | Higher-ticket jobs, profile and review-driven pros | Contractors who can respond in minutes and screen leads |
| Watch out for | Contract length and cancellation terms | Paying for leads that are not a fit for your service or area |

**The number to calculate is cost per signed job:** total platform spend divided by jobs won. A $90 lead that closes one time in four costs you $360 per job. A $50 lead that closes one in ten costs $500. If your average job is $5,000, that difference is small. If your average job is $400, it can decide whether the platform is profitable at all.

<Callout type="warning">Ask about every fee before signing: subscription or membership fees, per-lead charges, credits for bad leads, cancellation terms and any extra fees on completed jobs. Get the answers in writing.</Callout>

## Lead quality: what to expect

Lead quality depends on your trade, your service area and how well your profile and filters are set up. These patterns show up repeatedly:

- **Speed decides who wins.** If a lead is sent to several contractors, the homeowner often talks to whoever answers first. Reply within minutes, not hours.
- **Job size filters help.** Both platforms let you limit by service type and area. Set a minimum job size if the platform allows it, so you do not pay for small repairs you would decline anyway.
- **Price-shoppers exist on both.** Expect some leads that are only collecting quotes. Screening protects your time.

For high-ticket work such as remodels or HVAC installs, paying more per lead for better intent can make sense. For low-ticket work, volume with fast screening usually matters more. Test both before deciding.

<Callout type="tip">Start with a small test budget on each platform, such as a few hundred dollars over 30 to 60 days, and track every lead in a spreadsheet. Your own close rate will tell you more than any published average.</Callout>

## Setting up and managing your account

1. **Complete your profile.** Add licenses, insurance, a clear service list and photos of finished work. A thin profile loses leads before the first call.
2. **Set a realistic budget.** Begin small and adjust after two to four weeks of data.
3. **Define filters.** Limit service types, service area and, where available, project size.
4. **Respond fast.** Turn on app and text alerts, and assign someone to respond during business hours. A quick "got your request, calling in 15 minutes" text keeps you in the running.
5. **Track every lead.** Record date, source, cost, whether you responded within five minutes, whether you booked an estimate, and the job value if won.
6. **Review monthly.** Calculate cost per lead, cost per estimate and cost per signed job for each platform. Drop or reduce whichever is not paying back.

## How to qualify leads before you spend time on them

Many contractors burn time on unqualified leads. A simple two-step screen helps:

- **Step 1: automated text or email** within minutes asking project size, whether they have other quotes, and their timeline.
- **Step 2: a short call** for leads that answer sensibly. Confirm budget range, property ownership and any constraints before booking a site visit.

If a prospect will not discuss budget or timing, move on. This protects your schedule and improves your cost per signed job.

## When to choose one over the other

- **High-ticket, low-volume work (remodels, decks, installations):** Angi's profile-and-review approach may suit you, since homeowners compare pros before contacting them.
- **Lower-ticket, higher-volume work (repairs, maintenance):** HomeAdvisor-style leads can work if you can respond instantly and your per-lead cost is small compared with the job value.
- **Competitive markets:** compare each platform's cost in your own ZIP codes. Prices differ sharply between a big metro and a small town.
- **Need work this week:** paid leads can arrive within days, but quality varies, so keep your screening strict.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Angi better than HomeAdvisor for contractors?**
A: Neither is better for everyone. Angi often suits higher-ticket work and pros with strong profiles and reviews, while HomeAdvisor is typically a pay-per-lead volume option. Compare cost per signed job from a small test on each.

**Q: How much do Angi and HomeAdvisor leads cost?**
A: It varies widely by trade, location and product. Ask each platform for pricing in your category and ZIP codes, and calculate cost per signed job instead of looking only at cost per lead.

**Q: Are Angi and HomeAdvisor leads exclusive?**
A: Often not. Many leads are sent to multiple contractors, so response speed and screening matter. Confirm exclusivity terms for your plan before you buy.

**Q: Can I get a refund for bad leads?**
A: Some products offer credits or disputes for invalid leads, but policies change. Ask for the current policy in writing and document each problem lead.

**Q: What is a cheaper alternative to Angi and HomeAdvisor?**
A: Google Local Services Ads, a well-optimized Google Business Profile, referrals and your own Google Ads campaigns are common alternatives. Many contractors use a mix rather than relying on a single marketplace.
