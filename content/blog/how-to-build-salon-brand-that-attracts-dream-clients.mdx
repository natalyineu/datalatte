A salon brand that attracts the clients you want rests on three decisions: a specialty you can name in one line, a business name you can use everywhere without variation, and a look and tone that show up the same on your door, website, Google profile and Instagram. Decide these first, then protect the name if you plan to grow.

This guide is for independent hair salon owners and chair renters. It uses platform rules from Google, Instagram and the FTC, and a US trademark fee from a law-firm summary. No source publishes what a brand adds to pricing, so the example uses labelled assumptions.

## What Is a Salon Brand?

A brand is what a client expects before booking. It comes from your specialty, your name, your photos, your prices and how you reply to messages. A salon known for colour correction is judged on that, not on being cheap or open late.

Write one sentence: "We do X for Y." Examples: balayage for clients with long hair, or gentle cuts for children. If you cannot finish the sentence, the marketing will stay vague. ## How Do You Choose a Specialty?

Choose from three inputs: what you do best, what your best-paying clients already book, and what nearby salons do not offer. Check your booking software for the services with the highest ticket and the most repeat visits. Then check Google Maps and Instagram for who else claims that specialty near you.

You do not have to drop other services. Put the specialty first on your website, in your Google categories and in your Instagram bio. Our [Business Profile categories guide](/blog/google-business-profile-categories-guide) explains how to choose a primary category.

## Should You Trademark the Salon Name?

Consider it if you plan more locations, a product line or a franchise. According to a Fish and Richardson summary (secondary source), the USPTO base application fee is $350 per class from January 18, 2025, with extra fees of $100 for missing information and $200 for free-form descriptions of goods or services. The USPTO page itself returned a 404, so check the current fee before filing.

A trademark does not guarantee you the name. Search existing marks first and talk to a trademark attorney for anything beyond a single local salon. For local visibility, a registered business name also needs to match your Google listing.

## How Do You Keep the Name and Details Consistent?

Google's guidelines say the business name should reflect your real-world name, and "including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted", so no keywords, hours or services in the name field. The address must be a real physical location and the phone number should connect to your location.

Use the same name, address and phone on your website, Instagram, booking page and directories. ## How Do You Show the Brand on Instagram?

Pick one colour palette, two fonts and a photo style, and write them down. Instagram's creator FAQ asks for original content and advises removing third-party watermarks that may hurt reach. Post your own work consistently and name the service and the stylist.

If you pay creators or give free services for posts, the FTC says the connection should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. ## What Can a Clear Brand Do for Pricing? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions**, not published benchmarks.

| Input | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specialty positioning | General | Colour specialist |
| Clients per month | 80 | 70 |
| Average ticket | $60 | $85 |
| Monthly revenue | $4,800 | $5,950 |
| Stylist hours (assumption) | 100 | 90 |
| Revenue per hour | $48 | $66 |

In this example, fewer clients at a higher ticket bring in more revenue in fewer hours. The shift only happens if clients actually want the specialty and the work backs it up. Test it on one service first, with a small price change, and track bookings in your software.

## FAQ

### What is the first step in branding a salon?

Choose a specialty you can state in one line, then make the name, look and messages consistent everywhere.

### Do I need a logo to have a brand?

A logo helps, but consistency matters more: same name, colours, fonts and photo style across every channel.

### How much does a US trademark cost?

A law-firm summary reports a $350 base fee per class from January 18, 2025, plus possible surcharges. Confirm the current figure with the USPTO.

### Can I put keywords in my Google business name?

No. Google says including unnecessary information in the name is not permitted. Use your real-world name.

### Do I have to disclose free services given to influencers?

Yes. The FTC says a material connection such as payment or a free service should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously.
