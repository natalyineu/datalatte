Google Display is itself a form of programmatic buying. Programmatic means ads bought by software through automated auctions, and Google's Display Network sells its inventory that way. Google says Ad Manager is a real-time marketplace partnered with the Display Network. The practical difference is the tool you use: Google Ads reaches Google's network, while a demand-side platform (DSP) such as StackAdapt or Display & Video 360 buys across many exchanges.

This guide is for local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Every figure comes from a page we opened on 29 October 2026. Where a vendor does not publish a number, we say so.

## Is Google Display Network the Same as Programmatic?

Mostly yes in mechanics, no in access. StackAdapt defines programmatic advertising as using technology to buy, sell and optimize ad placements, and real-time bidding (RTB) as bidding on impressions through auctions. IAB Tech Lab describes RTB as putting an individual impression up for bid in real time. According to Google's help page, Google Ads first runs an internal auction for a publisher's inventory, passes the winning bid to Ad Manager, and "Ad Manager will then take all eligible bids and run an auction."

That is automated, impression-level auctioning. Google does not label Display with the word programmatic on the pages we read, so treat that label as our reading of the mechanism. See our [plain-English guide to programmatic advertising](/blog/what-is-programmatic-advertising-plain-english-guide-for-business-owners) for the basics.

## Google Display Network vs Programmatic: How Do They Compare?

The table separates the two routes a small business can take. "Programmatic" here means a self-serve DSP. Vendor terms change, so confirm with the vendor before you commit.

| | Google Display (Google Ads) | Programmatic via a DSP |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory | Display Network: 35 million sites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail (Google) | Many exchanges and publishers, depending on the DSP; Display & Video 360 lists a Marketplace of broadcaster and publisher deals |

## Programmatic vs Display Advertising: Is There a Difference?

Yes, but it is a difference of category. Display advertising is a format: banners and similar ads on websites and apps. Programmatic is a buying method that can deliver display, video, audio, connected TV and digital out-of-home. So you can buy display programmatically, and Google Display is one example.

Google's responsive display ads, for example, "adjust their size, appearance, and format to fit just about any available ad space." A DSP can buy the same kind of banner space on other exchanges. For the mechanics, see [how programmatic buying works for local business](/blog/how-programmatic-buying-works-local-business).

## GDN vs Programmatic: What Does Each Cost?

Neither side gives a flat price list. Google bills Display by clicks or by viewable impressions: with vCPM bidding "You don't pay for any impressions that were not viewable." Google's budget page states your average daily budget and a cap of about double that on a given day, but no minimum.

On the DSP side, Google's Display & Video 360 help describes a fee calculated as a percentage of media spend and shows an example with a 20% markup, which is an example and not a rate card. StackAdapt says it has no minimum spend and lists CPM, CPC and CPE pricing. The Trade Desk publishes no minimum or fee we could open. Third-party sites quote $100,000-plus minimums and fee ranges, which we do not use. For measured prices, read [real programmatic CPM benchmarks](/blog/how-much-does-programmatic-advertising-cost-real-cpm-benchmarks-2026).

## Programmatic Advertising vs Google Ads: Which Suits a Local Business?

Google Display suits you if you want to launch fast from an account you already have, retarget site visitors, or test an offer cheaply. Google's Display targeting includes people who visited your site, plus keywords and placements, and the campaign needs 5 to 7 days of learning after a significant change.

A DSP suits you if you want reach beyond Google's network or channels such as connected TV, and you have a budget big enough to learn from. Display & Video 360 is sold through Google's sales team ("Talk to our sales team to get started"), so most small shops start elsewhere. Read our [StackAdapt review](/blog/stackadapt-review-local-business-programmatic) and [DV360 for small business](/blog/dv360-for-small-business-is-it-worth-it).

## What Does a Worked Example Look Like?

Take a salon with $600 a month to spend on awareness. All inputs below are assumptions for arithmetic, not benchmarks.

- **Assumption:** total monthly budget $600.
- **Assumption:** a DSP charges a 15% platform fee on top of media, so media is $600 / 1.15 = $521.74.
- **Assumption:** both routes win impressions at a $4 CPM.

At $4 CPM, the DSP buys $521.74 / $4 x 1,000 = about 130,400 impressions. If Google's revenue share is already inside the $4 CPM, the same $600 buys $600 / $4 x 1,000 = 150,000 impressions. The gap comes only from the assumed fee. In practice CPMs differ by inventory and targeting, so measure your own. Pair any test with a [local marketing budget plan](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).

## FAQ

### Is Google Display programmatic?

Mechanically yes: the Display Network sells inventory through automated auctions run by Google Ads and Ad Manager. Google's pages do not use the word programmatic for it, so that is an interpretation.

### What is the difference between a DSP and an SSP?

A DSP is software that lets advertisers buy ad inventory through automation. An SSP is the publisher-side tool that sells it. RTB is the auction between them, as StackAdapt and IAB Tech Lab describe.

### Does programmatic have a minimum spend?

It depends on the vendor. StackAdapt states no minimum. The Trade Desk and Display & Video 360 do not publish one on pages we could open, so ask their sales teams.

### Is programmatic cheaper than Google Display?

Not by default. The only fee evidence is a percentage-of-media fee at DV360, with no published rate. Compare your own cost per result.

### Can I run Display campaigns locally?

Yes, Google Display supports audience and placement targeting. Check location settings in your own account before launch, because we did not verify location options on the pages we read.
