To measure your local business website's ROI, subtract what it costs you (build, hosting, tools, ads, your time) from the profit it brings in, then divide by that cost. The maths is easy. The hard part is knowing which calls, bookings and sales really came from the website, and that takes a few settings in Google Analytics and Google Business Profile.

This guide shows the formula, what to track, and a worked example. No benchmark for typical website ROI is quoted, because none we could verify exists for local businesses.

## What is the website ROI formula?

ROI = (profit attributed to the website - website cost) / website cost. Use profit, not revenue. If a booking brings $90 and costs you $40 to deliver, the profit is $50.

Website cost includes the build or monthly plan, domain and hosting, maintenance, paid ads that send traffic to it and your own time at a fair hourly rate. See our [website maintenance costs](/blog/website-maintenance-cost-small-business) and [hosting guide](/blog/website-hosting-for-small-business) for the usual line items.

## Which actions should you count?

Count the actions that lead to money, not visits. For most local businesses that is calls, booking-form submissions, direction requests and orders.

Google Analytics defines a key event as "an event that measures an action that's particularly important to the success of your business". Create an event for each action, then mark it as a key event. Google Business Profile reports calls, direction requests, website clicks, bookings and messages. Bookings only appear when handled through a booking provider.

## How do you know where visitors came from?

Tag the links you control. Google says to always use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, and warns that missing values show up as "(not set)" in reports. Tag links in emails, social bios, ads and Google Business Profile website links.

Link your Business Profile to Google Analytics if you want those metrics in the same reports. Google notes the data goes back only six months, so export it regularly.

## What about calls and walk-ins?

Calls from the website are easy to count if you track taps on the phone number as an event. Walk-ins are harder. Ask "How did you hear about us?" at checkout or booking and record the answer in a simple sheet. It will not be perfect, but a consistent question beats guessing.

Google Business Profile counts calls as taps on the call button, which is not the same as completed conversations. Treat it as demand, not confirmed customers.

## Worked example: a salon website

All inputs are **assumptions** for illustration.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly website cost (plan, hosting, maintenance) | $60 |
| Your time on updates, 2 hours at $25 | $50 |
| Total monthly cost | $110 |
| New bookings a month from website forms and taps | 14 |
| Profit per booking after costs | $38 |

Profit from the website: 14 x $38 = $532. ROI = ($532 - $110) / $110 = 3.84, or 384%. If only 5 bookings can be traced, profit is $190 and ROI is 73%. The result depends on honest attribution, so label which bookings are confirmed and which are estimates.

## What mistakes distort website ROI?

- Counting all revenue instead of profit.
- Crediting the website with customers who would have booked anyway, such as repeat clients on a standing appointment.
- Ignoring cost of time.
- Judging after one month for a service with a long decision cycle.
- Mixing paid-ad results into website results without separating the ad cost.

## FAQ

### What is a good ROI for a small business website?

No verified benchmark exists in the sources reviewed here, so compare the website with your other channels and your own break-even point instead.

### Should I count Google Business Profile calls?

Count them as demand from your online presence, not as website results. Keep them separate from tracked website events.

### Do I need Google Analytics?

It is the most common free option, but any tool that records events and sources works. The method matters more than the tool.

### How long until a website pays back?

It depends on your costs and booking value. Divide the total upfront cost by monthly profit from the site to get months to payback.

### How can I improve website ROI?

Raise conversions with a clearer call to action, faster pages and better proof. See our [conversion rate guide](/blog/website-conversion-rate-optimization).
