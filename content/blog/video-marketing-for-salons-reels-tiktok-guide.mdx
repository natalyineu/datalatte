Start with Instagram Reels if your clients are 30 or older, add TikTok if you want to reach under-30s, and treat YouTube Shorts as free reposting. Film one hour a week: a before-and-after, a stylist tip, a booking-slot reminder. Do not pay to promote a video until it has already produced bookings on its own. Pew's 2025 survey found 62% of US adults aged 30 to 49 use Instagram versus 44% for TikTok, so the default choice is clear for most local salons.

## Which platform should a salon start with?

Use your clients' age as the tiebreaker. Pew Research Center's [2025 social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) (5,022 US adults) reports:

| Platform | All adults | Ages 18-29 | Ages 30-49 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 50% | 80% | 62% |
| TikTok | 32% | 63% | 44% |

Instagram leads TikTok in both age groups (YouTube is used by 84% of all adults). TikTok is a strong second for under-30s, so a salon that sells trendy colour work or prom styling to students should post there too. These are usage rates across all adults, not salon-specific reach, so check your own follower ages in each app's analytics.

Do one platform well for 8 weeks before adding another. For a deeper TikTok plan, read our [TikTok marketing for hair salons](/blog/tiktok-marketing-for-hair-salons) guide.

## What video formats work for a local salon?

Pick formats that answer a booking question, not formats that chase trends. Four repeatable ones:

1. **Before and after:** one transformation per post, with the service name and starting price in the caption.
2. **Stylist tip:** 20 to 30 seconds on home care for the colour or cut you just did.
3. **Open-slot reminder:** a specific day, time and service, with the booking link in your bio.
4. **Process clip:** hands, tools and mixing, so people who dislike being on camera can still post.

Keep videos short and focused on one service. YouTube's [Shorts help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070) says Shorts can be up to 3 minutes long.

For ideas on caption structure and before-and-after content, see [hair salon TikTok before and after content](/blog/hair-salon-tiktok-strategy-before-after-content).

## How do I fit video into one hour a week?

Assumption for this worked example: a solo stylist with 5 working days and a phone.

- **15 minutes:** film 3 clips during services (with the client's permission).
- **30 minutes:** trim, caption and add the service and price.
- **15 minutes:** schedule posts and reply to comments.

That is 3 posts a week, or 12 in a month. Post the same vertical clip to Reels, TikTok and Shorts if you want wider reach, since all three accept vertical video.

Get the client's consent before posting their face or hair.

## How do I know if video is booking appointments?

Track three numbers weekly: link clicks from your bio, new bookings that mention video, and profile visits. Add a "How did you hear about us?" question at booking.

Decision rule: after 12 posts, if you have at least one booking that you can trace to video, keep going. If you have none, change the call to action before changing platform: name a day, time and price, not "book now". Do not read video views as success, because views do not pay rent.

Worked example (assumption): 12 posts produce 3 bookings at $90 each. That is $270 of revenue for about 4 hours of work, or roughly $67 an hour before costs. Run the same math with your own average ticket.

## Should I pay to promote my salon videos?

Only after a video has proven itself organically. TikTok's [help centre](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/budget-and-bidding-faq) lists a $50 minimum at the campaign level and $20 at the ad group level, and its [Spark Ads page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/spark-ads) says you can run ads from your own organic posts.

Use search-ad benchmarks as your hurdle. LocaliQ's [2026 benchmarks](https://www.localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/) put average cost per lead for beauty and personal care at $39.25, with a 10.35% conversion rate. A boosted video that cannot beat roughly that cost per lead is not earning its budget. Compare it with [Google Ads for hair salons](/services/google-ads), where people are already searching.

Worked example (assumption): you spend $60 on one proven video and receive 2 booking requests. That is $30 per lead, which beats the $39.25 benchmark. If you receive 1, it is $60 and you should stop.

## FAQ

### Do I need to appear on camera?

No. Film hands, tools, the mirror and the finished result. Many salon videos never show a face.

### How often should a salon post video?

Three posts a week is a workable target for one hour of effort. Consistency matters more than volume, so choose a number you can sustain for 12 weeks.

### Is Instagram or TikTok better for booking?

For clients aged 30 to 49, Pew data shows higher Instagram use (62%) than TikTok (44%). For under-30s, both are used heavily, so test both on a small scale.

### What equipment do I need?

A recent phone is enough. Add a clip-on light or mic only if your clips are dark or the audio is unclear.

### How long until I see bookings?

There is no reliable benchmark. Judge after about 12 posts using traced bookings, not views.
