Organic TikTok is worth a coffee shop's time only when a small share of local viewers walk through the door. On the assumptions below, a coffee shop needs about 11 new customers a month from TikTok to cover 8 hours of filming and editing valued at $25 an hour. Pew Research Center's 2026 survey finds 37% of US adults ever use TikTok, so reach is real, but reach alone does not pay the bill. This guide covers what to film, how to point each video at the door, how to count visits, and how to run the break-even check with local numbers. All dollar figures are illustrative assumptions.

## Is Your Customer Actually on TikTok?

Check this before filming anything. These are US figures, and "ever use" is not "uses daily," so they show reach rather than attention. They also say nothing about a particular street. Look at who is in the queue at 8am, and compare that with till data. If regulars are mostly students and adults under 35, the audience is likely there. If most are retirees, a TikTok-first plan will struggle.

## Why Do Views Not Turn Into Customers?

A video can reach 100,000 people and bring none of them in, because most viewers live in another city. The useful equation is local viewers multiplied by a clear reason to visit, multiplied by an easy way to find the shop. Every video should do one of three jobs: earn reach with a satisfying process shot, build trust with faces and routines, or signal location with content that only makes sense nearby.

Mixing the three works best. Reach videos fill the top of the funnel. Location videos do the converting, so they should hold a fixed share of each week's output rather than appear as an occasional extra.

## Which Videos Should a Coffee Shop Film?

Process videos are the easiest start: milk steaming, an espresso pour, latte art, pastries coming out of the oven. Keep the real sound, keep the clip short, and film near a window in natural light. Behind-the-scenes clips, such as opening at 5:30am or the end-of-shift clean-up, make a small shop feel like people rather than a logo.

Local hooks bring neighbours in. Examples include "the best seat on the street to get work done," a short walk along the block at 7am, or "three things to order on a first visit." These often get fewer views than process clips, but the people watching are far more likely to live nearby. ## How Should the Account Be Set Up?

Fill the bio with the shop name, the neighbourhood and one reason to visit, such as "Specialty coffee in [neighbourhood], open 7 to 3." Put the street name in captions of location videos, not only the city. If the app offers a location tag on posts, use it. If it does not, say the neighbourhood aloud in the first few seconds and show it on screen.

Music needs care. Check the current commercial-use rules inside the app before posting a trending sound from a business account, because a track that works for a personal creator may not be licensed for a shop. ## How Do You Count Visits Instead of Views?

Views measure attention, not footfall, so build a simple count from the start. Train staff to ask every new face "How did you find us?" and keep a tally by the till or in a shared note. A video-only offer, such as a free milk upgrade for anyone who mentions the video, also gives a count. Only run offers the shop can honour.

Repeat visits matter as much as first visits. Once the team knows who came from TikTok, a short message sequence can bring them back, provided each person has agreed to receive messages. ## What Is the Break-Even Math?

| Spend per visit (assumption) | $9 | $9 | $9 |
| Visits in first 90 days (assumption) | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Revenue per new customer in 90 days | $27 | $27 | $27 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 65% | 65% | 65% |
| Gross profit | $44 | $527 | $3,159 |
| Time cost (8 hours at $25, assumption) | $200 | $200 | $200 |
| Net | -$156 | +$327 | +$2,959 |

Break-even equals the time cost divided by gross profit per new customer: $200 divided by ($27 x 0.65 = $17.55), which is about 11.4 new customers a month. The Strong column is a rare outcome, so planning should not rely on it. The Tough column is common, since many accounts post for months before a video travels locally.

Repeat visits are the multiplier. If each new customer makes six visits in 90 days rather than three, the shop needs about half as many new customers to break even. Local share also matters more than raw views: a video with half the views but twice the local share delivers roughly the same result.

## What Should You Do This Week?

1. Compare regulars' ages with the Pew figures above, using the actual queue as the check.
2. Confirm the account type and music rules in the app before posting trending sounds.
3. Rewrite the bio with the shop name, neighbourhood, hours and one reason to visit.
4. Film one batch of six clips: two process, two behind the scenes, two local hooks. Post three this week.
5. Start the "How did you find us?" tally and review it on the same day each week.

Paid testing comes later. ## FAQ

### How often should a coffee shop post on TikTok?

No platform publishes a required frequency in these sources. Three or four posts a week is a workable starting point for a small team, and consistency over 8 to 12 weeks matters more than short bursts.

### Does 37% of US adults mean the channel is worth it?

It shows reach, not return. Use it to check whether the customer base overlaps with TikTok users, then judge the channel on the visit tally and the break-even math.

### Why do videos get views but no customers?

Usually the viewers live elsewhere, or the video gives no reason and no clear way to visit. Add the neighbourhood, a specific offer and an easy address or link, then measure with the tally.

### Should a coffee shop run TikTok ads?

Only after organic content shows local visits, and only if the budget allows a fair test. The [paid TikTok guide](/blog/tiktok-ads-for-coffee-shops-step-by-step-guide) covers that step.
