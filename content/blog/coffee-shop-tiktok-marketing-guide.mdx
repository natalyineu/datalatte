## Is Your Customer on TikTok?

Check before you film anything. Pew Research Center's [2025 social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) (5,022 US adults surveyed Feb. 5 to June 18, 2025) reports the share who ever use TikTok:

That is US data and "ever use" is not "uses daily," so it shows reach, not attention. Still, if your regulars are students and under-35s, the audience is there; if they are mostly over 65, it mostly is not. Look at who is in your queue at 8am and be honest.

There is also a discovery angle. In July 2022, Google's Prabhakar Raghavan said at Fortune's Brainstorm Tech conference that "almost 40% of young people, when they're looking for a place for lunch" go to TikTok or Instagram instead of Google Maps or Search ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2022/07/14/tiktok-instagram-google-search-traffic/)). Google told TechCrunch the figure came from internal research on US users aged 18 to 24, and it was never published. It is old, narrow and about lunch, not coffee. Read it as a signal that some young people discover places in video feeds, not as a measure of your market.

## Why Views Are Not the Goal

A video can reach 100,000 people and bring none of them through your door, because most of those viewers live in another city. The equation that matters is local viewers x a clear reason to visit x an easy way to find you. So build every video around one of three jobs:

1. **Reach:** a satisfying process shot that earns views.
2. **Trust:** faces and routines that make strangers feel they know you.
3. **Location:** content that only makes sense if you live nearby.

Mix them. Reach videos fill the funnel, location videos convert it.

## Content That Works for Cafés

### Process videos

Milk steaming, an espresso pour, latte art, pastries coming out of the oven. Keep it close, keep the real sound, and keep it short. There is no magic length; aim to cut everything that is not the interesting part. Film in natural light near a window or use a cheap ring light.

### Behind the scenes

Opening at 5:30am, the first customer, the team making each other coffee, the end-of-shift clean-up. These make a small café feel like people, not a logo.

### Local hooks

These are the videos that bring neighbours:

- "The best seat in [neighbourhood] to get work done"
- "Why we use [local supplier] milk (or bread, or beans)"
- A short tour of your street at 7am
- "Three things to order if it is your first time here"
- A regular's usual order, with their permission

They often get fewer views than process videos, but the people watching are more likely to be nearby.

### Formats to borrow, not copy

POV skits of a regular's morning, "before and after the rush" and "rate this order" all suit a counter. Trends change quickly, so check what is on your For You page this week rather than relying on a list from a blog. ## Set Up the Account Properly

**Use a business account, and use cleared music.** TikTok's ads blog on the [Commercial Music Library](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/audio-library-royalty-free-music) says the songs in its general library are not cleared for any brand or business, and that using them through a personal account to promote a business risks copyright infringement. A Business Account is needed to see the in-app Commercial Music Library, which is free to use. TikTok's [help page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library) says it holds about 1 million songs and covers organic content as well as ads. A trending sound that is not in that library may not be safe for your café's account.

**Fill in the profile.** The bio should say the café name, the neighbourhood and one reason to visit, for example "Specialty coffee in [neighbourhood]. Open 7 to 3." Add your website or a Google Maps link if your account allows a link (availability depends on the account and region, so check the profile editor in your app). Put the street, not just the city, in the caption of local videos.

**Check for the location tag.** TikTok's posting screen may offer an "Add location" option depending on your account and region. The exact rules could not be confirmed from TikTok's own documentation, so check your app. If it is available, tag your café. If not, say the neighbourhood out loud in the first seconds and put it on screen.

## Make Each Video Point at Your Door

Before posting, run this checklist:

- Does the first three seconds show something specific, not "hey guys"?
- Does the video say where you are, out loud or as on-screen text?
- Is there one reason to come today: a new drink, a time-limited item, a free add-on for people who mention the video?
- Does the caption give the neighbourhood and opening hours?
- Is the link in your bio working?

If the answer to the last four is no, you are making entertainment, not marketing. Entertainment is fine, but know which one you are doing.

## Count Visits, Not Views

TikTok's own numbers show views, not footfall. Use three cheap checks:

1. **Ask.** Train the team to ask new faces "How did you find us?" and tally TikTok on a sheet by the till or in a notes app.
2. **Use a video-only offer.** "Say 'TikTok latte' for a free oat milk upgrade" gives a count without any software. Only run an offer you can honour.
3. **Watch your Google Business Profile.** Google's [performance help page](https://support.google.com/plus/answer/3154834?hl=en) lists the metrics a profile can show: views, searches, directions, calls and website clicks. After a strong video, look for a rise in directions and searches. This shows correlation, not proof, so combine it with the tally. ## The Math

Take a monthly time cost of about 8 hours: 4 batch-filming sessions of one hour plus 16 short edits at about 15 minutes each. Every row marked assumption is mine, not a benchmark, and local-view shares are the hardest to know until you try.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly views (assumption) | 5,000 | 20,000 | 60,000 |
| Share from your area (assumption) | 10% | 15% | 20% |
| Local viewers | 500 | 3,000 | 12,000 |
| Local viewers who visit in 30 days (assumption) | 0.5% | 1% | 1.5% |
| New customers | 2.5 | 30 | 180 |
| Spend per visit (assumption) | $9 | $9 | $9 |
| Visits in first 90 days (assumption) | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Revenue per new customer in 90 days | $27 | $27 | $27 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 65% | 65% | 65% |
| Gross profit | $44 | $527 | $3,159 |
| Time cost (8 hours at $25, assumption) | $200 | $200 | $200 |
| Net | -$156 | +$327 | +$2,959 |

Break-even is the time cost divided by gross profit per new customer: $200 / ($27 x 0.65 = $17.55) = about 11.4 new customers a month. The Strong column is a rare, viral outcome, so do not plan around it. The Tough column is also common, since many accounts post for months before one video travels.

Two things follow:

- **Repeat visits are the multiplier.** A café sells habit. Raising visits per new customer from 3 to 6 in 90 days halves the customers you need. That is a loyalty and consistency question, not a TikTok question.
- **Local share matters more than views.** A video with half the views but twice the local share is worth the same. That is why the location hook beats chasing a trend.

## A Sustainable Routine

Pick a rhythm you can keep. As a starting point, my suggestion rather than a platform rule: film once a week for an hour, shoot 8 to 12 clips, post three or four times a week, and look at the numbers on Sunday. Retire formats that get views but no visits, and repeat the ones that bring people in. Cross-post your best clips to Instagram Reels to save time.

If a video performs well and you want to push it to nearby people, read our [paid TikTok guide](/blog/tiktok-ads-for-coffee-shops-step-by-step-guide) first, since TikTok sets minimum budgets for ads. ## Do This Today

1. Open your till records or ask the team: what age are your regulars? Compare with the Pew figures above.
2. Switch to a business account, and check the Commercial Music Library works in your app.
3. Write your bio with the café name, neighbourhood, hours and one reason to visit.
4. Film one batch of six clips: two process, two behind the scenes, two local hooks. Post three this week.
5. Put a "How did you find us?" tally by the till and add a weekly 20-minute slot to read your Google Business Profile directions and searches.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should a coffee shop post on TikTok?**
A: TikTok does not publish a minimum. Three or four posts a week is a sustainable starting point (my suggestion); consistency over months matters more than daily bursts.

**Q: Do I need a business account?**
A: If you want to use music legally for a business, yes. TikTok says the general music library is not cleared for brands and that the Commercial Music Library requires a Business Account.

**Q: Why do my videos get views but no customers?**
A: Usually the viewers are not local, or the video gives no reason or way to visit. Add the neighbourhood, a specific offer and a visible link or address, then measure with the tally.

**Q: Should I run TikTok ads?**
A: Only after organic content works, and only if you can afford the test. TikTok sets minimum ad budgets; see the [paid guide](/blog/tiktok-ads-for-coffee-shops-step-by-step-guide).

**Q: TikTok or Instagram first?**
A: Check where your customers are using the Pew figures and your own tally. You can film once and post to both, so you rarely have to choose.
