## What free data does South Carolina offer small businesses?

Through the state's partnership with SizeUp, the SC Department of Commerce offers small businesses and entrepreneurs across South Carolina data to benchmark against competitors, according to [SizeUp SC](https://sc.sizeup.com/). Communities can add SizeUp to their small business ecosystem at a reduced rate, and the page states that local businesses will have free access to its program features.

The local version is customized to a community's geography. It adds hourly wage, open hours, digital footprint and in-depth demographics to the existing statewide datasets. The page says the community tool is available 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, on the Chamber, EDO or City website, and that it covers any South Carolina geography, including cities, counties, regions and districts.

For a small firm, the useful questions are concrete. When do competitors in your category list their hours? What does their digital footprint look like? How does the demographic mix of your trade area compare with the place you actually sell? Write the answers down before you commit any budget.

## Which local listings should you check first?

Listings are often where buyers first verify a business. The South Carolina Commission for Community Advancement and Engagement runs a [small business directory](https://advance.sc.gov/small-businesses/small-business-directory?combine=&field_business_city_value=&field_service_area_value=&field_services_value=&page=40&order=field_class&sort=asc), and its entries show address, city, ZIP and year established. For example, Endra's Trucking, LLC is listed in Santee with a year established of 2018.

Consistent details make a business easier to verify. Check three things in order:

1. Confirm that your business name, address and phone number match across the state directory, your Chamber listing and your Google Business Profile.
2. Add your year established wherever a directory asks for it, since it is a simple trust signal.
3. Review your hours and note any coastal or seasonal changes you already know about, so the listing reflects reality.

Keep a single spreadsheet of every listing URL and the date you last checked it.

## How can community dates shape your calendar?

Local dates give small businesses a reason to publish timely posts. National Prescription Drug Take Back Day is set for Oct. 24 across South Carolina, according to a related [ABC News 4 headline](https://abcnews4.com/news/local/story/january-gym-surge-brings-new-faces-and-a-push-for-consistency). A pharmacy, clinic or wellness studio can plan a notice or service reminder around that date.

A salon or cafe may not have an obvious tie to a health observance, so use dates that fit your trade. Plan posts for a date several weeks ahead rather than the week of. Keep the calendar local: a Charleston restaurant, a Greenville salon and a coastal rental agency will each have different events worth marking.

## What does a $600 monthly ad test look like?

Every input in this worked example is an **assumption**, not a sourced benchmark. Replace each one with your own account data after your first test period.

| Line | Value (assumption) |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $600 |
| Cost per click | $3.00 |
| Clicks | 200 |
| Click to lead rate | 8% |
| Leads | 16 |
| Leads who become customers | 40% |
| New customers | 6.4 |
| Cost per new customer | $94 |
| First-year value per customer | $120 |
| First-year revenue | $768 |

On these assumptions, $768 of first-year revenue against $600 of spend looks positive. That comparison ignores cost of goods and staff time, so subtract your margin before you decide.

Now change one input. If cost per click doubles to $6.00 (assumption), the same $600 buys 100 clicks, about 8 leads, about 3.2 customers and a cost per customer near $188. First-year revenue falls to $384, which is below spend. The lesson is that click price decides whether the test works, so measure it in your own account rather than borrowing someone else's figure. ## Where should a South Carolina business start?

A practical order is to fix listings, use the benchmarking data to see how competitors present themselves, and then run a capped paid test. Owned assets like your profile and directory entries keep working between campaigns. Paid clicks stop the moment the budget stops.

Limit the first test to the services where your buyers already search, and track which calls, bookings or form fills came from it. ## FAQ

### How much should a South Carolina business spend on Google Ads?

This guide does not give a South Carolina click price, so test with your own numbers. The worked example uses a $600 monthly budget and labelled assumptions. Start with an amount you can afford to lose while you learn what your market costs.

### Is the SizeUp SC data really free?

The state partnership page says local businesses will have free access to SizeUp SC's program features. Check the live page for current terms, because access rules can change.

### Which listings should I update first?

Start with your Google Business Profile and any state or Chamber directory where your business is already listed. Match the name, address and phone number across them, and keep hours current.

### Should I plan marketing around community dates?

Yes, where the date fits your business. National Prescription Drug Take Back Day on Oct. 24 suits a pharmacy or health provider better than a restaurant. Choose dates that connect naturally to what you sell.
