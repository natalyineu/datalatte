Group keywords by what the searcher wants to buy. Each theme gets its own ad group and its own landing page.

## Which Match Types Should You Use?

Google's help describes three. Broad match, the default, shows ads on searches "related to your keyword." Phrase match covers searches that carry your keyword's meaning and sits between broad and exact. Exact match is the most controlled and reaches the fewest searches.

For a small firm, start with phrase and exact. Google says Smart Bidding works best with broad match because the wide set of searches lets it learn, so test broad only with conversion tracking and a firm negative list. Do not assume broad will pay off with a small budget.

## What Negative Keywords Do Accountants Need?

Negative keywords exclude searches you never want. Google notes they do not match close variants, so "course" will not block "courses" unless you add both. Phrase and exact negatives block word order more tightly.

Start with these themes, then add from your Search terms report weekly:

- Free and DIY intent: "free," "template," "calculator," "software"
- Training and jobs: "course," "jobs," "salary," "certification," "exam"
- Services you do not offer, such as "forensic" or "payroll software"
- Places you do not serve

## When Should an Accountant Advertise?

Tax work is seasonal. The IRS says calendar-year individual filers had until April 15, 2026, and that an automatic six-month extension requires Form 4868 by the original due date. An extension gives more time to file, not to pay.

That deadline shape means searches rise ahead of it, though no source here measures by how much. Plan ads for tax preparation before the deadline and keep bookkeeping and small business accounting running through the year. Check the IRS for next season's date when it is announced.
