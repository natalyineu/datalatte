Daycare centers fill enrollment with Google Ads by bidding on local parent searches such as "daycare near me" and "infant care in [city]", sending clicks to a page built to book tours, and tracking calls and tour requests. The key is a tight service area, specific keywords by age group, and a landing page that answers parents' safety and cost questions fast. Start with a modest budget you can sustain for a couple of months, then scale what produces enrolled children.

## Why Google Ads Fits Daycare Marketing

Parents choosing childcare usually search Google, read reviews, and call or book a tour with a short list of providers. Social posts and flyers rarely reach parents at that moment, while search ads do. You also control the geography, which matters because families rarely travel far for daily drop-off.

Reviews and your Google Business Profile matter alongside ads, since parents often check both. See our guide to [local SEO for daycare centers](/blog/local-seo-for-daycare-centers) and our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

<Callout type="warning">Childcare is a sensitive category. Check Google's current advertising policies, especially around targeting and children, and your local licensing rules before claiming anything about ratios, certifications or safety in your ads.</Callout>

## Step 1: Set Up Your Google Ads Account

Create an account with a single clear goal: tour requests and phone calls. Choose your service area carefully, and set conversion actions (calls, form submissions, tour bookings) before you spend anything.

## Step 2: Choose the Right Keywords

Parents search by need, age and location. Start with specific phrases rather than broad ones:

* "daycare near me"
* "infant care in [city]"
* "toddler daycare [neighborhood]"
* "after school care [city]"
* "Montessori daycare [city]" (only if you offer it)

Use Google's Keyword Planner to check search volume and estimated costs for your area. Group keywords by program (infant, toddler, preschool, after-school) so each ad and landing page matches the search.

## Step 3: Create Ad Groups and Ads

Each ad needs a clear headline, a benefit, and a call to action. Be specific and truthful.

**Example**
```
Headline 1: Infant & Toddler Care in [City]
Headline 2: Licensed Daycare | Tours Available
Headline 3: Schedule Your Tour Today
Description: Warm, play-based care for ages [X] to [Y]. See our
rooms and meet our team. Call or request a tour online.
```

Only mention "licensed", ratios or credentials if they are accurate and verifiable. Avoid inventing urgency such as "only 3 spots left" unless it is true.

## Step 4: Set Bids and Budgets

Costs per click vary by city and keyword, so check Keyword Planner rather than relying on a generic figure. Start with a daily budget that produces a few clicks a day, use manual or maximize-clicks bidding while you gather data, then move to a conversion-focused strategy once you have tracked leads.

A useful way to think about budget is to start from your enrollment goal. If one enrolled child is worth many months of tuition, you can afford a meaningful cost per enrollment. Work backwards: how many tour requests do you need, how many tours become enrollments, and what can you pay per tour request?

An empty spot costs you every day. Even so, avoid setting budgets you cannot sustain, since the first weeks are for learning.

## Step 5: Build a Landing Page That Books Tours

Sending clicks to your homepage is a common mistake. Build a dedicated page per program that includes:

- A headline that matches the ad ("Infant Care in [City]")
- Photos of your actual rooms and outdoor space
- Hours, ages served, and clear "from" pricing or a way to request it
- Licensing information, if applicable
- A short form and a visible phone number, optimized for mobile
- A "Schedule a Tour" button above the fold

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Targeting too broadly

If your location radius is too large you will pay for clicks from parents who would never make the commute. Tighten the radius around your center, check Google's location reports to see where clicks come from, and exclude areas you do not serve.

### Mistake 2: Relying on broad match keywords

Broad keywords can trigger irrelevant searches. Start with phrase and exact match, and add negatives such as "jobs", "free", "diploma", "training", "certification", "babysitter jobs", "dog" and "puppy". Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) covers more.

### Mistake 3: Skipping ad extensions

Use call, location and sitelink extensions (tour booking, tuition, programs, contact). They make the ad bigger and give parents more ways to act.

### Mistake 4: Sending all traffic to the homepage

Match the page to the ad. A parent searching for infant care should land on infant care information with a tour button, not a general mission statement.

### Mistake 5: Setting it and forgetting it

Schedule 30 minutes a week to review the search terms report, click-through rate, cost per lead and any change in competitor activity (Auction Insights). Refresh ad copy when performance slips.

## Writing Ad Copy That Parents Trust

Parents' biggest concerns are safety, care quality and cost. Good copy addresses one concern clearly:

* **Problem and solution:** "Worried About Daycare Quality? Tour Our Center" followed by facts you can back up.
* **Differentiator:** program type (play-based, Montessori), hours, or location, if true.
* **Clear action:** "Schedule a Tour", "Call Today".

Run several ads per ad group and replace the weakest one regularly. Test headlines that emphasize your neighborhood and personal touch against more generic versions; local, specific wording often feels more trustworthy.

## Measuring What Matters

Clicks are not enrollments. Track:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Cost per lead (calls, forms, tour requests) | Shows how efficiently ads create enquiries |
| Cost per enrollment | The number that decides whether ads are profitable |
| Landing page conversion rate | Tells you whether the page or the ad needs work |
| Quality Score | Higher scores generally lower cost per click |

Set up call tracking, form-submission tracking and tour-booking tracking. If you can, record which enquiries became enrolled children and, where possible, import that back into Google Ads so it learns which keywords bring real enrollments. ## FAQ

**Q: How much should a daycare spend on Google Ads?**
A: It depends on your market and enrollment goal. Check estimated click costs in Keyword Planner, set a budget that gives you enough clicks to learn, and hold it steady for at least two months. Larger cities generally cost more per click than small towns.

**Q: How long does it take for Google Ads to work for a daycare?**
A: Clicks can start within a day or two of launching, but tour requests and enrollments usually take a few weeks. Avoid drastic changes in the first couple of weeks, when the system is still learning.

**Q: Can I run Google Ads myself, or should I hire a professional?**
A: You can if you can spend time each week on search terms, ad copy and tracking. If your time is better spent running the center, hire someone with experience in local service businesses. See our [Google Ads management service](/services/google-ads).

**Q: How do I compete with large daycare chains?**
A: Compete on local relevance rather than budget. Use neighborhood and program-specific keywords, show real photos, and highlight what is true and distinctive about your center. Chains often bid on broad terms, leaving specific searches for smaller providers.

**Q: What if my ads get clicks but no enrollments?**
A: Check the landing page first: speed, mobile layout, phone number and tour button. Then check whether the ad promises something the page does not deliver, and whether pricing or waitlists are clear. Ask families who toured but did not enroll what held them back.
