A daycare has something most advertisers do not: a hard cap on how many customers it can serve. If you have two open infant spots and are willing to pay about $300 in ads for each enrolled child, your useful ad budget for that room is roughly $600, not $1,500. Past that, you are buying tours for a waitlist you did not plan to build. The $300 is my assumption, and the rest of this guide shows how to work out your own figure.

This is for centers and home-based programs in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Dollar figures are US-based; run the same math in your own currency. It covers the benchmarks that exist (and the childcare ones that do not), how to set up Search, what Google's policies say about children, tracking tours and calls, and a spend-by-open-seats formula. For the neighbourhood-listing side, see our local SEO for daycare centers guide; for pets rather than children, see [Google Ads for dog daycare](/blog/google-ads-for-dog-daycare).

For comparison, the same page lists all-industry averages of $5.42 per click, a 6.64% click-through rate, an 8.18% conversion rate and a $66.69 cost per lead. Education is a proxy, not a measurement of daycares. A vendor roundup from CUFinder lists a $4.15 click and 7.2% conversion rate for daycare, but it states no source or method for those numbers, so this guide does not build on them.

Two lessons. First, a single-digit click price is plausible, not a promise: parents search in your town, and your competitors set the price. Second, "conversion" means whatever you counted. A phone call, a form and a booked tour are different events with different values, which is why the funnel below starts at the tour.

## Why Tuition Changes the Math

Child Care Aware of America's [Child Care in America: 2025 Price and Supply report](https://info.childcareaware.org/price-and-supply-2025) puts the national average annual price of child care at $13,184 in 2025, up from $13,128 in 2024, averaged across three calculation methods in 47 states. Its tables show infant center care as the most expensive category, between $15,015 and $15,728 a year depending on the method. The report also finds centers down about 1% nationwide from 2024 to 2025, reversing an upward trend, while family child care homes grew 1.4%.

What that means for ads: one enrolled child is worth over a thousand dollars of tuition each month, so a cost of a few hundred dollars per enrollment can be rational. But the price is revenue, not profit, and your own tuition and average length of stay are what matter. Pull those from your billing system.

## Pick the Right Campaign Type

**Start with Search.** A parent typing "infant care near me" or "toddler daycare [neighborhood]" has a problem to solve. Search ads catch that moment, you choose the keywords, and the clicks come from a defined area. Keep one campaign per location.

**Add Performance Max later, if at all.** It works from conversion signals, and a single center with a handful of tour requests a month gives it little to learn from. Prove Search first.

## The Policy Limits That Apply to a Childcare Advertiser

Two Google policies deserve a read before you build audiences.

- **Under-18 users.** Google's [personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) says "Users under the age of 18 are not eligible for personalized advertising of any kind." Your audience is parents, not children, so keyword-targeted Search ads are unaffected. It is a reason to avoid any audience setup that implies you are targeting kids.
- **Children's data.** The same page prohibits advertisers using advertiser-curated audiences from uploading customer information from viewers of child-directed content. Google's [customer data policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7475709) says you may not upload information about minors, nor data you know relates to individuals under 13, and lists "Status as a child under 13" among its sensitive categories. In practice: never upload a child's name, birth date or enrollment record to Google Ads, and do not feed enrollment lists into customer match audiences. Track outcomes inside your own spreadsheet or CRM, and ask your compliance adviser before sharing any data about families with ad platforms. This is not legal advice.

On claims, Google's [unreliable claims policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936857) bars ads that give inaccurate information or entice with improbable results. It does not mention urgency tactics, so "only 3 spots left" is not clearly banned, but it is my advice to use it only when it is literally true, because a parent who tours and finds a waitlist feels misled.

## Set Up the Campaign

### 1. Location: draw it around the drive, not the zip code

Most families choose a center near home or work. Pull your last 30 enrollments, map each family's address, and draw the ad radius around that spread. Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) says you can target a radius around a location, exclude locations, and that targeting is best effort rather than exact. Use the radius plus exclusions for neighborhoods you do not serve, and review where clicks come from after two weeks.

### 2. Keywords by room, not by center

Build ad groups around the age group that has an open spot. If your toddler room is full, do not bid on toddler terms.

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infant | "infant care near me", [infant daycare town], "daycare for 6 month old" | Phrase + exact |
| Toddler | "toddler daycare town", [toddler care near me] | Phrase + exact |
| Preschool | "preschool near me", "pre-k town" | Phrase + exact |
| After-school | "after school care town", "school pick up daycare" | Phrase + exact |
| Brand | [your center name] | Exact |

Google's [match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) says broad match is the default, that phrase match reaches searches including the keyword's meaning, and that exact gives the most control. It also says it is critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match. On a small budget, start with phrase and exact.

### 3. Negative keywords

Add on day one: `jobs`, `hiring`, `salary`, `certification`, `course`, `degree`, `training`, `babysitter jobs`, `nanny`, `dog`, `puppy`, `pet`, `curriculum pdf`, `free`, `coloring pages`. Review the search terms report weekly. Our negative keywords guide covers lists and campaign-level settings.

### 4. Ads that answer the worry

Responsive search ads accept up to 15 headlines of 30 characters and 4 descriptions of 90, per Google's [responsive search ad help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791). Use real, checkable facts. Examples of the style:

- Headlines: `Infant Care in [Town]`, `Tour Our Rooms This Week`, `Hours 7am to 6pm`, `Ages 6 Weeks to 5 Years`
- Description: `Small rooms, daily updates and a play-based day. Request a tour or call us.`

Mention licensing, ratios or accreditation only if you can show them on the landing page. Leave pinning to one or two key headlines, as Google says heavy pinning reduces testing.

### 5. Assets

Add a call asset, a location asset linked to your Business Profile, and sitelinks (Tour, Tuition, Programs, Parent Reviews). Parents use phones, often at lunch or after bedtime.

## Landing Page: One Room, One Action

Send clicks to the page for the age group in the ad. Each page needs:

- A headline that repeats the search ("Infant Care in [Town]")
- Real photos of the room and outdoor space
- Hours, ages, and "from" tuition or a clear way to get it
- Licensing information if applicable, and a plain statement of open spots or waitlist
- A short form and a tap-to-call number
- A single button: "Book a tour"

Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) go through layout. Offering online tour slots rather than a bare contact form often saves a day of phone tag.

## Tracking: Count Tours, Not Clicks

Set three conversion actions before spending:

1. **Tour request form or booked tour slot.** Track the thank-you page. See our conversion tracking setup guide.
2. **Calls from the ad** with a minimum call length. Google says a call counts only if it lasts longer than the minimum you set, which filters short calls.
3. **Calls from the website.** Google's [website call conversions help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) explains the phone snippet swaps your number for a Google forwarding number, tracks one number per page by default, and Tag Manager is not recommended with dynamic number insertion. See call tracking for local business.

Then record by hand which tours became enrollments. Count them in a spreadsheet, not in Google Ads, for the data reasons above.

## The Math: Spend by Open Seats

Start from the seats you can fill, not from a budget you feel comfortable with. All funnel rates below are **assumptions** except the $4.81 click and the conversion rates in the Base and Strong columns, which are taken from LocaliQ's all-industry and Education figures. The tour and enrollment rates are mine.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Cost per click | $7.00 (assumption) | $4.81 (LocaliQ Education) | $4.81 |
| Clicks | 143 | 208 | 208 |
| Click to inquiry rate | 5% (assumption) | 8.18% (LocaliQ all-industry) | 13.14% (LocaliQ Education) |
| Inquiries | 7 | 17 | 27 |
| Cost per inquiry | $140 | $59 | $37 |
| Inquiry to tour (assumption) | 40% | 50% | 60% |
| Tours | 2.9 | 8.5 | 16.4 |
| Tour to enrollment (assumption) | 30% | 40% | 55% |
| New enrollments | 0.9 | 3.4 | 9.0 |
| Ad cost per enrollment | $1,167 | $294 | $111 |
| Monthly tuition (assumption, near the $13,184 annual average) | $1,099 | $1,099 | $1,099 |

How to use it:

- **Seats first.** Budget equals open seats multiplied by the most you will pay per enrollment. Two infant seats at $300 each is $600.
- **Check the Base column against capacity.** It produces 3.4 enrollments from $1,000. If you only have two seats, you are overbuying by 40%.
- **Break-even click price.** Maximum CPC = target cost per enrollment x inquiry rate x tour rate x enrollment rate. At $300, 8.18%, 50% and 40%, that is $300 x 0.0818 x 0.5 x 0.4 = $4.91. Above that, you lose on average.
- **The Strong column is better than any benchmark on the page.** LocaliQ's own cost per lead for Education is $77.48, higher than the $37 here, so its funnel differs from mine. Do not plan around it.
- **Your close rate is the cheapest lever.** Going from a 30% tour-to-enrollment rate to 55% cuts cost per enrollment by almost half without touching the ad account. That is about how the tour feels, how fast you reply, and whether tuition was clear before the visit.

## Weekly Routine (20 Minutes)

1. Read the search terms report and add negatives.
2. Pause an age-group ad group when its room is full, and turn another on.
3. Check calls by hour and match ad hours to when someone answers.
4. Rotate one headline every two weeks.
5. Log last week's tours and enrollments.

Pair this with a complete [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist); parents read reviews before they click.

## Do This Today

1. List your open seats by age group and the tuition for each, and decide the most you will pay per enrollment.
2. Multiply seats by that number to set the monthly cap for each room.
3. Build one Search campaign with ad groups only for the rooms with openings, phrase and exact match, plus the negative list above.
4. Build or fix one landing page per room with real photos, tuition guidance and a "Book a tour" button, then set up form, ad-call and website-call conversions.
5. Create a spreadsheet to log every inquiry, tour and enrollment, and set a rule to answer new inquiries within the hour.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a daycare spend on Google Ads per month?**
A: Use seats times what you will pay per enrollment, then check that against your market's click price. Vendor guides quote ranges, but the ones found did not state a method, so treat them as unverified.

**Q: Can I target parents by age or interests?**
A: Keyword-targeted Search is the safest route. Google says users under 18 are ineligible for personalized ads, and its data policies restrict uploading information about minors, so avoid audience uploads that contain family data.

**Q: What if my ads get clicks but no tours?**
A: Check the page matches the search, loads fast on mobile and has a visible tour button and phone number. Then check whether tuition is unclear. Ask families who toured but did not enroll what held them back.

**Q: Should I run ads when I have a waitlist?**
A: Usually not for that age group. Pause that ad group and keep ads on rooms with openings.

**Q: Is Google Ads better than a directory listing?**
A: They are different tools. Directories can add inquiries, but ads give you control of the keywords, area and message. No verified cost figures for directories were found, so test both against cost per enrollment.
