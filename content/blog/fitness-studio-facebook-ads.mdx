Facebook and Instagram ads (run through Meta Ads Manager) can fill a fitness studio's classes if you target people within a few miles, lead with a low-risk offer such as a free class or intro week, and follow up quickly with everyone who responds. A typical starting budget is about $10 to $30 a day for four to six weeks, which is enough to learn what works.

This step-by-step guide covers account setup, offers, targeting, creative, budgets and measurement for yoga, pilates, boutique gyms, martial arts and similar studios.

## Before you spend a dollar

Ads send people somewhere. Check these basics first:

- **A clear offer.** "Free first class", "$49 for two weeks unlimited" or "Try a class, bring a friend free."
- **A simple landing page or form.** One page, one action: book a trial. Our [website service](/services/website) can build this if your current site is slow or confusing.
- **Fast follow-up.** Someone should reply within minutes, not days. Leads cool quickly.
- **A way to track.** At minimum the Meta Pixel on your site, and a note in your booking tool of "how did you hear about us?" For a deeper tracking setup, see our [analytics service](/services/analytics).
- **A decent profile.** New prospects will check your Instagram, Facebook page and Google reviews before booking.

## Step 1: Set up your accounts

1. Create or confirm your Facebook Page and Instagram professional account.
2. Open Meta Business Suite and Ads Manager, and link your Page, Instagram, ad account and payment method.
3. Verify your domain and install the Meta Pixel or use the Conversions API through your website platform. Most builders (Squarespace, WordPress, Wix, Mindbody and similar booking tools) include a Pixel field.
4. Check ad policies. Fitness ads must avoid before-and-after claims, body-shaming language and implied knowledge of someone's personal health. Keep wording about the class experience, not about a person's body.

## Step 2: Choose a campaign objective

Meta changes labels from time to time, but for a studio the most useful options are usually:

- **Leads**, using an instant form or a website form, for free-trial sign-ups.
- **Sales or conversions**, if you sell intro offers online and have the Pixel recording purchases.
- **Awareness or reach**, only for a grand opening and only for a short burst.

Start with Leads or Conversions. They optimise for people who take action, not just people who watch.

## Step 3: Target locally

Studios live and die by distance. A sensible starting point:

- **Location:** a radius of roughly 3 to 5 miles (5 to 8 km) around your studio in cities, wider in rural areas. Check your existing members' postcodes to see how far they actually travel.
- **Age and gender:** use your real customer mix, but stay broad. Meta's delivery system usually does better when it has room to find responders.
- **Interests:** optional. Interests like yoga, strength training or wellness can help early, but many advertisers get good results with broad targeting and strong creative.
- **Custom audiences:** upload your member and lead list (with consent) to exclude current members and to build lookalike audiences of similar people.

See our [Facebook ads targeting guide](/blog/facebook-ads-targeting-guide) for more depth.

## Step 4: Build offers that fit studio life

| Offer | Why it works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Free class or trial | Low risk, high sign-up | Follow-up needed to convert to paid |
| Intro week or month | Commitment and habit | Price must cover costs |
| New member bundle | Higher upfront revenue | Clear terms |
| Bring-a-friend class | Social and referral value | Capacity limits |
| Challenge (4 or 6 weeks) | Structure and community | Needs coaching time |
| Event or workshop | Warms cold audiences | Short window, one-off |

## Step 5: Create ads that stop the scroll

What tends to work in this niche:

- **Real photos and short video** from your own studio, with natural light and real members who have signed a photo release. Stock images are easy to spot and usually underperform.
- **Instructor intros.** A 15 to 30 second clip of a coach saying what a first class feels like reduces newcomer anxiety.
- **Specificity.** "Beginner-friendly reformer pilates in Eastfield, Tuesdays 6:30 pm" beats "Transform your body."
- **A single call to action**, such as "Book your free class."

Write your primary text in plain language: who it is for, what to expect, how to book. Prepare a few variations (different photo or video, same offer) and let Meta test them. Our [Facebook ads creative guide](/blog/facebook-ads-creative-guide) has more formats to try.

Placement-wise, Reels and Stories are worth including with vertical creative, while automatic placements are a fine starting default.

## Step 6: Set a budget and schedule

For most single-location studios:

- Start at roughly $10 to $30 per day per campaign, depending on your area and the value of a new member.
- Run for at least a week before judging, since the system needs time to learn. Avoid editing the ad constantly during that period.
- Align the start with natural demand peaks: January, September, and the weeks after summer holidays are common for fitness. Local spikes vary.

## Step 7: Follow up fast

For lead forms, enable instant notifications and use a templated text or email reply such as: thank-you, class times, and a one-tap booking link. Call new leads the same day if you can. An automated reply plus a human call usually beats either alone. For nurturing those who do not book at once, see [email marketing for fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-for-fitness-studios-fill-classes-reduce-churn).

## Step 8: Measure the numbers that matter

Do not stop at clicks. Track:

- **Cost per lead** (a trial sign-up).
- **Show-up rate** for those who booked.
- **Conversion to paid membership.**
- **Cost per new member**, calculated as total ad spend divided by new paying members.
- **Retention**, since a member who stays six months pays back much more than one who leaves after the intro.

Compare cost per new member to your average member value. Costs vary widely by city and offer, so benchmark against your own data after the first few weeks rather than industry averages.

## Common mistakes

- Targeting a whole city when most members live within a short drive.
- Sending traffic to a homepage instead of a booking page.
- Changing ads daily and resetting learning.
- Ignoring leads for days.
- Using body-image language that breaks ad policies and puts off newcomers.

## A four-week test plan

1. **Week 1:** Launch one campaign with two or three creative variations and one offer. Do not edit during the first few days.
2. **Week 2:** Pause the weakest creative, keep the strongest, and check lead quality by calling a few leads.
3. **Week 3:** Add a retargeting ad for website visitors who did not book, reminding them of the free class.
4. **Week 4:** Calculate cost per new member and compare it to what a member is worth. Decide whether to scale, change the offer or pause.

Write results down each week so decisions come from your own numbers.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a fitness studio spend on Facebook ads?**
A: Many studios begin with $300 to $900 a month and adjust once they know their cost per member. The right figure depends on your area and what a member is worth.

**Q: Do Facebook ads still work for gyms and studios?**
A: They can, especially for local offers with strong creative and quick follow-up. Results vary by market and offer, so test with a modest budget first. Read our [honest answer on whether Facebook ads work for local businesses](/blog/do-facebook-ads-actually-work-for-local-businesses-practical-answer).

**Q: Should I run lead forms or send people to my website?**
A: Try both. Instant forms are easier to complete on a phone, while website forms give you more qualified, higher-intent leads. Test which produces more paying members.

**Q: How long before I see results?**
A: Expect some leads within days, but give it four to six weeks before drawing firm conclusions.
