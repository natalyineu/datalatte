A pet groomer gets the most from email by sending a small number of useful messages to clients who have already booked: a welcome note, a rebooking reminder and a seasonal notice. Start with a free plan and a list of consenting clients. Mailchimp's published all-user averages are a 35.63% open rate and a 2.62% click rate, but they cover every industry, and Mailchimp lists no pet services category.

Under US law, every marketing email needs a postal address and a working opt-out. Examples here are labelled as assumptions.

## Why Is Email a Good Fit for a Grooming Business?

Grooming is repeat business, and your clients are already on your booking system. APPA reports that US owners spent $14.3 billion in 2025 on the "other services" category, which includes grooming, boarding, training, pet sitting and walking, with $14.9 billion projected for 2026. The figure is not grooming alone, so it only shows that the service category is large.

Email lets you reach clients without paying for each click. It works best for reminders, not for finding strangers. Our [email marketing guide for groomers](/blog/email-marketing-for-pet-groomers-keep-clients-booking) covers booking flows as well.

## What Emails Should a Groomer Send First?

Three messages cover most of the value, and each needs a single action.

| Email | When | One action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome | Right after the first visit | Save the next appointment |
| Rebooking reminder | Around the interval you recommend for that coat | Book now |
| Seasonal notice | Before busy periods you choose | Reserve a slot |

Set the reminder interval yourself, by breed and coat. The American Kennel Club says how often you groom a dog depends on size, breed, coat type and lifestyle, so no single weekly figure fits every dog. A good rule: segment by the groom type you do (full groom, bath and brush, nails only) and write each reminder for that group. See [email templates for groomers](/blog/pet-groomer-email-marketing) for ready wording.

## Which Email Tool Should You Use?

Pick the tool you can keep using. Mailchimp's pricing page lists a Free plan at $0 a month with 250 contacts and a maximum of 500 emails per month or 250 per day, as of the date this article was checked.  If your booking software already sends email, test that first.

For setup help, read [pet groomer email with Mailchimp](/blog/pet-groomer-email-marketing-with-mailchimp) and the [Klaviyo guide](/blog/using-klaviyo-for-pet-groomer-email-marketing). Compare the list limits before you pay for anything, because a small salon may never need a paid tier.

## What Are the Legal Rules for Marketing Emails?

The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide lists the basics: no false header information, no deceptive subject lines, a clear statement that the message is an ad, your valid physical postal address, a clear opt-out, and honoring opt-outs within 10 business days. Each separate violating email can carry a penalty of up to $53,088. Hiring another company to send the email does not remove your responsibility.

Gmail's bulk sender rules apply to senders of more than 5,000 messages a day, so a small grooming shop falls below that line. Still, one-click unsubscribe and a low spam rate are good habits. Google advises keeping the spam rate below 0.10% and not reaching 0.30%.

## How Do You Know If It Works?

Open rates are unreliable. Mailchimp's own benchmark page says Apple's Mail Privacy Protection can affect open-rate accuracy. Track clicks and, more importantly, bookings. Add a unique booking link or a discount word to each email so the front desk can match an appointment to a send.

Check these three numbers monthly: bookings that came from email, unsubscribes per send, and complaints. Mailchimp's average unsubscribe rate is 0.22% across its users, a benchmark to compare against, not a target.

## What Could a Month of Email Produce? A Worked Example

All numbers are **assumptions** unless noted. A groomer has 400 consenting clients on the free plan and sends one rebooking reminder.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Emails sent | 400 |
| Click rate | 2.62% (Mailchimp all-user average) |
| Clicks | about 10.5 |
| Clicks that become bookings (assumption) | 25% |
| Bookings | about 2.6 |
| Average groom price (assumption) | $70 |
| Revenue | about $184 |
| Tool cost | $0 on the free plan |

That is a small return for one email, so the value comes from repeating it every month and from the clients who book without clicking. Do not stretch the numbers: the click rate is a cross-industry average, and your own list may behave differently.

## FAQ

### How often should a pet groomer email clients?

There is no published standard. Start with one rebooking reminder per client cycle and one seasonal notice, then watch unsubscribes.

### Do I need consent before emailing clients?

CAN-SPAM requires an opt-out and honest content, but check local rules if you serve clients outside the US. Collect permission on the booking form either way.

### What is a good open rate for groomers?

Mailchimp does not list pet services. Its all-user average is 35.63%, though Apple's Mail Privacy Protection affects open data.

### Which tool is cheapest?

Mailchimp advertises a Free plan with 250 contacts. Check the current limits before relying on it.

### Can I offer a discount for a Google review by email?

Google prohibits offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews. Ask for the review, but do not pay for it.
