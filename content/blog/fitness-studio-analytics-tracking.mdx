A fitness studio can track success with free tools: Google Analytics 4 for website actions, Google Business Profile for calls and direction requests, and your booking or membership software for sign-ups and cancellations. Track five numbers: leads, trial bookings, new members, cost per new member and member retention. The Health and Fitness Association's 2025 benchmarking report puts industry member retention at 66.4%.

This guide is for studio owners who want to know which marketing dollars bring members. Figures are sourced at the end, and the worked example is labelled as an assumption.

## Which Numbers Should a Studio Track?

Start with five, in this order:

1. **Leads:** calls, form fills, trial requests and DMs, counted by source.
2. **Trial or intro bookings:** how many leads book a first class.
3. **New members:** how many trials join.
4. **Cost per new member:** marketing spend divided by new members.
5. **Retention:** the share of members who are still members a year later.

Everything else is supporting detail. If you cannot count the first four by source, fix tracking before you spend more on ads.

## What Does Google Analytics 4 Track for You?

Google's help says any event you collect can become a key event, which appears in reports such as Landing pages and User acquisition. A studio's key events might be a trial form submit, a click on the booking button or a click on a phone number. Mark those events as key in GA4 so you can see which channels produce them.

Google also says a conversion in Google Ads is created from a GA4 key event and is used for bidding and audiences. Standard GA4 properties keep event data for 2 or 14 months for explorations and funnel reports. Set it to 14 months, then export a monthly summary yourself. For setup, see our [GA4 guide for small business](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business).

## What Do You Get From Google Business Profile?

Google Business Profile shows calls, direction requests, website clicks, views, searches and, if you use a supported booking provider, bookings. Google notes calls count taps on the call button, and directions are adjusted to filter repeated taps and spam. Performance data requires a verified profile.

These are actions on Google, not proof of visits. A direction request means someone wanted to come, not that they did. Compare them with the new-member count each month. Only metrics that apply to your business appear.

## How Do You Connect Ads to Members?

Ad clicks end at your site or booking tool, and members are recorded in your membership software. Connecting them takes a manual step: add a "How did you hear about us?" field at sign-up, save UTM tags in your forms where possible and record the answer next to each new member.

LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks give a sense of cost. Health and Fitness averages $6.17 per click on Google Ads with 6.94% conversion and $67.36 per lead. On Facebook lead campaigns the same sector averages $1.84 per click, 7.98% conversion and $27.11 per lead. Your numbers will differ, so use them as a sanity check.

## The Math: Is a Member Worth the Cost?

All inputs below are **assumptions** except the retention benchmark.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Marketing spend for the month | $900 |
| New members that month | 15 |
| Cost per new member | $900 / 15 = $60 |
| Monthly fee | $99 |
| Gross margin | 60% |
| Gross profit per member per month | about $59 |
| Annual retention | 66.4% (HFA benchmark) |
| Implied monthly retention | about 96.6% (0.664 to the power 1/12) |
| Expected months a member stays | about 30 (1 divided by 3.4% monthly churn) |
| Lifetime gross profit | about $59 x 30 = $1,770 |

Under these inputs, a member who costs $60 to acquire is worth about thirty times that in gross profit. The 30-month life is a simple calculation from a benchmark, not a measured average, so replace it with your own cancellation data. If your retention is much lower, the payback shortens quickly.

## How Often Should You Review the Numbers?

A routine works better than constant checking. Weekly, spend 15 minutes on leads and trial bookings by source. Monthly, calculate cost per new member and compare it with the $59 or so of monthly profit per member in the example above. Quarterly, review retention and cancellations. This cadence is a recommendation, not a published standard.

## FAQ

### Do I need a paid analytics tool?

No. GA4 and Business Profile insights are free, and your booking software will report sign-ups. Add a paid tool only when you cannot answer a specific question with these.

### What is a good retention rate for a fitness studio?

The HFA's 2025 benchmarking report shows 66.4% member retention across a sample of 175 companies, 114 of them in the US. Studios differ from large gyms, so compare against your own trend first.

### How do I track referrals and walk-ins?

Ask at sign-up and record the answer. A referral code makes referrals countable.

### How long should I keep GA4 data?

Set event data retention to 14 months in GA4 settings if you use explorations. Standard reports are not affected, according to Google.
