Here is the number to work toward. Say you spend two hours a month keeping an Instagram shop tidy, your time is worth $25 an hour, each order leaves you $12 after costs, and 3% of people who tap a product tag go on to buy. Then you need about 139 tag taps a month just to cover your time. Those four inputs are my assumptions, not benchmarks, and you will swap in your own. The reason to do the sum first: Instagram Shopping is free to set up, but a catalog, product pages and weekly posts cost you hours.

This guide is for local businesses that sell physical products in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: coffee shops and roasters, salons with retail shelves, pet groomers, studios with merch. It covers what changed with checkout, who is allowed to use shopping tags, the setup steps, and whether it is worth your hours. Availability differs by country, so confirm your market with Meta before you start.

## What Changed: Checkout Is on Your Website Now

Older guides, including earlier versions of this one, describe buying inside Instagram. That is no longer how it works. Shopify's help pages say that as of August 26, 2025, Meta no longer handles checkout natively for new orders, and that customers are redirected to the merchant's online store to complete the purchase ([Shopify Help: Facebook and Instagram by Meta requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/facebook-instagram-by-meta/requirements)). Shopify adds that Meta no longer acts as a marketplace tax facilitator for those orders, so you are responsible for collecting taxes ([Shopify Help: setup](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/facebook-instagram-by-meta/setup)). This is second-hand reporting from Shopify, not a Meta page, so read Meta's own notices in your account before relying on the detail.

What that means in practice:

- **Instagram is a shop window, your website is the till.** A shopper sees a tagged product, taps, and ends up on your product page.
- **Your product page is now the whole sales process.** If it loads slowly on a phone or hides the price, tags will not save you.
- **You need a website that can take payment.** A booking page or a PDF menu does not count.
- **You handle sales tax and customer service yourself.**

## Who Can Use Shopping Tags

Two filters decide this: what you sell and where you are.

**What you sell.** Meta's [Commerce Policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce) say:

- Commerce content may not promote downloadable digital content, digital subscriptions or digital accounts.
- Services cannot be listed for sale except on the WhatsApp Business app or through Appointments on Facebook or Instagram.
- Alcohol, tobacco, weapons, prescription drugs and gambling are prohibited.
- Event tickets and gift cards or vouchers are restricted and allowed only for approved sellers.
- Violations can lead to rejected or removed listings or product tags, or suspension of commerce access.

That matters for local businesses. Selling a haircut, a grooming slot or a class as a product is out. A gift card is a restricted category, so do not plan your shop around one. Retail haircare, pet shampoo, coffee beans, mugs and apparel are fine.

**Where you are.** Shopify says your business must be in a country supported for Shops on Facebook and Instagram and points to a separate Meta list of supported countries. Check that list for your country before you build anything.

**Account requirements.** For the Shopify route, Shopify lists a professional or creator Instagram account associated with your business's Facebook Page, a published Page and a business portfolio connected to it. Shopify's own [Instagram Shopping guide](https://www.shopify.com/blog/instagram-shopping) (May 2026) also lists a supported country, an accepted product, and a domain connected to your business account.

## Set It Up Step by Step

Which route you take depends on how you sell today.

### If you already sell on Shopify

1. In Shopify admin go to **Settings, then Sales channels**, open the Shopify App Store and add the Facebook and Instagram channel.
2. Connect your Facebook Page and business portfolio, accept the terms and finish setup. Your Facebook account needs full control of both, per Shopify.
3. Check **Settings, then Taxes and Duties** before you finish, because you now collect tax on these orders.
4. Wait for Meta to approve the shop. Shopify says only Meta can approve use of the channel and that some errors mean the shop is still in review. Neither Shopify page gives a review time, so plan for waiting, not a deadline.
5. Your catalog syncs from Shopify, and Shopify says setting up an Instagram Shop is free.

### If you use another platform or none

Meta's own route is Commerce Manager, where you create a catalog from a feed, a connected platform or manual entries. Meta changes these screens often, so follow the steps shown inside Commerce Manager and your platform's own documentation rather than any guide's screenshots, including ours.

### Get the product data right

For every product, have a clear title, an accurate price that matches the product page, a stock status, a link to the page where it can be bought, and one clean photo on a plain background plus a lifestyle shot. Mismatched prices between the catalog and the page are the commonest reason a listing gets flagged. Keep stock updated, because tagging sold-out items wastes a tap.

## How to Tag Products Without Looking Like a Catalog

Once approved, you can tag products in feed posts, Stories and Reels. Shopify notes that users can also tag your products in their own posts.

- **Tag around a reason.** A new roast, a seasonal mug, a restock. Weekly is plenty.
- **Show the product in use.** Pouring the coffee, a groomer using the shampoo, a client leaving with the styling clay.
- **Keep non-selling posts the majority.** Aim for at least three people-and-place posts for every tagged one.
- **Name the detail in the caption.** Size, flavour, who it suits.

Pew Research Center's survey of US adults (fielded February to June 2025) found 50% use Instagram, including 80% of those aged 18 to 29 and 62% of those aged 30 to 49 ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)). If your customers sit in those age groups, the audience is there. That says nothing about whether they will buy through a tag, which is why you track it.

## If You Sell Services, Not Products

Meta's commerce policy blocks service listings outside Appointments, so shopping tags are the wrong tool for a haircut or a class. Use this instead:

- A booking link in your bio, kept current.
- Reels showing results, with the booking link in your bio and a clear caption call to action.
- Story link stickers for limited slots.
- Retail products only, if you have them, in the shop.

## The Math

Take a coffee roaster selling 12 oz bags online. Every input is an **assumption** for illustration, not a benchmark. Replace them with your own numbers after a month of real data.

| | Slow | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product-tag taps per month (assumption) | 100 | 250 | 500 |
| Share of tappers who buy (assumption) | 3% | 3% | 3% |
| Orders | 3 | 7.5 | 15 |
| Contribution per order (assumption) | $12 | $12 | $12 |
| Contribution total | $36 | $90 | $180 |
| Monthly upkeep: 2 hours at $25 (assumption) | $50 | $50 | $50 |
| Net per month | -$14 | +$40 | +$130 |

How to read it: break-even is the upkeep divided by contribution per tapper, which is $50 / ($12 x 3%) = about 139 taps. The one-off setup (say 6 hours at $25, so $150) needs about 12.5 more orders to repay, which is roughly 417 extra taps. The numbers are small because the shop is small. Instagram Shopping for a local business is a modest extra revenue line, and it earns its keep mostly when the people already following you buy more often.

Three levers move it:

- **Contribution per order.** Bundles and a free-shipping threshold lift the order value without lifting the number of taps you need.
- **Page conversion.** Doubling that 3% to 6% halves the taps you need. Fix the product page before you chase more reach.
- **Time.** If the catalog syncs automatically, the upkeep falls toward zero.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | What to do instead |
| --- | --- |
| Tagging every post | Tag around launches, bestsellers and seasonal items |
| Catalog price differs from page price | Sync from your store, or update both on the same day |
| Linking to the home page | Link to the product page |
| Treating a service as a product | Use booking links and Appointments; services are not allowed as products |
| Ignoring tax and returns | Check your tax settings and returns policy before launch |
| Never checking which tags get taps | Open Insights monthly and keep the products that earn taps |

## Do This Today

1. Check that you sell physical products that Meta's Commerce Policies allow, and confirm your country is on Meta's supported list.
2. Open your own product page on a phone and fix anything slow, unclear or missing: price, photo, a pay button, delivery or pickup information.
3. Pick your three best sellers and shoot a clean photo and a lifestyle shot of each.
4. Connect your store (Shopify's Facebook and Instagram channel, or Commerce Manager), set up taxes and submit for review.
5. When approved, tag one product a week for a month, then compare taps and orders against the break-even above.

## FAQ

**Q: Can customers still check out inside Instagram?**
A: Not for new orders, according to Shopify's help pages: since August 26, 2025 customers are redirected to your own online store to pay. Check your own Meta account notices for your market.

**Q: Does Instagram Shopping cost anything?**
A: Shopify says setting up an Instagram Shop is free. You still pay for your store platform, payment processing and any ads, and you spend time on photos and upkeep.

**Q: Can I sell gift cards or services through shopping tags?**
A: Services are not allowed except through Appointments or WhatsApp Business, per Meta's Commerce Policies. Gift cards and vouchers are restricted to approved sellers, so do not count on them.

**Q: Why can't I see the shopping option?**
A: Common reasons are a personal (not professional) account, an unlinked Facebook Page, a country that is not supported, a product category Meta does not allow, or a shop still in review. Check the messages in Commerce Manager.

**Q: How often should I tag products?**
A: Weekly is enough for most small shops. Tag when there is a reason: a launch, a restock or a season.
