Instagram Shopping lets you tag products in your posts, Stories and Reels so customers can tap a tag, see the price and go to buy, either inside Instagram or on your website. To use it you need a business account, a connected Facebook Page, a product catalog and an account that meets Meta's commerce requirements.

Features and availability change often, so check Meta's current help pages for your country before you commit. In particular, Meta has been scaling back in-app checkout in some markets, which means many small shops now use product tags to send people to their own website to complete the purchase. This guide covers the setup and how local businesses can use it well.

## Is Instagram Shopping right for your business?

Shopping tags suit businesses that sell physical products people can buy online or reserve for pickup:

- **Coffee shops and roasters** selling beans, merch, gift boxes or brewing gear.
- **Salons and barbershops** selling retail haircare and styling products.
- **Pet groomers and pet shops** selling shampoos, accessories and treats.
- **Fitness studios** selling apparel, bottles or gift cards.

If you sell only services, shopping tags are less useful. In that case focus on booking links and a clear bio link instead. We cover that further down.

## What you need before you start

Meta's requirements can vary by region, so treat this as a checklist to verify:

- An Instagram **professional (business or creator) account**.
- A **Facebook Page** connected to the Instagram account.
- A **product catalog** in Meta Commerce Manager.
- A **website domain** where customers can buy (often a requirement for tagging).
- Compliance with Meta's **commerce policies** and merchant agreement.
- Products that are **physical goods** and allowed under the policies.

Meta reviews the account before it enables shopping. Review time varies, so allow a few days.

## Step 1: Set up your catalog

The catalog is your product list. You can create one in several ways:

1. **Connect an ecommerce platform.** Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce and Square Online typically have integrations that sync products automatically.
2. **Upload a spreadsheet or feed.** A CSV with product name, description, price, image link and availability works for small catalogs.
3. **Add products manually** in Commerce Manager, which is fine for under about twenty items.

For each product you need a clear title, an accurate price, an availability status, a link to the product page and at least one good image. Photograph on a plain, bright background, and add a lifestyle shot where you can.

## Step 2: Connect the catalog to Instagram

In Instagram settings, open the business tools and choose the shopping setup. Select your catalog, submit the account for review and wait for approval. Once approved, you can turn on shopping and tag products.

If you cannot find the option, the usual reasons are:

- The account is personal and not professional.
- The Facebook Page is not linked.
- The business is in a region where a feature is not available.
- The domain has not been verified.

## Step 3: Tag products in posts, Stories and Reels

When you create a post, choose "Tag products" and pick the item from your catalog. Place the tag on the product in the image. You can tag several products in one photo, and you can add tags to older posts too.

Practical tips:

- **Tag only what is visible and in stock.** Tagging a product that is sold out frustrates customers.
- **Use captions that explain.** Mention the key detail, such as flavour, size, or who it is good for.
- **Mix promotional and non-promotional posts.** A feed of only tagged products feels like a catalogue, not a business with a personality.

## Step 4: Decide where customers buy

Depending on your region and Meta's current setup, customers may be able to:

- **Check out inside Instagram** (where this is still offered).
- **Go to your website** to finish the purchase.
- **Message you** to order, which suits made-to-order or local pickup items.

If in-app checkout is not available to you, use tags to drive people to a clear, fast product page. Check that the page loads quickly on mobile, shows the price and offers an easy way to pay. Our [website service](/services/website) can help if your shop page needs work.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What to do instead |
| --- | --- |
| Tagging every post | Tag selectively, around launches, bestsellers and seasonal items |
| Using dark or cluttered product photos | Use bright, simple images that show the product clearly |
| Setting up the catalog once and forgetting it | Update prices and stock weekly, or sync it automatically |
| Sending people to a slow or confusing page | Link directly to the product, not your home page |
| Ignoring messages and comments | Reply quickly; shopping questions often arrive in DMs |

## If you sell services, not products

Service businesses cannot tag a haircut or a grooming session as a product in the same way, but you can still turn Instagram into a booking channel:

- Put your **booking link** in the bio and update it for promotions.
- Use **Reels** to show results and link to booking in the caption or comments.
- Use **Story link stickers** for limited availability or seasonal offers.
- Sell **gift cards** as a product if your platform allows.

## A simple launch plan

Do not try to list your whole range on day one.

1. **Pick three to five products** with good photos and steady demand.
2. **Build the catalog** and confirm each product page works on a phone.
3. **Submit for review,** and use the waiting time to shoot a few extra photos.
4. **Post one tagged product a week** for a month, mixing in normal content.
5. **Check which tags get taps** in Insights, and put more effort into those products.

Review the results after a month. If tags bring few clicks, improve your photos and product pages before adding more products or spending on ads.

## FAQ

**Q: Does Instagram Shopping cost anything?**

A: Setting up shopping and tagging products has typically been free. Fees can apply if you use in-app checkout (where available), plus the cost of your ecommerce platform and payment processor. Check Meta's current terms.

**Q: Why can I not see the shopping option?**

A: Usually the account is not professional, the Facebook Page is not linked, the domain is not verified, or shopping is not available in your region. Check Commerce Manager for specific errors.

**Q: Can a local shop use it for in-store pickup?**

A: Yes, in practice. You can tag products and direct customers to your site or DMs to reserve for pickup, depending on the options Meta offers in your country.

**Q: How often should I tag products?**

A: Only when it fits the post. Weekly is plenty for most small shops. Tag around launches and bestsellers.
