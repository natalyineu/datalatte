Mobile marketing for a local business comes down to five things: a complete Google Business Profile, a website that works on a phone, a tap-to-call path, location-limited ads and permission-based text messages. Start with the free ones. Pew Research's 2026 survey found 91% of US adults own a smartphone, so most of your customers will meet you on one.

This guide ranks those five by cost and effort, with a worked budget example. Every number comes from a source at the end. I removed older claims about average phone time and mobile ad ROAS because I could not trace them.

## Who Is Actually on Mobile?

Pew's 2026 fact sheet (survey run 30 January to 18 June 2026) reports 98% of US adults own a cellphone and 91% a smartphone. Ownership is 97% for ages 18 to 29 and 30 to 49, 91% for 50 to 64 and 79% for 65 and over.

Pew also reports that 16% of adults are smartphone-only internet users, meaning they have a smartphone but no home broadband. For them, your phone experience is your whole website. These are US figures; the pattern in the UK, Canada and Australia may differ and I did not check it.

## Step 1: What Should Your Mobile Presence Look Like?

Fix the free surfaces first. Your Google Business Profile should have correct hours, phone number, categories and photos, because that is what shows on a phone map search. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) walks through it.

Then check your website on a phone. Google says it uses the mobile version of a site for indexing and ranking, so the mobile page needs the same primary content, titles, descriptions, structured data and image alt text as desktop. See [mobile optimization for local business websites](/blog/mobile-optimization-local-business) and [website speed optimization](/blog/website-speed-optimization-small-business).

## Step 2: How Do You Make Calling Easy?

Put a tap-to-call button at the top of every page and in your ads. In Google Ads, a call asset puts a call button under a search ad on devices that can make calls. Google says call-asset clicks cost the same as headline clicks, and the asset does not show on every impression because the auction decides.

Turn on call reporting to count calls as conversions; Google creates a "Call from ads" conversion action and it works only on the Search Network. More ideas in [how to get more phone calls from your website](/blog/how-to-get-more-phone-calls-from-your-website).

## Step 3: How Do You Keep Mobile Ads Local?

Radius targeting needs a radius of at least 1 km, according to Google. Google recommends "Presence or interest" for Search, which also reaches people interested in your area. "Presence" reaches only people physically there, which suits businesses that cannot serve visitors from elsewhere.

Review the search terms report weekly and add negative keywords. Budget guidance for the whole mix is in the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).

## Step 4: Is Text Messaging Worth It?

For businesses with repeat customers, such as salons, groomers and cafes, texts are cheap to send and read on the phone. You need clear opt-in consent before sending marketing texts, and an easy way to stop. I did not open a regulator page for the exact US rules, so check them before you start. Our [SMS marketing guide for local business](/blog/sms-marketing-for-local-business-text-campaigns-that-work) covers consent and timing.

## Step 5: Should You Build an App?

Probably not. An app only pays off if customers return often and you can afford to build and maintain it. For most single-location businesses a fast site, a complete profile and a booking link do the same job. I found no source giving a reliable app cost, so I give none.

## Worked Example: A $500 Monthly Mobile Budget

All figures are **assumptions**.

| Item | Monthly | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Ads, local search with call asset | $300 | Radius around the shop; weekly negative keywords |
| Text messages to opted-in customers | $50 | Vendor price varies; check your platform |
| Photos and profile updates | $0 | Your time, about 1 hour |
| Reserve for testing a second ad | $150 | Only if calls are tracked |

Track calls from ads, tapped directions and booking clicks. After 60 days, move the reserve to whichever of the three has the lowest cost per customer you can actually count.

## FAQ

### What is mobile marketing for a small business?
It is marketing designed for people on phones: local search listings, a phone-friendly site, click-to-call, local ads and texts.

### Do I need a separate mobile website?
No. A single responsive site is simpler. If you do keep separate versions, Google says content, titles, structured data and alt text should match.

### How much should I spend on mobile ads?
There is no standard number. Start with an amount you can lose, track calls and visits for 60 days, then adjust.

### Are text messages legal for marketing?
Only with proper consent. Rules differ by country, so read the regulator's guidance for yours.

### Do most customers search on phones?
Most US adults own smartphones, but I found no source for the exact share of local searches done on phones, so I do not give one.
