A social media content calendar is a table that lists every planned post by date, channel, post type, goal, message, visual, link, owner and status, usually built one month at a time. A spreadsheet is enough for most small businesses. [Bellwether Coffee](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/social-media-marketing-for-coffee-shops) advises planning 3 to 4 hours weekly for content creation and scheduling. No source gives a posting frequency that suits every business, so the frequency in this guide is a judgment call.

## What columns should a content calendar include?

Use one row per post with nine columns: date, channel, post type, goal, message, visual, link, owner and status. The goal column matters most. A post with no goal, such as a booking, a visit or a sign-up, is hard to judge later, so write the goal before the caption.

The owner column does a similar job. Each task needs one named person, even if that person is you, or the plan slips in the second week. The status column (planned, drafted, scheduled, published) keeps a shared sheet accurate.

Post type should match the format's purpose. [Associated Hair Professionals](https://www.associatedhairprofessionals.com/updates/blog-posts/power-social-media-your-hairstyling-brand) notes that Instagram and Facebook stories are temporary, so they suit time-limited offers rather than evergreen tips (secondary).

One way to use the four disciplines from [RedFox Digital](https://redfox.digital/insights/digital-marketing-strategy-for-local-business/) is to fill the goal column first (Clarify), write and shoot the posts (Create), link each one to a next step (Convert), and review results at month end (Compound) (secondary).

## How far ahead should you plan?

Sources disagree on lead time. [Boltic](https://www.boltic.io/blog/marketing-calendar-template) advises beginning calendar planning 90 days or one month before execution (secondary). [Salesforce Canada](https://www.salesforce.com/ca/small-business/marketing/smb-marketing-guide/?bc=OTH) suggests scheduling content between one and six months ahead, leaving room for delays or postponements (secondary).

The two fit together. Plan the next month in detail, then keep a looser list of seasonal dates further out. [MarketingCoach.ie](https://marketingcoach.ie/marketing-calendar/) describes a traditional marketing calendar as one all-inclusive resource for planning every project and campaign in one place, offered as a full-year template (secondary).

Keep the long-range list short. A 12-month list of dates such as holidays, openings and events is useful, but only the next four weeks need full captions and visuals.

## How many posts per week should a small business publish?

No source sets a universal number, so choose a frequency you can sustain through a full month. Output that exceeds capacity usually shows up as weaker posts.

Boltic describes a team that pushed five content pieces a week, found quality suffered, and cut output to three, with each piece sharper and better aligned to goals (secondary). The same author suggests planning only for what you can sustain, for example "two high-quality blogs a month and four strong newsletters."

For busy weeks, prepare reserves. [Appointy](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/) recommends creating at least 5 to 10 posts in each content category in advance for days when you are too busy to generate fresh content (secondary). For a studio-specific approach, see the [fitness studio content guide](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-content).

## How do you batch content for a month?

[Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) states that batching is 3 to 4 times more efficient than creating content one post at a time (vendor claim, secondary). [One of One Group](https://oneofonegroup.co.uk/social-media-marketing/hair-salons/) states that a month of salon content can be shot in a single visit (secondary). The same principle applies to a café or studio.

1. Set the month's themes and goals in the calendar.
2. Shoot all photos and short clips in one visit.
3. Write captions directly into the calendar rows.
4. Schedule the posts and mark them as scheduled.
5. Keep two or three spare posts for the busiest days.

## How much time does a calendar take each week?

Bellwether Coffee suggests dedicating 2 to 3 hours weekly to shooting and writing content, on top of 3 to 4 hours for planning and scheduling. The budget below tests whether a typical month fits that range. All inputs are assumptions.

| Input or result | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pieces per month | 14 | assumption, from the café example below |
| Minutes to shoot and write each piece | 40 | assumption |
| Creation time per month | 9.3 hours | 14 × 40 minutes = 560 minutes |
| Creation time per week | 2.3 hours | 9.3 ÷ 4 weeks |
| Bellwether benchmark, shooting and writing | 2 to 3 hours weekly | sourced |
| Bellwether benchmark, creation and scheduling | 3 to 4 hours weekly | sourced |

At 2.3 hours a week, creation sits inside the benchmark and leaves room for scheduling. If your real figure is 60 minutes per piece, the same month takes about 14 hours, which is above the range. In that case, cut pieces or simplify visuals before adding posts.

## Can AI tools reduce the planning work?

AI tools can cut drafting time, but treat vendor figures as claims to test. [Sintra](https://sintra.ai/blog/ai-social-media/best-social-media-ai-agents) says an AI agent drafting and adapting posts across platforms can cut 10 to 15 hours a week of rewriting (vendor claim, secondary). Its example also says a month of Instagram posts can be planned in under an hour (secondary).

[HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/ai-agents-for-social-media) reports that one in three marketers are already deploying AI for content creation and data analysis (HubSpot research, secondary). HubSpot also suggests a 30 to 60 day probationary period before letting an agent draft social content.

Sintra describes SocialBee as organizing content into categories such as promotions, tips, testimonials and behind-the-scenes, then cycling through them automatically (secondary). Those categories map neatly onto the theme column in a calendar. For setup steps, see the [AI agent for social media posting guide](/blog/ai-agent-for-social-media-posting-local-business).

## Which tools fit a small calendar?

A small calendar rarely needs more than a spreadsheet and one scheduler. [Clear Cut Creative](https://clearcut-creative.com/blog/the-best-ai-tools-for-local-businesses-right-now/) lists content planning among the jobs the best AI tools for local businesses handle, alongside writing and workflow automation (secondary).

| Tool type | Job | Check before committing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spreadsheet | Plan, track and review | None; free to start |
| Social scheduler | Queue posts to several channels | Current plan limits on the vendor pricing page |
| Google Business Profile | Publish profile posts | Repeat and scheduling options on Google's live Help page |
| AI planning tool | Draft and categorize posts | Probation period and review steps |

This article does not quote current prices, so check each vendor's live pricing and help pages before choosing. To compare schedulers, see the [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social comparison for local businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business). Choose channels by where your customers are. Bellwether Coffee suggests TikTok is worth using if the target customer is under 35, citing significant organic reach for authentic content (secondary).

## What does a four-week calendar look like for a café?

All inputs in this example are **assumptions**.

| Week | Theme | Facebook and Instagram | Google profile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | New seasonal drink | 3 posts | 1 update |
| 2 | Behind the scenes | 2 posts | 0 |
| 3 | Customer favourite | 3 posts | 1 offer |
| 4 | Event or reminder | 3 posts | 1 event |

That is 11 social posts and 3 profile posts, 14 pieces in total. At 40 minutes each, the month uses about 9.3 hours of creation time, as in the budget above. After the month, record which posts led to a visit, call or booking, then drop the weakest theme and replace it. ## FAQ

### How many times a week should a small business post?

No source gives a universal number. Pick a frequency you can sustain through a full month, then review results before raising it. Boltic's author cut output from five pieces to three a week after quality suffered (secondary).

### Do I need a paid scheduling tool?

Not at the start. A spreadsheet covers planning, and many platforms include native scheduling. Check each platform's live help page for current options before paying for a tool.

### How far ahead should I plan?

Plan the next month in detail and keep a looser list of seasonal dates further out. Sources range from 90 days (Boltic) to one to six months (Salesforce Canada), both secondary.

### Should every post sell?

No, but each post needs a goal, and the result should be checked against that goal after the month. Stories are temporary by design, so they suit time-limited offers (secondary).
