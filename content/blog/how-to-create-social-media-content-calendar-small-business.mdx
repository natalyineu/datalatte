A social media content calendar is a table with a date, channel, post type, message and link for every post, planned a month at a time. For a small business, a spreadsheet is enough. Buffer's free plan allows 3 channels with 10 scheduled posts each, and Google Business Profile lets you schedule and repeat posts at no extra cost. No source gives a posting frequency that works for everyone.

## What Goes Into a Content Calendar?

Use one row per post with these columns: date, channel, post type, goal, message, visual, link, status. The goal column matters most. A post that has no goal (a booking, a visit, a sign-up) is hard to judge later.

Add a column for the owner of each task. Without one, the plan slips in week two.

## How Do You Plan a Month?

1. List your goals for the month, such as a seasonal offer or a new service.
2. Mark fixed dates: holidays, events, opening hours changes. Our [holiday marketing plan](/blog/holiday-marketing-strategy-local-business) gives the 2026 dates.
3. Divide the rest into 3 or 4 repeatable themes, for example offer, behind the scenes, customer result, tip.
4. Choose a frequency you can keep. This is a judgment call; no source tells you the right number.
5. Fill the table, then batch-produce photos on one day.

For a 12-month view, the [marketing calendar](/blog/local-business-marketing-calendar-week-by-week-12-months) lists seasonal prompts.

## Which Tools Can Schedule the Posts?

| Tool | What it does | Limit or price read on 2026-11-01 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spreadsheet | Plan and track | free |
| Buffer | Schedule to many channels | Free: 3 channels, 10 scheduled posts per channel. Essentials: $5 a month per channel, or $60 billed yearly (the page is inconsistent about the annual discount) |
| Meta Business Suite | Schedule Facebook and Instagram posts | Meta has a help article on scheduling in its mobile app; its steps could not be read for this article |
| Google Business Profile | Schedule and repeat posts | "Schedule this post" with a "Repeats" option (weekly, monthly or custom) |

Check your own platform's current features before you commit to a tool. On Buffer's free plan a slot frees up once a post publishes, so 10 means 10 queued at a time. Our guide to [profile posts](/blog/how-to-create-google-my-business-post-that-gets-clicks) covers the Google side.

## A Worked Example: Four Weeks for a Cafe

All inputs are **assumptions**.

| Week | Theme | Facebook / Instagram | Google profile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | New seasonal drink | 3 posts | 1 update |
| 2 | Behind the scenes | 2 posts | 0 |
| 3 | Customer favourite | 3 posts | 1 offer |
| 4 | Event or reminder | 3 posts | 1 event |

That is 11 social posts and 3 profile posts, or 14 pieces for the month. On Buffer's free plan with two channels, the queue holds 20 posts, so the 11 social posts fit; the profile posts are scheduled in Google. With 3 channels the queue holds 30. After the month, record which posts led to a visit or booking and cut the lowest theme. Pair this with [email](/blog/local-business-email-list-from-zero) so every post has a place to send people.

## FAQ

### How many times a week should a small business post?

No source gives a universal number. Choose a frequency you can keep for three months.

### Do I need a paid scheduling tool?

Not at the start. A spreadsheet plus the free scheduling in Google Business Profile and your platform's own tools may be enough.

### How far ahead should I plan?

Plan one month in detail and keep a looser 12-month list of seasonal dates.

### Can I schedule Google Business Profile posts?

Yes. Google lets you pick a date and time and set weekly, monthly or custom repeats.

### Should every post sell?

No, but each should have a goal. Track the result against it.
