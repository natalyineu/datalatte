Amazon ads are worth it for a local business only if you sell physical products that are already listed on Amazon and still earn a margin after Amazon's fees.

If you sell a service, such as a haircut, a grooming appointment or a class, shoppers do not look for it on Amazon, and Google is the better channel. This guide shows how to decide in five minutes and how to test with a small budget if the answer is yes.

## Should a Local Business Advertise on Amazon?

Use this table. The test is simple: can the thing you sell go in a shopping cart?

| Your business | Fit | Better first channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sells bagged coffee, merch or gifts nationwide | Possible test | Your own site plus Amazon test |
| Pet groomer with a branded shampoo or treats | Possible test | Google Ads for bookings first |
| Salon with its own haircare line | Possible test | Google Business Profile for bookings |
| Fitness studio selling gear | Weak | Local ads for memberships |
| Plumber, electrician, yoga studio, barbershop | No | Google Ads and local SEO |

Amazon is a retail marketplace. Its advertising reaches people who are already shopping for products. For services near them, see [Amazon Ads vs Google Ads for local businesses](/blog/amazon-ads-vs-google-ads-local-business), which compares the two in more detail.

## How Big Is Amazon Advertising?

Large, which is why it is tempting. Marketing Dive, reporting Amazon's results on February 6, 2026, said [Amazon's ad revenue surpassed $68 billion in 2025, with $21.3 billion in Q4, up 22% year over year](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/amazon-annual-ad-revenue-passes-68b-boosted-by-full-funnel-strategy/811569/). That scale means a crowded auction for popular products.

Size does not equal fit. Most of that spend comes from product sellers and brands, and your competitors in a category like pet shampoo include large brands with big budgets.

## What Do Amazon Ads Cost?

Amazon says its Sponsored Products ads are [pay-per-click, with no monthly or upfront fees, and you set a daily budget](https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/sponsored-products). They are available to professional sellers, vendors and agencies, and products must be eligible for the Featured Offer. Selling itself costs money:

- The Professional plan is [$39.99 a month plus referral fees](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing); the Individual plan is $0.99 per item sold.
- Referral fees are a percentage of the sale price. Examples listed by Amazon include 15% for Pet Supplies, 15% for Home and Kitchen, and 8% up to $10 then 15% above $10 for Beauty, Health and Personal Care.
- Fulfilment by Amazon adds storage and delivery fees, which Amazon lists in a separate calculator.

There is no official Amazon average click cost. WisePPC, an ad-management tool, published a [benchmark of 69 accounts](https://wiseppc.com/blog/amazon-sponsored-products-benchmarks/) for April to June 2026 with a median US cost per click of $0.82 (from only 19 accounts), a median conversion rate of 5.85% and a median ACoS (ad spend as a share of ad sales) of 28.2%.

Its accounts all use an optimisation tool, so treat it as a rough guide, not a market average.

## What Does Break-Even Look Like?

Break-even ACoS equals the margin you keep before ad spend. Here is an example where every figure is an **assumption**:

| Item (assumption) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Product price: 8 oz pet shampoo | $20.00 |
| Amazon referral fee (15% for Pet Supplies) | $3.00 |
| Product cost | $6.00 |
| Packing, shipping or fulfilment | $5.00 |
| Profit before ads | $6.00 |
| Break-even ACoS | 30% |

Now add ads at the benchmark figures. Cost per order equals cost per click divided by conversion rate: $0.82 / 5.85% = $14.02. That is an ACoS of 70%, so you lose about $8 on each ad-driven order. To break even you need a conversion rate of about 13.7% ($0.82 / $6.00), more than double the median, or a better margin.

Decision rule: run ads only when your break-even ACoS comfortably exceeds the benchmark ACoS for your category, or you can lift price and margin. Otherwise, sell on your own site, in your shop, or through a local channel. The monthly seller fee also needs about seven sales at $6 profit just to cover itself.

## How Do You Test Amazon Ads Cheaply?

If the math works, start with Sponsored Products only. Do not skip the basics:

1. Make sure the listing has clear photos, a title with the product type, and a fair price. Ads cannot fix a weak listing.
2. Start with automatic targeting and a small daily budget, for example $10 (assumption).
3. After two weeks, move converting search terms into manual campaigns and add negative terms for the rest.
4. Judge by profit per order, not by sales.

## Which Better Options Exist for Service Businesses?

For appointments and local customers, spend where people search near you: a complete Google Business Profile, local search ads and reviews. ## FAQ

**Q: Can a service business advertise on Amazon?**
A: Sponsored Products promotes products in Amazon's store, so a service business generally has nothing to list. Use Google instead.

**Q: How much does it cost to start?**
A: Amazon states there are no monthly or upfront fees on Sponsored Products and no stated minimum spend, but you need a selling account, which costs $39.99 a month on the Professional plan.

**Q: What is a good ACoS?**
A: A good ACoS is one below your break-even ACoS, which equals your margin before ads. A benchmark median of 28.2% is only a reference point.

**Q: Is Amazon DSP for small businesses?**
A: Amazon's DSP page does not state a minimum spend. It offers self-service and managed-service options. For most local shops, Sponsored Products is the realistic starting point.
