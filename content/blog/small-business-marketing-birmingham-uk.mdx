## Who Lives in Birmingham?

The ONS Census 2021 area profile gives a population of about 1,144,900, up 6.7% from just over 1,073,000 in 2011, slightly above England's 6.6% growth. The median age rose from 32 to 34 and is the lowest in the West Midlands, against 40 for England. Birmingham is also the most densely populated local authority in the region.

The profile also shows the population has become more diverse: the Asian, Asian British or Asian Welsh group rose from 26.6% to 31.0%, the White group fell from 57.9% to 48.6%, and 72.0% of residents were born in England. For a business, that means customers differ a lot by area, so look at the profile for your own ward before choosing images, languages and offers.

## What Should Come First: Google Business Profile?

Complete your profile before you pay for anything. Google's help pages define the performance metrics you can watch: calls count taps on the call button (only if you list a phone number), directions count people who requested directions, website clicks count taps on the website link, and bookings count completed reservations through a booking provider.

Add accurate hours, services, photos and a phone number, and reply to reviews. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) walks through each field.

## How Do You Target Neighbourhoods Rather Than the Whole City?

Customers in a large city rarely travel across it for a coffee or a haircut, so target the areas around your premises. Google's help says radius targeting needs a radius of at least 1 km, and it recommends "Presence or interest" for Search as a general best practice, while "Presence" suits a business that only wants people physically in the area.

Add area names that real customers use as keywords and in ad text, and review the Search terms report weekly. Add negative keywords for jobs and unrelated products. Google notes that negatives do not match close variants, so add plurals. Our [UK hair salon Google Ads guide](/blog/google-ads-hair-salons-uk) shows the setup for one trade.

## What Are the Rules for Emails and Texts?

The ICO says you must not send marketing emails or texts to individuals without specific consent, with one exception, the soft opt-in. You may message your own past customers about similar products or services only if you gave them a clear chance to opt out when you collected their details and in every message. Bought-in lists and prospects are not covered.

Sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals, while companies and LLPs can be contacted, though keeping a do-not-contact list is good practice. Read our [UK email and SMS guide for salons](/blog/email-sms-marketing-uk-salons) for a workable setup.

## Is There Free Local Support?

Birmingham City Council runs the Birmingham and Solihull Growth Hub, which the council describes as a point of contact for small and medium businesses. Search results describe the advice as free, but the council's page could not be opened for this guide, so check eligibility, any funding cap and the current contact details on the council website before you rely on it.

## FAQ

### How much should a Birmingham small business spend on marketing?
No verified local figure exists. Start with an amount you can run for at least two months and judge it by new customers, not clicks.

### Is Google Ads worth it in Birmingham?
It can be if your customers are within a few miles and each customer is worth more than the cost to win them. Test a small radius campaign first.

### Can I email customers without asking?
Only past customers under the soft opt-in, and only about similar products or services with an opt-out in every message. Otherwise you need consent.

### Which area names should I use?
Use the names your customers use for the streets and districts around you, and check what people actually search in Google Ads Keyword Planner.

### Where can I get free advice?
Ask the Birmingham and Solihull Growth Hub. Confirm eligibility and current contact details with the council first.
