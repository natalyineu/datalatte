## Does Customer Experience Matter More Than the Product?

Salesforce's local marketing guide says its research found 80% of shoppers consider the experience a company provides just as important as its products or services. For a small business, that experience is often personal. The owner knows regular customers, local events and seasonal patterns, and can tailor offers to them.

Salesforce's example is a hardware store that competes with a corporate chain. It wins by hosting community events and making promotions that match what its neighbours actually need. The same logic applies to a coffee shop, a salon or a plumber. Clear hours, fast replies and staff who remember customers are part of the experience, and they show up in what people say about the business.

A useful first step is to list the three things a customer notices before they buy. Then check whether the online profiles, website and phone answers support those three things.

## Why Is Lead Confidence at a Nine-Year Low?

Borrell Associates' podcast episode "Small Businesses Have a Big Lead-Gen Problem," released November 2, 2026, reports that small businesses are focusing more on generating leads while their confidence in those efforts has dropped to a nine-year low. The episode discusses new research with vcita's CMO and argues that advertising may not be the main problem.

The episode summary points to where leads are being lost, but the figures behind that point are not reproduced in the source, so this guide does not quantify them. The practical reading is that spending more on ads will not fix a leaky process. Track each enquiry from first contact to booked job, and find the step where people drop out before increasing the budget. Service businesses that take bookings can compare their quote-to-job steps with a tool built for that workflow, such as the [Jobber versus ServiceTitan comparison](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business), which covers scheduling and dispatch for trades.

## Does Local Targeting Really Convert Better?

Salesforce compares two campaigns for a bakery with the same $1,000 budget. A broad citywide online ad reached 10,000 people and produced 100 customers, a 1% conversion rate. Targeted ads aimed at residents within a 5-mile radius reached 2,500 people and produced 200 customers, an 8% conversion rate.

The local campaign reached a quarter of the audience yet doubled the customers. That works out to $10 per customer for the citywide ad and $5 per customer for the local one. These are Salesforce's illustrative figures, not a benchmark for any particular market. Test a radius in your own area and measure results over several weeks before deciding.

The lesson holds even if the numbers differ: relevance and proximity can matter more than reach. The guide to [local marketing in Australia](/blog/local-marketing-australia-small-business-2026) covers how the same principle applies to businesses outside the US.

Assume the ads cost $4 per click (assumption), which gives 150 clicks from $600. If one in ten clicks books an appointment (assumption), that is 15 customers, or $40 per customer. Compare that figure with what a customer is worth over a year before scaling the spend.

## FAQ

### What is the biggest local marketing trend for 2026?

Based on the sources here, it is customer experience. Salesforce reports that 80% of shoppers consider the experience a company provides as important as its product or service.

### Are small businesses confident in their lead generation?

Not at a high level. Borrell Associates reports that confidence in lead-generation efforts has plunged to a nine-year low, according to its November 2026 podcast episode.

### Does targeting a smaller area really work better?

Salesforce's bakery example shows a 1% conversion rate for a citywide ad and 8% for a 5-mile radius ad. These are illustrative figures, so test your own area before relying on them.

### Where should a small business start with a limited budget?

Start with a free step: check the business profile and reply to reviews. Then test one paid channel with a fixed cap and a stop date.
