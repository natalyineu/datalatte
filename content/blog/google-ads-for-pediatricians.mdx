A pediatric practice's Google Ads cost per new family depends on three things: the price of a click, how many clicks become booking requests, and how many of those families stay. Public pediatric benchmarks are thin. A [local advertising guide from Wizerunek w Sieci](https://wizerunekwsieci.com/local-advertising-on-google-for-small-businesses-ready-to-grow/) (secondary) puts typical healthcare and dental CPC between $1.50 and $6.00, with cost per booked appointment averaging $25 to $60. The worked example below uses assumed inputs and lands at about $63 per new ongoing patient.

This guide is for independent pediatric practices in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It is not legal advice, so check anything about health data and privacy with your compliance adviser.

The guide also notes that clicks on search headlines, directions and call buttons are generally billed on a cost-per-click basis. That means you pay for the click, not for impressions, so the rate you see depends on competition for your keywords in your area. Use the range as a sanity check. If your first month sits far outside it, look first at keywords, location targeting and the landing page.

## Which Campaign Type Should a Pediatric Practice Start With?

Start with Search. Parents type specific needs such as "pediatrician near me", "pediatrician accepting new patients" or "pediatrician open saturday". Keyword targeting catches that intent directly, and it avoids depending on audience tools that may be restricted for health advertisers.

If you are weighing paid platforms beyond Google, the comparison in [Amazon Ads vs Google Ads for local business](/blog/amazon-ads-vs-google-ads-local-business) sets out the trade-offs. For a single practice, Search on Google is usually the place to begin.

Build five ad groups: new patient, convenience hours, routine care such as well-child visits and school physicals, insurance, and your brand name. Add negative keywords from day one for job, residency and symptom searches, since those parents need urgent care, not a new pediatrician.

## What Health Ad Rules Apply to a Practice?

Google restricts how health advertisers can target people, and the detail depends on which services your ads promote. Read the live advertising policy help pages for health before launch, because the rules can change and this article cannot list every case.

Until you have checked, keep the account on Search with keyword targeting, and do not build the plan around remarketing or uploaded customer lists. Write about access, such as hours, new-patient availability and insurance accepted, and avoid claims that promise an outcome. Ask your compliance adviser whether any tag or pixel may sit on a booking page, since US HIPAA and the privacy rules in the UK, Australia and Canada each treat health data differently.

## How Should You Track Families Without Health Data?

Count events that carry no health details. Use a form submission that lands on a thank-you page with a plain URL, a call from the ad with a minimum call length you choose to match a real booking call, and a forwarding number for calls from your site.

Do not upload appointment records from your practice system into the ad platform. Then close the loop by hand: ask every new family at registration how they found you and log the answer in your own systems. That gives you the real conversion count without sending health data to an ad tool.

## What Does the Budget Math Look Like?

Every input in the table below is an assumption except the calculated rows, which follow from them. Replace the assumptions with your own numbers after one month of data. The $1,000 budget sits inside the $500 to $1,500 test range from the guide above.

| Step | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $1,000 | Assumption, inside the $500 to $1,500 range |
| Cost per click | $3.00 | Assumption, inside the $1.50 to $6.00 range |
| Clicks | 333 | Budget divided by CPC |
| Click to booking request | 8% | Assumption |
| Booking requests | 26.7 | Clicks times 8% |
| Cost per request | $37.50 | Calculated |
| Request to kept visit | 80% | Assumption |
| Kept visits | 21.3 | Requests times 80% |
| Cost per kept visit | $46.88 | Calculated, compare the $25 to $60 range |
| Kept visit to ongoing patient | 75% | Assumption |
| New ongoing patients | 16.0 | Kept visits times 75% |
| Cost per new patient | $62.50 | Calculated |

The cost per kept visit of $46.88 falls inside the $25 to $60 range for cost per booked appointment, which is a useful sign the inputs are not wildly off. To set a ceiling on click price, multiply the most you would pay for one new patient by the three rates. If you would pay $100 for a new family (assumption), the ceiling is $100 × 0.08 × 0.80 × 0.75 = $4.80 a click. Front-desk habits such as answering the phone and confirming appointments move the request-to-visit and visit-to-patient rates without changing anything in Google.

## How Do Referrals Fit Alongside Paid Search?

According to Impact's referral analysis, as reported by [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-build-a-referral-program-that-actually-drives-growth) (secondary), 83% of consumers say they trust recommendations from friends and family over all other forms of advertising. For a pediatric practice, that points to a simple referral card or a question at registration asking which family recommended you.

Referrals do not replace paid search, and this article has no measured figure for how many families a referral program brings in. Track the referral answers alongside your ad answers, and compare the two over several months. ## What Should You Do This Week?

1. Pull your last 50 new families' zip or postcodes and draw your ad radius from them.
2. Set the most you would pay for one new patient, then calculate your maximum click price with the formula above.
3. List every page that collects health details, and agree with your compliance adviser that no ad tag goes on them.

## FAQ

### Can pediatricians use remarketing in Google Ads?

It depends on how Google classifies the services you promote, and health advertisers face restrictions on audience tools. Plan without remarketing until you have checked the live health policy pages and your compliance adviser has reviewed your tags.

### Can I upload appointment data to Google Ads?

Treat it as off limits. Health information is a sensitive category, so use on-site events such as form submissions and calls instead, and confirm the current rules on the platform's policy pages.

### How much should a pediatric practice spend per month?

The guide cited above describes $500 to $1,500 per month as enough to test local ads and judge cost per lead and foot traffic. Choose a figure based on what a new family is worth to your practice.

### How long before ads bring in families?

Clicks and calls can start within days. Give the campaign at least four to six weeks before judging cost per patient, since a single month rarely gives enough bookings to read.
