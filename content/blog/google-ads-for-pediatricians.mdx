LocaliQ's healthcare benchmarks put the median click for General Practice and Family Medicine at $5.47. At that price a $1,500 monthly budget buys about 274 clicks. If 8 in 100 of those clicks become a booking request, 4 in 5 requests turn up, and 3 in 4 of those families stay, you pay about $114 for each new patient. Every one of those conversion rates is my assumption, not a benchmark. The point is that a pediatric practice can work out its own number before spending a dollar.

This guide is for independent pediatric practices in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers the benchmark that exists (and the one that does not), a Search campaign setup, the Google policies that apply to health advertisers, tracking that does not put patient data into ad tools, and the math. It is not legal advice. Anything about HIPAA below should be checked with your compliance adviser.

For General Practice and Family Medicine, the same page lists a 5.50% click-through rate, an 11.63% conversion rate and a $62.80 cost per lead. There is no pediatrics line, so family medicine is the nearest proxy and your own numbers will differ. These are averages across practices, locations and bidding setups, and a "lead" here is whatever each advertiser counted as a conversion.

Use them as a sanity check: if your first month shows $15 clicks or a $300 cost per lead, something in your keywords, location or landing page needs fixing.

## Pick the Campaign Type

**Start with Search.** Parents type exactly what they need: "pediatrician near me", "pediatrician accepting new patients", "child doctor open Saturday". Search catches that moment, and you can target by keyword rather than by audience, which matters for the policy reasons below.

**Local Services Ads are worth checking, not assuming.** Eligibility depends on your category and country, and Google's help pages do not state that pediatric practices are covered everywhere. Check in your account, and if you qualify, see our [Local Services Ads guide](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide).

**Skip remarketing for now.** The next section explains why.

## The Policies That Actually Apply to a Pediatric Practice

Three Google policy areas matter.

### 1. Health is a sensitive interest category

Google's [Personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) lists Health among its sensitive interest categories. Advertisers in those categories "are unable to use advertiser-curated audiences". Google's [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855) page names the unsupported tools: Customer Match, your data segments (website-visitor remarketing), audience expansion and lookalike segments. Predefined Google audiences such as in-market segments, affinity audiences and location targeting stay available.

What counts as Health? The page lists physical or mental health conditions, products, services or procedures for managing chronic conditions, intimate-body health, invasive procedures such as cosmetic surgery, and disabilities, with examples such as diabetes treatment, CPAP machines and counseling for depression or addiction. Routine primary care, well-child visits and school physicals are not named. The list is described as non-exhaustive, so Google does not tell us either way. My practical reading, not Google's statement:

- If your ads or landing pages focus on a condition (asthma, ADHD assessments, autism evaluations, anxiety or other behavioral health), assume the Health restrictions apply and plan a Search-only account without remarketing.
- If you advertise general services such as well-child visits and new-patient availability, you still may find audience tools restricted, so do not build your plan on remarketing.
- Keywords are not audiences. The restriction on that page is about advertiser-curated audiences, so keyword-targeted Search ads remain your main tool.

Also note: Google says users under 18 are not eligible for personalized advertising of any kind. Your audience is parents, which is a separate thing, but it is another reason to target the adult searcher by what they type.

### 2. Do not upload patient conversions

Google's [Customer data policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7475709) list "Health or medical information, like purchases of medical services, prescription drugs or medical devices" among sensitive categories and say you may not upload conversion information related to them. They also say these conversions cannot be used for measurement in enhanced conversions. In practice: do not push appointment records from your practice management system or EHR into Google Ads, and do not turn on enhanced conversions for health pages. Count generic events on your website instead, such as a "booking request submitted" or a call of a minimum length.

### 3. The Healthcare and medicines policy targets specific services

Google's [Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) is about things like online prescribing and dispensing of prescription drugs (online pharmacies and telemedicine providers), restricted drug terms, pharmaceutical manufacturers, addiction services, clinical trial recruitment and health insurance. An ordinary in-person pediatric office is not one of the listed items. If you add a telehealth service that prescribes online, read that page first, because it may require certification and local registration. Google also says advertisers are expected to know the law in the places they target.

### Claims

Google's [Unreliable claims policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936857) bars claims that present an unlikely result as the expected outcome "even if this result is possible". It also lists harmful health claims and anti-vaccine advocacy as prohibited. Write ads about services and access ("Same-day sick visits", "Evening hours"), not outcomes or medical advice.

<Callout type="warning" title="HIPAA and your tags">
Google says in its [Analytics HIPAA help page](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/13297105) that it makes no representations that Google Analytics satisfies HIPAA, that it does not offer Business Associate Agreements for it, and that HIPAA-regulated customers must not expose protected health information to Google. It says authenticated pages are likely covered by HIPAA and unauthenticated pages related to health care services are also more likely to be covered. In June 2024 a federal court in Texas vacated part of HHS's online tracking bulletin, the part about IP addresses on public pages about health conditions or providers, according to [Holland and Knight's summary](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/06/american-hospital-assn-v-becerra-are-tracking-tools-ok-again). The rest of the bulletin was not vacated, per the same summary, and patient portals were not part of the challenge. That account is second-hand, from a law firm summary rather than the HHS bulletin itself. Keep tags off portals and booking pages where parents type health details, and ask your compliance adviser before any pixel goes live.
</Callout>

## Set Up the Campaign

### Location and schedule
Set a radius your families will actually travel, often a handful of miles, and check your last 50 new patients' zip or postcodes. Run ads when someone answers the phone.

### Keywords and match types
Group by what the parent wants:

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New patient | "pediatrician near me", [pediatrician your town], "pediatrician accepting new patients" | Phrase + exact |
| Convenience | "pediatrician open saturday", "same day pediatric appointment" | Phrase |
| Routine care | "well child visit", "school physical", "kids vaccinations" | Phrase + exact |
| Insurance | "pediatrician that takes [insurer]" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your practice name] | Exact |

Add negatives from day one: `jobs`, `salary`, `residency`, `fellowship`, `nurse`, `course`, `free`, `pdf`, `symptoms`, `rash`, `fever`, `home remedy`, `online`. Symptom searches rarely become bookings and put you in front of a parent who needs urgent care, not a new pediatrician. See our [negative keywords guide](/blog/negative-keywords-google-ads) for campaign-level and shared-list setup.

### Ads
Be specific and verifiable: `Pediatrician in [Town]. New Patients Welcome`, `Same-Day Sick Visits`, `Evening and Saturday Hours`, `Most Insurance Accepted` (only if true and listed on your site). Add a call asset, a location asset linked to your Business Profile, and sitelinks to New Patients, Services, Insurance and Hours.

### Landing page
Send clicks to a page that matches the ad: the offer in the headline, hours, insurance list, a map, a click-to-call button and a short form (parent name, child's age band, phone or email, and no symptoms or diagnosis). Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) cover layout.

## Tracking Without Health Data

Track three things:

1. **Form submissions** on a thank-you page that carries no health details in the URL.
2. **Calls from the ad.** Google's [website call conversion help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) says a call counts only if it is "longer than a minimum length you set", which filters out misdials. Choose a length that matches a real booking call. The 30 to 60 seconds you will see suggested is my starting point, not Google's.
3. **Calls from your site** via a forwarding number. See [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).

Then close the loop by hand. Ask every new family at registration how they found you and log it. That is your real conversion data, and it stays inside your practice systems.

## The Math

Take a $1,500 monthly budget. Every row except the base CPC is an assumption; the base CPC of $5.47 is LocaliQ's family medicine figure above. Replace them after one month of data.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget (assumption) | $1,500 | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Cost per click | $7.00 (assumption) | $5.47 (LocaliQ) | $4.50 (assumption) |
| Clicks | 214 | 274 | 333 |
| Click to booking request (assumption) | 4% | 8% | 11.63% (LocaliQ conversion rate) |
| Booking requests | 8.6 | 21.9 | 38.8 |
| Request to kept visit (assumption) | 70% | 80% | 85% |
| Kept visit to ongoing patient (assumption) | 60% | 75% | 85% |
| New patients | 3.6 | 13.2 | 28.0 |
| Ad cost per new patient | $417 | $114 | $54 |

Note the Strong column is better than any benchmark here, so do not plan around it. Two uses of this table:

- **Your maximum click price.** Max CPC = most you will pay per new patient x request rate x show rate x stay rate. In the Base column the product of the three rates is 0.08 x 0.80 x 0.75 = 0.048. If a new family is worth enough that you will pay $200 for one, the ceiling is $200 x 0.048 = $9.60 a click.
- **The levers you control.** Answering the phone and confirming appointments move the second and third rates without touching Google. Going from 70% to 85% kept visits is a front-desk fix.

Remember that value to the practice depends on your payer mix and how long families stay. Work that out from your own billing before you set a ceiling.

## Review Weekly

Read the search terms report and add negatives, pause keywords that spent about twice your target cost per booking request with none, test one new headline every two weeks, and check which hours calls go unanswered. [Google Ads for dentists](/blog/google-ads-for-dentists) covers a similar patient-acquisition funnel.

## Do This Today

1. Pull your last 50 new families' zip or postcodes and draw your ad radius from them.
2. Decide the most you will pay for one new patient and compute your maximum CPC with the formula above.
3. Write down which pages collect health details, and agree with your compliance adviser that no ad tag goes on them.
4. Build one Search campaign with the five ad groups above, the negative list, and no remarketing or Customer Match.
5. Set up form and call conversions (minimum call length chosen), and start a "how did you find us" field at registration.

## FAQ

**Q: Can pediatricians use remarketing in Google Ads?**
A: It depends on whether your ads fall in Google's Health category. Google lists website-visitor data segments, Customer Match, lookalikes and audience expansion as unsupported for sensitive categories. Google does not say whether routine pediatric care counts, so plan without remarketing.

**Q: Is Google Analytics HIPAA compliant?**
A: Google says it makes no representations that Analytics satisfies HIPAA and does not offer a Business Associate Agreement for it. Take advice before using it on any page that collects health information.

**Q: Can I upload appointment data to Google Ads?**
A: Google's customer data policy says you may not upload conversion information related to sensitive categories, which include health or medical information. Use on-site events such as form submits and calls instead.

**Q: How much should I spend per month?**
A: There is no official figure. At LocaliQ's $5.47 median family medicine click, $1,500 buys about 274 clicks, and $500 about 91, which is thin for learning. Work backward from what a new family is worth to you.

**Q: How long before ads bring in families?**
A: Clicks and calls can start within days. Give it four to six weeks, my rule of thumb, before judging cost per patient.
