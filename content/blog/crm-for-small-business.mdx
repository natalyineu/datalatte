The best CRM for a small business is the one your team will use every day. For most local businesses such as cafes, salons, pet groomers and studios, that means starting with a free CRM like HubSpot or Zoho, or with the client records built into your booking software, and moving to a heavier tool only when you outgrow it. HubSpot's free CRM covers up to two users and 1,000 contacts, and Zoho's free edition covers up to three users (both checked Oct 2026), so a small team can start at no cost.

Here is the scene a CRM is supposed to fix. A regular you have not seen in four months used to come in every other Friday. Nobody noticed she stopped. No message went out, no "we saved your spot" text, nothing. She did not leave angry. She just drifted. A spreadsheet will not tap you on the shoulder about that. A CRM with one simple rule ("no visit in 90 days, send a nudge") will.

This guide compares five options, shows the arithmetic of whether one pays for itself, and lists the mistakes that make small businesses abandon their CRM within a few months.

## What a CRM does for a local business

A CRM (customer relationship management) tool keeps contact details, visit history, notes and follow-ups in one place. For you that means remembering a regular's drink order, sending a rebooking reminder to a salon client, or seeing which channel brought someone in. If you run on spreadsheets, paper notebooks and memory, even a simple CRM is a step up.

## How to choose

- **Ease of use:** can your whole team use it after one short training session?
- **Plan limits:** what does the free or entry plan include, and what triggers an upgrade?
- **Integrations:** does it connect to your booking system, payments, email and accounting?
- **Mobile app:** can you log a note from your phone at the counter?
- **Scale:** will it fit when you add staff or a second location?

## The math: does a CRM pay for itself?

**Example only: every figure below is an assumption, not a result from a real business.** Say a salon has 400 contacts. Assume 25% have not visited in 90 days, a reminder gets 8% of them to rebook, and the average visit is $60.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Lapsed clients | 400 x 25% | 100 |
| Rebook after reminder | 100 x 8% | 8 bookings |
| Revenue recovered | 8 x $60 | $480 |
| Cost: free plan | HubSpot free, under 1,000 contacts | $0 |
| Cost: paid plan | $9 x 2 users = $18 a month, billed annually (so $216 up front) | $18 a month |
| Revenue per $1 spent (paid plan) | $480 / $18 | about 27 |

Even if the rebook rate is only 2%, that is 2 bookings and $120, still above $18. If your list is half the size, halve the revenue. Swap in your own contact count, lapse rate and ticket. And note the real cost is not the license, it is the 15 minutes a week someone must spend sending the nudge.

## Common mistakes

**1. Buying the biggest plan.** Start free or at entry level and upgrade when you hit a real limit.

**2. Skipping training.** Run one session: log an interaction, schedule a follow-up, tag a contact. Name one person as CRM champion.

**3. Importing dirty data.** Remove duplicates, standardize names and phones, delete contacts who have not engaged in a long time. A clean small list beats a large messy one and protects email deliverability. Mind the caps: HubSpot's free plan stops at 1,000 contacts.

**4. Never looking at the data.** Pick one number to review weekly: new contacts, rebooking rate or lapsed customers. A recurring 15-minute check turns storage into decisions.

**5. Automating everything on day one.** Start with an appointment reminder. Add a post-visit thank-you with a review link next.

## Do this today: a 30-minute setup

1. **Import a lean list** (5 minutes): name, email, phone, last visit date. Nothing else.
2. **Create 5 stages** (5 minutes): New Lead, First Visit, Repeat Customer, Regular, At Risk.
3. **Write one template** (10 minutes): a thank-you with a link to your booking or Google review page.
4. **Set one automation** (5 minutes): send that message after the first visit.
5. **Book a weekly slot** (5 minutes): 15 minutes every week to review new contacts, repeats and at-risk customers.

## Connect it to what you already use

- **Booking system:** sync bookings so nobody retypes data. Many tools have native integrations or work through automation tools such as Zapier; check plan limits.
- **Email and SMS:** keep one source of truth for subscribers so unsubscribes stay in sync. See our [email and SMS marketing service](/services/email-sms).
- **Payments or accounting:** linking purchases to customers shows lifetime value.

## Metrics that matter

Skip total contacts. Track:

- **Response time to new enquiries:** reply while the lead is still comparing options.
- **New contacts per week:** shows whether marketing works.
- **Repeat customer rate:** the share who return within a set period.
- **Customer lifetime value:** average spend per visit x visits per year x years as a customer.
- **Hours saved:** time per month you no longer spend on manual follow-up.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best free CRM for a small business?**
A: HubSpot CRM (up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts) and Zoho CRM (up to 3 users) both offer free plans with no expiry, per their own sites as of October 2026. If you use booking software, its built-in client records may cover your needs at no extra cost.

**Q: How much does a CRM cost for a small business?**
A: Prices range from free to paid plans charged per user per month; for example, Freshsales lists its entry plan at $9 per user per month billed annually. Add-ons can raise the total, so compare the plan you would actually use rather than the headline price.

**Q: Do I need a CRM if I already have booking software?**
A: Not always. Many salon and studio booking apps include client records, reminders and basic marketing. Add a separate CRM when you need more advanced email automation, reporting or lead tracking.

**Q: How long does it take to set up a CRM?**
A: A basic setup can take a few hours, and most small businesses can be live within a few days if they start with a small, clean contact list and one automation.

**Q: Can a CRM integrate with my other tools?**
A: Most CRMs integrate with popular email, booking, payment and accounting tools, either natively or through automation platforms. Check each vendor's integration list for the tools you use.
