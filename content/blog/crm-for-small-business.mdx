The best CRM for a small business is the one your team will actually use every day. For most local businesses such as cafés, salons, pet groomers and studios, that means starting with a free or low-cost CRM like HubSpot or Zoho, or using the CRM features already built into your booking software, and moving to a heavier tool only when you outgrow it. Pricing and free-plan limits change often, so check each vendor's current plans before you commit.

This guide compares five common options, explains how to choose, and covers the mistakes that make small businesses abandon their CRM within a few months.

## What a CRM Does for a Local Business

A CRM (Customer Relationship Management) tool keeps customer contact details, visit history, notes and follow-ups in one place. For a local business that can mean remembering a regular's drink order, sending a rebooking reminder to a salon client, or tracking which marketing channel brought someone in. If you currently rely on spreadsheets, paper notebooks and memory, even a simple CRM is an improvement.

## How to Choose a CRM

Consider these factors:

- **Ease of use:** Can your whole team use it after a short training session?
- **Price and plan limits:** Check what the free or entry plan includes and what triggers an upgrade.
- **Integrations:** Does it connect to your booking system, payment tool, email platform and accounting software?
- **Customer support:** How easy is it to get help?
- **Mobile app:** Can you log a note from your phone at the counter?
- **Scalability:** Will it still fit when you add staff or a second location?

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Buying the most expensive plan.** Start with a free tier or an entry plan. If you are a solo operator or have a small team, you probably do not need enterprise features. Upgrade only when you hit a real limit.

**2. Skipping staff training.** Block out a short onboarding session, show everyone how to log an interaction, schedule a follow-up and tag a contact, and name one team member as the CRM champion. If staff keep notes in a shared document instead, the CRM will go unused.

**3. Importing dirty data.** Before importing, remove duplicates, standardize name and phone fields, and delete contacts who have not engaged in a long time. A clean, smaller list beats a large messy one, and it protects your email deliverability.

**4. Never looking at the data.** Pick one metric to review each week, such as new contacts added, rebooking rate or lapsed customers. A recurring 15-minute weekly check turns a CRM from storage into a decision tool.

**5. Automating everything on day one.** Start with one simple automation, usually an appointment reminder. Add a post-visit thank-you with a review link next, then more once the first ones work.

## A 30-Minute Setup Plan

1. **Import a lean contact list.** Name, email, phone and last visit date are enough to start.
2. **Create simple stages** such as New Lead, First Visit, Repeat Customer, Regular and At Risk.
3. **Write one email or text template,** for example a thank-you message with a link to your booking page or Google review page.
4. **Set up one automation,** such as sending the thank-you message after the first visit.
5. **Schedule a weekly check-in** to look at new contacts, repeat visits and at-risk customers.

## Connect Your CRM to the Tools You Already Use

- **Booking system:** Sync new bookings into your CRM so you do not re-type data. Many tools offer native integrations or connect through automation tools such as Zapier (check current free-plan limits).
- **Email and SMS:** Keep one source of truth for subscribers so unsubscribes and updated emails stay in sync. See our [email and SMS marketing service](/services/email-sms).
- **Accounting or payments:** Connecting purchases to customer records helps you see lifetime value.
- **Mobile app:** Log a quick note after a customer interaction while it is fresh.

## Metrics That Matter

Skip vanity metrics like total contacts and track these instead:

- **Response time to new enquiries:** Faster replies generally convert better.
- **New contacts per week:** Shows whether your marketing is working.
- **Repeat customer rate:** The share of customers who come back within a set period.
- **Customer lifetime value:** Average spend per visit times visits per year times years as a customer.
- **Time saved by automation:** Hours per month you no longer spend on manual follow-up.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best free CRM for a small business?**
A: HubSpot CRM and Zoho CRM both offer free plans, though limits on users, features and contacts change, so check current terms. If you use booking software, its built-in client records may also cover your needs at no extra cost.

**Q: How much does a CRM cost for a small business?**
A: Prices range from free plans to paid plans charged per user per month, and add-ons can raise the total. Compare the plan you would actually use, including any extras, rather than the headline price.

**Q: Do I need a CRM if I already have booking software?**
A: Not always. Many salon and studio booking apps include client records, reminders and basic marketing. Add a separate CRM when you need more advanced email automation, reporting or lead tracking.

**Q: How long does it take to set up a CRM?**
A: A basic setup can take a few hours, and most small businesses can be live within a few days if they start with a small, clean contact list and one automation.

**Q: Can a CRM integrate with my other tools?**
A: Most CRMs integrate with popular email, booking, payment and accounting tools, either natively or through automation platforms. Check each vendor's integration list for the tools you use.
