The best CRM for a small business is the one your team will use every day. For most local businesses such as cafés, salons, pet groomers and studios, that means starting with a free CRM like HubSpot or Zoho, or with the client records built into your booking software, and moving to a heavier tool only when you outgrow it. HubSpot's free CRM covers up to two users and 1,000 contacts, and Zoho's free edition covers up to three users (both checked Oct 2026), so a small team can start at no cost.

This guide compares five common options, explains how to choose, and covers the mistakes that make small businesses abandon their CRM within a few months.

## What a CRM does for a local business

A CRM (customer relationship management) tool keeps contact details, visit history, notes and follow-ups in one place. For a local business that can mean remembering a regular's drink order, sending a rebooking reminder to a salon client, or tracking which channel brought someone in. If you currently rely on spreadsheets, paper notebooks and memory, even a simple CRM is an improvement.

## How to choose a CRM

- **Ease of use:** Can your whole team use it after a short training session?
- **Plan limits:** Check what the free or entry plan includes and what triggers an upgrade.
- **Integrations:** Does it connect to your booking system, payment tool, email platform and accounting software?
- **Support:** How easy is it to get help?
- **Mobile app:** Can you log a note from your phone at the counter?
- **Scalability:** Will it still fit when you add staff or a second location?

## Common mistakes to avoid

**1. Buying the most expensive plan.** Start with a free tier or entry plan, and upgrade only when you hit a real limit.

**2. Skipping staff training.** Run a short onboarding session, show everyone how to log an interaction, schedule a follow-up and tag a contact, and name one person as the CRM champion.

**3. Importing dirty data.** Remove duplicates, standardize name and phone fields, and delete contacts who have not engaged in a long time. A clean, smaller list beats a large messy one, and it protects email deliverability. Mind the free-plan contact caps too: HubSpot's free plan stops at 1,000 contacts.

**4. Never looking at the data.** Pick one metric to review weekly, such as new contacts, rebooking rate or lapsed customers. A recurring 15-minute check turns a CRM from storage into a decision tool.

**5. Automating everything on day one.** Start with an appointment reminder, add a post-visit thank-you with a review link next, then build from there.

## A 30-minute setup plan

1. **Import a lean contact list.** Name, email, phone and last visit date are enough.
2. **Create simple stages** such as New Lead, First Visit, Repeat Customer, Regular and At Risk.
3. **Write one template,** for example a thank-you message with a link to your booking or Google review page.
4. **Set up one automation,** such as sending that message after the first visit.
5. **Schedule a weekly check-in** on new contacts, repeat visits and at-risk customers.

## Connect your CRM to the tools you already use

- **Booking system:** Sync new bookings into your CRM so you do not re-type data. Many tools offer native integrations or connect through automation tools such as Zapier; check current plan limits.
- **Email and SMS:** Keep one source of truth for subscribers so unsubscribes stay in sync. See our [email and SMS marketing service](/services/email-sms).
- **Accounting or payments:** Linking purchases to customer records shows lifetime value.
- **Mobile app:** Log a quick note while the interaction is fresh.

## Metrics that matter

Skip vanity metrics like total contacts and track:

- **Response time to new enquiries:** Reply quickly while the lead is still comparing options.
- **New contacts per week:** Shows whether your marketing is working.
- **Repeat customer rate:** The share of customers who return within a set period.
- **Customer lifetime value:** Average spend per visit times visits per year times years as a customer.
- **Time saved by automation:** Hours per month you no longer spend on manual follow-up.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best free CRM for a small business?**
A: HubSpot CRM (up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts) and Zoho CRM (up to 3 users) both offer free plans with no expiry, per their own sites as of October 2026. If you use booking software, its built-in client records may cover your needs at no extra cost.

**Q: How much does a CRM cost for a small business?**
A: Prices range from free to paid plans charged per user per month; for example, Freshsales lists its entry plan at $9 per user per month billed annually. Add-ons can raise the total, so compare the plan you would actually use rather than the headline price.

**Q: Do I need a CRM if I already have booking software?**
A: Not always. Many salon and studio booking apps include client records, reminders and basic marketing. Add a separate CRM when you need more advanced email automation, reporting or lead tracking.

**Q: How long does it take to set up a CRM?**
A: A basic setup can take a few hours, and most small businesses can be live within a few days if they start with a small, clean contact list and one automation.

**Q: Can a CRM integrate with my other tools?**
A: Most CRMs integrate with popular email, booking, payment and accounting tools, either natively or through automation platforms. Check each vendor's integration list for the tools you use.
