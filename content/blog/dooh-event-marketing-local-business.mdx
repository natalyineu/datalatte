To use digital out-of-home (DOOH) screens around a local event, choose screens near the venue and on the routes into it, schedule the ad for the hours before and during the event, and measure results with a unique offer code or landing page. [AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/answers/dooh-campaigns/how-much-does-dooh-digital-out-of-home-advertising-cost) lists programmatic DOOH CPMs at $3 to $20, depending on inventory quality and location. Some self-serve sellers have no monthly minimum, so a small test is possible.

## How Do You Target Screens Around an Event?

Start with the venue and the roads people use to reach it. Pick screens near the venue entrance, the main approach road, and any transit stop on the route. Screens in these spots are the ones most likely to be seen by people heading to the event.

Self-serve platforms let you set the schedule as well as the place. [Blip](https://www.blipbillboards.com/how-it-works/) describes its setup as choosing locations, a schedule, a spend amount and uploading artwork. Those four choices map directly onto event timing: the location is the route, and the schedule is the hours before doors open.

For the wider planning logic, see [event marketing for local business](/blog/event-marketing-for-local-business). Plan from the public event calendar, and confirm the start and end times with the organiser before you set the schedule.

## How Much Lead Time Should You Allow?

None of the sources behind this guide set a booking lead time, so check each seller's current help page for creative deadlines and review rules. Do not assume a same-week launch is possible.

Work backwards from the event date. Confirm the seller and its minimum first, then approve the artwork, then set the schedule and locations, and only then pay. Build in a buffer for artwork review, because a rejected file can cost the window you planned for.

Seasonal events need the same discipline. If the event is tied to a holiday, plan it on a calendar well ahead, as described in [Chinese New Year marketing for local business](/blog/chinese-new-year-marketing-local-business).

## What Does DOOH Cost for an Event?

Costs depend on the channel you buy. [AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/answers/dooh-campaigns/how-much-does-dooh-digital-out-of-home-advertising-cost) lists monthly digital billboard costs from $1,200 in smaller markets to $15,000 or more in major cities. Indoor mall screens typically run $750 to $2,500 per month.

Programmatic campaigns carry a different floor. AdQuick says they generally require a minimum spend of $10,000 to $50,000. For small budgets, the same source points to SMB-focused platforms that bring minimums down to $1,500 to $3,000 per campaign.

[Blip](https://www.blipbillboards.com/pricing/) states there is no required minimum and no contract, and that you can start with a few dollars per day to test a single market. Its pricing page gives a starting rate of $0.01 per 8-second play. Check the price for your own markets before you commit, since starting rates rarely reflect the mix of screens a specific event needs.

## How Are DOOH Impressions Counted?

Impressions are not the same as people. [AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/answers/programmatic-dooh/what-is-the-best-affordable-programmatic-dooh-platform-for-small-businesses) says its CPMs are delivered-impression CPMs, computed from delivered ad plays rather than full-loop traffic. It adds that these figures are not directly comparable with advertiser-share CPMs on directly booked digital inventory.

That matters when you compare quotes. AdQuick's marketplace reports a median delivered-impression CPM of $8.00 for roadside digital venues, with a range of $7.50 to $8.00, based on July 2025 to June 2026 data. A quote from a different seller may count impressions on another basis.

Ask each seller two questions: is the CPM based on delivered plays, and who verifies the delivery? Only compare CPMs that use the same basis.

## How Do You Measure an Event Campaign?

Give the campaign its own offer code, QR code or landing page, so redemptions can be tied to the event ads. Use a code that appears only on the event creative, so a customer who uses it is clearly responding to that campaign.

Compare event-week results with a similar week without ads. The event itself will raise footfall and sales, so the comparison shows the combined effect of the event and the ads, not the ads alone. A recurring event, such as a weekly market, makes this easier because you can run one week with screens and one without.

For more on tracking, see [programmatic DOOH strategy for local business](/blog/programmatic-dooh-strategy-local-business).

## Do People Look at the Screen Long Enough?

Attention is short. [Take 10 Media](https://take10media.com/blog/self-serve-dooh-trends-2026-local-brands/) claims that highway billboard viewers often have dwell times of only 3 to 5 seconds. That is a vendor claim, not an independent measure, but it is a reasonable design brief.

Write the creative for a glance. Use one headline, one offer and a clear logo. If the event has a specific time, put that time in the headline, because a passing driver cannot read a paragraph.

The same vendor says its self-serve option is pay-as-you-go and can be paused or adjusted at any time. For an event, that means you can stop the campaign once the event ends or if early results are poor.

## FAQ

### Can a small business afford DOOH?
Yes, on some platforms. Blip says it has no required minimum and no contract, and SMB-focused platforms list campaign minimums of $1,500 to $3,000. Managed programmatic campaigns usually need far more. Compare the minimum before you plan.

### How far ahead should I book?
None of the sources behind this guide give a booking lead time. Confirm creative deadlines and review rules with the seller before you commit to a date.

### Can I measure store visits?
Use an offer code, QR code or landing page as the main measure. Store-visit data depends on the seller's verification method, so ask how visits are measured before you rely on them.

### Can I pause the campaign during the event?
Some self-serve sellers allow pausing or adjusting at any time. Take 10 Media describes this as a feature of its self-serve option. Check that your chosen seller offers the same flexibility in its current terms.
