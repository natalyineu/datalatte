DOOH (digital out-of-home) advertising works for restaurants by putting your dish, offer and location on digital screens near your restaurant at the moments people decide where to eat, such as lunch and dinner. Programmatic DOOH is typically bought by CPM, often in the $4 to $10 range for local campaigns, and many platforms ask for roughly $500 to $1,000 minimums, so a test budget of $400 to $800 a month is realistic. Prices vary by market and screen type, so check current quotes.

Restaurant advertising has a timing problem that most channels can't solve. Google Ads reach people actively searching. Social media reaches people passively scrolling. Neither reliably catches someone in the short window before they decide where to eat.

DOOH can. A digital screen at a transit stop near your restaurant, showing your lunch special at 11:30am, reaches someone in precisely the right mindset — on the move, starting to think about food, within walking distance. A street-level digital billboard at 6pm showing your dinner menu and reservation QR code intercepts the dinner-decision window.

That's the DOOH advantage for restaurants: contextual timing at the physical moment of decision.

---

## Why DOOH Works Differently for Restaurants Than Other Categories

Most local businesses use advertising to capture intent (Google) or build awareness over time (Meta). Restaurants need both — but they also need a third thing: **impulse capture at the moment of hunger**.

A dental clinic doesn't benefit from someone seeing its ad at 12pm on a Tuesday. A restaurant absolutely does. The lunch decision happens within a 30-minute window, the dinner decision within a 60-minute window, and both are heavily influenced by what someone sees in that window.

DOOH screens in the right locations — transit stops, office lobbies, street-level near your block — are physically inside that decision window in a way that digital channels can't replicate. The person isn't on their phone; they're in the physical world, about to make a physical decision about where to eat.

This is why restaurants are often considered a natural fit for DOOH. The product (food), the timing (hunger), and the medium (physical location) align in a way that's unusual in advertising. Results still vary, so test small and measure.

## Best Screen Placements for Restaurants

Not all DOOH screens are equal for restaurants. Placement matters a great deal: the right screen in the wrong location converts poorly, while the right screen close to your restaurant can drive measurable walk-ins.

| Screen Type | Distance from Restaurant | Best For | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transit stops | 100–500m | Lunch, quick-service | Commuters in decision window |
| Street-level digital billboards | 1–3 block radius | All types | High repeat for regulars |
| Office building lobbies | Within 400m | Business lunch, higher-end | Professional lunch crowd |
| Gym lobby / changing room | 300m–1km | Health menus, smoothie bars | Post-workout hunger |
| Gas station pump screens | 500m–2km | Casual, family dining | 3–5 min captive dwell time |
| Parking garage exits | Adjacent blocks | Dinner service | Leaving office at dinner time |
| Shopping centre corridors | Same complex or adjacent | Family dining, fast casual | Shoppers extending their trip |
| Hotel lobbies | Within 1km | Brunch, dinner, business meals | Visitors unfamiliar with area |

**The directional vs. awareness split:** Most restaurant DOOH campaigns combine two layers:

1. **Directional** (100–500m radius): Screens close enough to physically direct foot traffic. "3 blocks north" or "turn right at the light." These work for lunch walk-ins and spontaneous dinner decisions.

2. **Awareness** (1–3km radius): Screens that build neighborhood recognition so your name surfaces when people are choosing where to eat later. These drive reservations, delivery orders, and planned visits.

Budget split recommendation: 60% directional, 40% awareness. Adjust based on whether your business is more walk-in-dependent or reservation-dependent.

## DOOH Creative for Restaurants: What Actually Works

Restaurant DOOH creative has one job: make people hungry and tell them how close you are. Everything else is secondary.

### What Works

**Lead with food photography.** Your best dish, full bleed, high saturation. Not the restaurant exterior — the dish. A good food photo on a large screen is one of the most effective appetite triggers in advertising.

**One message, large type.** People see DOOH screens for 1–3 seconds while walking or at a transit stop. You have time for: dish photo + restaurant name + one line of context. That's it.

**Proximity and timing cues:** "2 blocks north," "Open until 11pm," "Lunch special until 3pm." These make the ad immediately actionable rather than aspirational.

**QR codes work at pedestrian-level screens.** Transit stops, gas station pumps, office lobby screens — anywhere with 10+ seconds of dwell time. Link to your OpenTable/Resy/direct booking page with UTM tracking.

### What Doesn't Work

- Long descriptions or menus — no one reads them at full walking speed
- Multiple messages — pick one call to action per creative
- Stock food photography — it reads as generic immediately
- Small text — design for someone 5 meters away in motion
- Logos only without a food image — missed appetite opportunity

### Daypart Creative Rotation

The biggest DOOH advantage restaurants underuse: **dynamic creative by time of day.**

Most programmatic DOOH platforms support scheduling different creatives at different times automatically. Use it:

| Time Window | Creative Message | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| 6–9am | Coffee, breakfast menu, "Open from 7am" | Morning commuters |
| 10am–2pm | Lunch special, today's feature dish, "3 min walk" | Lunch decision window |
| 2–5pm | Afternoon coffee + pastry, happy hour teaser | Bridge period |
| 5–9pm | Dinner atmosphere shot, reservation CTA, QR code | Dinner decision window |
| Friday/Saturday | Weekend special, event night, "Book a table" | Planned dining |

Setting this up takes 20 minutes in your DSP and doubles creative relevance throughout the day.

## How to Buy Restaurant DOOH: Three Routes

### 1. Programmatic DSP (Recommended for Local Restaurants)

Self-serve platforms that let you buy digital screen inventory across a network of screens by location, audience, and time of day.

Best options for local restaurants:
- **Vistar Media** — strongest attribution tools, good restaurant category
- **Place Exchange** — large screen network, good transit coverage
- **StackAdapt** — combined DOOH + digital retargeting, strong local features
- **Hivestack** — strong in international markets (UK, Australia, Canada)

These platforms let you target by GPS radius, screen type, and time of day, with minimum campaign budgets of $500–1,000. You upload your creative, set your targeting, and the platform buys screen impressions across its inventory.

### 2. Outdoor Advertising Operators (Direct Buy)

Companies like Clear Channel, Lamar, or JCDecaux own their own screen networks. A direct buy with a local rep gives you access to premium locations with guaranteed placements.

**When direct makes sense:** You want a specific, high-profile screen location (a busy intersection, a transit station with high foot traffic) and you're willing to commit to a 4-week minimum contract.

**When programmatic makes sense:** You want flexibility, dayparting, and the ability to swap creative instantly. For most restaurants, programmatic is the better starting point.

### 3. Digital Agency with DOOH Access

A local marketing agency with DSP relationships can manage DOOH alongside your Google Ads and Meta campaigns — useful if you want a single point of contact but adds a management layer and fee.

## Budget Benchmarks for Restaurant DOOH

The table below is an illustration based on typical CPMs of roughly $5 to $8. Your actual impressions and screen coverage depend on your market, so ask the platform for a forecast before you commit.

| Monthly Budget | Approx. Impressions (at $5–$8 CPM) | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| $400 | 50,000–80,000 | Testing a tight directional layer |
| $800 | 100,000–160,000 | Directional plus local awareness |
| $1,500 | 190,000–300,000 | Wider neighbourhood coverage |
| $3,000+ | 375,000+ | Established restaurant or expansion |

For a restaurant just starting DOOH, **$400–800/month** is a reasonable test budget. At a $5 CPM, $500 buys about 100,000 impressions. Concentrated in a small radius around your restaurant, that can give meaningful frequency with your actual walk-in audience.

The critical question is whether your customer lifetime value justifies the acquisition cost. For example, if a typical customer visits twice a month at $35 per visit, that is roughly $840 a year in revenue (use your own numbers). If DOOH brings in even a handful of repeat customers, the spend can pay back, but only measurement will tell you.

## Integrating DOOH with Your Digital Channels

DOOH works best as part of a channel mix, not in isolation. Here's how to connect it:

### DOOH + Google Business Profile

This is the most common and effective integration. The sequence:

1. DOOH ad creates awareness: "Il Forno — Fresh pasta, 2 blocks north"
2. Someone searches "Italian restaurant near me" on their phone later
3. Your Google Business Profile appears (optimised, 4.8 stars, photos, menu)
4. They navigate to you

DOOH plants the name. Google Maps harvests the intent. Before running any DOOH campaign, ensure your Google Business Profile is fully optimised — photos, hours, menu, regular posting, 30+ reviews. The advertising spend is wasted if the GBP isn't ready to convert the search.

### DOOH + Google Ads

DOOH exposure typically increases branded search volume — people who see your billboard are more likely to search your name specifically. This improves your branded Google Ads CTR and can reduce your non-branded CPC as brand familiarity increases.

Track this by monitoring branded search impressions and clicks in Google Ads during and after DOOH campaigns.

### DOOH + Meta Retargeting

Set up website custom audiences in Meta Ads. After someone visits your website (perhaps from a QR code they scanned on a DOOH screen), retarget them with a follow-up social ad. DOOH creates initial contact; Meta closes with a specific offer or reminder.

## Measuring DOOH Campaign Results for Restaurants

Attribution is harder with DOOH than with Google Ads — you can't directly track a walk-in to a specific screen impression. But several methods give useful signal:

**QR code tracking:** Include a unique QR code in creatives on screens with dwell time. Track scans by campaign. Each scan represents a high-intent contact who noticed your ad enough to act.

**Promo code tracking:** "Show this ad for a free dessert" on screens near your location. Count redemptions. Works well for quick-service and casual dining.

**Branded search volume:** Monitor Google Search Console for branded query impressions during and after DOOH campaigns. Significant uplift suggests the campaign is building recognition.

**Foot traffic measurement:** Some DSPs (Vistar, Place Exchange) offer foot traffic lift measurement using anonymized mobile device data — comparing movement patterns to your location before vs. during the campaign. Not perfectly accurate, but directionally useful.

**Direct attribution ask:** "How did you hear about us?" on your booking form or receipt. Underestimates DOOH (people often don't say), but gives directional data.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does DOOH advertising cost for a restaurant?**
A: Local programmatic DOOH typically runs about $4 to $10 CPM, and many platforms have $500 to $1,000 minimums. A $400 to $800 monthly test is a realistic starting point. Check current quotes for your market.

**Q: Can DOOH advertising replace my Google Ads for a restaurant?**
A: No, they serve different purposes. Google Ads captures people actively searching for restaurants right now. DOOH builds neighbourhood awareness with people who aren't yet in search mode. They work best together, and DOOH exposure may increase branded searches over time. See our [Google Ads for restaurants guide](/blog/google-ads-for-restaurants).

**Q: How do I track walk-ins from a DOOH campaign?**
A: Three main methods: (1) Unique promo code in the ad creative — "show this screen for a free side," redeemed at the register; (2) QR code on pedestrian-level screens linking to a UTM-tagged booking page; (3) Foot traffic lift measurement from your DSP, which uses aggregated mobile data to compare location visits before vs. during the campaign. Method 3 is the most comprehensive but requires running through a DSP with attribution capabilities like Vistar Media or Place Exchange.

**Q: Is digital out-of-home advertising expensive for a small restaurant?**
A: Often less than owners expect. A $400 monthly campaign at typical CPMs buys tens of thousands of impressions near your restaurant during lunch and dinner windows. The barrier is usually creative sizing and platform setup rather than media cost.

**Q: What is the minimum budget to test restaurant DOOH?**
A: Roughly $300 to $500 for a 30-day test is the practical minimum to get usable data, and many programmatic platforms set their own minimums around $500 or more. Below that, impressions may be spread too thin to build frequency.

**Q: What is the difference between DOOH and traditional OOH?**
A: Traditional OOH (paper/vinyl billboards) is fixed creative, fixed location, fixed term — typically 4-week minimums. You design it, print it, it runs until the contract ends. Programmatic DOOH is flexible: you buy impressions across a network of digital screens, can swap creative instantly, run dayparted schedules (different ads at different times), and start or pause campaigns anytime. For a restaurant with seasonal specials, lunch/dinner variation, and responsive messaging needs, programmatic DOOH is almost always more practical than static billboard advertising.

**Q: Which DSP should a restaurant use for DOOH?**
A: Options commonly used by local advertisers include Vistar Media, StackAdapt, Place Exchange and Hivestack (often used in the UK, Canada and Australia). Features and minimums change, so compare self-serve access, attribution tools and minimum commitments before choosing. Platforms with high minimums are usually aimed at larger brands.
