DOOH (digital out-of-home) advertising puts your dish, offer and location on digital screens near your restaurant at the moments people decide where to eat, such as lunch and dinner. Programmatic DOOH is bought by CPM (cost per thousand impressions). StackAdapt states that DOOH CPMs range from $2 to $15 depending on screen type and campaign scope ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/digital-out-of-home-advertising-costs), checked Oct 2026). Minimum budgets differ by platform and are not published consistently, so ask for a written quote and forecast before you commit.

It is 11:30 on a Wednesday. Forty office workers are standing at a bus shelter two blocks from your door, thumbs on phones, undecided about lunch. Google cannot reach them, because they are not searching yet. A screen at that shelter can, with your best dish and "2 blocks north." That is the whole pitch for DOOH: timing near the physical moment of decision. And the price is public: StackAdapt puts DOOH CPMs at $2 to $15, so a $500 test buys between roughly 33,000 and 250,000 impressions.

## Why DOOH Works Differently for Restaurants

Google captures intent. Meta builds awareness. Restaurants need both, plus **impulse capture at the moment of hunger**. A restaurant gains from an ad seen at noon on a Tuesday; a dental clinic gains little. Screens at transit stops, office lobbies and street level near your block sit inside the lunch or dinner decision. Results still vary, so test small and measure.

## Best Screen Placements for Restaurants

| Screen Type | Best For | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| Transit stops | Lunch, quick-service | People on the move near food decisions |
| Street-level digital billboards | All types | Repeat exposure for local regulars |
| Office building lobbies | Business lunch, higher-end | Weekday lunch crowd |
| Gym lobbies | Health menus, smoothie bars | Post-workout hunger |
| Gas station pump screens | Casual, family dining | Longer dwell time while fuelling |
| Shopping centre corridors | Family dining, fast casual | Shoppers extending their trip |
| Hotel lobbies | Brunch, dinner, business meals | Visitors unfamiliar with the area |

Most restaurant campaigns combine two layers:

1. **Directional:** screens close enough to send foot traffic your way ("3 blocks north"). These work for lunch walk-ins and spontaneous dinners.
2. **Awareness:** screens across the wider neighborhood that build recognition, so your name surfaces when people choose where to eat later. These support reservations, delivery orders and planned visits.

Put more budget on directional screens if you depend on walk-ins, and more on awareness if you depend on reservations. Adjust after your first month of data.

## DOOH Creative for Restaurants

Restaurant DOOH creative has one job: make people hungry and say how close you are.

### What works

- **Lead with food photography.** Your best dish, full bleed, high saturation, not the restaurant exterior.
- **One message, large type.** Passers-by see a screen only briefly. Use dish photo, restaurant name and one line of context.
- **Proximity and timing cues:** "2 blocks north," "Open until 11pm," "Lunch special until 3pm."
- **QR codes on pedestrian-level screens** where people stand still, such as transit stops, pumps and lobbies. Link to your booking page with UTM tracking.

### What doesn't work

- Long descriptions or menus, or more than one call to action
- Stock food photography, which reads as generic
- Small text; design for someone several meters away and in motion
- Logo-only creative with no food image

### Daypart rotation

Many programmatic DOOH platforms schedule different creatives by time. Check that yours does, then use it:

| Time Window | Creative Message |
|---|---|
| Morning | Coffee, breakfast menu, opening time |
| Late morning to early afternoon | Lunch special, today's feature dish, walking time |
| Mid-afternoon | Coffee and pastry, happy hour teaser |
| Evening | Dinner atmosphere shot, reservation CTA, QR code |
| Friday/Saturday | Weekend special, event night, "Book a table" |

## How to Buy Restaurant DOOH: Three Routes

### 1. Programmatic DSP (usually best for local restaurants)

Self-serve or managed platforms let you buy screens across a network by location, audience and time of day. Names to ask about:

- **Vistar Media**, which offers foot traffic studies measuring visits to a brand's locations after DOOH exposure, using data partners ([Vistar Media](https://www.vistarmedia.com/news/data-providers-measurement-suite-dooh), checked Oct 2026).
- **StackAdapt**, which combines DOOH with other digital channels.
- **Hivestack**, which provides programmatic DOOH technology for media owners and advertisers ([ATDB](https://www.theatdb.com/companies/hivestack), checked Oct 2026).

Place Exchange is a supply-side platform that connects screen owners with DSPs ([ATDB summary](https://www.theatdb.com/companies/place-exchange), checked Oct 2026), so as a local restaurant you would normally reach its inventory through a DSP or agency rather than buying from it directly. Ask each vendor about minimum spend, self-serve access and attribution before choosing.

### 2. Outdoor operators (direct buy)

Companies such as Clear Channel, Lamar and JCDecaux own screen networks. A direct buy gives you a specific, high-profile location, but contract terms and minimums vary, so get them in writing.

### 3. Agency with DOOH access

A local marketing agency with DSP relationships can run DOOH alongside Google Ads and Meta. One point of contact, plus a management fee.

Programmatic is usually the better start for restaurants because it allows dayparting and quick creative swaps. Static billboards suit one fixed message on a long contract.

## The Math: What $500 of DOOH Could Buy

Impressions = budget divided by CPM, times 1,000. The CPM range ($2 to $15) is StackAdapt's published range. Everything else below is an example with assumed numbers, not a market statistic and not a client result.

Assumptions: $500 test budget, 1 in 5,000 impressions leads to a visit (0.02%), average spend $35 per visit. Swap in your own numbers.

| CPM | Impressions | Visits (impressions / 5,000) | Cost per visit | First-visit revenue ($35 each) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $2 | 250,000 | 50 | $10.00 | $1,750 |
| $5 | 100,000 | 20 | $25.00 | $700 |
| $10 | 50,000 | 10 | $50.00 | $350 |
| $15 | 33,333 | about 6.7 | about $75 | about $233 |

Break-even on the first visit alone needs $500 / $35 = 15 visits (rounded up from 14.3). Under these assumptions the $2 and $5 rows clear it, the $10 and $15 rows do not, unless customers return. If a typical customer visits twice a month at $35, that is $840 a year, so even the $10 row can pay back with repeat visits. Only measurement tells you which row you are in.

## Do This Today

1. Pick the 3 screens nearest your door that sit on a lunch or dinner route.
2. Ask two vendors for a written forecast: impressions, CPM and minimum spend for a 2-week test within 1 mile.
3. Make one creative: one dish photo, your name, one distance line, one QR code with a UTM tag.
4. Print a promo code ("show this screen") and brief the register staff before launch.
5. Ask your first 10 customers this week how they found you, to set the baseline before any ad runs.

**DOOH + Google Business Profile.** A DOOH ad plants your name; later someone searches "Italian restaurant near me" and finds your profile. Before any campaign, make sure it has current photos, hours, menu and recent reviews. See our [Google Business Profile service](/services/google-business-profile).

**DOOH + Google Ads.** DOOH exposure may increase branded searches. Track branded impressions and clicks during and after the campaign.

**DOOH + Meta retargeting.** Build a website custom audience in Meta Ads so people who scanned your QR code see a follow-up offer.

## Measuring DOOH Results

You can't link a walk-in to a specific screen impression, but several methods give signal:

- **QR codes:** unique codes per campaign, tracked by scans.
- **Promo codes:** "Show this ad for a free dessert," counted at the register.
- **Branded search volume:** watch Google Search Console during and after the campaign.
- **Foot traffic lift:** some DSPs offer studies that compare visits to your location before and during a campaign using anonymized mobile location data. Treat them as directional.
- **Ask customers:** "How did you hear about us?" on booking forms. It underestimates DOOH but adds context.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does DOOH advertising cost for a restaurant?**
A: Programmatic DOOH is bought by CPM, and StackAdapt states CPMs range from $2 to $15 depending on screen type and scope ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/digital-out-of-home-advertising-costs)). Minimum budgets differ by platform, so request a quote and impression forecast for your market.

**Q: Can DOOH advertising replace my Google Ads for a restaurant?**
A: No, they serve different purposes. Google Ads captures people actively searching right now. DOOH builds neighborhood awareness with people who aren't yet in search mode. They work best together. See our [Google Ads for restaurants guide](/blog/google-ads-for-restaurants).

**Q: How do I track walk-ins from a DOOH campaign?**
A: Use a unique promo code redeemed at the register, a QR code linking to a UTM-tagged booking page, and, if your DSP offers it, a foot traffic lift study comparing visits before and during the campaign.

**Q: What is the minimum budget to test restaurant DOOH?**
A: It depends on the platform. Ask for the minimum campaign budget and an impression forecast near your location, and test with money you can afford to lose.

**Q: What is the difference between DOOH and traditional OOH?**
A: Traditional OOH uses fixed creative in a fixed location for a fixed term. Programmatic DOOH lets you buy impressions across a network of digital screens, swap creative quickly, schedule by time of day and pause campaigns, which suits restaurants with changing specials.

**Q: Which DSP should a restaurant use for DOOH?**
A: Vistar Media, StackAdapt and Hivestack are commonly used by local advertisers. Compare self-serve access, attribution and minimum commitments before choosing.
