Marketing automation matters because triggered messages reach people at the moment they act, and the available data says those messages perform far better than one-off blasts. Klaviyo's 2026 benchmarks show a 5.58% click rate for automated flows against 1.69% for email campaigns. Flows also produced nearly 41% of email revenue from 5.3% of sends.

That data comes from online stores using Klaviyo, not from salons or gyms. No source checked here publishes local service results, so treat the numbers as direction, not a forecast for your shop.

## What Does Marketing Automation Actually Do?

It sends a message or runs a task when a rule is met. A new subscriber gets a welcome email. A booking triggers a reminder. A customer who has not visited in 60 days gets a note. A person sets the rule once, and the tool repeats it. For a plain-language intro, read [what marketing automation does](/blog/what-does-marketing-automation-do-plain-english-guide-for-small-business-owners).

## Do Automated Emails Really Perform Better?

In Klaviyo's 2026 benchmark page, flows show a typical good open rate of 32.2% against 31% for campaigns, a click rate of 5.58% against 1.69%, and an order rate range of 1.62% to 2.17% against 0.97% to 1.34%. Klaviyo also says flows earn nearly 18 times the revenue per recipient of campaigns.

Reasons are plausible: flows go to people who just did something. But the data is from one vendor's customers, mostly online sellers. A local business has fewer contacts and different triggers, so measure your own results.

## Which Benefits Apply to a Local Business?

These are practical benefits. They are reasoned from how the tools work, and none has a published local percentage:

| Benefit | How it shows up | What to measure |
|---|---|---|
| Faster follow-up | New lead gets a reply in minutes | Time to first reply |
| Fewer missed rebookings | Reminder or win-back goes out on schedule | Rebookings per month |
| Consistent review requests | Message after every visit | New reviews per month |
| Less manual work | Fewer copy-paste tasks | Hours spent on admin |
| Cleaner data | Leads land in one list | Contacts with a source |
| Steady communication | Messages continue when you are busy | Messages sent per month |

Pick one row, build one automation, and measure it before adding another.

## Where Does Automation Go Wrong?

Common failures: sending to people who did not opt in, setting a rule that fires twice, and writing messages that sound like a form letter. The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide requires honest subject lines, a postal address and a working opt-out honored within 10 business days, with penalties up to $53,088 per violating email.

Test each flow with your own address first. Read the messages as a customer would. See our [workflow automation guide](/blog/workflow-automation-for-small-business) for testing steps.

Worked example, all inputs are assumptions: a salon sends a monthly newsletter to 600 contacts and adds a welcome email for new clients. It gets 20 new clients a month. If 8 of them click the booking link in the welcome email, that is a 40% click rate on a tiny list. It is encouraging but small. The salon keeps the flow for three months before judging it.

## FAQ

### Is marketing automation worth it for a small business?

It can be, when you repeat the same message often. The only quantified evidence here comes from online stores, so run a small test on one flow and measure it.

### Will automation make my marketing impersonal?

Only if the messages are generic. Use details you already have, such as the service booked and the date.

### Do I need a paid tool?

Not to start. Several tools have free plans with small limits, listed above.

### How many automations should I begin with?

One. A welcome message or an appointment reminder is enough for the first month.

### Does automation work for SMS too?

It can, but SMS has its own consent rules. Check the rules in your country before sending. Our [local business marketing automation guide](/blog/local-business-marketing-automation) goes further.
