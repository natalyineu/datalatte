A fitness studio should run a Facebook Page for class information and bookings, add a Group only if it can moderate one, and schedule posts in advance. Pew Research Center found 74% of US adults used Facebook in 2024, as cited by [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/auto-dealership-advertising-driving-showroom-traffic), and its [2026 fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) still lists Facebook among the most widely used US platforms. Studios that also serve younger members often run Instagram alongside it.

This guide covers the Page, the Group, a posting rhythm, claims rules and a worked cost example for organic Facebook. Paid ads are covered in the [Facebook ads guide for gyms and fitness studios](/blog/facebook-ads-for-gyms-fitness-studios-complete-guide).

## Who is on Facebook, and does that match your members?

Facebook's reach is broad, but the right platform depends on who already books with you. Pew's 2024 figures put Facebook at 74% of US adults and Instagram at 51%. A separate 2025 figure from [Admanage](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-for-construction-companies), citing Pew, puts Facebook at 71% of US adults. The gap between sources is a reminder to check the primary Pew report before quoting a number.

Australia shows a similar pattern. A [2023 local marketing guide](https://www.zipzipe.com.au/blog/local-marketing/) lists Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn among the most used platforms there.

Compare the platforms against your own member list. Pull the age and postcode fields from your booking software and count how many members are over 40 and how many live nearby. If most are, Facebook deserves the first place in your plan. If most are under 30, Instagram may bring more trial sign-ups, and [Facebook and Instagram together](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-marketing-strategy) is the usual compromise.

## What should the Facebook Page do?

The Page should answer the questions a stranger asks before visiting: where you are, when classes run, what a first visit costs, and how to book. Pin one post that explains the first-visit offer so it is the first thing a new visitor sees.

A [2020 Appointy guide](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/) notes that Instagram and Facebook let businesses add a Book Now button to their profile. Check that the button points to your current booking page, not an old link.

Reply to comments and messages quickly. A [Buffer analysis of nearly 2 million posts](https://adwave.com/resources/social-media-customer-service-responding-to-d-ms-and-comments-fast) found Facebook posts with replied-to comments saw a 9% increase in reactions. Treat each reply as part of the post, not as admin work.

## Which organic tactics can run through Facebook?

Facebook works best as the place where offers are announced and members talk to each other. An [eight-tactic list for gyms](https://rhinofit.ca/gym-advertising-ideas-to-pump-up-revenue-at-your-facility/) includes social media fitness challenges, referral programmes, local business partnerships, community events and a fitness newsletter. Each one gives you a reason to post that is not just a class photo.

Challenges work well on Facebook because members can comment on each other's progress. Referral offers can be announced once a month with a single link. Partnership posts with a local café or physio clinic reach people who do not yet know the studio.

For ideas on what to post each week, see [fitness studio social media content](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-content).

## Should a studio start a Facebook Group?

A Group works only if someone will moderate it. It is a good place for schedule changes, challenge check-ins and questions from members. It is a poor place for a studio that cannot answer posts within a day, because silence in a members-only group is visible and discouraging.

Start with a clear purpose and a simple rule set, such as no selling in comments and no medical advice. Post at least a few times a week so the Group feels active. If you cannot commit to that rhythm, use the Page and a pinned post instead.

Check Facebook's live help pages for current Group settings, since options change over time.

## How often should you post on Facebook?

Buffer's benchmarks list a median of 35 posts per month for Facebook, which is about eight a week, based on its large dataset of brand accounts. A [Bookeo guide for salons](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) suggests one to two Facebook posts a week is sustainable for many service businesses. For a single studio, the second figure is the more realistic starting point.

Batch your content. An [Appointy guide](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/) advises creating at least 5 to 10 posts in each content category in advance for busy days. Write a month of class timetables, member stories with permission and offer reminders in one sitting, then schedule them.

Scheduling tools such as those covered in the [marketing automation guide for fitness studios](/blog/marketing-automation-for-fitness-studios) can post at set times, so you are not logging in every day.

## What claims are safe in posts?

Member results and testimonials carry legal risk in the US and elsewhere, so write claims carefully. Avoid promises of specific weight loss or fitness outcomes that you cannot back up. Describe what a class involves and what members have said about it, rather than what they will achieve.

Get written permission before posting a member's photo, name or story. Do not post only the happiest members' comments if the aim is to suggest all members feel the same way. Check current FTC endorsement guidance for US posts before you publish a testimonial.

This is general information, not legal advice.

## How do you know Facebook is bringing in members?

Facebook itself is free to use, so the main cost is time. A [Marketing 360 guide for Pilates studios](https://blog.marketing360.com/local-advertising/10-pilates-marketing-ideas-tips-and-strategies-this-is-how-to-grow-your-pilates-business-online/) makes the same point for its platforms: the cost is the time to create and post content. That makes the return easy to judge with a simple sum.

UpSwell, a gym marketing agency, states that social media drives leads within days and that most gyms see measurable growth within two months. Use that as a rough check window, not a guarantee. Ask "How did you hear about us?" on every booking form, and record Facebook answers in a spreadsheet each week.

## How many members does a Facebook Page need to cover its time? (worked example)

Every input below is an **assumption** for illustration. Replace each one with your own figures.

- Staff time: 2 hours a week at $30 an hour, about $260 a month (assumption)
- Membership: $120 a month, with members staying 6 months on average, so $720 gross per member (assumption)
- Funnel: the Page brings 10 trial sign-ups a month, and 2 of them join (assumption, 20% conversion)

Monthly time cost is $260. One new member is worth $720 in gross revenue over six months, so the break-even point is about 0.36 members a month, or roughly one new member every three months. The two members from the funnel bring $1,440 in gross revenue, which is more than five times the time cost. This ignores class costs and is revenue, not profit.

Decision rule: if two months pass with no member traced to Facebook, fix the offer and booking link before spending more time or money on ads.

## FAQ

### Is Facebook still worth it for a fitness studio?

For adults overall, yes. Pew's 2024 data put Facebook at 74% of US adults, and Pew's 2026 fact sheet still lists it among the most widely used platforms. Whether it is worth your time depends on whether your members use it.

### Do I need both a Page and a Group?

The Page is the essential part. A Group is optional and only worth running if someone can moderate it regularly.

### How often should I post?

Buffer's benchmark median is 35 posts a month for Facebook across brands. For a single studio, start with a schedule you can keep for eight weeks, then review sign-ups.

### Can I post member transformation photos?

Only with written permission and without misleading claims. Avoid guaranteed outcomes, and check current FTC endorsement guidance before publishing testimonials.
