A fitness studio should run a Facebook Page for information and bookings, add a Group if it can moderate one, and schedule posts in free tools. Facebook reaches 70% of US adults, 76% of rural adults and 59% of those aged 65 and over, according to Pew Research Center in 2026. For ages 18 to 29, Instagram is bigger at 79%, so many studios run both.

This guide covers the Page, Group, posting plan and claims rules for organic Facebook. It does not cover paid ads; see the [Facebook ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-facebook-ads-and-ai). No engagement rates are quoted because no source used here publishes them for studios.

## Who is on Facebook, and does that match your members?

Pew's 2026 survey of 5,511 US adults (January 30 to June 18, 2026) found Facebook used by 70% of adults. Rural adults use it more (76%) than urban adults (67%). Instagram shows the reverse: 58% of urban adults against 40% of rural.

A studio in a small town with a 40-plus membership will find more of its audience on Facebook. A boutique studio in a city centre aimed at 20-somethings should lean on Instagram, covered in our [Reels guide for studios](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-instagram-reels). Check member ages in your booking software before choosing.

## What should the Facebook Page do?

Make the Page the place a stranger confirms you are real: address, hours, class timetable, price or trial offer, and a booking link. Pin one post that explains the first-visit offer.

- Add the timetable as a regular post or image so it is easy to find
- Post class photos only with member permission
- Reply to messages and comments the same day

Google results often show your Page, so keep details consistent with your Google profile; our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) shows which fields to match.

## Should a studio start a Facebook Group?

A Group works if someone will moderate it. Facebook's help centre describes Groups as places for people with shared interests to connect, learn and share, which can be public or private, and lists "Pages in Groups" among its guides. Meta's pages on running a Group could not be opened in detail for this article, so read them before you start.

A members-only Group suits challenges, schedule changes and questions. It needs steady attention, so launch only if you can post and reply most days. For post ideas see [fitness studio social media content](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-content).

## What claims are safe in posts?

Member results and testimonials fall under FTC endorsement rules. The FTC's guidance says endorsements must reflect honest opinions, that asking only satisfied customers for reviews is misleading, and that you should disclose when customers were told they might be featured in an ad.

Avoid promising specific weight-loss or fitness outcomes unless you can substantiate them. Get written permission before posting a member's photo or story. This is general information, not legal advice.

## How do you schedule and measure?

Buffer's guide says Meta Business Suite is free to use, schedules feed posts, Reels and Stories for Facebook and Instagram, and needs a Facebook business Page or Instagram business account (secondary source). Batch a week of posts in one sitting.

Measure trial sign-ups and first-class bookings by asking "How did you hear about us?" on the booking form. For a plan to compare channels, read the [social media marketing strategy for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-marketing-strategy).

## How many members does a Page need to pay off? (worked example)

All inputs are an **assumption**: 1 hour a week to run the Page, valued at $30 an hour (about $130 a month), a monthly membership of $120, and members who stay 6 months on average ($720).

- Break-even: 1 new member over six months covers about $720 of lifetime revenue, which is more than five months of the time cost.
- Funnel: if the Page brings 10 trial sign-ups a month and 2 become members, that is $1,440 of expected revenue.

Decision rule: if two months pass with no member traced to Facebook, fix the offer and booking link before spending on ads.

## FAQ

### Is Facebook still worth it for a fitness studio?

For adults overall, yes: Pew puts Facebook at 70%, and 76% in rural areas. For under-30s, Instagram is bigger at 79%.

### Do I need both a Page and a Group?

The Page is essential. A Group is optional and only worth it if you can moderate it regularly.

### How often should I post?

No source used here sets a number. Choose a schedule you can keep for eight weeks, then review sign-ups.

### Can I post member transformation photos?

Only with written permission and without misleading claims. Follow the FTC guidance on testimonials and avoid guaranteed outcomes.
