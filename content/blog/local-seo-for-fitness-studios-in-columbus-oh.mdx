Local SEO for a fitness studio in Columbus, OH comes down to three things: a complete Google Business Profile, business details that match across the web, and a steady stream of recent reviews and photos. Google's Business Profile is free and shows your hours, phone number, address, reviews and photos directly in search results ([S1](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google), [S7](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). The Map Pack is the block of local listings that appears for searches such as "personal training studios near me" ([S1](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google)). This guide covers what to fix first, which numbers to track, and a worked example you can adapt.

## What does the Map Pack show for fitness searches?

The Map Pack sits inside Google Maps results and lists nearby businesses that match a search ([S1](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google)). Google does not publish a full ranking formula, so the useful evidence comes from how practitioners weigh the profile. In a BrightLocal survey cited by Ahrefs, 36% of SEOs said a Google Business Profile is the most important ranking factor for the map pack in 2021 ([S2](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo)). That share was 23% in 2013 ([S2](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo)).

The practical reading: your profile is the asset you control most directly. Columbus studios compete in the same Maps results as everyone else searching from the city. You can check the live results yourself by searching your category plus "Columbus" in a private browser window and noting which studios appear.

## Which Google Business Profile fixes come first?

Work through the profile in this order before touching anything else:

1. Confirm the profile is claimed and the name, address and phone number match your real studio exactly.
2. Choose the primary category that describes your core service, then add secondary categories for specific modalities such as yoga, Pilates or HIIT.
3. Write a description that names the service, the neighborhood and one clear differentiator, without stuffing keywords.
4. Upload at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes in session and coaches ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), secondary).
5. Publish posts on a regular schedule. A post appears publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, and posts that are Pending or Not approved do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).
6. Seed the Q&A section with the questions prospects ask most, such as "Is there a free trial?"

For more on profile setup for studios, see [the fitness studio Google Business Profile guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization). If you want to test AI-assisted drafting for posts and descriptions, [this AI profile guide](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization) covers the workflow.

## How many reviews and photos should a studio aim for?

Reviews are visible to searchers. Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). That makes review volume and tone part of the first impression, not just a ranking input.

One landscaping-focused blog claims that businesses with over 50 reviews and a 4.7+ average rating dominate the top map pack spots, but it cites no evidence for that claim ([AccountabilityNow](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), secondary). Treat 50 reviews and a 4.7 average as a working target to measure against your competitors, not as a confirmed threshold. Check the review counts of the studios currently ranking in your own area.

When you ask for reviews, ask clients to describe what they valued, such as coaching quality, class size or beginner friendliness. Specific wording gives prospects and search engines more to work with than a generic five-star rating.

## Does citation consistency change local rankings?

Citations are mentions of your name, address and phone number on other websites. The advice is to keep those details identical everywhere. One landscaping business reported an 18% jump in local pack appearances after cleaning up its NAP data, but that is a single anecdote from one firm, not a controlled result ([AccountabilityNow](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), secondary).

Start with the listings you already have. Fix the mismatched ones before adding new directories. Check industry platforms your clients already use, plus your city and state chamber directories. If you run several locations, Google Ads can pull location data from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and corrections made there flow into linked accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). Where the standard Business Profile option does not work, you can select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)).

## Worked example: what is a review gap worth?

The inputs below are assumptions for illustration. None of them come from the sources, so replace them with your own numbers from GBP Insights and your booking system.

| Step | Assumption or calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly profile actions (calls, directions, website visits) | Assumption: 40 | 40 |
| Share of actions that book a trial | Assumption: 25% | 10 trials |
| Share of trials that become paying members | Assumption: 40% | 4 members |
| Monthly value of one member | Assumption: $120 | $480 per month |
| Lift from a stronger review and photo profile | Assumption: 10% more actions | 4 extra actions |
| Extra monthly value from that lift | 4 × 25% × 40% × $120 | $48 per month |

The point is not the exact figure. It is the method: if a stronger profile adds a few actions a month, the value depends on your conversion rate and member value. A profile upgrade is cheap to test, so measure the actions before and after you make changes.

## How should you track whether the work pays off?

Set your baseline before you change anything. Record GBP Insights for searches, views and actions each month, and note your current Map Pack position for your top five keywords by checking them in a private window. Repeat the same checks on the same schedule so the comparison is fair.

Google Search Console will show organic impressions and clicks by page, which helps you judge the website side of local SEO. Keep notes on what you changed and when, so a jump in actions can be linked to a specific fix rather than to chance.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for fitness studios?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as a free tool for managing your presence across Google Search and Maps ([Google](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/); [Keepers Digital](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/), secondary).

### Do new Google Business Profile posts appear straight away?

Not necessarily. Google reviews each post, and it appears publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Check the status after you publish.

### How many photos should I upload to my studio profile?

A Vertical Impression guide recommends uploading at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes and staff ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), secondary).

### Can a multi-location studio link several locations to Google Ads?

Google Ads can use location data from Google Maps and Google Business Profile. Where the standard option does not work, you can select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link to your Ads account, provided those locations are yours ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). If you also run paid search, the [Google Ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/google-ads-for-fitness-studios-complete-2026-guide) explains how the two channels fit together.
