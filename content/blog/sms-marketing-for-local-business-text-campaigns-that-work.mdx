A local business should start with three text campaigns: an appointment reminder, a last-minute slot fill, and a win-back message to lapsed customers. Marketing texts need prior express written consent in the US, so build the opt-in first. Prices run about 8 cents a text: Fresha charges $0.08 per marketing text, and SimpleTexting's $39 plan with 500 credits works out to 7.8 cents.

This guide covers six campaign types, what they cost and the consent rules behind them. No source used here publishes open or click rates for local businesses, so the guide does not quote any.

The attendance figure comes from healthcare trials, not shops. The [Cochrane review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD007458_mobile-phone-text-messaging-reminders-attendance-healthcare-appointments) found moderate-quality evidence from seven trials (5,841 participants) that reminders raise attendance (risk ratio 1.14). Treat it as evidence that reminders work, not as a forecast for your business.

## Which text campaigns should a local business run first?

Start with messages tied to something the customer already did. They are the easiest to write and the least likely to annoy.

| Campaign | When to send | Consent type to plan for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appointment reminder with confirm or reschedule reply | 1 day before the visit | Service message; keep a consent record anyway |
| Cancelled-slot fill | Same day, to people who booked that service before | Marketing consent |
| Win-back | When a regular has not booked for longer than their usual gap | Marketing consent |
| Review request | A few hours after a visit | Ask your platform and lawyer how to classify it |
| Seasonal or new-service offer | Before a slow period | Marketing consent |
| Birthday or loyalty reward | On the date, or when points are earned | Marketing consent |

The FCC rule text defines prior express written consent around advertisements and telemarketing messages. Reminders for a booked visit are informational, but the safest habit is to collect one clear opt-in at booking that covers both.

## What do you need before you send a marketing text?

Under 47 CFR 64.1200, prior express written consent means a signed agreement, where a valid electronic signature counts, that clearly authorizes the seller, includes the phone number, and states that signing is not a condition of purchase.

Revocation is covered by the same regulation: a customer may opt out by any reasonable method, and you must honor it within a reasonable time not to exceed ten business days. Pick a platform that processes STOP replies automatically. For the full list of rules, read [SMS marketing best practices](/blog/sms-marketing-best-practices). This is general information, not legal advice.

## What does one campaign return? (worked example)

Every input is an **assumption**: 300 opted-in customers, the $39 SimpleTexting plan (500 credits, so about 7.8 cents a credit), an average ticket of $60, and a win-back text to the 120 customers who have not booked in 90 days.

- Cost: 120 credits, about $9.36 of the plan's allowance.
- Response: if 3% book, that is 3 bookings and $180 in revenue.
- Monthly check: if you run two such campaigns, the $39 plan has to return more than $39 in booked revenue. Here it returns $360 before costs of service.

Decision rule: keep the channel if booked revenue from texts is at least three times the plan cost after two months. If it falls short, check list size and consent wording before blaming the channel.

## FAQ

### How many texts a month is too many?

None of the sources used here sets a number. Start with one or two marketing texts a month plus reminders, and watch your opt-out rate. If opt-outs climb after a send, send less.

### Can I text customers whose numbers I already have?

Not for marketing unless they gave written consent that covers promotional texts. A phone number from a past booking is not the same as an opt-in.

### What should a first text say?

Name your business, state the offer or reminder in one line, and tell people how to opt out. Keep it under 160 characters so it uses one credit on SimpleTexting.

### Do texts work for every local business?

The evidence cited here is from healthcare reminders. Run a 60-day test and compare booked revenue against the plan cost.
