## Which Keywords Should a Dental Practice Start With?

Group keywords by what the patient wants, then by how soon. The table lists themes to test, with the intent behind each. It is a starting list, not a ranking.

| Theme | Example search | Intent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Urgent care | "emergency dentist [city]" | Needs an appointment today |
| New patient | "dentist accepting new patients [city]" | Ready to book |
| High-value treatment | "dental implants [city]", "Invisalign [city]" | Researching, higher fee |
| Cosmetic | "teeth whitening [city]", "veneers [city]" | Comparing options |
| Insurance | "dentist that takes [insurer] [city]" | Checks cover before booking |
| Family | "pediatric dentist [city]" | Parent looking for a practice |

Broad one-word terms such as "dentist" collect many clicks from people who are researching or not in your area. Specific phrases cost the same to bid on but are more likely to match a real booking. ## Which Match Type Should You Use?

Google Ads Help describes three types. Broad match is the default and the most comprehensive, and Google says it is critical to pair it with Smart Bidding. Phrase match shows ads on searches that include your keyword's meaning and reaches more searches than exact but fewer than broad. Exact match gives the most control and the fewest searches.

For a new dental account, start with phrase and exact match on your core terms so you control which searches trigger ads. Add broad match only when you have conversion tracking and enough data for Smart Bidding. A phrase keyword already matches everything the same keyword in exact would.

## What Negative Keywords Do Dentists Need?

Negative keywords stop your ad on searches you choose. Google's help says a broad-match negative blocks a search only if it contains all your negative terms, and that negatives do not match close variants, so you must add plurals, singulars and synonyms yourself. Too many negatives can reduce how many customers see your ads.

Start with these groups and then prune using the Search terms report:

- Job and training searches: jobs, salary, hiring, school, course
- Do-it-yourself and free: DIY, home remedy, free
- Supplier and equipment: supplies, wholesale, equipment
- Services you do not offer, for example a specialty you refer out

Review the Search terms report weekly in the first month and add new negatives as they appear.

## How Should You Set Location Targeting?

Google says radius targeting must be at least 1 km, and recommends Presence or Interest as the best practice for Search. Presence only limits ads to people physically in the area, which Google suggests for sensitive verticals or when you want to exclude people elsewhere.

A dentist serves patients within driving distance, so test Presence if you see clicks from places you cannot serve. Pair location settings with a call asset or form so mobile searchers can book quickly. ## FAQ

### How much does a click cost for a dentist on Google Ads?

LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark for Dentists and Dental Services is $8.00 per click. Your cost depends on your city, keyword and quality of the landing page.

### What is a good cost per lead for dentists?

The same benchmark shows $72.97 per lead, against $66.69 for all industries. Treat it as a reference, then use your own account data.

### Should I use broad match?

Only with conversion tracking and Smart Bidding, according to Google's help. Phrase and exact match give tighter control for a new account.

### How do I find search volumes for my city?

Use Google Keyword Planner. We did not publish volumes because none were available from a source we could open.

### Do I need negative keywords?

Yes. Add them early and check the Search terms report each week. Remember they do not match close variants.
