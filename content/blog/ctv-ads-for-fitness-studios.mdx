A streaming TV ad makes sense for a fitness studio only if enough reached households join. At an assumed $25 CPM and a cap of six views per household, $1,500 reaches about 10,000 households. If each new member is worth an assumed $220 in ad cost, about seven joins cover the spend, a join rate near 0.07%. The sources below explain why the channel is worth testing. ## Why do studios consider streaming TV at all?

Streaming TV reaches a large share of households. A secondary source citing industry projections, [NETINT's CTV overview](https://netint.com/research/blog/the-gen-ai-revolution-in-ctv-advertising/), states that CTV ad spending was projected to pass $40 billion a year by 2025 and reach 90% of US households. That is a vendor-led figure, so treat it as direction, not a measured audience size.

[Qujam](https://qujam.com/2026/09/24/ctv-advertising/), a CTV ad platform, describes the appeal as television-scale attention with digital campaign controls. Its wording matters for a studio: CTV is bought and measured more like digital media, so you can set a budget, pick audiences, and review delivery while a campaign runs. The same vendor notes that inventory quality, frequency and creative all affect results, so no channel is perfectly targeted.

CTV works as awareness, not a click channel. A viewer who sees a 15-second spot is unlikely to join that night. The job is to be familiar when that person later searches for a studio. That is why CTV belongs after search and social are working. DataLatte's [fitness studio Google Ads guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-ads-guide) covers the search side first.

## What does CTV cost compared with a traditional TV buy?

The sources do not give a current CTV price per thousand views, so any CPM in this article is an assumption. What they do give is the structure of the cost. Qujam says its self-serve platform has no daily minimums and contrasts this with enterprise platforms that, in its account, still enforce high monthly minimums. Those are vendor claims, so check them with each provider in writing.

NETINT's article also states that traditional campaigns often demand production budgets above $50,000 and weeks of planning, which shuts out smaller businesses. That is a vendor claim about the market, not a price a studio would pay. The practical takeaway is to ask every vendor for three numbers: CPM, minimum spend, and the smallest geography they will target.

## How do you work out the break-even join rate?

Search ads are priced per click, but CTV is priced per thousand views. So start from what a member is worth, then work backwards. Every input in the table below is an assumption except the cost-per-click row, which is shown only as a reference from search benchmarks.

| Input | Cheap inventory | Typical | Premium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,500 | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| CPM (assumption) | $15 | $25 | $45 |
| Ad views (budget / CPM) | 100,000 | 60,000 | 33,333 |
| Views per household, capped (assumption) | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| Households reached | 16,667 | 10,000 | 5,556 |
| Monthly fee per member (assumption) | $55 | $55 | $55 |
| Average months retained (assumption) | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| Revenue per member | $440 | $440 | $440 |
| Max ad cost per member (50% of revenue, assumption) | $220 | $220 | $220 |
| Members needed to break even ($1,500 / $220) | 6.8 | 6.8 | 6.8 |
| Join rate needed, reached households | 0.041% | 0.068% | 0.123% |

The same budget reaches a third as many homes at premium inventory, so it needs three times the response. Premium placements are not wrong, but they must earn their price through tighter targeting or a better audience. Note that this counts ad cost only. Revenue is not profit, and studio costs such as rent and coaching pay come on top.

If your studio converts fewer than seven of every 10,000 reached households, the TV buy loses money on its own. Many studios will find that they cannot measure this directly, which is why the measurement plan below matters as much as the CPM.

## When should a studio time its TV flight?

Sourced data on the timing of gym signups is limited in this set, so the calendar is a planning choice, not a fact. Plan the flight to finish before your busiest sign-up month, so that viewers have seen the ad before they search. Start three to four weeks early as a working rule, and check the effect against your own signup log.

Competition for attention also rises in peak months. The labour side of the market is growing: the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/fitness-trainers-and-instructors.htm) projects 7% growth for fitness trainers and instructors from 2025 to 2035, with about 388,400 jobs in 2025. Trainers work in health clubs and yoga studios and often promote their classes on social media, so you are competing for the same local attention. The BLS page also reports a 2025 median pay of $47,160, which is a useful reminder that many trainers are part-time.

## How should a studio target a catchment?

Set the geography around where members actually live, not around the whole city. Pull the postcodes of your last 50 sign-ups (a suggested step) and draw the catchment from them. A studio drawing from a few miles gains little from a metro-wide buy at any CPM.

Google's display network gives a sense of how targeting works in a large system. [Google's display campaign help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190) says Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail, and that optimized targeting uses information such as keywords on your landing page to find likely customers. That is a Google Display feature, not a TV one. Ask any CTV vendor how they target, and whether they can use ZIP or postcode lists, or a custom zone drawn around your studio.

Qujam describes custom zones for specific spaces, such as a building or a competitor location, and says its geofencing can capture audiences from those areas. Test whether the vendor can draw a tight zone that matches your catchment, not just a radius.

## What should the creative and measurement look like?

Fifteen seconds is enough for a local studio. The structure below is a suggestion to test, not a platform rule. Open with real class footage or a member moment, state one difference in the middle, and end with one action and your neighbourhood. Put the offer in on-screen text, and use members only with their permission.

Measurement without clicks is harder, and Qujam claims its platform offers direct attribution through Conversion Zones. Treat that as a vendor claim until you test it. A practical setup uses several signals together:

1. A web address or phone number that appears only in the TV ad.
2. A promo code used only on that spot.
3. A signup question asking how the member heard about you.
4. Branded search trends for your studio name over the flight.

Every enquiry needs to be logged, including calls. DataLatte's [AI receptionist guide for fitness studios](/blog/ai-receptionist-for-fitness-studio) explains how to capture calls that a TV ad produces when nobody fills in a form.

## What should you do before you buy?

1. Pull your last 50 new members and note each postcode to define your catchment.
2. Work out your monthly fee, average months retained, and the most you will pay for one member, using the table above.
3. Ask two vendors for a written CPM, minimum spend, and the smallest geography they will target.
4. Film 15 seconds of real footage and set up a TV-only web address and promo code.

## FAQ

### How much does CTV advertising cost for a fitness studio?

The sources here do not provide a current CTV price, so the CPM in the example is an assumption. At an assumed $25 CPM, a $1,500 budget buys about 60,000 views. Get written quotes from vendors, since minimums and rates vary by platform.

### Is CTV better than Google or Meta ads for a gym?

They do different jobs. Search catches people ready to join now, and social reaches people while they scroll. CTV builds familiarity before either. Most studios should have search working first. For the social side, see DataLatte's [Facebook ads guide for gyms](/blog/facebook-ads-for-gyms-fitness-studios-complete-guide).

### Can a small studio run CTV without a big budget?

Possibly, if the platform has no daily minimum and you can target a tight area. Qujam makes that claim for its self-serve platform, but it is a vendor statement. Confirm the minimum spend before you commit.

### How do I know if the TV ad worked?

Not through clicks. Combine a TV-only web address, a promo code, a signup question, and branded search trends. Retention also matters once members join, so track it alongside signups.
