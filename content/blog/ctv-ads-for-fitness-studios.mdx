Here is the number that decides whether a streaming TV ad makes sense for your studio. At an assumed $25 CPM and a cap of six views per household, $1,500 reaches about 10,000 households. To pay back at $220 of ad cost per new member, about seven of those 10,000 households must join. That is a 0.07% response rate. It sounds tiny, but you cannot click-track it, so most studios cannot tell whether they hit it. This guide is about that gap: what CTV costs a fitness studio, when to buy it, and how to prove it worked without a click.

It is written for studio and gym owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Prices are US benchmarks. For general CTV pricing and platform choices, our [CTV cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data) and [CTV platforms comparison](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026) go deeper; here we focus on the fitness math and calendar.

## Why Streaming TV Is Worth a Look

Nielsen's May 2026 Gauge, as reported by [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/07/29/nielsen-youtube-takes-13-8-of-us-tv-watch-time-in-may/), puts streaming at 48.6% of US TV watch time, a record. YouTube alone took 13.8%, The Roku Channel 3.1% and Prime Video 4.5%. Broadcast had 19.2% and cable 20.4%. For a studio, the point is simple: a large share of local households now watch on a TV screen through apps that sell ads you can geo-target, not through a cable box.

CTV is an awareness channel, not a click channel. The person who sees your 15-second spot on the couch is unlikely to join that night. The job is to be familiar when they later search "pilates near me" or see your Instagram ad. That is why we treat it as a second step after search is working, as in our [Google Ads for gyms guide](/blog/google-ads-for-gyms).

## What It Costs

CTV is sold by CPM, the price of 1,000 ad views. [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/average-ctv-cpm-q2-2026) reports a blended 2026 market average of about $26 (credited by AdWave to eMarketer), with most campaigns at $25 to $35 and the full market at roughly $15 to $45. It lists free ad-supported services such as Tubi, Pluto and The Roku Channel at $15 to $25, and standard programmatic on premium services such as Hulu and Peacock at $25 to $45. [Vibe](https://www.vibe.co/blog/roku-advertising-cost) estimates Roku at $20 to $60 and says Roku does not publish an official minimum, though campaigns typically start around $500. Both are vendor blogs, so read the numbers as estimates and get a live quote.

Two self-serve doors worth knowing. Fox announced a [Tubi self-serve ad platform](https://www.foxcorporation.com/?p=6482) in May 2025, aimed at businesses of all sizes. And Google's [video action campaign help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10147229?hl=en) says ads can run on YouTube and Google video partners, including the YouTube app on TV, and notes that all Video Action campaigns were upgraded to Demand Gen as of May 2026, so check which options your account offers.

## The Math: What Response Rate Do You Need?

Search ads give you a cost per click. CTV gives you a cost per thousand views, so work backwards from what a member is worth. Every row below is an **assumption** except the three CPMs, which are chosen from the $15 to $45 market range above.

| | Cheap inventory | Typical | Premium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,500 | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| CPM (market range, AdWave) | $15 | $25 | $45 |
| Ad views | 100,000 | 60,000 | 33,333 |
| Views per household, capped (assumption) | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| Households reached | 16,667 | 10,000 | 5,556 |
| Monthly fee (assumption) | $55 | $55 | $55 |
| Average months retained (assumption) | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| Revenue per member | $440 | $440 | $440 |
| Max ad cost per member (50% of revenue, assumption) | $220 | $220 | $220 |
| Members needed to break even | 6.8 | 6.8 | 6.8 |
| Join rate needed, reached households | 0.041% | 0.068% | 0.123% |

How to read it: the same $1,500 needs a three times higher response from premium inventory than cheap inventory, because you reach a third as many homes. Premium is not wrong, it just has to earn its price with better targeting or a better audience. Also note that this counts ad cost only, and that revenue is not profit.

If your studio only converts four of every 10,000 reached households, CTV loses money on its own. If you treat it as a lift for search and social, the answer can differ, which is why you need the measurement plan below, not click counts.

## Timing: Buy Ahead of the Calendar

NBC News reported in 2019, citing a 2013 US News story, that January accounts for more than 12% of annual gym memberships against about 8% in other months. That is dated and second-hand, so use it as a direction, not a forecast. Applied to a studio that signs 240 members a year, it implies roughly 29 joiners in January against about 19 in an average month (our arithmetic). Check your own signup log to see your shape.

Practical rules (our suggestions, not platform requirements):

- Start the flight three to four weeks before the month you want to fill, so people have seen the ad before they search.
- Expect search and social to be busier in January, since every competitor is advertising. Our fill classes in January and July guide covers the off-peak side.
- Plan a second flight before your slow season rather than doubling January spend.

## Targeting a Catchment, Not a City

Roku's geo-targeting reaches viewers in specific regions, per Vibe's overview, and audience filters include age, interests and streaming behaviour. Set the area to where members live: use your last 50 sign-ups' postcodes. A studio that draws from a 3-mile radius gains nothing from a metro-wide buy at $25 CPM. Ask any vendor whether you can target by ZIP or postcode, and what the minimum geography is.

Vibe also recommends capping how often each person sees your ad during a campaign, but gives no number. Our table assumes six views per household over the month; test between four and eight.

## Creative That Works on a TV

Fifteen seconds is enough for a local studio. A structure to test:

- **0 to 5 seconds:** real class footage or a member moment.
- **5 to 12 seconds:** your difference, if true: small classes, coaches who know your name, no long contract.
- **12 to 15 seconds:** one action and your location: "First class free at [Studio], [Neighbourhood]."

Use real members with permission, put the offer in on-screen text, and keep a 10-second end card with your name and a short web address.

### Use a QR code, but let the platform build it

Google's [QR codes for connected TV help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16903036?hl=en) says QR codes are available for Demand Gen and Performance Max campaigns on CTV, that the system generates them automatically, and that you should not embed a QR code in your video file. Your device targeting must include TV screens, and some older TVs will not show QR codes. The page does not describe reporting for scans, so check what your account shows before relying on it.

## Measuring Without Clicks

1. **Dedicated URL or phone number.** Use one that appears only in the TV ad, so every enquiry on that path came from it.
2. **Promo code.** A code such as TVFREE shows how many trials came from the spot.
3. **Branded search.** Compare searches for your studio name in Google Search Console before and during the flight.
4. **Signup question.** Ask "how did you hear about us?" and tally it after 60 to 90 days.
5. **A simple geo split (our suggestion).** If your vendor can target by ZIP, run the ad in some ZIP codes and not in comparable others, then compare trial signups. It is rough, but better than guessing.

## Do This Today

1. Pull your last 50 new members and note each postcode to define your catchment.
2. Work out your monthly fee, average months retained and the most you will pay for one new member. Use the table above.
3. Ask two vendors for a written CPM, minimum spend and geography options for your catchment.
4. Film 15 seconds of real class footage and set up a TV-only URL and promo code.
5. Check that search and your Google Business Profile are working first. If they are not, fix them before buying TV.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does CTV advertising cost for a fitness studio?**
A: It is priced by CPM. AdWave reports a 2026 blended average near $26, with a market range of about $15 to $45. Vibe says Roku campaigns typically start around $500. At $25, a $1,500 budget buys about 60,000 views.

**Q: Is CTV better than Google or Meta ads for a gym?**
A: They do different jobs. Search catches people ready to join now and Meta can reach people scrolling; CTV builds familiarity before either. Most studios should have search working first. See our [Facebook ads for gyms guide](/blog/facebook-ads-for-gyms-fitness-studios-complete-guide) for the social side.

**Q: Can I run CTV for one small studio?**
A: Yes, if you can set a tight geography and accept that the minimum may exceed what you spend on search. Self-serve options such as Tubi's platform were designed for smaller advertisers, but check minimums in writing.

**Q: How do I know if it worked?**
A: Not through clicks. Combine a TV-only URL, a promo code, branded search trends, a signup question and, if possible, a ZIP-level comparison.

**Q: Does it work for yoga and Pilates studios?**
A: The same math applies. See our [CTV ads for yoga studios](/blog/ctv-ads-for-yoga-studios) guide, then replace the fee and retention with your own numbers.
