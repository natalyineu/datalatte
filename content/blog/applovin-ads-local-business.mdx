AppLovin's own advertiser guidance says to set a daily budget that produces at least 15 to 20 conversions a day. If one lead costs your salon or studio $30 (an assumption), that is $450 to $600 a day, or roughly $13,500 to $18,000 a month. That single sum explains why AppLovin is, for most local businesses, an interesting channel to understand but a poor first or second place to put money.

This guide is for owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers what AppLovin is, whether a local business can now sign up, what the documentation says about targeting and billing, the math on budget, and what to do instead. Where a number is a guess it is labelled as one, and AppLovin does not publish a minimum budget or a local-business price list in the pages we opened.

## What AppLovin Is, in Plain English

AppLovin is a mobile advertising company. Its advertising platform, called Axon, serves video and interactive ads inside mobile apps, mostly games. AppLovin's long-time advertisers were app developers buying installs. Since late 2025 it has been pushing into websites and e-commerce.

On the company's Q1 2026 earnings call, per a [third-party transcript from The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/applovin-app-q1-2026-earnings-transcript), management said: "Come June, advertisers across the world will be able to sign up for Axon and start running campaigns." It described the target as advertisers "whether apps or websites, e-commerce or other categories," named gaming as the largest revenue contributor, and said lead-generation categories such as insurance and food delivery are being tested but are not expected to have a material financial impact in 2026. Since the transcript is third-party, treat the wording as close but not guaranteed.

## Can a Local Business Sign Up?

Mostly yes in principle, with big caveats in practice.

- **Open sign-up.** AppLovin's [account creation page](https://support.applovin.com/en/growth/ad-accounts/creating-an-account) says "AppLovin Ads is now available to all advertisers" with "No referral code required." The page carries no date beyond a 2026 copyright, so confirm in the live flow.
- **Website goals only.** The sign-up asks you to choose "Purchases on my website" or "Leads on my website." There is no store-visit or phone-call goal in that flow. A business whose conversion is a phone call or a walk-in has to push people to a web form or booking page.
- **Where the effort is aimed.** An agency write-up from [Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/applovin-q2-2026-earnings-ecommerce-advertisers) (a DTC e-commerce agency, so a commercially interested source) says the e-commerce expansion starts with mid-market advertisers through strategic partnerships. Treat that as one agency's reading, not AppLovin policy.

The situation has moved quickly, so the rule is to check the current sign-up page rather than rely on any article, including this one.

## What the Documentation Says About Targeting

This is where a local business hits a wall. The [web campaign API reference](https://support.applovin.com/en/growth/promoting-your-websites/api/axon-campaign-management-api-web) lists these geographic options:

- **Country** (ISO codes such as US).
- **Region**: US state abbreviations, and only when the country is US.
- **Metro**: US metropolitan areas such as DETROIT, again only for the US.
- **Not listed:** cities, zip codes, or a radius.

If you run a coffee shop in Leeds, a groomer in Brisbane or a barbershop in Calgary, the pages we opened show country-level targeting only. A US business can narrow to a state or a metro area, which for a single suburb is still very wide. That mismatch with a delivery or service radius of a few miles is the main reason AppLovin is hard to justify for neighbourhood businesses.

## How It Is Billed and Optimised

- **Billing model.** The API reference lists a single bidding strategy, auto-bidding with CPM billing, which "aims to fully use your budget while maximizing results at the lowest possible cost per result" and charges on every ad impression. You pay for exposure, not clicks.
- **Goals.** The launch checklist lists cost per lead, cost per purchase and return on ad spend, with a Day 0 or Day 7 window.
- **Payment.** Per the [billing page](https://support.applovin.com/en/growth/introduction/billing), you must add a credit or debit card; there is a daily 0:00 UTC charge for the next day's budget, and raising a budget mid-day triggers extra charges.
- **Pixel and creative.** The [launch checklist](https://support.applovin.com/en/growth/promoting-your-websites/welcome-to-applovin/launch-checklist) says to install the pixel via Shopify or Google Tag Manager and validate it with the Pixel Helper, and to supply at least one video and one interactive or image asset per creative set. It notes video ads are skip-delayed by at least five seconds, so state the offer early, and recommends videos of 30 seconds or longer with subtitles.
- **Minimum budget.** None of these pages states one.

## The Math

The budget guidance quoted in the hook comes from AppLovin's [app campaign scaling page](https://support.applovin.com/en/growth/promoting-your-apps/track-and-optimize/scale-your-campaign) and is written for app installs. The website checklist only says budgets should be "high enough to generate a meaningful volume of actions." So the 15 to 20 per day figure is the nearest published yardstick, not a rule for local services. Every other input below is an **assumption** you should replace.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Target conversions per day (AppLovin app guidance) | 15 | 15 | 15 |
| Cost per lead (assumption) | $60 | $30 | $15 |
| Daily budget needed | $900 | $450 | $225 |
| Monthly budget (x 30) | $27,000 | $13,500 | $6,750 |
| Lead to customer rate (assumption) | 15% | 25% | 35% |
| New customers per month | 68 | 113 | 158 |
| Ad cost per customer | $400 | $120 | $43 |
| Customer lifetime gross profit (assumption) | $300 | $300 | $300 |

How to read it: leads per month is 15 x 30 = 450, and customers equal leads times the close rate. The Base case looks profitable on paper at $120 per customer against $300 of lifetime profit, but it still ties up $13,500 a month before you know if the assumptions hold. The Tough case loses money on every customer. The Strong case needs leads at $15, which no source we opened supports for local services. A single-location business seldom has $6,750 to $27,000 a month to test with, and may not have the lead volume nearby to absorb it.

Now compare with a channel that targets by search intent. A $1,500 monthly Google Ads budget at an assumed $3 click gives 500 clicks. At an assumed 5% enquiry rate that is 25 enquiries. See [Google Ads for gyms](/blog/google-ads-for-gyms) for a worked version of this funnel and the Google Ads budget guide for sizing.

## When AppLovin Could Make Sense

It is a reasonable test, not a first move, if all of these are true:

1. You sell something that ships or is bookable online across a large area, or you run a multi-location brand with a US metro to cover.
2. You have a website pixel that already tracks purchases or leads cleanly.
3. You have already got Search, a Business Profile and Meta producing predictable results.
4. You can fund a multi-week test at the daily budget your lead cost demands, and afford to lose it.
5. You can produce several video creatives with the offer in the first seconds.

It is a poor fit if your customers come from a few miles around, your conversion is a phone call, or your lead value is under about $100.

## If You Do Test It: A Cautious Plan

1. Ask AppLovin or your agency for current minimums in writing, since none are published in the pages we opened.
2. Send traffic to one landing page with one offer and a form, with the pixel validated before launch.
3. Set the goal to cost per lead and a target you can afford, and cap the test length (for example, four weeks).
4. Add a source question to your booking form and a unique code, because in-app ads rarely produce a trackable click path on their own.
5. Compare cost per new customer with your best channel; stop if it is more than double.

## Do This Today

1. Write down your cost per lead and cost per customer from your best current channel.
2. Multiply your cost per lead by 15 to see the AppLovin-style daily budget your business would imply.
3. Check whether your service area fits country, state or metro targeting; if it does not, skip the channel for now.
4. Confirm your website pixel records leads or purchases accurately.
5. Put AppLovin on a "revisit next quarter" list, and put the money into search, your Business Profile and reviews first.

## FAQ

**Q: Can a local business advertise on AppLovin?**
A: AppLovin's account page says AppLovin Ads is available to all advertisers with no referral code, but the sign-up goals are website purchases or leads. Whether a small local business gets good results is unproven in anything we opened.

**Q: Can I target a radius around my shop?**
A: The web campaign API reference lists country, US region and US metro targeting, and no radius, zip code or city option. Check the live interface, which may differ.

**Q: What is the minimum budget?**
A: None of the pages we opened state one. AppLovin's app guidance recommends a daily budget that yields at least 15 to 20 conversions per day, so ask for a written minimum and test with money you can afford to lose.

**Q: Are AppLovin ads cheaper than Meta or Google?**
A: We found no verified comparison for local services, so we do not quote one. The platform bills by CPM, so compare cost per new customer, not cost per thousand views.

**Q: Is it only for mobile games?**
A: Ads appear mainly inside mobile apps, and gaming is its largest revenue source per management's Q1 2026 remarks. The sign-up flow now offers website goals as well.
