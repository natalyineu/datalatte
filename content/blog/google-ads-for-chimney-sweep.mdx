Here is the sum that decides whether chimney ads pay. Take a click that costs $8.33, an 8% chance that a click becomes a call or form, and a 40% chance that a lead becomes a booked job. Each booked job then costs about $258 in ads. If a first visit earns you $175 gross profit, you lose money on the first job and make it back only if that customer rebooks. That is the whole game for a sweep: ads buy the first visit, repeat business pays for it.

This guide is for chimney sweeps and fireplace specialists in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers what clicks tend to cost, how to build a search campaign by season, whether Local Services Ads are an option, how to track calls, and the break-even math. Dollar figures are US benchmarks for a wider category, not chimney-specific, so rerun the math with your own numbers.

## What the Benchmarks Say (and What Nobody Can Tell You)

## Know Your Season From Your Own Data

Chimney demand is seasonal, but the exact shape depends on your climate, so measure it instead of guessing.

- Pull your bookings by month for the last two or three years.
- Check Google Trends for "chimney sweep" in your state or region.
- Note when emergency calls (smoke, smells, storm damage) come in versus planned cleanings.

The risk side is real: NFPA data for 2016 to 2020, as summarised in search results, reports that 46% of home heating equipment fires happened in December to February. We could not open the NFPA report body, so this figure is second-hand. The practical reading is that your best message in late summer and early autumn is "get it done before the first fire", while winter calls skew urgent.

## Set Up the Search Campaign

### 1. Location: the roads you can reach

Target the towns, counties or postcodes you serve, and exclude the rest. Google's [location options help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038) says "presence or interest" is the default and recommended option for Search, while "presence only" shows ads only to people likely to be in the area. For a sweep, presence only is a reasonable test if your search terms report shows out-of-area clicks. That is my suggestion, not a Google rule. Check the locations report after two weeks and exclude places that spend without calling.

### 2. Keywords by intent and season

Use phrase and exact match at the start and build ad groups by what the person wants:

| Ad group | Example keywords | When to push |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine cleaning | "chimney sweep near me", "chimney cleaning [town]" | Late summer to autumn |
| Inspection | "chimney inspection", "fireplace inspection before winter" | Autumn, and house-sale periods |
| Urgent | "chimney smoking", "chimney fire damage", "chimney sweep emergency" | Winter, after storms |
| Repair | "chimney cap installation", "chimney liner repair", "chimney crown repair" | Spring and summer |
| Stoves | "wood stove cleaning", "log burner sweep" | Autumn |
| Brand | [your business name] | Always, small budget |

Keep each ad group to a few closely related terms so the ad can echo the search.

### 3. Negative keywords

Add on day one: `diy`, `how to`, `youtube`, `costume`, `jobs`, `course`, `training`, `salary`, `brush`, `kit`, `rod`, `for sale`, `used`, `toy`, `story`, `santa`. The Santa and costume terms are real traps in November and December. Review the search terms report weekly. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) covers lists and match types.

### 4. Ads that answer the three questions

Searchers want to know what you do, where, and how to book. Responsive search ads take up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions. Fill them with true, specific lines:

- `Chimney Sweep in [Town]. Book Online`
- `Inspection and Cleaning Included`
- `Insured Local Sweep. Free Quote`
- `Book Before the First Fire`

Only state certifications and insurance you actually hold, and name the body that certified you. Add call assets, a location asset linked to your Business Profile, and callouts such as "Photos of your chimney after every visit" if you really do that.

## Budget Through the Year

Give the campaign a floor in quiet months and move up for the weeks that matter. Two warnings from Google's own help page on [seasonality adjustments](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10369906): they are meant for short events of 1 to 7 days where you expect a big change in conversion rates, they may perform worse if used for more than 14 days at a time, and Smart Bidding already handles routine seasonal patterns. So do not use them to cover an entire autumn. Instead, raise the daily budget by hand when your own call volume tells you demand is building, and lower it when your calendar is full.

Capacity matters as much as demand. When your crew is booked three weeks out, extra leads just irritate customers. Pause the campaign, or narrow the radius, until you catch up.

## Local Services Ads: Check Before You Plan

Google's [help page on how leads work](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435) says Local Services Ads charge per valid lead, that invalid or low-quality leads are not charged, and that missed calls ending before 20 seconds are not charged. Prices vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding mode.

The unknown is eligibility. A third-party list of Google's US categories that we found does not show a chimney entry, and Google's help pages we opened do not name chimney work either. Eligibility varies by country and changes over time, so look in your account or Google's signup page before you build a plan around it. If chimney work is not offered, standard search ads are the route. For how the formats compare, see our [Local Services Ads guide](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide).

## Landing Page: Book in One Screen

Send "chimney sweep near me" clicks to a page that repeats the service and town, with a tap-to-call button, a three-field form (name, phone, address), your price or what is included, a few real photos of your work, reviews, and your credentials. Make sure it loads quickly on a phone, since most emergency searches happen there. Keep seasonal offers on their own pages, such as "Pre-Winter Inspection".

## Track Calls Like Revenue

Most chimney leads ring. Set up conversion actions for ad calls, website calls and form submissions. Google's [website call conversions help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) explains that a phone snippet swaps your number for a Google forwarding number, and that a call only counts as a conversion if it lasts longer than a minimum length you set. Choose a length that matches a real enquiry. Our guides on call-only campaigns and call tracking for local business cover the options.

Then log every lead: source, job type, quoted price, booked yes or no, and final revenue. Ad platforms count leads; only your diary knows jobs.

## The Math

Assume a $1,500 monthly budget in peak season. Every input is an **assumption** except the Base CPC and lead rate, which come from the Home & Home Improvement category benchmark above (not chimney-specific). Replace everything with your data.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,500 | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Cost per click | $11.00 | $8.33 | $6.00 |
| Clicks | 136 | 180 | 250 |
| Click to lead | 5% | 8.05% | 10% |
| Leads | 6.8 | 14.5 | 25 |
| Cost per lead | $221 | $103 | $60 |
| Lead to booked job (assumption) | 25% | 40% | 50% |
| Booked jobs | 1.7 | 5.8 | 12.5 |
| Ad cost per booked job | $882 | $258 | $120 |
| Gross profit per first visit (assumption) | $175 | $175 | $175 |
| First-visit profit after ads | -$1,203 | -$485 | +$688 |
| Add: half rebook next year (assumption) | +$149 | +$508 | +$1,094 |
| Profit after ads including rebooking | -$1,054 | +$23 | +$1,782 |

Gross profit is revenue minus crew time and materials, before ads. For example, a $275 average visit at about 64%. Use your own.

Read it like this:

- **Break-even click price = gross profit x lead rate x booking rate.** On first visit only: $175 x 0.0805 x 0.40 = $5.64. That is below the $8.33 benchmark, which is why the Base case loses on the first visit.
- **Count rebooking honestly.** If half of new customers rebook next year at the same profit, the effective value is $262.50 and the ceiling rises to $8.45. Only count repeats you can show in your own records.
- **The booking rate moves results as hard as click price.** Answering every call and returning every form quickly is the cheapest improvement. Track it.
- **Rising click prices at peak squeeze the Base case.** Check your own account in October against May.

If your margin is thinner than $175, the Base case is unprofitable even with rebooking, so fix pricing or lead quality before adding budget.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a chimney sweep spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official figure. Work backwards: decide the most you will pay for a booked job, then divide by your lead and booking rates to get a click price ceiling. At an $8.33 click, 8% lead rate and 40% booking rate, $1,500 buys about 5 or 6 jobs.

**Q: When should I start running ads?**
A: Set up and test tracking several weeks before your busy period, then raise the budget when your own call volume rises. Your own bookings history beats any national pattern.

**Q: Should I use Local Services Ads or regular Google Ads?**
A: Check whether chimney work is offered in your country and area; we could not confirm it from Google's pages. If it is, test it beside search and compare cost per booked job.

**Q: What keywords should a chimney sweep target?**
A: "Chimney sweep near me", "chimney cleaning [town]", "chimney inspection", "log burner sweep", plus repair terms in spring. Add negatives for DIY, jobs, costume and similar.

**Q: Why are my ads costing a lot but not bringing calls?**
A: Usually loose location settings, broad match without negatives, or no call tracking. Read the search terms and locations reports first.
