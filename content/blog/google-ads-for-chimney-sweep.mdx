Yes, Google Ads work well for chimney sweeps, because homeowners usually search for "chimney sweep near me" or "chimney cleaning" at the moment they need someone, and demand is strongly seasonal. The setup that works is simple: local targeting, call-friendly ads, a tight keyword list with negatives, and a budget that rises into autumn and eases off when demand falls. Local Service Ads may also be an option where Google offers them for your trade and area.

## Who Is Searching and When

Homeowners with fireplaces, wood stoves and older chimneys look for a sweep when the weather turns cold, before the first fire of the season, or when something goes wrong (smoke, smells, damage after a storm). In many northern regions, demand builds from late summer, peaks in autumn and early winter, and drops in spring. Check your own booking history by month and your local Google Trends data, because the pattern depends on your climate.

## Setting Up Your Google Ads Campaign

- Create a Google Ads account and link your Google Business Profile so your address, reviews and phone number can appear in ads
- Set up conversion tracking for phone calls and form submissions
- Start with specific keywords such as "chimney sweep near me", "chimney cleaning [city]", "chimney inspection" and "fireplace cleaning"
- Target the area you actually serve, using radius or postal-code targeting, and choose the option for people located in that area

## Ad Copy and Landing Pages

Your ad and landing page should answer three things quickly: what you do, where you do it and how to book. Consider highlighting:

- Certifications and insurance (state only what you actually have)
- Clear pricing information or what is included in a standard visit
- Appointment availability and emergency service, if offered

Send each ad to a page built for that service, with a visible phone number, a short form, real reviews and photos of your work. Make sure it loads fast on a phone.

## Budget and Bidding

Click prices vary widely by city, so check Google's Keyword Planner for your area before setting a budget. Many new campaigns start with manual bidding or Maximize Clicks to learn which keywords convert, then move to a conversion-based strategy once there is steady data. Watch automated bidding closely, because it can raise costs if tracking is wrong.

<Callout type="warning">Be cautious when using automated bidding strategies, as they can lead to increased costs if you are not tracking conversions accurately or monitoring them regularly.</Callout>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Broad Match Keywords Without Negatives

Broad keywords like "chimney repair" can match DIY and unrelated searches. Start with phrase and exact match, build a negative keyword list ("how to", "DIY", "free", "costume", "jobs", "course", "training") and check the search terms report weekly. See our guide to [Google Ads negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).

### Mistake 2: Poor Location Targeting

Chimney sweeps are hyperlocal. Choose the option that targets people in your service area rather than people who merely show interest in it, exclude areas you do not serve, and review the location report to see where clicks and calls come from.

### Mistake 3: Sending Traffic to a Generic Page

Someone who searched "chimney sweep near me" wants to book. Give them a dedicated landing page with a clear call to action: a click-to-call button on mobile, a short form (name, phone, address), reviews and trust signals. If you need a faster page, see our [website and landing page service](/services/website).

### Mistake 4: Setting and Forgetting

Chimney work is seasonal, and a campaign left untouched will waste money in slow months and run out of budget in busy ones. Review search terms, keyword performance and budget at least every couple of weeks in season, and cut spend when demand drops.

### Mistake 5: Not Using Calls

Many searchers on a phone want to call straight away. Add call assets to your ads and consider a call-focused campaign. Track calls with Google forwarding numbers or a call tracking tool. ## Optimizing for Seasonal Peaks and Valleys

Chimney demand behaves more like a wave than a steady stream, so match spend to it.

**Build early.** Set up campaigns, keywords and landing pages several weeks before your busy season, so ads have time to be approved and tuned.

**Raise the budget into peak, lower it after.** Increase spending as demand rises in autumn, then reduce it in the slower months. Use your own booking history to decide how much.

**Use seasonal keywords.** In autumn, "chimney sweep before winter" and "fireplace inspection" are typical; in winter, urgent terms like "chimney repair" and "smoke from fireplace"; in spring and summer, "chimney cap", "liner repair" and "masonry repair". Separate ad groups by season so you can adjust budgets independently.

**Offer early-booking incentives in the off-season.** A modest incentive for booking ahead can smooth demand and fill your calendar before the autumn rush.

**Adjust for weather if it fits your market.** Cold snaps can drive urgency. You can use Google Ads' ad schedule and bid adjustments, and in some cases scripts or rules, to react.

**Manage capacity.** If your crew is full, lower the budget or pause campaigns so you do not generate leads you cannot serve. Google Ads automated rules can pause campaigns at set times.

## Local Service Ads and Extensions

Google Local Service Ads (LSAs) appear at the top of results in some areas and trades, and you pay per lead rather than per click. Availability depends on your country, trade and location, and they involve a verification process (typically business licensing, insurance and background checks), so check whether chimney services are supported in your area. Read our [complete guide to Google Local Service Ads](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide). If LSAs are available, many local service businesses run them alongside standard search ads.

Useful ad extensions (assets):

- **Call assets** with your phone number
- **Location assets** showing your address
- **Callouts** such as "Licensed and insured" or "Same-day service" (only if true)
- **Structured snippets** listing services: cleaning, inspection, liner repair, cap installation

## Measuring What Matters

- **Cost per lead:** ad spend divided by calls and form submissions
- **Lead-to-booking rate:** how many leads become paying jobs; listen to call recordings (with consent) if it is low
- **Average revenue per job:** cleanings, repairs and relining are different tickets, so track what each campaign produces
- **Search terms and locations:** which queries and areas bring the best leads

Log LSA leads and standard ad leads separately so you can compare quality. Use Google Analytics and conversion tracking to see which keywords, devices and times produce bookings. Our [analytics and reporting service](/services/analytics) can help set this up.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a chimney sweep spend on Google Ads?**
A: It depends on your market and season. Check Keyword Planner for estimated click costs in your area, start with an amount you can sustain for several weeks and scale up as autumn demand arrives.

**Q: When should I start running ads?**
A: Set campaigns up several weeks before your busy season so you have time to test ads and fix tracking, then ramp up as searches rise.

**Q: Should I use Local Service Ads or regular Google Ads?**
A: Use LSAs where Google offers them for your trade and area, and complement them with search ads for repairs and specialist work. Compare cost per lead and lead quality over a couple of months.

**Q: What keywords should a chimney sweep target?**
A: Start with "chimney sweep near me", "chimney cleaning [city]", "chimney inspection" and "fireplace cleaning", and add seasonal terms. Add negatives such as "DIY", "how to", "costume" and "jobs".

**Q: How do I stop wasting money on irrelevant clicks?**
A: Use precise match types, negative keywords, tight location targeting and a weekly check of the search terms report.
