A good local business About page says plainly what you do, who is behind it, where you are, and why a customer should trust you, then points to one next step. Nielsen Norman Group's research says users expect companies to explain what they do, their origins, how they view business and how they relate to the community, in plain, human language.

Google's guidance on helpful content also asks whether it is clear who created content and whether pages link to an author or About page. This guide uses those sources; where a number is an example, it is labelled as an assumption.

## What Should an About Page Include?

Use this outline. Each section answers a question a stranger has before they buy.

| Section | Question it answers | Example content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What we do | Am I in the right place? | One sentence: service, area, who it is for |
| Who runs it | Who am I dealing with? | Names, photos, role, training or years in the trade |
| Origin | Why does this business exist? | Two or three true sentences |
| How we work | What will happen if I buy? | Process, guarantees you actually keep |
| Proof | Do others vouch for you? | Genuine reviews, licences, memberships |
| Place | Where are you? | Address, hours, map, parking |
| Next step | What do I do now? | Book, call or visit button |

Keep every claim true. Google's page warns against deceptive authorship information, such as made-up names or false credentials.

## Why Do Credentials and Reviews Matter?

NN/g found that users doubt testimonials are representative but still read them and compare them with third-party review sites. It recommends honest, balanced testimonials on your own site and making sure details on third-party listings are accurate and up to date.

So align your About page with your Google Business Profile: same name, address, hours and description. Our [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers the profile side, and our [testimonials page guide](/blog/website-testimonials-page-local-business) covers how to display reviews.

## How Do You Write It in Plain Language?

NN/g's study found that plain, candid wording reduced skepticism and made people more willing to reach out. Write as you would speak to a new customer at the counter. Avoid "passionate about excellence" phrases, which say nothing a competitor could not say too.

Replace claims with specifics: the year you opened, the neighbourhood you serve, what your team trained in, the supplier you use. If you cannot prove a claim, cut it. NN/g also advises explicit link labels over jargon, so name the button for what it does: "Book a haircut", not "Learn more".

## What Should You Add for Search?

Mark up the business details with structured data. Google's LocalBusiness documentation lists `address` and `name` as required, and `telephone`, `url`, `openingHoursSpecification` and `geo` among recommended properties. Add them on your contact or home page, and keep them in line with the visible text.

Link to the About page from author bylines on blog posts, since Google's questions about expertise mention linking to an author page or the site's About page. Do not stuff keywords; write for the visitor. See our [website conversion rate guide](/blog/website-conversion-rate-optimization) for how to measure what happens next.

## Worked Example: What Is an About Page Worth?

All inputs are **assumptions**, not benchmarks.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly About page views | 400 |
| Visitors who click the booking button from it | 8% = 32 |
| Those who become customers | 25% = 8 |
| Value per customer | $60 |
| Monthly revenue linked to the page | 8 x $60 = $480 |

If a rewrite lifted the click rate from 8% to 10%, you would get 40 clicks, 10 customers and $600. Measure the real rates in your analytics before and after, and change one thing at a time.

## FAQ

### How long should an About page be?

Long enough to answer the seven questions above. There is no verified ideal length; 300 to 500 words is a reasonable starting point as a judgement, not a benchmark.

### Should I lead with my story or my credentials?

Lead with what you do and for whom, then credentials, then story. NN/g says users want the basics stated clearly.

### Do I need photos of my team?

Real photos help people see who they are dealing with. Avoid stock images of people who do not work for you.

### Where should I link the About page?

In the main menu, the footer and your Business Profile description. See our [local business website checklist](/blog/local-business-website-checklist).

### Can I put prices on it?

Only if price is part of your positioning. Otherwise use a services page.
