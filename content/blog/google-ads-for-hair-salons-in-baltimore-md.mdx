## How many people search for hair salons near me?

Google Ads keyword data captured in April 2026 estimated about 1.5 million U.S. searches per month for "hair salon near me" and about 110,000 for "hair stylist near me" ([RŌZ hair education page](https://rozhair.com/blogs/education/hair-salon-advertising-ideas?srsltid=AYCX9vIdNXXuxGV-rKdglolA2QOQJbEGs8irFigzpePpcaBIjnjcFKs6), secondary). Those are national totals, so they say little about one city.

Check Baltimore volume for your exact phrases in Google Ads Keyword Planner before you commit budget. Trafft's salon ads guide notes that people also search with phrases like "spa near me" and "hair salon near me" plus a city name ([Trafft](https://trafft.com/salon-ads/)). Build your list to include both the near-me terms and city-based variants.

## Which keywords should a Baltimore salon bid on first?

Begin with exact match on your top three to five converting terms. These are usually service plus "near me," such as "hair salon near me" or "haircut near me." Exact match gives you control over which searches trigger your ads, which matters most while you are still learning what converts.

Once those terms are profitable, add phrase-match campaigns that pair a service with a Baltimore neighbourhood, such as Federal Hill, Canton or Hampden. Neighbourhood names act as location modifiers in ad copy and keyword variants. For a full keyword breakdown, see [best Google ad keywords for hair salons in 2026](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026).

## How do you target Baltimore without wasting spend?

Set a radius around your address rather than targeting the whole metro area. A tight radius keeps your ads near clients who can realistically book a visit. Test a few radius sizes and compare cost per booking before settling on one.

Add negative keywords to block searches that rarely turn into bookings. Common examples include job-related terms like "jobs" and "hiring," DIY or training terms like "how to" and "course," and irrelevant modifiers like "wholesale" or "supplier." ## What should your landing page do?

Clicks alone do not fill a chair. Sunnystorm's Google Ads guide for salons argues that if a salon gets 100 visitors and no bookings, the landing page is probably the problem ([Sunnystorm](https://sunnystorm.marketing/blog/google-adwords-salons)). Check that the page loads quickly on a phone, shows your services and prices, and offers a one-tap booking or call button.

If bookings are low despite clicks, fix the page before raising the budget. Also review the search terms report to see which queries brought the clicks.

## How do you track whether ads bring bookings?

Set up conversion tracking before you judge any campaign. Track these actions as conversions:

1. Phone calls from the ads, using a tracking number
2. Completed booking form submissions on your website
3. Direction requests from your Google Business Profile
4. Clicks on "book now" or contact buttons as a secondary signal

Compare cost per booking across keywords and neighbourhoods each month. Pause the terms that cost the most per booking and move budget toward the terms that bring returning clients.
