## How Much Should a Fort Worth Fitness Studio Spend on Meta Ads?

[Mariana Tek notes that many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/). Within that budget, Adwave's split for fitness and wellness businesses is 45–55% Meta, 30–40% connected TV and the balance in local platforms. A standalone Meta buy often costs $300–$1,000 per month, according to [Adwave's channel table](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels). Those figures are national planning ranges, so use them as a starting frame rather than a local forecast.

The worked example below uses assumed inputs to show how the numbers fit together for one studio.

- Monthly studio revenue: $12,000 (assumption).
- Total marketing at 5–10%: $600–$1,200.
- Meta share at 45–55% of that total: $270–$660.
- Test budget chosen: $500 per month (assumption).
- Impressions at $5–$15 per thousand: about 33,000 to 100,000.
- Clicks at $1–$3 each: about 167 to 500.
- Share of clicks that book a trial: 5% (assumption), giving 8 to 25 bookings.
- Cost per booking: roughly $20 to $63.

Replace the 5% booking rate with your own figure after two weeks of data. The gap between the cheap and expensive ends of the range is the reason to start small and measure before scaling. ## Which Ad Formats Should Studios Run First?

Short-form video is the strongest starting format. Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, which says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners), secondary). [Mariana Tek advises posting short-form video at a steady cadence of three to five times a week](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) rather than in bursts. For shot lists and posting rhythm, see the [Instagram Reels guide for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-instagram-reels).

Stories placements suit time-limited trial offers and class schedules. The [Instagram Stories ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/instagram-stories-ads-fitness-studios) covers setup. Competitor research is also useful. The Sprites gallery lists 746 static ads from 81 gyms and studios in the Meta Ad Library, with 705 running at the time of listing ([Sprites](https://www.sprites.ai/gym-static-ads), secondary). Search the Ad Library for studios near you and note which formats they keep running.

## Who Should Studios Target in Fort Worth?

Meta Ads Manager covers Facebook, Instagram and Messenger ([Turrboo](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media)). Instagram ads run through the same system as Facebook ads because Instagram is owned by Meta, so one campaign can run on either app or both ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business)). Meta's apps reach an average of 3.56 billion daily active people ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/facebook-advertising-for-small-business)). The audience pool is large, so the real task is narrowing to people who can reach your studio and book.

Start with a location radius matched to how far members drive, set in Ads Manager. Test age ranges and interests in separate ad sets so the results can be compared. Build a custom audience from past members and visitors, then use it for retargeting. Adwave lists a typical Meta retargeting CPM of $5–$15 ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/retargeting-ads-small-business)), which makes a retargeting ad set a useful low-cost test once you have an audience.

## How Should Studios Handle Leads After the Click?

Instagram and Facebook let businesses add a Book Now button to their profile ([Appointy](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/)). Use it when the booking page matches the offer in the ad, so a visitor does not land on a generic homepage. Mariana Tek advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, since interest fades fast after a pop-up ends ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). The [marketing automation guide for local fitness studios](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-fitness-studios) explains how to build that sequence.

## What Should Be Set Up Before Launching?

Work through these steps before any ad goes live:

1. Open the trial or booking page on a phone and confirm it loads and the sign-up form works.
2. Install the Meta Pixel on the booking confirmation page and set one lead event for trial sign-ups.
3. Add UTM parameters to every ad link so Google Analytics can separate Meta traffic from other sources.
4. Pick one trial offer and one booking page for each ad set, so results map to a single offer.
5. Schedule the 24-hour follow-up sequence in your email or messaging tool.
6. Set the test budget and a review date two weeks out, and change targeting only at that review.

Meta Blueprint is also worth a look for staff training. According to a 2026 guide from Sascom247, it offers free Facebook and Instagram advertising courses with certification on completion ([Sascom247](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/), secondary).

## Which Mistakes Should Studios Avoid?

The most common error is asking cold audiences for a full membership before they have tried a class. A free trial or first session lowers the commitment and gives the follow-up sequence something to work with. The second mistake is changing targeting and creative every few days, which resets learning and makes results impossible to read. The third is letting leads sit for days without a reply. A 24-hour follow-up keeps the interest that the ad created.
