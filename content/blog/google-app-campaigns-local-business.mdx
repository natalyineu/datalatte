Most local businesses do not need Google App campaigns. Google's help says they are for promoting a mobile app, and you need an app published on Google Play or the Apple App Store to run one. A café, salon, studio or groomer without its own app should use Search, Performance Max with store goals, or Local Services Ads instead.

This guide is for owners deciding whether an app justifies paid installs. Google does not publish a typical cost per install for local businesses, so none is quoted here. Examples marked as assumptions are illustrations.

## What Are Google App Campaigns?

Google's [About App campaigns](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6247380) describes them as campaigns that promote an app across Google properties. You supply text, a bid and assets, and the system optimizes the ads. Ads can run on Google Search and search partners, Google Play, YouTube, the Display Network (including Gmail and other apps) and Discover.

The page lists three types: installs, engagement and pre-registration. It says campaigns can optimize for actions such as installs or in-app purchases. It does not mention websites, store visits or local businesses. An App campaign cannot send people to your opening hours or your booking page.

## Does a Local Business Need an App at All?

Usually not. Apps earn their place when customers come back often and the app does something a website or Business Profile cannot, such as a loyalty card, order-ahead or a booking history. Even then, most customers will not install one for a shop they visit twice a year.

Ask first whether the app already works and has customers using it. Paying to install a weak app buys uninstalls. Our [app marketing guide for local businesses](/blog/app-marketing-for-local-business) covers how to get installs from your counter and receipts before you spend on ads, and the [in-app advertising guide](/blog/in-app-advertising-local-business-guide) covers the other side, showing your ads inside other apps.

## What Should You Use Instead of App Campaigns?

If the goal is footfall, calls or bookings, three other Google options fit better.

| Goal | Better option | Source of the claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People searching for you now | Search campaign with location targeting | [Google location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722043) |
| Store visits and directions | Performance Max with store goals | [Google store goals help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12971048) |
| Calls and booked jobs | Local Services Ads | [Local Services Ads guide](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide) |

Google says Performance Max for store goals measures store visits, store sales and local actions such as call clicks and direction clicks. Ads can appear on Maps, Search, YouTube, Display, Business Profiles and Gmail, and you define the stores by linking a Business Profile. Our [Performance Max vs standard ads comparison](/blog/performance-max-vs-standard-google-ads-which-should-you-run) explains when each fits a small budget.

## How Would You Test an App Campaign Cheaply?

If you do have an app, run a small installs test and judge it by what users do afterwards, not by installs alone. Google's help says in-app actions can be tracked as conversions through Firebase, and that you can choose which Firebase events count in Google Ads. Set the event that matters, such as a first order or first booking, before spending.

Google's location help notes that very small radii may serve intermittently, and the engagement campaign page says location and language targeting can shrink the audience. For an app that works only in your area, keep that trade-off in mind: a narrow area can leave the system with too little to learn from.

## The Math: When Do Paid Installs Pay Back?

All inputs are **assumptions**, because no cost per install is published for local businesses. Replace them with your own test results.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Cost per install (assumption) | $4 |
| Installs that place a first order (assumption) | 15% |
| Cost per first order | about $26.70 |
| Margin on a first order (assumption) | $6 |
| Extra repeat orders per user over a year (assumption) | 3 |
| Margin on repeat orders | $18 |
| Margin per paying user over a year | $24 |

At these figures, each paying user returns $24 in margin against $26.70 of ads, so the test is just under water. A cost per install of $3, or a 20% order rate, tips it positive. Small changes in the order rate matter more than the bid, which is why you track the first order and not the install.

## FAQ

### Do App campaigns work for a business without an app?

No. Google's help says you need an app published on Google Play or the Apple App Store, and the page describes only app promotion.

### How much do App campaigns cost?

Google does not publish a typical cost per install, and LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks do not cover app installs. Run a small test and measure your own cost per paying user.

### What is an App campaign for engagement?

It is a Google campaign type that targets people who already have your app and relies on audience targeting and your Play Store listing, according to Google's overview. The page does not list requirements, so check the setup article before planning one.

### What if I want customers to visit my shop?

Use Search, Performance Max with store goals or Local Services Ads. Start with a complete [Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).
