Most local businesses do not need a Google App campaign. These campaigns drive installs of a mobile app, so the first question is whether you have an app people can download from a store. A café, salon, studio or groomer without one should usually spend on Search with location targeting, Performance Max for store goals, or Local Services Ads. Vendor data puts install costs between $0.50 and $3.00 for many categories, which only makes sense when each install leads to repeat revenue. The sections below show how to test that cheaply and when the numbers do not justify it.

## What Does a Google App Campaign Actually Buy?

Adwave describes the setup as one where you [set a target cost per install](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-users-mobile-app), and Google's machine learning then optimizes delivery across Search, Display, YouTube and Google Play. The campaign is measured on downloads. It is built to get people to install, not to visit your shop, call you or book a job.

That matters for a local business. An install campaign does not send a customer to your opening hours or your booking page. If the thing you sell happens in person, the campaign is buying a result that sits one step away from your revenue, and each step loses people.

## Does a Local Business Need an App at All?

Usually not. An app earns its place when customers return often and use something a website or Business Profile cannot provide, such as a loyalty card, order-ahead or a booking history. For a shop that customers visit twice a year, few will bother to install an app.

Check first whether the app you already have is in use. Paying to install a weak app buys uninstalls. The [app marketing guide for local businesses](/blog/app-marketing-for-local-business) covers how to get installs from your counter and receipts before you spend on ads.

## How Much Do App Installs Really Cost?

Vendor figures vary a lot. Adwave gives an [average cost per install through Google of $0.50 to $3.00](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-mobile-apps), depending on category and geography. The same vendor's [app advertising roundup](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-mobile-apps) says many teams see productive ranges from roughly $1 to $3 for some casual games, up to $20 to $80 or more for subscription and finance apps.

Neither range is specific to local service businesses. Treat them as a rough guide, then measure the cost per install and the cost per paying user in your own account.

## Which Google Option Fits Instead?

If the goal is footfall, calls or bookings, these options fit better than an install campaign.

| Goal | Better option | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customers searching for you now | Search campaign with location targeting | Reaches people already looking for your service |
| Store visits and directions | Performance Max with store goals | Built around a linked Business Profile |
| Calls and booked jobs | Local Services Ads | Adwave notes Google may [reimburse an unhappy customer up to a lifetime cap](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-mobile-mechanics) |

Location-targeted Google Ads can [start at $10 to $50 a day](https://geotargetly.com/blog/what-is-hyperlocal-marketing), with competitive industries needing more. If you want visual reach beyond search, the [Demand Gen guide](/blog/google-demand-gen-campaigns-local-business) explains how those campaigns differ.

## How Do You Test an App Campaign Without Overspending?

Run the test for longer than a weekend. Adwave says [social media campaigns typically need two to four weeks to optimize](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners), and app campaigns also rely on machine learning, so a very short run mostly shows the start-up phase.

Set the conversion you care about before spending. For a salon, that is a first booking. For a café, a first order. An install on its own is not the goal. Google can count in-app events as conversions through Firebase, so check the platform's live help page for the current setup steps.

If you would rather have someone else set this up, compare the options in the [AI vs human marketing comparison](/blog/ai-vs-human-marketing-local-business-honest-comparison) before you hire.

## When Do Paid Installs Pay Back?

Every input below is an **assumption** for illustration. Replace them with your own test results.

| Input or output | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget over 30 days (assumption) | $300, about $10 a day |
| Cost per install (assumption) | $1.50, inside the vendor range |
| Installs bought | 200 |
| Share of installs that place a first order (assumption) | 15% |
| Paying users | 30 |
| Cost per paying user | $10.00 |
| Margin per paying user over a year (assumption) | $25 |
| Total margin from paying users | $750 |
| Net result after ad spend | +$450 |

At these assumed figures, the test pays back. The break-even cost per install is the margin per paying user multiplied by the order rate: $25 × 15% = $3.75. At a $4 install cost, the cost per paying user rises to about $26.67, which is above the $25 margin, so the campaign loses money.

The order rate matters more than the bid. Small changes to the share of installs that become paying users move the result more than changing the cost per install does.

## How Can a Local Business Get Installs Without Ads?

Put the app offer where customers already are. A printed QR code on the counter or on a receipt is one option. An Adwave page notes that [100% of people scanning a QR code are on a phone](https://adwave.com/resources/ad-with-qr-code), so the page they land on has to load quickly and work on a small screen.

Expect a slow path too. B2B Lead Finder states that [most local business owners need 3–5 touchpoints before they buy](https://b2bleadfinder.io/blog/list-of-businesses-without-websites). One install is rarely a sale, so plan follow-up messages and an offer that rewards the second visit.

## FAQ

### Do App campaigns work for a business without an app?

No. An install campaign needs an app that people can download from a store. Without one, the budget has nothing to promote, so use Search, Performance Max or Local Services Ads instead.

### How much do App campaigns cost?

Adwave gives an average of [$0.50 to $3.00 per install](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-mobile-apps) for many categories, and higher ranges for subscription and finance apps. Run your own test, because your category and location will change the figure.

### Should I choose App campaigns or Local Services Ads?

For calls and booked jobs, Local Services Ads fit better than an app install campaign. Adwave notes that Google [may reimburse an unhappy customer up to a lifetime cap](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-mobile-mechanics) for bookings made through them.

### Can I test an app campaign with a small budget?

Yes. A $10 a day test is at the low end of the [$10 to $50 a day](https://geotargetly.com/blog/what-is-hyperlocal-marketing) range cited for location-targeted Google Ads. Run it for weeks, not days, and judge it by paying users rather than installs.
