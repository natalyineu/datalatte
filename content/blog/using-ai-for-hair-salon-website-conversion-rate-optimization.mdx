Take a salon website with 400 visits a month. If 3% of visitors tap the booking button and 60% of those finish booking, that is about 7 bookings. Lift the tap rate to 4.5% and it is about 11, which is roughly 4 extra appointments a month, or $324 at a $90 ticket. All of those inputs are my assumptions, not benchmarks, and the full table is below. The honest takeaway is that a small site can only move so far on conversion alone, so the work has to be cheap, and AI is useful mostly because it makes the cheap part cheaper.

This guide is for salon owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It shows how to use an AI assistant to audit your site, rewrite the pages that matter, fix the booking path, and check your own numbers. It also explains why most salons should not run A/B tests, using a calculation you can do on the back of an envelope.

## What AI Is Good At Here, and What It Is Not

AI assistants are strong at reading a page the way a stranger would and telling you what is unclear. They are weak at knowing your real numbers, your prices or your chairs. Use them for these jobs:

- **A cold read of each page.** Paste in the text of your home page and ask what a first-time visitor would not understand.
- **Rewriting service descriptions** so each one says what it includes, how long it takes and where to book.
- **Drafting answers** to the questions clients ask before booking.
- **Writing alt text and page titles** that describe the real content.

And these are jobs for you, not for AI: choosing what to charge, deciding which photos are honest, and confirming every fact.

Google's guidance on generative AI content is blunt about that last point. It says models "don't retrieve facts, but predict a likely sequence of words", so outputs can contain hallucinations, and it advises you to "manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing". The review extends to title elements, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text. Google also warns that generating many pages without adding value may violate its spam policy on scaled content abuse ([Google Search Central: Using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)). For a salon, the practical rule is simple: fix the pages you have, do not mass-produce "hair salon in every suburb" pages.

## Step 1: Measure Your Baseline First

You cannot tell whether a change worked without a starting number. You need two counts per month: visits to your site, and taps on your booking button or phone number. Our [salon Google Analytics guide](/blog/google-analytics-for-hair-salons) shows how to set that up. If your booking software is on a separate domain, also note how many bookings it reports, so you can see how many button taps become bookings.

Write down the three numbers before you change anything: visits, booking taps and completed bookings.

## Step 2: Run the AI Audit, Page by Page

Start with the page most visitors see, usually the home page, then your main service page and your booking page. For each, paste the visible text into an AI assistant with a prompt like this:

> "You are a first-time client choosing a hair salon in [town]. Read this page. List (1) anything you do not understand, (2) questions you still have before booking, (3) where you would expect a booking button and could not find one, and (4) any claim that sounds vague. Do not rewrite anything yet."

Then read the answer with a skeptical eye. A good audit usually turns up the same handful of problems:

| Common problem | The fix |
| --- | --- |
| No price signal anywhere | Add "from" prices or a price range for core services |
| Service names only a stylist understands | Rename "Balayage + toner" to what the client gets and how long it takes |
| The booking button appears once, at the bottom | Put it at the top and repeat it after each service block |
| No answer on first-visit questions (consultation, patch tests, parking, cancellation) | Add a short FAQ block written from your real policies |
| Photos with no context | Caption each with the service and approximate time |

AI can draft the replacement text. You supply the facts: prices, durations, policies and the name of the stylist.

## Step 3: Fix the Booking Path

The cheapest gains on a salon site usually come from cutting the steps between "I like this" and "I'm booked". Check these on your own phone, because many clients will arrive on one:

1. **A tap-to-call number and a booking button visible without scrolling.**
2. **One booking link** that opens the booking system straight to your service list, not a login page.
3. **Your hours, address and map** on the page the button is on.
4. **A Google Business Profile booking link** that goes to the same page. Google's help page explains you can add booking links from the Booking section of your profile, up to 10 per booking-related category, and mark one as your preferred link ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6218037)). Google's page also notes that some third-party booking links may appear automatically, so check what your listing already shows.
5. **Speed.** Google's PageSpeed Insights reports real-user field data from the Chrome User Experience Report over a 28-day window, plus a simulated lab test ([PageSpeed Insights documentation](https://developers.google.com/speed/docs/insights/v5/about)). A page passes Core Web Vitals when the 75th percentiles of all three metrics are in the "Good" band. Those thresholds are 2.5 seconds or less for Largest Contentful Paint, 200 milliseconds or less for Interaction to Next Paint and 0.1 or less for Cumulative Layout Shift ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)). Large unoptimised photos are the usual culprit on salon sites. If your website builder is part of the problem, our [Squarespace review](/blog/squarespace-for-local-business-review) covers what it does and does not let you fix.

## Step 4: Add Trust Where the Decision Happens

Visitors deciding on a stylist look for proof. Put two or three real reviews next to the booking button, name your stylists with photos, and state your policies in plain words. We cover the order of priority in [trust signals for small business websites](/blog/trust-signals-website).

One AI warning belongs here. Never use AI to write reviews or testimonials. The US Federal Trade Commission's rule on fake reviews bars creating or selling fake reviews and testimonials, including those that misrepresent that they are by someone who does not exist, "such as AI-generated fake reviews", and lets the FTC seek civil penalties against knowing violators ([FTC press release, August 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). Ask real clients for real reviews, and use AI only to draft the polite request message.

## Step 5: Decide Whether a Chat Assistant Belongs on the Site

A chat widget that answers hours, prices and availability can capture visitors who would otherwise leave. It is also the step most likely to go wrong, because a bot that invents a price or a time slot costs you a client. Before you buy one, read our guide to [AI agents for hair salons](/blog/ai-agents-for-hair-salons), which covers what such tools cost, what they should be allowed to do and the guardrails to write first. If your booking software already includes automated replies, test that before paying for a second tool.

## Why Most Salons Should Not A/B Test

An A/B test splits visitors between two versions of a page and compares conversion. It needs a lot of visitors, which a one-site salon does not have. A simple rule of thumb (Robert Lehr's, from a 1992 paper in Statistics in Medicine) estimates the sample for each version as n = 16 x s^2 / d^2, for 80% power at a 5% two-sided significance level, where for proportions s^2 is p x (1 - p) using the average of the two rates and d is the difference to detect ([Lehr, 1992](https://jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca/c607/ch07/16_s_sq_over_d_sq.pdf)). It is a ballpark, as Lehr says, not a substitute for proper software.

Apply it to a lift from 3% to 4%. The average rate is 3.5%, so s^2 = 0.035 x 0.965 = 0.0338, and d = 0.01. That gives n = 16 x 0.0338 / 0.0001, or about 5,400 visitors for each version, 10,800 in total. At 400 visits a month, that is more than two years. Meanwhile Google has stopped offering its free Optimize testing tool: Google Analytics Help says Optimize and Optimize 360 are no longer available as of September 30, 2023, and that Google is investing in third-party A/B testing integrations ([Google Analytics Help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12979939)).

So test the way a small site can:

- **Make bigger changes, not button-colour tweaks.** Only big effects show up in small numbers.
- **Change one thing at a time** and compare four weeks before with four weeks after.
- **Watch the seasons.** Wedding season or the week before Christmas will swamp your result, so compare like with like.
- **Judge on bookings, not visits.** Tap and booking counts matter more than bounce rate.

The bigger levers are usually the number of visitors (a complete Google profile and local search) and what happens after the tap: reply speed, reminders and rebooking. Do the cheap fixes here first, then put your effort where the volume is.

## Do This Today

1. Write down last month's website visits, booking-button taps and completed bookings.
2. Paste your home page text into an AI assistant with the audit prompt above and list the answers that surprise you.
3. Add a booking button at the top of the page and after each service, plus a tap-to-call number.
4. Run your booking page through PageSpeed Insights on mobile and note the three Core Web Vitals results.
5. Check that your Google Business Profile booking link opens the same page, then set a reminder to compare four weeks before and after.

## FAQ

**Q: Can AI redesign my salon website for me?**
A: It can draft copy and point out confusing sections, but it does not know your prices, policies or chair availability. You must check every fact it writes, as Google's own guidance advises for AI-generated content.

**Q: Do I need an A/B testing tool?**
A: Probably not. By the rule of thumb above, detecting a lift from 3% to 4% needs roughly 5,400 visitors per version, far more than a typical salon site gets. Make larger changes and compare before and after instead.

**Q: Will AI-written text hurt my Google rankings?**
A: Google's guidance says to fact-check and review AI-generated content before publishing and warns that mass-producing pages with no added value may breach its spam policy. Keep everything specific to your salon and edit it yourself.

**Q: Is a chatbot worth it?**
A: It can be, if it only does a few things well and cannot invent prices or slots. Start with the guardrails in our AI agents guide, and test the tool as a client would before it goes live.

**Q: What is a good conversion rate for a salon website?**
A: Published benchmarks mix industries and definitions, so use your own baseline. Compare your own month with the previous month, not with a number from a blog.
