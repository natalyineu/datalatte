A pet groomer should automate four things first: booking confirmations and reminders, a rebooking message after each groom, a review request, and a win-back message for lapsed clients. Most grooming booking software already does the first one. MoeGo's mobile grooming plans, for example, start at $49 a month with automated reminders by text, email and phone.

This guide shows what to automate, what it costs, and the legal rules for texts and emails. Prices and rules come from the sources listed at the end. The example is labelled as an assumption, and no result numbers are promised.

## What Should a Pet Groomer Automate First?

Start with the messages that protect your calendar. A reminder before the visit reduces forgotten slots, and a confirmation right after booking removes doubt. Next comes the message after the groom: thanks, a rebooking link and a review ask. Last, a short note to clients who have not booked in a while.

Skip anything that needs personal judgment, such as complaints, matted-coat decisions or price disputes. Our guide to [email marketing for pet groomers](/blog/pet-groomer-email-marketing) and [SMS marketing for pet groomers](/blog/sms-marketing-for-pet-groomers) cover the message wording.

## What Are the Rules for Texts and Emails?

For email, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide lists eight requirements, including accurate sender details, a non-deceptive subject line, a valid postal address, a clear opt-out and honoring opt-outs within 10 business days. Each violating email can carry penalties up to $53,088. The law makes no exception for business-to-business email.

For texts, US rules treat marketing messages differently from service messages,. Collect clear written permission when a client books, store it, and honor STOP at once. Ask a lawyer or your software vendor about the current text rules in your state.

## How Do You Ask for Reviews Without Breaking Google's Rules?

Google's Business Profile help says you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code. It also says offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. The Maps content policy adds that merchants may not selectively solicit positive reviews or discourage negative ones.

So send the same review link to every client after the visit, without a discount attached. Do not ask only happy clients. Our [guide to automating Google review requests](/blog/how-to-automate-google-review-requests) shows the setup, and the [Google Business Profile guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-for-pet-groomers) covers the rest of the profile.

## How Do You Automate Rebooking?

Rebooking is the highest-value automation because grooming is a repeat service. Tie a message to the visit date: for example, a note at the end of the groom with a link to book the next slot, and a second note near the interval you usually recommend. Pick the interval from your own appointment history, not a generic rule.

For clients who have lapsed, a short message with a booking link works better than a long newsletter. See [how to keep pet grooming clients long term](/blog/how-to-retain-pet-grooming-clients-long-term) for the retention side. The market is large: the American Pet Products Association reports 95 million US households own a pet and $14.3 billion spent in 2024 on services such as grooming, boarding and training, but this says nothing about your local demand.

## FAQ

### Do I need to be technical to set up automation?

Most grooming software ships with ready reminder templates. Turn them on, edit the wording to sound like you, and send a test to your own phone first.

### Can automation replace talking to clients?

It handles repeat messages. Keep calls and personal replies for complaints, health notes and new puppies.

### Is it legal to text clients reminders?

Appointment reminders and marketing texts are treated differently, and rules change. Collect consent when booking and honor opt-outs. Confirm current rules with your vendor or a lawyer.

### Can I give a discount for a review?

No. Google's help says incentives for reviews are strictly prohibited.

### How do I know it works?

Compare no-shows, rebooking rate and review count for the 60 days before and after. Do not rely on any generic percentage.
