To set up Nextdoor ads, claim your free Nextdoor Business Page, open the Ads tab, choose a goal (website visits, a sale or discount, or messages), pick your target area by radius or ZIP code, set a daily budget, then upload a photo-led ad with one clear local offer. Nextdoor says its current ad flow replaced per-neighborhood pricing with a daily budget and lets you target a radius of up to 30 miles around your business ([Nextdoor Ads update](https://business.nextdoor.com/local/localdealstransition), checked Oct 2026). Plan on a test of about a month before you judge it.

Picture a Saturday morning. A neighbor posts: "Can anyone recommend a good groomer? Our dog is a mess." People reply with names. If yours is not one of them, an ad will not fix that alone. Nextdoor says a business with just one recommendation sees 3x more page views and 4x more messages ([Nextdoor recommendations guide](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/blog/small-business-guide-to-getting-nextdoor-recommendations), checked Oct 2026). So the order is: page first, recommendations second, ads third.

Nextdoor reaches people in your immediate area who are already reading local news and recommendations. It is not the highest-volume platform. For cafes, salons, groomers and yoga studios it reaches people who could walk to your door today. Menu names, minimums and formats change, so check the Ads tab for current options.

## What Nextdoor ads are

Nextdoor is a neighborhood social network. Its current ad setup offers three goals ([Nextdoor Ads update](https://business.nextdoor.com/local/localdealstransition), checked Oct 2026):

- **Increase website visits:** image-based ads that send people to your site.
- **Promote a sale or discount:** the updated Local Deal format, built around a specific offer such as 10% off or a free first class.
- **Get more messages:** text-based ads that encourage people to contact you directly.

Unlike Google, Nextdoor ads are awareness-driven. Nobody on Nextdoor is typing "hair salon near me". They are reading neighborhood news. Your ad has to create the desire, not capture it.

## The 7 steps

### Step 1: Claim your Business Page

Claim your free page at **business.nextdoor.com**. Nextdoor describes it as a free way to build an online presence, collect recommendations and post unlimited free updates ([Nextdoor Business Page](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/getting-started/business-page), checked Oct 2026). Nextdoor pulls initial details from third-party public data, so search for your business and correct anything wrong. Fill every field: category, phone, hours, website, and photos of the exterior, interior, products or services, and your team.

### Step 2: Open the Ads tab

Sign in and open the Ads tab to create ads, manage them and see a performance dashboard ([Nextdoor Ads update](https://business.nextdoor.com/local/localdealstransition), checked Oct 2026).

### Step 3: Choose a goal

For most local service businesses starting out, **Increase website visits** or **Get more messages** fits best, because you want people to book or contact you. Use **Promote a sale or discount** for a time-limited offer.

### Step 4: Set targeting

Nextdoor says you can set the target area with a slider up to 30 miles around your business, or choose ZIP codes within your radius ([Nextdoor Ads update](https://business.nextdoor.com/local/localdealstransition), [ZIP code targeting](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/small-business/resources/blog/zip-code-targeting-on-nextdoor), checked Oct 2026). Older neighborhood-based targeting was converted to ZIP code targeting.

Start tight: the ZIP codes or radius your customers realistically travel from. Nextdoor's agency product also advertises targeting by demographics and interests ([Nextdoor for public agencies](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/public-agency/ads), checked Oct 2026), but small-business filters may differ, so check your Ads tab. For a cafe or fitness studio, broad targeting inside your area usually beats heavy filtering, which starves a small audience.

### Step 5: Set the budget

Nextdoor now uses a daily budget and duration, with three recommended starter plans or a custom one ([Nextdoor Ads update](https://business.nextdoor.com/local/localdealstransition), checked Oct 2026). We could not confirm a published minimum or CPM, so read the numbers in your own Ads tab. A very low daily budget will run but may not give enough impressions to judge. Pick a test budget you can hold for a month, and compare cost per customer, not cost per impression. The next section shows how.

### Step 6: Create the ad

Nextdoor's creative specs list newsfeed, For Sale and right-hand-rail placements. Images can be square (1080 x 1080 pixels) or rectangular (1080 x 540 pixels), in JPG or PNG up to 30 MB, and headlines can be up to 120 characters. Nextdoor advises that lifestyle photography performs better than images with words ([Nextdoor creative specs](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/small-business/resources/ads-creative-specs), checked Oct 2026). Specs vary by placement, so check the current page.

```
Headline: "[Business Name]: [One-line value prop]"
Body: Specific offer or reason to visit, plus nearest landmark or intersection
CTA: Book Now / Learn More / Call Now
```

Example for a hair salon: headline "Glow Hair Studio: New Clients Welcome", body "First colour service 20% off. 2 blocks from Central Park. Online booking open.", CTA Book Now.

### Step 7: Run it for a month, then review

A two-week test is usually too short. You can pause or cancel with a toggle and edit the target area, copy, photo, call to action and daily budget from the dashboard.

## The math: what a test must earn

Nextdoor gives no public price list we could confirm, so work backward from what a customer is worth.

**Example only, with invented inputs. Swap in your own.** A salon runs $20 a day for 30 days. Average ticket $85, with 50% left as profit ($42.50 a visit).

| | Counting first visit only | If a new client books 4 visits a year |
|---|---|---|
| Test spend | $20 x 30 = $600 | $600 |
| Profit per new client | $42.50 | $42.50 x 4 = $170 |
| New clients to break even | $600 / $42.50 = 14.1, so 15 | $600 / $170 = 3.5, so 4 |
| Per week | about 3.5 clients | about 1 client |

The honest read: judged on the first visit alone, a month of ads needs about 15 new clients. Judged on a year of visits, it needs 4. Which view is right depends on how well you keep clients, so decide before you start. The 4 visits a year is an assumption, not a statistic.

## What good creative looks like

People on Nextdoor are in neighborhood mode: community, recommendations, what is nearby.

- **Real photos of your place:** exterior, interior, the team. Not stock.
- **A local reference:** "near Central Station" makes the ad feel relevant.
- **A specific offer:** "Free consultation this week" beats "Come see us".
- **Real social proof:** "Recommended by neighbors" only if you have actual recommendations, and use your real count.

What underperforms: stock photos, vague copy ("best service in town"), no call to action, and small text in a mobile-first feed.

## Before you pay for ads

People who see your ad will click through to your profile. It either validates you or does nothing.

1. **Complete the Business Page** with real photos.
2. **Collect recommendations.** Ask your best local customers for one. In a Nextdoor survey, 72% of members said a business recommendation had influenced them ([Nextdoor report](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/nextdoor-releases-new-insights-report-revealing-the-power-of-neighbor-recommendations), checked Oct 2026).
3. **Post organically** to your neighborhoods a few times: introduce yourself, share a special, answer a question.
4. **Respond** when someone mentions needing a service you offer.

## Tracking results

Nextdoor attribution is less precise than Google Ads, so do not expect a clean "10 bookings came from this ad."

- **Promo code.** A Nextdoor-only code; count redemptions.
- **UTM link.** `?utm_source=nextdoor&utm_medium=paid` shows traffic and conversions in your analytics.
- **"How did you hear about us?"** Underestimates Nextdoor but gives a signal.
- **On/off test.** Run one month, pause one month, compare busy and slow periods.

## Do this today

1. Search for your business on Nextdoor and fix any wrong detail on the page.
2. Upload at least 5 real photos: exterior, interior, team and two of your product or service.
3. Message your 10 best local customers this week and ask each for one recommendation, with a link to your page.
4. Write the break-even from the table above using your own ticket and margin.
5. Create a code like NEIGHBOR10 so you can count redemptions.
6. Only then set the daily budget and choose a 30-day window.

## Nextdoor, Facebook and Google

| Attribute | Nextdoor | Facebook/Meta | Google Ads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience intent | Awareness | Awareness + interest | High intent (searching now) |
| Geographic precision | Radius or ZIP | Radius/zip | Radius/zip |
| Best for | Trust with neighbors | Visual content, retargeting | Capturing search intent |
| Typical timeline | Slower, builds with frequency | Weeks | Often the fastest to show results |

For most local businesses I would start with Google Ads, add Meta Ads for retargeting, and use Nextdoor as the neighborhood awareness layer. Nextdoor alone is rarely enough, but it can amplify the others.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should I spend on Nextdoor ads to see results?**
A: Enough to gather meaningful data over several weeks. Nextdoor offers recommended starter plans and custom daily budgets, so check the options in your Ads tab and pick a test budget you can sustain for at least a month. See our breakdown in [Nextdoor ads cost 2026](/blog/nextdoor-ads-cost-2026).

**Q: Can I target only homeowners on Nextdoor?**
A: Nextdoor has offered audience filters such as homeowner versus renter, which suit home services such as cleaning, painting and lawn care. For salons, cafes and fitness studios, extra filters often shrink your audience too much. Confirm the current options in your Ads tab.

**Q: Is Nextdoor better than Facebook Ads for local businesses?**
A: Neither is universally better. Facebook offers stronger creative formats and retargeting, while Nextdoor offers neighborhood trust. Many local businesses run both with separate budgets. See [Nextdoor vs Facebook local ads](/blog/nextdoor-vs-facebook-local-ads).

**Q: How do I get more Nextdoor recommendations?**
A: Ask your best local customers directly with a text or email that links to your Business Page. Check Nextdoor's current rules before offering any incentive.

**Q: Can I run Nextdoor ads without a website?**
A: Yes, for some goals. The "Get more messages" goal is built around text-based ads that encourage direct contact. A simple landing page still helps with tracking and lead capture.

**Q: How long before Nextdoor ads show results?**
A: Nextdoor builds awareness rather than search intent, so plan for several weeks before judging the channel.
