Connected TV (CTV) advertising lets a Boston business place video ads in front of households watching streaming services such as Hulu, Peacock, and Tubi. Targeting by location means the ad reaches people watching at home rather than commuters or visitors. CTV builds familiarity before a customer needs the business, while search captures demand that already exists. Published sources do not cover CTV pricing, so this guide uses assumption-labelled budgets and benchmarks from Google Ads for the paid search that usually runs alongside.

## What can a Boston business expect from CTV advertising?

CTV ads run on streaming services rather than on search results pages. A Boston coffee shop, salon, or fitness studio can choose a geographic target so the ad is shown to households near the business. The ads are typically longer than social video and play full-screen, which suits brand-building goals.

Clicks are rare in this format. Success depends on brand recall and on whether people later search for the business by name. A business should set its expectations around those outcomes before judging the channel by cost per click.

## Which streaming platforms suit Boston local businesses?

The platforms most often named for local campaigns are Hulu, Peacock, Tubi, Amazon Prime Video with ads, and Paramount+ with Pluto TV. Each platform has a different audience mix, so the choice should follow where the target customers actually watch. A business that serves families and one that serves commuters may need different platforms.

Before committing, request quotes from two or three options and compare the audience data each platform offers for the chosen ZIP codes. Brand familiarity with a platform is a weak reason to choose it, because the audience and price matter more than the name.

## How do Google Ads benchmarks set expectations for CTV?

Benchmarks cannot predict what a CTV campaign will cost, because CTV pricing is outside the sources used here. They do give a reference point for the paid search that often runs alongside. WordStream's compiled averages show that across all industries, search click-through rate is 3.17% and display click-through rate is 0.46%. The average search cost per click is $2.69, and the display average is $0.63 ([WordStream, secondary](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks)).

## How should a Boston business measure a CTV campaign?

Measure CTV through signals that show recall rather than clicks. Compare direct searches for the business name in Google Search Console and the Google Business Profile for the weeks before launch with the flight period. Branded search volume in Google Ads is another signal, since people who remember an ad often search for the name later.

Ask every new customer how they heard about the business, and record the answers in the same format each time. Website direct traffic during the flight is also useful. A few weeks is rarely enough for a brand-building channel, so plan for a campaign long enough for these patterns to appear.

## FAQ

### Is CTV advertising worth it for a small Boston business?

It can be, if the goal is brand recall in a defined area and the budget can support a campaign of several months. Compare the cost with paid search using the same arithmetic shown above. Search delivers clicks quickly, while CTV builds familiarity over time.

### Which platform should a Boston business start with?

Start with the platform where the target customers already watch. Compare audience profiles and quotes from two or three options before committing.

### How do I know if CTV is working?

Look for changes in direct searches for the business name, branded search clicks, and "how did you hear about us" answers during and after the flight. One week of data is not enough to judge the channel.

### Should CTV replace Google Ads?

No. CTV and search serve different stages of the customer journey. Search captures demand that already exists, while CTV builds the name recognition that makes those searches more likely.
