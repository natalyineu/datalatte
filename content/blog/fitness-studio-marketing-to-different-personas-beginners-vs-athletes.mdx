Market to beginners by lowering the barrier to the first visit and making a small weekly habit feel doable. Market to experienced athletes with progress, programming and community. The CDC's adult guideline gives beginners a concrete target of 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus muscle-strengthening on 2 days. Athletes already beat that, so they need a different reason to join you.

No source we opened publishes how many beginners drop out of a typical boutique studio, so this guide avoids that number. It uses three published sources and a worked example with labelled assumptions.

## How Do Beginners and Athletes Differ as Customers?

The two groups ask different questions. A beginner wonders if they will fit in, be able to keep up and afford it. An athlete wants to know whether you will improve their numbers, whether classes are programmed well and who else trains there.

| | Beginner | Experienced athlete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main worry | Looking out of place | Wasting time on easy sessions |
| Message | First visit is easy, coaches show you | Programming, progression, measurable goals |
| Offer | Intro class or short trial | Trial of advanced classes, challenge or event entry |
| First 30 days | Welcome, booking help, early check-ins | Goal setting, progress tracking, community |
| Page to send them to | Class guide for new people | Schedule by level and coach profiles |

This table is a planning frame based on how the groups behave, not a published finding.

## What Should You Tell a Beginner?

Give them a target they can understand. The CDC says adults should aim for 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, and work all major muscle groups on at least 2 days. It also says the total can be split into shorter sessions and that any amount of activity has some benefit.

A studio can translate that into a plan: two or three classes a week, with a short walk or home session filling the gap. Lead with the first visit, not the contract.

## What Do Athletes Respond To?

Athletes join for programming, coaching and people who push them. ACSM's 2026 fitness trends survey, which drew about 2,000 industry professionals, ranks traditional strength training at 7, adult recreation and sport clubs at 9 and functional fitness training at 10. The survey reflects what professionals expect, not what your local athletes will buy.

If you have strength or conditioning classes, show the programming: weekly structure, progressions and how results are tracked. Our [guide to Reels for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-instagram-reels) covers short videos that show coaching.

## Which Trends Suit Other Groups?

ACSM's same list ranks fitness programs for older adults second, exercise for weight management third and balance, flow and core strength fifth. Those categories often attract newer exercisers, so a studio with gentle classes can speak to them directly.

Be careful with health claims. Describe what the class involves and who it suits, and leave medical promises out of ads.

## How Fast Should You Reach Out to Lapsed Members? (Worked Example)

A retrospective study of 5,242 members of one fitness centre in Rio de Janeiro followed each person for 12 months after they cancelled. About 38% of dropouts returned to exercise within 12 months, and more than half of those returned within the first month. That is one gym in one country, so treat it as a prompt to test, not a forecast.

Here is the arithmetic with **assumptions**. A studio has 50 members who lapsed this year. If 38% returned, that is 19 people. If just over half came back in the first month, that is about 10 returns, so the first-month message matters most.

| Step | Assumption | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lapsed members | 50 | |
| Return rate (study figure, not yours) | 38% | 19 |
| Returned in month one | about half | about 10 |

## How Should You Split Your Budget?

No source gives a correct split between beginners and athletes, so decide by your goal. If classes are empty, favour intro offers. If beginners join but leave, spend on onboarding and retention. If your advanced classes are full and beginner classes are half empty, put the budget behind beginners.

## FAQ

### What percentage of beginners drop out?

No reliable figure for studios is published. Retellings of a 2016 Brazilian gym study give conflicting numbers, so we do not quote them. Track your own 30, 60 and 90 day retention instead.

### How do I market to athletes at my studio?

Show programming, coaching credentials and progress tracking. Offer a trial of your harder classes and invite them to an event or challenge, so the first visit feels like a good fit.

### Should beginners and athletes train in the same classes?

That depends on your format. If they do, offer clear options within the class and tell newcomers which level to start with, so nobody feels lost.

### How can I convert beginners into regulars?

Help them reach a weekly pattern. The CDC's 150-minute target gives you something to build toward, and early check-ins help them book the next class before motivation drops.
