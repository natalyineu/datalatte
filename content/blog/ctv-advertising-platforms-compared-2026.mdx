Small businesses should start with self-serve CTV tools that do not require an agency: Tubi (through Universal Ads), Roku Ads Manager, Disney Campaign Manager (Hulu and Disney+) and Google's YouTube-on-TV placements. Premium platforms such as Peacock, Max and Amazon are usually bought through sales teams, DSPs or aggregators, and carry larger budgets.

You have $1,000 and a vague feeling that streaming TV is where your customers went. Picture a Tuesday night: someone on a couch, a full-screen 15-second spot for your salon, no search box in sight. Then picture the dashboard a month later showing 40,000 impressions and zero bookings you can name. That is the usual CTV story, and the gap is almost always in the setup, not the screen.

Two dates matter for your shortlist. Roku launched Ads Manager in September 2024, and Fox announced Tubi's self-serve platform in May 2025. Both mean a small business can now buy without a sales call. Minimums, CPMs and targeting change often, and many published figures come from vendors who sell the product, so this guide compares how each route works and what to ask. Where a figure is not confirmed in an official source, we do not give one.

## The Two Ways to Buy CTV

**Direct or self-serve platform buys.** You buy from the streamer's own tool or sales team. Tubi, Roku and Disney each offer a self-serve tool. Larger streamers sell mainly through ad sales teams and demand-side platforms (DSPs).

**Aggregators and DSPs.** Platforms such as Vibe.co, Skybeam (Simulmedia), StackAdapt and Adwave pool inventory from several publishers so you can buy across many apps in one account. You usually trade some targeting precision for access at a smaller budget. Each sets its own minimum, so compare them in writing.

For a small local budget, a self-serve tool or an aggregator is the practical starting point.

## Platform-by-Platform Breakdown

### Tubi (Fox)

Fox announced a self-serve ad platform for Tubi, powered by Universal Ads, in May 2025. It is aimed at small and medium-sized businesses of all sizes ([Fox Corporation](https://www.foxcorporation.com/?p=6482), checked Oct 2026). Tubi is free and ad-supported, so it is a natural place for a small test. Ask about minimum spend, geo-targeting and creative tools when you set up an account; the Fox announcement does not state a minimum.

### Roku Ads Manager

Roku launched Ads Manager in September 2024 as a self-service tool for buying CTV video ads, with shoppable campaigns that connect to Shopify Checkout ([Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2024/09/18/roku-launches-ads-manager/), checked Oct 2026). Roku does not appear to publish an official minimum in that announcement, so confirm current budget requirements inside the tool.

### Hulu and Disney+ (Disney Campaign Manager)

Disney Campaign Manager is Disney's streaming ad-buying platform for Disney+ and Hulu, letting you set up, launch and optimize campaigns in one place ([Disney Advertising](https://disneyadsales.com/), checked Oct 2026). Check the current minimum campaign budget and targeting options in the tool before planning.

### YouTube on connected TV

Google's help page states that all video action campaigns were upgraded to Demand Gen as of May 2026, so that older campaign type is no longer something you set up new ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10147229?hl=en), checked Oct 2026). The same page says ads can run on YouTube and Google video partners, including in the YouTube app on TV. Google says Demand Gen campaigns that include TV screens drove an average of 7% more conversions at the same ROI, based on an internal Google experiment from 2025, so treat that as a vendor claim ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/demand-gen-drop-january-2026/), checked Oct 2026). QR codes for CTV are available on Demand Gen and Performance Max campaigns, and only some TVs support them ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16903036?hl=en), checked Oct 2026). If you already run Google Ads, a Demand Gen campaign is the lowest-friction way to test TV-screen placements, and you can check device settings to see where your video ads served.

### Peacock, Max, Pluto TV, Samsung TV Plus and Amazon

These are generally reached through sales teams, DSPs or aggregators. Amazon's DSP, for example, lists Prime Video, Twitch and Thursday Night Football among its inventory ([Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/amazon-dsp), checked Oct 2026), but premium and live-sports inventory is priced accordingly. For live NFL options see our [Amazon Prime Video Thursday Night Football guide](/blog/amazon-prime-video-thursday-night-football-ads) and the [Peacock live sports guide](/blog/peacock-live-sports-advertising-local-business). Unless you have an established budget and a metro-wide audience, start with the self-serve options above.

## How to Compare Platforms

Ask every platform or DSP the same questions and put the answers in a spreadsheet:

| Question | Why it matters |
|----------|---------------|
| What is the minimum campaign or daily budget? | Sets whether you can test at all |
| What CPM should I expect for my targeting? | Lets you estimate impressions |
| Can I target by ZIP code or DMA? | Local businesses waste money on broad targeting |
| Which apps and channels will my ads run on? | Inventory differs between a single streamer and an aggregator |
| What reporting and measurement do I get? | You need more than impressions |
| Are there platform or creative fees? | Hidden fees change cost per impression |

To estimate impressions, divide your budget by the quoted CPM and multiply by 1,000. A $1,000 budget at a $25 CPM buys about 40,000 impressions. Spread across a metro area, that is enough to test an offer, not to saturate a market.

## The Math: What $1,000 Buys on CTV

Example only. The CPMs, frequency and response rate below are assumptions for illustration, not quotes from any platform and not market statistics. Swap in the numbers from your written quote.

Assumptions: budget $1,000, each household sees the ad 3 times, 0.1% of households become customers.

| Quoted CPM (assumed) | Impressions | Households (impressions / 3) | Customers at 0.1% | Cost per customer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $15 | 66,667 | 22,222 | about 22 | about $45 |
| $25 | 40,000 | 13,333 | about 13 | about $75 |
| $35 | 28,571 | 9,524 | about 9.5 | about $105 |

If your average first visit is worth $60, the $25 row loses about $15 per new customer on the first visit, and wins only if customers come back. That is the honest test: CTV pays when lifetime value, not the first ticket, covers the $75. Compare the same $1,000 on search at your own cost per click before you decide.

## Third-Party Data and Targeting

DSPs let you layer audience segments from data providers on top of geography. This can improve relevance but usually adds a data fee on top of media cost, so ask for it to be itemized. At small budgets, standard geographic and demographic targeting is usually enough. Add data layers only once the basic campaign is producing results you can measure.

## Do This Today

1. Open Roku Ads Manager or Tubi's self-serve page and write down the minimum budget shown.
2. Ask two aggregators for a written CPM for your ZIP codes.
3. Create one promo code or vanity URL for CTV, and nothing else, so you can count it.
4. Put the cost-per-customer table above into a spreadsheet with your own average ticket and repeat rate.
5. Ask the first 10 customers this week whether they use Tubi, Roku or Hulu. Decide on the answers, not on a hunch.

## How to Choose a CTV Platform

- **Small test budget:** start with a self-serve tool (Tubi, Roku, Disney Campaign Manager) or an aggregator with a low published minimum.
- **Mid-sized budget:** compare an SMB-focused DSP that covers several streamers against a single self-serve tool.
- **Larger budget:** consider a multi-channel DSP, and add website retargeting once you have traffic to retarget.
- **Always:** use a promo code, vanity URL or tracked phone number so you can tell whether the campaign produced bookings. Read our [CTV cost and CPM guide](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data) before setting a budget.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best CTV advertising platform for a small business?**
A: There is no single best platform. Tubi, Roku Ads Manager, Disney Campaign Manager and YouTube on TV all offer self-serve routes, so compare their minimums, targeting and reporting for your market.

**Q: Can I run CTV ads on a small monthly budget?**
A: Often yes, because several self-serve platforms and aggregators allow small campaigns without an agency. Confirm each platform's current minimum, and remember a small budget buys a limited number of impressions, enough to test creative and offers but not to saturate a market.

**Q: How do I measure if my CTV ad actually worked?**
A: Use promo codes, vanity URLs and call tracking, then compare bookings or foot traffic during the campaign with the prior 30 days. Platforms report impressions, but trackable actions are what pay the bills. See our guide to [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).

**Q: What is the difference between CTV ads and YouTube ads?**
A: YouTube is largely user-initiated (search or browse) and offers deep Google targeting, while CTV ads play during streaming shows in a full-screen, lean-back setting. YouTube campaigns can also serve on TV screens ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10147229?hl=en)). For small budgets, YouTube and search often do better for direct response, and CTV is typically stronger for awareness. Our [CTV vs traditional TV comparison](/blog/connected-tv-ads-vs-traditional-tv-ads-why-ctv-wins-for-local-business) covers more.

**Q: Should I run CTV if I already use Google Ads or Meta Ads?**
A: Usually only after those channels are working. Treat CTV as a test layer with a small share of your ad budget, and compare cost per booking against your search and social results.

**Q: Is CTV worth it for a home-service business like a plumber?**
A: Often not as a first channel. Home-service demand is mostly triggered by an urgent need, so search ads tend to be more efficient. Test CTV only after search is optimized, and track calls with unique numbers.
