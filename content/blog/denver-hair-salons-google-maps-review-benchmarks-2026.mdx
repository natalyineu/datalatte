## What the numbers mean for a new hair salon

**Expect a high rating bar.** When most nearby salons average close to five stars, one or two lukewarm reviews on a small base are very visible. Early on, every review carries weight, so the quality of every appointment matters.

**Do not obsess over the 200 mark.** Over half of the salons we analysed have 200 or more reviews, but nearly all of them have been collecting for years. A salon with 60 recent, detailed reviews can still look credible, especially if those reviews mention specific services and stylists.

**Make asking part of the appointment.** Salons have a natural moment that coffee shops do not: the mirror reveal. A client who loves their colour or cut is the most likely to leave a good review in that minute. Ask then, or send a text with the link before they get to the car.

**Use your stylists.** Reviews that name a stylist and a service ("balayage", "men's cut", "curly cut") help future clients self-select and add relevant language to your listing. Encourage clients to say what they had done, without scripting what they say.

**Fill in every part of the profile.** With fewer than 50 reviews, completeness is your lever: services with descriptions, booking link, photos of finished work (with client permission), accurate hours and your category. ## What the numbers mean for an established hair salon

If you already sit above the median, you are in the majority group. Roughly three in five analysed salons have 200 or more reviews, so volume alone no longer sets you apart.

**Differentiate on recency and detail.** Look at your newest ten reviews. Are they recent? Do they mention services, not just "great salon"? If your review stream stalled a year ago, restart it.

**Defend a very high rating.** At a 4.87 market average, a handful of low reviews can drag you below the pack. Reply to criticism quickly and offer a fix. Many unhappy clients will update a review after a good resolution, though you should never pressure anyone to do so.

**Reply to every review.** In a market full of strong ratings, replies are one of the few ways to show personality. Mention the service or stylist, keep it short and sign with a real name.

**Spread the load across your team.** If all of your reviews are for one stylist, new stylists will struggle to fill their books. Encourage reviews that name each team member.

**Check your details quarterly.** Prices, services and booking links go stale. Our [local SEO guide for Denver hair salons](/blog/local-seo-for-hair-salons-in-denver-co) goes deeper on keeping everything consistent across Google and other directories.

## A 30-day review and Google Business Profile plan

Plan on about 30 to 45 minutes per week, plus a few seconds per client.

### Week 1: Audit and clean up

1. **Verify ownership** of your Google Business Profile and confirm that name, address, phone and website match your signage and booking page.
2. **Check categories**: choose the one that best describes you (for example, hair salon) and add secondary categories only if they are true.
3. **List services** with short, plain descriptions and, where you are comfortable, starting prices.
4. **Add your booking link** so a visitor can book from the listing.
5. **Read your last 20 reviews** and note what people praise (a stylist, a service, the atmosphere) so you can reflect it in your photos and descriptions.

### Week 2: Build the asking habit

1. **Create your review link and QR code** from your profile.
2. **Set a moment**: at the mirror reveal, at checkout or in your post-visit text message. Choose one and stick with it.
3. **Write a one-sentence ask** your team can say naturally, such as "If you loved it, a quick Google review helps us a lot."
4. **Never incentivise**: no discounts, freebies or contests in exchange for reviews, and do not only ask people you expect to leave five stars. Both break Google's rules.
5. **Reply to every review** from the past 60 days.

### Week 3: Show your work

1. **Upload ten to fifteen photos** of finished work, your space and your team, with client consent where faces are visible.
2. **Publish a Google post** announcing a seasonal service or open slots.
3. **Check your Q&A** and add answers to common questions such as patch tests, consultation policy and cancellation.
4. **Ask returning clients** who already love you. Existing clients are often your best source of reviews, because they have the most to say.

### Week 4: Review the results

1. **Count new reviews** and note how many mention a service and stylist.
2. **Check Performance** in your profile for calls, direction requests and website or booking clicks.
3. **Make one change**: if the mirror moment worked, add a QR code to the station mirror cards. If the text message worked better, automate it. Our guide to [automating Google review requests](/blog/how-to-automate-google-review-requests) explains how.

## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a Denver hair salon need?

In our October 2026 sample, the median Denver salon had 203 reviews and about 55% had 200 or more. That is the market you will be compared against. You do not need to match it on day one, but you should build a steady stream of genuine reviews.

### What is a good Google rating for a hair salon in Denver?

The average across the 84 salons we analysed was 4.87. Staying close to that average keeps you in line with local expectations. A rating well under it may make you look weaker than competitors, especially with a smaller review count.

### Will more reviews get me booked solid?

More reviews help people trust you, but they are not a guarantee. Availability, pricing, photos of your work, location and a smooth booking process all influence who books. Think of reviews as one part of a healthy profile.

### Do reviews affect Google Maps ranking?

Google says local ranking depends on relevance, distance and prominence, and review information is part of prominence. How much weight any one factor has is not public, so focus on what you can control: genuine, recent, detailed reviews, good replies and complete profile information.

### What if a client leaves an unfair review?

Reply politely and factually, without sharing private client details. If it breaks Google's content policies, you can report it through your profile. Do not argue in public.

### Can stylists in a shared suite have their own profile?

That depends on your setup and Google's guidelines for practitioners and shared locations. Check the current Google Business Profile guidelines before creating a second listing for the same address.
