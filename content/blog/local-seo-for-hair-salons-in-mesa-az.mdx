Hair salons in Mesa, AZ win Google map pack spots by completing their Google Business Profile, keeping name, address and phone details identical across the web, and collecting reviews that name specific services. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today) defines the local pack as the top three business listings in local search results. This guide covers the profile checklist, citation rules, review signals, a booking calculation and how to measure progress.

## What decides who appears in the Mesa map pack?

The local pack is built from Business Profile data. [Adwave's lawn care guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business) states that the top three businesses in the map pack are pulled directly from their Google Business Profile listings. A salon that wants a place in that group therefore needs a profile that is complete, accurate and active.

Ranking weight is debated, so treat the numbers as directional. An [Adwave multi-location audit](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit) cites a 2025 benchmark that puts Business Profile signals at 32% of local pack ranking weight. A separate [Adwave guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) gives the same 32% without naming a source. [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo) reports that in a BrightLocal survey, 36% of SEOs in 2021 rated the profile the most important map pack factor, up from 23% in 2013.

## How do you set up the profile so it can rank?

Start with the category. Adwave recommends reviewing the top three to five businesses in local Google Maps results for your service search and noting which category they use, then choosing the primary category that matches. The [same guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) says the primary category is the top ranking factor for entering the local pack, based on analysis whose source is not named.

Next, write the description. [Adwave's beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) notes that a Business Profile description allows 750 characters. Lead with the service, the neighbourhood and one differentiator, and avoid repeating keywords.

Hours, phone number and address must be accurate. [Adwave's marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) says they should be 100% accurate and recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, since a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls.

## Which profile features bring clients to book?

Posts are free and appear on the profile in Search and Maps, according to [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Google [reviews each post](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en) and shows it publicly only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not display, so check status after publishing. Adwave suggests at least one new post a week.

Booking is the second feature. [Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing) states that Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search or Maps in under 30 seconds. Whether your booking system qualifies depends on Google's current setup, so check the live help page before promoting it.

If you work from home or serve clients at their location, you can hide your street address and become a Service Area Business, according to [Adwave's beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). A storefront salon should keep its address visible.

## Why must your name, address and phone match everywhere?

Consistency is the basis of citation work. [Adwave's voice search guide](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses) says the name, address and phone (NAP) must be exactly the same on your website, your Business Profile and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places. Small differences count, including punctuation and whether "St." or "Street" is written out.

Fix existing listings before building new ones. A duplicate or mismatched citation adds a second version of the business for search engines to reconcile, so auditing what already exists comes first.

Apple is a separate directory to check. [Adwave's Apple Business Connect article](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring) reports that Apple Business Connect has captured a 7% share in a maps-only comparison and is growing. The same article describes a Profile Views metric that counts how many people saw the business card in Apple Maps. The article's usage split between Google Maps and Apple Maps does not name a source, so treat it as unverified.

## How many reviews do you need, and what should they say?

Reviews appear next to your profile. Google's [review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) says they show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search, so they are visible at the point of decision as well as in ranking.

A review count benchmark exists, but it describes correlation. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business) cites a third-party analysis of 2 million Business Profile pages, which found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. Use that as a benchmark for growth, not a target with a deadline.

Ask clients to mention the service they booked. Review text is public and tells prospective clients, and Google, what the salon does. Keep the request natural rather than scripted, and avoid anything that breaches Google's review policy.

## Which profile details feed Google's AI answers?

Google's question and answer feature launched in Maps in 2017, according to [Adwave's Q&A guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a). Adwave's summary of Accrisoft's transition notes that responses in Google's AI-driven answering draw on business profile data, website content and reviews. That makes the profile, the website and the review text one connected set of signals.

Write answers to the questions clients really ask about cuts, colour and booking, then keep the same facts on your site. A profile that says one thing and a website that says another weakens both. For a fuller process, see [AI for Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization).

## Do location data and keywords support the map pack?

If you also run Google Ads, the data is linked. [Google's help page on location assets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en) states that location asset data is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and that successful corrections there flow into linked Ads accounts. Fixing the profile therefore fixes both channels.

When the profile and chain options do not work, Google allows you to select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link with your Ads account. These must be your own locations, or they may be disapproved. Keyword choice for salon ads is covered in [best Google ad keywords for hair salons in 2026](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026) and the [complete guide to Google Ads for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide).

Put a map embed on your contact page as well. The mechanics are covered in [embedding Google Maps on a local business website](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

## How do you track whether the map pack is moving?

Track the profile metrics on a fixed schedule so changes can be linked to actions. Check the six metrics Adwave lists and compare the same month across years, since salon demand is seasonal.

[Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) states that a business may begin ranking for its own name in the map pack a few weeks after verification. This is the vendor's estimate, not a guarantee. For keyword positions, check the top three in a private browser window and record the results with the date.

## FAQ

### Does a salon need a website to appear in the Mesa map pack?

A Business Profile is free to set up, according to [Google's Business Profile page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/). Adwave reports that Google's AI answers draw on website content as well as profile data and reviews, so a website that matches the profile supports visibility.

### Is 200 reviews a requirement for the local pack?

No. The [Adwave analysis](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business) of 2 million profiles found that businesses with over 200 reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. That is a correlation, so it is a benchmark, not a rule.

### Can a storefront salon hide its address?

Only in limited cases. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) says you can hide the street address if you work from home or serve customers at their location. A storefront salon should keep its address visible.

### Should a salon claim its Apple Maps listing?

Yes, if you want coverage beyond Google. [Adwave's Apple Business Connect article](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring) reports growing use and a Profile Views metric, so the listing can be tracked alongside your Google data.
