A wealth management firm should start marketing with its compliance rules, then build visibility where prospects already look: a verified Google Business Profile, a clear website, referrals and useful content. Paid search can add leads, but it converts slowly. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark for Finance & Insurance shows $3.39 per click yet only a 2.64% conversion rate and $74.44 per lead, so ads work as support for relationships, not a replacement.

This guide is general marketing information, not legal advice. Check every ad, testimonial and performance claim with your compliance officer or regulator.

## What Rules Apply to Wealth Management Marketing?

Rules depend on where and how you are registered. In the US, SEC-registered investment advisers are covered by Rule 206(4)-1, the Marketing Rule. The SEC's [Marketing Compliance FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/division-investment-management-frequently-asked-questions/marketing-compliance-frequently-asked-questions), last updated January 15, 2026, covers testimonials and endorsements, and performance presentation including gross and net figures shown over the same period and methodology.

State-registered advisers, broker-dealers, and firms in the UK, Canada and Australia have their own regulators. Treat anything that quotes returns, shows client praise or pays for referrals as needing review before it is published.

## Does Google Require Financial Advertisers to Verify?

In some countries, yes. Google's [financial services verification page](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/10770884) lists countries including Australia, the United Kingdom and many EU markets in its location dropdown. For Australia it describes a process that starts with third-party verification through G2RS and then a Google form. It says verification can be revoked and the account suspended for false information or policy violations.

The US and Canada are not listed on that page, and it does not say whether they require verification. Press reports about future US and Canadian requirements are secondary, so check Google's current policy page before you plan ads.

## How Do Wealth Managers Win Clients Without Ads?

Trust and referrals do most of the work in this field, though no public study measures the exact share. Build the base that makes a referred prospect convinced when they search your name.

- **Google Business Profile:** verify it, choose the right category and keep hours and services accurate. Our [profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) lists the fields.
- **Website:** state who you serve, how you are paid, your registration and one way to book a call. Avoid claims you cannot document.
- **Referral partners:** accountants, estate lawyers and business brokers meet people at life events. Offer to explain your process to them, within the rules on referral payments.
- **Content:** answer the questions clients ask in meetings. See [content marketing for financial services](/blog/content-marketing-for-financial-services).

## Is LinkedIn Worth It for Advisors?

For firms serving business owners and professionals, LinkedIn is where those people read and connect. Post useful, compliant content under the adviser's name and connect with people you already know. Our [LinkedIn marketing guide for consultants](/blog/linkedin-marketing-for-consultants) covers the habits, and [content marketing for consultants](/blog/content-marketing-for-consultants) shows how to turn client questions into posts.

Keep records of what you publish, since regulated firms often have to retain marketing materials. Ask your compliance officer how long and in what format.

## What Do Paid Ads Cost for a Wealth Manager?

LocaliQ's [2026 benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/) (last updated June 1, 2026) list Finance & Insurance at $3.39 per click, a 9.83% click-through rate, a 2.64% conversion rate and $74.44 per lead. LocaliQ sells ad management, and the page gives no sample size. The category includes insurance, loans and tax services, so a high-net-worth adviser's real cost per click may differ.

A click costs less than average, but conversions are rarer. Qualified leads for advisers usually need a phone call, so track calls and form submissions, and exclude job seekers and DIY investors with negative keywords.

## The Math: What Does a New Client Cost?

All inputs are **assumptions** built on the Finance & Insurance benchmark. A "lead" is an enquiry, not a qualified prospect.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Cost per lead (benchmark) | $74.44 |
| Leads who book a first meeting (assumption) | 25% |
| Meetings that become clients (assumption) | 20% |
| Leads per new client | 20 |
| Ad cost per new client | about $1,489 |
| Annual fee per client (assumption) | $6,000 |

At these inputs, a client costs about $1,489 in ads and pays $6,000 in the first year, before the cost of servicing the client. If only 10% of meetings convert, the ad cost per client doubles to about $2,978. The fee level is the biggest unknown, so use your real average and compare it against your own cost per lead. ## FAQ

### What is the best marketing channel for wealth managers?

No public study ranks channels for this field. Referrals and a strong Google presence suit most local firms, with paid search tested after tracking is in place.

### Can I use client testimonials?

Only after compliance review. The SEC FAQ addresses testimonials and endorsements under the Marketing Rule, and other regulators have their own rules.

### Do I need Google financial verification?

It depends on where you advertise. Google's page lists some countries, including Australia and the UK, and does not list the US or Canada. Check the page for your market.

### How much should a wealth manager spend on ads?

There is no standard figure. At LocaliQ's $74.44 average cost per lead, a $1,500 monthly budget buys roughly 20 enquiries.
