Here is the number that matters for a window company: about $200. WordStream and LocaliQ's 2025 home services benchmark, as relayed by [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/garage-doors-and-windows-google-ads-statistics), puts the cost per lead in the Doors & Windows Sales category at $200.34, with a 4.41% conversion rate. If you close 1 in 5 of those leads, a booked job costs you roughly $1,000 in ads. On an $8,000 install that can be a great deal. On a $900 repair, it is a disaster. Which one you are depends on three dials: click price, lead rate and close rate.

This guide is for window, door and glazing companies in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers what clicks and leads tend to cost, how to build a search campaign by service, where Local Services Ads fit, how to track calls and forms, and how to check whether the whole thing pays. Dollar benchmarks are US-based; for pounds or Australian and Canadian dollars, run the same math with your own click prices.

## What the Benchmarks Say (and Why to Treat Them as Rough)

Benchmarks for this niche are thin, and many come from agencies that sell lead generation. The most solid starting point is the WordStream/LocaliQ 2026 Google Ads Benchmarks (13,000+ US search campaigns, April 2025 to March 2026), as summarised by [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/garage-doors-and-windows-google-ads-statistics). Webtonic says that CPC is the second-highest of the categories tracked, about 54% above the $5.42 all-industry average. The narrower Doors & Windows Sales line, from the 2025 WordStream/LocaliQ home services benchmark, shows $200.34 per lead at a 4.41% conversion rate.

A quick sanity check, which is my arithmetic and not a published number: cost per lead equals click price divided by conversion rate, so $200.34 at 4.41% implies an average click of about $8.84. That fits the $8.33 category CPC nicely.

Other sources push higher. A guide from [The Valley Marketing Group](https://thevalleymarketinggroup.com/blog/window-replacement-google-ads-cost-per-lead-2026/) cites PPC Chief figures of $15 to $30 per click for window replacement keywords, and Home Service Direct's range of $85 to $250 per lead. Those are agency claims relayed second-hand, and the article does not reconcile them with each other: at 4.41%, a $15 to $30 click would mean $340 to $680 per lead. So the sensible reading is that a click in your city could cost anywhere from about $8 to $30, and the only way to know is to test.

## Pick the Right Campaign Type

**Start with Search.** Someone typing "replacement windows near me" or "patio door installation [town]" is close to asking for a quote. Search catches that intent. Run one campaign per service area, with separate ad groups per product.

**Consider Local Services Ads, but check eligibility first.** Google's help says Local Services Ads charge [per valid lead, not per click](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en). A valid lead includes a phone call you answer or return, or a missed call where the customer stays on the line more than 20 seconds, or a text, email or voicemail. Prices vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding mode. Invalid or low-quality leads are not charged.

The catch: availability depends on your job category and location. Third-party lists do not confirm window or door installation as an eligible category everywhere, and Google lets you check on its signup page by entering your location and category. If it is available to you, it is worth a test beside Search. Google is also moving these campaigns into Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals, with a phased rollout that Google's [migration page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585) says started in August 2026 for select US home service categories and continues into 2027 for others. The page does not mention windows by name.

## Set Up the Search Campaign Step by Step

### 1. Location: only where you can install

Draw your targeting around the towns, zip codes or postcodes you can actually reach in a working day. Window jobs need surveys and installs, so a far-flung enquiry costs you drive time. Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) describes the combined "presence or interest" option as the default and recommended setting on the page, which is written about Demand Gen campaigns. Check what your own Search campaign uses. For an installer, I recommend people in or regularly in your area. That is my advice, not a Google rule.

### 2. Keywords by service, match types kept tight

Google's [match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) defines broad match as the default, phrase match (in quotes) as reaching searches with your keyword's meaning, and exact match (square brackets) as the most control and the fewest searches. Google also says broad match works best with Smart Bidding. On a small account with little conversion history, start with phrase and exact.

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Replacement windows | "replacement windows [town]", [window replacement near me], "new windows installation" | Phrase + exact |
| Energy-efficient | "energy efficient windows [town]", "double glazing [town]", "triple pane windows" | Phrase |
| Patio and sliding doors | "patio door installation", [sliding door replacement], "bifold doors [town]" | Phrase + exact |
| Entry doors | "front door replacement [town]", "entry door installation" | Phrase + exact |
| Your brand | [your company name] | Exact |

Searches such as "window replacement cost" show research intent. They often convert worse than "window installation near me", so give them their own ad group and a lower bid rather than letting them eat the main budget.

### 3. Negative keywords from day one

Block people who are not buying: `diy`, `how to`, `parts`, `repair kit`, `handle`, `lock`, `film`, `tint`, `blinds`, `curtains`, `jobs`, `careers`, `salary`, `wholesale`, `manufacturer`, `used`, `free`. If you do not do repairs, add `repair`, `fix` and `broken` too. Review the Search Terms report weekly. Our negative keywords guide shows how to apply lists.

### 4. Ads that say something checkable

Put the service and the town in at least a few headlines: `Window Replacement in [Town]`, `Free In-Home Quote`, `Licensed and Insured`, `Manufacturer-Backed Warranty`, `Finance Options Available`. Use only claims you can prove. Add a call asset, a location asset linked to your Google Business Profile, and sitelinks (Windows, Doors, Financing, Gallery). Strong reviews help here.

## Landing Page: One Service, One Action

Send "patio door installation" clicks to a patio door page, not your homepage. It needs a headline with service and town, a tap-to-call number, a short quote form (name, phone, postcode), photos of real finished jobs, reviews, warranty and licence details, and financing information if you offer it. Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) go through the layout.

## Tracking: Count Calls and Forms

Without tracking you cannot tell a profitable ad group from an expensive one. Set up form submissions, calls from ads and calls from the landing page. Our conversion tracking setup guide covers the steps. Then do the manual part: log each lead's source, whether you quoted, and whether you won the job. Ad platforms count leads; only your sales log knows which became revenue.

## The Math

Take a $3,000 monthly budget and run three scenarios. Every row is an **assumption** for illustration, except that the Base scenario uses the $200.34 cost per lead reported for Doors & Windows Sales and the Strong scenario uses the $8.33 category CPC with the 8.05% category conversion rate. Replace the rest with your own numbers after a month of data.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $3,000 | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| Cost per lead | $500 (CPC $15 at 3%, assumed) | $200 (benchmark) | $103 (CPC $8.33 at 8.05%) |
| Leads | 6 | 15 | 29 |
| Lead to booked job rate (assumption) | 15% | 20% | 30% |
| Jobs won | 0.9 | 3 | 8.7 |
| Ad cost per job | $3,333 | $1,002 | $345 |
| Average job value (assumption) | $8,000 | $8,000 | $8,000 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 30% | 30% | 30% |
| Gross profit per job | $2,400 | $2,400 | $2,400 |
| Monthly gross profit after ad spend | -$840 | +$4,200 | +$17,900 |

Read it carefully. The Strong column beats the benchmarks on both click price and close rate together, so do not plan on it. The Tough column loses money, and that is a very ordinary outcome for a small account with weak follow-up. The difference between Base and Tough is mostly the lead-to-job rate and landing page, which you control.

Two formulas follow:

- **Your maximum cost per lead** = gross profit per job x close rate. At $2,400 and 20%, that is $480. Pay more than that on average and you lose money.
- **Your maximum click price** = maximum cost per lead x conversion rate. At $480 and 4.41%, that is about $21 a click.

Those ceilings shift fast if your margin is thinner than 30%, so use your real gross margin after materials and installers, not your sale price. Also remember that one job often needs several quotes. Track by booked job, not by enquiry.

## Optimise Weekly, Not Daily

Clicks are lumpy at window-company volumes, so avoid daily tinkering. Each week:

1. Read the Search Terms report and add negatives.
2. Pause keywords that have spent about twice your target cost per lead with no enquiries.
3. Check calls by time of day and align ad hours with when someone answers.
4. Test one new headline or offer every two weeks.
5. Raise budget by about 20% only on ad groups that hit your target cost per job.

Avoid fully automated bidding until you have a steady flow of recorded conversions; with too little data, automation can chase low-quality clicks. For a related trade, compare [Google Ads for contractors](/blog/google-ads-for-contractors).

## Do This Today

1. Work out your gross profit per window or door job, and your realistic close rate on quoted leads. Use the formulas above to find your maximum cost per lead.
2. Create one Search campaign for one service area, with ad groups for windows, patio doors and entry doors, phrase and exact match, and the negative list in this article.
3. Build one landing page per service, each with a tap-to-call number and a three-field quote form.
4. Set up form, ad-call and landing-page-call conversion tracking before turning on the ads.
5. Set a 10-minute callback rule for every new lead, and book a weekly 20-minute slot to read the Search Terms report.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a window company spend on Google Ads per month?**
A: There is no official number. At the reported Doors & Windows Sales cost per lead of about $200, a $3,000 budget buys roughly 15 leads, so decide your monthly spend from the number of jobs you want and your own close rate.

**Q: I spend $500 a month and get nothing. Do I need more?**
A: Not necessarily. At click prices between roughly $8 and $30, $500 buys only about 17 to 60 clicks. Check location, search terms, tracking and landing page first. If the keywords are simply expensive, narrow to fewer, more specific ones or raise the budget.

**Q: Are Local Services Ads better than Search for windows?**
A: They bill per valid lead rather than per click, which can suit high-ticket work. But category and location availability varies, so check Google's signup page for your area, and test rather than switching entirely.

**Q: Should I use broad match?**
A: Google says broad match pairs best with Smart Bidding. Without enough recorded conversions, start with phrase and exact and review the Search Terms report weekly.

**Q: How long before ads bring jobs?**
A: Calls can arrive within days, but quote cycles for window projects are longer. Give it four to six weeks, my rule of thumb rather than a Google figure, before judging cost per booked job.
