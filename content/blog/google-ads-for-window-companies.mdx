Google Ads can work well for window and door companies because the people searching for "window replacement near me" or "patio door installation" are close to requesting a quote. To get leads without burning budget, target your service area tightly, build ad groups around specific services, send each to a matching landing page, and track calls and form submissions as conversions.

Window and door jobs are high-ticket, so even a small number of well-qualified leads can pay for the campaign. The trade-off is that clicks in this industry are often expensive, so wasted clicks hurt quickly.

## What Does Google Ads Cost for Window and Door Companies?

Costs per click in home-improvement categories are often higher than for most local services, and they vary a lot by city, season and keyword. Instead of relying on a generic average, check **Keyword Planner** in your own account for the terms you want, then work backwards:

1. Estimate the value of an average job and your closing rate on leads.
2. Decide what you can pay for a lead and still make a profit.
3. Compare that number to your estimated cost per click divided by your landing-page conversion rate.

If the math does not work on your target keywords, narrow your targeting or choose more specific, less competitive phrases.

## Setting Up a Successful Google Ads Campaign

* **Target your location:** limit ads to the cities and ZIP codes you can actually serve. Use the setting that targets people *in* your area, not people merely interested in it.
* **Choose intent-rich keywords:** "window replacement [city]," "door installation near me," "energy-efficient windows [city]," "sliding patio door replacement."
* **Set a budget you can sustain:** several weeks at minimum, because clean data takes time.
* **Write specific ad copy:** include your service area, warranty, financing, and a clear call to action.
* **Track conversions:** phone calls, form submissions and chat leads, ideally with [call tracking](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business).

## The Importance of Ad Copy

Your ad copy separates you from every other installer in the results.

* **Lead with the service and location:** "Window Replacement in [City]. Free In-Home Quote."
* **Highlight what is true and checkable:** licensed and insured, manufacturer warranty, financing options, years in business.
* **Use social proof:** star ratings and review counts from your Google Business Profile. Keep that listing strong with our [Google Business Profile service](/services/google-business-profile).

## Keyword Strategy and Negative Keywords

Group keywords by service so each ad matches its search: windows, entry doors, patio doors, storm doors, garage doors if relevant.

Add negatives early to avoid DIY and research traffic: "DIY," "how to," "parts," "repair kit," "jobs," "salary," "cost of materials," and "wholesale" unless you serve those buyers. See our guides on [negative keywords](/blog/negative-keywords-google-ads) and [stopping wasted budget](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).

Start with phrase and exact match. Broad match can discover new terms but needs close monitoring of the Search Terms report.

## Landing Pages That Convert Window and Door Leads

Send people to a page that matches what they searched. Someone looking for "patio door installation" should see patio doors first, not a homepage about everything.

A simple, effective page includes:

* a clear headline with the service and city
* a phone number that is clickable on mobile
* a short quote form
* proof: finished-job photos, reviews, warranty details, licenses
* financing or incentive information if you offer it

See our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices).

<Callout type="warning">Review ad position and bidding strategy carefully. Automated bidding with too little conversion data can overspend on low-quality clicks.</Callout>

## Measuring What Matters

Clicks are not leads, and leads are not jobs. Track the whole path:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Cost per lead | Shows whether ads generate inquiries at an affordable price |
| Lead-to-quote rate | Exposes poor-quality leads or slow follow-up |
| Quote-to-job rate | Reveals sales process issues |
| Cost per job | The number that decides if the campaign is profitable |

Call your own leads back within minutes where possible. In high-ticket categories, fast follow-up often matters as much as the ad.

## FAQ

**Q: I'm already spending $500 a month on Google Ads and getting nothing. Do I need to spend more?**
A: Not necessarily. Check structure first: location targeting, search terms, landing pages and conversion tracking. A small budget in an expensive category buys very few clicks, so if your keywords cost a lot, you may need either a higher budget or narrower, more specific targeting.

**Q: How long before I see results from Google Ads?**
A: Clicks start almost immediately and calls may come within days. Judge performance after at least a few weeks of data, and avoid changing everything after a few days.

**Q: Does Google Ads work for small towns?**
A: It can, but volume is lower. A wider radius or multiple nearby towns may be needed to collect enough data. In towns with few advertisers, even a few leads can be valuable.

**Q: Should I use broad match or exact match keywords?**
A: Start with phrase and exact match. Use broad match only with strong negatives, conversion tracking and weekly Search Terms review.

**Q: Do I need a landing page or can I send people to my homepage?**
A: A dedicated landing page that matches the search usually converts better than a general homepage. Keep it simple: service, proof, phone number and form.

**Q: Can I run Google Ads myself?**
A: Yes, if you can check the account weekly. Mistakes can get expensive in a high-CPC category, so many owners hire help for setup and review. Compare any agency fee with the cost of wasted clicks.
