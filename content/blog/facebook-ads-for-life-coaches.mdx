## Which Facebook Ad Types Suit a Life Coach?

Victoria Gibson, a life coach who runs Facebook ads for her own business, recommends focusing on a small number of ad types rather than trying everything. Her blog post names engagement ads and lead ads among the four types she suggests. Engagement ads aim for clicks, likes, comments and shares, and can reuse a popular post or live video. Lead ads collect contact details that you can follow up by email or phone. [Gibson's post](https://victoriagibson.com/3-2/) is a coach's first-person account, so treat it as secondary evidence rather than a platform rule.

Start with one format. A lead ad is the simpler choice when the goal is a booked discovery call, because it gives you a contact to follow up. An engagement ad suits a coach who already has posts that get reactions and wants to reach new people with them.

## How Do You Test Two Ad Versions Fairly?

Meta's A/B testing help page explains how to compare two versions of an ad fairly. Each version is shown to a separate segment of your audience, so nobody sees both. The test then shows which version performs better on cost per result or cost per conversion. The page recommends using the same budget for both versions so the comparison is fair.

Meta also advises against testing by switching ad sets on and off by hand. Manual switching can create overlapping audiences and unreliable results. A simple sequence:

1. Choose one variable to change, such as the image, the headline or the audience.
2. Write a hypothesis, for example that a custom audience will outperform an interest audience.
3. Create the test in Ads Manager or the Experiments tool, using the same budget for both versions.
4. Run it long enough to collect enough results to compare, then compare cost per result, not clicks.

## Who Are You Reaching on Facebook?

Pew Research's 2026 survey of 5,511 US adults shows Facebook is still one of the most widely used platforms. Of US adults, 70% say they use Facebook, and 51% say they use Instagram. The survey has a margin of error of plus or minus 1.8 percentage points. Pew's figures cover the United States only.

This matters for coaches in the UK, Australia and Canada. The sources reviewed here do not give platform usage for those countries, so check local data before assuming the same reach. The reach figure shows that the audience exists. It does not show how many of those people will book a call, which only a test can answer.

## What Does a $600 Monthly Budget Buy?

The example below uses assumptions throughout, because no source gives coaching figures on Facebook. Replace each assumption with your own numbers after your first test.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget (assumption) | $600 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $2.00 |
| Click-to-lead rate (assumption) | 5% |
| Lead-to-call rate (assumption) | 30% |
| Call-to-client rate (assumption) | 25% |
| Program price (assumption) | $900 |

On these assumptions, $600 buys 300 clicks, which gives 15 leads, 4.5 discovery calls and about 1.1 new clients. The cost per client is about $533. Revenue of about $1,013 from one client at $900 covers the spend. At a $300 program price, the same funnel brings in about $338, which is below the $600 spend. The result turns on your price and your call-to-client rate, so test those before committing a budget. ## What Rules Should You Check Before Launch?

Before you launch, open Meta's current advertising standards and check that your headline, image and offer comply. Do not rely on a claim you have not read on the platform's own pages.

Keep your ad copy about the goal rather than the person. Your headline and offer should be accurate, and you should not promise results you cannot support.

## How Do You Track Leads and Clients?

Track five numbers: cost per lead, share of leads who book a call, share of calls that become clients, cost per client, and revenue per client over the length of the program. Ads Manager shows the first. The rest need a simple sheet you update after each call. Without them, a cheap lead can hide a poor-fit client.

A tracking pixel records what visitors do on your site, such as booking a call. Set it up before the test starts, so the conversions you compare are recorded from day one. The [Facebook pixel setup guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-setup-guide) covers the steps. For retargeting people who engaged but did not book, see [Facebook custom audiences](/blog/facebook-custom-audiences).

## Should a Coach Compare Facebook With Google Ads?

Facebook and Google reach people at different stages. Facebook ads interrupt a feed and suit building interest. Google search ads catch people already looking for help, which is why the Google averages above run higher per click. Many coaches test one platform first, then add the other once their offer converts.

If you want to decide between them, start with [whether Facebook ads work for local businesses](/blog/do-facebook-ads-actually-work-for-local-businesses-data-backed-answer), then compare costs. A coach who sells a high-priced program may find that a search campaign for a specific problem converts better than a broad feed ad. Test both on a small budget before choosing one.

## FAQ

### How much does a Facebook lead cost for a life coach?

No source reviewed here publishes a Facebook cost per lead for coaching. Your figure depends on the offer, audience and creative, so run a small test and measure cost per booked call.

### Does Facebook still reach enough people to be worth using?

Pew Research's 2026 survey found 70% of US adults use Facebook. That covers the United States only. Check local usage data if you coach in the UK, Australia or Canada.

### How do I test two ad versions without wasting budget?

Use Meta's A/B test tool, give both versions the same budget, and change only one variable. Avoid switching ads on and off manually, because that can overlap audiences and give unreliable results.

### What should I track besides clicks?

Track cost per booked call, the share of calls that become clients, cost per client and revenue per client. Clicks show interest but do not show whether the ad pays for itself.
