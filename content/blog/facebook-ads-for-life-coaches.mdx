## How Much Do Facebook Leads Cost for a Coach?

No published benchmark covers coaching directly, so use nearby rows as a range. LocaliQ's 2026 lead-campaign figures give these costs per lead: Education and Instruction $26.31, Health and Fitness $27.11, all industries $27.39, and Personal Services $38.09.

Click costs sit between $1.62 and $1.84 in those rows. A coach selling a high-priced program has to turn a lead into a paying client, which these averages do not measure.

## What Offer Should a Life Coach Advertise?

Ask for the smallest step that shows fit. A free discovery call, a short workshop, or a downloadable guide each fit a lead campaign. The ad has to say who the offer is for and what happens next.

Which of these gets more leads for coaches is not published, so test one against another with the same budget. ## What Does Meta Not Allow in Coaching Ads?

Meta's ad standards matter more for coaches than for most local trades. The Health and Wellness policy says weight loss, weight gain, supplement and cosmetic ads must target people 18 or older, and bars clickbait, such as promised outcomes in a set timeframe without qualifiers. It also bars claims to cure listed incurable conditions.

The wider ad standards say ads must not assert or imply personal attributes, such as physical or mental health or financial status. A headline like "Are you broke and burned out?" implies one. Write about the goal, not the person.

## What Should a Lead Form Ask?

Keep the form short. Meta's ad standards state that ads must not request health information, such as medical or mental health conditions, or financial information, such as income, without prior permission. A coaching intake form that asks about anxiety or debt could breach this.

Ask for name, email and one qualifying question about the goal. Collect sensitive details on a call, after the person has agreed to talk.

## How Do You Retarget People Who Showed Interest?

Show a second ad to people who watched a video, opened the form but did not finish, or visited your booking page. Meta's own help pages for custom audiences returned no readable content when we opened them, so we cite no audience size or retention limit here. Check the current limits in Ads Manager.

Keep the second ad different. It can answer an objection, show how a call works, or link to a booking page. For a wider channel comparison, see [Google Ads for business coaches](/blog/google-ads-for-business-coaches), where people search for help.

## What Should You Track?

Five numbers are enough:

- Cost per lead
- Share of leads who book a call
- Share of calls that become clients
- Cost per client
- Revenue per client over the program length

The first is shown in Ads Manager. The other four need a simple sheet that you fill in after each call. Without them, a cheap lead can hide a poor client.

## FAQ

### How much does a Facebook lead cost for a life coach?

No coaching figure is published. LocaliQ's 2026 lead campaigns average $27.39 per lead across industries and $38.09 for Personal Services. Your cost depends on offer, audience and creative.

### Are life coaches a restricted category on Meta?

The ad standards we opened do not list coaching as a restricted category. Health and wellness rules apply if you advertise weight loss or health outcomes, and personal-attribute rules apply to all ads.

### Can I use client results in ads?

The Meta pages we opened state no testimonial rule. Meta bars deceptive or misleading content and promised outcomes in a set time without qualifiers, so avoid guaranteed results.

### Should I use lead forms or send people to my site?

Which is better for coaches is not published. Lead forms are quicker to complete. A landing page lets you explain the offer first. Test both.

### How long before ads work?

No source gives a time. Run a campaign long enough to collect enough leads to judge, and compare cost per client, not clicks.
