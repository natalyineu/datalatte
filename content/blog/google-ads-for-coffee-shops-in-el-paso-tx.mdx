## Why Does the Business Profile Come First?

Per Diem recommends choosing the "Coffee shop" category or a related term rather than a broad category such as "Restaurant." BrewRound says you can select one category at setup and add more later, so pick the closest match on day one. Per Diem also suggests answering common questions in the Q&A section before customers ask, such as free Wi-Fi, dairy-free options or dogs on the patio.

Google allows people to message a business directly through its listing, according to Per Diem. Someone who messages a shop from a search result is close to a visit, so reply quickly or set a clear hours note. The [Business Profile guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization) covers the fields to check before any ad runs.

## How Much Does Local Search Matter for Coffee?

BrewRound states that 46% of all Google searches are for local businesses. That page dates to August 2020 and cites no source for the figure, so treat it as a rough signal rather than a current measurement. Its point still stands: local searches are a natural home for a coffee shop.

Time cost is smaller than many owners expect. Bellwether Coffee lists Google Business at about 5 minutes a day for a coffee shop. The Restaurant HQ recommends posting on social media at least once a week, ideally one to two times a day, which is more work than the listing itself.

## How Do You Set the Campaign Radius and Keywords?

Draw the radius from where your regulars actually live and work. Ask for ZIP codes at the counter for one week and map them. Pull the radius in if most sales come from a few neighbourhoods, and widen it only if the data shows travel from further away.

Local phrases usually beat generic ones on cost. seoplus says a localized phrase such as "coffee shop downtown Phoenix" is likely to have less competition than the generic term "coffee shop," which can mean a lower cost per click. The same logic applies to "coffee shop el paso" against "coffee shop." Start with these groups:

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Near me | "coffee shop near me", [coffee near me], "cafe open now" | Phrase and exact |
| Local | "coffee shop el paso", "specialty coffee el paso" | Phrase |
| Need | "cold brew near me", "breakfast burrito and coffee" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your shop name] | Exact |

Add negatives from the start: `jobs`, `hiring`, `beans`, `wholesale`, `recipe`, `machine`. Plurals and close variants may need their own entries, so check the search terms report weekly. The [keyword list guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-coffee-shops-in-2026) has more negative examples.

## What Is a Click Worth in El Paso?

No El Paso coffee click price is published, so the worked example below uses assumptions throughout. Replace each one with your own sales data. The method is what matters: work backwards from the value of a regular customer to the most you can pay per click.

| Input | Value (assumption unless noted) |
| --- | --- |
| Average ticket | $6.50 |
| Margin after ingredients and cups | 65% |
| Visits per regular per year | 75 |
| Gross profit per regular per year | 75 x $6.50 x 0.65 = about $317 |
| Most you would pay for one new regular | $150 |
| Click to first visit | 6% |
| First visit to regular | 20% |
| Maximum click price | $150 x 0.06 x 0.20 = $1.80 |
| Assumed actual click price | $2.00 |

At $2.00, the cost per regular is $2.00 divided by (0.06 x 0.20), which is about $167. That is above the $150 target. The shop needs either a better click-to-visit rate or a lower cost per click before the campaign pays for itself.

Now run a $500 budget at the assumed $2.00 price. That buys 250 clicks, which at 6% gives 15 visits and at 20% gives 3 regulars, or about $167 each. If a sharper offer lifts click-to-visit to 9%, the same budget gives 22.5 visits, about 4.5 regulars, or about $111 each. The offer is the lever to test first.

## What Should You Do Before the First Campaign?

1. Count competing coffee shops within a ten-minute drive of your address using Google Maps search for "coffee."
2. Complete the Business Profile, verify it, choose the coffee category and answer the common questions.
3. Launch one campaign with the four ad groups above, a single radius and a counter offer you can track, such as a code shown at the till.
4. Judge the campaign by cost per regular, not cost per click. Set a minimum before deciding, such as 200 clicks and at least 20 counted redemptions (assumption, not a platform rule).

## FAQ

### How much does Google Ads cost for an El Paso coffee shop?

No El Paso coffee figure is published in the sources used here. In the worked example, the assumed $2.00 click price means $500 buys 250 clicks. Your real price depends on your keywords and competition, so check your own account data.

### Should I run Spanish-language ads?

It is worth testing a Spanish variant against an English one and comparing cost per counted visit. No source here gives a share of El Paso searchers who use Spanish, so let your own results decide.

### Should I target all of El Paso?

Only if customers travel across town to reach you. A radius around your shop usually matches how coffee trips work, but test a wider radius against your tight one before deciding.

### How long before I know if it works?

Set a minimum number of clicks and counted redemptions before judging, such as 200 clicks and 20 redemptions. This threshold is an assumption to adjust to your budget, not an official period.
