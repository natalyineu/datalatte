A pet groomer should verify a Google Business Profile, choose the most specific primary category, enter hours and special hours, add real photos, and ask every happy customer for a review. A salon with a shopfront shows its address. A mobile groomer who does not meet customers at a base can set a service area and hide the address. Google says no one can pay for a better local ranking.

The market is large but price-sensitive. ## What Does Google Look At When Ranking a Groomer?

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Distance is how far you are from the person searching. Prominence includes how many sites link to you and how many reviews you have, with more reviews and better ratings helping.

You cannot change distance, so the work is in complete information and reviews. Whitespark's 2026 expert survey (47 experts, 187 factors) put the primary category, proximity and keywords in the business title at the top. That is expert opinion, not Google. Do not add keywords to your business name: Google tells you to enter it exactly as on your signage. The wider picture is in our [Maps ranking factors guide](/blog/google-maps-ranking-factors-what-moves-needle-2026).

## Should You Show Your Address or Use a Service Area?

Google describes two settings: a business address and a service area. If you serve customers within a specific local area, you can enter that area by cities, zip codes or other places, as specific and accurate as possible. If you do not meet customers at your address, you can choose not to show it.

Google's page does not mention groomers. Applying the rule, a van-based mobile groomer would use a service area and hide the address, while a salon with walk-in clients shows its address. That is an inference from the rule, so read the current guidelines in your dashboard before you decide.

If you move after verification, you must verify again.

## How Do You Pick Categories?

The first category you choose is your primary one. Google says to choose the most specific category that represents your main business. You can add up to 9 more. Do not use categories as keywords or to describe features, and do not use categories that match nearby related businesses.

A groomer who also sells food or boards pets should keep grooming as the primary category if it is most of the trade, and add the others as secondary. Look at the category list Google offers in your own dashboard, since the available names vary by country and language.

## How Do You Set Hours and Details?

Set main hours, and use special hours for holidays and temporary changes. Pet owners book around work and holidays, so wrong hours cost trust. Keep the phone number and booking link the same as on your website.

Add photos and videos that show your salon, the grooming area and finished dogs. Get the owner's permission before you post a photo of someone else's pet. Google lists photos and videos as a way to show what you offer, but gives no minimum number.

## How Do You Get Reviews Without Breaking the Rules?

Share a link or QR code that opens the review form. Customers need a Google Account to post. Google says reviews must reflect a genuine experience, and that offering discounts, free items or other perks in exchange for reviews is classed as fake and misleading content, which is strictly prohibited.

So do not offer a free nail trim for a five-star review. Ask in person at pickup, when the dog looks good and the owner is happy. A short message with the link an hour later also works.

Reply to reviews briefly and with something useful. For a complaint, acknowledge it, avoid private details about the pet or the owner, and offer a phone or email contact.

## What Can You Post on the Profile?

Google supports Update, Offer and Event posts. Posts older than 6 months are archived unless you set a date range. A groomer might use an Event for a summer de-shedding day or an Update for new opening hours. Details of each type are in our [post types guide](/blog/google-my-business-post-types-for-local-seo).

Check your numbers monthly. ## FAQ

### Can a mobile pet groomer hide their home address on Google?

Google says that if you do not meet customers at your business address, you can choose not to show it, and you can enter a service area instead. Check the current guidelines in your dashboard.

### How many categories can a pet groomer add?

One primary category plus up to 9 more. Choose the most specific primary category for your main business.

### Can I give a discount for a Google review?

No. Google treats perks in exchange for reviews as fake and misleading content and strictly prohibits them.

### Do I have to verify my profile?

Yes, to show on Search and Maps you must add or claim the business and verify it. Verification also makes it more likely you appear in results.

### Is grooming demand growing?

Dog ownership is: APPA reports 71 million US households in 2025, up 4%. But APPA also says spending on salon and mobile grooming fell in 2025, and 51% of owners groom at home. Price clarity on your profile and site helps.
