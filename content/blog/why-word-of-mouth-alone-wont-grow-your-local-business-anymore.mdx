Word of mouth still works, but alone it caps your growth. A recommendation reaches only the people your happy customers already know, and it ends with a search. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, so the friend's tip is checked online before anyone walks in. If your listing is thin, the referral leaks.

This is written for owners of cafes, salons, groomers and studios in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. I do not claim word of mouth is dead. It is the best lead source you have. The point is to make it measurable and to add channels that do not depend on who already knows you.

## Is Word of Mouth Still the Most Trusted Channel?

Yes. A 2021 Nielsen Trust in Advertising study of about 40,000 people, fielded in August and September 2021, found that 89% most trust recommendations from people they know. I am citing this through a Canadian trade summary (DMN), not Nielsen's own report, so treat it as secondary. Other write-ups quote 88% or 92% for the same measure.

The lesson is not to stop earning referrals. It is that trust is high and reach is low. A recommendation is only as large as one customer's circle, and you cannot buy more of it on demand.

## Why Isn't a Recommendation Enough Anymore?

Because the recommendation now continues online. BrightLocal reports that 49% of consumers trust online reviews about as much as personal recommendations from people they know. After reading positive reviews, 54% visit the business's website, 31% visit the location and 20% contact the business.

So a friend says "try the new groomer", then the listener searches your name. They see your rating, how recent the reviews are, and whether you reply. BrightLocal found 31% only use businesses rated 4.5 stars or higher, and 47% won't use one with fewer than 20 reviews. A great reputation with a poor listing loses the sale at the last step.

## Where Are New Customers Looking Instead?

Not in one place. BrightLocal found consumers use an average of six review sites. Google was used by 71% (down from 83% in 2025), Apple Maps rose from 14% to 27%, and 45% now use ChatGPT and other generative AI tools for local recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier.

That spread is the case against relying on one referral stream. You need a consistent name, address and review base across places people look. Start with the one that matters most: Google. ## How Do You Make Word of Mouth Work Harder?

Treat each recommendation as a signal to capture, not just a nice surprise:

1. **Ask where they heard of you.** Put it on the booking form or ask at the till, and log the answers weekly.
2. **Turn the referral into a review.** Send the review link after the visit. Google bans incentives, so ask without offering a reward.
3. **Make your profile referral-ready.** Hours, photos, categories and a booking link should be right before anyone searches.
4. **Add a simple referral habit.** Our [word-of-mouth marketing strategy](/blog/word-of-mouth-marketing-strategy) covers ways to nudge referrals without gimmicks.

## When Should You Add Paid Ads?

When you want customers faster than referrals arrive, or in a season when referrals dip. WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks, drawn from thousands of Google Ads and Microsoft Ads campaigns, put the all-industry average at $5.42 per click, a 6.64% click-through rate, an 8.18% Google Ads conversion rate and $66.69 per lead.

Those are averages across industries. Your niche and city will differ, so start small and measure. Search ads capture people already looking for what you sell, which is the same moment a friend's tip leads to.

## FAQ

**Q: Is word of mouth marketing still worth it?**
A: Yes. Nielsen's 2021 study, as summarised by DMN, found 89% most trust recommendations from people they know. It is your most trusted channel, but its reach is limited to your customers' circles.

**Q: Do online reviews replace word of mouth?**
A: They extend it. BrightLocal found 49% trust online reviews about as much as personal recommendations, and 97% read reviews for local businesses.

**Q: How many reviews do I need?**
A: BrightLocal found 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, so 20 is a sensible first target.

**Q: Should a small business run Google Ads?**
A: It can help if your numbers work. Benchmarks vary by industry, so test a small budget and track cost per lead before scaling.

**Q: Do customers use AI to find local businesses?**
A: BrightLocal's 2026 survey says 45% use ChatGPT and other generative AI tools for local recommendations, up from 6% in 2025.
