Here is a number to hold onto. LocaliQ's Facebook benchmarks list $50.91 as the average cost per lead for Beauty & Personal Care lead campaigns. If 4 in 10 of those leads turn into a client who actually shows up, a new client costs about $127 in ads. If a typical new client leaves you around $128 in gross profit over six months (an assumption we will unpack below), you are breaking even, not winning. That is why a salon Facebook strategy is mostly about the three things you control: the offer, the follow-up and the tracking.

This guide is for salon and barbershop owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers benchmarks, how to set up tracking, how to target locally, which offers to test, and the maths that tells you whether to keep spending. Dollar figures are US benchmarks; use the same sums with your local currency.

Read them carefully:

- For lead campaigns, the beauty cost per lead ($50.91) is nearly double the all-industry average on the same page ($27.39), and the conversion rate is lower (5.63% against 8.54%).
- For traffic campaigns, beauty clicks are cheap ($0.50, against $0.60 for all industries) and click-through is high (2.73%). Cheap clicks are not bookings. LocaliQ notes that traffic campaigns report no conversion rate or cost per lead, so you cannot compare them with lead campaigns.
- A "lead" in the benchmark is a form or similar action, not a client who sat in your chair. Your own lead-to-client rate decides the real cost.

Treat these as a planning range for your first test, not a promise. A salon with strong reviews and a clear offer can do better, and a salon with a clunky booking page will do worse.

## Step 1: Set Up Tracking Before You Spend

If you cannot see which ads produce bookings, you cannot improve them.

- **Install the Meta Pixel** on your website, through your site builder's built-in integration if it has one. Add an event for a completed booking, or for a click on your booking button if the booking happens on another platform.
- **Add the Conversions API as well, if your platform supports it.** Meta's developer documentation says it recommends that advertisers [implement the Conversions API alongside the Pixel](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/conversions-api/deduplicate-pixel-and-server-events.md), which it calls a redundant setup. It requires deduplication, so that one booking is not counted twice. Per the same page, Meta matches events by an event ID and deduplicates events received within 48 hours of each other. Your website platform or developer should handle this; ask them how.
- **Check the events arrive.** Open Events Manager after a test booking and confirm the event appears once.
- **Back it up offline.** Add "How did you hear about us?" to your booking form and give each ad a unique offer code. Platform reports show leads; only your front desk knows which became paying clients.

Our [Facebook Pixel setup guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-setup-guide) covers installation in more detail.

## Step 2: Target a Real Travel Distance

Most salon clients come from a short drive away. Pull the postcodes or zip codes of your last 50 new clients and look at the spread. Use that to set a radius in Ads Manager around your address. Early guides described a minimum radius of about one mile, but Meta's current options change, so check which location settings your objective offers. Keep age and interest targeting broad and let the creative do the filtering: a photo of balayage attracts people who want balayage.

If you have a client list and permission to use it, build a custom audience from it and exclude current clients from new-client offers. They deserve a different message.

## Step 3: Build the Offer Before the Ad

The offer carries more weight than the audience. Test, one at a time:

| Offer idea | Why it can work | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New-client consultation with a stylist | Low commitment, builds trust | Needs a quick-to-book calendar |
| Free add-on (gloss, scalp treatment) with first colour | Protects your price | Costs product and time |
| Service-led ad (balayage, extensions, bridal trial) | Reaches high-ticket buyers | Needs real photos of that exact service |
| Quiet-slot offer (weekday mornings) | Fills empty chairs | Do not train regulars to wait for deals |

For the creative, use your own work: before-and-after photos, a 10 to 20 second video of a finish, a stylist introducing herself. Get client consent. Say what the offer includes, the price and when it ends. Keep one idea and one button.

## Step 4: Pick the Objective and Budget

If your tracking works, choose a leads or sales (conversions) style objective so Meta can optimise for bookings. If tracking is not ready, start with traffic to your booking page and fix tracking in week one.

For budget, work backwards from what a client is worth. The maximum you can pay per lead is gross profit per client multiplied by your lead-to-client rate. Our [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) goes through the options, and [how much Facebook ads cost for small businesses](/blog/how-much-do-facebook-ads-cost-for-small-businesses-2026-benchmarks) covers broader benchmarks.

Meta's delivery system needs conversion events to learn. Third-party guides, summarising Meta's learning phase documentation, widely cite about 50 optimisation events within 7 days per ad set, and Meta's own page could not be opened to confirm it. Treat that as a reason to run one or two ad sets rather than ten, not as a rule to chase.

## Step 5: Follow Up Like a Business, Not a Billboard

An ad that generates leads can still lose money if nobody replies. Reply to every enquiry the same day, ideally within an hour in business hours. If you offer online booking, make the first available slot visible on the landing page. If you take bookings by phone, make sure someone answers the number in the ad.

## Step 6: Retarget the People Who Almost Booked

Most people do not book the first time they see an ad. With the pixel running, build an audience of people who visited your booking page or engaged with your page, and show them a different message: a client review, a before-and-after of the service they looked at, or "two appointments left this Thursday". Rotate the creative so the same ad does not follow them around.

## Step 7: Read the Numbers Weekly

Once a week, open Ads Manager and look at:

1. Cost per lead or booking (the one that matters).
2. Which ad and audience produced the most bookings.
3. Click-through rate (is the creative getting attention?).

Pause the weakest ad, keep the best, test one new thing. Do not judge on likes and comments. Do not change everything at once. Give an ad one to two weeks before deciding; that is my rule of thumb, not a Meta figure.

## The Math

Take a $600 monthly ad budget. Only the "Average" cost-per-lead column uses a published figure ($50.91, LocaliQ's Beauty & Personal Care lead benchmark). Every other number is an **assumption** for illustration. Replace them with your own.

| | Tough | Average | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend (assumption) | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per lead | $80 (assumption) | $50.91 (benchmark) | $30 (assumption) |
| Leads | 7.5 | 11.8 | 20 |
| Lead to attended client rate (assumption) | 25% | 40% | 50% |
| New clients | 1.9 | 4.7 | 10 |
| Ad cost per new client | $320 | $127 | $60 |
| Average ticket (assumption) | $85 | $85 | $85 |
| Visits in first six months (assumption) | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 50% | 50% | 50% |
| Gross profit per client over six months | $128 | $128 | $128 |
| Gross profit minus ad spend | -$361 | +$1 | +$675 |

How to read it: at the benchmark cost per lead and a 40% lead-to-client rate, you roughly break even over six months. All the profit comes from the second and third visit, which is why rebooking at checkout matters as much as the ad. The Strong column is possible but better than the benchmark, so do not plan around it.

Two formulas worth keeping:

- **Maximum cost per lead** = gross profit per client x lead-to-client rate = $128 x 0.40 = about $51.
- **Maximum cost per new client** = gross profit per client over your chosen period.

The lever that moves the result most is not the ad. Going from a 25% to a 50% lead-to-client rate halves your cost per client with no change to the account. That is answering enquiries quickly and offering a time you can actually do.

## Common Mistakes

- **Targeting a whole county.** People will not drive that far for a haircut.
- **No tracking, then arguing about results.** Fix this before spending.
- **Sending traffic to a slow or confusing booking page.** Test it on your phone, on mobile data.
- **Judging a creative on reach or likes.** Judge on bookings.
- **Discounting too deeply.** A free add-on usually protects your pricing better than a big percentage cut.
- **Turning ads off after one quiet week.** Allow a fair test, then cut what does not work.

## Do This Today

1. Pull your last 50 new clients' postcodes and decide your ad radius.
2. Work out your gross profit per new client over six months and your maximum cost per lead using the formula above.
3. Check your booking link works on a phone, then install the Meta Pixel and a booking event. Ask your developer or site builder about the Conversions API.
4. Write one specific new-client offer, with real photos from your salon, and add an offer code or a "how did you hear about us" question.
5. Launch one campaign with one or two ad sets, set a four-week window and a written continue-or-stop number.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does a lead cost for a hair salon on Facebook?**
A: LocaliQ's benchmarks list $50.91 per lead for Beauty & Personal Care lead campaigns (page updated September 2026, sample size not stated). Your own number depends on your offer, area and landing page.

**Q: Do I really need the Meta Pixel?**
A: You can run ads without it, but Meta cannot optimise for bookings and you cannot see which ads produce them. Meta also recommends running the Conversions API alongside the Pixel.

**Q: How long should I run an ad before deciding?**
A: One to two weeks as a rule of thumb, longer if you only get a few leads. Meta's learning phase is widely reported at about 50 optimisation events in 7 days, though a small salon will often fall short of that, so judge on cost per booking.

**Q: Should I boost posts or use Ads Manager?**
A: Ads Manager for bookings. Boosts give you less control over objective and tracking.

**Q: Facebook or Instagram?**
A: Meta ads run on both from the same account. Test placements together first, then look at results by placement. See our [Instagram ads guide for hair salons](/blog/instagram-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide).
