A good hair salon Facebook ads strategy has four parts: install the Meta Pixel (and ideally the Conversions API) so you can track bookings, target people within a short travel distance of your salon, show real before-and-after work with a clear offer, and review results every week. Do those four things and you can find out within a few weeks whether Facebook and Instagram ads are bringing you appointments at a cost that makes sense for your business.

Results depend on your area, your prices and your offer, so treat the numbers you see in your own account, not generic claims, as the real measure.

## Step 1: Know Who You Want to Reach

Decide who you most want to book. New clients who live nearby? Regulars for color services? Bridal parties? Different goals need different ads. For example, a salon in a busy city center might target professionals who want a quick appointment, while a neighborhood salon might target families within a few miles.

In Meta Ads Manager you can target by location (a radius around your salon), age range and interests. For local services, keep targeting simple, with a tight radius and broad interests, and let the ad creative do the filtering. Also build a custom audience from your client list (if you have permission to use it) and consider a lookalike audience to find similar people.

## Step 2: Set Up the Meta Pixel

The Meta Pixel (still often called the Facebook Pixel) is a small piece of code on your website that tells Meta who visited and what they did, such as clicking "Book Now". That lets Meta optimise toward people more likely to book and lets you see which ads produce appointments.

- Install it through your website platform (WordPress, Squarespace, Wix and similar have built-in options), or ask a developer
- Add the Conversions API if your platform supports it, since browser tracking is less reliable than it used to be
- Set up an event for a completed booking or a click on your booking link
- Use Meta's Pixel Helper browser tool to confirm it fires

If your booking happens on a third-party platform that you cannot add a pixel to, track "clicks to book" on your own site and use a unique offer code or "how did you hear about us?" question at booking. See our [Facebook Pixel setup guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-setup-guide).

<Callout type="tip">Install the pixel before you spend on ads, so you collect data from day one.</Callout>

## Step 3: Create Ad Content That Gets Booked

- **Use real work.** Before-and-after photos, short videos of a transformation and clips of your stylists tend to work better than stock photos. Always get client permission.
- **Be specific about the offer.** "New client color consultation, book this week" beats "Great hair at our salon".
- **Keep the message short.** One idea, one call to action.
- **Test formats.** Try image, short video and carousel ads and compare them in your own account, since results differ by audience.

Do not rely on someone else's chart of which format wins. Run a small test of a few variations and let your data decide.

## Step 4: Choose a Budget and Objective

There is no universal budget. Meta's delivery needs enough conversions to learn, so very small budgets can take a long time to produce useful data. Choose an amount you can keep running for at least four weeks, and consider budgeting by cost per booking you can afford rather than by what feels comfortable. Our [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) explains how to size a budget.

Pick an objective that matches your goal: leads or conversions if you have the pixel set up and want bookings, or traffic or engagement if you are starting with an audience-building offer.

## Step 5: Track and Optimise

Open Ads Manager weekly and look at:

- Cost per click and click-through rate (is your creative getting attention?)
- Cost per booking or lead (is it affordable?)
- Which ad and audience produce the most bookings

Do not change everything at once. Pause the weakest ad, keep the best, and test one new variation at a time. Give each ad enough time to collect data before judging it; very short tests are misleading.

<Callout type="warning">Do not judge an ad on likes and comments. Bookings and cost per booking are what matter. Try new creatives and audiences, but change one thing at a time.</Callout>

## Offers That Work for Salons

The offer matters as much as the targeting. Ideas to test, always with margins in mind:

- **New-client offer:** a complimentary add-on (gloss treatment, scalp massage or style finish) with a first appointment, rather than a deep percentage discount
- **Service-specific campaigns:** balayage, extensions, bridal trials or men's cuts each have a different audience, so give each its own ad
- **Fill-the-gap offers:** a small incentive for quiet weekday slots, which protects your busy times
- **Referral and rebooking prompts:** retargeting past clients with a "time for your next appointment" reminder

Always say clearly what the offer includes and when it expires.

## Retargeting: Your Best Second Chance

Most people do not book the first time they see an ad. Use the pixel to build audiences of people who visited your site, clicked your booking link or engaged with your Instagram, and show them a different message, such as a client review or an available-this-week reminder. Keep the retargeting audience window reasonable and refresh the creative so the same ad does not follow them around.

## Track Bookings Honestly

If online bookings happen inside a third-party system, add a "how did you hear about us?" question and a unique offer code for ads. Compare the number of new clients mentioning Facebook or Instagram with your ad spend each month. That keeps you from relying only on platform-reported numbers.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Boosting posts instead of using Ads Manager.** Boosts give you less control over targeting and goals.
- **Targeting too wide.** A whole county will waste money on people who will not travel to you.
- **No tracking.** Without a pixel or an offer code, you cannot tell what is working.
- **Sending traffic to a slow or confusing booking page.** Test your booking flow on your phone.
- **Giving up too soon, or never turning off a loser.** Allow a fair test period, then cut what does not work.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a small salon spend on Facebook ads?**
A: It depends on your area and goals. Start with an amount you can sustain for at least a month, enough for a few clicks a day, and judge results by cost per booking. See our [budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend).

**Q: Do I really need the Facebook Pixel?**
A: You can run ads without it, but you will have less information on what is working and Meta will have less data to optimize toward bookings. Installing it before you spend is strongly recommended.

**Q: How long should I run an ad before deciding it does not work?**
A: Give each ad a fair test period, often one to two weeks at minimum, depending on your budget and volume. Avoid changing it constantly while it is still learning.

**Q: Should I boost posts or use Ads Manager?**
A: Use Ads Manager for most campaigns. It gives you control over audience, placement, objective and tracking.

**Q: Can I run ads without a website?**
A: Yes, you can send people to a booking link, your Facebook page or Messenger, but a simple one-page site with your services, prices, photos and booking button usually converts better.
