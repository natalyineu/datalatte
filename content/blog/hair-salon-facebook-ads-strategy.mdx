A hair salon can use Facebook ads profitably only if it knows what a new client is worth before it spends. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a 2025 median pay of $35,880 a year, or $17.25 an hour, for barbers, hairstylists and cosmetologists. Those figures describe worker pay, not salon revenue, but they show how tight margins are in this trade. The maximum cost per client should be set first, and the campaign built around it: tracking, a specific offer, fast follow-up and a weekly review of bookings.

This guide is for salon and barbershop owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Dollar figures below are US; the same arithmetic works with your local currency.

Two points matter for advertising. Growth means more competition for the same local clients, so a clear offer matters more each year. The BLS also notes that salon work often includes evenings and weekends. Those are the slots an ad should fill, and the ones a booking page should show first.

## How Do You Work Out What a New Client Is Worth?

Start with gross profit per client, not the ticket price. Gross profit is the ticket minus product costs, time-related costs you can attribute, and other direct costs. Multiply that by the number of visits you expect in the first six months.

The maximum cost per lead equals gross profit per client multiplied by the lead-to-client rate. If a client is worth $128 in gross profit and four in ten enquiries become paying clients, the salon can afford about $51 per lead before the campaign loses money. | Gross profit minus ad cost, per client | $27.50 | $127.50 minus $100 |
| Net result for the month | $165 | 6 clients times $27.50 |

The campaign only pays if clients return. Rebooking at checkout moves the result more than a small change to cost per lead. In this example, cutting cost per lead from $40 to $30 gives 20 leads and 8 clients, with a net result of about $420 a month, assuming the same lead-to-client rate, ticket and visit count.

## Which Tracking Setup Should a Salon Start With?

If you cannot see which ads produce bookings, you cannot tell whether the maths above holds for your salon. Set up tracking before spending a pound, dollar or local equivalent. Use this order:

1. Confirm the booking page works on a phone using mobile data, and that the first available slot is visible without scrolling.
2. Install the Meta Pixel through your website builder's integration, or with a developer.
3. Set one event for a completed booking, or for a click on the booking button if the booking happens on another platform.
4. Ask your developer whether the Conversions API can run alongside the Pixel, and how duplicate events are removed. Check Meta's current developer documentation directly, because pages change.
5. Make a test booking and confirm the event appears once in Events Manager.
6. Add a "how did you hear about us" question to the booking form, and give each ad its own offer code.

Platform reports show leads. Only the front desk knows which leads paid for a service, so the offline question is not optional.

## Who Should You Target, and How Far?

Most salon clients come from a short drive away, so a county-wide radius wastes budget. Pull the postcodes or zip codes of the last 50 new clients and look at where they cluster. Use that spread to set a radius around the salon's address in Ads Manager, and check which location options your chosen objective allows.

Keep age and interest targeting broad. The creative does the filtering: a photo of a balayage finish reaches people who want one. If you have a client list and are allowed to use it, build a custom audience and exclude current clients from new-client offers, since they should receive a different message.

## Which Offers Should You Test First?

Test one offer at a time, because a mixed message makes results impossible to read. A new-client consultation with a stylist has low commitment and builds trust, but it needs a calendar that can be booked quickly. A free add-on, such as a gloss or scalp treatment with a first colour, protects the price better than a large percentage cut, though it costs product and time.

A service-led ad for balayage, extensions or a bridal trial reaches higher-ticket buyers, but only with real photos of that exact service. A quiet-slot offer on weekday mornings fills empty chairs, although regular clients should not learn to wait for deals. For creative, use before-and-after photos of the salon's own work, get client consent, and state the price, what is included and when the offer ends.

## How Do You Follow Up Leads Without Losing Them?

An ad that generates enquiries still loses money if nobody answers. Reply to every enquiry the same day, and within an hour during business hours where possible. If the phone number appears in the ad, someone must answer it, or the spend is wasted.

Retargeting helps too. People who visited the booking page but did not book can see a different message, such as a client review, a before-and-after of the service they viewed, or a note that two appointments remain this week. Rotate the creative so one ad does not follow the same person for weeks.

Keep follow-up honest. Only promise slots the salon can actually give, because a client offered a same-day appointment that does not exist is lost, along with the ad cost that brought them in.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid?

- Targeting a whole county. Most clients will not drive far for a cut.
- Spending before tracking works, then arguing about results.
- Sending traffic to a slow or confusing booking page.
- Judging creative on likes or reach rather than bookings.
- Discounting deeply when a free add-on would protect the price.
- Switching off an ad after one quiet week, before a fair test has run.

## FAQ

### Is Facebook advertising worth it for a small hair salon?

It can be, but only when the maths works. Use the worked example in this guide with your own ticket, visit count, margin and lead-to-client rate. If the net result is negative at a realistic cost per lead, change the offer or the follow-up before raising the budget.

### How long should an ad run before a decision?

One to two weeks is a reasonable test if enough enquiries come in to judge. If only a few arrive each week, allow longer and decide on cost per booking rather than early clicks.

### Does a salon need the Meta Pixel to start?

Ads can run without it, but campaigns cannot be linked to bookings. Without that data, the weekly review in this guide cannot be done properly.

### Should a salon boost a post or use Ads Manager?

Use Ads Manager for booking campaigns. A boosted post gives less control over the objective and tracking, which makes it harder to link spend to bookings.

### What should a salon do if its lead-to-client rate is low?

Check response time first, then the booking page, then the offer. A low rate usually points to slow replies or an offer that attracts the wrong clients, rather than to the audience itself.
