To win more driveway, patio and concrete repair jobs, a concrete company needs three things working together: a well-built Google Business Profile that shows up in local map results, a website with a page for each service and area, and a steady stream of photo-rich reviews. Paid Google Ads can speed things up, but they work best once those foundations are in place.

Concrete is a considered purchase. Homeowners compare quotes, want proof you do tidy work and often search the same day they notice a cracked slab or decide on a patio. This guide covers where to put your time and money, what to avoid, and how to keep leads flowing outside the busy season.

## How customers find a concrete contractor

Typical routes include:

- **Google searches** such as "concrete driveway installers near me" or "stamped concrete patio [town]".
- **Google Maps** and the Map Pack, where reviews and photos are very visible.
- **Referrals** from neighbours, builders, landscapers and realtors.
- **Facebook community groups** and Nextdoor recommendations.
- **Lead marketplaces** such as Angi, Thumbtack or HomeAdvisor.

A good local marketing plan uses a mix, but starts with the channels where people already look for a contractor on purpose.

## Step 1: Fix your Google Business Profile first

For a local contractor, this is the highest-return piece of marketing for the time involved.

1. **Claim and verify** your profile. If you work from a yard or office, list it. If you work only at customers' homes, set up a service-area business and hide your address.
2. **Choose a primary category** such as "Concrete contractor". Add accurate secondary categories only if they describe real services.
3. **List services** with descriptions: driveways, patios, sidewalks, stamped or decorative concrete, concrete repair, foundations, retaining walls, whatever you actually do.
4. **Set your service area** realistically. Ranking far from your base is difficult.
5. **Add photos regularly.** Concrete is visual. Upload before-and-after shots, forms being set, finished stamped patterns and your crew at work. Avoid stock photos.
6. **Add licence and insurance details** where the platform allows, or mention them in the description.

Use our [Google Business Profile optimisation checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) as a working list, and read [local SEO for contractors](/blog/local-seo-for-contractors) for a broader view.

## Step 2: Ask for reviews after every job

Reviews often sway which contractor gets the call. Build a habit:

- Ask at job completion, while the customer is looking at the finished work.
- Send the direct review link by text or email the same day.
- Ask them to mention the type of job and neighbourhood if they are comfortable. Do not script it.
- Reply to every review, and handle complaints calmly and publicly.

Do not buy reviews or offer incentives, which can violate platform rules. Read our guide to [Google Business Profile reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews).

## Step 3: Build a website that turns visitors into quote requests

Your site needs to answer a homeowner's questions fast:

- What do you do, and where?
- Can I see work like mine?
- How do I get a quote?

A solid structure is:

| Page | What to include |
|---|---|
| Home | What you do, service area, phone, quote button, key proof (licence, years in business, reviews) |
| One page per service | Driveways, patios, stamped concrete, repair, each with photos, typical process, factors affecting price |
| Service-area pages | Only for areas you truly serve, with local project photos and details |
| Gallery or projects | Real photos with short captions |
| About | Who you are, crew, licences, insurance, warranty terms |
| Contact or quote form | Short form, tap-to-call number, clear response time |

Make sure it loads quickly on mobile and that the phone number is tappable. See our [website design checklists](/blog/website-design-for-plumbers) for a similar trade, and the [DIY vs professional website](/blog/diy-website-vs-professional-developer) comparison if you are deciding who builds it.

Be careful with pricing. Concrete costs vary by region, access, thickness, finish, reinforcement and site prep, so give factors and typical approach rather than invented numbers. Many contractors share "from" ranges or explain how a quote is built.

## Step 4: Use content to answer real questions

Homeowners often search before they call. Useful topics include:

- How long does a concrete driveway last, and what affects it?
- When can you drive on new concrete? (Ask your crew or supplier for guidance for your mix and weather.)
- Stamped concrete versus pavers versus asphalt.
- Why concrete cracks and what can be repaired.
- Sealing and maintenance.
- Permit requirements in your area.

Write from your experience on real jobs. This builds trust and can attract search traffic over time. See [blog content strategy for local business](/blog/blog-content-strategy-local-business) for planning.

## Step 5: Consider Google Ads for demand that already exists

Search ads put you in front of people ready to hire. They can work well for concrete, but they are not cheap in many markets, because home-service clicks often cost more than hospitality clicks. Costs vary widely by city and keyword, so test with a modest budget before scaling.

Tips for contractors:

- Start with high-intent keywords such as "concrete contractor near me" and "stamped concrete patio installation".
- Add negative keywords for DIY and irrelevant intent ("how to", "bag of concrete", "ready mix prices", "jobs").
- Use location targeting for your real service area.
- Track calls and form submissions, or you will not know what works. See [Google Ads conversion tracking setup](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup).
- Use call extensions and consider call-only campaigns. Read [call-only campaigns for service businesses](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns-service-businesses).

Answer the phone or call back fast. Many homeowners contact several contractors, and speed often wins the quote.

## Step 6: Use social media as a portfolio

Facebook and Instagram are not usually where homeowners first search, but they are a good place to show finished work and collect local recommendations. Post project photos and short time-lapse clips, tag the location and respond to comments. Local [Facebook ads for contractors](/blog/facebook-ads-for-contractors) can also reach homeowners in specific neighbourhoods, for example near a recently completed job.

## Step 7: Build local relationships

- Introduce yourself to landscapers, builders, fencing companies, plumbers and real-estate agents who may refer work.
- Ask suppliers or local trade associations about directory listings.
- Sponsor a local team or event if it suits your budget.
- Request links from partners' websites where it makes sense.

## Step 8: Plan for seasonality

Concrete work often slows when the ground freezes or in extreme weather in some regions. Use quieter months to:

- Update your site and photo gallery.
- Collect reviews from last season's customers.
- Offer early-booking slots for spring (if your capacity and pricing allow).
- Email past customers about sealing, repairs or add-on projects. See [email marketing for small business](/blog/email-marketing-for-small-business).

## Track what is working

Keep a simple lead log: date, source (Google, referral, Facebook, ads), job type, quote amount, outcome. After a few months you will see which channels produce the best jobs, not just the most calls. Ask every caller how they found you.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best marketing for a concrete company?**
A: For most, a strong Google Business Profile, steady reviews and a clear website bring the best return, with Google Ads added to fill gaps. Referrals from trade partners remain important.

**Q: How much should a concrete contractor spend on marketing?**
A: There is no universal figure. Many local service businesses set a percentage of revenue, and what is sensible depends on your margins, capacity and goals. Start modestly and measure cost per lead and per job.

**Q: Are lead marketplaces like Angi worth it?**
A: They can produce leads quickly but you may share them with several competitors and pay per lead. Track close rates and compare costs against your own channels.

**Q: Do I need a separate page for every town I serve?**
A: Only where you genuinely work and can add useful local detail. Thin, near-duplicate pages are not helpful for customers or search engines.
