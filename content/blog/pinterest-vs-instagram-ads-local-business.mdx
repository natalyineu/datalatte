For most local businesses, Instagram (through Meta Ads) is the better first test for fast local reach, offers and bookings, while Pinterest is worth testing if your customers plan ahead and search for ideas, such as weddings, hair, home or events. Neither platform wins in every case, so run a small, equal-budget test and compare cost per booking or visit.

The two behave differently. Instagram is a social feed where people discover and follow. Pinterest is closer to a visual search engine where people save ideas to revisit later. Here is how to choose and how to test.

## Pinterest vs Instagram: Key Differences

| | Pinterest | Instagram |
|---|---|---|
| User mindset | Planning and searching for ideas | Browsing, following, social discovery |
| Content lifespan | Pins can be found for a long time | Posts and Stories fade faster |
| Ad format | Standard and shopping pins, video pins | Feed, Stories, Reels |
| Local targeting | Location available; best for planning-stage topics | Strong location and radius targeting via Meta |
| Best for | Weddings, beauty looks, home, food ideas, gifts | Local offers, events, daily-life businesses |
| Management | Separate ad platform | Meta Ads Manager, shared with Facebook |

Platform features, audience sizes and costs change, so confirm current options in each ads manager.

## What About Cost?

You will see many published averages for CPC, CPM and CTR, but they vary widely by industry, location, creative and season, and they are rarely specific to local businesses. A rule that applies everywhere: do not choose a platform based on someone else's average cost per click. Choose based on your own cost per booking, call or visit.

For a fair test:

- Spend the same modest amount on each platform for the same period.
- Use similar offers and creative adapted to each format.
- Track clicks, but judge by bookings, calls, coupon redemptions or visits.
- Run for at least two weeks, longer if volume is low.

## Which Platform Drives More Foot Traffic?

It depends on your business and customer:

- **Cafes and restaurants:** Instagram suits daily-life, location-based promotion such as new menu items and events. Pinterest can help with seasonal ideas such as brunch, desserts and party catering.
- **Salons and barbershops:** Instagram is strong for before-and-afters and local offers. Pinterest suits styling inspiration and bridal or event looks.
- **Pet groomers:** Instagram works for cute results and neighborhood reach. Pinterest can capture grooming tips and breed-specific style ideas.
- **Fitness and yoga studios:** Instagram suits class promotions, intros and community. Pinterest may suit workout and wellness planning content.

Both let you narrow audience by location. For immediate local bookings, Instagram's local targeting and Stories offers are usually easier to execute. See our guide to [Meta ads](/services/meta-ads) and [social media management](/services/social-media).

## Creative Best Practices

**Pinterest**

- Use tall, high-quality vertical images (a 2:3 ratio such as 1000 x 1500 px is the common standard).
- Add a short text overlay with the key idea or keyword.
- Link to a specific landing page, not your homepage.
- Write descriptions with searchable phrases, since Pinterest works like search.

**Instagram**

- Use vertical formats for Stories and Reels (1080 x 1920 px).
- Show real people, real results and your actual location.
- Keep video short and put the offer early.
- Use a clear call to action such as "Book now" or "Get directions".

Keep your branding consistent across both so people recognize you. Avoid cramming too much text into any image.

## Measuring ROI

The biggest mistake is counting clicks as sales. Tie every campaign to an action:

1. Add UTM tags to every link (for example `utm_source=pinterest` and `utm_source=instagram`).
2. Use a unique offer code for each platform.
3. Ask new customers how they found you.
4. Log spend and results weekly in a simple sheet.

ROI = (revenue from ad-driven customers minus ad spend) divided by ad spend. Use real figures from your test, not benchmarks. Learn more about measurement in our [analytics service](/services/analytics).

## Decision Guide

| If you... | Start with |
|---|---|
| Need bookings quickly and have a local promotion | Instagram |
| Sell something people plan for (weddings, events, home) | Pinterest |
| Have a very small budget | Whichever your customers already use, tested for two weeks |
| Already have strong visual content | Both, adapted for each format |
| Want long-lived content that keeps being found | Pinterest, alongside organic pins |

Also consider search ads. When people are actively looking for what you do, [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and a strong [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) usually come before either platform.

## Quick Start Checklist

1. Define the goal: bookings, visits or coupon redemptions.
2. Pick a test budget you can afford to run for two weeks.
3. Create platform-specific visuals (vertical, branded, clear offer).
4. Write short copy with one clear call to action.
5. Add UTM tags and a platform-specific offer code.
6. Target a realistic radius around your location.
7. Check performance every few days, pause weak ads and keep what works.

## FAQ

**Q: I only have a small budget. Should I pick Pinterest or Instagram?**
A: Pick the platform where your customers already spend time, and run a two-week test with a small equal budget on each if you can. Judge by cost per booking or visit.

**Q: How do I track whether an ad brought someone into my store?**
A: Use a unique offer code, a tracked phone number, UTM links on booking pages and a "how did you hear about us?" question. No single method is perfect, but together they work.

**Q: Will Pinterest work for a coffee shop selling low-price drinks?**
A: It can, but direct drink sales may not cover ad costs. Consider promoting higher-value items such as gift boxes, bean bags, catering or events, and use Pinterest to build awareness.

**Q: How long should I run a test?**
A: At least two weeks, and long enough to collect meaningful conversions. A few days of data can mislead.

**Q: Can I reuse my Instagram photos on Pinterest?**
A: You can, but images designed for Pinterest (vertical, with clear text overlays and searchable descriptions) generally perform better than repurposed feed photos.
