Pew Research's 2025 survey found that 50% of US adults use Instagram and 37% use Pinterest. More people on a platform does not mean cheaper bookings for you, and nobody can tell you in advance which one will win in your town. So the sensible plan is a small, equal test: $300 on each for four weeks, judged on one number, cost per booking. This guide shows how to run it, what the maths looks like, and when Pinterest beats Instagram on paper.

It is written for owners of local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: cafes, salons, groomers, studios. Dollar figures are examples; use the same sums in your own currency.

## What We Can and Cannot Say About Each Platform

Here is what is actually confirmed, and what is not.

**Audience size (confirmed).** Pew's [2025 social media report](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/) surveyed 5,022 US adults between February 5 and June 18, 2025. Instagram reached 50% of adults, with 55% of women and 44% of men, and about 80% of people aged 18 to 29 against 19% of those 65 and older. Pew's [social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) lists Pinterest at 37% of US adults and Facebook at 71%. Those pages carry no Pinterest breakdown by gender or age, so we do not quote one.

**How ads appear (confirmed).** Pinterest's Help Center says ads are "regular Pins that you pay to be placed where people are most likely to see them", and that they surface in the home feed, category feeds and relevant search results. Instagram ads run through Meta Ads Manager and can appear in Feed, Stories and Reels.

**Planning versus browsing (a common description, not confirmed).** The popular line is that Pinterest users plan ahead and save ideas while Instagram users browse and follow. We could not find that stated in Pinterest's help pages, so treat it as a hypothesis your test will confirm or kill.

**Average ad prices (not confirmed).** Pinterest does not publish an average cost per click that we could find, and third-party averages mix industries and countries. Our [Pinterest ads cost guide](/blog/pinterest-ads-cost-2026) goes into detail. For a Facebook-side reference point, [LocaliQ's Facebook advertising benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/facebook-advertising-benchmarks/) (page updated September 23, 2026; it does not state sample size or data period) list a $0.60 average cost per click for traffic campaigns across all industries and $27.39 per lead for lead campaigns. Those are Facebook figures, not Instagram-only and not Pinterest. Use them as a loose yardstick, nothing more.

## How Each Platform Charges You

Pinterest's [bidding help page](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/set-your-bid) says every campaign has a minimum bid that is specific to the campaign and country, that you pay only the minimum required at any moment regardless of your set bid, and gives an example: a $3 bid against a $0.50 auction minimum costs $0.50. With Performance+ bidding, "bids are updated by Pinterest automatically several times a day", and costs may fluctuate at the start of a campaign while the system tests outcomes. Budgets can be lifetime, Performance+ daily or fixed daily, and the [budget page](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/set-up-campaign-budgets) does not state a minimum amount, so read the figure shown in your account.

Instagram ads use the same auction logic through Meta. In both cases the rule is the same: the platform's price per click tells you very little. Your price per booking tells you everything.

## Which Platform for Which Business

These are judgement calls, not research findings. Use them to decide which test to run first.

- **Cafes and restaurants.** Instagram suits a new menu item, an event or a launch week. Pinterest suits seasonal ideas such as brunch, desserts and catering, which people may save weeks ahead. A $5 coffee will rarely repay a paid click on either, so promote catering, gift boxes or events, or use ads to build an email list.
- **Salons and barbershops.** Instagram suits before-and-afters and quiet-slot offers. Pinterest suits bridal, prom and colour-inspiration searches, where a client may take months to book. Our [Pinterest ads guide for salons and spas](/blog/pinterest-ads-for-salons-and-spas-guide) goes deeper.
- **Pet groomers.** Instagram suits neighbourhood reach and the cute-result photo. Pinterest can carry breed-specific style ideas, though the booking decision is usually fast and local.
- **Fitness and yoga studios.** Instagram suits class promotions and intro offers. Pinterest suits wellness and home-workout planning, which may not turn into a local membership.

**The urgency test.** If your customer needs you this week (a cut, a grooming slot, a drop-in class), start with Instagram, and put Google Ads and your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) ahead of both. If the purchase is planned months out and visual (a wedding, an event, a home project), Pinterest deserves a real test.

## Creative That Fits Each Platform

**Pinterest.** Pinterest's ad specs recommend a 2:3 aspect ratio, or 1000 x 1500 pixels, for standard image ads, and warn that wider ratios may be cropped. Videos can be MP4, MOV or M4V, and 6 to 15 seconds is recommended. Write a clear title and description, since Pinterest ads can surface in search results. Link to a specific page, not your home page.

**Instagram.** Meta's Ads Guide lists 9:16 and 1440 x 2560 pixels for Reels, and asks you to keep roughly 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% of each side free of text and logos so the interface does not cover them. Put the offer in the middle of the frame, show a real person or a real result, and use a plain call to action such as "Book now".

**Reusing images.** You can reuse a Reel's content on Pinterest, but a 9:16 frame with a safe-zone gap along the bottom is not a 2:3 Pin. Re-export for each shape, and test whether the Pinterest version needs more text, because people may find it through search.

## Tracking: Make Both Platforms Report Bookings

Both platforms offer the same four events that matter to a local business. Pinterest's [event codes page](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/add-event-codes) defines Lead ("when someone shows an interest in your product or service"), Contact ("when someone contacts you through phone, email, chat or other methods"), Schedule ("when someone schedules an appointment at one of your business locations") and FindLocation ("when someone finds your business location"). Meta's Pixel reference lists the same four, with Schedule defined as "when a person books an appointment to visit one of your locations".

Set these up before spending:

1. Install the Pinterest tag and the Meta Pixel, and fire Schedule on a confirmed booking (or Lead on a form).
2. Add UTM tags to every ad link. Use `utm_source=pinterest` or `utm_source=instagram` (these values are our suggestion; Meta's own URL builder example uses `facebook`), plus a campaign name.
3. Create one offer code per platform, and ask "How did you hear about us?" at checkout.
4. Allow for lag. A Pinterest viewer may save your pin today and book next month, so a four-week window could undercount Pinterest. Note that in your review.

Our [Pinterest ads setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-pinterest-ads-local-business) walks through the tag.

## The Math

Every number below is an **assumption** for illustration. Pinterest publishes no average click price we could confirm, and your market will differ. The aim is to show how the answer changes with the business you run, then let you insert your own figures after the test.

Test budget: $300 per platform over four weeks. Two business types: a quick booking such as a haircut with an $85 first visit, and a planned high-ticket booking such as a $250 bridal trial.

| | Quick booking, Instagram | Quick booking, Pinterest | Planned booking, Instagram | Planned booking, Pinterest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spend | $300 | $300 | $300 | $300 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $1.00 | $0.80 | $1.00 | $0.80 |
| Clicks | 300 | 375 | 300 | 375 |
| Click to booking rate (assumption) | 4% | 2% | 1% | 2% |
| Bookings | 12 | 7.5 | 3 | 7.5 |
| Cost per booking | $25 | $40 | $100 | $40 |
| Revenue from first booking | $1,020 | $638 | $750 | $1,875 |
| Revenue minus ad spend | $720 | $338 | $450 | $1,575 |

How to read it. In the left pair, we assumed Instagram converts quick local bookings twice as well, which makes it the winner even though its click costs more. In the right pair, we assumed a planned, high-ticket purchase converts better on Pinterest, which reverses the result. Those conversion rates are guesses chosen to show the mechanism, not evidence that either platform converts better. The only lesson is that the winner depends on your ticket size and sales cycle, which is exactly why you test.

Two formulas to keep:

- **Maximum cost per click you can afford** = revenue per booking x gross margin x click-to-booking rate. At an $85 ticket, 50% margin and a 4% booking rate: $85 x 0.5 x 0.04 = $1.70.
- **Break-even booking rate** = cost per click divided by (revenue per booking x gross margin). At $1.00 per click, $85 and 50%: $1.00 / $42.50 = about 2.4%.

Before you read anything into a $300 test, know its limit. A test with 7 or 12 bookings is a sketch, not proof. If the result is close, extend it two more weeks rather than declaring a winner.

## How to Run the Test

1. **Same offer, same landing page, same period.** Change only the platform.
2. **Same targeting logic.** Choose a radius or postal codes from where your last 50 customers live. Pinterest allows country, region, metro area or postal code targeting; metro availability varies by country, with the US, UK, Australia and Canada among the countries listed.
3. **One campaign per platform**, one or two ads each, so the budget is not spread too thin.
4. **Do not touch anything for the first two weeks**, apart from fixing broken links.
5. **Judge at week four** on cost per booking and, if you can, on repeat visits. Add a note on assisted bookings from the "how did you hear about us" answers.
6. **Keep the winner, retest the loser** with a better offer, and keep a small organic Pinterest and Instagram presence going because it feeds both.

## Do This Today

1. Write down your average first-visit revenue, your gross margin and the number of bookings you need to break even on $300.
2. Decide the urgency question: do your customers book this week, or plan months ahead? That tells you which platform to start with.
3. Install the Pinterest tag and the Meta Pixel and set a Schedule or Lead event. Make a test booking and confirm it shows up.
4. Make one offer, one landing page, and two sets of creative: a 9:16 version for Instagram and a 2:3 version for Pinterest.
5. Put a four-week review in your calendar with your break-even number written beside it.

## FAQ

**Q: Pinterest or Instagram for a small budget?**
A: Start where your customers already are and book soon. If they need you this week, begin with Instagram. If the purchase is planned and visual, test Pinterest. With only $300 total, run one platform properly rather than two badly, and judge by cost per booking.

**Q: Is Pinterest advertising cheaper than Instagram?**
A: We could not confirm an official Pinterest average click price, and third-party averages mix industries. Compare your own cost per booking after a test, not someone else's cost per click.

**Q: How long should the test run?**
A: Four weeks is a reasonable starting point, longer for Pinterest if your customers take months to decide. These are our rules of thumb, not platform guidance.

**Q: Can I target a single neighbourhood on Pinterest?**
A: You can target by country, region, metro area or postal code, according to Pinterest's help page, although metro and postal availability depends on the country. Instagram uses radius and area options in Ads Manager.

**Q: Can I reuse my Instagram photos on Pinterest?**
A: You can, but re-export them. Pinterest recommends 2:3 images at 1000 x 1500 pixels, while Reels use 9:16 with margins kept clear. A reused frame may be cropped or look sparse.
