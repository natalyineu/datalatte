For most local businesses, neither platform wins on paper. Pinterest content has a search lifespan of months, while Instagram posts fade within about 48 hours, according to [PinsMachine's 2026 comparison](https://pinsmachine.com/blog/pinterest-vs-instagram-which-drives-more-blog-traffic) (secondary). That lifespan gap matters for planned purchases, but it does not tell you what a booking costs in your town. The practical answer is a small, equal test: the same budget on each platform for four weeks, judged on one number, cost per booking.

## Do Pinterest and Instagram ads work differently for local businesses?

Yes, mainly in how people arrive. Pinterest is a visual search engine: users type what they want, save ideas, and come back later. Instagram is a feed where people scroll for entertainment and updates. A business blog on [Pinterest-to-website traffic](https://pinterestaura.com/pinterest-vs-instagram/) (secondary) describes Pinterest users as planners and Instagram users as scrollers. That is a useful hypothesis, but it is not measured data.

For a local business, the question is simple. Does your customer need you this week, or do they plan weeks ahead? A same-week need, such as a cut or a drop-in class, often starts on Instagram because the audience is already following local accounts. A planned purchase, such as a bridal trial or a party booking, gives Pinterest more time to work.

The platform choice should follow your sales cycle, and your test will show which one fits.

## How long does each platform keep your content working?

Pinterest is built to keep content findable. A pin published today can still be found in search months later, which is why its average lifespan is often quoted at about 4 months (secondary, [PinsMachine](https://pinsmachine.com/blog/pinterest-vs-instagram-which-drives-more-blog-traffic)). Instagram posts lose most of their reach within 24 to 48 hours of publishing, which means a steady stream of new posts is needed to stay visible.

This matters for ads too. A Pinterest ad can keep working after the paid period ends if the pin is saved and searched, while an Instagram ad stops reaching people as soon as the campaign stops. Organic Pinterest pins can therefore build a small baseline of visits that a paid test can build on.

The lifespan figures above come from a single secondary source, so treat them as a reason to test, not as a guarantee.

## Which platform sends more clicks to your website?

The same secondary source claims Pinterest pins earn average click-through rates of 1.5% to 4%, against under 0.5% for Instagram, because Instagram captions cannot hold a clickable link and users must tap through a profile link. Those figures are sourced only to a blog, with no sample size or date range given.

Your own numbers will differ. Your ad creative, offer and audience decide clicks more than the platform does. Run the test and count clicks from each platform's ads separately, using UTM tags on every link so the numbers are not mixed up.

If Pinterest does earn a higher click rate for your offer, that is useful, but the real measure is what those clicks become. The worked example later in this article shows how a higher click rate can still lose to a lower one.

## Which local businesses suit Instagram and which suit Pinterest?

These are judgement calls rather than research findings, so use them to pick the first test, not as settled rules.

1. Cafes and restaurants: Instagram suits a new menu item, an event or a launch week. Pinterest suits seasonal ideas such as brunch or catering, which people may save weeks ahead.
2. Salons and barbershops: Instagram suits before-and-after photos and quiet-slot offers. Pinterest suits bridal and colour-inspiration searches, where a client may take months to book.
3. Pet groomers: Instagram suits neighbourhood reach and new-client offers. Pinterest can carry breed-specific style ideas, but the booking is usually fast and local.
4. Fitness and yoga studios: Instagram suits class promotions and intro offers. Pinterest suits home-workout and wellness planning, which may not turn into a local membership.

Local search also matters. If the customer searches for a service today, a [Google Local Services Ads](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) campaign may fit the job better than either social platform.

## What creative sizes should you use on each platform?

Pinterest standard vertical images are commonly listed at a 2:3 aspect ratio, 1000 by 1500 pixels, according to [Pinterest aura](https://pinterestaura.com/pinterest-vs-instagram/) (secondary). Instagram feed images are often shown at square 1:1 or vertical 4:5, according to [PinsMachine](https://pinsmachine.com/blog/pinterest-vs-instagram-which-drives-more-blog-traffic) (secondary). Check current specs in each ad manager before you build, because they change.

Do not assume one file works for both. A square Instagram image will be cropped or look sparse as a Pin. Export a separate 2:3 version for Pinterest with the offer in clear text, since people may find the ad through search. Keep the call to action plain, such as "Book now" or "Get your slot".

## How do you set up tracking before spending?

Track bookings, not just clicks. Both platforms support conversion events, and your booking system should fire the same event type for each. Set this up before spending a dollar, and confirm with a test booking that the event appears in the ad account.

Use UTM tags on every ad link, with a platform name in the source field and a campaign name. Create a separate offer code for each platform, and ask every new client how they heard about you. That answer will catch bookings the pixel misses, such as a client who saw a pin, then booked by phone.

A step-by-step walkthrough of the Pinterest side is in [how to set up Pinterest ads for a local business](/blog/how-to-set-up-pinterest-ads-local-business).

## How do you run the $300 test and read the result?

Give each platform the same $300 for four weeks, with the same offer and landing page. Every figure below is an assumption for illustration; none comes from a platform price list. Two business types are shown: a quick booking worth $85 at the first visit, and a planned booking worth $250.

Assumptions: gross margin 50%. Cost per click is $1.00 on Instagram and $0.75 on Pinterest. Booking rates are 4% quick and 1% planned on Instagram, and 2% for both on Pinterest.

| | Quick booking, Instagram | Quick booking, Pinterest | Planned booking, Instagram | Planned booking, Pinterest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spend (assumption) | $300 | $300 | $300 | $300 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $1.00 | $0.75 | $1.00 | $0.75 |
| Clicks | 300 | 400 | 300 | 400 |
| Booking rate (assumption) | 4% | 2% | 1% | 2% |
| Bookings | 12 | 8 | 3 | 8 |
| Cost per booking | $25 | $37.50 | $100 | $37.50 |
| Gross profit after ad spend | $210 | $40 | $75 | $700 |

The left pair shows Instagram winning on a quick booking because its conversion rate is assumed to be higher, even though its clicks cost more. The right pair shows Pinterest winning on a planned, high-ticket booking, where the assumed conversion rate reverses the result. The lesson is the mechanism, not the numbers: the winner depends on ticket size and sales cycle.

Two formulas help before and after the test. Maximum cost per click you can afford equals revenue per booking, times gross margin, times booking rate. At $85, 50% and 4%, that is $1.70. Break-even booking rate equals cost per click divided by revenue per booking times margin. At $1.00 per click, $85 and 50%, that is about 2.4%.

A test with 8 or 12 bookings is a sketch, not proof. If the result is close, extend the test two weeks rather than declaring a winner.

## What should you do today?

1. Write down your average first-visit revenue, your gross margin, and the number of bookings you need to cover $300 of ad spend.
2. Decide whether your customers book this week or plan months ahead. That tells you where to start.
3. Set up the booking event on your site and in each ad account, and confirm with a test booking.
4. Prepare one offer, one landing page, and two creative sizes: a 1:1 or 4:5 version for Instagram and a 2:3 version for Pinterest.
5. Book a four-week review and write your break-even booking count beside it.

## FAQ

### Is Pinterest or Instagram better for a small ad budget?

Start where your customers already are and how soon they buy. With $300 in total, run one platform properly rather than two badly, and judge it by cost per booking after four weeks.

### Is Pinterest advertising cheaper than Instagram?

Not on the evidence available here. The only cost-per-click figures in this article are assumptions. Compare your own cost per booking after a test, not someone else's average.

### How long should the test run?

Four weeks is a sensible starting point. Give Pinterest longer if your customers take months to decide, because saves and searches can convert after the test ends.

### Can I reuse my Instagram photos on Pinterest?

You can, but re-export them. Pinterest standard images are commonly listed at 2:3 and 1000 by 1500 pixels, so a square or 9:16 frame will need cropping and rework.

### Which platform works better for a neighbourhood business?

Instagram often suits same-week offers and local reach, while Pinterest suits planned purchases. Many neighbourhood businesses will find a local search campaign matches demand faster than either social test.
