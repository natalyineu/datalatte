AI-driven customer loyalty programs use software to track visits and spending, then tailor rewards and reminders to each customer. For a local business, the practical version comes down to five ideas: points tied to visits or spend, milestone rewards, win-back messages for lapsed customers, welcome offers, and birthday perks. Most small businesses budget $50–150 a month for a loyalty program, according to [CostBench](https://costbench.com/best/loyalty-program-for-small-business/) (secondary), so cost is the first thing to check before paying for any AI feature.

## Should points be earned by visits, spend, or items?

Points are the simplest starting point, and the earning rule matters as much as the reward. [Square Loyalty](https://squareup.com/us/en/software/loyalty) lets a business choose whether customers earn points by visits, amount spent, or items or categories purchased. A café that wants more regulars can reward visits. A shop with high-margin products can reward spend or specific categories.

Square's VIP tier example gives Bronze customers 1.5x points, Silver 2x and Gold 3x. Tiers let a business reward its most frequent customers without changing the base offer for everyone else.

Where AI fits: the tool should suggest which earning rule suits your sales mix, based on your own transaction history. Ask the vendor to show that report before you sign up. If it cannot, you are paying for a points program, which works without AI.

## Do milestone rewards like "buy six, get one free" work?

Milestone cards are the easiest loyalty idea to explain at the counter. [EMTWO](https://www.emtwodigital.com/4-ideas-to-get-more-clients-for-your-pet-grooming-salon/) (secondary) proposes "buy 6 subs, get 1 free" as a model for dog grooming loyalty. Meevo's salon example offers a free deep conditioning treatment after every five visits or a 10% discount on every tenth visit, presented as an illustrative example ([Meevo](https://www.meevo.com/blog/salon-automation/), secondary).

Choose the milestone to match how often people visit. If a client books every six weeks (assumption), a six-visit card takes about eight months to fill. A café customer visiting weekly would reach the same milestone far sooner, so the reward cadence should differ by business type.

## How do you win back customers who have stopped visiting?

Win-back is often the most useful loyalty work, because lapsed customers are already known to you. [Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/customer-reactivation-campaign-strategies) (secondary) identifies email as the primary channel for most reactivation programs because of its scalability and personalizability. For a messaging-led market, see the [Brazil guide on WhatsApp-based local marketing](/blog/local-marketing-brazil-small-business-whatsapp-meta).

Set a lapse rule first. For example, flag anyone with no visit in 60 days (assumption), then send one offer rather than a long series. AI-assisted tools can build that list automatically, so check that the lapse date is calculated from your own visit data.

Winback Engine says loyalty programs can increase retention by 5–30% ([Winback Engine](https://winbackengine.com/blog/customer-reactivation-guide/), secondary). That range is a vendor claim, not a forecast for your shop, so test one offer against last quarter's results.

## Can a QR code welcome offer grow your customer list?

BonusQR's sample salon copy promises 10% off for scanning a code to join a VIP club ([BonusQR](https://bonusqr.com/article/10-actionable-hair-salon-marketing-ideas-to-keep-your-chairs-full), secondary). The offer is simple, and the code creates a sign-up point at the chair or counter rather than waiting for someone to ask.

Put the discount in the first line of the welcome message, and keep the join step short enough to finish while the customer waits (test the time on your own phone). For mobile-first customers, a [Kenya guide on mobile-first local marketing](/blog/local-marketing-kenya-small-business-mobile-first) covers the same principle in a different market.

## Is a birthday perk worth running?

T2's Tea Society includes a free birthday brew as a member benefit, as described in the [Zipzipe local marketing guide](https://www.zipzipe.com.au/blog/local-marketing/) (secondary). Birthday perks need only a date field and one message, so they are cheap to run. The cost depends on what you give away, and a Japanese messaging-app approach is covered in the [Japan guide to LINE and Google](/blog/local-marketing-japan-small-business-line-google).

Confirm that your sign-up form collects a birth date, and check whether the tool sends the message automatically. Many tools depend on that field, so a missing date means no birthday messages at all.

### Worked example: a single café budget

Loop's illustrative café model is used below as a template. All inputs are assumptions, not Loop prices or typical results ([Loop](https://authors.loop.fans/blog/how-much-does-a-loyalty-program-cost), secondary).

Assumptions: 300 active members; 30 reward redemptions a month at a $2 marginal cost each; two hours of staff administration a month at $25 an hour; $40 a month for software and messaging.

Monthly cost: redemptions $60 + staff $50 + software and messaging $40 = $150 a month. That sits at the top of the $50–150 budget band.

Cost per active member: $150 ÷ 300 = $0.50 a month (assumption-based).

Break-even: assume a $3 gross margin per extra visit (assumption). Covering $150 needs 50 extra visits a month, or roughly one extra visit for every six members (assumption). If the café cannot expect that lift, a cheaper tool or a smaller member base is the honest choice.

## How do you check whether the program pays off?

Check the numbers once a week. Compare redemptions, repeat visits and lapsed-customer returns against the same weeks last year, not against a guess. Square's own survey reports that 82% of restaurant leaders say their program drives repeat visits and 83% say it boosts order size ([Square](https://squareup.com/ca/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/marketing-plan-for-a-coffee-shop)). Those figures are self-reported by operators, not a controlled test, so treat them as a reason to measure, not as proof.

Ask one question each week: who is about to leave, and which offer would bring them back? Then send that offer. If the answer stays blank for a month, the program is not working, whatever the software promises.

## FAQ

### Do I need AI for a loyalty program, or will a punch card work?

Points, milestones and welcome offers work without AI. A paper card or a rule-based digital program can run the same ideas. The AI question is whether the tool reads your sales data to suggest rules or pick who gets which offer. Check that feature before paying a premium for it.

### How much does a loyalty program cost each month?

CostBench's median across 12 priced loyalty tools is $207 a month, while its guidance for most small businesses is $50–150 a month ([CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/software/loyalty-program/), secondary). Only two of the 16 listed tools offer free tiers, so most small shops should expect to pay for something or build the program with free tools and staff time.

### Does Square Loyalty cost money after the trial?

Square Loyalty offers a free trial lasting 30 days ([Square Loyalty](https://squareup.com/us/en/software/loyalty)). Square also says the built-in CRM tools it integrates with are free. Check the live pricing page for what follows the trial, because plans and fees can change.

### Is a loyalty program worth it for a shop with a few hundred customers?

It depends on the margin and the number of extra visits it produces. In the worked café example above, a $150 monthly cost needs about 50 extra visits a month at an assumed $3 margin. Run the same calculation with your own member count, reward cost and margin before committing.
