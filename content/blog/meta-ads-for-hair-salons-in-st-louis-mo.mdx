## How Much Should a St. Louis Salon Spend on Meta Ads?

Vendor guides give a starting range rather than one price. Bookeo suggests starting Facebook and Instagram ad spend at USD 750 to USD 1,500 monthly ([Bookeo's social media guide](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/)). Adwave puts a standalone Meta buy at often $300 to $1,000 per month ([Adwave's channel guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels)). The lower figure suits one location testing a single offer. The higher figure gives a campaign more reach to learn from.

Costs also depend on how Meta prices delivery. Adwave's typical bands are $5 to $15 per thousand impressions and $1 to $3 per click ([Adwave's marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-small-business-guide)). Treat these as planning bands. Your St. Louis results will depend on creative, offer and audience, so check the numbers after the first few weeks of spend.

## What Does a $750 Monthly Test Return for a Salon?

The example below uses assumption inputs so you can swap in your own figures. Every input marked assumption is an illustrative value, not a sourced fact.

- Monthly budget: $750 (assumption, the bottom of Bookeo's suggested range)
- Cost per thousand impressions: $10 (assumption, midpoint of Adwave's $5 to $15 band)
- Cost per click: $2 (assumption, midpoint of Adwave's $1 to $3 band)
- Click-to-booking rate: 4% (assumption)
- Average booked visit value: $85 (assumption)

Calculation:

- Impressions: 750 / 10 x 1,000 = 75,000
- Clicks: 750 / 2 = 375
- Bookings: 375 x 4% = 15
- Cost per booking: 750 / 15 = $50
- Booked revenue: 15 x $85 = $1,275
- Return on spend: 1,275 / 750 = 1.7x

This is return on booked revenue, not profit, because product and staff costs are excluded. The midpoint inputs also imply a click-through rate of about 0.5%, so the creative has to beat that to pay off. If the booking rate drops to 2%, bookings fall to about 7 to 8 and the return falls to about 0.85x.

## Which Ad Formats Work Best for Hair Salon Meta Campaigns?

Short-form video is the clearest format signal in the sources. Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, which says short video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram ([Adwave's home services guide](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners)). For a salon, that points to Reels that show a cut, colour or styling process, not only a finished photo.

Stories suit time-sensitive offers. Associated Hair Professionals notes that Instagram and Facebook stories are temporary and that top influencers use them to share insights into their lives ([Associated Hair Professionals](https://www.associatedhairprofessionals.com/updates/blog-posts/power-social-media-your-hairstyling-brand)). Use Stories for same-week openings and keep the full service menu in feed posts.

Filming costs less than many owners expect. One of One Group, a London social media agency, says a month of salon content can be shot in a single salon visit ([One of One Group](https://oneofonegroup.co.uk/social-media-marketing/hair-salons/)). Pair that footage with the [Instagram marketing guide for hair salons](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-hair-salons) to keep organic and paid posts consistent.

## How Do You Target a Local Salon Audience?

Bookeo advises setting a 5 to 10 mile radius around the salon for Facebook and Instagram ads ([Bookeo's social media guide](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/)). Radius matters more than raw audience size. Adwave reports that Meta's apps reach an average of 3.56 billion daily active people ([Adwave's Facebook guide](https://adwave.com/resources/facebook-advertising-for-small-business)), so for a single salon the limit is local relevance, not reach.

Start with your existing clients. A custom audience built from your client list gives Meta a clear signal of who already books, and a lookalike audience can be built from that list. Test age and gender splits by service rather than assuming one profile fits every treatment. For a Facebook-only setup, see [Facebook ads for hair salons](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons).

## Where Do You Set Up and Manage the Campaigns?

Meta Ads Manager, formerly Facebook Ads Manager, works as a central point for running ads across Instagram and Facebook ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising)). Instagram ads run through the same system because Instagram is owned by Meta, and one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook or both ([Adwave's Instagram guide](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business)). Turrboo lists Messenger alongside them ([Turrboo, secondary](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media)). Check placements before launch and turn off any that do not fit a local salon audience.

Meta also offers training. A secondary source describes Meta's Blueprint Facebook and Instagram advertising courses as free, with certification on completion ([Sascom247, secondary](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/)). Check Meta's live help page for current course details before you enrol.

## Should Salons Post Organically Alongside Ads?

Bookeo says most salon owners find three to four Instagram posts and one to two Facebook posts a week sustainable ([Bookeo's social media guide](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/)). Its guide also states that Facebook and Instagram can build trust and reach audiences without spending on the platform itself. Check peak follower activity in the Insights tab, which requires a business account.

Bookeo also claims that a client who has seen a salon's brand on Facebook 5 to 10 times converts at 2 to 3 times the rate of a cold searcher. The page gives no source for that figure, so test it with a retargeting audience before using it in planning.

## How Should You Track Bookings From Meta Ads?

Before spend begins, confirm three things. The Meta Pixel should be installed and firing on the booking confirmation and contact pages. A Lead event should fire when a booking form is submitted. UTM parameters should be on every ad URL so analytics can separate Meta traffic. Without these, the only numbers visible are impressions and reach, which do not show whether chairs are filling.

Follow-up matters as much as the ad. The reminder and rebooking steps in [marketing automation for hair salons](/blog/marketing-automation-for-hair-salons) turn a first booking into a repeat one. For reporting, One of One Group sends a monthly report on followers, reach and enquiries ([One of One Group](https://oneofonegroup.co.uk/social-media-marketing/hair-salons/)), which works as a template for your own monthly review. If search also brings clients, compare cost per booking against the [Google Ads guide for St. Louis hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-in-st-louis-mo).

## What Mistakes Waste a Salon's Meta Budget?

Two errors waste budget quickly. The first is sending clicks to a generic homepage instead of a booking page, which breaks the path from ad to appointment. The second is running one creative for months. Refresh the creative when results fall, and check your figures against the worked example above before raising spend.

## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Meta ads each month?

Bookeo suggests starting Facebook and Instagram spend at USD 750 to USD 1,500 monthly. Adwave gives a standalone Meta buy of often $300 to $1,000 per month. Start at the low end, measure cost per booking, and raise spend only if the numbers hold.

### Are Reels or static posts better for salon ads?

Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, which says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images. Test Reels against static posts with the same offer, and keep whichever produces more bookings.

### Can Facebook and Instagram ads be managed in one place?

Yes. Meta Ads Manager runs ads across Instagram and Facebook, and one campaign can run on both. Adobe Express describes it as a centralized point for this, and Turrboo lists Messenger as an additional placement.

### Is there free training for Meta advertising?

A secondary source says Meta's Blueprint courses on Facebook and Instagram advertising are free and include certification on completion. Check Meta's live help page for current course details before you enrol.
