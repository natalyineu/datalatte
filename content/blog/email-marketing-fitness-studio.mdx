Acquiring a new gym member costs 5–7 times more than retaining an existing one. Yet most fitness studios invest heavily in ads to attract new members while barely communicating with the ones they already have.

Email is the most cost-effective retention channel available to fitness businesses. It doesn't depend on algorithm changes, doesn't require a large budget, and reaches members directly in their inbox — where they're more attentive than on a social feed. For fitness studios specifically, a well-run email program reduces churn, reactivates lapsed members, and increases revenue per member through class pack upsells and referrals.

This guide covers the exact sequences to build, the platforms to use, and the benchmarks to measure.

## The 5 Email Sequences Every Fitness Studio Needs

### 1. Welcome Sequence (Triggered Automatically at Sign-Up)

The first 30 days of a membership are the most critical for long-term retention. Research consistently shows that members who attend at least 3 classes in their first two weeks are far more likely to stick around for 12 months. Your welcome sequence's job is to drive that third visit.

**Email 1 (Day 1) — Welcome and orientation**
Practical information: class schedule, how to book, who to ask for help, parking or access instructions. Warm tone. Keep it short — they're excited, not ready to read 800 words.

**Email 2 (Day 3) — Community introduction**
Share a trainer profile or member story. Help them see themselves as part of something larger than a gym membership. One paragraph about your studio's ethos goes a long way here.

**Email 3 (Day 7) — Attendance-based branch**
- *If they've attended:* "You've already taken your first class! Here are three more we think you'll love based on what you booked." Celebrate the early wins.
- *If they haven't attended yet:* "We noticed you haven't made it in yet — here's a beginner-friendly class that's great for your first visit." A specific suggestion converts better than a general "come anytime."

**Email 4 (Day 14) — Challenge or milestone prompt**
A class spotlight, a 21-day challenge invitation, or a goal-setting prompt. "Trying to build a habit? Members who book in advance are 3× more likely to attend — here's this week's schedule."

**Email 5 (Day 28) — Personal check-in**
"How are you settling in? Reply if there's anything we can help with." Studios that actually respond to replies build the kind of loyalty that advertising can't buy.

### 2. Weekly or Bi-Weekly Newsletter

The goal of this email is not sales — it's community. Send it consistently so members start to expect it.

**What to include:**
- Class schedule highlights for the coming week (especially new or popular sessions)
- Trainer spotlight — helps members connect with instructors before they try a new class
- Member success story (with permission) — social proof that motivates
- One fitness tip or educational piece (short and practical — under 150 words)
- Single call to action: "Book your spot for Saturday's class →"

**Format:** Under 400 words. Members scan emails on mobile — make the most important thing visible in the first scroll.

**Frequency:** Weekly for studios with daily programming. Bi-weekly for smaller studios with simpler schedules.

### 3. Re-Engagement Sequence (For Members Who've Gone Quiet)

A lapsed member is someone who hasn't visited in 3–4 weeks and hasn't cancelled. They're at-risk — and reachable.

**Email 1 (3 weeks of inactivity):** "We miss you." Warm, not guilt-inducing. "It's been a while since we've seen you — we hope everything's okay. We've got some great new classes on the schedule if you want to ease back in."

**Email 2 (5 weeks):** A specific reason to return. "Come back this week and we'll save you a spot in our popular Saturday morning yoga — no booking required." A reserved spot removes the friction of searching the schedule and committing cold.

**Email 3 (7 weeks):** More information, less emotional. "Your membership is still active — here's what's new since your last visit." Include any new class types, trainers, equipment, or programming. Practical updates reactivate people whose lapse was situational (travel, illness, busy period) rather than motivational.

**Email 4 (Before cancellation):** Direct. "Before you go — is there anything we can do to make the studio work better for you?" Offer a pause option, a discounted class pack, or simply ask. The response rate on this email is low, but the conversion rate among those who respond is high.

A well-built re-engagement sequence can retain 10–20% of at-risk members who would otherwise quietly cancel.

### 4. Seasonal and Special Occasion Emails

These drive short-term revenue spikes and show members you're paying attention to them as individuals.

**Birthday email:** "Happy birthday from [Studio Name] — enjoy a free class on us this month." Surprisingly effective at driving attendance and generating goodwill. Set up as an automation triggered by the member's birthday date.

**New Year campaign (late December/early January):** Highlight new programs, challenges, or intro offers. January is the single highest gym-interest month of the year — email your existing members before the ad spend for new members goes through the roof.

**Pre-summer push (April–May):** "Summer is coming — join our 6-week transformation challenge." Specific programming with a defined start date converts better than a general "get in shape" message.

**Referral campaign:** "Know someone who needs a fitness community? Bring a friend for a free trial class — and get a free session for yourself." Referrals from satisfied members convert at dramatically higher rates than cold ad traffic and cost you only a free session.

### 5. Class Reminders and Post-Class Follow-Ups

If your studio uses a booking system (Mindbody, Glofox, Zen Planner, Pike13), these should be fully automated.

**Pre-class reminder (24 hours before):** "Your spot in Tuesday's 6:30am HIIT class is confirmed. See you tomorrow!" Reduces no-shows by 15–25%.

**Post-class message (2–4 hours after):** "Great class today! Ready to book your next session?" Drives re-booking while motivation is highest. The best post-class emails include a direct link to the booking page pre-filtered to similar class types.

## List Growth: How to Build Your Email List

If you don't have a solid member email list, start here:

**At sign-up:** Collect email as part of the membership form — this is the highest-value source.

**Free trial opt-in:** Offer a free trial class in exchange for name and email. These leads are warm and convert well to paid membership.

**Pop-up on website:** A simple "Join our community — get the weekly class schedule and fitness tips" captures visitors who aren't ready to book yet.

**In-studio QR code:** A QR code at the front desk linking to a simple sign-up form for members who want workout tips, community updates, or early access to new class launches.

## What to Measure

| Metric | Target for Fitness Studios | What It Tells You |
|---|---|---|
| Open rate | 35–50% | Subject line relevance, sender reputation |
| Click rate | 5–15% | Content relevance, CTA clarity |
| Unsubscribe rate | Under 0.3% per email | Frequency or relevance issues |
| Re-engagement rate | 10–20% of lapsed members | Sequence effectiveness |
| Attendance change | Primary metric | Are emails driving actual visits? |

The most important metric is the one not in your email platform: **member attendance**. Track whether members who receive your emails attend more frequently than those who don't. In most studios, email-engaged members churn at significantly lower rates.

## Common Mistakes That Kill Fitness Studio Email Performance

**Sending too infrequently:** Monthly emails from a gym feel like marketing, not community. Weekly or bi-weekly at minimum.

**Sending only promotions:** If every email is "buy a class pack," your open rates fall fast. The 80/20 rule: 80% value (community, education, updates), 20% offer.

**Not personalizing the sender:** "noreply@studiox.com" has a 30–40% lower open rate than a real person's name. Use "Natalie at Studio X" as the sender.

**Ignoring mobile:** 60–70% of fitness studio members read emails on mobile. Test every email on your phone before sending. Long paragraphs and small text destroy mobile readability.

**No segmentation:** Sending the same email to a new member and a 3-year member. At minimum, segment: new members (first 30 days), active members, lapsed members. Each group needs different messaging.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should a fitness studio send marketing emails?

Weekly is the sweet spot for most studios with regular programming. Bi-weekly works for smaller studios with simpler schedules. Monthly is too infrequent — members start to feel like strangers by the time they hear from you, and open rates drop. Promotional emails (special offers, seasonal campaigns) are in addition to your regular newsletter, not instead of it. Members who receive a mix of value emails and occasional offers have higher lifetime value than those who only receive promotions.

### What is a good email open rate for a gym or fitness studio?

For member emails (people who already know your studio), target 35–50%. This is significantly higher than B2C marketing averages (20–25%) because your audience has opted in and has an ongoing relationship with your brand. If your open rates are below 25%, check three things: your subject lines (are they specific and compelling?), your sender name (real person beats "noreply"), and your list hygiene (are you sending to inactive addresses that drag down your engagement score?).

### What email platform should a small fitness studio use?

For studios just starting email marketing, Mailchimp's free plan (up to 500 contacts) or Mailerlite (free up to 1,000) are the right starting points. Once you need automated welcome sequences and re-engagement triggers, ActiveCampaign ($29/month) is worth the upgrade — its automation capabilities are significantly better than Mailchimp's paid plans at a similar price. Studios using Mindbody or Glofox should first evaluate whether those platforms' built-in email tools can handle their needs before adding a separate platform.

### How do I get more gym members on my email list?

Collect email at sign-up (required), offer a free trial class in exchange for email for non-members, add a simple newsletter opt-in on your website, and use a QR code at the front desk for members who aren't in your system yet. Don't buy email lists — bought lists have poor deliverability and damage your sender reputation. Organic lists of 500 engaged members outperform purchased lists of 5,000 disengaged contacts every time.

### What should fitness studio email subject lines say?

Specific beats clever. "This week's schedule + a new class you'll love" outperforms "Your weekend starts here." Numbers work well ("3 classes this week that are filling fast"), questions work in moderation ("Are you making this recovery mistake?"), and personalization helps when available ("Alex, your next challenge is ready"). Avoid "SALE," "% OFF," and all-caps in subject lines — they trigger spam filters and look like mass marketing rather than community communication.
