A fitness studio should send five kinds of email: a welcome series, a regular newsletter, a win-back message for members who have stopped coming, seasonal offers, and class reminders. Start with the welcome series and the win-back, because those reach members at the points where they are most likely to drift. The [Vertical Impression gym marketing guide](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026) sets its retention emails at Day 7, Day 30 and Day 60 after sign-up, and recommends automated win-back sequences for members who have missed 3 or more classes.

The retention stakes are significant. Accelo research, as cited by that guide, found that increasing customer retention by just 5% can boost profits by 25–95%.

Judge email by visits and bookings rather than opens. Opens are a weak signal, so the rest of this guide focuses on what members do after they read a message.

## Which Emails Should a Fitness Studio Send?

Five types cover most needs. The table gives a starting structure for your own testing, not a published standard.

| Email | Trigger | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome series | Sign-up or trial booking | Get the first classes booked |
| Newsletter | Weekly or fortnightly | Keep the studio in members' minds and fill the schedule |
| Win-back | No visit for a period you choose | Bring drifting members back before they cancel |
| Seasonal or offer | Calendar dates, new programmes | Fill specific classes or courses |
| Reminder and follow-up | Class booking | Cut no-shows and prompt the next booking |

The newsletter is the ongoing channel, and the [fitness studio email newsletter guide](/blog/fitness-studio-email-newsletter) covers its format. Automated sequences do the heavy lifting for the other types, since email follow-up sequences are among the repetitive, rule-based tasks that [RingCentral's marketing automation list](https://www.ringcentral.com/us/en/blog/10-marketing-automation-tools-every-smb-needs/) says can be automated.

## How Should the Welcome Series Be Timed?

The Vertical Impression sequence runs in three steps. Day 7 is a welcome and tips email. Day 30 celebrates a milestone. Day 60 encourages the habit and includes a referral ask. Each step can be a short message with one call to action, such as booking the next class.

The early weeks matter most. Second Measure reports that 66% of the January 2017 new-member cohort at Planet Fitness was still active at 10 months, compared with an average of 59% for the preceding 11 cohorts, based on vendor data. Those figures come from one large chain, so treat them as context rather than a target for a studio.

Studios with a January surge should also read the [New Year membership marketing guide](/blog/fitness-studio-new-year-membership-marketing). A Wisconsin gym member told Spectrum News that crowds taper off within weeks and months after Jan. 1. That is one member's observation, not data, but it fits the case for an early sequence.

## When Should a Win-Back Email Go Out?

The sourced threshold is 3 or more missed classes. For a member who normally attends weekly, that is a practical trigger, and a studio can adjust it to its own booking pattern.

Keep the message short and warm. Offer one specific class and time rather than a general "we miss you." If there is no reply, a second message with news, such as a new class or a schedule change, is a sensible next step. Our [membership retention tactics guide](/blog/fitness-studio-membership-retention-tactics) covers what to do when a member does not respond.

## How Should Class Reminders Work?

Mariana Tek advises sending an SMS reminder a few hours before class to reduce no-shows. Its guidance on pop-up leads is to follow up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades fast after a pop-up ends.

GoConverso's scheduling guide states that email and SMS reminders reduce no-shows by 60–70%. That guide does not name a source for the figure, so use it as a claim to test in your own bookings rather than a benchmark.

## What Is a Realistic Email Budget?

Mariana Tek says many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing. The worked example below uses assumed inputs. Replace them with your own numbers.

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Members (assumption) | 120 |
| Monthly fee (assumption) | $110 |
| Monthly revenue | 120 x $110 = $13,200 |
| Marketing budget at 5% | $660 |
| Marketing budget at 10% | $1,320 |
| Members emailed in one win-back round (assumption) | 15 |
| Members who return and stay 3 more months (assumption) | 3 |
| Revenue kept | 3 x $110 x 3 = $990 |

Three rescued members out of 15 emails is a 20% response, which is an assumption and not a benchmark. If only one member stays, the revenue kept is $330. Set that against the cost of the email tool and the staff time to write and schedule the messages. A free plan keeps the tool cost near zero, but time still has a price.

## Can Email Help Members Refer Friends?

The ClubRight guide gives an illustrative example: even if only half of a studio's members each refer one friend, the membership base would still double. This is a thought experiment, not a forecast, so check it against your own referral numbers.

The Day 60 referral ask in the welcome sequence is the natural place to start. Referrals can also come up in social posts, and the [social media marketing strategy for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-marketing-strategy) covers how to combine the two.

## What Does the Law Require?

Marketing email to US recipients falls under the CAN-SPAM Act. The FTC's [CAN-SPAM compliance guide for business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) sets out the requirements, including accurate header information and a working opt-out. Read the current text before you build a sequence, because the rules are specific.

If your studio emails people in the UK, Canada or Australia, check local rules separately. Those requirements differ from US law.

## FAQ

### How often should a studio email members?

There is no universal number. Weekly or fortnightly is a common pace for a newsletter, but test the rhythm against bookings and unsubscribes in your own list.

### What open rate should a fitness studio expect?

Opens are a weak signal because email privacy features can register opens automatically. One vendor case study, Gleantap's account of Studio Svelte, reports a 50% open rate on personalized fitness campaigns run with automation. Judge your emails by visits and bookings instead.

### Which email platform is best?

Start with what your booking software offers. If you need a separate tool, compare free plans and check live pricing, since costs rise with list size.

### Can I buy an email list?

No. Send only to people who gave you their address, and include a working opt-out in every marketing email.
