A fitness studio should send five kinds of email: a welcome series, a regular newsletter, a win-back message for members who stopped coming, seasonal offers, and class reminders. Start with the welcome and win-back messages, because they act on the moment members are most likely to drift. MailerLite's benchmark data for December 2024 to November 2025 shows health and fitness emails with a 47.81% average open rate and a 1.45% click rate, against 43.46% and 2.09% for all industries.

Open rates are inflated by privacy features that pre-load emails, so judge success by visits and bookings, not opens. Every figure below comes from a source listed at the end.

## Which Emails Should a Fitness Studio Send?

Five types cover most needs. The timing in the table is a starting suggestion, not a tested standard.

| Email | Trigger | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome series | Sign-up or trial booking | Get the first classes booked |
| Newsletter | Weekly or fortnightly | Keep the studio in members' minds, fill the schedule |
| Win-back | No visit for a period you choose | Bring drifting members back before they cancel |
| Seasonal or offer | Calendar dates, new programmes | Fill specific classes or courses |
| Reminder and follow-up | Class booking | Cut no-shows and prompt the next booking |

Research on 1,726 new members found that visits fall from a mean of 7.48 in month one to 0.92 in month twelve, and that early attendance predicts who keeps attending. That is why the welcome and win-back emails come first. See our [retention tactics](/blog/fitness-studio-membership-retention-tactics) for the member-side detail.

## How Should a Welcome Series Work?

Keep it to three or four short emails over the first month. A practical order: practical information (schedule, booking, access), then an introduction to an instructor, then a message that depends on whether the person has attended yet, then a check-in near day 28.

The branch matters. A person who has attended gets class suggestions. A person who has not gets one beginner-friendly class with a booking link. Our guide to a [trial-to-annual email sequence](/blog/fitness-studio-email-sequence-trial-to-annual-member) shows a longer version.

## When Should You Send a Win-Back Email?

Pick a threshold from your own booking data, such as no visits for three weeks for a member who normally comes weekly. Send a short, warm message offering one specific class and time. If there is no reply, a second message with practical news, such as new classes or schedule changes, works better than a guilt-trip.

No published benchmark gives the share of lapsed members this wins back, so track your own reply and return rates.

## What Does the Law Require?

For US marketing email, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide lists eight requirements. The main ones: accurate header information, honest subject lines, a clear ad disclosure, your physical postal address, and a working opt-out. Opt-outs must be honoured within 10 business days. Each violating email can carry a penalty of up to $53,088.

Members are not exempt: the FTC says subscribers and members can opt out of marketing email too. If you email in the UK, Canada or Australia, check local rules separately; they were not reviewed for this article.

## The Math: What Is One Rescued Member Worth?

All inputs are **assumptions**. A studio with 120 members, a $110 monthly fee, sends a win-back email to 15 members who have not visited in three weeks.

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Members emailed | 15 |
| Members who return and stay 3 more months (assumption) | 3 |
| Revenue kept | 3 x $110 x 3 = $990 |
| Cost of the email tool | $0 on a free plan |

Three rescued members is a 20% response in this example, which is an assumption, not a benchmark. If only one stays, the revenue kept is $330. Compare either number with your own tool cost and the time to write the email. ## FAQ

### How often should a studio email members?

No source sets a number. Weekly or fortnightly is common, and the right pace is the one where unsubscribes stay near the 0.30% MailerLite reports for health and fitness.

### What is a good open rate for a fitness studio?

MailerLite's 2025 data gives 47.81% for health and fitness and 47.69% for sports. Treat it as a rough guide, because opens are inflated by email privacy features.

### Which email platform is best?

For a small list, a free plan from MailerLite or Mailchimp is enough to start. Move up when you need more than a few automations.

### Can I buy an email list?

Don't. Send to people who gave you their address, and include your postal address and an opt-out in every marketing email.
