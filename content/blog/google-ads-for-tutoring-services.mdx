Say a click on "SAT tutor" costs $10. If 1 in 12 clicks turns into an enquiry and 1 in 4 enquiries becomes a student, one new student costs you $480 in ads. Whether that is a bargain or a disaster depends entirely on what one student is worth to you. Those three inputs are assumptions, not benchmarks, and the point of this guide is to help you replace them with your own numbers before you spend anything.

It is written for independent tutors and small tutoring centers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Dollar figures are US-based; use the same math with your local currency and your own click prices. We cover what clicks tend to cost, how to build a Search campaign parents will actually respond to, what to track, and how to work out your break-even.

## What the Numbers Say (Treat Them as Directional)

There is no clean public benchmark for tutoring alone. Two sources give a rough picture.

**Education as a category.** [Webtonic's benchmark page](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/google-ads-benchmarks) lists Education & Instruction search medians for April 2025 to March 2026, drawn from the WordStream 2026 benchmarks report (more than 13,000 search campaigns across 23 industries, Google and Microsoft search combined):

Education covers universities, bootcamps and courses, so a local K-12 tutor is a small corner of it. Use it as a sanity check, not a target.

**Tutoring keywords specifically.** A [2020 post by tutor Max Youngquist on Tutor Scale](https://tutors.substack.com/p/are-google-ads-worth-it), using Keyword Planner screenshots, reports top-of-page bids of roughly $3.27 to $13.74 for "math tutor", $2 to $5 for English tutoring, over $10 for ACT and SAT tutoring and $7 to $30 for LSAT. It adds that the real price paid is usually below the bid (his "math tutor" example: about $2.62 a click) and warns that the vast majority of clicks will not turn into students. That is a single, six-year-old, self-published source with no stated location, so treat the ranges as an illustration of how much subjects differ, not a quote for your town.

What to take from both:

- Subject matters more than anything. Test prep tends to cost more per click than general homework help, according to the tutor source.
- Most clicks will not become students. Plan for it in your budget.
- Check your own prices: Google's [Keyword Planner help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243) says it shows monthly search estimates and the average cost to appear on a keyword. Run your five main keywords for your own city before you commit.

## Step 1: Pick One Offer Per Audience

Tutoring searches are specific, so your campaign should be too. Decide which of these you will advertise first:

- **Parents of school-age children:** "reading tutor for 2nd grader", "maths tutor year 9"
- **Test prep:** "SAT tutor", "ACT prep near me", "11 plus tutor" (UK), "NAPLAN tutoring" (AU)
- **Adults and college students:** "calculus tutor", "statistics help"
- **Format:** in-person, in-home or online

Start with one. A single focused campaign teaches you more than five thin ones.

## Step 2: Campaign Type and Match Types

Use a **Search** campaign. Search ads show when someone types a query, which is exactly the moment a parent is worried about a report card. Keep Performance Max and Display for later, once you have steady conversions.

Google's [match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) says broad match is the default and can show on related searches without the exact words, phrase match reaches searches with your keyword's meaning, and exact match gives the most control but the fewest searches. Google also says Smart Bidding is critical with broad match. With a small budget and no conversion history, start with phrase and exact.

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject + place | "math tutor [city]", [reading tutor near me] | Phrase + exact |
| Test prep | "SAT tutor [city]", "ACT prep tutoring" | Phrase + exact |
| Need-led | "tutor for dyslexia", "help with fractions" | Phrase |
| Format | "online tutor", "in home tutor [city]" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your tutoring business name] | Exact |

### Negative keywords on day one

`free`, `worksheets`, `pdf`, `answers`, `homework answers`, `jobs`, `become a tutor`, `tutor jobs`, `salary`, `courses`, `app`, `youtube`, `chegg`. The tutor source recommends excluding "tutoring jobs", "tutor salary" and "tutor openings" for the same reason: tutors searching for work click ads too. ## Step 3: Write Ads Parents Respond To

Parents want to know you can fix their specific problem and that starting is easy.

- Headlines: `Math Tutor in [City]`, `Free 15-Minute Intro Call`, `SAT Prep: 1:1 or Small Group`, `Reading Help, Grades K-5`
- Description: `Experienced tutors, flexible scheduling, progress updates for parents. Book a free consultation today.`
- Assets: call, location, sitelinks (subjects, pricing, how it works, reviews)

**Watch your promises.** Google's [unreliable claims policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936857) says that if you guarantee certain results you must have a clear, easily accessible refund policy, and testimonials claiming specific results need a visible disclaimer that results are not guaranteed and can vary. "Raise your SAT score 200 points guaranteed" is the kind of line that invites a disapproval. "Small-group SAT prep with weekly practice tests" is safe. Only claim credentials you hold.

## Step 4: A Landing Page That Gets the Call

Send each ad group to a page that repeats the subject. It needs a headline matching the ad, subjects and grades covered, a visible phone number and short form above the fold, a free intro option (if you offer one), two or three real reviews with permission, and a clear price or next step. Click your own ad on your phone: if you cannot contact yourself in a few seconds, fix that first. Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) go through the layout.

## Step 5: Budget, Bidding and Seasons

Pick a test budget you can sustain for at least four to six weeks (my rule of thumb, not a Google figure). Google needs conversions to learn, and tutoring is seasonal: back to school, report cards, exam and test registration periods, with quieter stretches in summer for some subjects. Check your own inquiry history before judging a single slow week.

1. Start with manual CPC or Maximize Clicks with a CPC cap while data builds.
2. Track phone calls, forms and booked intro sessions as conversions. See our [conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup).
3. Move to a conversion-based strategy once conversions are steady.
4. Raise budgets before known peaks, not during them.

If most parents phone, consider [call-only campaigns](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns), but test them against a landing page with reviews and pricing.

## Step 6: Track Enquiries, Then Students

Ad platforms count enquiries. Only you know which became paying students. Keep a simple sheet: date, source, subject, enquiry type, booked intro, became a student. Answer fast: a parent usually contacts more than one tutor, so reply the same day and call back missed calls.

## The Math

Take an $800 monthly budget. Every input marked "assumption" is illustrative, not a benchmark. The Base CPC sits near the $4.81 education median above, and the Tough CPC sits inside the tutor source's higher test-prep range; the enquiry and close rates are placeholders because no trustworthy tutoring-specific data exists. Replace them with your own after a month.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $800 | $800 | $800 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $8.00 | $4.81 | $3.00 |
| Clicks | 100 | 166 | 267 |
| Click to enquiry rate (assumption) | 4% | 8% | 10% |
| Enquiries | 4 | 13.3 | 26.7 |
| Cost per enquiry | $200 | $60 | $30 |
| Enquiry to student rate (assumption) | 15% | 25% | 35% |
| New students | 0.6 | 3.3 | 9.3 |
| Ad cost per student | $1,333 | $240 | $86 |
| Gross margin per student (assumption) | $384 | $384 | $384 |
| Margin after ad cost | -$949 | +$144 | +$298 |

The margin row assumes a student pays $60 an hour for one session a week over 16 weeks ($960 in fees) and that you keep 40% after paying tutors and costs. Those are my numbers; swap in yours. Base lands below the $77.48 education cost per lead reported by Webtonic, so a Tough-to-Base result is more realistic for a brand-new account than Strong.

Two formulas worth keeping:

- **Break-even click price.** Maximum CPC = margin per student x enquiry rate x close rate. With the Base inputs: $384 x 0.08 x 0.25 = $7.68. Above that, you lose money on average.
- **The lever that moves most.** Going from a 15% to a 35% close rate cuts cost per student by more than half without touching the ad account. That is a speed-of-reply and intro-session problem.

Also check retention. A student who leaves after four weeks gives you a quarter of the 16-week value in the example.

## Do This Today

1. Calculate your margin per student: weekly fee x average weeks a student stays, minus tutor pay and costs. This is your ceiling for ad cost.
2. Put your five main keywords into Keyword Planner for your city and write down the CPC range.
3. Use the break-even formula to see the highest click price you can afford.
4. Build one Search campaign for one subject and audience, with phrase and exact match, the negative list above and a landing page with a visible phone number and intro-session form. Set up call and form conversions before launch.
5. Create a one-sheet log of every enquiry and a same-day reply rule. Review search terms every week.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the minimum budget for tutoring Google Ads?**
A: There is no official minimum we could verify, but tiny budgets gather little data. Take the cost-per-student math above and make sure your budget buys at least a few dozen clicks per ad group per month. At an assumed $5 click, $800 buys about 160.

**Q: How much does a click cost for tutoring keywords?**
A: It varies by subject and city. A 2020 tutor-written post reports $2 to $5 for English and over $10 for ACT and SAT. Education overall had a $4.81 median CPC in the WordStream-based 2026 data Webtonic reports. Check Keyword Planner for your own market.

**Q: Why am I getting clicks but no calls?**
A: Usually the keywords are too broad or the landing page makes contact hard. Read the search terms report, add negatives and test your page on a phone.

**Q: Google Ads or Facebook ads?**
A: Google reaches parents already searching; Facebook and Instagram can reach parents who have not started looking yet. See our [Meta ads service](/services/meta-ads). Many tutors start with Google.

**Q: Do I need a website?**
A: A simple landing page with subjects, reviews and a clear way to contact you is enough to start. Our [website service](/services/website) covers the build.
