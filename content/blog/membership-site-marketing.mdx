A paid membership turns repeat customers into predictable monthly revenue. To market one, sell what members get that others cannot, run the fee math before you launch, and track churn monthly. The worked example below shows a $29 plan costing about $1.34 per member per month in Stripe fees, and a 5% monthly churn rate that implies an average 20-month membership. You do not need a separate website or app to start.

## What counts as a membership for a local business?

For a local business it is a recurring paid plan that gives access, priority or a product bundle. Examples include unlimited classes at a studio, a monthly coffee plan or a grooming club. A "membership site" is only the page where people join and manage the plan. A simple sign-up page plus a payment link is enough at the start.

This is different from a free loyalty scheme. Loyalty rewards spending, while a membership asks for a payment up front. For loyalty basics, see [coffee shop loyalty program ideas without an app](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app).

## What does it cost to collect member payments?

Fees are small but not zero. According to [Stripe's pricing page](https://stripe.com/billing/pricing), Billing costs 0.7% of Billing volume on pay-as-you-go, card payments cost 2.9% plus 30 cents per successful charge, and ACH direct debit costs 0.8% capped at $5.

Worked example (assumption): a $29 monthly plan paid by card.

- Card fee: 2.9% of $29 is $0.84, plus $0.30, so $1.14.
- Billing fee: 0.7% of $29 is $0.20.
- Total: about $1.34 per member per month, or 4.6% of the price.

Square also offers a loyalty tool with a 30-day free trial, per its help pages, but check the current pricing for your country before choosing.

## How do you price and test a membership before building?

Use the survey method: ask 50 repeat customers whether they would pay for a specific bundle at a specific price. If fewer than a fifth say a definite yes, change the bundle before building anything. This threshold is a rule of thumb, not an industry standard.

Then check break-even. If the plan costs $29 and a regular visit is worth $8 in margin, the member must visit four times a month before the plan beats paying per visit. Price so that average usage is comfortably above break-even for you and below the value for the customer.

## Which membership model fits your business?

- **Unlimited access:** best for classes and services with spare capacity.
- **Tiered:** a basic and a premium level, such as priority booking.
- **Prepaid bundle:** a set number of visits, useful where capacity is tight.
- **Perk-based:** early access, member-only items, free add-ons.

Frame it around access, not a discount. A plan sold as "15% off" trains customers to wait for a sale, while "priority booking and a monthly member item" sells something non-members cannot get. ## How do you market a membership to existing customers?

Existing customers convert best because they already know you. A 5-step launch:

1. **Offer it in person.** Add a counter sign and a short staff script.
2. **Email your own list.** Send one announcement and one reminder, with an honest unsubscribe link and your postal address as required by [CAN-SPAM](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).
3. **Post it.** Share the offer on Instagram and Facebook, with a link to the join page.
4. **Add a founding-member perk.** A small, time-limited bonus gives people a reason to decide this week.
5. **Track sign-ups by source.** Use a code per channel.

## How do you reduce member churn?

Measure it first. Monthly churn is members lost in the month divided by members at the start. Worked example (assumption): 100 members and 5 cancellations is 5% churn, so the average membership lasts 1 divided by 0.05, or 20 months. At $29 a month, that is about $580 of revenue per member before fees.

Ways to lower it:

- Contact members who have not used the plan in 30 days.
- Offer a pause instead of a cancel.
- Send a short welcome sequence in the first two weeks.
- Review cancellation reasons monthly.

## What are the cancellation and billing rules?

Make cancelling easy. The FTC's "click to cancel" rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on July 8, 2025, but [Cooley's legal analysis](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-07-11-click-to-cancel-just-got-cancelled-eighth-circuit-vacates-entirety-of-ftcs-negative-option-rule) notes that the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA) still requires clear disclosure of automatic renewal terms before billing details are collected, express consent and a simple way to cancel. State auto-renewal laws may add rules. This is general information, not legal advice, so check your state.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a separate membership website or app?**

No. A payment link or booking-tool plan plus a simple page is enough to test demand.

**Q: How many members do I need to launch?**

There is no fixed number. A practical test is to survey about 50 repeat customers and see whether at least a fifth would definitely buy.

**Q: What fees should I expect?**

On Stripe, about 4.6% of a $29 plan, per the worked example above. Fees vary by processor and country.

**Q: How do I stop a membership from becoming a discount club?**

Sell access and priority, not savings.

**Q: What if I sign up only a handful of members?**

Treat it as test data. Ask those members why they joined and what nearly stopped them, then adjust the offer.
