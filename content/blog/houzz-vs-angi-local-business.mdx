Angi charges a fee for each lead it matches to you, while Houzz shows free profiles by relevance and content quality and sells paid placements ranked partly by what you pay. Neither vendor publishes a price list in the pages we opened, so a dollar-for-dollar cost comparison is not possible here. This guide compares the rules, then shows how to work out your own cost per closed job.

It is written for remodelers and design-build contractors in the US. The vendor pages were read on 1 November 2026. Where a figure is illustrative it is labelled as an assumption.

## How Does Angi Charge for Leads?

Angi's service professional FAQ says pros pay "a nominal fee for each lead you match to" and that "different sized jobs have different Lead Fees." It gives no dollar amounts. The same page says customers are connected with multiple Angi Leads professionals, that there is no guarantee on volume, and that you can turn the service on or off by adjusting your profile.

This is a lead marketplace. You pay when a lead is matched, whether or not you win the job, and other pros may receive the same request. Our [Angi vs Thumbtack comparison](/blog/angi-vs-thumbtack-for-local-business) covers how that compares with another marketplace.

## What Is Angi's Lead Credit Policy?

You must request a credit within 30 days of the charge. Credits are issued as store credit applied to future charges, and they expire six months after issue ([Angi Leads Pro](https://pro.homeadvisor.com/terms/lead-credit-guidelines/)).

Qualifying cases include a duplicate request, a competitor testing the system, wrong or disconnected contact details, and a wrong zip code or job type that is not in your profile. A wrong job type that is in your profile can earn partial credit.

Not covered: the customer does not call back, found someone else, changed their mind, is only shopping, or you did not win the job. Budget for those lost leads, because they are part of the price.

## How Does Houzz Rank and Charge Pros?

Houzz describes two kinds of placement. Organic listings are ordered by category, relevancy (such as distance and how well your description matches the search) and content quality, such as the number of reviews and photos saved to ideabooks. Paid placements are ordered by category, relevancy and customer status, which reflects the price a professional paid for advertising. Paid listings are marked "sponsored" ([Houzz Pro Help](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-help/r/pros-shown-on-houzz)).

Houzz also says its matching tool considers the user's budget and location, a pro's advertising status and responsiveness, and that advertising price factors into prioritization. Emails feature local pros who bought advertising. We could not open a Houzz page with ad prices, so none are quoted here. For setup, see [Houzz ads for contractors](/blog/houzz-ads-home-services-contractors).

## Which Platform Fits Which Contractor?

Houzz is organised around photos, reviews and ideabooks, so a remodeler with strong project photography has material to rank on for free. Angi matches by job type and zip code, so it suits a business that wants a stream of requests and has staff to answer fast. Neither claim comes from a vendor guarantee, so test before committing.

Price structure matters more than reputation. A per-lead model puts the risk of unqualified leads on you, while an advertising model puts the risk of weak placement on you. Check each contract for minimum terms and cancellation before you pay.

## How Do You Work Out Your Cost per Closed Job?

Use one formula for both: total spend divided by jobs you closed. All inputs below are **assumptions**, not platform data.

| Input | Platform A (lead fee) | Platform B (advertising) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly spend | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Leads or inquiries received | 20 | 8 |
| Share you reach and quote | 60% | 75% |
| Quotes | 12 | 6 |
| Close rate | 15% | 25% |
| Jobs closed | 1.8 | 1.5 |
| Cost per closed job | about $556 | about $667 |

A cheaper lead does not win by default. In this example the platform with more leads has the lower cost per job, but a small change in close rate flips the result. Track each lead from source to signed contract, then compare. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows how to plan a test.

## FAQ

### Which is cheaper, Houzz or Angi?
Unknown. Neither vendor page we read lists prices. Angi charges per matched lead and sets fees by job size. Houzz paid placement depends on what you pay. Compare cost per closed job from your own data.

### Can I get a refund for a bad Angi lead?
You can request store credit within 30 days for cases such as duplicates, wrong contact details, or a competitor testing the system. Customers who change their mind or choose someone else do not qualify.

### Does Houzz let me appear for free?
Yes, there is an organic listing ordered by relevancy and content quality such as reviews and photos. Paid placements are marked sponsored.

### Do Angi leads go to more than one contractor?
The FAQ says the customer is connected with multiple Angi Leads professionals for each request.

### Should I use both?
You can test both with limited budgets for 60 days and compare closed jobs. Do not judge on a handful of leads.
