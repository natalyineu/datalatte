The best Google Ads keywords for a barbershop are local, service-specific searches such as "barber near me", "men's haircut [city]", "fade haircut [city]", "beard trim near me" and "kids haircut [city]". They capture people who are ready to book. Use phrase and exact match, add negative keywords for jobs, schools and supplies, and review your Search Terms report every week.

Click costs and search volumes differ by city and season. Use Google Keyword Planner and your own account data to check real numbers for your area instead of relying on averages from articles.

## Keyword types that tend to convert

Group your keywords into three simple buckets:

1. **Location-based:** "barber near me", "barber [city]", "barbershop [neighbourhood]"
2. **Service-specific:** "fade haircut [city]", "beard trim near me", "hot towel shave", "kids haircut near me"
3. **Long-tail:** "men's haircut for thinning hair", "best barber in [city] with reviews", "walk-in barber open Sunday"

Match types: use phrase match for broader local terms like "barber near me" and exact match for your highest-value service terms. Avoid pure broad match for "barber" until you have conversion data, because it can trigger searches for barber schools, supplies and jobs.

## Location-based keywords

Most people who book a barber search with a place in mind, either "near me" or a city or neighbourhood. Always include these.

| Keyword type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Near me | "barbershop near me", "barber open now near me" |
| City plus service | "barber in [city]", "men's haircut [city]" |
| Neighbourhood | "barbershop [neighbourhood]", "fade [neighbourhood]" |

In dense cities, keep your location targeting tight (a few miles). In suburbs, a wider radius may be reasonable. Test both and compare cost per booking. Do not trust any one-size-fits-all radius.

## Service-specific keywords

Match your ads to what you actually do:

| Service | Keyword ideas |
|---|---|
| Haircuts | "men's haircut [city]", "fade haircut [city]", "taper fade near me" |
| Beards | "beard trim near me", "beard shaping [city]" |
| Shaves | "hot towel shave near me", "straight razor shave [city]" |
| Kids | "kids haircut near me", "first haircut [city]" |
| Premium | "luxury barber [city]", "premium barbershop [city]" |

Keyword Planner shows the search volume for each, so use it to pick the ones that matter in your city.

## Long-tail keywords

Phrases of four or more words usually have lower volume but clearer intent. Ideas:
- "cheap men's haircut near me" (check this fits your pricing before you bid)
- "best barber in [city]"
- "haircut for thinning hair men"
- "walk-in barber near me"

To find more, use Keyword Planner and Google's autocomplete (type "barber" plus your city and note the suggestions). You can also see what questions people ask with tools such as AnswerThePublic.

## Seasonal and event keywords

Barbershops often see busier periods around back-to-school, summer, weddings and the holidays. Examples:
- "back to school haircut [city]"
- "wedding groomsmen haircut [city]"
- "holiday barber gift card [city]"

Use Google Trends for your city to see when searches rise, and plan campaigns a few weeks ahead.

## Competitor keywords

You can bid on rivals' names, but proceed with care. Competitor clicks tend to be expensive and these searchers often want the other shop. A safer approach is to study what rivals advertise (search your city plus "barber" and read the ads) and write better, more specific ads for your own services. Read our guide to [Google Ads competitor keywords](/blog/google-ads-competitor-keywords) before bidding on names.

## Negative keywords every barbershop needs

Negative keywords stop you paying for the wrong clicks. Start with:

- **Training and jobs:** "barber school", "barber license", "barber jobs", "apprenticeship", "how to become a barber"
- **Supplies and DIY:** "clippers", "supplies", "wholesale", "barber chair", "DIY", "at home"
- **Business-side:** "franchise", "for sale", "booth rental" (unless you rent booths)
- **Services you do not offer:** "women's haircut", "perm", "hair colour", "extensions"
- **Wrong intent:** "free haircut" (unless you run models nights)

Review the Search Terms report weekly and add new negatives as irrelevant queries appear. See our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).

## Common mistakes to avoid

**1. Bidding on your own name without a plan.** Branded searches often cost little, and a brand campaign can protect you from rivals bidding on your name. But if your Google Business Profile and website already dominate results, test whether the brand campaign adds anything. Keep brand and non-brand campaigns separate so you can measure each. See [Google Ads brand campaigns](/blog/google-ads-brand-campaigns).

**2. Using broad match too early.** It can trigger searches for schools, supplies and jobs. Start with phrase and exact match.

**3. Skipping negative keywords.** Without them, wasted clicks add up quickly.

**4. Ignoring mobile.** Many "barber near me" searches happen on phones. Make sure your ads include a call button, your landing page loads quickly on mobile, and consider bid adjustments for devices once you have data.

**5. One campaign for every service.** Separate your main services (for example, haircuts, beards, kids) so ad text and landing pages match the search.

For more, read [Google Ads mistakes barbershops make](/blog/google-ads-mistakes-barbershops-make-and-how-to-fix-them).

## A simple account structure

- **Service campaigns:** one per main service group, with one or two ad groups each, 5 to 10 phrase or exact keywords per group, and two or three responsive search ads
- **Local campaign:** "barber near me" and city terms, with call and location assets
- **Optional brand campaign:** your own name, kept separate and low budget

Use dedicated landing pages or sections for each service rather than sending everything to your homepage, and make booking one tap away.

## Keyword research tools

- **Google Keyword Planner** (free in Google Ads): volume and suggested bids for your area
- **Google Trends** (free): seasonality
- **AnswerThePublic or autocomplete** (free options): questions and long-tail ideas
- **Your Search Terms report** (in your account): what people actually typed. This is the most valuable source once your ads run.

## FAQ

**What Google Ads keywords work best for barbershops?**
Location-specific and service-specific terms: "barber near me", "men's haircut [city]", "fade haircut [city]", "kids haircut near me" and "beard trim near me". They show clear intent to book.

**How much does it cost to run Google Ads for a barbershop?**
It depends on your city and competition. Check Keyword Planner for estimated click costs where you operate, then set a budget from your target cost per new client. Track bookings to see the real number.

**What negative keywords should barbershops use?**
At minimum: school, license, jobs, apprenticeship, supplies, clippers, wholesale, DIY, franchise and any service you do not offer. Add more from your Search Terms report each week.

**Should barbershops use broad match or exact match?**
Start with phrase and exact match. Consider broad match only after you have solid conversion tracking and a thorough negative list.

**Do Local Services Ads work for barbershops?**
Local Services Ads are only available for certain business categories and countries, and barbershops are often not eligible. Check Google's current list for your area.
