The best Google Ads keywords for a barbershop are local, service-specific searches: "barber near me", "men's haircut [city]", "fade haircut [city]", "beard trim near me" and "kids haircut [city]". They come from people ready to book. Start with phrase and exact match, add negative keywords for jobs, schools and supplies, and review your Search terms report every week.

Click costs and search volumes differ by city and season. Use Keyword Planner and your own account data to check real numbers for your area rather than trusting averages from articles. Keyword Planner shows estimated monthly searches, forecasts and average cost estimates, and needs a Google Ads account with billing set up ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243), checked Oct 2026).

## Keyword types that tend to convert

Group your keywords into three buckets:

1. **Location-based:** "barber near me", "barber [city]", "barbershop [neighbourhood]"
2. **Service-specific:** "fade haircut [city]", "beard trim near me", "hot towel shave", "kids haircut near me"
3. **Long-tail:** "men's haircut for thinning hair", "best barber in [city] with reviews", "walk-in barber open Sunday"

## Match types: start narrow

Google describes three positive match types ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529), checked Oct 2026):

- **Broad match** is the default and can show your ad on searches that are only related to the keyword.
- **Phrase match** shows ads on searches that include the meaning of your keyword. It reaches fewer searches than broad but more than exact.
- **Exact match** is the most restrictive and gives you the most control.

For a barbershop, use phrase match for local terms like "barber near me" and exact match for your best-converting service terms. Avoid broad match for "barber" until you have conversion data, because related searches can include barber schools, supplies and jobs.

## Location-based keywords

Most people who book a barber search with a place in mind, either "near me" or a city or neighbourhood.

| Keyword type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Near me | "barbershop near me", "barber open now near me" |
| City plus service | "barber in [city]", "men's haircut [city]" |
| Neighbourhood | "barbershop [neighbourhood]", "fade [neighbourhood]" |

In dense cities, keep location targeting tight, a few miles around the shop. In suburbs, a wider radius may make sense. Test both and compare cost per booking.

## Service-specific keywords

| Service | Keyword ideas |
|---|---|
| Haircuts | "men's haircut [city]", "fade haircut [city]", "taper fade near me" |
| Beards | "beard trim near me", "beard shaping [city]" |
| Shaves | "hot towel shave near me", "straight razor shave [city]" |
| Kids | "kids haircut near me", "first haircut [city]" |
| Premium | "luxury barber [city]", "premium barbershop [city]" |

Check each term's volume in Keyword Planner and bid first on the ones that match what you actually sell.

## Long-tail keywords

Phrases of four or more words usually have lower volume but clearer intent:
- "cheap men's haircut near me" (only if it fits your pricing)
- "best barber in [city]"
- "haircut for thinning hair men"
- "walk-in barber near me"

To find more, type "barber" plus your city into Google and note the autocomplete suggestions, or use a free tool such as AnswerThePublic.

## Seasonal and event keywords

Barbershops often see busier periods around back-to-school, summer, weddings and the holidays:
- "back to school haircut [city]"
- "wedding groomsmen haircut [city]"
- "holiday barber gift card [city]"

Check Google Trends for your city to see when searches rise, and launch campaigns a few weeks ahead.

## Competitor keywords

You can bid on rivals' names, but competitor clicks tend to be expensive and these searchers usually want the other shop. A safer approach is to read what rivals advertise (search your city plus "barber") and write more specific ads for your own services. Read our guide to [Google Ads competitor keywords](/blog/google-ads-competitor-keywords) before bidding on names.

## Negative keywords every barbershop needs

Negative keywords stop your ads showing on searches you exclude. Negative broad match, the default, blocks a search only when it contains all of your negative terms, so use negative phrase or exact match when you want tighter control ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972), checked Oct 2026). Start with:

- **Training and jobs:** "barber school", "barber license", "barber jobs", "apprenticeship", "how to become a barber"
- **Supplies and DIY:** "clippers", "supplies", "wholesale", "barber chair", "DIY", "at home"
- **Business-side:** "franchise", "for sale", "booth rental" (unless you rent booths)
- **Services you do not offer:** "women's haircut", "perm", "hair colour", "extensions"
- **Wrong intent:** "free haircut" (unless you run model nights)

The Search terms report lists the real searches that triggered your ads and lets you add irrelevant ones as negatives ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708), checked Oct 2026). Review it weekly. See also our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).

## Common mistakes to avoid

**1. Bidding on your own name without a plan.** Branded searches often cost little, and a brand campaign can stop rivals taking clicks on your name. If your Google Business Profile and website already dominate results, test whether the campaign adds anything. Keep brand and non-brand campaigns separate. See [Google Ads brand campaigns](/blog/google-ads-brand-campaigns).

**2. Using broad match too early.** Related searches can bring schools, supplies and jobs.

**3. Skipping negative keywords.** Wasted clicks add up quickly on a small budget.

**4. Ignoring mobile.** Many "barber near me" searches happen on phones. Add a call asset so people can phone you from the ad ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453991), checked Oct 2026), and make sure your landing page loads fast.

**5. One campaign for every service.** Separate your main services (haircuts, beards, kids) so ad text and landing pages match the search.

For more, read [Google Ads mistakes barbershops make](/blog/google-ads-mistakes-barbershops-make-and-how-to-fix-them).

## A simple account structure

- **Service campaigns:** one per main service group, with one or two ad groups each, 5 to 10 phrase or exact keywords per group, and two or three responsive search ads
- **Local campaign:** "barber near me" and city terms, with call and location assets
- **Optional brand campaign:** your own name, kept separate and low budget

Send each service to a dedicated landing page or section rather than your homepage, and make booking one tap away.

## FAQ

**Q: What Google Ads keywords work best for barbershops?**
A: Location-specific and service-specific terms such as "barber near me", "men's haircut [city]", "fade haircut [city]", "kids haircut near me" and "beard trim near me". They show clear intent to book.

**Q: How much does it cost to run Google Ads for a barbershop?**
A: It depends on your city and competition. Check Keyword Planner for estimated click costs where you operate, then set a budget from your target cost per new client and track bookings to see your real number.

**Q: What negative keywords should barbershops use?**
A: At minimum: school, license, jobs, apprenticeship, supplies, clippers, wholesale, DIY, franchise and any service you do not offer. Add more from your Search terms report each week.

**Q: Should barbershops use broad match or exact match?**
A: Start with phrase and exact match. Consider broad match only after you have reliable conversion tracking and a thorough negative list.

**Q: Do Local Services Ads work for barbershops?**
A: In the United States, "Barber Shop" is on Google's list of supported Local Services Ads business types, but availability varies by country and area, and Google is migrating Local Services Ads into Performance Max campaigns in stages ([Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841), [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585), checked Oct 2026). Check whether your location is eligible. Regular search campaigns and a strong Google Business Profile remain the usual starting points.
