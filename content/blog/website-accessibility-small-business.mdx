To make a small business website accessible, fix the failures that appear most often: low-contrast text, images without alt text, form fields without labels, empty links and empty buttons. WebAIM's 2026 analysis of one million home pages found detectable WCAG 2 failures on 95.9% of them, averaging 56.1 errors per page. Most of these fixes take minutes each.

WCAG 2.2 sets the contrast minimum for normal text at 4.5:1 (success criterion 1.4.3, Level AA). This guide is general information, not legal advice. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Are the Most Common Accessibility Problems?

WebAIM's analysis (conducted February 2026) lists the six most common failures by share of home pages. The same list is a good order for your own fixes.

| Failure | Share of home pages | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low-contrast text | 83.9% | Darken text or lighten the background until it reaches 4.5:1 |
| Images missing alt text | 53.1% | Describe informative images; leave decorative alt empty |
| Form inputs missing labels | 51% | Add a visible label tied to each field |
| Empty links | 46.3% | Add link text or an accessible name, such as a name for a social icon |
| Empty buttons | 30.6% | Give icon buttons a text label |
| Missing document language | 13.5% | Set the page language attribute |

These are failures that automated tools can detect. Automated scans cannot judge everything, such as whether alt text is meaningful, so a clean scan does not prove a page is fully accessible.

## How Do You Test Your Own Site?

Start with free checks. Run the WebAIM contrast checker on your text and button colours, and use a browser extension that scans the page. Then test by hand: tab through the page with the keyboard only, zoom to 200%, and read the page with a screen reader for five minutes.

Look for the points where a visitor could get stuck: a booking form, a menu, a pop-up you cannot close with the keyboard. These matter most for a local business, because the visitor is trying to book or call. Our guide to [website speed](/blog/website-speed-optimization-small-business) and [mobile-first design](/blog/mobile-optimization-local-business) cover related checks.

## Which Fixes Should You Make First?

Work in this order:

1. Text and buttons: fix contrast across the template once.
2. Forms: label every field and write error messages that say what to fix.
3. Images: add alt text to photos that carry meaning, such as a menu or a service result.
4. Links and buttons: replace "click here" with the destination, and name icon-only controls.
5. Keyboard: make sure focus is visible and nothing traps it.
6. Media: add captions to video you control.

Soft brand palettes are a frequent cause of contrast problems. Our [colour scheme guide](/blog/website-color-schemes-local-business) shows how to pick colours that stay readable.

## Does the ADA Require Your Website to Be Accessible?

The ADA.gov first-steps page for the web rule covers state and local government entities under Title II and requires WCAG 2.1 Level AA. The page says a later 2026 interim final rule extended the compliance dates, so check it for current ones. It does not say whether the rule covers private businesses, which fall under Title III, so ask a lawyer whether your business faces a legal duty or demand letters in your state.

The legal question is separate from the practical one. Accessible pages work better for older visitors, people on phones in glare and anyone with a temporary injury.

## Do Accessibility Overlays Solve It?

Be careful. In April 2025 the FTC approved a final order requiring accessiBe to pay $1 million. The FTC alleged that the company's claims that its accessWidget made websites WCAG-compliant were false, misleading or unsubstantiated, and that it presented paid reviews as independent. The order bars such claims without evidence. It applies to that company, not to every overlay, but it shows why a one-line script is not proof of compliance.

## FAQ

### What WCAG level should a small business aim for?

Level AA is the usual target. The ADA web rule for public entities names WCAG 2.1 Level AA, and WCAG 2.2 adds further criteria.

### Is a free scanner enough?

No. Automated tools detect only some problems. Add manual keyboard and screen-reader checks.

### Do I need to fix every PDF and menu?

Publish menus as real text where you can, so they are readable by screen readers. See the [FAQ page guide](/blog/website-faq-page-local-business) for how to answer common questions in text.

### Can I just install an accessibility widget?

Not as proof of compliance. See the FTC action above.

### How does accessibility help SEO?

Clear headings, alt text and link text help machines read the page. No study was verified for ranking impact, so treat it as a likely benefit, not a promise.
