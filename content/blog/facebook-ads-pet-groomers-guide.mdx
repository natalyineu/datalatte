Say you have 120 clients who have not booked in months, and Meta can match half of them, so 60 people. If a $60 month of ads rebooks 6 of them, that is $10 a visit. Under the assumptions below, winning a brand-new client costs several times more. Those figures are illustrations, not results, and we will lay out the assumptions below. The point is where most groomers should point their first Facebook budget: at the people who already know and trust them.

This guide is for salon and mobile groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It does not repeat the general Meta Ads setup in our [Meta Ads for pet groomers guide](/blog/meta-ads-for-pet-groomers) or the broader [Facebook ads what-works guide](/blog/facebook-ads-for-pet-groomers-what-works-2026). It covers one job: using Facebook and Instagram ads to bring back clients who have drifted, keep puppy clients on a schedule, and use the same creative to attract neighbours who look like your best customers.

## Why Rebooking Is the Right First Job

Grooming demand is built into the dog's coat. The American Kennel Club says how often a dog needs professional grooming depends on breed, coat length and type, how much time it spends outside getting dirty, the climate, and the style you ask for, with visits ranging from every six weeks to three times a year. Puppies are different: PetMD says to aim for a first groomer visit by 12 to 14 weeks of age if boosters are up to date, and that more frequent trips, every two to three weeks, may help young puppies under six months build positive routines.

Two things follow for your ads:

- Your clients have a natural next-visit date, which you already know from your booking software. A cold stranger does not.
- A puppy client who comes once and disappears is a lost chain of future visits. A short sequence of reminders and a friendly offer in the first six months can catch it.

Facebook is not the only way to reach them. For clients with a phone number, a text is often better; see [SMS marketing for pet groomers](/blog/sms-marketing-for-pet-groomers) and our [client retention guide](/blog/how-to-retain-pet-grooming-clients-long-term). Ads fill the gap for clients who have not answered a text or whom you have no consent to text.

## Step 1: Build Your Client List Segments

Export your clients from your booking software with name, email, phone and last visit date. Then sort them into four groups:

| Segment | Who | What the ad says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Due soon | Last visit around their usual gap | Gentle reminder, easy booking link |
| Overdue | Past their usual gap by 4 weeks or more | Offer to restart, availability |
| Lapsed | No visit in 6 months or more | "We would love to see them again", a bigger incentive |
| Puppies | First visit, now under 6 months old | A simple plan for the next visits |

Do not advertise to anyone who booked in the last couple of weeks. It wastes budget and annoys people.

**Upload each group as a separate customer list audience.** Meta's marketing API documentation says customer file audiences are built from data such as emails, phone numbers and names, that the data must be hashed (SHA256, lowercase) before upload through the API, and that changes can take up to 24 hours to take effect. If you upload through Ads Manager, follow its on-screen instructions. The same documentation says the audience must be free of flags and that you must accept Meta's Custom Audience Terms. Not every client will match a Meta account. Third-party guides put typical match rates at 30 to 70%, but Meta publishes no benchmark, so our math below uses 50% as an assumption.

**Check your permission.** Only upload people who gave you their details as customers, and check the privacy rules where you operate (for example UK GDPR, Canada's PIPEDA or Australia's Privacy Act). We are not lawyers; ask yours if unsure.

## Step 2: Write an Offer That Fits the Segment

Skip generic discounts. A lapsed client has usually not left because of price; their schedule slipped. The offer should make rebooking easy.

- **Due soon:** "Mochi's coat is due for a tidy. Same-week slots open Tuesday and Thursday." No discount needed.
- **Overdue:** "A coat that goes too long between groomings can mat. Rebook this month and we will do a free nail trim with any full groom." (Nail trim is an example; use whatever costs you little and helps the dog.)
- **Lapsed:** "It has been a while. Book a refresh groom this month and get 10% off." The 10% is an example; test what your margin allows.
- **Puppies:** "Puppy's first months matter. Book a short, calm visit every few weeks while they learn that grooming is nothing to fear."

**Mind Meta's privacy rules in your wording.** Meta's advertising standards say ads must not imply that the advertiser is aware of someone's personally identifiable information, such as their name. A pet's name is not obviously the owner's name, but we found no Meta ruling on it, so test one version with the pet's name and one with "your dog", and use whichever gets approved. Do not write copy that implies you know personal facts about the viewer.

## Step 3: Creative That Makes a Client Rebook

People rebook when they remember the good result and the easy booking. Use real photos, not stock.

1. **The regular, transformed.** Before and after of a client's dog (with the owner's written OK), captioned with the service and the date range you have openings.
2. **A 15-second reel of the reveal:** the dog coming out of the dryer, the owner's reaction at pickup. Authentic phone video works.
3. **A seasonal nudge:** shedding season, summer coat, wet-weather mud, the holiday rush. Seasonal timing gives a reason to book now.
4. **A review in a graphic:** a real review, with the owner's permission, to remind lapsed clients why they chose you.
5. **A "next openings" post:** a screenshot or simple graphic showing 3 or 4 actual open slots. Urgency based on real capacity, not invented scarcity.

Send each ad to your booking page with one clear button. If your booking tool supports a link with a pre-selected service, use it. Keep your Facebook page consistent with the ad; our [Facebook page optimisation guide](/blog/pet-groomer-facebook-page-optimization) covers the basics.

## Step 4: Add a Small Cold Audience Later

Once the rebooking campaign works, you can ask Meta to find neighbours who resemble your clients. Meta's marketing API documentation on lookalikes says the source audience must have at least 100 members, that a country is required, and that the audience size ratio can be set from 1% to 20%. If you have fewer than 100 matched clients, skip this step for now. Many solo groomers will not have enough in their first year, and a tightly targeted local radius with a new-client offer is simpler. If you do build one, use a small ratio first, and keep location set to where you will really drive. ## The Math

All numbers are **assumptions**, except where a benchmark is named. Neither segment's figures are guaranteed, and the point is how to compare, so replace them with your own after one month.

| | Win-back (lapsed list) | New-client ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $60 | $150 |
| Audience | 120 lapsed clients x 50% matched = 60 people (assumption) | Local radius, new people |
| Cost per lead | n/a | $38.09 (LocaliQ Personal Services proxy) |
| Leads or rebookings | 6 rebookings (10% of 60, assumption) | 3.9 leads |
| Booked rate (assumption) | n/a | 50% |
| Visits booked | 6 | 2.0 |
| Cost per visit or new client | $10 | $77 |
| Average groom price (assumption) | $85 | $85 |
| Revenue from first visit | $510 | $168 |

For the new-client column, $150 / $38.09 = 3.9 leads, halved is about 2 new clients, so about $77 each. A new client may return for more visits, which is the real prize, so look at first-year value. But the lapsed list costs about a seventh as much per booked visit under these assumptions, because the audience already knows you.

**Test whether the ads caused the bookings.** Many lapsed clients would have come back anyway. Split a lapsed segment in half at random, advertise to one half only, and compare rebooking after 30 days. If both halves rebook at the same rate, the ads are not what brought them back. It is a cheap experiment, and it tells you more than a click count.

The usual catch: audiences of 60 people get tired fast. Rotate creative every two or three weeks, cap spend, and remove anyone who has rebooked. Expect small totals, and do not be tempted to spread a $60 budget thin across many ad sets.

## What to Measure

- **Rebookings:** count clients from the list who book within 30 days of first seeing an ad, using your booking software's records, not Meta's report.
- **Cost per rebooked visit:** monthly spend divided by that count.
- **Reply quality:** for any lead form or message, log whether it became a booking.
- **Frequency:** if the same 60 people see the ad many times in a week, rotate the creative.

Do not judge by reach or likes. A reel can get thousands of views and zero bookings.

## Do This Today

1. Export your clients with last visit dates and split them into due soon, overdue, lapsed and puppies.
2. Check how many people each segment has, and confirm you have permission to use their details in ads.
3. Pick one segment (start with overdue or lapsed), write one offer and make two ads with real photos or a short reel.
4. Upload the segment as a customer list audience, set a small daily budget and send ads to your booking page.
5. Split the list in half if it has 40 or more people, advertise to one half, and mark a calendar date 30 days out to compare rebookings.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a pet groomer spend on Facebook ads for rebooking?**
A: There is no official figure. The math above uses $60 a month as an assumption for a list of about 60 matched people. Start low, because a small audience cannot absorb more than a few dollars a day without the same people seeing the ad over and over.

**Q: Do I need a pixel for this?**
A: Not for a customer list campaign. You need the pixel only if you want to show ads to website visitors or measure on-site bookings; then follow the [pixel setup guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-setup-guide).

**Q: What if I only have 40 clients?**
A: A customer list audience can still work for rebooking, but lookalike audiences need at least 100 source members according to Meta's documentation. With a small list, use text and email first and keep ads to a local radius with one clear offer.

**Q: Is it okay to put a client's dog in my ads?**
A: Ask for permission first, in writing if you can. Use photos you took yourself, and avoid showing the owner or any personal details.

**Q: Facebook or Instagram?**
A: Run both placements first and look at the results by placement after a month. Our general guidance on Instagram creative is in the [Instagram ads guide for pet groomers](/blog/instagram-ads-for-pet-groomers-guide).
