A groom repeats roughly every 6 weeks for many breeds, so the business problem is rarely awareness. It is the client who drifted to a nearer salon, went overdue during a busy season, or forgot. Ads that solve forgetting outperform ads that introduce the salon.

## Which audience should a groomer advertise to first?

Upload the lapsed list first: clients whose last groom passed the usual interval by 30 days or more. Custom audiences from the booking system reach people who already trust the handling, so the ad only needs a reason and a slot. Data protection rules still apply; upload only customers with a lawful marketing basis.

Cold targeting comes second, inside the distance clients actually drive. Pull recent bookings and map them before choosing a radius; mobile groomers draw differently from fixed salons. [Meta ads for hair salons](/blog/hair-salon-meta-ads) uses the same map-first method for a neighbouring trade.

Website visitors form the third audience. Anyone who viewed prices or the booking page and left is warm. [Instagram retargeting](/blog/instagram-retargeting-how-to-re-engage-website-visitors) covers the pixel setup both salon and groomer campaigns depend on.

## What offer brings lapsed grooming clients back?

Specificity beats discounting: a named groomer, the dog's usual service if the system supports it, and two concrete appointment windows. A generic percentage off invites price comparison; a concrete slot invites a yes.

Protect the price list where possible. A complimentary add-on, such as a de-shedding or nail service, gives lapsed clients a reason without retraining regulars to wait for sales. Reserve true discounts for clients absent 6 months or longer.

**Assumption example.** Assume a salon uploads 400 lapsed clients and 3% rebook from a $150 monthly campaign and follow-up sequence. That is 12 grooms at an assumed $85 average, or $1,020 against $150 of ads plus reminder time. The rates are assumptions; replace them with the booking system's real return count after one cycle.

## What creative works for grooming ads?

Before-and-after photos of real dogs, with the owner's permission, outperform any stock image because coat result is the product. Short video of a calm dog during the groom answers the anxiety question every first-time client asks silently.

Caption with the service, starting price and suburb. Grooming searches are practical; hiding the price behind a message thread costs the busiest clients, who book the first clear option. Seasonal creative (summer clips, holiday bookings) should follow the interval calendar, not run unchanged all year.

The retention system behind the ads matters more than any single campaign. [Salon rebooking strategy](/blog/salon-rebooking-strategy-that-gets-clients-back) lays out the chair-side, 48-hour and interval touches a grooming business can copy directly.

## FAQ

### How much should a pet groomer spend on Facebook ads?

A readable test runs $5 to $15 a day for two to four weeks inside the real travel radius, on November 2026 platform practice. Point it at the lapsed list before cold audiences. Judge on rebooked grooms from the booking system, not on reach or profile visits, neither of which pays rent.

### Do Facebook ads work for mobile groomers?

Yes, with tighter geography. Mobile routes depend on clustering bookings by area and day, so targeting should follow the route map rather than a circle around a home base. Creative naming the suburbs served this week converts better than a general brand message, because the offer is really a route slot.

### Should groomers discount first appointments?

Prefer an add-on over a percentage for lapsed clients, and keep first-appointment discounts small and rare. Grooming capacity is the constraint; filling the book with discounted grooms displaces full-price regulars. If the book has genuine gaps, fill a named weekday rather than discounting the whole week.

### How often should grooming ads run?

Continuously at low spend beats bursts. Dogs go overdue every week, so the lapsed audience refreshes constantly. A small always-on campaign to recent lapsed clients, refreshed monthly with new photos, usually outperforms a quarterly big push that arrives after clients have rebooked elsewhere.
