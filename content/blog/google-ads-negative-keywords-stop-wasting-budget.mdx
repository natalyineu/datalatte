## What Is a Negative Keyword?

A negative keyword excludes a search term from your campaigns. Google's help page puts it as "Negative keywords let you exclude search terms from your campaigns." A hair salon that sells services can add "jobs" or "course" so the ad does not appear for people looking for work or training.

Negatives work on the search term, not on your keyword. If your keyword is "hair salon" and someone searches "hair salon jobs", the negative "jobs" stops the ad for that search. The Search terms report shows what people typed, which is where most negatives come from.

## How Do the Three Match Types Differ?

Google describes negative match types for Search campaigns as follows. Broad is the default.

| Match type | Ad is blocked when | Example negative | Blocked search | Not blocked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Broad | The search contains all the negative terms, in any order | cheap haircut | haircut cheap near me | cheap salon |
| Phrase | The search contains the terms in the same order, extra words allowed | cheap haircut | best cheap haircut near me | haircut cheap |
| Exact | The search is exactly those terms, with no extra words | cheap haircut | cheap haircut | best cheap haircut |

The examples apply Google's rules to invented search terms. Broad negatives surprise people: the ad can still show if the search contains only some of the negative terms. Use phrase or exact when you want a tighter block, and exact when you only want to remove one specific query.

## Do Negatives Catch Plurals and Synonyms?

No. Google states that negative keywords do not match close variants or other expansions, so an ad can still show for near-variations of a negative term. Singular and plural forms and synonyms need their own entries.

This is the most common reason a "blocked" term keeps appearing in reports. Add "job", "jobs" and "hiring" separately. Also add misspellings that show up in the Search terms report.

## Where Do Negatives Apply?

Account-level negatives apply to all eligible Search and Shopping inventory in relevant campaign types, according to Google. For Display and Video the rules differ: negatives can block a topic, some placements may still contain the excluded terms, and Google counts a maximum of 1,000 negative keywords at account level.

Performance Max is separate. Google's Performance Max page lists negative keywords among the campaign-level controls for blocking unwanted queries. The negatives page links to a dedicated article for that campaign type, so follow those instructions there. If you run both, see our [Performance Max step-by-step guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-performance-max-campaigns-step-by-step).

Google also warns that too many negatives can reduce how many customers your ads reach. Block clear waste, not every doubtful word.

## Which Negatives Should a Local Business Start With?

Start from what your own Search terms report shows. Categories that commonly appear for local service businesses:

- Job seekers: jobs, hiring, salary, careers
- Training and DIY: course, school, tutorial, how to
- Things you do not sell: free, wholesale, rental, if they do not apply
- Other places: cities and neighbourhoods outside your service area

These are suggestions, not a universal list. A word that wastes money for one business can be your best seller for another, so check each term against your own offers before blocking it. ## Worked Example: Estimating Your Own Waste

All numbers are **assumptions** for illustration, not benchmarks.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $900 |
| Cost per click | $4.00 |
| Clicks | 225 |
| Clicks from searches you would never serve (jobs, courses, wrong city) | 18% |
| Wasted clicks | about 40 |
| Wasted spend | about $162 |

In this example, a first-week review of the Search terms report finds 18% of clicks are junk. Blocking them returns about $162 a month to searches that can convert. If your report shows 3% waste, negatives are a small job. If it shows 30%, they are the first thing to fix. Count it from your own report rather than trusting a headline number. ## How Do You Keep the List Healthy?

Review the report weekly for the first month, then every two weeks. For each new negative, note the date and the search it came from, so you can undo it if conversions drop. Check that a new negative does not block a converting keyword: filter your converting search terms and compare them with the words you plan to add.

Keep one shared list for universal blocks such as jobs and courses, and campaign lists for specific ones. See our [call-only campaigns guide for service businesses](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns-service-businesses) for a campaign type where junk calls are a common problem.

## FAQ

### What are negative keywords in Google Ads?
They are terms that stop your ads from showing for matching searches. Google describes them as a way to exclude search terms from your campaigns.

### Do negative keywords match plurals and misspellings?
No. Google says negatives do not match close variants, so add plurals, synonyms and misspellings yourself.

### What is the difference between broad, phrase and exact negatives?
Broad blocks searches containing all negative terms in any order. Phrase blocks them in the same order. Exact blocks only the exact search.

### How many negative keywords can I add?
Google states a maximum of 1,000 negatives at account level for Display and Video. Its negatives page gives no limit for Search or Shopping, and it warns that too many can limit reach.

### How much budget do negative keywords save?
No source publishes a reliable figure. Calculate waste from your own Search terms report, as in the example above.
