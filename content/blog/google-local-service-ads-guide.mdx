**What are Google Local Service Ads, and how do you set them up?** Google Local Service Ads (LSAs) are pay-per-lead ads that appear at the top of Google search results for certain local service categories, such as plumbers, electricians, cleaners and locksmiths. You apply through the Local Services Ads platform, pass Google's verification (business, background and licensing checks, depending on the category), set a weekly budget and then pay for qualifying leads such as calls and messages instead of paying for clicks.

Eligibility, verification steps and pricing differ by country, city and service category, and Google changes them periodically, so always check the current requirements in your own Local Services Ads account. Note that many businesses we write about, such as cafes, hair salons and fitness studios, are not in LSA categories, and for them standard Google Ads and a strong Google Business Profile are the main paid options.

## How are Local Service Ads different from regular Google Ads?

| | Local Service Ads | Standard Google Ads (Search) |
|---|---|---|
| Where they appear | Top of results, in a separate ad unit with a badge | Text ads in the search results |
| You pay for | Qualifying leads (calls, messages, bookings) | Clicks |
| Keywords | You pick job types and service areas, not keywords | You choose keywords and match types |
| Verification | Required, with a badge such as Google Guaranteed or Google Screened where available | Not required |
| Reviews | Prominent and influence ranking | Shown only in extensions |
| Availability | Limited to supported categories and regions | Any business |

Because LSAs rank in part on reviews, response speed and profile completeness, a healthy [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) and review pipeline helps you.

## Step 1: Check eligibility

Confirm that your business category and location are supported by Local Service Ads. Visit Google's Local Services Ads site and enter your business type and postal code. If your category is not listed, use standard [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) instead.

## Step 2: Create your account and business profile

1. Sign in with a Google account and start the sign-up flow on the Local Services Ads platform.
2. Enter your business name, address or service area, phone number and website.
3. Choose your service categories and the job types you want leads for.
4. Add your hours, a description and photos.

## Step 3: Complete verification

Google verifies LSA advertisers before ads can run. Depending on your category, this can include business registration, licensing, insurance and background checks of owners or technicians. Have your documents ready and make sure details match exactly, because mismatches are a common cause of delays. Approval times vary, so plan for it to take some days to a few weeks.

## Step 4: Set your service area and budget

- **Service area:** Choose the areas you can genuinely serve. Declining leads outside your area wastes money, so keep it realistic.
- **Budget:** You set a weekly budget based on how many leads you want. Google can show different minimums and recommendations by category and market, so use the figures in your account.
- **Bidding:** You can typically choose between maximizing leads and setting a manual bid, depending on availability.

## Step 5: Manage leads, reviews and disputes

LSAs are only as good as your lead handling:

- **Answer every call and message quickly.** Response time can affect ranking.
- **Mark lead outcomes** (booked, job type, not a fit) in your dashboard, which helps Google improve lead quality for your account.
- **Dispute invalid leads** such as spam, wrong service or outside your area within Google's stated window. Not all disputes are approved.
- **Ask every happy customer for a review.** Review count and rating are a major factor in how your ad looks and ranks.

<Callout type="tip">Pay for performance only works if you can answer the phone. If you miss calls during business hours, set up a callback process or an after-hours message before you start.</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">Do not run LSAs without checking that the leads match what you can deliver. Marking lead quality accurately and reviewing your service area monthly keeps cost per real job down.</Callout>

## How much do Local Service Ads cost?

Cost per lead varies widely by category, city and competition, so there is no single number to plan around. Roughly, higher-value services in competitive metros cost more per lead than lower-ticket jobs in smaller markets. A practical way to evaluate: take your average job value and close rate, calculate what you can afford to pay per lead, then compare it to the cost per lead you see in your account over a few weeks.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Starting before you can answer calls or messages promptly
- Setting a service area wider than you can serve
- Ignoring review generation
- Never marking lead outcomes or disputing bad leads
- Judging results too early; give it several weeks of data

## FAQ

**Q: Can I run Local Service Ads for a business that operates from my home?**
A: Often yes, because you do not need a storefront, but you must pass address and business verification. Customers see your service area rather than your home address, depending on your settings.

**Q: What happens if a lead is outside my service area?**
A: You can dispute it within Google's process, but approvals are not guaranteed. If this happens often, tighten your service area.

**Q: Can I run LSAs and regular Google Ads at the same time?**
A: Yes. They are separate products and can run together. Many businesses start with LSAs for core job types and add standard Search campaigns for broader keywords. See our guide on [whether LSAs are worth it for local businesses](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business).

**Q: How long does approval take?**
A: It depends on your category and how complete your documents are. Plan for anywhere from days to a few weeks, and double-check licenses and insurance details before submitting.

**Q: Is there a minimum budget?**
A: Google may show recommended or minimum weekly budgets in your account by market and category. Check there rather than relying on a general figure, and make sure your budget is large enough to generate enough leads to learn from.
