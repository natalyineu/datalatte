Google Local Service Ads (LSAs) are pay-per-lead ads that appear at the top of Google for eligible local service businesses. You apply in the Local Services Ads platform, pass Google's verification checks, set a budget, and pay for qualifying leads such as calls, messages and bookings. [Google states](https://business.google.com/us/ad-solutions/local-service-ads/) that with LSAs you only pay for customers, not clicks. Eligibility, verification and pricing differ by category and city, so the figures in your own account are the ones that matter.

## Who qualifies for Local Service Ads?

Eligibility depends on whether your business category and location are supported. Not every local business qualifies. Cafés, hair salons and fitness studios, for example, may fall outside LSA categories, and for them standard Google Ads and a complete Google Business Profile are the main paid options. Check the category list in the Local Services Ads sign-up flow before you spend time on paperwork.

Verification is required. Google's ad page describes a Google Verified badge, and depending on the category, checks can include business registration, licensing, insurance and background checks. Make sure business names and addresses match your documents exactly, because mismatches are a common cause of delays.

## How do you set up a Local Service Ads account?

Setup follows five steps. Start with the eligibility check, then build the profile, complete verification, define your service area and budget, and finally manage leads. Doing them in this order avoids the most common delays.

| Step | What to prepare | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Eligibility | Business category and postal code | Your category is listed |
| 2. Profile | Business name, address or service area, phone, website, hours, photos | Profile is complete |
| 3. Verification | Registration, licence, insurance, staff details | Google approves the account |
| 4. Area and budget | Realistic service area, weekly budget, bidding choice | Budget matches lead capacity |
| 5. Lead handling | Response process, outcome tagging, dispute routine | Every lead has an owner |

Approval times vary and can take days to a few weeks, so plan the launch date around that window.

## How much do Local Service Ads cost per lead?

Cost per lead varies widely by category, city and competition, so there is no fixed price to plan around. A third-party reporting vendor puts typical pay-per-lead charges at [$5 to $150 per qualified lead](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/google-local-services-ads-reporting-tool) depending on category and market. The same secondary guide claims contractors can spend $2,000 to $8,000 a month on LSA leads, which is why tracking outcomes matters. Treat both figures as indicative and use your own account data once you have a few weeks of leads.

The better test is profitability. Take your average job value and close rate, work out the most you can pay per lead, then compare that with the cost per lead in your dashboard. Google's reports break down charged leads and total lead spend by call, message and booking, which shows where the money goes.

## Does a plumber's LSA budget pay off?

The inputs below are assumptions for illustration, not Google figures. Replace them with your own numbers.

- Monthly LSA budget: $1,200 (assumption)
- Cost per lead: $60 (assumption, inside the secondary range above)
- Leads received: $1,200 ÷ $60 = 20 leads
- Share of leads that become booked jobs: 30% (assumption), giving 6 jobs
- Average job value: $350 (assumption), giving $2,100 revenue
- Lead cost per booked job: $1,200 ÷ 6 = $200
- Revenue left after ad spend: $2,100 − $1,200 = $900, before materials, labour and time

If the same plumber could only book 2 of the 20 leads, revenue would fall to $700 and the ad spend would leave a loss. That is why lead-outcome tracking matters more than the cost per lead on its own.

## How should you track and manage leads?

Marking outcomes is what turns the lead list into useful data. In Google's Local Services Ads inbox you can mark a lead as booked, add a job type and customer name, and set a date for future jobs. Google notes that these tracking details are visible only to you. The booking feature is [documented for the US and Canada](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6226265?hl=en), so readers elsewhere should check what their account offers.

Answer calls and messages quickly, because response speed affects how the ad performs. Separate call tracking tools exist too. A [secondary reporting guide](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/google-local-services-ads-reporting-tool) lists CallRail plans at $30 to $150 a month. A simple routine helps whichever tool you use:

| Field to record | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Lead type (call, message, booking) | Shows which channel brings real work |
| Job type | Shows which services are profitable |
| Booked or not a fit | Feeds lead-quality reporting |
| Job value once completed | Lets you calculate cost per job |
| Dispute reason, if any | Supports invalid-lead claims within Google's window |

For teams that handle many messages, tools that draft replies and log outcomes can reduce missed follow-ups. For a broader view of tooling, see [AI tools that can replace a marketing assistant for local businesses](/blog/ai-tools-replace-marketing-assistant-local-business).

## What mistakes should you avoid with LSAs?

The most common mistake is starting before you can answer the phone. A missed call during business hours is a paid lead you cannot convert. Set up a callback process or an after-hours message first.

The second mistake is a service area wider than you can serve. Leads outside your area waste budget, and disputes are not guaranteed to be approved. Review the area monthly.

The third is ignoring reviews. Google's ad page says reviews and profile completeness shape how the ad looks and ranks, so ask every happy customer for one.

The fourth is judging results too early. Give the account several weeks of data before changing bids or budgets.

## Should you use LSAs alongside other Google Ads?

Yes, they are separate products and can run side by side. Many businesses start with LSAs for core job types and add standard Search campaigns for wider keywords. The [Google Ads API documentation](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/campaigns/local-service-campaigns) shows that Local Services campaigns are managed separately from standard campaigns, which is one reason to track them in their own reports. ## FAQ

### Can I run Local Service Ads from a home-based business?

It can be possible if your category is eligible, because a storefront is not always required. You still need to pass address and business verification. Check your settings so that the service area is displayed the way you want.

### What happens if a lead is outside my service area?

You can dispute it within Google's stated process, but approval is not guaranteed. If out-of-area leads keep appearing, narrow the service area in your account.

### Can I run LSAs and standard Google Ads together?

Yes. The two products are separate and can run at the same time. Keep their reporting apart so you can compare cost per job for each.

### How long does approval take?

It depends on your category and how complete your documents are. Plan for days to a few weeks, and double-check licence and insurance details before you submit.

### Is there a minimum budget?

Google may show recommended or minimum weekly budgets in your account for your market and category. Use the figure shown there rather than a general estimate, and make sure the budget is large enough to produce enough leads to learn from.
