A hair salon should choose the most specific primary category that matches what it wants to be found for, then add a few extra categories only for services it really offers. Google says categories affect local ranking, and Whitespark's January 2026 guide calls the primary category the number one local search ranking factor. This page shows how to pick them, with a worked example.

Google does not publish a ranking formula, so no one can promise a position. What you can control is accuracy: a profile that names the right business type gives Google the clearest signal to match you to searches.

## What Does Google Say About Choosing a Category?

Google's help pages give four rules. Pick a primary category that best describes the business, and make it as specific as possible: its example is choosing "Nail salon" over "Salon". If no specific category fits, use a more general one, because you cannot create your own. Use as few categories as possible. Do not use categories as keywords or to describe attributes.

Google also says broader categories are added for you when you pick a specific one, so adding a redundant general category brings nothing. Its example for a business inside another business is a coffee shop inside a bookstore: the bookstore should not add "Coffee Shop" to its own profile.

## Which Category Should Be the Primary One?

Whitespark advises selecting the primary category that most closely matches the term you want to rank for. For most salons that means the category closest to "hair salon" in your own dropdown. Names differ between regions and over time. Google's developer documentation, for example, lists the category ID gcid:hair_salon with the display name "Hairdresser", and a separate gcid:barber_shop ID also exists.

Open the category search in your profile, type "hair", and read the options yourself. Then decide which one a customer would type into Google. A barbershop that does only short cuts should lead with a barber category. Our [barbershop local SEO guide](/blog/barbershop-local-seo-guide) covers that case.

## How Many Additional Categories Should a Salon Add?

Whitespark says a profile can have up to nine additional categories, and that each tells Google to show the profile for that search term too. Google, by contrast, asks you to use as few as possible and not to add one for every service. For a salon, a practical limit is the number of services that bring in real revenue.

Check the list again every few months. Whitespark recommends looking for new categories every six months because Google updates them often. Do not rotate categories to chase trends; change them when your services change.

## How Do You Choose Categories for Your Services? (Worked Example)

The salon below is an **assumption**, built to show the method, not a recommended category list. It offers cuts, colour and hair extensions, and has no barber chair.

| Service | Real revenue? | Add a category? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Women's and men's cuts | Yes, main income | Primary category: the hair salon option in your dropdown |
| Colour | Yes, but covered by the primary type | No, the primary type already covers it |
| Extensions | Yes, a distinct service, 15% of revenue (assumption) | Yes, if a matching category exists in your dropdown |
| Short men's cuts only | No | No, skip the barber category |
| Waxing, sold twice a month | No | No |

Then check three competitors: search "hair salon near me" in your area, open the top three profiles, and see which categories they show. Copy the idea, not the list. If a competitor uses a category you cannot honestly claim, ignore it.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid?

- Stuffing every possible category. Google warns against a category for every service.
- Using a category as a keyword, such as a name that describes a style rather than a business type.
- Choosing a general category when a specific one exists.
- Forgetting to update categories when the salon adds a service such as extensions.

## FAQ

### Does the primary category matter more than the others?

Whitespark calls the primary category the number one local ranking factor in its January 2026 guide. Google also says categories affect local ranking.

### Can I make up my own category?

No. Google states you cannot create your own category, and if no exact match exists you should choose a more general one.

### How many categories can a hair salon have?

Whitespark says up to nine additional categories besides the primary one. Google recommends using as few as possible.

### Should a salon and barbershop under one roof use both categories?

Only if both services are real and sold to the public. Google says not to add categories for a separate business inside yours. If the barbershop is a separately run business, it should have its own profile.

### Will changing my category hurt my ranking?

Google does not say. Change categories when your services change, then watch your Search and Maps views for a few weeks.
