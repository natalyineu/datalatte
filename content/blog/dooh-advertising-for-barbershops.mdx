DOOH advertising can suit a barbershop as a small, time-limited test near the shop, not as a standing budget line. Self-serve platforms say you can start without a contract or minimum. [Blip](https://www.blipbillboards.com/pricing/) states it charges no setup fees, platform fees, agency fees or monthly retainers, and that you can start with a few dollars per day to test a single market. Traditional digital billboards cost far more, from $1,200 a month in smaller markets to $15,000 or more in major cities, according to [AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/answers/dooh-campaigns/how-much-does-dooh-digital-out-of-home-advertising-cost). Published data does not cover barbershop results, so the test has to produce your own numbers.

## What Does DOOH Cost for a Barbershop?

Costs depend on the format and the market. AdQuick lists digital billboard CPMs at $5 to $10 and says indoor screens are cheaper: mall screens typically run $750 to $2,500 per month, and digital supermarket screens roughly $800 to $1,500 per month. Those figures come from a vendor page, so ask each seller for a written price list before you sign anything.

Self-serve pricing works differently. Blip says its digital billboard advertising starts at $0.01 per 8-second play, with no monthly minimum and no contract. At that rate, a $5 day buys 500 plays before any other charge, since Blip says it charges no platform fees. Price is only half the decision. Compare the result with paid search too, using the [Bing Ads cost guide](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-cost-local-business), because a search click reaches someone already looking for a haircut.

## Does Public Attitude Justify a Test?

A 2024 OAAA and Harris Poll figure, cited by [Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), found 73% of adults view DOOH advertising favourably. That is an attitude measure, not a sales result, and it does not show that a barbershop customer will respond.

Inventory is also large. Blip describes access to thousands of digital billboards, and [Adomni](https://www.adomni.com/) claims more than 1 million digital screens. Those are vendor claims, and more inventory means more choice, not more customers. Start with one market and a handful of screens, then judge the results before buying more.

## Which Screens Suit a Barbershop?

Pick screens on roads your customers already drive within a short distance of the chair. Location matters more than inventory size. Blip lets you choose locations, schedule, spend amount and artwork, so the decision is yours.

Road screens need a short message. A vendor article from [Take10 Media](https://take10media.com/blog/self-serve-dooh-trends-2026-local-brands/) says highway viewers often have dwell times of only 3 to 5 seconds, a claim worth testing before you rely on it. For event-style placements near crowds, see the [DOOH event marketing guide](/blog/dooh-event-marketing-local-business).

## What Should the Ad Say?

A driver has a few seconds, so keep the message to four parts: the shop name, the street or exit, one offer or fact, and a large, high-contrast layout. Avoid a long phone number on a road screen. A short booking address or QR code suits a screen that pedestrians pass slowly.

Test one message at a time. If you change the offer, the new customer count will mix two effects, and you will not know which one worked.

## How Do You Measure a Barbershop DOOH Test?

Impressions are estimates. They show how many people could have seen the ad, not who booked a cut. Ask each vendor how it counts impressions and whether it uses verified data before you pay.

Track customers directly. Use a booking link that only appears in the ad, or give a code word that staff record at the chair. Ask every new customer how they heard about you, and compare the count with four weeks before the test, during it and after it.

## How Do You Set Up a Two-Week Test?

Work through these steps in order before you spend anything:

1. Pull the postcodes of your last 50 customers and mark the ones within about three miles of the shop.
2. Shortlist screens on the roads those customers use, and ask each vendor how it counts impressions.
3. Set a daily spend you can keep up for 14 days, and confirm in writing that there is no contract or setup fee.
4. Write one message with the shop name, the street and one offer, and check it reads at a glance.
5. Set up a booking link or code word that staff can record at the chair.
6. Log new customers for four weeks before launch to set a baseline.
7. Run the campaign for 14 days and log new customers each day.
8. Compare the count with the spend, then decide whether to repeat, change the screen or stop.

## FAQ

### How much does DOOH cost for a barbershop?

Blip states self-serve billboard advertising starts at $0.01 per 8-second play, and that you can start with a few dollars per day. Traditional digital billboards run from $1,200 a month in smaller markets, per AdQuick.

### Do I need a contract for a digital billboard?

Blip says there is no required minimum and no contract for its billboard advertising. Check the terms of any other vendor in writing before you sign.

### Is DOOH better than paid search for a barbershop?

No published source compares the two for barbershops. Search ads reach people already looking for a service, while DOOH reaches people passing by. Test both, and start with the channel you can measure more easily.

### How do I know who saw the ad?

You cannot know exactly. Impressions are estimates, so track new customers with a code word or a dedicated booking link.
