DOOH, or digital out-of-home advertising, can work for a barbershop only as a small, tightly targeted test near the shop. Self-serve platforms advertise campaigns from about $20 a day, but those are vendor claims and no source found publishes barbershop results. The channel is large: OAAA reports US out-of-home revenue hit a record $9.46 billion in 2025, and digital formats were 36.3% of it.

This guide explains what a barbershop can buy, what the published prices say, what to measure and how to compare it with cheaper local options. Figures come from the sources listed at the end, and the example is labelled as an assumption.

## What Is DOOH and Who Sells It to Small Shops?

DOOH covers digital billboards and screens in public places: roads, transit, gyms, malls and street furniture. For a small shop, the practical route is a self-serve marketplace where you pick screens, set a daily budget and upload a creative. Some traditional billboard companies still require contracts and minimum spends for digital placements, per Blip's guide.

OAAA's first-quarter 2026 results show the channel is still growing: US OOH revenue of $2.12 billion, up 7.1%, with digital up 12.9% and about 36% of the total. Billboards are the largest format. Growth in a channel does not mean it pays off for a single shop, so test before committing.

## What Does DOOH Cost for a Barbershop?

Prices vary by market and platform, and the numbers below come from a vendor, so treat them as marketing claims. Blip's guide says a small business can run a campaign in one city for under $20 a day, with pay-per-play pricing described as starting as low as $0.01 per blip, where a blip is one eight-second play. The same guide says traditional premium bulletins in major metros run into five figures a month.

Blip describes bidding that updates roughly every 10 minutes, with lower bids off-peak. It reports no CPM range and gives no proof-of-play detail, so ask any vendor how impressions are counted. Also compare to cheaper channels first. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows how to split a small budget.

## Which Screens Make Sense Near a Barbershop?

Pick screens on the routes your customers already take within a short drive of the chair: the main road to the shop, a nearby gym or a transit stop. Blip suggests choosing boards by verified daily impressions, audience data and cost efficiency, and says commute times of roughly 7 to 9 AM and 4 to 7 PM are peak periods, with mid-day slots often cheaper.

For a barbershop the best times may differ. If your busiest hours are Saturday mornings, ask for those slots. A map of your existing customers' postal codes tells you where to buy. ## What Should the Ad Say?

A passing driver has only a moment, so keep it short. Use the shop name, the street and one line: Walk-ins welcome or Book online. Add a short web address or QR code only if the screen is seen by pedestrians. Avoid a phone number on a road screen.

- Shop name and distance, such as the street or exit
- One offer or one fact
- Large type, high contrast, no photo collages

## How Do You Measure Results?

Impressions are estimates. They show how many people could have seen the ad, not who came in. Use a dedicated booking link, a code word to say at the chair, or ask every new customer how they found you for six weeks. Compare new-customer counts in the weeks before, during and after.

## A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions**, not published results.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Daily budget | $20 (Blip's stated start) |
| Campaign length | 14 days |
| Total spend | $280 |
| New customers who mention the ad | 4 |
| Value of a regular customer over a year | $480 (12 cuts at $40) |

$280 for 4 customers is $70 each. If each customer stays a year at $40 a month, the lifetime value is $480, so the test pays back in that case. If none stay past two visits ($80), you lose money. The 4 customers is a guess. Run the test, record the real number and compare with the cost of a Google Business Profile update, which is free.

<Callout type="tip" title="Do This Today">
Pull the postal codes of your last 50 customers. If most live within three miles, DOOH beyond that radius is wasted spend.
</Callout>

## FAQ

### How much does a digital billboard cost for a small business?

Blip says under $20 a day in one city on its self-serve platform, and five figures a month for premium bulletins in major metros. These are vendor claims.

### Is DOOH better than Google Ads for a barbershop?

No source compares them. Google Ads captures people searching now, while DOOH is awareness. Start with search and your Business Profile.

### Do I need a contract?

Blip says self-serve has no contract or minimum. Some traditional companies require contracts and minimums for digital placements.

### How do I know who saw it?

You cannot know exactly. Impressions are estimated. Track new customers with a code word or a booking link.

### Is DOOH growing?

OAAA reports digital revenue rose 10.5% in 2025 and 12.9% in Q1 2026.
