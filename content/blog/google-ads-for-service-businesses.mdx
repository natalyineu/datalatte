A service business should start Google Ads with one Search campaign per core service, call tracking, a tight location radius and a landing page that matches each search. Google does not publish one price for service leads, so test cost per lead in your own account. Plumbers, electricians and other trades should also compare Local Services Ads, which Google says charge only for high-quality leads rather than per click. The sections below cover setup, tracking and a worked budget.

This guide is for local service businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: trades, personal services and professionals. Sources are listed at the end. The worked example uses assumptions, which are labelled.

## How Much Does Google Ads Cost for a Service Business?

Costs vary too much by trade and city to quote one figure responsibly. Yelp advertising, for example, generally uses pay-per-click, and the advertiser sets a budget and pays when prospective customers engage with the ad. [Yelp's own pricing has no universal monthly price](https://www.icatchgroup.com/google-ads-vs-yelp-ads/) (secondary). Google Ads works on a similar principle: you set a budget and control spend.

Local Services Ads follow a different model. Google says you pay for leads that meet its quality rules, not for every click ([Google Local Services Ads](https://business.google.com/us/ad-solutions/local-service-ads/)). Accrisoft also notes that LSAs can cost more than other online advertising, especially for highly competitive keywords ([Accrisoft](https://www.accrisoft.com/blog/2024/06/03/service-industries/do-google-local-service-ads-work/), secondary).

Use your own numbers. After four weeks, divide spend by real inquiries to get cost per lead, then divide by jobs won to get cost per job.

## Should You Use Google Search Ads or Local Services Ads?

Many trades should test both, because they bill differently and suit different jobs. Search ads let you bid on the exact words customers type. LSAs appear at the top of results and carry a green checkmark that Google uses to show the business has been vetted ([Accrisoft](https://www.accrisoft.com/blog/2024/06/03/service-industries/do-google-local-service-ads-work/), secondary).

| Option | How you pay | Best for | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Search campaign | Per click, under your budget | Customers who type a specific service and city | Wasted clicks from wrong searches |
| Local Services Ads | Per qualified lead | Calls for emergencies and routine trade work | Lead prices vary, so compare cost per valid lead |
| Yelp Ads | Pay-per-click, set budget | Businesses already listed and reviewed on Yelp | No universal monthly price |

Google's Performance Max campaigns also state that you stay in control of your budget and only pay when someone engages with your ad ([Google Marketing Tools Support](https://business.google.com/us/support/)). Check the current billing terms in your account before you commit a budget.

## How Do You Structure the Campaign?

Keep the structure simple while the budget is small. Run one campaign per main service, with ad groups split by intent. Group emergency and repair searches apart from installation and quote searches, because they need different pages and offers.

Build each ad group around three kinds of keywords:

- Service plus location, such as the trade and your city
- Problem phrases customers type when something breaks
- Qualifiers such as "same day" or "licensed", used only when they are true

Start with phrase and exact match. Broad match can bring in wrong searches before you have enough data to filter them. [Our contractor keyword guide](/blog/google-ads-for-contractors) covers trade-specific examples, and the same structure works for plumbers and electricians.

## How Do You Keep Ads Local and Stop Wasted Clicks?

Set your location targeting to the area you actually serve. Be strict about the service radius, since a wide radius sends paid clicks to customers you cannot reach. If you only want people physically inside your service area, choose the setting that limits targeting to that presence.

Then add negative keywords for searches that never convert, such as jobs, careers, free, DIY and training if you do not offer them. Google's negative keyword rules mean close variants are not always excluded automatically, so add plurals and synonyms yourself. Review the search terms report weekly for the first month.

Useful reference points for a trade business are in [our Local Services Ads guide](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business), which covers when LSAs are worth testing and how to judge lead quality.

## How Do You Track Calls and Leads?

Track calls and form submissions separately, because a call and a form lead need different follow-up. Google's Local Services Ads include an app that lets you field, respond to and manage calls and leads from one place ([Slamdot](https://www.slamdot.com/blog/catering-marketing-101-four-google-marketing-tools-you-need-to-master/), secondary).

Google Ads also provides a dashboard for monitoring performance, where you can see leads generated, cost per lead and return on investment ([Accrisoft](https://www.accrisoft.com/blog/2024/06/03/service-industries/do-google-local-service-ads-work/), secondary). Use it, but check that the numbers match your own call log. Mark each inquiry as a real lead or junk, and track how many become paid jobs.

## What Should the Landing Page Include?

Send each ad group to a page about that service, not the homepage. The page should repeat the service and town from the ad, show a tap-to-call number near the top, and give one clear next step: call, request a quote or book.

Add proof you can back up, such as your licence number if your trade requires one, your review count and the area you serve. Remove menus that lead visitors away from the action. Change one element at a time, and give each test enough time to collect calls before you judge it.

## Worked Example: A $1,000 Month

Every input below is an **assumption**, chosen for illustration. Replace them with your own data after your first month.

| Input or result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,000 |
| Average cost per click (assumption) | $6 |
| Clicks | about 167 |
| Lead rate from clicks (assumption) | 8% |
| Leads | about 13 |
| Cost per lead | about $75 |
| Leads who become paid jobs (assumption) | 30% |
| Paid jobs | about 4 |
| Cost per job | about $250 |
| Average job value (assumption) | $600 |
| Job revenue | about $2,400 |

On these assumptions, $1,000 of ad spend produces about $2,400 in job revenue before labour and materials. The campaign breaks even at $250 per job. If your average job is worth less than that, the same budget loses money, so confirm your average job value before you set a budget.

## What Does a Vendor Case Study Show?

A vendor case study from an Orlando local service business reported a $1,500 monthly budget over 90 days, generating 138 qualified leads and 48 converted customers. The vendor reported $96,000 in revenue and a 12.4x return on ad spend ([ACT Global Marketing](https://actglobalmarketing.com/facebook-ads-for-local-businesses/), secondary). That case used Facebook Ads, not Google Ads, and the vendor produced it, so treat it as one data point rather than a benchmark.

Adstellar's guide to Facebook ads lists Awareness, Traffic, Leads and Sales as the main objectives, and says Leads suits service businesses that sell through calls or forms ([Adstellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/facebook-ads-for-local-businesses), secondary). For Google Ads, the equivalent is matching the search to a call or form on your site.

## FAQ

### How much should a service business spend on Google Ads?

There is no standard budget. Start with an amount you can run for at least a month, then judge it by cost per job rather than cost per click. The worked example above shows how the numbers connect.

### Do I need an agency to run it?

Not always. The work is weekly: review search terms, add negatives and check calls. If you cannot spend that time, a specialist or managed service can be cheaper than paying for wasted clicks. Compare them on cost per job, not on monthly fee alone.

### How long before Google Ads brings leads?

Leads can arrive in the first week, but judge results over a month or more. Early data is thin, so avoid changing bids after a few days.

### Are Local Services Ads cheaper than Search ads?

Google does not publish one price for LSAs, and lead prices vary. Compare cost per valid lead from LSAs against cost per job from Search in your own account.

### Should I also run Facebook ads?

Facebook can help with retargeting and offers, but start with search, because people searching for a service are already looking for a provider. Once Search works, test Facebook with a Leads objective.
