The best Google Ads keywords for a nail salon are local, service-specific phrases such as "nail salon near me", "gel manicure [city]", "acrylic nails [neighbourhood]" and "dip powder nails [city]", paired with a strong negative keyword list. These searchers are close to booking, so they usually convert better than broad terms like "nails" or "nail art". Start with a small, tightly themed list, add negatives from your Search Terms report each week, and expand from there.

Costs per click vary by city, season and competition. Google's [Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243) shows search volumes and the cost to target keywords in your area, so use it and your own account data rather than any fixed figure you read online.

## Why location-based keywords come first

A person searching "nail salon [city]" or "nail salon near me" wants an appointment, not inspiration. Generic terms such as "acrylic nails" bring in people researching designs, looking for DIY tips or living in another city. Local modifiers filter most of that out.

Two habits help:
- Restrict your location targeting to the area you realistically serve (for a single salon, usually the neighbourhoods a client would realistically travel from)
- Add neighbourhood names to your keywords, for example "East Austin nail studio" instead of just "Austin nail salon", when the city term is crowded

## High-intent "near me" and local keywords

| Keyword type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Near me | "nail salon near me", "nail spa near me", "gel nails near me" |
| City or neighbourhood | "nail salon [city]", "nail studio [neighbourhood]" |
| Open now / urgency | "nail salon open late [city]", "walk-in nails near me", "same-day nail appointment" |
| Price intent | "cheap acrylic nails near me", "affordable gel manicure [city]" |

Use Keyword Planner to check volume and the suggested bid range for your own location before you decide how much to bid.

## Service-specific keywords

Group keywords by service so each ad can match what the person typed:

- **Acrylics:** "acrylic nails [city]", "full set acrylics", "acrylic nail fill"
- **Gel:** "gel manicure near me", "gel nails [city]", "shellac nails"
- **Dip powder:** "dip powder nails", "dip manicure near me", "SNS nails"
- **Pedicures:** "spa pedicure [city]", "gel pedicure near me", "deluxe pedicure"
- **Nail art:** "custom nail art [city]", "3D nail designs", "hand-painted nails"
- **Bridal and events:** "bridal nails [city]", "wedding nail packages", "prom nails"
- **Niche services:** "nail salon for men [city]", "kids pedicure near me"

Long-tail phrases with four or more words usually have lower volume but clearer intent. Practical modifiers that real customers search include "with parking", "same-day", "gift cards" and "open Sunday". Only bid on those you can actually deliver.

## Seasonal keyword ideas

Demand for nails follows the calendar, so plan campaigns a few weeks ahead of each peak.

| Season | Keyword ideas |
|---|---|
| Spring | "Easter nails [city]", "pastel gel nails", "prom nails", "bridal nails" |
| Summer | "summer nails [city]", "gel pedicure near me", "vacation nails", "beach nails" |
| Autumn | "fall nail colours", "Halloween nails", "Thanksgiving nails" |
| Winter | "Christmas nail designs [city]", "New Year's nails", "Valentine's Day nails" |

Costs usually rise when many salons bid for the same event, so check your impression share and consider long-tail seasonal terms when generic ones become expensive. Use separate seasonal ad groups so you can pause them after the event instead of deleting your history.

## Negative keywords every nail salon should add

Negative keywords let you exclude search terms so your ads focus on the keywords that matter ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972), checked Oct 2026). The [search terms report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708) shows the real searches that triggered your ads, and Google advises adding irrelevant ones as negatives. Start with these themes, then review the report weekly:

- **DIY and home:** "DIY nails", "at home gel kit", "how to do acrylics", "nail tutorial"
- **Jobs and training:** "nail tech jobs", "nail salon hiring", "nail course", "nail school"
- **Products and supplies:** "nail polish", "acrylic powder wholesale", "nail supplies", "press-on nails"
- **Business-side searches:** "nail salon for sale", "nail salon equipment", "nail salon booth rental"
- **Other services:** "massage", "waxing" and "hair" if you do not offer them

## Five common mistakes that waste budget

**1. Bidding on "nail salon" with no location control.** If your targeting is too wide, you pay for clicks from people who will never visit. Use tight location targeting and add city or neighbourhood terms.

**2. Skipping negative keywords.** Without them your ads can appear for job, DIY and supply searches. Check the Search Terms report at least weekly in the first month.

**3. One generic ad for every keyword.** A search for "dip powder nails" should land on an ad and page about dip powder. Ads that mirror the search are more relevant to the searcher and usually perform better.

**4. Not tracking calls and bookings.** If bookings come by phone, set up call tracking and conversion actions, or you are guessing which keywords work. See our [Google Ads conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup).

**5. Pausing everything in slow months.** Rather than switching off completely, many salons lower budgets and switch the message to a quiet-season offer. Test this against a full pause to see what works for you.

## How to structure your nail salon campaigns

A simple structure is easier to manage and gives Google clearer signals.

1. **Service campaign:** one ad group per service (acrylics, gel, dip powder, pedicures, nail art, bridal). This is usually the biggest share of budget.
2. **Local campaign:** "near me", city and neighbourhood terms, with call and location assets turned on. Google lets you promote your address and a phone number through [location and call assets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7331111), which are the successors to what used to be called extensions.
3. **Offers campaign:** price-driven searches. Run it only if you really offer discounts.
4. **Brand campaign:** your own salon name, usually cheap and worth protecting.

Within each ad group, start with phrase and exact match for your core terms and let the Search Terms report guide what to add. Avoid very broad match until you have conversion data.

As a rough guide, give the largest share of budget to the services campaign, a smaller share to local and "near me" terms, a small share to offers if you run them, and the smallest share to your brand campaign. Adjust once you see which campaign produces bookings.

## Quick audit checklist

1. Do you have separate ad groups for each major service?
2. Is your location targeting limited to areas you serve?
3. Are negative keywords added and reviewed weekly?
4. Are calls, bookings and form fills tracked as conversions?
5. Do ads include a price, offer or booking detail that matches the keyword?
6. Is your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) complete, with reviews, photos and services listed? Ads and map results work together.

## FAQ

**Q: What are the best Google Ads keywords for a nail salon?**
A: Local and service-specific terms perform best: "nail salon near me", "gel manicure [city]", "acrylic nails [city]", "dip powder nails near me" and "bridal nails [city]". Start with a small, tightly themed list and expand based on your Search Terms report.

**Q: How much does a click cost for nail salon keywords?**

**Q: Should I use broad match or exact match?**
A: Begin with phrase and exact match so you control what triggers your ads. Test broad match later, once you have conversion data and a strong negative keyword list.

**Q: Which negative keywords should a nail salon use?**
A: At minimum: DIY, tutorial, jobs, hiring, school, supplies, wholesale, for sale and any service you do not offer. Add more from your Search Terms report every week.

**Q: Is Google Ads worth it for a nail salon?**
A: It can be, especially for new-client bookings, if you track bookings and keep your keywords local.
