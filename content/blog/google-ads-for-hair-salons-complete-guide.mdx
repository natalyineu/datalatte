Google Ads for a hair salon works best when campaigns are built around chair-hours, not clicks. Take a $75 haircut that fills one hour and a $250 balayage that fills four. The haircut earns $75 per chair-hour and the balayage about $62.

Bid hardest on the biggest ticket and you can pay more to win a client who earns the chair less. Those prices and durations are assumptions, so swap in your own.

This guide is for salon owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers splitting campaigns by service, what clicks cost, which settings matter, how to count bookings and what each service can afford to pay per click. Dollar figures are US-based; run the same arithmetic in your own currency. For keyword lists, use the [hair salon keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026).

## How Should You Structure Campaigns for a Salon?

Do not put everything into one campaign. A single budget lets the cheapest clicks eat the spend and tells you nothing about which service works.

| Campaign | What goes in it | Why separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core cuts and styling | "hair salon near me", "haircut [town]" | Highest volume, lowest ticket |
| Colour and treatments | "balayage [town]", "keratin treatment near me" | Fewer searches, higher ticket, longer chair time |
| Brand | [your salon name] | Cheap and protects your name |

Give each campaign its own budget. Inside each, keep ad groups tight: one for balayage, one for root colour, one for colour correction. A person typing "balayage [town]" should see "Balayage in [Town]" in the headline.

Google's [match type help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) says exact match gives the most control but reaches the fewest searches, and "It's critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match." At a salon budget, start with phrase and exact.

Google's [negative keyword help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972) says negatives do not match close variants, so add `school` and `schools` separately. Start with `jobs`, `career`, `beauty school`, `training`, `course`, `diy`, `box dye`, `wig` and `free`.

## How Do You Set the Radius and Daily Budget?

Pull your last 50 new clients and note where each lives or works. Draw the radius around that cluster rather than a round number. Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) says you can target a radius and exclude regions, and that accuracy is best effort, not guaranteed.

On budget, Google's [daily budget help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6385083) says to divide a monthly amount by 30.4. For most campaigns spend can reach twice the daily budget on one day, and up to 30.4 times it in a month. A $1,200 month is about $39.50 a day, and a $70 day is normal.

## What Should Salon Ads and Landing Pages Say?

Per Google's [responsive search ad requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791), you can enter up to 15 headlines of 30 characters and up to 4 descriptions of 90 characters. For a colour campaign: `Balayage in [Town]`, `Colour Consultation Included`, `Book Online in 60 Seconds`.

Add a call asset, a location asset, sitelinks to each service and your price list. Google's [assets help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7331111) says call assets can be scheduled for hours you can answer, and that assets show only when predicted to improve performance and the ad meets a minimum Ad Rank.

Send a "balayage [town]" click to a balayage page, not the home page. Include the ad headline, three to five photos of your own work, a starting price, the time it takes, two real reviews and one booking button. Avoid promises like "guaranteed blonde in one visit".

## How Do You Count and Value Bookings?

Set up tracking for online bookings, calls and forms before ads go live. Google's [call conversion help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) says a call counts only when it lasts longer than a minimum length you set, and the default tag tracks one number per page. A 30 to 60 second minimum is a sensible starting suggestion, not a Google default.

Google lets you [set a conversion value](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7197048), and says changes apply only to conversions that happen after the change. If colour and cuts use different conversion actions, each can carry its own value. Treat it as a revenue estimate, not a measured sale.

## What Does a $1,200 Month Return? A Worked Example

Split $1,200 as $600 per service campaign. Every row is an assumption, apart from the CPC range bracketing the $4.62 benchmark above.

| | Core cuts | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $4.00 | $6.00 |
| Clicks | 150 | 100 |
| Click to booking (assumption) | 8% | 5% |
| New bookings | 12 | 5 |
| Ad cost per booking | $50 | $120 |
| First visit price (assumption) | $75 | $220 |
| Visits in 12 months (assumption) | 4 | 3 |
| Revenue per client, 12 months | $300 | $660 |
| Revenue after ad cost | $250 | $540 |
| Chair-hours per client, 12 months (assumption) | 4 | 10.5 |
| Revenue after ad cost, per chair-hour | $62 | $51 |

Colour wins on revenue per client ($540 against $250) but loses per chair-hour ($51 against $62). If cutting slots are empty, the cut campaign fills them cheaply. If colourists are booked three weeks out, pause the colour campaign. All figures are revenue before wages, product and rent.

- **Break-even click price** = the most you will pay per booking x click-to-booking rate. At $60 per cut booking and 8%: $4.80 a click. For colour, $150 at 5% gives $7.50.
- **Booking rate is the biggest lever.** Moving colour from 5% to 8% cuts cost per booking from $120 to $75 with no bid change.

## What Should You Check Every Week?

1. Read the search terms report and add negatives for irrelevant queries.
2. Pause keywords that spent about twice your target cost per booking with no booking (a rule of thumb, not a Google rule).
3. Test one new headline every two weeks.
4. Check calls against opening hours and schedule the call asset.
5. Raise a budget by about 20% only when it hits your target cost per booking and you have chair-hours to sell.

For common errors, read [Google Ads mistakes hair salons make](/blog/google-ads-mistakes-hair-salons-make-and-how-to-fix-them). To test social, see [Facebook ads for hair salons](/blog/hair-salon-facebook-ads-strategy).

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a hair salon spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official figure. Work backwards from the most you will pay for a new client and your click-to-booking rate. See [how much a hair salon should spend on Google Ads](/blog/how-much-should-a-hair-salon-spend-on-google-ads).

**Q: What should a hair salon bid on first?**
A: Services with empty chair-hours and healthy margin, in your town, plus your own name. Generic "hair salon near me" is high volume but often your most expensive, least specific term.

**Q: Are Google Ads or Instagram better for a salon?**
A: Search reaches people already looking to book; Meta can show your work to people who were not searching. Many salons start with Google.

**Q: Should I fix my Google Business Profile first?**
A: Yes. Photos, hours, services and reviews affect free map results and trust in the ad people just clicked.

**Q: How long until bookings arrive?**
A: Clicks start within hours. Bookings timing varies; a four to six week review window is a rule of thumb.
