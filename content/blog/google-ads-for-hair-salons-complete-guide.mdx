Here is the number that changes how a salon should run search ads. Take a $75 haircut that fills one hour and a $250 balayage that fills four. The haircut earns $75 per chair-hour. The balayage earns about $62. If you bid hardest on the service with the biggest ticket, you can pay more to win a client who earns your chair less. Those prices and durations are my assumptions, so swap in your own. The point is that a hair salon sells time, and the ad account should be built around it.

This guide is for salon owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers how to split campaigns by service, what clicks cost, which settings matter, how to count bookings, and how to work out what each service can afford to pay for a click. Dollar figures are US-based; for pounds or Australian and Canadian dollars, run the same arithmetic with your own prices. ## Step 1: Work Out What Each Service Earns Per Chair-Hour

Before opening the ad account, fill in this table for your five most-booked services. The numbers below are examples (assumptions), not market data.

| Service | Price (assumption) | Chair time (assumption) | Revenue per chair-hour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Women's cut and blow-dry | $75 | 1 hour | $75 |
| Men's cut | $40 | 0.5 hour | $80 |
| Root colour | $110 | 2 hours | $55 |
| Balayage | $250 | 4 hours | $62 |
| Keratin treatment | $300 | 3 hours | $100 |

This is revenue, not profit, and it ignores product, assistants and the fact that colour clients often rebook sooner. It still tells you something a ticket price hides: the most expensive service is not always the most valuable use of a chair. Which services does your calendar need more of? Those are the ones to advertise.

## Step 2: Split Campaigns by Service Value

Do not put everything into one campaign. A single budget lets the cheapest clicks eat the spend and tells you nothing about which service is working. A simple structure for one salon:

| Campaign | What goes in it | Why separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core cuts and styling | "hair salon near me", "haircut [town]", "blow dry [town]" | Highest search volume, lowest ticket |
| Colour and treatments | "balayage [town]", "hair colour specialist [town]", "keratin treatment near me" | Fewer searches, higher ticket, longer chair time |
| Brand | [your salon name] | Cheap and protects your name |

Give each campaign its own budget so one cannot starve the other. Inside each, keep ad groups tight: one for balayage, one for root colour, one for colour correction. A person typing "balayage [town]" should see "Balayage in [Town]" in the headline.

### Match types for a small budget

Google's [match type help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) says broad match reaches the widest range of searches and that "It's critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match." Phrase match reaches searches that include the meaning of your keyword, and exact match gives the most control but reaches the fewest searches. At a salon budget, start with phrase and exact, and add broad only when you have steady booking data to feed automated bidding.

### Negatives: write them in the right match type

Google's [negative keyword help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972) points out one thing owners miss: "Negative keywords don't match to close variants." If you add `school` as a negative, a search for "schools" may still trigger your ad unless you add the plural as well. Start with `jobs`, `career`, `cosmetology school`, `beauty school`, `training`, `course`, `diy`, `at home`, `box dye`, `wig`, `extensions for sale`, `products` and `free`. Our negative keywords guide explains lists and campaign-level setup.

## Step 3: Location and Budget Mechanics

Pull your last 50 new clients and note where each lives or works. Draw your radius around that cluster rather than around a round number. Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) says you can use a radius around a location, exclude regions, and that targeting is Google's best effort rather than exact. Check the setting for who sees your ads in your own account; for a walk-in business I prefer to restrict it to people in or regularly in the area, which is my preference rather than a Google rule.

On budget, Google's [daily budget help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6385083) says to divide a monthly amount by 30.4 to get the daily figure, and that for most campaigns Google can spend up to twice your daily budget on one day, but not more than 30.4 times it in a month. A $1,200 month is about $39.50 a day. A $70 day is normal, not an error.

## Step 4: Ads and Assets That Match the Search

Per Google's [responsive search ad requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791), you can enter up to 15 headlines of 30 characters and up to 4 descriptions of 90 characters. Use them. Examples for a colour campaign:

- Headlines: `Balayage in [Town]`, `Colour Consultation Included`, `Book Online in 60 Seconds`, `Colourists With 10+ Years` (only if true)
- Description: `Consultation first, then a plan and a price before we start. See recent work and book online.`

Add a call asset, a location asset, sitelinks to each service page and your price list, and callouts such as "Online Booking" or "Consultation Included". Google's [assets help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7331111) says call assets can be scheduled to show only when you can answer, and that assets appear only when predicted to improve performance and the ad meets a minimum Ad Rank, so they will not show every time.

Do not promise results you cannot guarantee. Phrases like "guaranteed blonde in one visit" invite disapproval and complaints. "Free colour consultation" is safe if you honour it.

## Step 5: The Landing Page and the Booking Step

For a "balayage [town]" ad, send the click to a balayage page, not your home page. It needs the headline from the ad, three to five photos of your own work, a starting price or range, the time it takes, two real reviews, and one booking button that opens your booking system with the service pre-selected where your platform allows. See landing page best practices for the layout.

## Step 6: Count Bookings, Value Them Differently

Set up conversion tracking for online bookings, calls and forms before the ads go live. Our conversion tracking setup guide covers the steps, and call tracking for local business covers phone enquiries.

On calls, Google's [website call conversions help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) says a call counts as a conversion only when it lasts longer than a minimum length you set, and that the default tag can track only one number per page. Set the minimum to a duration that matches a real booking enquiry (30 to 60 seconds is my suggestion, not a Google default).

Now use the table from Step 1. Google lets you [set a conversion value](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7197048) for a conversion action, applied only to conversions after you change it. If your colour page and your cut page use different conversion actions, you can give each its own value. That lets the account see that a booking from the colour page is worth more, but treat the value as a rough label: it is a revenue estimate, not a measured sale.

## The Math

Take a $1,200 monthly budget split $600 and $600 between the two service campaigns. Every row is an **assumption** except that the CPC range brackets the $4.62 Beauty & Personal Care benchmark above.

| | Core cuts | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $4.00 | $6.00 |
| Clicks | 150 | 100 |
| Click to booking rate (assumption) | 8% | 5% |
| New bookings | 12 | 5 |
| Ad cost per booking | $50 | $120 |
| First visit price (assumption) | $75 | $220 |
| Visits in first 12 months (assumption) | 4 | 3 |
| Revenue per new client, 12 months | $300 | $660 |
| Revenue after ad cost, per client | $250 | $540 |
| Chair-hours per visit (assumption) | 1 | 3.5 |
| Chair-hours used per client, 12 months | 4 | 10.5 |
| Revenue after ad cost, per chair-hour | $62 | $51 |

Read it carefully. Colour wins on revenue per client ($540 against $250), but loses on revenue per chair-hour ($51 against $62) once ad cost is spread over the time it takes. If your calendar has empty cutting slots, the cut campaign fills them cheaply. If your colourists are booked out three weeks ahead, extra colour demand does not help and you should pause that campaign. Both columns are revenue, before wages, product and rent.

Two formulas follow:

- **Break-even click price** = the most you will pay per booking x your click-to-booking rate. If you will pay $60 per cut booking at 8%, your ceiling is $60 x 0.08 = $4.80 a click. For colour, paying up to $150 at 5% gives $7.50.
- **The lever that moves most is the booking rate**, which is a landing page, availability and phone-answering problem. Moving colour from 5% to 8% cuts its cost per booking from $120 to $75 with no bid change.

## Optimise Weekly

1. Read the search terms report, which Google describes as a list of search terms that triggered your ads, and add negatives for anything irrelevant. Turn on the Keyword column so you see which keyword matched.
2. Pause keywords that spent about twice your target cost per booking with no booking (my rule of thumb).
3. Test one new headline every two weeks.
4. Check calls against opening hours and schedule the call asset to match.
5. Raise a campaign's budget by about 20% only when it hits your target cost per booking and you have chair-hours to sell.

Pair the ads with a strong Google Business Profile, since people compare photos and reviews before booking. If you also want to test social, see our guide to [Facebook ads for hair salons](/blog/hair-salon-facebook-ads-strategy).

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a hair salon spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official figure. Work backwards: the most you will pay for a new client, divided by how many clicks it takes to win one, gives your break-even click price. See [how much a hair salon should spend](/blog/how-much-should-a-hair-salon-spend-on-google-ads) for budget tiers.

**Q: What should a hair salon bid on first?**
A: Bid on the services where you have empty chair-hours and a healthy margin, in your town, plus your own name. Generic "hair salon near me" is high volume but often your most expensive and least specific term.

**Q: Are Google Ads or Instagram better for a salon?**
A: They do different jobs. Search reaches people already looking to book; Meta can show your work to people who were not searching. Many salons start with Google and add Meta later.

**Q: Should I fix my Google Business Profile first?**
A: Yes. Photos, hours, services and reviews affect free map results and make people trust the ad they just clicked.

**Q: How long until I see bookings?**
A: Clicks start within hours and bookings often within weeks. Give it four to six weeks, my rule of thumb, before major changes.
