To turn WhatsApp messages into bookings, set up the free WhatsApp Business app with a complete profile, a catalog of services, quick replies for common questions, and a greeting and away message, then reply fast and always end with a clear next step, such as a booking link or two available times. Customers who already prefer messaging often book more readily when you make it easy to ask a question first.

WhatsApp is hugely popular in the UK, Australia, much of Europe, and with many communities in the US and Canada, though usage varies by region and customer group. Check how your own customers prefer to contact you before investing time.

## Is WhatsApp Right for Your Business?

WhatsApp works well when customers have quick questions before booking and prefer a conversational channel to a phone call or web form.

Good fits:

- **Salons and barbers:** "Do you have a slot Saturday?" and photo sharing for styles.
- **Pet groomers:** sharing a photo of the dog's coat, confirming breed and size.
- **Fitness and yoga studios:** class availability, trial bookings, reminders.
- **Cafes and bakeries:** pre-orders, catering enquiries, event bookings.
- **Mobile and home services:** quotes from photos.

Less suitable: businesses whose customers rarely use WhatsApp, or those that need heavy compliance controls. In those cases, SMS, Instagram DMs or web chat may work better. If you are comparing channels, our [email and SMS service](/services/email-sms) can help you choose.

## The Three Versions of WhatsApp for Business

| Option | Best for | Cost | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp Business app | Solo owners and small teams | Free | Business profile, catalog, labels, quick replies, greeting and away messages |
| WhatsApp Business Platform (API) | Larger teams, automation, multiple agents | Usage-based, via a provider | Needs a provider such as Twilio, 360dialog or MessageBird; supports templates and integrations |
| Personal WhatsApp | Not recommended | Free | No business tools, and mixing personal and business chats is messy |

Most local businesses should start with the free app. Move to the Platform only when message volume or the need for several staff on one number justifies it. Pricing for the Platform varies by country and conversation type, so check the provider's current rates.

## Step 1: Set Up the Business App

1. Download **WhatsApp Business** (separate from the regular app).
2. Register with a number you use for the business, ideally a dedicated number or landline, since the number is tied to the account.
3. Complete your **business profile:** name, category, description, address, opening hours, website and email.
4. Add a **profile photo** that customers recognise, such as your logo or storefront.
5. Link your Facebook or Instagram accounts if you want them shown on your profile.

Use the same business name, address and hours as on your Google Business Profile. Consistency builds trust. See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## Step 2: Build a Catalog

The catalog lets customers browse services or products inside the chat.

- Add each main service with a name, a short description, a photo and a price or "from" price.
- Keep descriptions short and clear.
- For services that vary, say what the price depends on, such as pet size or hair length.
- Add a booking link in the description where possible.

## Step 3: Set Up Greeting, Away and Quick Replies

These three features handle most routine conversations.

- **Greeting message:** sent automatically when a new customer messages you. Keep it short: who you are, and what you need from them.
- **Away message:** sent outside business hours, with when you will reply and a link to book online.
- **Quick replies:** saved responses triggered by typing a slash and a keyword. Create ones for hours, prices, location and parking, cancellation policy, booking steps, and what to bring.

Example greeting for a grooming salon:

> "Hi, thanks for messaging [Salon Name]. To help us reply quickly, please tell us your dog's breed, size, and the service you need. You can also book anytime at [link]."

Adapt wording to your own voice, and keep it friendly.

## Step 4: Use Labels to Keep Track

Labels turn WhatsApp into a light-touch CRM.

Suggested labels:

- New enquiry
- Quote sent
- Booked
- Needs follow-up
- Completed
- VIP or regular

Review "New enquiry" and "Quote sent" daily, so nothing gets lost. This is the simplest way to avoid dropped leads.

## Step 5: Move From Chat to Booking

The goal is a booking, not a long conversation.

1. **Answer the question directly.**
2. **Offer two specific options:** "I have Thursday 10:00 or Friday 14:30. Which suits you?"
3. **Share your booking link** if you use online scheduling.
4. **Confirm details** in writing: service, date, time, price range, address.
5. **Send a reminder** the day before.

Many people message late in the evening or on weekends, so a clear away message with a booking link can capture bookings while you are closed.

## Step 6: Add Click-to-WhatsApp Entry Points

Make it easy for customers to start a chat.

- **Website button or link** using a wa.me link with your number.
- **QR code** on your window, menu, flyers or receipts.
- **Google Business Profile:** add your WhatsApp link in the profile where allowed, or in the booking or appointment link field.
- **Instagram and Facebook:** add the WhatsApp button to your page or bio.
- **Click-to-WhatsApp ads** on Facebook and Instagram, which open a chat when tapped. See our [Meta Ads for local businesses guide](/blog/using-meta-ads-for-local-businesses) for how to run them.

## Step 7: Respect Consent and Rules

Messaging rules matter, and breaking them can get your number blocked.

- **Only message people who have contacted you or opted in.** Do not send unsolicited bulk messages.
- **Use broadcast lists carefully.** On the free app, broadcasts only reach contacts who have saved your number.
- **Make opting out easy** and respect it straight away.
- **Follow local privacy law,** such as GDPR in the UK and EU, and the Privacy Act in Australia. Avoid asking for sensitive data over chat.
- **Follow WhatsApp's Business and Commerce policies,** and check their current terms.

## Step 8: Automate Carefully

Once the basics work, automation can help.

- **Chatbots and AI assistants** can answer FAQs and collect booking details, through the Platform with providers such as Twilio, Respond.io or Wati. Always give a path to a human.
- **Automated reminders** reduce no-shows, using approved message templates.
- **Integrations** can send new enquiries to your CRM or calendar.

## What to Track

- **Number of new enquiries per week**
- **Reply time during opening hours**
- **How many chats turn into bookings** (use labels to count)
- **No-show rate** before and after reminders
- **Where chats come from** (website, QR code, Instagram, ads)

Keep it simple: a weekly tally in a spreadsheet is enough to see whether WhatsApp pays off.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Mixing personal and business chats on one number
- Replying slowly, then wondering why people booked elsewhere
- Sending marketing blasts without consent
- Having no booking link, so conversations drag on
- Forgetting to update hours and holiday closures

## FAQ

**Q: Is WhatsApp Business free?**
A: The WhatsApp Business app is free to download and use. The Business Platform (API) used by larger teams is paid through providers and charged by conversation or message type, so check current rates.

**Q: Can I use my personal number for WhatsApp Business?**
A: You can, but a separate business number is better. It keeps personal and business conversations apart and makes it easier to hand the number to a colleague later.

**Q: Can I send promotions to all my customers?**
A: Only to people who have opted in. On the free app, use broadcast lists sparingly and respect opt-outs. For larger-scale messaging you need the Platform and approved templates.

**Q: Does WhatsApp replace email or SMS?**
A: Not usually. It is one channel among several. Many businesses use WhatsApp for conversations, email for newsletters and SMS for reminders to customers who do not use WhatsApp.
