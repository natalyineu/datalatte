To set up a Performance Max campaign, create a new campaign in Google Ads, choose a goal (Sales, Leads, Website traffic or Local store visits and promotions), pick a budget, then fill an asset group with headlines, descriptions, images and a logo. Google then serves ads across Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps from that one campaign. Set up conversion tracking first, because the campaign optimises toward the conversions you tell it to count.

This guide follows Google's current help pages, which we opened on 25 October 2026. Where Google does not publish a number, such as a dollar minimum budget, we say so.

## What Is a Performance Max Campaign?

Google describes Performance Max as a goal-based campaign type that runs ads across all of Google's inventory from one campaign. It is meant to complement keyword-based Search campaigns. You supply assets (text, images, videos), bidding goals and settings, and Google's AI combines them and optimises toward your conversion goals.

Google also lets you add controls: brand exclusions, negative keywords, audience signals and content suitability filters. Audience signals are there to speed up learning, not to restrict who sees the ad. For a plain-language overview, see our [Performance Max guide for local business](/blog/google-performance-max-for-local-business-complete-guide-2026).

## What Should You Do Before You Start?

Check three things before you click "New campaign".

1. **Conversion tracking works.** Decide what counts: a booking form, a call, a store visit. Google creates a default call conversion called "Call from ads" when call reporting is on, and counting calls can affect automated bids.
2. **Your Business Profile is verified.** Store goals need your store locations, linked from a Business Profile or affiliate locations.
3. **The business name is clean.** Google says the name must match your domain or legally verified business name, with no extra symbols or promotional text, and be 25 characters or fewer.

## How Do You Create the Campaign?

Google's help page lists the objectives that support Performance Max: Sales, Leads, Website traffic, and Local store visits and promotions. You can also create a campaign without a goal. Choose the one that matches your main conversion.

1. In Google Ads, select New campaign and pick your objective.
2. Choose Performance Max as the campaign type.
3. Confirm the conversion goals.
4. Set a budget.
5. Build the asset group.
6. Review and publish.

On budget, Google's budget panel offers up to three daily suggestions (Low, Medium, High) and says the Low level is the minimum recommended. The pages we read give no fixed dollar minimum, so older claims of a set daily amount are not from Google. Read the suggestion in your own account.

## What Assets Does Performance Max Need?

Google publishes asset specifications for Performance Max. The main ones:

| Asset | Required | Google's limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headlines | Yes | 30 characters each; recommended 11 or more; minimum 3, maximum 15 |
| Long headlines | Yes | 90 characters; minimum 1, maximum 5 |
| Descriptions | Yes | 90 characters; minimum 2, maximum 5 |
| Landscape image | Yes | 1.91:1, 1200x628 recommended; minimum 600x314 |
| Square image | Yes | 1:1, 1200x1200 recommended; minimum 300x300 |
| Square logo | Yes | 1:1, 1200x1200 recommended; minimum 128x128 |
| Video | No | 10 seconds or longer; Google may auto-generate one if you add none |

All images are .jpg or .png, up to 5 MB. Google recommends at least four landscape and four square images. If you skip video, Google may generate one from your asset group. Upload your own in all three orientations if you want control.

## How Do You Set Up Store Goals for a Local Business?

Choose the objective Local store visits and promotions, then link the locations you want to promote. Google lists four goal types here: store visits, store sales, contacts (Google hosted) and directions (Google hosted). You can pick store visits or store sales, not both.

Eligibility matters. Store visits need an account that is already active and eligible for store visits. Google recommends at least 10 business locations for store visits or store sales, and at least 5 for local actions such as contacts and direction clicks. These are recommendations, so a single shop may still test local actions. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) helps with the locations side.

Google also says to avoid geographic targets, including radius targets, when you use location assets, because targeting comes from your locations. You can still exclude areas.

## How Do You Control Where Performance Max Shows?

Performance Max gives you fewer levers than Search, but they exist. Google's August 2025 update lists campaign-level negative keywords for Search and Shopping inventory, shared negative keyword lists, search terms reporting for all Performance Max campaigns, and up to 50 search themes per asset group.

Also note two priority rules from Google. A Search campaign with an exact match keyword is prioritised over Performance Max for that query. And Final URL expansion can send people to a different page and generate dynamic headlines, so you are responsible for reviewing those pages and assets. Review the generated assets and remove any you do not want.

See our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) for what to block, and [Performance Max vs standard Google Ads](/blog/performance-max-vs-standard-google-ads-which-should-you-run) to decide whether to run both.

## How Long Until You Can Judge Results?

Google's learning period page says Learning status never lasts more than 7 days, and that it can take 1 to 2 conversion cycles for performance to stabilise. A new bid strategy, a change to the campaigns in it, or a major change to the conversion goals starts Learning. Budget, target, creative and targeting changes do not.

For store goals, Google recommends running at least 30 days and waiting for 1 to 2 complete conversion cycles before judging or changing targets. Google does not publish a six-week learning rule, so do not plan around one. If your customers take two weeks to book, wait at least that long.

## What Does a Test Look Like?

All numbers here are **assumptions** for illustration. A cafe with one location sets up Performance Max with the goal Local store visits and promotions, if eligible, or with a Leads goal tracking call clicks if not. It assumes a 30-day test and a daily budget taken from Google's Low suggestion (assumption: $20).

| | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Daily budget | $20 |
| Test length | 30 days |
| Spend over the test | about $600 |
| Conversion value set per call (Google's example style) | $10 |
| Decision rule | Keep if cost per call is below what a new customer is worth |

Set a conversion value for each goal, as Google suggests, so bidding can tell a call from a store visit. Then compare results with your other campaigns that use the same conversion action.

## FAQ

### What is the minimum budget for Performance Max?

Google does not publish a fixed dollar minimum on the help pages we read. The campaign budget panel shows up to three daily suggestions, and Google says the Low one is the minimum recommended.

### How long is the Performance Max learning period?

Google says Learning status never lasts more than 7 days and that performance can take 1 to 2 conversion cycles to stabilise. It does not state a six-week period.

### Can a small local shop use Performance Max?

Yes, but store visits goals are recommended for accounts with 10 or more locations, and local actions for 5 or more. A single shop can test a Leads goal with call and form conversions.

### Does Performance Max replace Search campaigns?

No. Google positions it as a complement to keyword-based Search. An exact match keyword in a Search campaign takes priority over Performance Max.

### Can I add negative keywords?

Yes. Google lists campaign-level negative keywords for Performance Max, plus shared lists that apply to several campaigns.
