Choose website colors by function, not mood. Use at most three: a dark text color, a main brand color and one accent for buttons. Then check contrast: WCAG requires a ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. Nielsen Norman Group advises limiting a palette to three colors, and finds little proof that any color has a universal emotional effect.

This guide is for owners picking or fixing a site palette for a cafe, salon, studio or trade business. It gives a method, a worked contrast check and common mistakes. Statements about what colors mean are kept out unless a source supports them.

## Does Color Psychology Work for Websites?

Less than most guides claim. Nielsen Norman Group says there is little real research proving a universal effect of a particular color on emotions, and that interpretation varies from culture to culture. A claim such as "blue builds trust" is a design habit, not a measured law.

So pick colors that fit your brand and your premises, then test them with real customers. A cafe may borrow tones from its interior. A trades business may borrow from its van and uniforms. Consistency across site, sign and social profiles helps people recognise you. ## How Many Colors Should a Local Site Use?

Three roles are enough. NN/g advises limiting the palette to three colors and using each consistently, because consistency helps users understand what color means on the page.

| Role | Used for | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text and background | Body copy, page background | Highest contrast on the site |
| Brand color | Header, headings, links | Same color for the same job everywhere |
| Accent | Main button only, such as Book or Call | Do not reuse for decoration |

Grey tones for borders and secondary text do not count as extra brand colors, but they must also pass contrast. The 60-30-10 split often quoted online is a rule of thumb from interior design, and none of the sources checked here validate it for websites.

## What Contrast Do You Need for Readable Text?

WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.3 requires a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for text, and at least 3:1 for large text. Large means at least 18 point, or 14 point bold. The W3C notes that the ratio is not rounded, so 4.499:1 fails.

Light brand colors usually fail on white backgrounds. Test every pair of text and background, including button labels and footer links. The W3C also lists, as part of accessible content, that color should not be the only way to convey information, so mark links with underlines, not only a color change. ## A Worked Example: Checking a Cafe Palette

The colors below are **assumptions** chosen for illustration. Ratios were calculated with the WCAG relative luminance formula.

| Pair (assumption) | Ratio | Normal text (4.5:1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coffee brown #6F4E37 text on white | 7.44:1 | Pass |
| Caramel #C8A27A text on white | 2.36:1 | Fail |
| White text on caramel #C8A27A button | 2.36:1 | Fail |
| White text on darker caramel #8A5A33 button | 5.85:1 | Pass |
| Dark brown #3B2A20 on cream #F5EBDD | 11.58:1 | Pass |

The caramel looks warm but fails as text or as a button background with white labels. Darkening it to #8A5A33 keeps the warm tone and passes. Use a free contrast checker to verify your own hex codes, and test on a phone outdoors.

## How Do You Apply the Palette to the Site?

Define the three colors once, as variables in your builder or theme, and use them only by role. Apply the accent color to the main call to action so it stands out, as in our [hero section examples](/blog/website-hero-section-examples-local-business). Check the same palette on the Google Business Profile photos, menus and signage so the brand reads the same everywhere.

Test before launch. NN/g recommends checking legibility and accessibility related to both contrast and color blindness. Ask five customers or staff to find the booking button within five seconds, on a phone. If someone struggles, change the layout or contrast first, not the hue. ## FAQ

### What are the best colors for a local business website?

There is no verified best color. Choose brand colors that match your premises and audience, limit them to three, and make sure text meets the 4.5:1 contrast ratio.

### Does blue build trust?

Not as a rule. NN/g found little research proving universal emotional effects of specific colors, and says meanings differ across cultures.

### Can I use a light brand color for buttons?

Yes if the label contrast passes. White on a light caramel failed at 2.36:1 in the example, so darken the button or use dark text on it.

### Should I use dark mode?

Optional. If you offer it, check contrast again, because a palette that passes on white may fail on black.

### How do I test my colors?

Enter foreground and background hex codes into a contrast checker, review the site on a phone, and ask a few real customers to complete a task.
