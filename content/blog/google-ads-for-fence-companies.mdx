To get more residential and commercial jobs from Google Ads, a fence company should run Search campaigns on high-intent keywords like "fence installation near me", target only the towns it actually serves, send clicks to a dedicated quote page, and track phone calls and form fills as conversions. Most fence companies can start at roughly $20 to $60 a day, though costs vary a lot by city.

Fencing is a good fit for Google Ads because customers search at the moment they decide to buy: after a storm, before getting a dog, when selling a home, or when a commercial site needs securing. Here is how to set it up so the budget goes to real enquiries.

## Why Google Ads Suit Fence Companies

People looking for a fence usually want quotes quickly and compare a few local companies. Google Ads puts you at the top of the results exactly when they search. Compared with broad advertising, you pay when someone clicks, and you can restrict spend to the neighborhoods you serve.

Typical advantages:

- **High intent:** "fence repair near me" is closer to a purchase than a social media scroll.
- **Control over geography:** you can exclude areas outside your service radius.
- **Measurable:** with call and form tracking, you can see which keywords bring jobs.
- **Seasonal flexibility:** increase spend in spring and after storms, reduce in slow months.

The trade-offs: clicks can be expensive, and weak setup wastes budget fast.

## Step 1: Map Your Services to Keywords

Start from how people search, not from how you describe your work. Group keywords by service so each ad matches the search.

| Service group | Example keyword themes |
|---|---|
| Residential installation | wood fence installation, privacy fence contractor, vinyl fence installer, chain link fence cost |
| Repair | fence repair near me, storm damage fence repair, replace fence panels |
| Gates | driveway gate installation, automatic gate repair |
| Commercial | commercial fence installation, security fencing contractor, perimeter fencing |
| Specialty | pool fence installer, aluminum fence, horse fence |

Use the Google Keyword Planner (free inside Google Ads) to see relative volume and estimated cost in your area. Cost per click for home-services keywords is typically higher than for cafes or salons, so do not be surprised by costs of several dollars per click or more in competitive cities.

### Negative keywords

Add negatives from day one to stop wasted clicks. Common examples:

- "diy", "how to build", "plans", "free"
- "jobs", "careers", "salary"
- "rental" (unless you offer it)
- "used", "second hand"
- Words for products you do not sell, such as "electric fence for cattle"

Review your search terms report weekly and add new negatives.

## Step 2: Structure the Campaign

Keep it simple. A tidy structure beats a large messy one.

- **One campaign for residential, one for commercial.** The audience, budget and message differ.
- **Ad groups by service:** installation, repair, gates, and so on.
- **Match types:** start with phrase and exact match for control. Broad match can work with strong conversion tracking but needs closer monitoring.
- **Location targeting:** choose "people in or regularly in" your target locations, and exclude areas you do not serve.
- **Ad schedule:** show ads when you can answer the phone. Calls missed after hours are often lost to the next company on the list.

## Step 3: Write Ads That Qualify and Convince

Your ad should tell the searcher they have found the right type of company.

Include:

- The service and area ("Wood Fence Installation in [Town]")
- A reason to choose you (licensed and insured if true, years in business, workmanship warranty)
- A clear action ("Free on-site quote", "Call for same-week estimates")
- Honest pricing hints only if you can stand behind them

Use assets (formerly extensions) to widen your ad: call, location, sitelinks to your service pages, and callouts such as "Free estimates" or "Financing available" if accurate. See our [Google Ads extensions guide](/blog/google-ads-extensions-guide).

## Step 4: Build a Landing Page That Converts

Do not send paid traffic to a generic homepage. Create a page per main service, with:

- A headline that matches the search ("Vinyl Fence Installation in [Town]")
- A short form (name, phone, address or postcode, fence type) and a tap-to-call button
- Photos of your actual installs, not stock images
- Material and style options, with honest price ranges or what affects the price
- License and insurance details, and a few real customer reviews
- What happens after they enquire, such as a site visit within a certain number of days

Check it on a phone, because many searches for local services happen there. Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) cover more.

## Step 5: Track Calls and Forms

Without conversion tracking, you are guessing. Set up:

- **Form submissions** as a conversion event.
- **Click-to-call** from the ad and website, plus call tracking with a call length threshold so you count real conversations.
- **Offline results:** log which leads became quotes and jobs, and import or note this back into your reporting.

Our [Google Ads conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) walks through the setup. If calls are your main source of leads, also read about [call-only campaigns](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns).

## Step 6: Choose a Budget and Bidding Approach

Budgets vary by market. As a rough guide, many local contractors start between $600 and $1,800 a month and adjust once they see cost per lead.

- **Start with Maximize clicks or manual control** if you have no conversion data, with a cost-per-click cap.
- **Move to Maximize conversions, then target CPA** once you have a steady flow of tracked leads.
- **Judge by cost per lead and cost per job**, not clicks. A $120 lead is fine if your average fence job is worth thousands and you close a fair share of quotes.

For deeper reading, see our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business). Similar approaches apply to other trades, as in our articles on [Google Ads for roofers](/blog/google-ads-for-roofers) and [contractors](/blog/google-ads-for-contractors).

## Step 7: Optimize Every Week

A short weekly routine keeps campaigns healthy:

1. Check the search terms report and add negative keywords.
2. Pause keywords with lots of spend and no leads after a fair test period.
3. Compare ad variations and keep the clearer ones.
4. Review lead quality with your sales team.
5. Adjust budgets for the season, such as spring build season or storm weeks.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do Google Ads cost for a fence company?**
A: It varies by city and season. Many fence companies start at roughly $600 to $1,800 a month, with cost per click often in the several-dollar range in competitive areas. Track cost per lead and per job to decide whether it pays.

**Q: Should I target residential and commercial together?**
A: Usually it is better to run separate campaigns, because the keywords, budgets and landing pages differ. Commercial jobs can be larger but fewer, so give them their own tracking.

**Q: What keywords work best for fence companies?**
A: Service-plus-location searches, such as "wood fence installation [town]" and "fence repair near me", usually show strong intent. Add negative keywords to filter out DIY and job searches.

**Q: Are Google Local Services Ads worth it for fencing?**
A: They can be, where available for your trade and area, since they show a "Google guaranteed" or verified badge and you pay per lead. Availability varies, so check inside your Google Ads account.
