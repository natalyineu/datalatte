WordStream and LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put the average Home and Home Improvement click at $8.33. At that price a $1,500 month buys about 180 clicks. If 8% become leads, 6 in 10 leads get a site visit and 1 in 4 quotes is signed, you win roughly 2 fence jobs and each costs about $690 in ads. The lead, visit and close rates are my assumptions, not benchmarks. On a $4,500 fence that can be a good deal. On a $600 repair it is not, so the first job is knowing which one you are selling.

This guide is for residential and commercial fence companies in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers what clicks and leads tend to cost, how to structure a Search campaign by service, where Local Services Ads fit, how to track calls and quote forms, and the math from ad budget to signed jobs. Dollar figures are US benchmarks; for pounds or Australian and Canadian dollars, run the same math with your local click prices.

## What the Benchmarks Say, and What They Do Not

There is no public fence-specific benchmark we could verify. The nearest solid source is the WordStream and LocaliQ 2026 Google Ads Benchmarks (13,000+ US search campaigns, April 2025 to March 2026), as summarised by [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/garage-doors-and-windows-google-ads-statistics). Webtonic gives the all-industry CPC as $5.42, so home improvement runs well above average. It also reports a narrower Doors and Windows Sales line from the 2025 WordStream and LocaliQ home services benchmark: $200.34 per lead at a 4.41% conversion rate. That is another installed exterior product and the closest published proxy, but it is not fencing.

Two cautions. These are averages across many advertisers, and a "conversion" is whatever each advertiser counted: a call, a form, a click on a phone number. So a $91 lead is not a $91 quote request. And your city matters more than the category: a click for "fence installation" in a dense metro costs more than in a small town. Use the benchmarks to sanity-check month one, not to set a target.

## Pick the Right Campaign Type

**Start with Search.** Someone typing "vinyl fence installation near me" is close to asking for a quote. Search catches that. Run one campaign for residential and a separate one for commercial, because the budgets, keywords and landing pages differ.

**Test Local Services Ads beside it.** Google's help page [Getting started with Local Services Ads](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841) lists "Fencing services" as an eligible category, with no state or country note on that entry. The page also says the ads are available only for certain categories, so confirm in your own country and area on the signup page. Per Google's [how leads work page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435), you pay per valid lead rather than per click, a missed call is charged if the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds, and leads found to be invalid or low quality are not charged. Prices vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding mode.

For how this compares with other trades, see [Google Ads for window companies](/blog/google-ads-for-window-companies) and our [Local Services Ads guide](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide).

## Set Up the Search Campaign

### 1. Location: only where your crew can reach

Draw targeting around the towns or postcodes you can actually quote in a working day. Google's [location options help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038) says "presence or interest" is the default and recommended option for Search, while "presence only" suits cases where you want to exclude people who are interested but located elsewhere. For an installer, I recommend presence only, which is my advice and not a Google rule, because a clicker in another county is a wasted $8. The page also says exclusions use presence.

### 2. Keywords by service

Google's [match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) calls broad match the default and says "it's critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match". Phrase match reaches searches that include the meaning of your keyword, and exact match gives the most control and the fewest searches. With a small budget and little conversion data, start with phrase and exact.

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wood and privacy | "wood fence installation", "privacy fence contractor", [cedar fence your town] | Phrase + exact |
| Vinyl and aluminum | "vinyl fence installer", "aluminum fence installation" | Phrase + exact |
| Chain link | "chain link fence installation", "chain link fence cost" | Phrase |
| Repair | "fence repair near me", "storm damaged fence repair", "replace fence panels" | Phrase + exact |
| Gates | "driveway gate installation", "automatic gate repair" | Phrase |
| Commercial (own campaign) | "commercial fence contractor", "security fencing", "perimeter fencing" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your company name] | Exact |

Keep each ad group to a handful of tightly related keywords so the ad can echo the search.

### 3. Negative keywords

Fence searches attract plenty of people who will never hire you. Add on day one: `diy`, `how to build`, `plans`, `free`, `jobs`, `careers`, `salary`, `used`, `second hand`, `for sale`, `rental`, `electric fence`, `invisible fence`, `cattle`, `youtube`, `lowes`, `home depot`. Remove any that match what you sell. Then read the search terms report weekly. Our negative keywords guide shows how to apply lists at campaign level.

### 4. Ads that qualify the click

Responsive search ads let you supply multiple headlines and descriptions; fill them with real specifics: `Vinyl Fence Installation in [Town]`, `Free On-Site Quote`, `Licensed and Insured` (only if true and you can show the licence number), `Workmanship Warranty` (state the length if you have one), `Storm Damage Repairs`. Add a call asset, a location asset, and sitelinks to Gallery, Fence Types, Pricing Guide and Request a Quote. Our [extensions guide](/blog/google-ads-extensions-guide) has the details.

State a price signal only if you can stand behind it, such as "Typical installs from $X per linear foot". It filters out people who are not serious, which saves you quote visits.

## Landing Page: One Service, One Action

Do not send paid clicks to your home page. Build a page per main service with a headline that matches the search, a short form (name, phone, address or postcode, fence type, approximate length), a tap-to-call button, photos of your own installs, what drives the price (material, height, gates, slope, removal of the old fence), your licence and insurance details, a few real reviews, and what happens next ("a site visit within X days"). Test it on a phone. Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) give a checklist.

## Track Calls and Forms, Then Close the Loop

Fence enquiries arrive by phone and form, so set up three conversions: quote form submit, calls from the ad, and calls from the website. Google's [website call conversions help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) says a call counts only if it lasts longer than a minimum length you set, which filters misdials, and gives no default. It also says the phone snippet swaps your page number for a Google forwarding number, and that the default tag tracks one number per page. Choose a length that matches a real quote request. The 30 to 60 seconds often suggested is my starting point, not Google's. See call tracking for local business and the conversion tracking setup guide.

Then do the one thing platforms cannot: log every lead as quoted, signed or lost, with the source. A lead that never becomes a job is the most expensive thing in your account.

## Budget, Bidding and Seasonality

There is no official budget for a fence company, and we found no verified figure to quote. Start from the math below instead.

For bidding, a common path is to begin with manual control or Maximize clicks with a CPC cap while you have no data, then move to Maximize conversions and later a target CPA once you have a steady flow of tracked leads. That is general practice, not a Google requirement.

On seasonality, Google's [seasonality adjustments help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10369906) says they suit short events of roughly 1 to 7 days, may work less well beyond 14 days, and are for use with Smart Bidding when you expect a major conversion-rate change. They are not a tool for "spring season". For a longer build season, simply raise the budget when your crew has capacity and lower it when the schedule is full. Do not pay for leads you cannot serve. Our [chimney sweep guide](/blog/google-ads-for-chimney-sweep) covers a similarly seasonal trade.

## The Math

A $1,500 monthly budget. The Base CPC and conversion rate are the Home and Home Improvement figures above (Tough uses the Doors and Windows 4.41% conversion rate). Everything else is an **assumption** for illustration; replace it with your own after a month.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget | $1,500 | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Cost per click | $12.00 (assumption) | $8.33 (benchmark) | $6.50 (assumption) |
| Clicks | 125 | 180 | 231 |
| Click to lead | 4.41% (benchmark) | 8.05% (benchmark) | 8.05% |
| Leads | 5.5 | 14.5 | 18.6 |
| Cost per lead | $272 | $103 | $81 |
| Lead to site visit (assumption) | 50% | 60% | 70% |
| Quote to signed job (assumption) | 20% | 25% | 30% |
| Signed jobs | 0.55 | 2.2 | 3.9 |
| Ad cost per job | $2,720 | $690 | $385 |
| Average job value (assumption) | $4,500 | $4,500 | $4,500 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 30% | 30% | 30% |
| Ad cost as share of gross profit per job ($1,350) | 201% | 51% | 29% |

How to use it:

- **Your maximum click price.** Max CPC = most you will pay per job x lead rate x visit rate x close rate. If you will pay $700 per signed job, at 8.05%, 60% and 25% your ceiling is $700 x 0.0805 x 0.60 x 0.25 = $8.45 a click.
- **The Strong column is better than any benchmark here, so do not plan around it.**
- **Close rate is the biggest dial.** Going from 20% to 30% close cuts cost per job by a third without touching the ad account. That is quote speed, follow-up and price clarity.
- **Repairs change everything.** At a $600 repair, the Base cost of $690 per job loses money. Bid harder on installs and treat repairs as a lower bid or Local Services Ads job.

## Review Weekly

1. Read the search terms report and add negatives.
2. Pause keywords that spent about twice your target cost per lead with none.
3. Test one new headline every two weeks.
4. Check which hours calls go unanswered and match ad hours to when someone picks up.
5. Review lead quality with whoever quotes the jobs.

Pair ads with a complete Business Profile and photo-rich reviews after each job, because buyers check them after clicking. Similar logic applies to [Google Ads for contractors](/blog/google-ads-for-contractors), roofers and landscaping.

## Do This Today

1. List the postcodes or zip codes you can quote in one working day and set a presence-only radius around them.
2. Decide the most you will pay for one signed job and compute your maximum CPC with the formula above.
3. Build one residential Search campaign with the ad groups above and the negative list. Keep commercial separate.
4. Create one landing page per main service, then set up form, ad-call and website-call conversions before turning ads on.
5. Check Local Services Ads eligibility for your area, and start a spreadsheet logging every lead as quoted, signed or lost.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do Google Ads cost for a fence company?**
A: There is no official figure. At the 2026 WordStream and LocaliQ Home and Home Improvement average of $8.33 per click, $1,500 buys about 180 clicks. Work backwards from what a signed job is worth to you.

**Q: Should I target residential and commercial together?**
A: Usually not. The keywords, budgets and landing pages differ, so separate campaigns keep tracking clear. Commercial jobs can be larger but fewer.

**Q: Are Local Services Ads worth it for fencing?**
A: Google lists "Fencing services" as an eligible category and charges per valid lead, but availability and prices vary by location. Test them beside Search and compare cost per signed job.

**Q: What keywords work best?**
A: Service plus location searches, such as "wood fence installation [town]" and "fence repair near me", show the clearest intent. Judge them by cost per signed job, not by cost per click.

**Q: How long before ads bring in jobs?**
A: Calls can start within days, but quotes and signatures take longer. Give it four to six weeks, my rule of thumb, before judging cost per job.
