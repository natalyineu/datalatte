One average puts a home and home improvement click at $7.85, and home-services cost per lead ranges from $15 to $85+ depending on local competition. At $7.85, a $1,000 monthly budget buys about 127 clicks. Judge the spend by calls answered and arrangements made, not clicks.

This guide is general information for funeral homes and cremation providers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It is not legal advice. Advertising and price-disclosure rules for funeral providers differ by country and change over time.

## Is there a funeral-specific benchmark for cost per click?

No. Every figure above is a proxy from a neighbouring business, and each comes from a vendor or a secondary summary. None should be read as a funeral average. Lead Nurture Close reports, citing Simply Be Found and Ryan Goering of BaaDigi (2025), an average cost per click of $7.85 for home and home improvement businesses.

Ruban KT, a contractor-focused agency, says keywords such as "home renovation contractor" or "commercial builders near me" can cost $8 to $15 per click. Funeral searches are different from renovation searches, so these numbers are a starting range to test, not a forecast.

Run a two-week test with a fixed daily budget. Your town, your competitors and your exact keywords will move the price, and the search terms report will show what is actually triggering your ads.

## Which campaign type should a funeral home start with?

Search is the logical starting point. Ads appear when someone types a relevant query, so you respond to a stated need rather than interrupting someone who is not looking. One Search campaign for your service area, with separate ad groups for each type of family, keeps the messages clear.

Performance Max and Display reach people who have not searched, which means less control over where your name appears. For a business whose customers are grieving, that trade-off is hard to justify on a small budget. Revisit broader formats once you have call data of your own.

For a wider view of how two major platforms compare for local firms, see [Amazon Ads versus Google Ads for local businesses](/blog/amazon-ads-vs-google-ads-local-business).

## How should keywords be grouped around the family's situation?

Families arrive in three situations: an immediate need, a plan made ahead of time, or a search for information. Mixing them in one ad group sends someone who has just been bereaved to a page written for a pre-need shopper. Group keywords so each ad matches the page it lands on.

| Group | Example keywords | Landing page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate need | "funeral home near me", "funeral directors [town]", "cremation [town]" | Contact page, phone number at the top |
| Cremation | "direct cremation [town]", "cremation services" | Cremation options and price list |
| Burial and services | "burial services [town]", "memorial service planning" | Services overview |
| Pre-need | "pre-need funeral planning", "funeral pre-arrangement" | Planning guide and enquiry form |

Phrase and exact match give more control over what triggers an ad than broad match does, which suits a sensitive category on a small budget. Add negative keywords from the first day: jobs, careers, school, course, how to become, salary, free, diy, and obituary unless you run an obituary page.

## What should ad copy say, and what should it leave out?

Headlines should state what you offer and where: "Funeral and Cremation in [Town]", "Prices Listed on Our Website", and "Call Us Any Time" only if someone answers at any hour. A description might read: "A funeral director will answer your call. Our price list is online." Use "Family-Owned Since [Year]" only if it is true.

Leave out urgency, countdowns, discounts and any line suggesting a family will suffer if they wait. Those lines pressure people at their most vulnerable and do not fit the work.

Drafting tools can produce a first version quickly. [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) show how to request drafts, but a person should check every line for tone and accuracy before it goes live.

## Do price rules affect your landing page?

Regulators in each country set their own rules on what funeral providers must disclose about prices. Check the current guidance from your national or state regulator before you publish anything. The sources behind this guide do not cover those rules in detail.

As a practical matter, a page that hides prices is a poor destination for ads. Publish your price list on the website, one tap from the home page, and make the phone number tap-to-call. The landing page should also include:

- A plain statement of whether a person answers the phone, and when.
- A short explanation of what happens first after a call.
- Real photographs of the staff and the building.
- Prices, or a clear link to them, above the fold.
- For pre-need, a short form with phone or email as options.

## How do you track and follow up calls without breaking phone rules?

Most valuable contacts happen by phone, so track them. Set a minimum call length for what counts as a conversion, so that brief wrong-number calls do not inflate your results. Thirty to sixty seconds is a reasonable starting point, but test it against your own call logs.

If your staff phone prospects about pre-need plans, the rules are strict. US FCC rules prohibit telemarketing calls to a home before 8 a.m. and after 9 p.m., and telemarketers must comply immediately with any do-not-call request. Prerecorded telemarketing calls need prior written consent, which can be given through a website form or a telephone keypress.

Missed calls cost the most. On the question of automated answering versus a person picking up, see [AI versus human marketing for local businesses](/blog/ai-vs-human-marketing-local-business-honest-comparison).

## What does a $1,000 month look like in numbers?

This worked example uses one budget and two cost-per-click scenarios. The two click costs come from the sources above. Every input marked assumption is a placeholder to replace with your own call logs.

| | Base | Tough |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Cost per click | $7.85 (home and home improvement average) | $15 (top of contractor keyword range) |
| Clicks | 127 | 67 |
| Click to enquiry rate (assumption) | 5% | 5% |
| Enquiries | 6.4 | 3.3 |
| Cost per enquiry | $156 | $300 |
| Enquiry to arrangement rate (assumption) | 25% | 25% |
| Arrangements | 1.6 | 0.83 |
| Ad cost per arrangement | $625 | $1,200 |
| Revenue per arrangement (assumption) | $5,000 | $5,000 |
| Ad cost as share of revenue | 12.5% | 24% |

At these inputs, a $1,000 month produces fewer than two arrangements, so the enquiry-to-arrangement rate matters more than the click price. Raising that rate from 25% to 35% on the base clicks lifts arrangements to about 2.2 and cuts the ad cost per arrangement to roughly $450. Revenue here is not profit. Pre-need plans pay out later, so count them as enquiries and plans signed rather than same-month revenue.

## Does event marketing belong alongside paid search?

Paid search captures people who are already looking. Community presence reaches people before they need a funeral director, which suits a business whose customers come in at unpredictable moments. ## FAQ

### Are funeral home Google Ads allowed?

Check Google's current advertising policies for sensitive topics before you launch. They change over time and differ by market, so do not rely on a summary, including this one. Read the version that applies to your country.

### How much should a funeral home spend?

There is no funeral benchmark to set the figure. A modest local test, such as the $1,000 monthly example above, lets you judge the return by calls answered and arrangements made rather than by clicks.

### Should I bid on competitor names?

That is a judgement call. Bidding on competitor names can sit poorly with the tone of the work. Most funeral homes are better served by bidding on the services families actually search for.

### Should I use calls or forms?

Calls suit immediate need, when a family wants a person quickly. Forms can suit pre-need and information searches. Track both so you can see which one produces arrangements.
