Nobody publishes a reliable cost-per-click benchmark for funeral homes. WordStream and LocaliQ's 2026 report, as summarised by [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/funeral-services-google-ads-statistics), gives an all-industry average click of $5.42 and has no funeral or death-care category. At that price, $1,000 buys about 184 clicks. Treat it as a rough compass, not a promise, because your town, your competitors and your keywords will move it.

What matters more than the click price is what happens to the call. A family searching for a funeral director is usually in the worst week of their year, often on a phone, often at night. This guide is for funeral homes and cremation providers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada who want to use Google Ads without being pushy. It covers what Google's policies say about bereavement ads, how to structure a Search campaign around at-need and pre-need searches, the pricing rules that should shape your landing page, tracking calls, and the math. It is general information, not legal advice. Funeral advertising is regulated, so check your own rules.

## What Google's Policies Say About Bereavement

Read this section before you write a single ad. We checked Google's current policy pages directly.

**Bereavement is named as a sensitive interest category.** Google's "Relationship hardships" policy lists "Bereavement products or services" as an example (the list is marked non-exhaustive, and the page does not mention funeral services by name). A funeral home may well be treated as falling under this heading, though Google does not say so. Plan as if it does.

**What that restricts.** For sensitive interest categories, Google says advertisers "are unable to use advertiser-curated audiences." The pages list these as not supported: Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion and lookalike segments. They list these as still supported: in-market segments, affinity, life events, location targeting and, with conditions, custom segments. In plain terms, you cannot upload a list of past families to target or to build lookalikes. Search campaigns triggered by what someone types are the natural fit, and nothing on those pages restricts them. Google's pages also say your landing page content can affect where custom segments serve.

**Fear and pressure are a policy issue too.** Google's "Imposing negativity" policy gives as an example "Suggesting negative outcomes for users if they don't take specific actions." Copy such as "Don't leave your family with the burden" is exactly what that line describes. Our advice is the same on ethical grounds: do not use it.

**Sensitive events.** Google's "Sensitive events" policy is about large-scale events such as civil emergencies, natural disasters, public health emergencies, terrorism and mass violence. Its prohibited examples include "Using keywords related to a sensitive event to attempt to drive additional traffic." The page does not mention an individual person's death. Practically, do not bid on the name of a local disaster or tragedy. Never target a named family or an individual's name.

**Pre-need plans that include insurance.** If you sell insurance-funded plans, other Google rules (for example around financial services) may apply in your country. We have not covered them here. Read Google's current pages for your market before you advertise those plans.

## Who Is Searching, and What They Need

Three situations, three different pages:

1. **Immediate need.** A death has just happened. They want a phone number answered by a person, and reassurance.
2. **Pre-need.** Someone is planning ahead for themselves or a parent. They have time and compare providers.
3. **Information.** "What to do when someone dies," "how does cremation work." They are not choosing yet.

Mixing these in one ad group confuses searchers and Google's matching. Webtonic reports a Foresight finding that 78% of families plan to research on Google first, which is why your listing matters as much as your ads. ## Pick Campaign Types

**Search is the core.** Ads appear when someone types a relevant query. For a single location, one Search campaign with separate ad groups per situation is enough.

**Local Services Ads: check the status.** Google's US verification page lists "Funeral home" under People care, requiring a business check, an owner check, general and professional liability insurance, and state business and individual licences where applicable by local law. However, Google's help page on Local Services Ads moving into Google Ads says the first migration phase from August 2026 covers home and storefront services such as plumbing and HVAC, with further groups later in 2026 and 2027. Funeral homes are not named. Look at your own account to see what is offered in your country, and read our [Local Services Ads guide](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide).

**Performance Max and Display: skip at first.** Broader reach means less control over where your name appears. For a business whose customers are in grief, control matters.

## Build Keyword Groups Around the Situation

Google's [match type help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) defines three types. Exact match "gives the most control over who sees your ad" and reaches the fewest searches, phrase reaches searches that include the meaning of your keyword, and broad reaches related searches. Google says Smart Bidding "works best with broad match," but on a small budget in a sensitive category we recommend phrase and exact, so you see what is triggering your ads.

| Group | Example keywords | Landing page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate need | "funeral home near me", "funeral directors [town]", "cremation [town]" | Contact page, phone number at the top |
| Cremation | "direct cremation [town]", "cremation services" | Cremation options and your price list |
| Burial and services | "burial services [town]", "memorial service planning" | Services overview |
| Pre-need | "pre-need funeral planning", "funeral pre-arrangement" | Planning guide and an enquiry form |

Negative keywords to add on day one: `jobs`, `careers`, `school`, `course`, `how to become`, `salary`, `diy`, `free`, `obituary` (unless you run an obituary page), and `insurance quote` if you do not sell it. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/negative-keywords-google-ads) shows how to apply them by list.

## Write Ads That Inform, Not Pressure

Plain, warm, accurate. Examples:

- Headlines: `Funeral and Cremation in [Town]`, `Family-Owned Since [Year]` (only if true), `Prices Listed on Our Website`, `Call Us Any Time` (only if someone answers)
- Description: `A funeral director will answer your call. Our price list is online. Arrange a conversation in person or by phone.`

Two rules: only say "24 hours" if a person truly answers at 3am, and review every automatically generated asset for tone. Avoid discounts and countdowns.

## Your Price List Is Part of Your Ad Strategy

In the US, the FTC's Funeral Rule requires a General Price List. The FTC page says phone callers must receive accurate price information and that you "cannot require callers to give their names, addresses, or phone numbers before you give them the requested information." It also says the Rule applies to pre-need arrangements as well as at-need ones. It prohibits claiming that the law requires embalming when it does not, and saying a casket is required for direct cremation. The FTC guidance summarised here does not address websites, so ask your compliance adviser how the Rule applies to your online content.

In the UK, the CMA's Funerals Market Investigation Order took effect with a deadline of 16 September 2021 for a Standardised Price List on your premises and website, and the press release also bans referral payments to hospitals, hospices and care homes. Secondary coverage says the list must be reachable within one click of the home page; confirm the details on the CMA's pages.

For ads, the consequence is simple. Do not send a click to a page that hides prices. Publish your list, make the phone number tap-to-call, and do not gate pricing behind a form. Australian state and Canadian provincial rules are not covered here, so check them locally.

## Landing Page

- Phone number at the top, tap-to-call, and a clear statement of when it is answered by a person.
- A short "what happens first" explanation in plain language.
- Real photos of the staff and the building.
- Pricing, or a link to it, above the fold.
- For pre-need, a short form with phone or email as options, and a line about how you will use the details. Our [Core Web Vitals guide](/blog/core-web-vitals-local-business-website) helps with mobile speed.

## Track Calls, Not Just Clicks

Most valuable contacts are calls. Google's help says a call counts as a conversion only if it lasts longer than a minimum duration you set. Pick a length that fits your enquiries (30 to 60 seconds is our suggestion, not Google's). Google's website call conversions help explains that its phone snippet replaces your number on the page with a Google forwarding number so you can see which ads led to calls. See our [call tracking guide](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) and [conversion tracking setup](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup).

If you record calls, check your local recording laws and give any required notice. Do not send call details or names into ad platforms. Missed calls matter most: set up after-hours answering or forwarding before the ads go live. A deeper look at when call-only ads help is in our [call-only campaigns guide](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns-service-businesses).

## The Math

Take a $1,000 monthly budget. Every row is an **assumption** except where noted. Replace them with your own call logs.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Cost per click (assumption; Base is the all-industry $5.42) | $8.00 | $5.42 | $4.00 |
| Clicks | 125 | 184 | 250 |
| Click to enquiry rate (assumption) | 4% | 6% | 8% |
| Enquiries | 5 | 11 | 20 |
| Enquiry to arrangement rate (assumption) | 15% | 25% | 35% |
| Arrangements | 0.75 | 2.8 | 7 |
| Ad cost per arrangement | $1,333 | $361 | $143 |
| Revenue per arrangement (reference: NFDA median cremation with services and urn, $6,280) | $6,280 | $6,280 | $6,280 |
| Ad cost as share of revenue | 21% | 5.7% | 2.3% |

How to read it: the revenue row uses a US median reported by Webtonic from the NFDA study, so substitute your own average arrangement. It is revenue, not profit. Pre-need plans pay out later, so measure them as enquiries and plans signed, not as same-month revenue.

Two points follow. The answer rate is the biggest lever: going from 15% to 35% of enquiries becoming arrangements does more than any bidding tweak, and that is a phone-handling question. And a family may call two or three homes, so the first answer often wins. None of this is a reason to push. A calm, unhurried call is both the respectful approach and the one that converts.

## Do This Today

1. Read Google's Relationship hardships and Imposing negativity policy pages, then reread your draft ads and remove anything that sounds like pressure.
2. Publish your price list on your website, one tap from the home page, and make sure the phone number is tap-to-call.
3. Set up call conversions with a minimum call length, plus after-hours answering or forwarding.
4. Build one Search campaign for your service area with four ad groups (immediate need, cremation, burial, pre-need), phrase and exact match, and the negative list above.
5. Each week, read the search terms report and listen to a sample of calls, and write down what families asked that your page did not answer.

## FAQ

**Q: Are funeral home ads allowed on Google?**
A: Google's pages we checked do not ban them. Bereavement products or services are listed as a sensitive interest category, which limits audience tools such as Customer Match and lookalikes, not keyword-triggered Search ads. Rules differ by country and change, so read the current pages.

**Q: Can I remarket to people who visited my site?**
A: Google's sensitive-category pages say advertiser-curated audiences cannot be used, which would include your own data lists. Whether your account allows a particular remarketing setup depends on how Google classifies you, so check in your account.

**Q: How much should a funeral home spend?**
A: There is no funeral benchmark. Start with a modest local budget, such as the $1,000 in the math table, and judge it by calls answered and arrangements, not clicks.

**Q: Should I bid on competitor names?**
A: Our view is that it is rarely worth it and sits poorly with the tone of the work. Bid on the services families search for.

**Q: Calls or forms?**
A: Calls for immediate need. Forms are fine for pre-need and information. Track both.
