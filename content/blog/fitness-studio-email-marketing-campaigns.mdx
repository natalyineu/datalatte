A fitness studio needs about six one-off email campaigns a year on top of its automated sequences: a January join drive, an April push, a summer retention note, a September back-to-routine offer, a holiday gift or pass campaign, and a mid-year win-back. Send each to a segment, with one offer and one button.

Expect modest numbers: Benchmark Email's health and fitness average is a 19.85% open rate and a 0.95% click rate, so a 600-person list yields roughly six clicks per send.

## What should a fitness studio email calendar include?

Anchor campaigns to the moments people decide to join or return. An Anytime Fitness spokesperson told [TCB Magazine](https://tcbmag.com/new-year-new-gym-membership/) that about 13% of the chain's annual new memberships are added in January against 7% to 8% in an average month, with further spikes in April and September. That is one company's data, so check your own join dates by month before copying it.

Suggested calendar (the January, April and September slots follow the quote above; the others are our assumptions to test):

| Month | Campaign | Segment | Single goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | New-year join drive | Prospects and lapsed | Book a trial class |
| April | Pre-summer challenge | Prospects and active | Sign up for a 4-week challenge |
| June or July | Summer attendance streak | Active | Keep attendance up |
| September | Back-to-routine offer | Lapsed and prospects | Book a restart session |
| November or December | Gift pass or holiday schedule | Active | Buy a gift pass |
| Any quiet month | Win-back | No visit in 60 to 90 days | Return for one class |

Automated welcome and reminder sequences run alongside this calendar. Our guide to [email marketing for gyms and fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-fitness-studio) covers those flows in detail.

## What results should I expect from each send?

Use industry averages as a floor, not a promise. [Benchmark Email's industry table](https://www.benchmarkemail.com/email-marketing-benchmarks/) lists health and fitness at 19.85% opens, 0.95% clicks and 0.09% unsubscribes, against 25.54%, 1.45% and 0.10% across all industries. Its page does not state the data period.

[Mailchimp's benchmarks page](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-marketing-benchmarks/) shows an all-industry average of 35.63% opens and 2.62% clicks (December 2023 data) but no fitness row. Vendors measure opens differently, so compare your numbers with one source only.

Worked example (assumption): a list of 600 people.

- Opens at 19.85%: about 119 people.
- Clicks at 0.95%: about 6 people.
- If 1 in 4 clickers books a trial class: 1 to 2 bookings per campaign.

Decision rule: if your click rate is under 0.95% after three sends, fix the offer and the button before sending more. If you are above it, test a second offer rather than a higher send frequency.

## How do I segment each campaign?

Send each campaign to a segment you can name from your booking system. Three segments cover most needs:

- **Active:** attended in the last 30 days. Send reminders, challenges and referral offers.
- **Lapsed:** no visit in 60 to 90 days. Send win-back offers.
- **Prospects:** joined the list but never booked. Send trial offers.

The 60 and 90 day lines are working definitions you can change to match your class cycle. Export last-visit dates monthly from your booking tool and tag contacts. ## What do the law and Gmail require?

Fitness studios are not exempt from marketing email rules. The FTC's [CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) requires truthful header information, non-misleading subject lines, a clear disclosure that the message is an ad, a real physical address and a clear opt-out. Opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days, and penalties can reach $53,088 per violating email.

If you send more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail users, Google's [bulk sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) add SPF, DKIM and DMARC authentication and one-click unsubscribe, and ask you to keep reported spam rates below 0.30% (recommended below 0.10%). Most single-location studios send far less, but the same hygiene helps inbox placement. These are not legal advice; check requirements for your own country, since UK and EU rules differ.

## How do I write a campaign people click?

Follow one rule: one segment, one offer, one button. Give the date and the outcome in the subject line, for example "Restart week: 3 beginner classes, Sept 8 to 12". Put the button in the first screen. Name the instructor or class so it does not read as a mass blast.

Send a test to your phone before every campaign, because many readers will open on mobile. After each send, log opens, clicks, unsubscribes and bookings in a sheet so the next campaign beats the last. ## FAQ

### How many campaigns should a fitness studio send per year?

About six one-off campaigns plus automated sequences is a workable starting point. Adjust once you see your own join and cancel dates by month.

### What is a good open rate for a fitness studio?

Benchmark Email lists 19.85% for health and fitness. Treat this as one vendor's average, not a target.

### When is the best time to promote memberships?

Anytime Fitness reports January as its peak at about 13% of annual joins, with April and September as smaller peaks. Test those windows against your own history.

### Do I need permission to email members?

CAN-SPAM does not require prior consent in the US but does require a working opt-out and an honest sender identity. Other countries require consent, so confirm your local rules.

### Should I email inactive subscribers?

Send them a win-back message, then remove people who do not respond. Keeping complaints low protects inbox placement.
