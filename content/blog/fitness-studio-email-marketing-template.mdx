A fitness studio needs three email templates before anything else: a welcome email for new sign-ups, a re-engagement nudge for members who have stopped booking, and a win-back email for members who have been gone for months. Each template has one action, and each works in any email tool. Replace the bracketed parts with your own details. Check your plan's contact count and monthly send limit before you schedule anything, and avoid sending member lists from a free Gmail account. The subject lines and copy below are starting points to test, not proven winners.

## What Should the Welcome Email Say?

Send it right after someone signs up for a free class or the newsletter. Keep one action in the email: booking the first class. A broader newsletter strategy can follow later, and the [fitness studio newsletter guide](/blog/fitness-studio-email-newsletter) covers regular sends once the welcome step works.

> Subject: Your first class at [Studio name]
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> Thanks for signing up. Your first class is [free / $X]. To book:
>
> 1. Pick a class: [link to schedule]
> 2. Arrive 10 minutes early so a coach can show you around.
> 3. Bring a water bottle. [Mats and towels available / bring your own]
>
> Questions before you come? Reply to this email and a coach will answer.
>
> [Coach name], [Studio name], [address]

Gym software vendor Gleantap reports a 50% open rate for Studio Svelte's personalized fitness email campaigns, which ran with automated marketing. That is one vendor-reported case, not a benchmark for your list.

## How Do You Re-Engage a Member Who Stopped Coming?

Send this when a member has not booked for a period you choose. Three weeks is an example, not a rule. Keep the tone helpful rather than guilty, and point to specific classes so the next step is obvious.

> Subject: Your spot is waiting, [First name]
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> You have not booked in a few weeks. This week's classes: [two or three classes with times].
>
> If your schedule changed, reply with the times that work and a coach will point you to a class that fits.
>
> [Booking link]
>
> [Coach name], [Studio name]

## What Is a Good Win-Back Email?

Send this to members who have been gone for months. Offer one clear reason to return and make it easy to opt out. Choose the offer yourself and do not discount by habit. If you run a seasonal push, the [New Year membership marketing guide](/blog/fitness-studio-new-year-membership-marketing) shows how timing can shape the offer.

> Subject: A free week if you want it
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> It has been a while. If you want to come back, here is a free week of classes: [link]. No pressure.
>
> If you do not want these emails, [unsubscribe link].
>
> [Studio name], [address]

## How Often Should the Member Sequence Send?

For new members, one retention sequence sends automated emails at three points, according to the Vertical Impression guide (secondary):

1. Day 7: welcome and tips
2. Day 30: milestone celebration
3. Day 60: encouragement that the member is building a habit, with a referral ask

For lapsed members, the Propello win-back guide suggests three to five win-back emails, sent every three to five days. The page describes these as industry averages rather than laws, so treat them as a starting point and adjust to your own booking pattern.

## How Many Emails Can Your Plan Send Each Month?

Mailchimp's Standard plan includes 500 contacts, and its monthly email send limit is 12 times your maximum contact count. The Essentials limit is 10 times, and the Premium limit is 15 times. Overages apply on Standard if either the contact or send limit is exceeded, and sending is paused when a limit is exceeded. The worked example below uses assumed inputs to show the headroom.

| Input or result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Contacts on list (assumption) | 300 |
| Emails per contact per month (assumption) | 4 |
| Planned sends per month (300 × 4) | 1,200 |
| Standard monthly send limit (12 × 300) | 3,600 |
| Headroom left (3,600 − 1,200) | 2,400 |

If the list grows to 600 contacts (assumption), the Standard plan's included 500 contacts are exceeded, and overage terms apply. Check Mailchimp's current overage terms before you grow past that number. Other email tools set their own limits, so confirm yours on its pricing page.

## Can You Send Member Emails From a Free Gmail Account?

Scrap.io reports that a free Gmail account can reach 500 recipients per rolling 24 hours, while Google Workspace accounts can send up to 2,000 messages and 10,000 recipients per day (secondary). A list of 400 contacts in Bcc would use 80% of a free account's daily quota in a single send.

For a member list, a dedicated email tool is the safer choice. Gmail suits one-to-one replies, such as answering a member who asks about a class.

## What Does Email Cost a Small Studio?

Vertical Impression's monthly budget benchmark for a small independent studio under 200 members puts email and SMS tools at $100 to $200 a month (secondary). Litmus, cited by the same guide, puts the average return at $36 for every $1 spent on email. That is a cross-industry average, not a forecast for your studio, so use it to frame the budget rather than to promise results.

## How Do You Track Templates Before They Go Live?

A campaign calendar keeps every template in one place. MarketingCoach.ie describes a campaign calendar as a single template for organizing each campaign (secondary). Boltic's editorial calendar template uses a status field of IDEA, DRAFT, EDITING and LIVE (secondary). Apply the same flow to your emails:

1. Add each template to a campaign row with its send date and segment.
2. Move the status from IDEA to DRAFT to EDITING to LIVE.
3. Check the plan's send limit before scheduling a send.

## What Legal Checks Apply Before You Send?

Email rules differ by country and region, and this guide does not cover them. Check the current consent and opt-out rules for where your members live before you send, and include a visible unsubscribe link in every message. Get advice if you email members outside the country you operate in.

## FAQ

### Do I need a specific email tool to use these templates?

No. The templates are plain text and work in any email tool. Mailchimp is used here as the example for plan limits because its pricing page publishes them. Check your own tool's limits before scheduling a send.

### How many emails can a 300-contact list send on Mailchimp Standard?

Mailchimp's monthly send limit for Standard is 12 times your maximum contact count, so 3,600 emails a month for 300 contacts. The plan includes 500 contacts, so a 300-contact list stays within the included contacts.

### Can I send member emails from a free Gmail account?

A free Gmail account can reach 500 recipients per rolling 24 hours. A 400-contact Bcc list would use 80% of that daily quota in one send, so use a dedicated email tool for member lists.

### When should a win-back sequence stop?

Propello's guidance suggests three to five win-back emails, sent every three to five days. Stop after the planned sequence if a lapsed member does not return, and keep the unsubscribe link working.
