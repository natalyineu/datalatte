A fitness studio needs three email templates first: a welcome email for new sign-ups, a nudge for members who stopped coming, and a win-back for lapsed members. Below are the three, ready to paste into Klaviyo or any email tool. Replace the bracketed parts with your own details. Klaviyo's free plan covers up to 250 active profiles and 500 email sends a month, which is enough for a small studio to start.

The benchmarks and rules are sourced at the end. The subject lines and copy are starting points to test, not proven winners.

## What Do Fitness Email Benchmarks Look Like?

MailerLite's analysis of more than 3.6 million campaigns from 181,000 accounts, from December 2024 to November 2025, puts health and fitness at a 47.81% open rate, 1.45% click rate and 0.30% unsubscribe rate. The overall averages were 43.46%, 2.09% and 0.22%. Opens look strong and clicks weak.

Clicks and replies are tied to action, so judge the templates on those, not on opens. MailerLite's data is a cross-industry vendor sample, not a gym-only one. Mailchimp's public benchmark page, last updated December 2023, has no fitness row, so it is not used here.

## Template 1: What Should the Welcome Email Say?

Send it right after sign-up for a free class or newsletter. Keep one action: book the first class.

> Subject: Your first class at [Studio name]
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> Thanks for signing up. Your first class is [free / $X]. Here is how to book it:
>
> 1. Pick a class: [link to schedule]
> 2. Arrive 10 minutes early. We will show you where everything is.
> 3. Bring a water bottle. We have mats and towels.
>
> Questions before you come? Reply to this email and a coach will answer.
>
> [Coach name], [Studio name], [address]

## Template 2: How Do You Re-Engage a Member Who Stopped Coming?

Send it when someone has not booked for a period you choose, for example three weeks. The tone is helpful, not guilty.

> Subject: Your spot is waiting, [First name]
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> We have not seen you in a few weeks. Here is what is on this week: [two or three classes with times].
>
> If your schedule changed, tell us what times work and we will point you to the right class. Reply here, it comes to me.
>
> [Booking link]
>
> [Coach name]

## Template 3: What Is a Good Win-Back Email?

Send it to members who have been gone for months. Offer one clear reason to return and make it easy to opt out.

> Subject: A free week if you want it
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> It has been a while. If you would like to try us again, here is a free week of classes: [link]. No pressure.
>
> If you do not want these emails, [unsubscribe link] and we will stop.
>
> [Studio name], [address]

The offer is yours to choose. Do not discount by habit; our [fitness studio email campaign plan](/blog/fitness-studio-email-marketing-campaigns) shows how to space them through the year.

## How Do You Set These Up as Klaviyo Flows?

Klaviyo describes two kinds of flow triggers. Metric-triggered flows start when a customer completes an action, such as an order. List, segment and date-property flows start from profile information instead of events. A welcome email fits a list-added trigger, and a win-back can use a segment such as members without a booking in a set number of days, if your booking tool sends that data to Klaviyo.

That last point matters: Klaviyo needs the data. Check what your booking software integrates with before you plan the flow. For a simpler start, send the three templates manually to saved segments. ## What Does the Law Require?

For US recipients, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide lists requirements including no false header information, no deceptive subject lines, identifying the message as an ad, and giving your location. You must also tell recipients how to opt out and honor opt-outs within 10 business days. The FTC says each violating email can carry penalties of up to $53,088.

In the UK and EU, other rules apply, including consent requirements, and those were not reviewed for this guide. Get advice if you email there.

## The Math: What Could a Win-Back Return?

All inputs are **assumptions**, not benchmarks. Click and conversion rates for fitness win-backs are not published in the sources read.

| | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Lapsed members emailed (assumption) | 200 |
| Click rate (assumption, near MailerLite's 1.45% fitness average) | 2% |
| Clicks | 4 |
| Clicks that book the free week (assumption) | 50% |
| Free weeks started | 2 |
| Free weeks that convert to a paid membership (assumption) | 25% |
| New paid members | 0.5 |

At 200 emails that is half a member. The math shows why list size matters: win-back is cheap, but small lists give small returns. If a membership is worth $100 a month (assumption), one extra member for six months is $600 from a $0 send, plus your time. Build the list first.

## FAQ

### Do I need Klaviyo to use these templates?

No. They work in any email tool. Klaviyo's free plan covers up to 250 active profiles and 500 email sends per month; its paid prices were not available on the pricing page read for this guide.

### How often should a fitness studio email members?

No source read sets a standard. Start with a welcome series and one email a week or two, then watch unsubscribes against MailerLite's 0.30% fitness average.

### What subject lines work best?

No published test proves a winner for fitness. Test two versions on equal halves of your list and compare clicks.

### Do I have to include an unsubscribe link?

You must tell recipients how to opt out. The FTC says to honor requests within 10 business days. A visible link is the simplest way.

### Should I use opens or clicks to judge success?

Clicks and bookings, because they show action. For automation ideas see our [fitness email automation guide](/blog/marketing-automation-for-fitness-studios).
