For most local businesses, use email for everything that can wait (newsletters, offers, stories) and SMS for the few messages that are urgent (appointment reminders, last-minute openings). Email costs less per message and has no per-text fee. SMS costs roughly a cent per text, needs written consent, and carries legal risk if you get consent wrong.

This guide compares the two on cost, rules and fit, with a worked example you can rework with your own numbers.

## What Is the Difference Between SMS and Email Marketing?

Email reaches an inbox that people check when they have time. SMS lands on the lock screen as a text, so it feels personal and urgent. That difference decides which one to use for which message.

| | Email | SMS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost per message | Near zero once on a plan | About 1 to 2 cents each |
| Best for | Newsletters, offers, long content | Reminders, same-day openings |
| Length | As long as you need | One segment is short; longer texts cost more |
| Consent | Opt-in and an unsubscribe link | Prior express written consent for marketing |
| Main risk | Ends up unread or in promotions | Legal claims and unsubscribes |

## How Much Does Each Channel Cost?

Email software prices come from Mailchimp's [pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/). The Free plan is $0 with a limit of 250 contacts and 500 sends a month. Essentials shows $13 a month and Standard $20 a month, both quoted for the first 12 months, with Standard including 6,000 emails a month. The page does not say which contact tier those prices assume.

For SMS, two reference points help. Twilio's [US pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/us) is $0.0083 per outbound SMS segment, plus carrier fees of $0.0035 (AT&T) to $0.005 (Verizon). SimpleTexting's [pricing page](http://simpletexting.com/pricing/) showed one configuration at $39 a month for 500 credits on a local number, extra credits at 5.5 cents, and a $4 carrier registration fee. One credit is one SMS, two for a long message and three for a picture message.

## What Does It Cost in Practice? A Worked Example

Everything below is an **assumption**: a salon with 400 customers who consented to both channels and four messages a month each.

| Item | Email | SMS (Twilio, build it yourself) | SMS (SimpleTexting) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Messages a month | 1,600 | 1,600 | 1,600 credits |
| Message cost | Included in plan | $0.0118 to $0.0133 each | 500 credits in plan, 1,100 extra at $0.055 |
| Monthly cost | $20 (Mailchimp Standard, first-year price) | about $19 to $21 plus number fees | about $99.50 ($29 plan, $10 number, $60.50 extra) |

The Twilio row adds $0.0083 to the carrier fee and ignores number rental and registration, which the page does not price. The SimpleTexting row assumes the extra-credit rate applies to every credit over 500.

If one text fills a $60 appointment slot that would otherwise stay empty, about two filled slots pay for the $99.50 plan. Replace the price and the number of contacts with yours.

## Which Channel Gets Customers Back?

There is no reliable published figure comparing the two for local businesses, so avoid any claim that one has a "60% open rate". Mailchimp's own table shows email averages of 35.63% opens and 2.62% clicks across all its users, but it warns that Apple's Mail Privacy Protection can inflate opens. Clicks and bookings are the better signals.

Use the message type to decide:

1. **Appointment reminder or same-day opening:** SMS, because timing matters.
2. **Monthly offer, news or a loyalty update:** email.
3. **Win-back for lapsed customers:** email first, then one SMS to those who agreed to texts.
4. **Receipts and booking confirmations:** whichever your booking tool already sends.

## What Are the Rules for Texting Customers?

In the US, the Telephone Consumer Protection Act lets a recipient sue for $500 per violating message, and a court may increase the award up to three times, per [47 U.S.C. 227(b)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227). Marketing texts need prior express written consent, and it is safest to store a record of when and how each person agreed.

Law-firm summaries of the FCC's 2024 order (second-hand, via [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/tcpa-connect/fcc-adopts-revocation-rules)) say replies such as stop, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel or unsubscribe count as revoking consent, and opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days at most. Some provisions were reported as stayed, so check current status with a lawyer. This is not legal advice.

## Should You Use Both?

Yes, once you have an email list worth sending to. Start with email, because it is cheaper and lower risk, then add SMS for reminders only. Ask for text consent separately at booking, with plain wording about how often you will text.

## How Do You Win Back Lapsed Customers?

Send a short email to people who have not booked in 60 to 90 days, and only text those who opted in. Keep the offer specific and the message under one segment. ## FAQ

**Q: Is SMS better than email for local business?**
A: Neither wins everywhere. Email is cheaper and suits offers and news. SMS suits short, urgent messages such as reminders. Most local businesses do best with email as the base and SMS for reminders.

**Q: How much does SMS marketing cost?**
A: Twilio lists $0.0083 per US segment plus carrier fees of $0.0035 to $0.005. SimpleTexting showed 500 credits for $39 a month in one configuration.

**Q: Can I text my customers without permission?**
A: Not for marketing. You need prior express written consent, and the TCPA allows $500 per violating message in damages.

**Q: Is email marketing free?**
A: Mailchimp's Free plan allows 250 contacts and 500 sends a month. Beyond that you pay, from $13 a month in its first-year pricing.

**Q: Do SMS messages really have a 98% open rate?**
A: Do not rely on that figure, because no independent source backs it for local businesses. Measure replies and bookings instead.
