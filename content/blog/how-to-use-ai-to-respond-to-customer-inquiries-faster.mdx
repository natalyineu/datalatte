## Why Does Reply Speed Matter for a Local Business?

Harvard Business Review's 2011 study of online sales leads concluded that "most companies are not responding nearly fast enough." The full article sits behind a paywall, so its specific response-time multiples are not quoted here, and many blogs repeat them without the original.

A more recent look at one sector shows the gap. Clio's 2024 secret-shopper test of 500 law firms, reported by the ABA Journal, found only 40% answered a phone call and one-third replied to an email. Law firms are not cafes, but the pattern holds: many inquiries get no timely answer. No published benchmark for reply times at local shops was found.

## What Can You Automate for Free Today?

Start with what Meta already provides. Its help center defines instant replies as "messages sent automatically as your Page's first response to new messages." They can thank people for getting in touch and say when you will reply.

There is a catch. Meta states instant replies do not count toward your response rate or response time, and they are not sent when your Page is set to Away. An instant reply is an acknowledgement, not an answer. It buys time but does not replace a real reply, so pair it with a bot or a person who answers within the hours you promise.

## How Do You Set It Up in One Afternoon?

1. Copy the last 30 questions from your DMs, emails and call notes. Group them. Most shops find a handful of repeats: hours, prices, parking, booking, allergies or stock.
2. Write one approved answer for each. Include the booking link and the current price or "from" price.
3. Load the answers into the tool as its knowledge source. Keep the opening hours in one place so changes only happen once.
4. Set a handoff rule: complaints, refunds, medical or legal questions, and anything the bot is unsure about go to a named person.
5. Test with 20 real past questions before it goes live. Fix wrong answers first.
6. Read the unanswered list weekly and add what is missing.

Steps 4 to 6 are recommendations from this guide, not vendor requirements.

## What Should AI Never Answer on Its Own?

Keep humans on anything where a wrong answer costs money or trust: price quotes outside your posted list, refund decisions, allergy and health questions, and complaints. A bot that guesses a price creates an argument at the counter.

Decide in advance what happens if the bot quotes a wrong price. Many owners honor it once and fix the source answer, but that is a business choice, not a rule. Write down which answers the bot may give and which it must pass on.

## FAQ

### Will customers mind talking to a bot?

No reliable local-business survey was found for this guide. Make the handoff easy, say clearly that it is an automated assistant, and let people reach a person with one message.

### How much does an AI inquiry tool cost?

Listed prices run from free to $24.17 a month for Tidio Starter and $55 per agent a month for Zendesk Suite Team, plus per-resolution fees beyond the included five per agent. Prices were checked on 31 October 2026.

### Can AI also take bookings?

Some tools can, if they connect to your booking system. That depends on the integration, so check your booking software's list before choosing. For a deeper look, see our [guide to AI chatbots for booking and FAQs](/blog/ai-chatbot-for-local-business-booking-and-faqs).

### What if the AI gives wrong information?

Limit its answers to an approved list, test it on real questions and read the unanswered and flagged chats each week. Route price disputes to a person.
