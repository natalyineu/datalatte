## What Is the Map Pack, and Why Does It Decide Salon Visibility?

The Google Maps local pack shows the top three listings for a local search, and a salon that is not in those three gets far fewer taps. [Adwave states that the top three businesses are pulled directly from their Google Business Profile listings](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business), so the profile itself, not the website alone, decides who appears.

Adwave also reports that Google Business Profile signals carried [32% of local pack ranking weight in 2025, based on a benchmark it cites](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit). A separate Adwave guide gives the same 32% figure without naming a source, so treat it as a directional guide rather than an exact measure.

Search intent matters too. Adwave lists [six local metrics worth tracking](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent), covered later in this guide, that show whether map visibility is turning into calls, directions, and visits.

## Which Profile Settings Should a Jacksonville Salon Check First?

Start by confirming the listing is claimed. Adwave cites [11.1% of profiles as unclaimed](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), though it does not name a source for that figure. An unclaimed salon cannot edit its own details.

Next, match the primary category to what ranks. Adwave suggests [reviewing the top three to five businesses in local map results](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) for your main service search and noting their categories. Adwave also says the primary category is the number one ranking factor for the local pack, citing an analysis it does not name.

Hours, phone number, and address should be [100% accurate](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Holiday hours and stylist-specific phone lines are common sources of mismatch.

The description allows [750 characters](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Use the space to name the services you offer and the Jacksonville neighborhoods you serve, written as plain sentences.

Photos matter. Adwave recommends [at least 15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Before-and-after color work, stylist portraits, and the reception area all give customers a reason to call.

Finally, posts only count when they are visible. Google states that a post [shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), and Pending or Not approved posts do not show. Check the status after every post.

## How Do You Keep Name, Address, and Phone Consistent Across Citations?

Citations are listings of your name, address, and phone number on other sites. Google is not the only source a customer or search engine checks. [Adwave's voice search guide states the NAP must be exactly the same](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses) on your website, your Business Profile, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places.

Exact means exact. Adwave says the website must match the listing [right down to the comma and whether you use "St." or "Street"](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Fix mismatches in existing listings before adding new ones, because a new listing with the wrong detail adds a second conflict.

Apple Maps deserves attention. Adwave describes [Apple Business Connect as a Google Business Profile alternative](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring) and reports a 7% share of a maps-only comparison, which is growing, though the page does not attribute every figure. Apple Maps Actions can connect booking services directly to a profile.

Booking friction is also part of the picture. Booksy states that [Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing). Check whether your booking tool is connected before you spend on ads, and see [the complete Google Ads guide for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide) if you plan paid campaigns.

## How Many Reviews Does a Salon Need, and How Fast Should They Arrive?

A third-party analysis reported by Adwave found that [businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business). That analysis is secondary, so treat 200 as a practical target rather than a guaranteed threshold.

Reviews also show on the profile itself. Google says reviews [show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), so each review is visible to a searcher deciding which salon to tap.

Pace matters more than a single burst. Use this worked example, where every input is an assumption you should replace with your own numbers:

- Assumption: 60 client visits per week
- Assumption: 15% of clients leave a review after a text or email prompt
- Reviews per week: 60 x 0.15 = 9
- Assumption: the salon starts at 40 reviews, so 160 remain to reach 200
- Weeks to reach 200: 160 / 9, about 18 weeks

Change the inputs and the timeline moves. A salon with fewer visits or a lower response rate should plan for a longer runway and track progress monthly.

For review benchmarks from another US market, see [the Denver salon review benchmarks study](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026).

## Which Keywords and Website Pages Support the Profile?

Google's AI-driven answers, referred to as Ask Maps, draw on [business profile data, website content, and reviews, according to an Accrisoft summary cited by Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a). Clear service wording on your website, matching what your profile says, reinforces the profile.

Build one page per major service rather than one generic page. A page for balayage, keratin treatment, or men's cuts gives each service its own wording. Keep the city and neighborhood names natural and consistent with your Business Profile.

Seed the Q&A section yourself. Google launched Q&A in Maps in 2017, and Adwave's guide explains how questions answered by owners shape what customers see. Write the five questions your front desk hears most often.

For paid keyword research, see [the best Google Ads keywords for hair salons in 2026](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026). Google's location assets pull data [directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), so a correction in the profile flows into linked Ads accounts.

## What Should You Track to Know the Work Is Paying Off?

Adwave lists six useful local search metrics: [Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift over time](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent). Record a baseline for each before you start.

Check map placement by searching your main service terms in a private browser window, from the area your customers live in. Note the position and the listings above and below yours.

Set a monthly review. Compare calls, direction requests, and website visits against the baseline, and look at the review count and average rating.

For a maps embed on your contact page, see [how to embed Google Maps on a local business website](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

## What Does a 30-Day Checklist Look Like for a Jacksonville Salon?

1. Week 1: Confirm the profile is claimed and verified, then check hours, phone number, and address against your website.
2. Week 1: Compare your primary category with the top three to five businesses in local results for your main service search.
3. Week 2: Write a 750-character description naming your services and the neighborhoods you serve, and upload at least 15 photos.
4. Week 2: Audit citations against your website and fix every "St." versus "Street" or phone format mismatch.
5. Week 3: Publish your first post, then confirm its status is Live.
6. Week 3: Start asking for a review after each appointment, using a text or email prompt.
7. Week 4: Record baseline profile metrics and map placement, then repeat the check at 30 days.

Adwave's own estimate is that a business [may begin ranking for its own name a few weeks after verification](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Treat that as a vendor estimate, not a promise.
