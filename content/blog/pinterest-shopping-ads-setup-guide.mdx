## What are Pinterest Shopping Ads and who should run them?

A Shopping Ad connects a Pin to a product in your catalog. The Pin shows an image, a title, and a price, and it links to the page where a buyer completes the purchase. For a salon or studio, the "product" can be a bookable service, such as a colour package or a class pass, with the link going to a booking page.

Pinterest-specific audience figures were not available in the sources, so this guide does not state how many Pinterest users are in any country. The [Pew Research Center survey of US adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) gives context on where social attention sits. In its 2026 survey, 70% of US adults use Facebook and 51% use Instagram, while Pinterest does not appear in the 2026 table. A Pinterest test should therefore be treated as a hypothesis, not a proven channel.

Shopping Ads suit businesses with a clear offer, a visual product or service, and a page that takes a booking or sale in one step. If the offer is vague or the site is hard to use on a phone, fix those first. The [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) explains how to split a small budget across channels before committing to one.

## How do you set up a Pinterest business account and product catalog?

Start with a business account and verify your website and email. Add the city or service area in the profile description so the platform and your audience can see where you operate. Use a logo and a short description that states what you offer, not a slogan.

Next, build the catalog. If you use Shopify, WooCommerce, Square, or a similar platform, use the native connection so prices and availability update automatically. If you have only a few services, a spreadsheet with one row per item works. Each row needs a unique ID, a title, a description, a price, an image link, a product page link, and an availability status.

Images matter more than copy in a visual feed. Use bright photos of the product or service in real use, such as a finished haircut or a latte on a table. Keep the format consistent across all items so the feed passes validation and looks like one brand.

Track conversions before spending. Install the tag on the booking or checkout page, using the same logic as the [Facebook Pixel setup guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-setup-guide). Without a working tag, clicks appear but bookings do not, and there is no way to judge whether the ad paid for itself.

## How much should a local business spend on a first test?

Use a fixed, short test rather than an open-ended budget. A two-week window gives enough clicks to read the result without tying up cash. The worked example below uses inputs that are assumptions, not Pinterest benchmarks.

Assumed inputs for a hair salon:

- Daily test budget: $25 (assumption)
- Test length: 14 days, so total spend is $350 (calculated)
- Average cost per click: $0.60 (assumption)
- Clicks bought: $350 divided by $0.60, about 583 clicks (calculated)
- Booking rate from clicks: 2% (assumption), so about 12 bookings (calculated)
- Average booking value: $120 (assumption)
- Revenue: 12 bookings times $120, about $1,440 (calculated)
- Return on ad spend: $1,440 divided by $350, about 4.1 times (calculated)

The test pays off only if bookings cover the spend. Three bookings at $120 would cover the $350, so the test has room to fail. If the real booking rate is 0.5%, the same 583 clicks produce about three bookings, which only breaks even.

The table below compares three test sizes using the same assumed $0.60 cost per click and $120 booking value. Every figure is calculated from assumptions.

| Daily budget (assumption) | 14-day spend | Clicks at $0.60 | Bookings to break even | Booking rate needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $15 | $210 | about 350 | 2 | about 0.6% |
| $25 | $350 | about 583 | 3 | about 0.5% |
| $40 | $560 | about 933 | 5 | about 0.5% |

The break-even rate stays near 0.5% to 0.6% at every size, so the budget mainly changes how much data arrives, not whether the test is profitable. A smaller budget reads faster in cash terms but gives fewer bookings to judge.

Meta's [help documentation on A/B testing](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669) states that informal testing, such as switching ads on and off manually, can create overlapping audiences and unreliable results, and that both versions should use the same budget. The same discipline applies on Pinterest: change one variable at a time and keep spend equal.

## How do you target locally and measure results?

Set the location to your city or a radius around your address, and avoid broad national targeting. Interest categories such as coffee, hair styling, or pet care can narrow the audience further. Keep audience expansion off during the first test so the original targeting can be judged on its own.

Measure three numbers each week: cost per click, booking or sale rate from clicks, and return on ad spend. If clicks arrive but bookings do not, check the landing page first. A page that asks for too many fields, loads slowly on a phone, or hides the price will lose buyers no matter how good the Pin is.

Do not judge the campaign on the first two days. Early results mostly show whether the ad is delivering at all. Read the numbers at the end of the test window, then decide whether to pause, change one thing, or scale. Scaling in steps of about 20% per week, with the same guardrails, keeps risk low.

Some businesses also run Google Ads alongside social channels. [Google's Display documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190) describes reach across 35 million websites and apps, which is a different intent from a Pin. Compare channels by cost per booking, not by reach. The [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads) page covers how search and display campaigns can be set up for direct comparison.

## What checklist should you run before launch?

Work through this list before spending any money. Each item takes an afternoon or less.

- Confirm the business profile shows your city and phone number.
- Connect the catalog or upload a feed with at least one product or service.
- Check that each image shows the product or service in use and meets the platform's size guidance.
- Write titles that name the service and its benefit, for example "Balayage and Blowout, $150".
- Link every item to a page where a booking or purchase takes under a minute.
- Confirm the conversion tag fires on the booking confirmation page.
- Set a daily budget and a fixed end date for the test.
- Write down the break-even booking count before starting.
- Set a review date for day 14.

If any item is unchecked, delay the launch. A poorly linked catalog wastes the first week of data.

## FAQ

### Can I run Pinterest Shopping Ads if I only offer services?

Yes. Create a service item in the catalog with a clear name and price, then link it to the booking page. Services with a fixed price and a set duration are easiest to present. If pricing varies widely, use a starting price and state that in the title.

### How long should a Pinterest test run?

Plan for 14 days with a fixed budget. Shorter tests rarely produce enough clicks to judge booking rate. Use the same length for every test you compare so the results are fair.

### Do I need a full website to run Shopping Ads?

A full website is not required, but a page where someone can buy or book quickly is. A booking platform page or a simple sales page can work. What matters is that the page loads fast on a phone and shows the price before the buyer clicks through.

### Should I trust the cost-per-click numbers in older articles?

Use them only as rough context. [WordStream's industry benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks), a secondary source built on 2016 data, showed an average Google Ads search cost per click of $2.69 across industries. Costs have changed since then, so test your own numbers rather than relying on old averages.
