Facebook ads targeting for a fitness studio means choosing who sees your ad by location, interests and past behaviour, then matching the offer to each group. A practical starting point is a cold audience within 3–5 km of your studio, as [Vertical Impression's guide](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026) recommends. Add a retargeting audience for people who already visited your site, and follow up with every lead quickly. The budget example below shows how a small studio can split spend and track cost per lead.

## How wide should your Facebook audience radius be?

Start with a radius that matches how far members actually travel. [Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026) suggests targeting demographics within 3–5 km of the studio for cold audiences. That range keeps the ad close enough to feel local and wide enough to reach a workable audience.

Narrow the age and interest layers only after you see delivery. If the audience is too small for the budget, widen the radius before adding more interests. Adding interests too early often shrinks reach without improving the people who book.

Use the radius for prospects who do not follow your page. Existing members and followers need different messages, which the retargeting section covers.

## Which offer should your ad promote?

The offer matters more than the targeting once people see the ad. A fitness studio ad works best when it asks for a small step, such as a trial class, a consultation or a day pass, rather than a long-term membership.

Keep the promise on the ad and the landing page identical. If the ad says a free trial class, the booking page should show that same class and that same price. A mismatch makes people leave before they book.

Instagram and Facebook also let businesses add a Book Now button to their profile, which can shorten the path from ad to booking. Check Meta's current help pages for the exact button options available to your account.

## How much should a small studio spend on Facebook ads?

[Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) notes that many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing. That is a benchmark, not a rule, but it gives a starting range. The worked example below applies it to an assumed revenue figure.

| Line | Input | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly studio revenue | $20,000 (assumption) | Base for the budget |
| Marketing budget at 5% | 5% of $20,000 | $1,000 |
| Facebook ad spend | $1,000 (assumption, full marketing share) | $1,000 per month |
| Retargeting share | 25% of ad spend (assumption) | $250 |
| Cold audience spend | Remaining 75% (assumption) | $750 |
| Cold cost per lead | $15 (assumption) | 50 leads |
| Retargeting cost per lead | $5 (assumption) | 50 leads |
| Blended cost per lead | $1,000 ÷ 100 leads | $10 |
| Members from leads | 10% of leads convert (assumption) | 10 members |
| Revenue from new members | 10 × $80 per month × 6 months (assumptions) | $4,800 |
| Return on ad spend | $4,800 ÷ $1,000 | 4.8x |

The first month may not pay back the spend, because members often stay for several months. Judge the campaign on the six-month revenue and the blended cost per lead, not on the first week's results.

## How fast should you follow up with new leads?

[Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/) advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades quickly after a pop-up ends. The same logic applies to Facebook leads. A person who filled in a form about a trial class has a short window before they forget.

Set up an automatic email or text that confirms the booking, explains what to bring, and offers a clear next step. [The email marketing guide for fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-fitness-studio) covers sequences in more detail. A slow reply often costs more than the ad that generated the lead.

## How do you retarget people who did not book?

Retargeting works best when it targets the people who showed interest but did not act. Install the Facebook Pixel on your booking page and create an audience of visitors who did not complete a booking in the last 30 days. Show them a reminder that names the same offer from the original ad.

Exclude people who already booked or joined. Showing a trial offer to a current member wastes budget and can feel careless. Keep the retargeting share of your budget modest at first, then adjust based on cost per lead, as the worked example shows.

For people who watched part of a studio video, you can build a separate audience and show a booking-focused message. Keep the creative fresh: the same clip repeated many times tends to lose response. [The Facebook ads and AI guide](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-facebook-ads-and-ai) explains how to rotate creative with less manual work.

## What should you check each week?

Review your campaigns once a week rather than daily. Small budgets need several days to gather enough results to judge.

1. Record the spend, leads, and bookings for each ad set.
2. Calculate cost per lead by dividing spend by leads.
3. Check that the landing page and the ad show the same offer.
4. Confirm that each new lead received a follow-up within 24 hours.
5. Compare the cold and retargeting cost per lead side by side.
6. Pause any ad set that has spent its test budget without a single booking, and replace it with a new audience or creative.

Content for ads also needs a steady supply. [The social media strategy guide](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-marketing-strategy) covers how to plan short videos, and [the personas guide](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-to-different-personas-beginners-vs-athletes) helps you write separate messages for beginners and experienced athletes. [Seasonal timing](/blog/fitness-studio-new-year-membership-marketing) matters too, because January and summer demand differ.

## FAQ

### How big should my first Facebook audience be?

Start with a size that gives the platform enough people to test without spreading the budget too thin. A cold audience within 3–5 km of the studio is a practical first step. If delivery is very slow or the audience is too small, widen the radius before adding more interest filters.

### Should I use the same ad for existing followers and new prospects?

No. Followers already know the studio, so they need a different message, such as a referral or member reward. Prospects need an introduction to the studio, the class format and the first step. Keeping the messages separate makes it easier to see which group responds.

### How much should I budget to test a new audience?

The worked example in this guide uses an assumed $1,000 monthly spend, which includes both cold and retargeting audiences. Your actual figure should come from your own revenue and the 5–10 percent benchmark from [Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/). Test one change at a time, and give each test several days before judging it.

### What if my cost per lead rises?

Check the offer and landing page first, then the audience, then the creative. A rising cost per lead often means the ad has been shown to the same people too many times, or that the offer has lost appeal. Replace one element at a time so you can see which change made the difference.
