Most TikTok advice for salons skips the two facts that decide whether a test is even possible: TikTok sets minimum budgets, and only part of your clientele is on the app. This guide starts from those facts, then gives you a 14-day test you can price before you spend anything.

Source: [Pew Research Center, 8 facts about Americans and TikTok](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/03/02/8-facts-about-americans-and-tiktok/). Pew reports overall use is up from 21% in 2021. Adults 65 and older sit at 12%.

## What the numbers mean for a hair salon

If your best clients are 30 to 49, 44% of that group uses TikTok. That is a large enough pool to test. If your chair is full of clients 65 and older, only 12% use the app, so TikTok is a weak first channel for you. Check your own booking software: pull the ages of your last 100 clients before you decide.

## The budget floor most guides ignore

TikTok's own help center states that for campaigns, both daily and total budgets must exceed 50 USD, and for ad groups, daily budgets must exceed 20 USD. Lifetime ad group budgets are calculated as the 20 USD daily minimum times the scheduled days. Source: [TikTok Business Help Center, About daily budgets](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-daily-budgets).

Practical consequence: a "$10 a day" test is not possible in Ads Manager. The cheapest single ad group runs just over $20 a day, which is roughly $600 a month if left on.

## Worked example (assumptions, not benchmarks)

The numbers below are assumptions to show the method. Replace them with your own.

- Average ticket: $85 (a cut and style)
- Ad group budget: $21 a day for 14 days = $294 total
- Assumed cost per click: $1.00, giving about 294 clicks
- Assumed click-to-booking rate: 3%, giving about 9 bookings
- Cost per booking: about $33
- Booked revenue: about 9 x $85 = $765

Now the decision rule. Your break-even cost per booking is the profit on the first visit, not the revenue. If a $85 visit leaves $40 after product, chair rent share and payment fees, then $33 per booking is roughly break-even on the first visit and profitable only if clients rebook.

If your rebooking rate is low, stop at day 14. If you do not know it, find out first from your booking system.

Compare against a paid channel with published benchmarks: LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook lead campaign data for Beauty and Personal Care shows a cost per lead of $50.91 (see [LocaliQ Facebook benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/facebook-advertising-benchmarks/)). A lead is not a booking, so the two are not directly comparable, but it gives you a ceiling to sanity-check against.

<Callout type="warning">TikTok does not publish a salon-specific cost per click. Any figure quoted for "hair salon TikTok CPC" without a named dataset is a guess. Use your own first 14 days as the benchmark.</Callout>

## The 14-day test plan

1. Days 1 to 3: install the TikTok Pixel on your booking page. TikTok describes it as code placed on your website that sends events to TikTok so you can measure and optimize campaigns ([TikTok Pixel help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-pixel)). If your booking tool is hosted and does not let you add code, track bookings by asking "how did you hear about us" at booking.
2. Days 1 to 3: make 3 short vertical videos from real work: a before-and-after, a 15-second color process, and a price-led offer such as "Balayage consultations, free this month".
3. Days 4 to 14: one ad group at the minimum budget, location set to your service area, age range matched to your real client ages, all three videos as separate ads.
4. Day 14: kill any ad with clicks but no bookings. Keep the one with the lowest cost per booking and decide using the break-even rule above.

<Callout type="tip">Send the click straight to your booking page, not your homepage or Instagram profile. Every extra step between the ad and the calendar loses people.</Callout>

## When to skip TikTok ads

- Your clients skew 50 and older (30% of ages 50-64 and 12% of 65+ use TikTok, per Pew).
- You cannot afford to run about $300 and see zero bookings.
- You have no booking link that works on a phone.

In those cases start with [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and [Meta ads](/blog/using-meta-ads-for-hair-salon-marketing) instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is the minimum I can spend on TikTok ads?**

Per TikTok's help center, campaign budgets must exceed 50 USD and ad group daily budgets must exceed 20 USD. A single ad group for 14 days at $21 a day is $294.

**Q: Do I need to post organic videos first?**

It is not a platform requirement. It is a conversion judgment: people who see an ad often tap your profile. An account with a few real videos looks more trustworthy than an empty one. Read the [organic playbook for salons](/blog/hair-salon-tiktok-strategy-before-after-content) for what to post.

**Q: Will TikTok reach clients over 40?**

Partly. Pew found 44% of adults 30-49 and 30% of adults 50-64 use TikTok, so the audience exists but thins with age.

**Q: How do I target my city?**

Set the location in the ad group to your service area. For a deeper walk-through see the [TikTok targeting guide](/blog/tiktok-ads-targeting-guide).

**Q: How long until I know if it works?**

Plan for 14 days, then judge on cost per booking, not views.
