To optimize a Google Business Profile, fill in every field accurately, pick the most specific category, keep your real business name, collect and answer reviews, and update hours and photos as things change. Google says local results rest on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot change distance, and Google states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. The checklist below covers the parts you control.

Every rule comes from Google's help pages or a named survey, listed under Sources. Where a point is a judgment call rather than a Google rule, the text says so.

## What Decides Your Local Ranking?

Google's help page names three factors. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Distance is how far you are from the person searching. Prominence covers how well known you are, including links from other sites and your review count. Google adds that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

That gives the checklist its order. Relevance is fixed by completeness and categories. Prominence is moved mainly by reviews. Distance is out of your hands, which is why a business two blocks away can outrank a better one across town.

## How Should You Fill In the Basics?

Google's guidelines are strict on a few fields, and breaking them is the most common reason for suspensions and edits.

- **Name:** it must match your real-world name on the storefront, website and stationery. Taglines, hours, phone numbers, URLs, service descriptions and location details do not belong in it.
- **Categories:** use as few as possible, each as specific as possible. Google's test is that a category completes "This business is a", not "This business has a".
- **Address:** a real location. P.O. boxes and virtual offices are not eligible. Service-area businesses should generally hide their address.
- **Hours:** regular customer-facing hours, with special hours for holidays. Appointment-only businesses should not list hours.
- **Phone and website:** they must represent the individual location, and the number must be under your control.

## What Goes in the Description, Services and Attributes?

The description holds up to 750 characters. Google suggests saying what you offer, what sets you apart and how long you have operated. Do not add links, special promotions or prices to it. Google also notes an AI-suggested description can appear, which you can edit, so check the text before it goes live.

Add each service with a plain-language name, the way customers would say it. For attributes, tick only what is true, such as dine-in, wheelchair access or payment methods. No verified figure exists for how much services or attributes lift rankings, so treat them as completeness items, not ranking tricks.

## How Many Photos and Posts Do You Need?

Google publishes no required number, and Google gives no official figure for how photos affect ranking. Use the practical test: a customer should be able to find the entrance, see the inside, and see the work or product. Photos and videos must follow Google's content policies.

Posts come in three types: updates, offers and events. Google says posts do not expire on a fixed schedule, but posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set. A post with an unverified phone number in the text may be removed, so use the Call now button instead. A weekly post is a habit suggestion, not a Google requirement.

## How Do You Handle Reviews?

Reviews are where you can move prominence. Google says you can ask customers to leave reviews through a link or QR code, and that customers need a Google Account to post. Offering discounts or free goods in exchange for reviews counts as fake and misleading content and is prohibited.

BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US consumers gives the bar to aim at: 97% read local reviews, 68% will only use a business rated 4 stars or more, and 31% want 4.5 or more. For replies, 89% expect owners to respond, 32% expect an answer by the next day, and 42% are unlikely to use a business that never responds.

Google's advice on replies: keep them short and professional, address the reviewer by name, do not paste the same thank-you everywhere, and do not use replies to offer deals. See our [review management guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) for a reply routine.

## What Happened to the Q&A Section?

Google is removing the public Q&A. The Business Profile Q&A API was discontinued on November 3, 2025, and on December 3, 2025 Google confirmed an AI-powered Ask button would replace Q&A in Maps, according to PPC Land (secondary source). The rollout is still in progress, and Ask Maps was reported as US and India only in March 2026.

What to do: keep hours, services and attributes accurate, and answer any questions still visible in your dashboard. Reports say Ask Maps answers draw on profile data, reviews and the website, so reviews and your site now feed those answers. Google had not said how owners can correct a wrong AI answer.

## The Math: How Many Reviews to Reach 4.5 Stars?

All inputs here are **assumptions**. A shop has 30 reviews averaging 4.2 stars. Every new review is 5 stars. How many are needed to reach a 4.5 average?

| Step | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Current points (30 x 4.2) | 126 |
| Needed: (126 + 5n) / (30 + n) = 4.5 | n = 18 |
| Check: (126 + 90) / 48 | 4.5 |

Eighteen consecutive 5-star reviews is the best case, and real reviews will not all be 5 stars, so plan for more. At two new reviews a week that is roughly nine weeks of steady requests. The point is that a rating moves slowly, so a request habit beats a one-time push. ## FAQ

### Will Google call me to sell ads if I claim my profile?

Profiles are free. Promotional offers come from Google Ads only if you sign up. No Google statement on cold calls was found, so treat any caller who asks for payment to "verify" a listing with caution and contact Google through your dashboard instead.

### Can I delete a bad review?

No, but you can flag one that breaks Google's content policies. Google also recommends a professional reply that acknowledges real mistakes and moves complex issues to phone or email.

### Can I add keywords to my business name?

No. Google says the name should reflect your real-world name and lists taglines, service descriptions and location details as things not to add.

### Do I need to show my home address?

Service-area businesses should generally hide their address. Use a service area instead.

### How many reviews do I need?

Google gives no number. It says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. BrightLocal's survey shows what customers expect: 68% want 4 stars or higher.
