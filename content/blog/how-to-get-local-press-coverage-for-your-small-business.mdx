To get free local press coverage, send a short, personal email to the one reporter who covers your topic, with a story that affects people in their community, and offer a named person for interview plus a photo. Muck Rack's 2026 survey of journalists found 88% immediately delete pitches that miss their beat, and 54% seldom or never respond to PR pitches. Relevance and brevity decide who gets coverage, not budget.

This guide covers what journalists say they want, how to build a short media list, what a pitch should contain and how to follow up once. The data is from a survey of journalists across outlet types, not only local ones, so local editors may differ.

## Do Journalists Really Use Pitches From Small Businesses?

Yes, but selectively. Muck Rack surveyed 1,044 journalists between 30 January and 2 March 2026, with 897 responses kept after data cleaning. Of those, 86% said at least some of their stories start with a PR pitch, and 51% said 1% to 10% of their stories do.

The same survey shows the pressure on inboxes: 29% receive 6 to 10 pitches on a normal day and 14% receive more than 20. Only 3% say pitches are always relevant to what they cover, and 43% say they seldom are.

The sample is not limited to local reporters, so treat the numbers as a guide to how newsrooms think. The lesson for a small business is encouraging: most pitches are poor, so a relevant one stands out.

## What Makes a Journalist Delete Your Email?

Muck Rack asked what causes immediate deletion. Irrelevance leads at 88%, followed by an overly promotional or advertorial tone at 71%, a mass email look at 50%, repeated sends without response at 46%, a pitch that is too long or unclear at 40%, the wrong name or outlet at 35%, and no access to sources at 26%.

So avoid the language of an advert. "Award-winning, best in town, grand opening" reads as promotion. Say what happened and who it affects. Check the reporter's name and outlet twice.

Do not send the same text to fifty addresses with a blind copy. One of Muck Rack's quoted respondents advises pitching selectively, then widening if needed. A personalised note to five people beats a blast to fifty.

## What Counts as a Local Story?

Journalists said a pitch feels genuinely relevant when it directly affects the community their audience belongs to (78%). Another 45% look for clear local or cultural context, and 28% value voices from the impacted community.

For a small business, that points to stories about people and places rather than products. Examples: a café that hires from a local training scheme, a groomer offering free nail trims to a rescue's dogs on a set day, a gym opening a class for older residents, or a barber collecting coats. These work only if they are true and dated.

An opening or a sale rarely qualifies alone. Link it to something readers care about, such as a closed shop reopening on a street that lost three businesses. Our [local lead generation guide](/blog/lead-generation-for-local-business) covers turning attention into enquiries once a story runs.

## How Do You Build a Short Media List?

Start with outlets your customers already read: the local paper's website, community news sites, neighbourhood newsletters, radio stations and active local social pages. Find the person who wrote the three latest stories closest to yours and note their email from the byline or the outlet's contact page.

Keep a simple sheet with columns for outlet, reporter, last relevant story, date pitched and result. Aim for five to ten names, not hundreds.

Read before you write. A reporter who covered a rival's opening last month wants to know what is different about yours. A reporter who only covers council news is the wrong target for a new menu. The goal is to match one story to one beat.

## What Should a Pitch Contain?

Muck Rack asked what a PR person should offer with a pitch. The answers: clear relevance to the beat (70%), interview access to relevant sources (58%), original data or research (40%), high-resolution images (37%), exclusive or embargoed content (33%) and pre-written quotes (14%).

Cover those in under 200 words, since 69% prefer pitches of that length and 62% prefer one-to-one email. A workable layout:

- A subject line stating the news and the place.
- Two sentences: what is happening, when, and who it affects.
- The name and phone number of one person available to talk.
- A note that photos are available, with a link.
- One line saying why this reporter in particular.

Send before noon if you can, since 78% of journalists say they want pitches then. Keep your Google listing current so the reporter can verify you; see our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## How Many Times Should You Follow Up?

Once. In Muck Rack's eight-year summary, 50% of journalists say one follow-up is ideal and 51% say it should come three to seven days later. Repeated pitches without a response make 46% delete the email.

If there is no reply, move on and keep the contact. Use it later with a new story, not the same one. Respond fast if someone does reply, because a deadline is usually the same day.

When a story runs, share it, thank the reporter and add the link to your website and profiles. Coverage also helps people check you out; ask satisfied customers for [reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop) around the same time.

## FAQ

### Is local press coverage really free?
You pay no fee to pitch a journalist, but it costs time and there is no guarantee. Paying for coverage is advertising, not press.

### How long should a pitch be?
Under 200 words. Muck Rack's 2026 report says 69% of journalists prefer that length.

### Should I send a press release?
You can include one, but the email itself should carry the story. Muck Rack found 71% reject pitches that are overly promotional or advertorial.

### How often should I follow up?
Once, three to seven days later. 50% of journalists say one follow-up is ideal.

### What if no one replies?
That is common: 54% of journalists in the survey seldom or never respond. Check the angle and the reporter fit, then try a different story.
