Grammarly's [plans page](https://www.grammarly.com/plans) shows a Free plan at $0 a month and Pro at $12. For a salon the question is not "which plan" but "which three pieces of writing does a typo hurt most". Our answer: the appointment reminder with a wrong time, the review reply that goes public under your name, and the service page where someone decides whether to book. Grammarly can catch typos in all three. It cannot check the price, the date or whether the post sounds like you.

This guide is for salon owners and stylists in the US, UK, Australia and Canada who write their own captions, emails and replies between clients. It covers what each plan includes, a setup that takes ten minutes, a weekly routine, where Grammarly goes wrong, privacy settings worth checking and a simple payback calculation.

## What You Get on Each Plan

According to Grammarly's [plans page](https://www.grammarly.com/plans):

| | Free | Pro |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price shown | $0 a month | $12 (billing period not labelled on the page; check at checkout) |
| Spelling and grammar | Yes | Yes |
| Tone visibility | Yes | Yes, plus tone adjustment |
| Full-sentence rewrites | No | Yes |
| AI prompts per month | 100 | 2,000 |
| Plagiarism and AI-text detection | No | Yes |

Pro comes with a 7-day free trial per the same page. Plans, prices and AI limits change, so confirm them before paying.

One naming note. Grammarly's privacy policy page now redirects to the Superhuman site, the name of its parent company, but the product is still called Grammarly.

**Our take, not Grammarly's:** start on Free. Spelling, grammar and basic tone cover most salon writing. Test Pro only if you write a lot of long-form content, such as a monthly newsletter and several service pages.

## Setup in Ten Minutes

1. Install the browser extension, and the desktop app if you write on a computer.
2. Choose your English variant: American, British, Canadian or Australian. A salon in Manchester writing "colour" should not see it flagged.
3. Add salon words to the personal dictionary: balayage, Olaplex, keratin, toner, your stylists' names and your neighbourhood.
4. Write a half-page style sheet (below) and keep it open while you write.
5. Check the privacy settings in the next section before pasting anything sensitive.

## Privacy: What to Check Before You Paste

The [Superhuman privacy policy](https://superhuman.com/legal/privacy-policy) says the company earns money from paid purchases, "not by selling or monetizing user content". It also says it uses collected information to train its AI models, and that you can decide whether it uses your user content for training by adjusting the controls in your account settings. It gives no fixed retention period for user content; retention depends on the data type and your settings.

What that means for a salon: open your account settings and look at the training control, and keep client names, phone numbers and treatment notes out of any online writing tool unless you have a good reason. Check your local data rules as well. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

## A Weekly Routine That Fits Between Clients

Total time: about 30 minutes. Do it on your slowest day.

1. **Draft first, by hand** (10 minutes). Write the week's captions, one Google Business Profile post and any email in your own words. Use a voice note on your phone if typing is slow.
2. **Run Grammarly** (5 minutes). Accept spelling and obvious grammar fixes. Decline anything that makes you sound like a brochure.
3. **Read it aloud** (5 minutes). Would you say this to a regular in the chair?
4. **Add the human details** (5 minutes). A stylist's name, a real photo, a seasonal tip.
5. **Check the facts yourself** (5 minutes). Dates, times, prices and the booking link. Grammarly will approve a wrong price without blinking.

## Where to Use It First

### Appointment reminders and confirmations
A reminder with the wrong day is worse than a typo. Proofread, then compare the date and time against your calendar. If you automate reminders, test one message to yourself first. Our guide to [AI agents for appointment reminders](/blog/ai-agent-for-appointment-reminders-reduce-no-shows) covers the automated route.

### Review replies
Google's [review management help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050) says replies appear under the review as from the business, not from an individual, and that Google checks replies against its content policies before they appear. It also says helpful, positive replies can show you are responsive. A calm, specific reply to a difficult review is where Grammarly's tone feedback earns its place. Keep it short, thank the person, mention one real detail, and do not reveal anything about their appointment. Our [Google Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) has reply templates.

### Service pages
Pages for colour, cuts, extensions and bridal are often written once and ignored. Tidy them, then check each page states what the service involves, how long it takes, a price or "from" price, who it suits and a booking link. See [website design for hair salons](/blog/website-design-for-hair-salons).

### Captions and emails
Say the action plainly: "Two Saturday colour slots open. Link in bio." Grammarly will tidy a stray double space; you decide on emojis and hashtags. For what to send by email, see [email marketing for hair salons](/blog/email-marketing-for-hair-salons).

## Where Grammarly Falls Short

- It cannot verify facts, prices or availability.
- It does not know your area or your clients.
- It can flatten personality. A punchy caption may get "fixed" into something bland.
- AI rewrites can sound generic. If you use them, edit hard.
- It will flag trade terms unless you add them to the dictionary.
- It does nothing for ranking on its own. Google's [local ranking tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) say local results rely mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that review count and ratings can help. Spelling is not on that list.

## Pair It With a Style Sheet

Write half a page and keep it handy:

- Your tone: "warm, relaxed, a little cheeky" or "polished and luxurious".
- Words you use and words you avoid.
- Correct spellings of services and products.
- Standard lines: booking line, cancellation policy, opening hours.
- How you sign off.

Paste it into any AI drafting tool you use, and add the terms to Grammarly's dictionary so every stylist sounds like the same salon. Our [AI content tools comparison](/blog/ai-content-creation-tools-local-business-2026) covers drafting options beyond proofreading.

## The Math

Does a paid plan pay for itself? These are assumptions to replace with your own figures, except the $12 from the plans page.

| Input | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pro price | $12 a month, $144 a year | Grammarly plans page (billing period not labelled; annual figure assumes monthly billing) |
| Average ticket for a booked appointment | $90 | Assumption |
| Share kept after products, chair rent and wages | 40% | Assumption |
| Profit per appointment | $36 | $90 x 40% |
| Appointments to cover Pro for a year | 4 | $144 divided by $36 |

So Pro needs to protect or win about 4 appointments a year, which is one every quarter. The honest test is whether a paid feature changes your output. Free covers typos. If Pro's rewrites and tone adjustment save you time on the long pieces, that is worth something. If you only post captions, they probably will not.

A cheaper win is a wrong-time reminder. If one wrong time a month causes one missed appointment (assumption), that is $36 of profit lost each month, which dwarfs any software fee. Proofreading a reminder costs 30 seconds. The bigger lever is checking the facts, not buying a plan.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the free version of Grammarly enough for a salon?**
A: For spelling, grammar and tone visibility, usually yes. Grammarly's plans page lists full-sentence rewrites and 2,000 monthly AI prompts under Pro, with 100 prompts on Free.

**Q: Will Grammarly make my posts sound robotic?**
A: It can if you accept every suggestion. Decline changes that remove your personality.

**Q: Can Grammarly help me rank on Google?**
A: Not directly. Google's ranking tips point to relevance, distance and prominence, with review count and ratings helping. See our [local SEO for hair salons](/blog/local-seo-for-hair-salons) guide.

**Q: Is it safe to paste client information into Grammarly?**
A: Avoid it. The privacy policy says user content can be used for AI training unless you change the control in your account settings, so check that setting and keep client details out.

**Q: Does Grammarly work with British or Australian spelling?**
A: Yes, you can choose American, British, Canadian or Australian English in settings.
