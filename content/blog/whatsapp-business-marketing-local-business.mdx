## The pricing rules in plain English

From Meta's [WhatsApp pricing documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/):

- You are charged per template message delivered, at a rate set by the template category and the recipient's country calling code.
- A 24-hour customer service window opens whenever a customer messages you. Inside it, non-template (free-form) messages are free, and utility templates sent inside the window are free.
- Marketing templates are always charged, even inside an open window. Utility templates sent outside a window are charged.
- If a customer reaches you through a Click to WhatsApp ad or a Facebook Page call-to-action button and you respond within 24 hours, that opens a 72-hour free entry point window in which all messages are free.
- Meta changes rates only on the first day of a quarter, with at least a month of notice. Rates by country sit on Meta's rate card, so check the current figure for your country before budgeting.

The free WhatsApp Business app is for small teams answering chats by hand. The paid per-message pricing above applies to the Business Platform (API), which you reach through a provider. If you only reply to customers who write first, the model costs you nothing for those replies.

## What this means for a local business

Customer-initiated conversations are cheap; broadcasts are where the bill grows.

- Booking confirmations and answers to "are you open Sunday?" happen inside the window and cost nothing in message fees.
- Reminders sent days later are templates outside the window: charged as utility.
- Promotions to a list are marketing templates: always charged, and require opt-in.
- Ads that click to WhatsApp give you a 72-hour free conversation window.

## Worked example (assumptions, not benchmarks)

Say a salon sends 300 appointment reminders and one 300-person promotion per month. Assume a utility rate of U and a marketing rate of M per message from the rate card for your country. Monthly message fees equal 300 times U plus 300 times M. Because marketing messages are always charged and utility reminders sent outside the window are too, the promotion usually dominates the bill. Decide with one number: if a promotion converts at C%, fees per booking are 300 times M divided by (300 times C%). Look up M, pick a realistic C from your own past email results, and send only if the booking value comfortably exceeds that cost.

## Should you use the app or the platform?

- Under roughly one person's worth of chat volume and no automation: the free Business app.
- Need automated reminders, several staff on one number, or click-to-WhatsApp ads: the Business Platform through a provider, and budget for provider fees on top of Meta's.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is WhatsApp Business free?**
Replying to customers who message you first is free inside the 24-hour window. Template messages sent outside it, and all marketing templates, are charged per message.

**What counts as a marketing message?**
Meta classifies templates by category (marketing, utility, authentication). Promotions and offers are marketing; appointment confirmations are typically utility. Meta decides the category, so check it when you create the template.

**How much does a message cost?**
It depends on the category and the country calling code of the recipient. Use Meta's current rate card rather than a blog figure, because rates change quarterly.

**Do ads that click to WhatsApp cost extra to answer?**
If you respond within 24 hours, Meta opens a 72-hour window in which all messages are free. You still pay for the ad itself.

**Can I message customers who never contacted me?**
Marketing templates require opt-in and are always charged. Collect consent in person or at booking.
