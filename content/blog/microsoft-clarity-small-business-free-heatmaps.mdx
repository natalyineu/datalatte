Microsoft Clarity is a free behavior analytics tool that shows how visitors use your website through session recordings, heatmaps and AI-powered insights. Microsoft's documentation calls it a free service forever with no traffic limits. A small business can install one script, wait a few hours for data, and see where visitors click, scroll and get stuck before booking or calling.

This guide covers setup, what to review first, privacy duties and data retention. Every fact comes from Microsoft's Clarity documentation listed at the end. The worked example uses assumptions that are labelled.

## What Does Microsoft Clarity Show You?

Clarity's overview lists session recordings, heatmaps (click, scroll and area maps), a dashboard of insights, funnels, filters and segments, and a Copilot feature for AI summaries. In plain terms: recordings show what a visitor did, heatmaps show what many visitors did on one page, and insights flag patterns.

Microsoft defines two behaviors worth watching. Rage clicks occur when a user repeatedly clicks in a clustered area within a short period. Dead clicks occur when a user clicked somewhere and no response was detected. Both point to something on the page that looks clickable or is broken. These are the fastest things to find on a small site.

## How Do You Install Clarity?

Sign up on the Clarity site, create a project, and install the tracking code. Microsoft's FAQ says you need access to your site's head section to add the code manually, and that each site has its own code. It also says Clarity works with platforms such as WordPress, Shopify, WooCommerce, Google Tag Manager and Wix, with a supported platforms list in the docs.

The standard snippet looks like this, with your project ID in place of the placeholder:


Microsoft says it takes a few hours for data to show in the dashboard after install, and that the script is asynchronous, so it does not affect how quickly a page loads.

## What Should You Check First?

Start with the pages that earn you bookings: home, services, contact and booking.

1. **Click heatmap on the contact page.** Are visitors tapping the phone number, the form button, or something that does nothing? Dead clicks show up here.
2. **Scroll heatmap on the home page.** See how far visitors get before they leave and whether your main call to action sits below that point.
3. **Recordings filtered by rage clicks.** Watch a handful. Repeated clicks on the same element usually mean a broken link or a button that looks clickable but is not.
4. **Recordings of visitors who reached the booking page and left.** Look for the step where they stop.

Fix one problem at a time and compare the same heatmap afterwards. Our [heatmap tools comparison](/blog/heatmap-tools-for-websites) lists alternatives, and [Google Analytics 4 for small business](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business) covers the numbers side.

## How Long Is Your Data Available?

Microsoft's retention page says playback data is retained for 30 days, labeled or favorited sessions for 9 months, and heatmap data for 9 months. Data is deleted after that and cannot be recovered. Use the favorite or label option on recordings that show a clear problem.

## What Does a Worked Example Look Like?

All inputs are **assumptions** for an imaginary service business, not Clarity results.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Sessions per day | 40 |
| Recordings within the 30-day window | about 1,200 |
| Recordings reviewed in a weekly 30-minute session | 10 |
| Contact page visits per month | 300 |
| Form completion before the fix | 6% (18 enquiries) |
| Form completion after fixing a dead button | 9% (27 enquiries) |

Under these assumptions, one fix adds nine enquiries a month. The point is the method: find one friction point in recordings, change it, and measure the same page for a month. Check whether the gain is real with your own analytics rather than assuming it.

## Do You Need a Privacy Notice or Consent?

Yes. Microsoft's FAQ says you must inform users about the cookie Clarity uses, and its disclosure page recommends a site notice and a privacy policy entry saying you use Clarity and what it captures. For visitors in the EEA, UK and Switzerland, Clarity requires explicit consent before placing cookies. Without it, Microsoft says some features, such as session recordings, may be affected.

On masking: Clarity masks sensitive content by default, with Balanced mode as the default. Text in input boxes and dropdowns is masked in every mode. Microsoft also says Clarity should not be used on sites that target users under 18. ## FAQ

### Is Microsoft Clarity really free?

Microsoft's documentation says it is a free service forever, with no traffic limits and no obligation to upgrade. Check the Clarity pricing page for the current terms.

### Does Clarity slow down my website?

Microsoft states the Clarity script is asynchronous and does not affect how quickly a website loads. Measure your own pages before and after if speed matters to you.

### Do I need cookie consent for UK and EU visitors?

Microsoft says explicit consent is required for users in the EEA, UK and Switzerland before cookies are placed. Using Clarity without consent does not violate its terms, but it reduces functionality, for example by fragmenting sessions.

### Can I use Clarity with Google Analytics?

Yes. Microsoft documents a Google Analytics integration, and you must finish the Clarity setup first. Clarity creates a custom dimension in your property, which can fail if you reach Google's custom dimension limit.

### How long does Clarity keep my recordings?

Playback data is retained for 30 days. Favorited or labeled sessions and heatmap data are kept for 9 months.
