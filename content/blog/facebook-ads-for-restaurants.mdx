## How Much Do Facebook Ads Cost for a Restaurant?

LocaliQ's Facebook benchmarks, updated September 23, 2026, list Restaurants and Food traffic campaigns at a 2.68% click-through rate and $0.45 per click. The all-industry traffic average is 1.93% and $0.60. No restaurant-specific row is published for lead campaigns, where the all-industry average is $1.80 per click, an 8.54% conversion rate and $27.39 per lead.

For comparison, Google Search for Restaurants and Food averages $2.05 per click, a 6.83% click-through rate, an 8.05% conversion rate and $30.57 per lead. Facebook clicks cost about a fifth of Google's, but Google searchers are already looking for a meal. Compare the cost per booking, not the cost per click.

## Facebook or Google: Where Should the First Dollar Go?

Start with Google if people in your area already search for what you serve, because the demand exists. Add Facebook when you want to create demand: a new menu, an event, a slow weekday or a new opening. Our [restaurant keyword list](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-restaurants-in-2026) covers the search side.

A free profile matters first. Check your Google Business Profile hours, menu link and photos before you pay for traffic, since an ad viewer will often search your name next.

## Who Should You Target?

Use a tight radius around the restaurant and describe the audience by the occasion, not by food. A brunch spot and a late-night bar reach different people on different days. Run one ad set per offer so you can read the result.

If you sell alcohol, Meta's alcohol policy sets rules. Ads may never target anyone under 18, and Meta lists 21 as the minimum age in the United States, with other ages by country, such as 19 in Canada. Some countries ban alcohol ads altogether. Follow local law and keep an eye on the policy page, which changes.

## What Should the Ad Show?

Show food that you serve today, in natural light, with the price or offer in the text. A short video of the dish arriving, the kitchen at work or the dining room filling is enough. Keep to one action: reserve, order or visit.

Avoid promises you cannot check. State opening hours and the address in the ad. Use real photos, not stock images of dishes you do not sell. Rotate creative every few weeks and watch for a falling click-through rate against the 2.68% benchmark.

## How Do You Measure Walk-Ins?

Online ads create offline results, so you need a count at the door. Use methods that do not rely on guessing:

- **Offer code:** one word per campaign, entered at the till or booking form
- **Reservation source:** ask "how did you hear about us" in the booking form
- **Landing page link:** use UTM tags such as utm_source=facebook, utm_medium=paid and utm_campaign=brunch. Google Analytics requires those three on a tagged link and treats values as case sensitive

Our [UTM parameters guide](/blog/utm-parameters-track-every-marketing-campaign) explains the tags. Compare codes redeemed to ad spend each week.

## FAQ

### How much should a restaurant spend on Facebook ads?

There is no standard budget. At $0.45 per click, $450 buys about 1,000 clicks, enough to learn which offer works. Scale only after you know your cost per reservation.

### Are Facebook ads better than Google ads for restaurants?

They do different jobs. Google captures people searching now. Facebook builds awareness and promotes offers. LocaliQ lists Google at $2.05 per click for the category and Facebook traffic at $0.45.

### Can restaurants advertise alcohol on Facebook?

Yes, with rules. Meta bans targeting under 18, lists 21 as the US minimum, and requires you to follow local law.

### How do I know if the ads bring people in?

Use a code per campaign and ask on the booking form. Compare redemptions to spend each week.

### Should I boost posts?

The sources checked give no comparison. Use Ads Manager when you need to control audience and objective.
