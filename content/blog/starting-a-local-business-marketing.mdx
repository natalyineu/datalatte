A new local business should do four things in order: research who buys and who competes, claim a free Google Business Profile, collect reviews, and only then test paid ads with a small budget. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of US consumers read reviews for local businesses, so a verified profile with real reviews comes before any ad spend.

This checklist is written for owners opening a café, salon, grooming shop or studio in the US, UK, Australia or Canada. Examples that use numbers are labelled as assumptions.

## What Should You Do Before You Spend Anything?

Start with research. The US Small Business Administration lists six questions: demand for your offering, market size, economic indicators such as income and employment, where customers are, who already competes, and what customers pay for alternatives. It also points to free data tools including the Census Business Builder and Bureau of Labor Statistics demographics.

For a local shop, turn this into three tasks. Write down your ideal customer in one sentence. List the five nearest competitors and note their prices and review counts. Decide what you do better, such as hours, speed, specialty or atmosphere.

## How Do You Set Up Google Business Profile?

Claim it first. Google says adding or claiming a Business Profile is free. Eligible businesses have a physical location customers can visit, or travel to customers. Virtual offices and rented mailing addresses do not qualify. Service-area businesses should use one profile with a service area, and can hide a home address.

Fill in category, hours, photos, services and a phone number, then complete verification. Follow our [25-point profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) for the rest.

## How Do You Get Your First Reviews?

Ask every happy customer, in person, on the day. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 41% of consumers always read reviews, 74% look for reviews written in the last three months, and 32% look for reviews from the last two weeks. Recent reviews matter, so a steady trickle beats a one-off push.

Do not buy reviews or offer rewards for positive ones. Our [review management guide](/blog/ai-agent-for-google-reviews-management) covers replying at scale.

## Which Marketing Channels Come After That?

Order by cost and speed. Free first: your profile, a simple website with address, hours and booking, and word of mouth. Low cost next: email or SMS to people who already bought, so they come back. Paid last: Google Ads for searches like "dog groomer near me".

Meta ads can help for visual businesses, but they reach people who were not looking. For low-cost ideas, read [cheap marketing ideas](/blog/cheap-marketing-ideas-small-business). For deciding how much to spend, see the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).

## What Do Google Ads Cost for a Local Business?

WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks list all-industry averages of $5.42 per click, a 6.64% click-through rate, $66.69 per lead and an 8.18% conversion rate. Some categories relevant here:

| Category | Avg CPC | Conversion rate | Cost per lead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personal Services | $7.17 | 12.34% | $54.60 |
| Beauty and Personal Care | $4.62 | 10.35% | $39.25 |
| Animals and Pets | $4.06 | 16.22% | $31.50 |
| Health and Fitness | $6.17 | 6.94% | $67.36 |
| Restaurants and Food | $2.05 | 8.05% | $30.57 |

These are averages across advertisers, and a "conversion" is whatever the advertiser counted, so your cost will differ.

## FAQ

### What is the first marketing step for a new local business?

Research your customers and competitors, then claim a free Google Business Profile.

### How much should a new business spend on marketing?

No figure is verified for all businesses. Start with a small test, such as the $600 example above, and measure cost per new customer.

### Do I need a website?

Yes, a simple one with address, hours, services and booking helps, and your profile can link to it.

### Are online reviews that important?

BrightLocal's 2026 survey says 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses.
