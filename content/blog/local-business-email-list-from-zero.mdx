To build a local business email list from zero, ask every customer at the moment they are in front of you, make joining one tap or one line, and send only to people who agreed. A target of 500 subscribers in 90 days is about 39 sign-ups a week, or roughly 6 a day. That is a goal to plan around, not a typical result, because no public benchmark says how fast local lists grow.

This guide covers the legal basics, the free tools, three places to collect addresses and a worked example. Rules are from the US FTC and the UK ICO. Check your own country's law if you are elsewhere.

## What Are the Rules for Collecting Emails?

In the US, the FTC's [CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) says every commercial email needs accurate header information, a non-deceptive subject line, a clear ad disclosure, your physical postal address and a working opt-out. Opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days. The page says each violating email can cost up to $53,088, and that hiring a sender does not transfer your responsibility.

In the UK, the ICO's [email marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/) says you must not send marketing emails to individuals without specific consent. A soft opt-in covers existing customers only if you offered an opt-out when you collected their details and include one in every message. Bought lists are not covered. Never buy a list.

## Which Free Tool Should You Start With?

You do not need a paid plan on day one. Mailchimp's [pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/) lists a free plan with a limit of 250 contacts and a maximum of 500 sends a month or 250 a day, checked on 31 October 2026. It shows the Essentials plan from $13 a month, with the later price not shown. Plans change often, so check before you commit.

Mailchimp's help describes [embedded and pop-up sign-up forms](https://mailchimp.com/help/customize-embedded-signup-form/). Whichever form you use, you need a page with a web address to point a QR code at, and any QR generator can encode it. Check which form types your plan includes. At 250 contacts the free plan will fill during the 90 days, so decide in advance whether to upgrade. ## Where Do You Collect the First 100 Addresses?

Start with people already in contact with you. They are the only group that is warm, legal to ask and free to reach.

| Where | How | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At the counter or desk | Staff ask, tablet or QR code on the till | Highest intent; ask at payment |
| Receipts and booking confirmations | Link to the sign-up form | Customers see it after the visit |
| Website and Google Business Profile | Form on your site or a link to it | Needs traffic first |
| Social profiles | Link in bio | Followers are not subscribers until they sign up |

Give a reason to join that you can deliver, such as early access to a seasonal menu or an offer on the next visit. Say what you will send and how often. The [email list building strategies](/blog/email-list-building-strategies) guide adds more tactics, and the [1,000 subscribers guide](/blog/how-to-grow-email-list-1000-subscribers-local-business) covers the next stage.

## What Should the First Email Say?

Send a welcome message within a day of sign-up, while the person remembers you. Keep it short: thank them, deliver the offer you promised, say what to expect and how often. Include your postal address and an unsubscribe link, as the FTC guide requires.

After that, pick a rhythm you can keep. A fortnightly email you actually send beats a weekly plan that stops in week five. Each message should do one job, such as announcing a new product, an event or an opening-hours change. For a local example, see [coffee shop email newsletters](/blog/coffee-shop-email-newsletters).

## The Math: Can a Counter Reach 500 in 90 Days?

All figures are **assumptions** for a single-location shop. Replace them with your own.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Customers served per week (assumption) | 400 |
| Share asked to join (assumption) | 50% |
| Share of those asked who agree (assumption) | 15% |
| Counter sign-ups per week | 30 |
| Other sources per week (assumption) | 5 |
| Total per week | 35 |
| Total after 13 weeks | about 455 |

At 35 a week the shop lands near 455 after 90 days, a little short of 500. To close the gap, ask more customers (70% instead of 50% adds about 12 a week) or improve the offer. If the shop sees only 150 customers a week, the same rates give about 11 a week and roughly 140 after 13 weeks, so a smaller goal fits.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to build an email list from zero?

It depends on how many customers you see and how many you ask. The example above shows about 455 in 90 days at 400 customers a week, under stated assumptions. No public benchmark gives a typical local business figure.

### Can I email customers who gave me their address at the till?

In the US, CAN-SPAM allows commercial email if you follow its rules, including an opt-out. In the UK, you need consent or a valid soft opt-in. Tell people what they are signing up for either way.

### Is it OK to buy an email list?

No. The ICO says bought-in lists are not covered by the soft opt-in, and a purchased list also carries no reason for people to open your messages.

### Do I need a paid email tool?

Not at the start. Mailchimp's free plan allows 250 contacts and 500 sends a month as of 31 October 2026. Move up when you pass the limit.
