A coffee shop website converts when a hungry visitor on a phone can see hours, the menu and an order or directions button within seconds. For a cafe, a conversion is usually a tap on directions, a call, an online order or a catering enquiry. Google's Core Web Vitals give measurable speed targets: Largest Contentful Paint of 2.5 seconds or less, Interaction to Next Paint of 200 milliseconds or less and Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less.

This guide covers what to fix first, which tools show where visitors get stuck and how to link your site to your Google Business Profile. It does not quote cafe conversion rates, because no reliable published figure for coffee shop websites was found. The worked example uses assumptions.

## What Should a Coffee Shop Homepage Show First?

Put the answer to the visitor's question at the top: where you are, whether you are open and how to order. Nielsen Norman Group found that 79% of test users always scanned a new page and only 16% read word by word. Its advice is to lead with the conclusion, use plain subheadings and avoid promotional language.

For a cafe that means an address, today's opening hours, a menu link and one main button above the fold on a phone. Move the story and the team photos lower. Keep one primary action, such as Order ahead, and one secondary action, such as Get directions. Use real photos of your drinks and space, with sizes set so the page does not jump while loading.

## How Fast Should the Site Load?

Use Google's thresholds as the target. web.dev says a page passes Core Web Vitals when all three metrics meet their good thresholds at the 75th percentile of page loads, measured separately for mobile and desktop. You can read your own results in Search Console or PageSpeed Insights.

| Metric | What it measures | Good threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Largest Contentful Paint | Loading speed | 2.5 seconds or less |
| Interaction to Next Paint | Responsiveness | 200 milliseconds or less |
| Cumulative Layout Shift | Visual stability | 0.1 or less |

Common cafe-site causes of slowness are oversized hero photos, auto-playing video and embedded menus saved as large PDFs. Compress images, replace PDF menus with a text menu and avoid pop-ups that cover the page. Google says that beyond Core Web Vitals, other page experience aspects do not directly help rankings, so treat speed as a customer experience fix first.

## How Do You Make Ordering and Booking Easy?

Link straight to the action. Google's Business Profile help says you can add links for booking, reservations, food orders and shopping orders, up to 10 links per category, and mark one as Business preferred so it appears at the top. You can also add a single link for a menu. Use the same ordering URL on your website and your profile.

If you take online orders, reduce checkout friction. Baymard Institute's review of 50 studies puts average cart abandonment at 70.22%, and the top reasons shoppers gave were extra costs (40%), forced account creation (18%) and a long or complicated checkout (17%). So show fees early, allow guest checkout and keep the form short. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) shows the rest of the profile setup.

## Which Tools Show Where Visitors Get Stuck?

Microsoft Clarity's documentation lists session recordings and heatmaps, including click maps and scroll maps, as core features. Watching ten recordings from phone visitors will show whether people can find the menu, tap buttons accurately or give up at the order form. Our piece on [Microsoft Clarity for small businesses](/blog/microsoft-clarity-small-business-free-heatmaps) explains the setup.

If you record visitors, tell them. Clarity's documentation has sections on cookies, consent management and sample wording for a privacy disclosure, so read those before you install it. ## A Worked Example

All numbers are **assumptions** to show the arithmetic, not cafe benchmarks.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly website visitors | 2,000 |
| Share who tap directions, call or order | 4% |
| Share of those who buy | 50% |
| Average ticket | $9 |

2,000 visitors at 4% gives 80 actions, and half turning into a visit or order is 40 purchases, or $360 a month. If moving the menu and order button above the fold lifts actions from 4% to 5% (assumption), that is 100 actions, 50 purchases and $450. The gain is $90 a month, small but cheap. The real gain depends on your traffic. Measure with a before and after count of taps on each button for four weeks.

Need an extra visit driver? See [using AI for website CRO](/blog/using-ai-for-website-cro) for testing ideas.

## FAQ

### What is a good conversion rate for a coffee shop website?

No reliable published benchmark was found, so use your own baseline. Count taps on directions, calls and orders each month and try to improve it.

### How fast should my cafe website load?

Google's good thresholds are LCP of 2.5 seconds or less, INP of 200 milliseconds or less and CLS of 0.1 or less, measured at the 75th percentile.

### Does site speed help me rank?

Google says only Core Web Vitals are used by ranking systems among page experience aspects. Treat speed mainly as a customer experience fix.

### Should I put my menu as a PDF?

A text menu on the page loads faster and is easier to read on a phone. The sources here do not test PDFs directly, but large files slow loading.

### How do I add an order link to Google?

In your Business Profile, select Food ordering or Pickup and delivery, choose Add link and enter the URL. You can add up to 10 links per category.
