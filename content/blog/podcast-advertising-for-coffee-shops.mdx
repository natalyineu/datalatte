Here is a number to hold onto. Suppose a local weekly show gets 1,500 downloads per episode and charges the equivalent of a $25 CPM (cost per thousand). One spot costs about $37.50, and eight episodes cost $300. If a regular customer is worth about $29 in gross profit over six months, you need roughly 11 people to redeem your offer to break even. Those inputs are illustrative assumptions, not benchmarks, and the math is worked out below.

The point of starting there: for a single café, podcast advertising is not a brand-awareness gamble. It is a small, countable test. You buy a handful of spots on a show your neighbours listen to, give them a reason to walk in, and count who shows up.

This guide is for café owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Audience data is American where it says so; if you are in Australia, our [Australian podcast advertising guide](/blog/podcast-advertising-australia-local-business-guide) covers the local numbers.

## Who Is Actually Listening

The Infinite Dial 2026, a national survey by Edison Research of 2,050 Americans aged 12 and over, fielded in January 2026, found that 58% of Americans (about 167 million) consumed a podcast in the last month and 45% (about 130 million) in the last week ([Edison Research via SSRS](https://ssrs.com/insights/the-infinite-dial-2026/)). The same page reports that 68% of 35 to 54-year-olds consumed a podcast in the last month, which is a large slice of the people who buy coffee on the way to work.

On the money side, IAB and PwC's report puts US podcast advertising at $2.862 billion in 2025, up 17.6% year over year ([Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/?p=102310), reporting the IAB/PwC figures). Advertisers are spending more on podcasts, which also means the best national shows are expensive and crowded. A café does not need them. Reach is national; your customers live within about a mile or two.

## Three Ways to Buy, and Which One Suits a Café

**1. Direct sponsorship of a local show.** You contact the host, agree a flat fee for a number of episodes, and the host reads your copy. This is the best fit for a single location, because the audience is mostly people who could walk in.

**2. A podcast marketplace or network.** Platforms such as Libsyn's AdvertiseCast let you browse shows and buy by CPM. Useful for finding shows, but audiences are rarely local, so you pay for listeners who live in other cities.

**3. Streaming audio with geo-targeting.** Spotify and Pandora let you set a radius or ZIP code. This is a different product with its own minimums and rules, and we cover it in [digital audio advertising in 2026](/blog/digital-audio-advertising-2026-spotify-pandora-podcast-ads). This article stays with option 1.

## Find Shows Your Regulars Already Hear

Search your city name plus "podcast" on Apple Podcasts and Spotify, then look in places where local media hide their audio: newspaper and magazine sites, community radio, chambers of commerce, universities, sports clubs and local business associations. Good fits for a café:

- Local food, drink and "things to do" shows.
- Neighbourhood or city-politics shows with a loyal weekly audience.
- Shows run by the owners of non-competing local businesses (a bike shop, a bookstore, a co-working space).
- Local sports and high-school team shows, where the audience is parents who drive past your door.

Before you pay anyone, ask for three things:

1. **Downloads per episode over the last 30 days**, not total lifetime downloads. Ask for a screenshot from the hosting dashboard.
2. **Where listeners are.** Many hosting dashboards report listener location by city or country. You want most of the audience within your catchment.
3. **Which other businesses sponsor the show.** Listen to two or three episodes. If every sponsor is a local business, the audience trusts local recommendations. If sponsors are all national subscription brands, the audience may not be local at all.

Treat downloads as an upper limit on exposure, not a head count. A download is not proof someone pressed play, and one person can download an episode on two devices.

## What a Spot Costs (and Why Old Rate Cards Are Only a Yardstick)

Local shows rarely publish rates, so the price is whatever you and the host agree. To judge whether a quote is sane, convert it into a CPM: price divided by downloads, times 1,000.

For an outside yardstick, Libsyn's AdvertiseCast marketplace reported an average CPM of $22.24 for a 60-second spot in July 2023 ([Libsyn](https://libsyn.com/blog/?p=13697)). That is dated, and marketplaces skew toward larger shows, so treat it as a rough marker only. We found no current published average and no verified figure for 30-second spots, so we quote neither.

Practical rules for negotiating:

- Ask for a **package of 6 to 8 episodes** rather than one. One mention is easy to miss.
- Ask for a **discount for a small business** and for the right to renew at the same price if the test works.
- Ask for **one mid-roll read** (inside the episode) rather than a pre-roll, if the host has a view on which gets more attention. This is a question to ask, not a proven rule.
- Give the host a free coffee now and then. Hosts who like your product sound more natural.

## Host-Read vs Pre-Recorded

A host-read ad is the host speaking your message in their own voice. A pre-recorded ad is a produced spot, often from you. For a café, a short host-read is usually the better buy because a recommendation from a familiar voice is the whole point of local sponsorship.

There is research behind that, with a caveat. A roundup by the podcast platform Gumball (second-hand) credits Sounds Profitable and Edison Research, 2022, with finding that 81% of podcast listeners say they trust host recommendations and 51% are more likely to consider a product after a host-read ad ([Gumball](https://blog.gumball.fm/podcast-advertising-research-roundup/)). The original studies were not reviewed, so treat these as Gumball's reporting; they are four years old. They are a reason to prefer host-reads, not a prediction of your results.

### A Brief That Works

Give the host bullets, not a script. Hosts sound better in their own words. A good brief fits in a text message:

- Name and neighbourhood: "Mill Street Coffee, two doors down from the library."
- One thing you are known for: "Their cardamom bun and a proper flat white."
- One offer: "Say 'the podcast sent me' for a free bun with any drink, through the end of the month."
- One place to find you: "Open 7 to 3, Tuesday to Sunday."

Do not ask people to follow you, visit a website and call you. One action only. The offer is the reason someone acts today instead of eventually.

## Make It Countable

A podcast listener does not click anything. They hear your name while walking, driving or washing up, and may remember it hours later. Attribution has to be low-friction:

- **Spoken code or phrase.** "Say PODCAST at the counter." Train your team to tap a button on the till or put a tally mark on a card. A tally sheet by the register works.
- **A vanity URL.** A short page such as yourcafe.com/show with the offer and a map. Add a UTM tag so your analytics show visits from that source; see our [UTM parameters guide](/blog/utm-parameters-track-every-marketing-campaign).
- **A QR code on the cup sleeve or counter** for the same page.
- **A tracked phone number** if you take pre-orders by phone; see call tracking for local business.
- **Branded search.** Check Google Business Profile insights for searches on your café name after the first episode airs. A lift alongside redemptions is a good sign, not proof.

Also ask new customers "how did you find us?" for the whole test. People forget the code but remember the show.

Once you have listeners, send them somewhere that holds on to them. A fully filled [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-coffee-shops) makes sure the person who searches your name after the episode finds your hours, photos and reviews.

## The Math

Take a local show with 1,500 downloads per episode. You buy eight episodes. Every row below is an **assumption** for illustration, other than the $25 CPM, which is set near the AdvertiseCast 2023 average of $22.24 for a 60-second spot. Replace them with the show's real numbers and your own sales data.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Episodes bought (assumption) | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| Downloads per episode (assumption) | 1,500 | 1,500 | 1,500 |
| CPM (assumption) | $25 | $25 | $25 |
| Total cost | $300 | $300 | $300 |
| Share of listeners who live nearby (assumption) | 40% | 60% | 80% |
| Nearby listeners reached | 4,800 | 7,200 | 9,600 |
| Redemption rate (assumption) | 0.10% | 0.25% | 0.50% |
| Redeemers | 4.8 | 18 | 48 |
| Cost per redeemer | $62.50 | $16.67 | $6.25 |
| Visits per redeemer in 6 months (assumption) | 2 | 6 | 9 |
| Average ticket (assumption) | $7.50 | $7.50 | $7.50 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 65% | 65% | 65% |
| Gross profit per redeemer | $9.75 | $29.25 | $43.88 |
| Free pastry cost per redeemer (assumption) | $1.50 | $1.50 | $1.50 |
| Net gain or loss | -$260 | +$200 | +$1,734 |

How to read it: net gain is redeemers multiplied by (gross profit less the pastry), minus the $300 fee. The 0.25% base redemption rate means 1 in 400 reached listeners acts, which is a guess, and so is every other behavioural number here. The Strong column is a best case and should not be used for planning.

Two numbers matter more than the rest:

- **Break-even redeemers = cost / (gross profit per redeemer - pastry cost).** In the Base case that is $300 / $27.75, about 11 people. If you cannot picture 11 people from this audience walking in over eight weeks, pick another show.
- **Repeat visits decide the result.** Moving from 2 visits to 6 turns a loss into a profit with no change in the ad. That is why the offer should bring people back (a stamp card, a second-visit discount) rather than give away the whole margin on day one.

## Pilot, Then Decide

Run the test like this:

1. **Weeks 1 to 8:** the sponsored episodes air. Change nothing else in your marketing if you can.
2. **Weekly:** count redemptions, QR scans, vanity-URL visits and "how did you find us" answers.
3. **Week 9:** work out cost per redeemer and compare with your break-even. If you are at or below break-even and customers are returning, renew. If not, change the offer or the show, not both at once.

Podcast ads suit cafés with a loyal base, a clear specialty and decent reviews. If your Google rating is low, fix that first.

## Do This Today

1. List five local shows by searching your city name plus "podcast" on Apple Podcasts and Spotify, and by checking local newspaper, radio and chamber-of-commerce sites.
2. Email your top two with a short pitch: who you are, one thing you are known for, and a request for last-30-day downloads, listener locations and current sponsors.
3. Decide your break-even number: work out gross profit per new regular, then divide the spot cost by that figure.
4. Write a one-line offer and a three-bullet host brief, and set up a tally on the till, a vanity URL with a UTM tag and a QR code.
5. Agree a package of 6 to 8 episodes with a renewal option, and put a day-60 review in your calendar.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does a local podcast sponsorship cost?**
A: There is no standard rate for local shows; the price is negotiated. As a yardstick only, Libsyn's AdvertiseCast reported an average CPM of $22.24 for a 60-second spot in July 2023. Convert any quote to a CPM (price divided by downloads, times 1,000) and judge it against your break-even.

**Q: How many episodes should I buy?**
A: Six to eight is a sensible test. That is my recommendation, not an industry rule. One episode gives you almost no chance of being remembered, and a six-month commitment is too much before you have data.

**Q: Is a podcast better than Instagram or Google ads for a coffee shop?**
A: They do different jobs. Google Ads and your Business Profile catch people already searching. A podcast puts your name in front of people who are not searching yet. For the Instagram side, see [Instagram Reels for coffee shops](/blog/instagram-reels-for-coffee-shops). Most cafés should get search and profile basics right first.

**Q: What if the host will not share download numbers?**
A: Be cautious. Most hosting platforms show downloads per episode, so a host who will not share is either not tracking or not confident in the numbers. Offer a smaller, shorter test or move on.
