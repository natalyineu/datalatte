A pixel tag is a small snippet of code on your website that records visits and actions, such as a form submission or a booking, and sends them to an ad or analytics platform like Google, Meta, TikTok or Microsoft.

It lets those platforms show which ads led to real customers and lets you retarget people who visited your site. If you run paid ads and want to know which ones produce customers, you need one.

Technically, the snippet is JavaScript that you paste into your site or add through Google Tag Manager. Once installed, it runs every time someone loads a page. Without one, you know how many clicks you bought, but not whether any of them became customers.

## What does a pixel tag do?

When someone visits your website and your pixel tag is installed:

1. The script loads and sends a request to the ad platform's server. Historically this was a 1x1 invisible image, which is where the name "pixel" comes from.
2. The request carries details such as the page URL, the event type and identifiers the platform can match to a user, such as cookies.
3. If that person later sees your ad on Facebook, Google or TikTok, the platform can connect the ad view to the website visit.

Without a pixel, the ad platform cannot tell whether the people clicking your ads are the ones who later booked.

## Why do clicks alone mislead you?

**Assumption, not data:** the numbers below are made-up round figures, not a client result and not a market statistic. Swap in your own.

Two ads run for a week at the same price per click. Assumptions: both ads cost $2 per click. Ad A attracts casual browsers, and 4% of its clicks book. Ad B attracts people looking for the exact service, and 8% of its clicks book. A booking is worth $60.

| Line | Ad A | Ad B |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks | 150 | 100 |
| Spend (clicks x $2) | $300 | $200 |
| Bookings (clicks x rate) | 6 | 8 |
| Cost per booking | $50 | $25 |
| Revenue (bookings x $60) | $360 | $480 |
| Profit before other costs | $60 | $280 |

Ad A has 50% more clicks and earns less than a quarter of Ad B's profit ($60 vs $280). Without a pixel you keep Ad A, because clicks are all you can see. A pixel with a booking event makes the last four rows visible.

The same data feeds retargeting. Suppose (assumption) 1,000 people visit your site this month and 20 book. The other 980 are people who showed interest and left. A pixel lets Meta and Google build an audience from those 980 so you can show them one more ad, instead of paying to find strangers again.

## Which pixel tags should a local business install?

### 1. Meta Pixel (Facebook & Instagram)

Required for any business running Facebook or Instagram ads. The Meta Pixel:
- Tracks who visits your website from Meta ads
- Enables retargeting on Facebook and Instagram
- Builds "lookalike audiences" (new customers similar to your existing ones)
- Reports which ad clicks led to bookings or contact form submissions

**Where it goes:** in the `<head>` of every page on your website.

```html
<!-- Meta Pixel Code -->
<script>
!function(f,b,e,v,n,t,s)
{if(f.fbq)return;n=f.fbq=function(){n.callMethod?
n.callMethod.apply(n,arguments):n.queue.push(arguments)};
if(!f._fbq)f._fbq=n;n.push=n;n.loaded=!0;n.version='2.0';
n.queue=[];t=b.createElement(e);t.async=!0;
t.src=v;s=b.getElementsByTagName(e)[0];
s.parentNode.insertBefore(t,s)}(window, document,'script',
'https://connect.facebook.net/en_US/fbevents.js');
fbq('init', 'YOUR_PIXEL_ID');
fbq('track', 'PageView');
</script>
```

Replace `YOUR_PIXEL_ID` with the ID from your Meta Events Manager. Meta also offers the Conversions API, which sends events from your server; if you run both, Meta's documentation says to pass an `eventID` so the two copies can be deduplicated ([Meta Pixel reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/meta-pixel/reference), read 3 Nov 2026).

### 2. Google Tag (gtag.js)

Google says the global site tag (gtag.js) is now the Google tag, a single tag you add to your site that is configurable from Google Ads, Google Analytics and Campaign Manager 360 ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11994839), read 3 Nov 2026). One tag can serve both:
- **Google Analytics 4:** how many visitors, what they do on your site, where they came from
- **Google Ads:** which clicks led to bookings, calls, or form submissions (conversion tracking)

```html
<!-- Google Tag -->
<script async src="https://www.googletagmanager.com/gtag/js?id=G-XXXXXXXXXX"></script>
<script>
  window.dataLayer = window.dataLayer || [];
  function gtag(){dataLayer.push(arguments);}
  gtag('js', new Date());
  gtag('config', 'G-XXXXXXXXXX'); // Your GA4 Measurement ID
  gtag('config', 'AW-XXXXXXXXX'); // Your Google Ads Conversion ID (if running Google Ads)
</script>
```

### 3. TikTok Pixel and Microsoft UET tag

If you run TikTok ads, create a TikTok Pixel in TikTok Ads Manager. If you run Microsoft Advertising, its Universal Event Tracking (UET) tag does the same job as the Google tag.

## Is Google Tag Manager the easiest way to install them?

Instead of adding each pixel directly to your website code, use **Google Tag Manager (GTM)**, which Google lists as free and describes as a way to manage tags without changing your website code ([Tag Manager Help](https://support.google.com/tagmanager/answer/7582054), read 3 Nov 2026). It lets you manage many tags from one dashboard without editing your site for every new tag.

1. Install one GTM snippet on your website, once.
2. Add every other tag (Meta Pixel, Google Analytics, TikTok, LinkedIn) through the GTM interface, with no code edits.

```html
<!-- Google Tag Manager - paste in <head> -->

'https://www.googletagmanager.com/gtm.js?id='+i+dl;f.parentNode.insertBefore(j,f);
})(window,document,'script','dataLayer','GTM-XXXXXX');</script>

<!-- Also paste in <body> immediately after opening tag -->
<noscript><iframe src="https://www.googletagmanager.com/ns.html?id=GTM-XXXXXX"
height="0" width="0" style="display:none;visibility:hidden"></iframe></noscript>
```

If you use more than two ad platforms, GTM usually saves effort. If you only use Google, the Google tag alone is fine.

## Which events should a pixel track?

A pixel firing on a pageview is just the baseline. **Event tracking** tells the platform when something valuable happened. The six events below are Meta standard events ([Meta Pixel reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/meta-pixel/reference), read 3 Nov 2026).

| Event | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| `PageView` | Someone loaded your page | Basic reach measurement |
| `ViewContent` | Someone viewed a page you care about, such as a service page | Mid-funnel intent signal |
| `Lead` | Someone completed a sign-up or enquiry | Direct lead attribution |
| `Schedule` | Someone booked an appointment to visit your location | Conversion, highest value |
| `Contact` | Someone contacted you by phone, SMS, email or chat | Offline intent signal |
| `Purchase` | A purchase or checkout was completed | E-commerce revenue tracking |

For a salon, fitness studio or coffee shop, track at minimum `Lead` (contact form submission) and `Schedule` (booking confirmation). These are the events that prove an ad worked.

**Meta Pixel event example** (add after the base pixel on your booking confirmation page):

```html
<script>
fbq('track', 'Schedule'); // fires when booking is confirmed
</script>
```

**Google Ads conversion example** (add to your "thank you" page):

```html
<script>
gtag('event', 'conversion', {
  'send_to': 'AW-XXXXXXXXX/XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX'
});
</script>
```

## What about privacy and consent?

Privacy rules depend on where your visitors live. This is general information, not legal advice.

- **UK and EU:** consent rules for cookies and similar tracking technologies apply, and the usual approach is to block non-essential pixels until the visitor actively accepts. The UK regulator's guidance page returned an access error when this article was written, so no rule is quoted from it. Read the current text or ask an adviser.
- **California:** the California Attorney General says the CCPA applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California and meet any of these: gross annual revenue over $25 million; buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more California residents or households; or earning half or more of revenue from selling personal information. Businesses that sell personal information must provide a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link ([California Attorney General](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa), read 3 Nov 2026). Many small local businesses fall below those thresholds, but check.

If you have visitors from the UK or EU, use a consent management platform that blocks pixels until the visitor accepts and works with Google Tag Manager.

## How do you check that your pixel works?

Do not assume installation worked.

- **Meta Pixel:** install the Meta Pixel Helper Chrome extension, visit your website and check whether the pixel fired and which events triggered.
- **Google Tag:** use Google Tag Assistant to see which tags fire on your pages and whether they report errors.
- **GTM Preview Mode:** in Google Tag Manager, use Preview and enter your URL to watch tags fire as you navigate.

## What is a 20-minute pixel check?

1. **Minutes 0 to 5:** open your site with the Meta Pixel Helper and Tag Assistant. Write down which tags fire.
2. **Minutes 5 to 10:** make a test booking or send a test contact form. Did `Lead` or `Schedule` fire exactly once?
3. **Minutes 10 to 15:** open Events Manager and GA4 Realtime. Does the test show up?
4. **Minutes 15 to 20:** list your two main ads. For each, write clicks, bookings and cost per booking, like the table above. Which one would you cut?

## FAQ

### Can I install multiple pixel tags on the same website?

Yes. Each platform's tag fires independently, and Google Tag Manager makes managing several easier. Too many tags can slow page loads, so keep only the ones you actively use.

### What is the difference between a pixel tag and a cookie?

The pixel tag is the code that runs when someone visits your page. A cookie is a small file the code may store in the browser to recognise that visitor later. Browsers and privacy laws limit tracking, so first-party data such as email lists and CRM records is worth building.

### Do I need a pixel tag if I only run Google Search Ads?

You need the Google tag with conversion tracking set up. Google says that to use Smart Bidding you need conversion tracking enabled, and without it Google Ads shows clicks but not which ones led to bookings.
