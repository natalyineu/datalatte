Connected TV (CTV) advertising in Phoenix lets a local business show video ads on streaming services to households in the ZIP codes or radius it chooses. You can buy it through a self-serve platform or a programmatic DSP, and many small businesses start with a modest test budget for four to six weeks before scaling. Minimums and prices change, so confirm current terms with any platform before you commit.

This guide covers the Phoenix CTV landscape: which kinds of platforms are available, how to set up targeting, how to plan a budget and how to measure results.

## The Phoenix CTV Market

Phoenix is one of the largest metro areas in the US, with a growing population, many new residents from other states, a large Hispanic community and a strong winter seasonal population. That mix makes local targeting and creative choices matter. Streaming viewing is widespread, and several services offer ad-supported tiers, though availability and ad formats change often.

**Niches that often suit CTV:** fitness studios, hair salons, pet groomers, family services and local restaurants, especially businesses with a defined neighbourhood and a visual offer. ## Why CTV Can Work for Local Businesses

Unlike social media, which reaches people who follow certain accounts, CTV can reach households in a chosen area while they watch shows. These are potential recurring customers: they live nearby, they watch regularly and they see your ad in a full-screen environment.

Three advantages for Phoenix businesses:

1. **ZIP-code or radius precision:** target the neighbourhoods where your customers live.
2. **Household-level reach:** the ad appears on the living-room screen, not a single phone.
3. **TV-style brand building:** a short, well-made video can build familiarity faster than a small display ad.

CTV is a top-of-funnel awareness channel. It supports your search and map visibility rather than replacing it.

## Types of Platforms for Phoenix Advertisers

Small businesses usually reach streaming inventory in one of three ways. Availability and minimums change, so ask for current details.

- **Self-serve ad managers from streaming services:** Some services offer self-serve tools for smaller advertisers, with local targeting. Check whether they have a minimum spend.
- **Programmatic DSPs:** Platforms like StackAdapt and The Trade Desk can buy across many streaming apps from one dashboard. They offer more control, and often higher minimums.
- **Managed services:** A partner handles buying, creative direction and reporting for you, usually for a fee.

Common inventory includes ad-supported tiers of major streaming services, free ad-supported platforms such as Tubi and Pluto TV, and sports and live content. Free ad-supported services are often cheaper per impression, though audiences are broader. Ask any provider exactly which apps your ads would run on.

## Targeting Setup for Phoenix

**ZIP-code or radius targeting** is the most important lever. Enter your address and select a realistic radius, or hand-pick the ZIP codes where your best customers live. Phoenix is spread out, so a driving-distance mindset helps. If you serve a specific sub-market such as Scottsdale, Tempe, Mesa or Chandler, say so in your targeting.

**Demographic and interest targeting:** Layer in age, household income or interests if your provider offers them. A fitness studio might target a specific age range, while a family-oriented salon might target households with children.

**Daypart targeting:** Viewing peaks in the evening, and some providers sell off-peak slots at lower prices. A breakfast cafe might test morning slots, but compare results.

**Frequency cap:** Set a cap so the same household does not see your ad too often. A few views per household per week is a common starting range. Too few views makes the ad forgettable, and too many creates fatigue.

## Seasonality in Phoenix

Phoenix has a distinctive calendar. Summer heat keeps many people indoors, which can mean heavier streaming, and the winter season brings seasonal residents and visitors. Check your own booking and sales history for the periods that matter most, and plan campaigns to start a few weeks before your busy periods. Common windows include the January fitness rush, back-to-school, and the pre-holiday beauty season.

## Creative Specifications

Specifications vary by platform, so confirm requirements before you produce your video. Typical needs include:

- **Format:** MP4 or MOV
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 landscape, for full-screen TV viewing
- **Duration:** 15 seconds is a common starting point, with 30 seconds for more complex offers
- **Audio:** Sound is usually on in CTV, so plan good audio, and add on-screen text for your offer
- **Resolution:** Full HD or better

A simple 15-second structure works well: a couple of seconds to establish who you are, most of the time on the core benefit shown visually, and the last few seconds on one clear call to action with your location or a short URL. Keep it simple, and do not try to say everything.

## Budget Planning for Phoenix

There is no single right budget. A practical approach:

| Stage | Goal | Approach |
|-------|------|----------|
| Trial run | Test audience response and creative | A modest budget for four to six weeks in a tight area |
| Core campaign | Build recognition | A steady monthly budget for about three months, with frequency capping |
| Seasonal burst | Support a peak period | Concentrate spend for six to eight weeks before the season |

Ask each provider how many impressions your budget buys at your chosen targeting and what minimum spend applies. Avoid spreading a small budget across too many ZIP codes, because it dilutes frequency.

## Measuring Results

CTV does not generate clicks like search ads. It generates familiarity, so measure with several signals during and after a campaign:

- **Branded searches:** use Google Search Console and your Google Business Profile to see if searches for your name rise
- **Direct website traffic:** a lift during the campaign can indicate recall
- **A campaign-specific phone number, URL or promo code**
- **"How did you hear about us?"** on bookings and intake forms
- **Provider attribution reports:** some platforms model store visits, which are helpful but estimates

Give a campaign at least 60 to 90 days to show measurable effects, and compare against your own baseline. Our [analytics service](/services/analytics) can help set this up.

## Getting Started

1. **Fix your foundation.** A complete [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile), good reviews and a fast landing page make every channel work harder.
2. **Choose a platform.** Compare a self-serve option with a DSP or managed partner, based on your budget and how much control you want.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does CTV advertising cost in Phoenix?**
A: CTV is typically priced per thousand impressions (CPM), and rates vary by platform, targeting and season. Many local businesses start with a modest monthly test budget. Ask providers for current CPMs and minimums for the Phoenix area.

**Q: Can I target specific ZIP codes in Phoenix?**
A: Yes, most CTV platforms and DSPs offer ZIP-code or radius targeting. Choose areas that match where your customers actually live, and avoid spreading a small budget too thin.

**Q: Which streaming platforms should a Phoenix business use?**
A: It depends on your audience and budget. Self-serve tools and DSPs give access to a mix of ad-supported streaming services and free ad-supported channels. Ask which apps your ads would run on, and start with a small test.

**Q: How long does CTV take to show results?**
A: Plan on 60 to 90 days. CTV builds awareness, so you will usually see effects in branded search, direct traffic and customer mentions before you see direct sales.

**Q: Is CTV worth it for a small Phoenix business?**
A: It can be, if you have a clear offer, a defined neighbourhood and strong basics like reviews and a fast site. If those basics are missing, fix them first.
