A demand-side platform (DSP) is the software advertisers use to buy ad space, and a supply-side platform (SSP) is the software publishers use to sell it. As a local business owner, you sit on the buy side. You usually reach a DSP through an agency or a self-serve platform. The SSP belongs to the website, app or streaming service that shows your ad, so you rarely need to touch it directly.

## What Is the Difference Between a DSP and an SSP?

The two platforms sit on opposite sides of the same transaction. A DSP helps an advertiser decide which ad impressions to buy and how much to bid for each one. An SSP helps a publisher offer its ad space to many buyers at once and set the terms it will accept for each sale.

| | DSP | SSP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Used by | Advertisers, brands, agencies | Publishers: sites, apps, streaming services |
| Main job | Choose which impressions to buy and what to bid | Offer impressions to many buyers and set price terms |
| Typical connections | Exchanges, SSPs, ad networks | Exchanges, DSPs, ad networks |
| Does a local shop need one? | Only through an agency or a self-serve platform | No, unless it runs its own site or app |

The table shows why the terms get confused. Both platforms touch the same ad slot, but they work for different parties.

## How Does a Programmatic Ad Sale Work?

Programmatic buying runs as an automated auction that finishes in a fraction of a second. One guide on connected TV platforms describes the core rule simply: the highest bid wins, and the ad plays for the viewer ([Strategus](https://www.strategus.com/blog/ctv-platforms-a-guide-to-the-top-connected-tv-providers), secondary). The general sequence looks like this:

1. A viewer opens a page or app, and an ad slot becomes available.
2. The publisher's SSP passes that slot to demand sources that can bid on it.
3. Each DSP bids for its advertiser, using the targeting that advertiser has set.
4. The highest bid wins, and that advertiser's ad plays.

Exact rules vary by exchange and platform. Ask your provider how its auction works before you assume how your bids compete.

## Which Side Do Local Businesses Use?

The answer is always the buy side. A coffee shop has no ad inventory to sell, so there is no reason to contact an SSP. You either use a DSP that offers self-serve access, or you hire a partner who runs campaigns on one.

Minimums differ widely. Adwave's Netflix guide says programmatic access starts at $30 a day, but the same page also gives a $50 starting point. Confirm the current figure in writing before you commit. For a wider look at what small local buys cost, see [local TV advertising costs for small businesses](/blog/local-tv-advertising-cost-small-business).

## How Do Buyers Target Local Audiences?

Connected TV targeting usually falls into three types. Demographic targeting uses factors such as age, gender and income. Behavioral targeting uses viewer behaviors and interests. Contextual targeting places ads based on the content being watched ([International Advertising Solutions](https://www.internationaladvertisingsolutions.com/blog/connected-tv-advertising-everything-you-need-to-know/), secondary).

For a local shop, the most useful option is geographic. Adwave's page says programmatic platforms let you target viewers by metro area, state, or zip code radius ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/netflix-advertising-small-business), vendor page). Ask whether the radius you want is available on the platform you choose. For location-triggered tactics beyond streaming, see [geofencing advertising for local businesses](/blog/geofencing-advertising-local-business-guide).

## What Does a Small Budget Buy?

The worked example below uses assumptions to show how to read a proposal. The impression figures come from Adwave's page, but the fee and budget are assumptions, not market rates.

- Monthly media budget (assumption): $200
- Programmatic CPM (assumption, within Adwave's stated $35-50 range): $40
- Agency management fee (assumption): 20% of media, or $40
- Total monthly cost (assumption): $240

At a $40 CPM, $200 of media buys about 5,000 impressions ($200 ÷ $40 × 1,000). Adwave's page gives the same range for a $200-400 test, roughly 5,000-10,000 impressions. Including the assumed fee, the effective cost is $48 per 1,000 impressions ($240 ÷ 5,000 × 1,000). Ask the agency to show that split in a line-item report.

## How Does DSP Buying Compare with Google Ads?

Search and programmatic buying charge you differently. The table below sets out the difference.

| Channel | How you pay | Metrics to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Ads search | Pay per click | Click-through rate, conversion rate |
| Programmatic display or CTV | CPM, cost per 1,000 impressions | Impressions, reach, calls or bookings you can track |

Google Ads operates on a pay-per-click model, so you pay only when someone clicks ([Calabasas Blooms](https://calabasasblooms.com/mastering-google-ads-for-florists-what-you-need-to-know/), secondary). That guide defines click-through rate as the share of people who clicked after seeing the ad, and conversion rate as the share of clicks that lead to a purchase or inquiry. Programmatic buys are priced per thousand impressions, so judge them on the actions they drive. For a Microsoft search option, see [Bing Ads and Microsoft Advertising for local businesses](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business).

## What Should You Ask a DSP or Agency Before You Buy?

Ask questions that show how your money moves, because fees can sit on both sides of the sale.

- Which DSP runs the campaign, and do I own the account and the audience data?
- What is the minimum spend, and is the management fee a percentage or a flat amount?
- Which publishers and streaming services will show the ad, and can I see the placements?
- How will calls, store visits or bookings be measured, not just impressions?
- What happens to my campaign history and data if I stop?

## FAQ

### Is a DSP the same as an ad network?
No. A DSP is software for buying across many sources, including exchanges, SSPs and ad networks. An ad network is only one of those sources.

### Do I need an SSP to advertise?
No. SSPs are used by publishers to sell their ad space. As an advertiser, you work through a DSP.

### Who wins a programmatic auction?
According to the Strategus guide, the highest bid wins and the ad plays for the viewer ([Strategus](https://www.strategus.com/blog/ctv-platforms-a-guide-to-the-top-connected-tv-providers), secondary). Exact rules vary by platform.

### What does CPM mean for a local budget?
CPM is the cost per thousand impressions. On Adwave's programmatic Netflix access, CPMs range from $35 to $50 depending on targeting ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/netflix-advertising-small-business), vendor page).

### Can a small shop start with a few hundred dollars?
Adwave's page estimates that a $200-400 monthly test at a $40 CPM reaches about 5,000-10,000 impressions across Netflix and other streaming platforms. Check the platform's live help page for current minimums and whether reporting is included.
