A DSP (demand-side platform) is the software advertisers use to buy ad space. An SSP (supply-side platform) is the software publishers use to sell it. As a local business owner you only ever deal with the DSP side, usually through an agency or a self-serve platform. The SSP belongs to the website, app or streaming service that shows your ad.

This guide explains the two roles, how a single ad sale moves between them, and what that means for a small budget. It uses definitions from Amazon Ads and the IAB Tech Lab. Neither publishes pricing for local campaigns, so no cost figures are quoted here.

## What Is the Difference Between a DSP and an SSP?

Amazon Ads defines a DSP as programmatic software for advertisers that provides automated, centralized media buying from multiple sources. It defines an SSP as programmatic software for publishers to facilitate sales of their advertising impressions. One side buys, the other sells.

| | DSP | SSP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Used by | Advertisers, brands, agencies | Publishers: sites, apps, streaming services |
| Job | Decide which impressions to buy and at what price | Offer impressions to many buyers and set price ranges |
| Connects to | Exchanges, SSPs, ad networks | Exchanges, DSPs, ad networks |
| Do you need one? | Only through an agency or a self-serve platform | No |

## How Does a Programmatic Ad Sale Work?

The IAB Tech Lab describes real-time bidding as a way of transacting media that lets an individual ad impression be put up for bid in real time, through a programmatic on-the-spot auction. In practice, the sequence from Amazon's description looks like this:

1. A person opens a page or app, and an ad slot becomes available.
2. The publisher's SSP sends that impression to exchanges, DSPs and ad networks.
3. DSPs evaluate the impression for their advertisers and bid. Amazon says a DSP does this within milliseconds.
4. The winning bid's ad is shown on the publisher's page.

Amazon says the SSP connects inventory to exchanges, DSPs and ad networks, so exchanges sit in the middle as the marketplace. Amazon also lists a third tool, a DMP (data management platform), which stores audience insights an advertiser can use for targeting.

## Which Side Do Local Businesses Use?

Always the buy side. A coffee shop does not own inventory to sell, so there is no reason to contact an SSP. What you choose is a DSP, either directly or through a partner who runs campaigns for you.

Amazon describes two models: self-serve, where you manage your own campaigns, and managed service, where professionals buy media for you. It states that self-serve Amazon DSP has no management fees, while managed service typically requires a minimum spend of $50,000 USD, which may vary by country.

Other DSPs set their own terms, so ask for minimums in writing. Our [DV360 vs The Trade Desk comparison](/blog/dv360-vs-the-trade-desk-which-dsp-is-right-for-you) and Amazon DSP for small business guide go through the options.

## What Should You Ask a DSP or Agency Before You Buy?

Ask questions that reveal how your money is used, because fees can sit on both the buy and the sell side.

- Which DSP do you use, and do I own the account and the data?
- What is the minimum budget and the management fee, and is it a percentage or a flat fee?
- Which SSPs and exchanges does the campaign buy from, and can I see the placements?
- How do you measure store visits, calls or bookings rather than impressions?
- What happens to my audience data if I leave?

## Worked Example: Reading a Proposal

All inputs are **assumptions** made up to show how to read a quote, not market rates.

- Monthly media budget (assumption): $1,500
- Agency management fee (assumption): 20% of media, or $300
- Total cost: $1,800

If the agency tells you "$1,800 buys impressions," ask what share reaches publishers after platform fees. Neither Amazon nor the IAB publishes an average platform take rate for small campaigns, so the only way to know is to ask the DSP or agency for a line-item report.

## Is Programmatic Right for a Small Local Budget?

It can be, if the audience is local and you can measure a result such as calls or booked appointments. It is a poor fit if the budget is only a few hundred dollars and nobody reviews reports. ## FAQ

### Is a DSP the same as an ad network?
No. A DSP is software for buying across many sources, including ad networks, exchanges and SSPs. An ad network is one source of inventory.

### Do I need an SSP to advertise?
No. SSPs are for publishers selling space.

### Who wins the auction?
Under real-time bidding the impression is put up for bid in an on-the-spot auction, per the IAB Tech Lab. Exact rules vary by exchange and are not published in the sources used here.

### What is a DMP?
A data management platform stores audience insights that an advertiser can use for targeting.

### Can a single shop use a DSP directly?
Some DSPs offer self-serve access. Terms and minimums differ, so check each one.
