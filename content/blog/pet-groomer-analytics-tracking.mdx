A pet groomer needs to track five things: calls, direction requests and website clicks from the Google Business Profile, leads from the website (calls, forms, booking clicks), and booked grooms at the front desk. Google Business Profile and Google Analytics 4 are free and cover the first four. A tally at the desk covers the fifth.

This guide shows the setup in order of effort. Figures come from the sources at the end. LocaliQ publishes no groomer-specific website benchmarks, so compare against your own history. Examples marked as assumptions are illustrations.

## What Does Google Business Profile Report?

Business Profile reports calls, directions, website clicks and bookings, plus the search terms people used to find you. Google defines calls as the number of times a customer clicked the call button, and says calls are only tracked if your profile has a phone number.

Directions counts how many people asked for directions. Google changed how it counts these to account for multiple taps, cancelled requests and spam. Website clicks are clicks on the website link. Bookings appear only when a booking provider manages them.

The searches metric updates at the start of each month, and the data can take up to 5 days to appear. For more on the profile itself, see our [Google Business Profile guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-for-pet-groomers).

## How Do You Track Website Actions in GA4?

Google Analytics defines a key event as an event that measures an action particularly important to your business. After you mark an event as a key event, you can see how many users perform it.

For a groomer, the useful actions are:

- A tap on the phone number
- A submitted enquiry or booking form
- A click on the online booking button

Our [GA4 and tracking guide](/blog/local-business-analytics-and-tracking) walks through setup, and [Google Tag Manager](/blog/google-tag-manager-for-local-website-analytics) is the usual way to add the events without editing code.

## How Do You Know Where Visitors Came From?

Add UTM parameters to links you control, such as the website link in an Instagram bio or an email newsletter. Google says that when you add parameters you should always use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign. Missing parameters show as "(not set)" in reports.

Values are case-sensitive, so "Google" and "google" are different. Google advises lowercase and one consistent convention. Examples from Google's page: one source value per platform, and mediums such as paid_social or organic_search.

Example link: ?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=spring-bio-link. That example is ours, built to Google's pattern.

## Can You Track Phone Calls From Ads?

Yes. Google Ads supports several call tracking types. Calls from ads and calls to a number on your website count as conversions only if they last at least the minimum length you set. Both need Google forwarding numbers.

Clicks on a number on your mobile website track the click, not the call itself. Google states that it can only track those clicks, not the phone calls.

Set the minimum length high enough to exclude wrong numbers. For budget context, see our [guide to Google Ads spend for pet groomers](/blog/how-much-should-a-pet-groomer-spend-on-google-ads).

## How Do You Count Bookings You Cannot See Online?

Many grooming bookings happen by phone, text or at pickup. Add one question to your booking process: "How did you find us?" with fixed choices such as Google Maps, Google search, Instagram, referral and walk-by.

Log the answer in a spreadsheet next to the booking. Treat it as a rough guide, because customers do not always remember. Over a few months it shows which channels bring grooms that profile reports cannot see.

## Which Numbers Belong on a Monthly Dashboard?

Keep it to one page:

| Metric | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Profile calls | Google Business Profile |
| Direction requests | Google Business Profile |
| Website clicks from profile | Google Business Profile |
| Website key events | GA4 |
| Booked grooms by source | Your desk log |
| Ad spend and leads | Google Ads, if you run ads |

Compare each month with the previous one and the same month last year. No public seasonal benchmark exists for groomers, so your own history is the baseline.

## The Math: What Does a Lead Cost? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions**, loosely modelled on the LocaliQ category figures.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend | $300 |
| Tracked leads (calls over the minimum length plus forms) | 10 |
| Cost per lead | $30 |
| Leads that book a groom | 50% |
| Cost per booked groom | $60 |
| First groom price | $70 |

At $300 and 10 leads, each lead costs $30, close to LocaliQ's $31.50 category figure for Animals and Pets. Half of leads booking gives 5 grooms at $60 each in ad cost. A $70 first groom barely covers that, so repeat visits decide whether ads pay. That is why tracking returning clients matters as much as first-time leads.

## FAQ

### What is the first number a groomer should track?
Profile calls. Business Profile counts clicks on the call button, and it is free.

### Do I need Google Analytics?
It helps if you have a website. A key event shows how many users complete an action such as a form.

### What are UTM links for?
They label where a click came from. Always use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, in lowercase.

### Can I track calls without a call tracking tool?
You can count profile call clicks and mobile number clicks. Google says click tracking cannot show the calls themselves.

### Is there a benchmark conversion rate for groomer sites?
LocaliQ publishes search ad data for Animals and Pets (16.22% conversion), not website data.
