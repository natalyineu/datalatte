Affordable marketing help means the lowest cost per new customer, not the lowest monthly fee. Run the break-even test first. At $1,000 a month in ads plus a $600 fee, and an assumed $300 profit per customer, you need about 5.3 new customers a month just to cover costs. A 2023 FSB study, cited by Miles Marketing, found that 38% of UK small businesses lack a marketing strategy, so a written plan is the cheapest improvement most owners can make.

This guide covers local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It explains how to set a budget, which tasks justify paying for help, how to vet a freelancer or agency, and how to protect yourself while you do it.

## Does Marketing Help Pay Off for a Local Business?

Use a simple test. Total monthly cost is the ad spend plus the fee. Break-even is that total divided by the profit you make from one new customer. The inputs below are all assumptions, so replace them with your own numbers after one month of data.

| Assumption-based example | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ad spend (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Management fee (assumption) | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per lead (assumption) | $80 | $50 | $35 |
| Leads | 12.5 | 20 | 28.6 |
| Lead-to-customer rate (assumption) | 20% | 30% | 40% |
| New customers | 2.5 | 6 | 11.4 |
| Profit per customer, first year (assumption) | $300 | $300 | $300 |
| Gross profit | $750 | $1,800 | $3,420 |
| Total cost per customer, including fee | $640 | $267 | $140 |
| **Net monthly result** | **-$850** | **+$200** | **+$1,820** |

Break-even is $1,600 divided by $300, or 5.3 new customers a month. The Tough column fails because cost per lead is high and few leads close.

The lead-to-customer rate is a lever you control. Holding cost per lead at the Base $50, moving the rate from 30% to 40% gives 8 customers instead of 6, which lifts the monthly result by about $600 in gross profit. That improvement usually comes from answering calls quickly and making booking easy, not from the freelancer.

Be strict about profit. Use revenue minus direct costs, and count repeat visits only after you have seen them happen.

A WordStream average of $2 in revenue for every $1 spent on Google Ads is often quoted. It is a secondary, averaged figure, and it is not a target for your account. Your own break-even number is more useful.

## What Is Worth Paying For, and What Can You Do Yourself?

Pay for work where mistakes are expensive and skill matters. For most local businesses, that means setting up paid search and conversion tracking correctly, a one-time review of your listings and booking or enquiry flow, and a reporting setup that connects spend to leads. Settings such as match types and tracking are easy to get wrong and can waste a month's budget.

Keep the work that needs your voice and your daily presence:

- Social posts built from your team's photos, jobs and customer stories.
- A monthly email to your own customer list, sent from a free or entry-level tool.
- Review requests built into your normal checkout or follow-up routine.
- Simple blog posts answering the questions your customers actually ask.

## Freelancer, Agency or DIY: Which Fits?

| | DIY | Freelance consultant | Agency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical structure | Your time | Flat monthly fee or percentage of ad spend | Monthly retainer, sometimes with a minimum ad spend |
| Best for | Social, email, reviews | One or two defined channels | Larger budgets and multi-channel work |
| Main risk | Slow and inconsistent | Depends on one person's capacity | Small accounts may get junior attention |
| What to check | Your weekly time | Past work in your type of business | Who actually runs your account day to day |

For a small account, a freelancer covering one or two channels is often the right starting point. An agency makes sense when the budget and the number of channels justify a team. Whatever you choose, check the day-to-day contact, not just the senior name on the proposal. ## How Do You Vet a Provider Without Getting Burned?

Use these five checks before you sign anything:

1. Ask for a review of your current setup before any pitch. Compare what two providers notice. If they only list problems you could spot yourself, keep looking.
2. Ask for work in your type of business and the numbers behind it, with the period covered. A claim such as "helped hundreds of small businesses", which The Small Biz Expert makes on its London consultant page, is a starting point to test, not evidence.
3. Keep ownership. Your ad accounts, Business Profile, analytics and domain should sit in your name, with the provider added as a user rather than running everything inside their own account.
4. Prefer short terms. A month-to-month arrangement or a 90-day trial with an exit shows the provider expects to earn renewal. Be cautious of lock-ins before you have seen results.
5. Agree on what you will receive. Leads, calls, cost per lead and booked jobs are useful. Impressions alone are not. If a provider cannot connect spend to enquiries, you cannot judge whether the fee is worth it.

## How Much Weight Should Headline Marketing Statistics Get?

Consultancy pages often quote big numbers. One C-Suite Strategy article attributes to Payscale the claim that around 82% of businesses that hired a consultant saw improved marketing strategies within six months. It attributes to an Aspen Ventures study a 20% increase in sales performance for businesses using data-driven strategies. It also cites Content Marketing Institute for the claim that businesses with blogs generate 67% more leads than those without.

All of these are secondary, second-hand attributions. None should be used as a forecast for your account. They are reasons to test an idea, not promises. The only figures that should set your budget are your own cost per new customer and your own break-even point.

## What Should You Do This Week?

1. Write your monthly budget as three numbers: ad spend, fee, and profit per new customer. Divide total monthly cost by profit per customer to get your break-even.
2. Confirm that you hold the logins and ownership for your Business Profile, ad accounts, analytics and domain. Fix any gaps first.
3. Pick one channel to start with, and list which tasks you will keep in-house.
4. Request two written quotes and a short review of your current setup. Compare how each provider would report results.
5. Set a 90-day target in numbers, and add a "how did you hear about us?" question to your booking or checkout.

## FAQ

### How much should a small business spend on marketing help?

There is no single figure that fits every business. Start with the break-even formula above: monthly fees plus ad spend, divided by the profit from one new customer. Published channel minimums, such as AdQuick's $1,500 to $3,000 per campaign, show the floor for some paid channels.

### Is a freelancer cheaper than an agency?

Not automatically. Compare what each fee buys: who works on the account day to day, how often you receive reports, and how easily you can leave. Get written quotes from at least two providers before comparing.

### Should I sign a long contract to get a lower price?

Only after you have seen results. Podbean's point that advertisers can set budget caps and stop a campaign at any time is a fair benchmark for flexibility. A lower price on a contract you cannot exit is not a discount if the work does not perform.

### How long before I know whether help is working?

Judge against the 90-day target you agreed before starting, using cost per new customer rather than clicks. Early calls can arrive quickly, but a single week rarely tells you enough.
