## Where Do Reels Fit Now That Explore Is Retired?

[Bons and Frazer](https://bonsandfrazer.com/insights/facebook-ads-on-a-budget/) reports that the standalone Instagram Explore feed was retired in January 2026, with delivery moving into Reels. That is a secondary report from a UK paid social agency, so check Instagram's current help pages before you plan around it.

For a studio, the practical result is that Reels carry more of the discovery work. A Reel that reaches someone who has never heard of you can start a relationship. A static post mostly reaches people who already follow you. The first two seconds of the clip and the first line of the caption should make a stranger want to keep watching.

## How Often Should a Studio Post Reels?

Mariana Tek recommends posting at a steady cadence rather than in bursts, and describes three to five short-form videos per week as a realistic, sustainable pace for most studios. The word sustainable matters more than the number. A schedule you can keep for six months beats a burst of daily posts that stops after two weeks.

If filming is the bottleneck, start at the low end. One Reel per class block is one practical way to get to about four a week, though the number of blocks you teach is an assumption you should check against your own timetable. Batch-film on one morning a week so that editing and captions do not eat into class time.

## What Should Each Reel Show?

Choose formats that are easy to finish and easy to send to a friend. Useful ideas for a studio include a 20-second form fix, a coach answering one beginner question, a warm-up routine that takes under a minute, a walkthrough of the room before a class starts, and a first-visit walkthrough with a member's approval.

Film vertically, in good light, with clear sound. Keep clips original to your studio. Re-uploading content from another brand or app makes the Reel less specific to your classes, and it weakens the trust that drives a trial booking. ## How Do Reels Turn Viewers Into Trial Bookings?

Views only help if they lead to a class. Instagram and Facebook let businesses add a Book Now button to their profile, according to [Appointy's guide to fitness studio social media](https://blog.appointy.com/2020/12/11/social-media-marketing-for-fitness-studio-gym/). That article dates from 2020, so confirm the current button options in the app before relying on them.

Speed matters after the first contact. Mariana Tek advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, since interest fades fast after a pop-up ends. A simple way to handle this without adding staff hours is to set up an automated sequence, which is covered in [AI tools that can replace a marketing assistant](/blog/ai-tools-replace-marketing-assistant-local-business).

Referrals also compound. [ClubRight's budget guide](https://clubright.co.uk/blog/effective-gym-marketing-budget-friendly-strategies-for-fitness-studios/) gives an illustrative case: even if only half of your members refer one friend, your membership base still doubles. That is an example, not a benchmark, but it shows why a Reel that members want to share is worth making.

## How Do You Measure Whether Reels Work?

Track three numbers for every Reel: saves, shares and trial bookings that you can trace to it. A simple weekly sheet with the Reel's date, its topic, those counts and any boost spend is enough for a studio to start. Ask each new trial guest how they heard about the studio, and record the answer next to the Reel.

Judge a format over four to six weeks rather than one post. A form-fix clip can perform well in saves while a room walkthrough drives more bookings. Keep the formats that bring people into classes, and drop the ones that only collect likes.

## FAQ

### How often should a fitness studio post Reels?

Mariana Tek describes three to five short-form videos per week as a realistic, sustainable pace for most studios. Start at the pace you can keep for months, and adjust once you have a few weeks of saves and bookings to compare.

### Do hashtags help a Reel get seen?

Trafft's blog notes that Instagram allows up to 30 hashtags in a single post, but that is a limit, not evidence of reach. Use a few relevant tags for context, and judge the Reel by saves, shares and trial bookings.

### How quickly should I follow up with a new lead?

Mariana Tek advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades fast after a pop-up ends. Set the sequence up before your next class block so no lead waits a week.

### Is a $50 boost enough to test a Reel?

It is enough for a small test in one local radius, as the [Vertical Impression guide](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026) suggests. Decide in advance how many trial bookings would justify another test, then compare the result with your membership fee.
