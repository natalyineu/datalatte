To build a route of 40 regulars as a mobile groomer, do three things in order: set a tight service area so you drive less, get found on Google with a service-area Business Profile and a few paid leads, and rebook every client before the van leaves.

Forty regulars on a 4 to 6 week cycle is roughly 7 to 10 appointments a week, so the goal is a steady, dense route rather than more one-off customers.

This guide covers where regulars come from, what leads cost, and a worked example you can rework with your own prices.

## How Many Regular Clients Fill a Mobile Grooming Route?

A route fills according to how often each client rebooks, not how many clients you have. Divide your regulars by the length of their grooming cycle to get weekly appointments.

| Regular clients | 4-week cycle | 6-week cycle | 8-week cycle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 40 | 10 a week | about 7 a week | 5 a week |
| 60 | 15 a week | 10 a week | about 8 a week |

If your van handles four appointments a day over four days, that is 16 slots a week. Forty regulars on a 4-week cycle fill most of it. The same 40 on an 8-week cycle leave it half empty, so shortening the cycle is as valuable as adding clients.

## How Do You Get Found as a Mobile Groomer?

New clients often start with a Google search for a groomer who comes to them. Two free steps matter first.

1. **Set up your Business Profile as a service-area business.** Google says that if you have no storefront where you receive customers, you must hide your address. You can list up to 20 service areas and should keep the whole area within about 2 hours of driving.
2. **Collect reviews.** BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read local reviews and 47% would not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.

## How Much Do Leads Cost?

LocaliQ's [2026 search benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/) for Animals & Pets show a 7.49% click-through rate, a $4.06 cost per click, a 16.22% conversion rate and a $31.50 cost per lead. The all-industry average cost per lead is $66.69. These blend Google and Microsoft Ads data and cover many pet businesses, so treat them as a rough guide to what your account might see.

Google's [Local Services](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6233859) page lists Pet grooming among supported business types, though availability depends on country and area. ## What Does It Cost in Practice? A Worked Example

Everything below is an **assumption**. A groomer charges $100 a visit and sees each client every 6 weeks, about 8 visits a year.

| Item | Figure | How it was calculated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost per lead | $31.50 | LocaliQ Animals & Pets average |
| Leads that become clients | 50% (assumed) | Replace with your own booking rate |
| Cost per new client | $63 | $31.50 divided by 0.5 |
| First-year revenue per client | $800 | 8 visits at $100 |
| Forty clients, first year | $32,000 | 40 times $800 |
| Acquisition cost for 40 clients | $2,520 | 40 times $63 |

Paid leads are not the only source, so the real cost is lower if referrals and Google Maps bring some. The point is the ratio: a client worth $800 a year justifies a lead that costs $63, as long as they keep rebooking. ## Why Does a Tight Service Area Pay Off?

Driving is the biggest hidden cost for a mobile groomer. The IRS sets the 2026 business standard mileage rate at 72.5 cents a mile, which is a recognised estimate of what it costs to run a vehicle.

Take another **assumption**. Grouping clients by area cuts a 90-mile day to 40 miles.

| | Unzoned day | Zoned day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Miles driven | 90 | 40 |
| Cost at $0.725 a mile | $65.25 | $29.00 |
| Saving a day | | $36.25 |
| Saving over 200 working days | | $7,250 |

Fewer miles also mean time for an extra appointment. Assign days to zones, offer new clients the next opening in their zone, and say plainly which days you serve their area. Our guide to [marketing for mobile businesses](/blog/marketing-for-mobile-businesses) covers more on route-based services.

## How Do You Turn One-Off Visits Into Regulars?

Book the next appointment before you leave, at the interval you recommend for the coat. If the client declines, send a reminder text near the end of that interval. A reminder removes the need for clients to remember on their own.

Practical steps:

1. Ask for the rebooking at the end of every visit.
2. Send a reminder two to three days before the cycle is due.
3. Ask happy clients for a Google review the same day.
4. Reward referrals with a free add-on rather than a discount on the core service.

## Which Marketing Mistakes Slow a Mobile Route?

1. **Accepting every postcode.** Long drives eat revenue. Set zones.
2. **Judging success by social followers.** Track enquiries and bookings instead.
3. **No rebooking system.** Regulars come from follow-up, not from luck.
4. **Few reviews.** Without about 20, a growing share of shoppers will skip you.
