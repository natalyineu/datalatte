To promote an event on Facebook, create the event from your Facebook Page, then boost it or build an event ad in Meta Ads Manager. Choose Engagement to collect Interested and Going responses, or Traffic or Sales if the event is in person with tickets. WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put Arts and Entertainment traffic clicks at $0.34 on average, but that is an industry average, not a quote for your town.

This guide is for local venues, studios and shops that run workshops, classes, tastings and shows. Meta's steps were read from its Business Help Center on 1 November 2026, and the menus can change. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## How Do You Boost an Event on Facebook?

Meta says you must first create a Facebook event, then make an event ad in Ads Manager or from your Page. From your Page, click Advertise, select Boost an event, choose the event and click Boost event. If the option does not appear, check that you created the event from your Page and not your personal profile.

For an in-person ticketed event you pick a goal. Sell tickets adds a Get tickets button, and Awareness adds an Interested button. Online or ticket-free events optimise for event responses. You then choose audience, duration and budget, and publish. The ad runs in Facebook Feed.

## Which Objective Should You Choose?

Meta's help page lists three objectives for event ads.

| Objective | What it optimises for | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engagement | Responses to your Facebook event (Interested or Going buttons) | Works with or without tickets; Facebook Feed only |
| Traffic | Visits to your website or event app, Get tickets button | In-person events with tickets only; single image or video |
| Sales | Ticket sales, Get tickets button | In-person events with tickets only; single image or video |

Traffic and Sales ads have more placements, including Marketplace, Stories and the right column (image media only). For a free class or open day, Engagement is the only fit. For a ticketed show, start with Sales if you can track ticket purchases, and Traffic if you cannot.

## How Much Do Event Ads Cost?

LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmarks list average costs across all industries of $0.60 per click for traffic campaigns and $1.80 for lead campaigns. For Arts and Entertainment, traffic campaigns average a 2.71% CTR and $0.34 per click. Lead campaigns average $0.88 per click, a 15.31% conversion rate and $14.59 per lead.

Event ads are not one of LocaliQ's categories, so use these as a rough range. Your cost depends on audience size, season and competing events, so judge by your own cost per RSVP or ticket after the first campaign.

## Who Should See the Ad?

Meta's event advertising guidance suggests these audiences:

- An event engagement custom audience of people who responded to your events.
- A custom audience from a list of past ticket buyers, uploaded in Ads Manager.
- A lookalike audience built from attendees or an email list.
- The Connections field, to reach people who responded to an event from your Page and their friends.
- A city and radius for local events. Meta recommends a separate event for each location.

Events custom audiences can look back up to 365 days and can take up to 24 hours to build, according to Meta's help pages. Audiences that include people under 18 are limited to location, age and gender targeting.

## How Do You Retarget Interested People?

Run a second ad set aimed at people who responded Interested but have not bought. In Ads Manager, edit Connections in the audience section and choose people who responded to your event, or build an Events custom audience from people who visited the event page or started checkout. Keep the message different: dates running out, seats left (only if true), parking or a menu.

Meta's help pages for event ads also list event ad metrics. Check the Meta results columns for RSVPs, link clicks and ticket purchases.

## The Math: What Could a $500 Ticket Campaign Return?

Click price uses the LocaliQ Arts and Entertainment traffic average. Everything else is an **assumption**.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Budget (assumption) | $500 |
| CPC (LocaliQ) | $0.34 |
| Clicks | about 1,470 |
| Clicks to the ticket page that buy (assumption) | 2% |
| Tickets sold | about 29 |
| Ticket price (assumption) | $25 |
| Ticket revenue | about $735 |
| Cost per ticket | about $17 |

On these numbers the ads cover their cost, with $235 left for event costs. If only 1% of clicks buy, tickets drop to about 15, revenue to about $368, and the campaign loses money. Your ticket page matters as much as the ad, so check it loads fast on a phone. For more on budgets, see our [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend).

## What Creative Works?

Meta advises that the image should contain little to no text and recommends an engaging image related to the event instead of a flyer. If you have none, stock images are available during ad creation. Edit the description, since it fills automatically from the event page.

Run ads in the weeks before the event, not only the last days, so there is time to retarget. ## FAQ

### Why can't I see the Boost event option?
Meta says to check that you created the event from your Facebook Page and not your personal profile.

### Can I run event ads for a free event?
Yes, with the Engagement objective, which optimises for event responses. Traffic and Sales are for in-person events with tickets.

### How long before the event should ads start?
Meta does not publish a rule. Start early enough to retarget interested people before the date.

### How much does a Facebook event ad cost?
There is no fixed price. LocaliQ's 2026 averages for Arts and Entertainment are $0.34 per traffic click, but your cost will vary.
