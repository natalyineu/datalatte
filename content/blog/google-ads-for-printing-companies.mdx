A print shop should run two Google Ads campaigns, one for business buyers and one for retail customers, because they search at different times, want different products and are worth different amounts. ## Why Run Separate B2B and Retail Campaigns?

A company ordering 500 business cards and a parent ordering graduation invitations are different buyers. Mixing them in one campaign forces one budget, one bid and one ad message onto both.

Separate campaigns let you set a daily budget per group, write ad copy for each, and schedule them. Google Ads help says ad scheduling controls when ads appear and lets you set bid adjustments for specific days and times. No public data shows when printing searches peak, so check your own Search terms and conversion reports by hour before cutting any time slot.

## Which Keywords Fit Each Segment?

Keyword volumes and prices for print terms are not published for free, so use Keyword Planner in your account. Themes to start with:

| Segment | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B2B | business card printing [city], brochure printing [city], banner printing [city] | Phrase |
| B2B rush | same day printing [city], rush printing near me | Exact |
| B2B trade | printing company near me, corporate printing services | Phrase |
| Retail | flyer printing near me, poster printing [city] | Phrase |
| Retail seasonal | graduation announcement printing, wedding invitation printing | Phrase |

Google describes phrase match as showing ads on searches that include the meaning of your keyword, and exact match as the same meaning or intent with the most control. Use exact for rush terms where you want precision.

## Which Negative Keywords Protect a Print Budget?

Print terms collect a lot of unrelated searches, such as printers, ink and 3D printing. Google's help says negative keywords do not match close variants, so add singular and plural forms.

- Do it yourself: free, template, diy, how to
- Equipment: toner, ink, cartridge, printer repair, 3d printing
- Work: jobs, careers, hiring
- Formats you do not sell: digital only, pdf only

Review the Search terms report weekly and extend the list. Our [negative keyword guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) shows the workflow.

## How Should You Structure the Account?

Keep it simple: one campaign per segment and one ad group per product group.

```
Campaign 1: B2B printing, local
  Ad group: Business cards
  Ad group: Brochures and marketing materials
  Ad group: Signs and banners
  Ad group: Rush and same day

Campaign 2: Retail printing, local
  Ad group: Event flyers and posters
  Ad group: Seasonal (graduation, holidays)
```

Send each ad group to a matching landing page with a quote form and a phone number. A homepage tries to serve everyone and converts none of them well.

## How Do You Track Quote Requests and Calls?

Phone calls often start a print order, so track them. Google Ads help says clicks on a call asset cost the same as headline clicks, and that you can count calls as conversions with call reporting using Google forwarding numbers.

Google also lets you set a minimum call length, and every call at least that long counts. Pick a length that matches a real order enquiry. Add a form conversion on your quote confirmation page. ## How Do You Compete with Online Printers?

You will not win on the cheapest price. Say what a local shop offers: pickup the same day, proofing, a person who answers, and trade accounts. Keep the claims true and specific, such as turnaround times you can actually meet.

Look at who else bids on your terms and what they promise. For similar local-versus-chain thinking, see [Google Ads for florists](/blog/google-ads-for-florists) and [Google Ads for contractors](/blog/google-ads-for-contractors).

## FAQ

### How much should a print shop spend on Google Ads?

No standard figure exists. At a $5.87 benchmark click, $800 a month buys about 136 clicks. Start with B2B, measure cost per order, then scale.

### Should I run B2B and retail together?

No. Separate campaigns give you separate budgets, bids and messages. Retail orders are smaller, so they should not consume B2B budget.

### Should I pause ads when the shop is busy?

Lowering the daily budget or pausing the retail campaign is safer than leaving calls unanswered. No published data covers Quality Score penalties after pausing, so test it in your account.

### Which conversions should I track?

Quote form submissions and phone calls with a minimum call length. Add website call button clicks if you get mobile traffic.

### How long until I know if it works?

There is no published timeline for printing. Judge by cost per order across at least a few weeks of data, and allow for B2B buyers who reorder later. The [10 dollars a day guide](/blog/is-10-a-day-enough-for-google-ads-complete-guide-for-2026) covers learning budgets.
