In BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, 45% of US consumers said they use AI tools such as ChatGPT for local recommendations, up from 6% the year before. If shoppers now ask a chatbot for "a dog groomer near me", the words you use on your site and profile matter more, not less. This guide gives you a seven-step workflow that uses AI for what it is good at (ideas and sorting) and real data for what it is not (demand and rankings).

It is aimed at single-location businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: cafes, salons, groomers, studios. The survey is a US panel of 1,002 adults from a vendor that sells review tools, so treat the figure as a signal rather than a forecast.

## What Local Keyword Research Is

Local keyword research means finding the words people in your area use for what you sell: "dog groomer near me", "balayage Leeds", "yoga classes Brooklyn", "flat white Fitzroy". You care about three things:

- **Intent:** ready to book, comparing options, or just learning?
- **Location:** which suburbs, towns and neighbourhoods do you really serve?
- **Competition:** who ranks now, and can you appear next to them?

Google says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity," with relevance meaning how well your Business Profile matches the search. Your keyword work therefore has to feed both your website and your profile. Our local keyword research guide covers the fundamentals.

## Where AI Helps and Where It Doesn't

| Task | AI useful? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brainstorm phrases and questions | Yes | Fast and wide |
| Group keywords by intent or page | Yes | Saves spreadsheet hours |
| Draft outlines and FAQs | Yes | Edit for accuracy |
| Search volume and cost per click | No | Use a data tool |
| Today's rankings | No | Check Google or a rank tracker |
| Knowing your neighbourhoods | Partly | You know them better |

Google's own guidance on generative AI explains why. It says these tools can be useful "when researching a topic, and to add structure to original content", but that models predict likely words rather than retrieve facts, so output can contain hallucinations and you should fact-check it. The same page warns that generating many pages without adding value may violate Google's spam policy on scaled content abuse.

## Step 1: Seed the List With What You Know

Write down every service, every area you serve and ten questions customers ask at the counter or on the phone. Then prompt an AI assistant:

> I run a dog grooming salon in [suburb, city]. Services: [list]. Generate 50 phrases a local customer might type into Google, grouped as ready to book, comparing options, and learning. Include nearby suburb variations. Do not give search volumes or difficulty scores.

The last line matters. An assistant will happily give you a volume for every phrase, and those numbers are guesses.

## Step 2: Check Demand With Real Data

Take your best 30 to 60 phrases and look at them in tools that measure something.

- **Google Keyword Planner.** Google describes it as a free tool for building keyword lists for Search campaigns, showing estimated monthly searches and costs. Access needs a Google Ads account with account setup, including billing details, completed. You do not have to run ads to look. Keywords with very low volume may not appear at all, so a missing result is not proof nobody searches it.
- **Google Search Console.** Free. The Performance report lists the queries your site already appeared for, with clicks, impressions and average position. Filter for queries with many impressions and a position between about 8 and 20 (my rule of thumb, not Google's): those are pages close to a breakthrough.
- **Business Profile performance.** Shows the search terms people used to find your profile, updated at the start of each month.
- **Autocomplete and "People also ask".** Free and shows real phrasing.
- **Paid suites.** Semrush lists its SEO plan at $139 a month on monthly billing, and Ahrefs lists Starter at $29 and Lite at $129 (the page does not make clear whether those are monthly or annual rates). One location rarely needs them.

For tool comparisons see our local SEO keyword research tools article.

## Step 3: Cluster by Page

Paste your cleaned list into the assistant:

> Group these keywords so each group can be targeted by one web page. Name each group, suggest a page title under 60 characters, and flag any group that needs its own page versus a section on an existing page.

Check the result by hand. Typical groups:

- **Service pages:** "puppy grooming", "deshedding treatment".
- **Area pages:** "dog groomer in [suburb]", but only where you genuinely serve and can add something specific.
- **Question content:** "how often should I groom my dog".
- **Price and comparison:** "dog grooming prices", "mobile vs salon grooming".

Do not mass-produce town pages with only the place name swapped. Google's spam policies name "multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as a doorway abuse example, and list pages that exist mainly to rank as scaled content abuse. One strong service page beats ten thin copies.

## Step 4: Score and Pick Five to Ten

Give each phrase 0 to 2 points on four questions:

1. **Relevance:** do you sell exactly this?
2. **Intent:** would a searcher book today?
3. **Competition:** search it yourself on a phone. If page one is all national brands and directories, a narrower phrase will be easier.
4. **Effort:** do you already have a page you could improve?

Take the top five to ten. Improve existing pages before you write new ones.

## Step 5: Write for People, Then Place the Phrase

Put the main phrase where a person would expect it: the page title, the main heading, the first paragraph, subheadings, image alt text and internal links. Google's title guidance says titles should be descriptive, concise and unique per page, and that repeating the same words does not help. Add the local detail AI cannot know: the parking, the nearest landmark, the suburbs you cover, what a first visit looks like.

If you let AI draft anything, read it all, correct it and remove claims you cannot back up. Google also notes that titles, meta descriptions, structured data and alt text deserve the same review because they can appear in search results.

## Step 6: Carry the Words to Your Google Business Profile

- **Name:** your real-world name only. Google says extra information such as services or location details in the name is not permitted and can lead to suspension.
- **Categories:** Google says choose as few as needed and as specific as possible, as in "This business IS a".
- **Description:** limit of 750 characters, with no links, per Google's help text as quoted by BrightLocal; put the key points in the first 250 characters, because BrightLocal says that is where it is cut off on search.
- **Services and posts:** plain names, accurate details.
- **Reviews:** you cannot script them, and Google's policy bans incentives and selectively asking only happy customers. But a quick "what did we do for you today?" in your request often prompts a specific review.

See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and [AI for local SEO](/blog/ai-for-local-seo).

## Step 7: Measure and Log

Track four things monthly: Search Console impressions, clicks and position for your target queries; Business Profile calls, directions and website clicks; form fills or call-button taps in GA4; and a phone search from your own area. Log each change with a date. Google does not publish how long ranking changes take, so judge across at least a few months, not days.

## The Math

Is a keyword worth a page? Here is a worked example for a hypothetical groomer. Every figure is an **assumption** for illustration only. Replace them with your Keyword Planner and Search Console numbers.

| | Weak phrase | Good phrase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly searches (assumption) | 40 | 90 |
| Share of those searches that click you (assumption) | 5% | 10% |
| Visits a month | 2 | 9 |
| Visit to booking rate (assumption) | 5% | 10% |
| Bookings a month | 0.1 | 0.9 |
| Value of a new client over a year (assumption) | $250 | $250 |
| Value per month | $25 | $225 |

The lesson is not the numbers but the shape: demand, click share, and booking rate multiply. A small phrase with strong intent (someone ready to book) can beat a bigger phrase with weak intent, and your booking page matters as much as your ranking. If a page takes you three hours at $30 an hour, $90, the good phrase earns that back in under a month at these assumptions, and the weak one takes about 3.6 months.

<Callout type="tip">
Never paste AI-estimated volumes into a plan. If a number did not come from Keyword Planner, Search Console, your Business Profile or a paid tool, write it as an assumption.
</Callout>

## Do This Today

1. List your services, the areas you really serve and ten questions customers ask you.
2. Run the Step 1 prompt, then keep the best 40 phrases.
3. Open Search Console and note the queries you already appear for. Add any that look relevant.
4. Run the clustering prompt, pick five to ten priority phrases using the 0 to 2 scoring, and choose one page to improve this week.
5. Update that page's title and heading, then check your Business Profile name, categories and description against the Step 6 rules.

## FAQ

**Q: Can ChatGPT replace a keyword research tool?**
A: No. It is good for ideas, grouping and outlines. It cannot give you reliable volumes or rankings, and Google's guidance warns that models can hallucinate. Pair it with Keyword Planner or Search Console.

**Q: How many keywords should a small local business target?**
A: Five to ten priority phrases tied to your main services and areas. Expand once those pages are solid.

**Q: Do I need a paid SEO tool?**
A: Not at the start. Keyword Planner, Search Console, your Business Profile performance page and autocomplete cover most single-location needs. Paid tools such as Semrush list from $139 a month on monthly billing, so wait until you are acting on the free data.

**Q: Does Google penalise AI-written content?**
A: Google's guidance says AI can be useful, but producing many pages without adding value may break its scaled content abuse policy. Quality, accuracy and usefulness decide, not the tool.

**Q: How long does local SEO take?**
A: Google does not give a timeline. Profile fixes can appear sooner than new content, but plan in months and keep a change log.
