BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 reports that 74% of consumers only look at reviews from the last three months, and 68% will only use a business rated four stars or higher. For a coffee shop, that is a brutal test. Someone standing on your street types "coffee near me", sees three listings, and picks in about ten seconds. If your newest review is from March, you have lost to the cafe next door whose latest review is from last week.

This guide is for independent cafes and coffee shops in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers seven fixes, built on Google's own published rules rather than folklore. It also corrects some advice that still circulates, because two features that older guides tell you to use (profile chat and the Q&A section) no longer exist. One caution on the survey: it is a US panel of 1,002 adults from a vendor that sells review tools, so treat the percentages as a signal, not a forecast.

## How Google Decides Which Cafe to Show

Google's help page on improving local ranking says results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Distance is how far you are from the person searching. Prominence is how well known you are. The same page says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

You cannot move your shop closer to the searcher. You can control relevance (a complete, accurate profile) and prominence (reviews, and how people talk about you). The seven fixes below target those two.

## 1. Get the Basics Exactly Right

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) are specific, and breaking them is the fastest way to get a profile suspended.

- **Name:** it must match your real-world name on signage. Marketing taglines, phone numbers, URLs, hours and location details are not allowed in it. So "Bean There Coffee", not "Bean There Coffee | Best Latte Downtown".
- **Categories:** Google says to use as few categories as possible, make them as specific as possible, and not to use categories solely as keywords. Pick the most specific primary category that fits what you really are (for example coffee shop or espresso bar, if your list offers them), and add a second only if it is a genuine part of the business, such as bakery.
- **Hours:** give regular customer-facing hours. Google says you can use current seasonal hours as your regular hours, and you can mark a business temporarily closed rather than listing hours in an off-season. Update hours before a holiday, not after.
- **Description:** describe what you serve and who you are. Google says not to focus on promotions, prices or sales, and not to include links.

Match your address, phone and website to what appears on your website and other directories.

## 2. Fill the Attributes and Menu Details People Search For

People do not search "cafe". They search "coffee shop with wifi", "oat milk latte", "dog friendly cafe" or "coffee drive thru". Add every attribute your profile offers that is true for you: Wi-Fi, outdoor seating, dog-friendly, takeaway, drive-through, wheelchair access. Add your menu or products where the profile allows it, with your real drink names and categories.

An honest caveat: which attributes appear depends on your category and country, and Google decides what it shows. Fill in what is offered and skip what is not.

## 3. Upload Real Photos on a Schedule

Google's [photo guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862) give hard limits: JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB, recommended 720 x 720 pixels, minimum 250 x 250, in focus, well lit, and without heavy filters or significant alterations. Videos can be up to 30 seconds, up to 75 MB, at 720p or higher. Google also says a logo and cover photo are separate from your general photos, and that a low-quality cover may be replaced by a customer-submitted image.

A simple shot list for a cafe:

- The exterior from the pavement or sidewalk, so a first-time visitor recognises the door
- Two or three interior shots in daylight, showing seating and power sockets if you have them
- Your top five drinks and top three pastries, as they are actually served
- The menu board, readable
- A 10 to 20 second video of a pour or a latte art finish

Phone photos in window light are enough. For naming, categories and ideas, see [how to optimize your Google Business Profile photos](/blog/how-to-optimize-google-business-profile-photos).

## 4. Build a Review Habit, Not a Review Burst

Reviews are where the 74% rule bites. BrightLocal's 2026 figures also say 32% want reviews from the last two weeks, and 89% expect owners to respond. A steady trickle of new reviews beats a big batch followed by silence.

Google's [review help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) confirms you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code. The rules you must stay inside, from Google's [prohibited and restricted content policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114):

- **No incentives.** Payment, discounts, free goods or services in exchange for a review are not allowed. That includes "free cookie for a 5-star review".
- **No selective asking.** Google bars merchants from discouraging negative reviews or selectively soliciting positive ones. Do not run a "how was it?" screen that sends happy customers to Google and unhappy ones to a private form.
- **Do not steer the content.** Soliciting is fine without attempting to influence the rating or contents of the review.

What works within those rules: a QR code at the till and on the pickup shelf, a printed line on the cup sleeve or bag, and a quick verbal ask from the barista when someone says the coffee was great. Ask everyone, not just the happy ones. For scripts and timing, see [how to get more Google reviews for a coffee shop](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop).

**Reply to every review.** Google's guidance is to keep replies professional and brief, avoid generic thanks where you can add something useful, skip promotions, protect the reviewer's privacy, and move complex complaints offline. Our [negative review reply scripts](/blog/how-to-respond-negative-google-reviews-scripts) give you wording for the hard ones.

## 5. Post Updates, but Know What Posts Do

Google Business Profile lets you publish updates, offers and events that show on Search and Maps. Google's [posts help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169) says posts older than six months are archived unless a date range is set. Older advice that says posts "disappear after seven days" is not in Google's current help page, so do not build your routine around it.

Whether posts move rankings is not something Google states, so treat them as a conversion tool: a fresh, photo-led post about the seasonal drink or this weekend's event gives a visitor a reason to choose you. One post a week is a realistic target. Use an event or offer type with a date range for time-limited items, such as a pumpkin spice launch.

## 6. Stop Relying on Chat and Q&A (They Are Gone)

Two common tips are out of date:

- **Profile chat is gone.** Google's [support page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14919062) says chat and call history were removed from Business Profiles as of July 31, 2024. Put your phone number, ordering link and website prominently instead.
- **The Q&A section is being replaced.** BrightLocal's guide (last updated April 2026) reports that Google has discontinued Q&A and replaced it with an AI feature called Ask Maps, which answers questions using your profile, reviews and website. BrightLocal says it launched in the US and India in March 2026, and that businesses cannot edit individual answers. That is second-hand reporting, and rollout may differ in your country.

The practical response is the same either way: put the answers on the page where Google can read them. Keep your attributes and hours complete, encourage detailed reviews (customers who mention "oat milk" or "quiet to work in" feed those answers), and add a short FAQ on your website covering dairy-free options, parking, Wi-Fi and card payments.

## 7. Audit Monthly Like a Customer

Open your listing on your phone, signed out, and search the way a stranger would: "coffee near me", "coffee shop open now", "[your street] cafe". Check that hours are right, the top photos are current, and the newest review is recent. Search for duplicates of your listing. Then open the performance data in your profile and note calls, direction requests and website clicks against last month.

## The Math

What does a review habit return? These are **assumptions** for illustration. Replace them with your own counts.

| | Without a system | With a system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customers asked per week (assumption) | 0 | 100 |
| Share who post a review (assumption) | n/a | 4% |
| New reviews per month | 1 to 2 (assumption) | 17 |
| Newest review age at any moment | weeks to months | days |
| Extra new customers per month from better conversion (assumption) | 0 | 10 |
| Average spend per visit (assumption) | $6 | $6 |
| Visits per new customer per month (assumption) | 6 | 6 |
| Extra monthly revenue | $0 | $360 |

How to read it: 100 asks a week is about 14 a day, which is a handful per shift. The 4% rate and the 10 extra customers are guesses, not benchmarks. The point is the cost side. The routine takes about 20 minutes a week and no ad spend, so even if the 10 extra customers were 3, it still pays. Run the same sum with your real ticket size and visit frequency, and track new-customer counts with a simple "how did you find us?" question.

## Do This Today

1. Open your profile and check the name, primary category, hours and description against Google's rules in Fix 1. Remove any taglines or keywords from the name.
2. Add a logo, a cover photo, and at least eight real photos (exterior, interior, drinks, pastries, menu).
3. Print a QR code that links to your Google review page and put it on the till, the pickup shelf and cup sleeves.
4. Reply to every review you have not answered yet, in under 40 words each.
5. Put a 20-minute weekly slot in the diary: reviews and replies, one post, a photo upload, and a check of hours.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the most important part of a coffee shop's Google Business Profile?**
A: Accurate basics first: name, address, hours and primary category. Then recent reviews. BrightLocal's 2026 survey says 74% of consumers only look at reviews from the last three months.

**Q: How many reviews does a coffee shop need?**
A: Google gives no target number and says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. Aim for a steady flow rather than a number, and compare yourself with the cafes that rank above you.

**Q: Can I give a free coffee for a Google review?**
A: No. Google's policy does not allow incentives such as payment, discounts or free goods or services in exchange for reviews.

**Q: Does my cafe still need to answer questions on Google?**
A: The classic Q&A section is being retired according to BrightLocal. Keep your profile and website complete so Google's AI answers draw on accurate information, and reply to reviews, which are still yours to manage.

**Q: How long until I see results?**
A: Corrections to hours and categories can show within days to weeks. Review momentum and ranking changes build over months. Check your profile's performance numbers month to month, not day to day.
