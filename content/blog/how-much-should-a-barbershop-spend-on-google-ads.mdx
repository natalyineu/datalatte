There is no published "right" Google Ads budget for a barbershop. What can be checked is the price of a click: WordStream by LocaliQ lists $4.62 per click for Beauty and Personal Care in 2026, so $300 a month buys roughly 65 clicks. Start with an amount you can lose while you learn, divide it by 30.4 to get the daily budget, and raise it only after the ads produce booked chairs.

This guide covers independent barbershops and barber-led salons. Benchmarks come from the sources at the end. Worked numbers are labelled as assumptions, because no public source reports barbershop-specific ad results.

## What Does a Click Cost a Barbershop?

The closest published category is Beauty and Personal Care: $4.62 per click, 6.75% click-through, 10.35% conversion rate and $39.25 per lead. The all-industry average is $5.42 per click and $66.69 per lead. Barbershops are not broken out, so treat the beauty figures as a starting range and replace them with your own numbers after a month.

A "conversion" in these benchmarks is whatever the advertiser counted, such as a call or a form. It is not a haircut. Your own cost per booked client will be higher.

## How Do You Turn a Monthly Budget Into a Daily Budget?

Google Ads takes a daily figure. Google's help page says to divide the monthly budget by 30.4: $304 a month gives a $10 average daily budget. It also says Google may spend up to twice the daily budget on a given day, and the monthly ceiling is about 30.4 times the daily figure.

So a $10 day can show a $20 spend on a busy Saturday, balanced by quiet days. Do not set a daily budget so low that the campaign runs out before lunch, because the ads will miss the morning "barber near me" searches.

## How Much Should You Spend in the First Three Months?

Treat the first month as paid research. Spend an amount you accept as a test, watch the Search terms report, and judge on cost per booked client. A bigger budget only helps once a smaller one is already profitable.

A simple ladder works for most shops:

- Month 1: a small test budget on two or three keyword groups
- Month 2: cut the terms that spent without a call and keep the rest
- Month 3: raise the budget only if cost per new client sits below what a client is worth to you in a year

## How Do You Stop Wasted Spend?

Negative keywords are the cheapest saving. Google's help page explains that negatives do not match close variants, so "barber school" will not block "barbering school" unless you add both. Add job, course and product words such as "hiring", "school" and "clippers" if you do not sell them.

Broad match is the default, and Google's match-type page says exact match gives the most control over who sees your ad. With a small budget, start with phrase and exact match, then widen once the search terms look clean.

## Is Google Ads Better Than Free Options First?

A verified Business Profile, current hours and recent reviews cost nothing, and they support every ad. ## FAQ

### How much should a barbershop spend on Google Ads per month?
No source publishes a standard figure. With a $4.62 average click in the beauty category, $300 buys about 65 clicks. Pick a test amount you can afford to lose and judge it on cost per booked client.

### Is $50 a month enough?
At $4.62 a click, $50 is about 10 clicks, which is too few to learn from. A tiny budget can still be useful for one tightly targeted keyword group, but expect slow data.

### How do I set the daily budget?
Divide the monthly amount by 30.4. Google may spend up to twice the daily figure on one day, but the monthly total should stay near 30.4 times the daily budget.

### Do Google Ads work for walk-in barbershops?
They can, if you count calls, direction taps and bookings. Walk-ins are harder to attribute, so ask new customers how they found you.

### Should I pay for Google Ads or Instagram first?
Fix your Business Profile first, then pick the channel where your customers look. Our [barbershop Instagram guide](/blog/barbershop-instagram-marketing) covers the organic side.
