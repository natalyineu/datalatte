## What Benchmarks Can a Restaurant Actually Use?

The only benchmarks in the verified sources are cross-industry averages from Google Ads. WordStream reports an average search click-through rate of 3.17% and an average display click-through rate of 0.46% across all industries, with average cost per click of $2.69 for search and $0.63 for display ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks)). Restaurants are not a separate category in that report, so these figures are a rough reference point, not a target for Glovo.

Google describes its Display Network as reaching people across 35 million websites and apps, including YouTube and Gmail ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)). That reach is a Google product, not Glovo's. It is useful mainly as a reminder that ad platforms show ads widely, so tracking must do the filtering.

Use the benchmarks as a sanity check only. If your Glovo cost per click is far above the cross-industry average, check whether your bid is too high for your margin. Do not treat a single benchmark as proof that a campaign is working.

## How Do You Track Which Glovo Orders Came From Ads?

Attach one unique promo code to each campaign and use it nowhere else. A code such as PIZZA15 for a pizza ad and LUNCH20 for a lunch ad lets you count redemptions per campaign. Without codes, you cannot separate ad-driven orders from regular customers who order anyway.

A simple tracking routine:

1. Create one promo code per campaign and write the code, offer, and start date in a shared sheet.
2. Confirm the code is applied at checkout in a test order before the campaign goes live.
3. Each Friday, count orders per code and divide the ad spend for that campaign by those orders.
4. Pause any campaign whose cost per order exceeds your cap for two consecutive weeks.
5. Retire codes after the campaign ends so they do not leak into organic orders.

## What Does a Worked Budget Look Like?

The example below uses assumed inputs. Replace them with your own menu prices, food cost, and Glovo commission before making decisions.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
|---|---|
| Gross profit per Glovo order (after food, packaging, and commission) | $12.00 |
| Orders attributed to ads per month | 60 |
| Target cost per order (50% of gross profit) | $6.00 |

Scenario A spends $900 per month: cost per order is $900 / 60 = $15.00, which is more than the $12.00 profit per order, so each ad order loses $3.00. Scenario B cuts spend to $360 per month: cost per order is $360 / 60 = $6.00, which meets the cap and leaves $6.00 per order before overheads. The same 60 orders cost $540 less in ad spend.

The cap of 50% of gross profit is a rule of thumb set by this example, not a figure from any source. Your own cap depends on overheads, delivery costs, and how often ad customers return. The method, dividing spend by attributed orders and comparing to profit per order, is what matters.

## How Should You Structure Budget and Timing?

Start with a daily cap rather than a total budget, so one busy evening cannot use up the month. Divide your monthly budget by the number of days you plan to run ads, then review after the first week. Small, steady spend makes it easier to see which campaign produced which orders.

Test time windows before committing to them. Run one campaign during lunch and another during dinner for a fixed period, using separate promo codes, and compare cost per order. The European budgeting principles in [local marketing budget guide for Europe](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide-europe) can help you decide how much to put behind each test.

Avoid large changes in the middle of a test. Change one variable at a time, such as the offer or the time window, so the results stay readable.

## Which Creative and Mistakes Matter Most?

Use real photos of your own dishes rather than generic stock images. Create at least two creative versions per campaign and compare their results using the campaign's own promo code. Google's Display campaigns use the same principle: upload several assets and let the system find the strongest combinations, which only works if you measure results ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)).

Check your menu page on a real phone before launch. If the page loads slowly or the add-to-order button is hard to tap, orders will drop regardless of ad quality. Keep your offer clear: one discount, one clear end time, and one item to order.

The most common error is judging a campaign on clicks or impressions. Clicks are not orders, and orders are not profit. Judge campaigns on cost per attributed order against profit per order.

## FAQ

### How much should a restaurant spend on Glovo ads?

Start with an amount you can lose for two weeks without harm. Then divide that amount by the number of attributed orders you need to make the cost per order fall below your cap. The worked example above shows how a lower budget can produce a better cost per order than a higher one.

### Can I use Glovo ads without promo codes?

You can, but you will not know which orders came from ads. Glovo dashboards and generic order counts cannot reliably separate ad-driven customers from people who would have ordered anyway. Unique codes are the simplest way to separate them.

### Are Glovo's own conversion figures reliable?

This guide does not use any Glovo-published figures because they are not in the verified sources. Check Glovo's partner dashboard and your own order data, and treat any platform figure as a claim to verify against your sales.

### Should I use Google Ads benchmarks for Glovo?

Use them only as a broad reference. The Google benchmarks cover all industries and do not measure food delivery platforms. Your own cost per order and profit per order are the numbers that decide whether a Glovo campaign is worth running.
