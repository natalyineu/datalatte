A bakery should use Google Ads for orders that need planning, such as custom, birthday and wedding cakes. Daily walk-in trade usually does better through a complete Google Business Profile and local search. Run a small Search campaign, send people to an enquiry form or call button, and judge the campaign by paid orders, not clicks. ## How Much Do Google Ads Cost for a Bakery?

WordStream's industry benchmark page, a secondary source last refreshed well before 2026, lists an all-industry average of $2.69 per click on Search and a 3.17% Search click-through rate. Those are the broadest figures available, and they are not bakery numbers. Consumer Services, a closer fit for a shop selling to consumers, shows a $6.40 average search cost per click on the same page, so local competition can push costs well above the average.

Treat any benchmark as a starting range. Replace it with your own click cost after one or two months of data. Your town's competition will matter more than a national average, so the local auction result is the figure to trust.

## Which Bakery Products Should You Advertise?

Pick products where a customer plans ahead and the order is large enough to justify paying for a click. Daily bread and pastries rarely meet that test. Stronger candidates include:

1. Custom and birthday cakes, where people search days ahead
2. Wedding and event cakes, which often need a tasting appointment
3. Corporate orders, such as breakfast platters or branded cookies
4. Seasonal boxes tied to holidays

Split these into separate campaigns or ad groups, each with its own budget, landing page and message. The [bakery keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-bakeries-in-2026) lists search phrases for each group, so the words people type can be matched to the page they reach.

## How Does Quality Score Affect Bakery Ad Costs?

Quality Score is a diagnostic from 1 to 10, set at the keyword level. Google says it is calculated from three components: expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing page experience. Each is rated "Above average," "Average," or "Below average" against advertisers whose ads showed for the same search over the last 90 days.

Google also states that Quality Score is not a key performance indicator and is not an input in the ad auction. Use it to find weak spots, not as a target. If a cake keyword shows "Below average" on landing page experience, the fix is usually a page that shows your cakes, prices or lead times more clearly, not more bids. Google's help page on the topic is the place to check the exact definitions before changing your setup.

## How Do You Keep Ads Close to the Shop?

Use location targeting for the area you can actually serve, and check your delivery rules before promising anything. If only people physically in your area should see the ads, choose presence-based targeting rather than general interest. Wedding customers may search from further away, so a separate wedding campaign can use a wider area than a birthday-cake campaign.

Google Display campaigns reach a very large network, with Google citing 35 million websites and apps. For a bakery, that breadth rarely helps. Search campaigns tied to a clear product and area are usually the better starting point, and Display can be tested later once you know which products convert.

## What Should the Ad and Landing Page Say?

State the product, the area and the next step in plain words, for example custom cakes, local pickup and an enquiry form. Avoid pointing every ad at the home page. Each ad group should land on a page that matches its search.

The landing page should show photos of real cakes, a lead-time note, and one short form that asks for date, serving size and budget. Sweet Crumbs Bakery, in an agency's self-reported case study, paired a clear 20% first-purchase offer with product-specific headlines and reported a 7.2% click-through rate against a 5% target. That agency's figures are unaudited, so treat them as one example of what a clear message can do, not a promise.

## Worked Budget Example: What Could $400 a Month Produce?

| Monthly budget (assumption) | $400 | $400 |
| Cost per click (WordStream average, secondary) | $2.69 | $2.69 |
| Clicks | about 149 | about 149 |
| Enquiry rate (assumption) | 5% | 5% |
| Enquiries | about 7 | about 7 |
| Orders from enquiries (assumption) | 2 | 1 |
| Average order value (assumption) | $120 | $650 |
| Revenue | about $240 | about $650 |
| Ad spend per order | about $200 | about $400 |

On these assumptions, the birthday campaign returns $240 on $400 of spend, so it only pays with repeat customers or add-on sales. The wedding campaign returns $650 on $400, but it depends on one booking a month, so one missed wedding can wipe out the gain. Track every enquiry in a simple sheet with the source, the product and the outcome, then replace the assumptions with real numbers after eight to ten weeks.

Here is a step checklist to set up the test:

1. Pick one custom cake and one wedding package as the first two ad groups.
2. Build one landing page for each, with photos, lead times and a single enquiry form.
3. Set a monthly budget that can run for at least two months.
4. Add a form field that asks how the customer heard of you.
5. Review the search terms report weekly and add negative keywords for recipes, jobs and supplies.
6. Compare cost per order against average order value after eight weeks.

## Should You Use Ads or Organic Local Search?

Use both, for different jobs. A complete Business Profile with photos, hours and products can help a bakery appear in local results for walk-in searches without paying per click. Ads are most useful when someone is comparing cake makers for a specific date and is ready to enquire. For a wider view of paid options for local businesses, the [Local Service Ads guide](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) explains when a different format may suit a business better, and the [direct mail guide](/blog/direct-mail-marketing-local) shows how offline outreach can support wedding and corporate leads.

## FAQ

### How much should a bakery spend on Google Ads?
No standard exists. At the all-industry search average of $2.69 per click, $400 a month buys about 149 clicks. Start with an amount that can run for at least two months, so there is enough data to judge the result.

### Are Google Ads worth it for a small bakery?
For custom and wedding orders they can be, because each order is worth more. For daily walk-in sales, a complete Business Profile is usually the more efficient channel.

### What keywords work for cake orders?
Combine product and place, such as custom cakes or wedding cakes in your town, and add negative keywords for recipes and supplies. The keyword guide linked above lists more examples.

### Can I advertise only to people near my shop?
Yes. Use location targeting for the real service area, and choose presence-based targeting if only people physically in that area should see the ads.

### How do I track cake orders from ads?
Use a form or call button with a source field, record which enquiries become paid orders, and compare cost per order with average order value each month.
