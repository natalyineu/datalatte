In Starbucks' latest quarterly dashboard, Rewards members made up 59% of spend at its US company-operated stores, and 35.8 million people in the US had used the program in the last 90 days. That is the engine behind the emails you see from the chain. You will never match that data, but the mechanics underneath are simple enough for a 1,000-name list.

One honest note first. Starbucks does not publish its email open rates, click rates or send schedules, so nobody outside the company can tell you exactly how its email programme performs. What it does publish is how its loyalty programme works, and email is one of the ways members hear about it. This guide uses those confirmed facts as the starting point, then turns each into a tactic for an independent café. The tactics are my recommendations, not Starbucks' internal playbook.

It is written for café and coffee-shop owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Starbucks figures are US numbers.

## What Starbucks Has Confirmed

Two primary sources:

- Starbucks' [Q3 FY26 investor dashboard](https://s203.q4cdn.com/326826266/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/Q3-FY26-Digital-IR-Dashboard.pdf) lists 35.8 million US 90-day active Rewards members and says Rewards members were 59% of tender dollars at US company-operated stores (stores it owns, not licensed ones).
- Starbucks' [January 2026 press release](https://investor.starbucks.com/news/financial-releases/news-details/2026/Starbucks-Unveils-Reimagined-Loyalty-Program-to-Deliver-More-Meaningful-Value-Personalization-and-Engagement-for-Members/default.aspx) describes the reimagined programme launching March 10, 2026.

From that press release: there are three levels (Green, Gold, Reserve). Gold needs 500 Stars earned in a 12-month period and Reserve needs 2,500. Green members' Stars are valid for six months, with a monthly qualifying activity extending them by a month, while Gold and Reserve Stars never expire. Green members get a free birthday beverage or food item, with a seven-day redemption window for Gold and 30 days for Reserve. Green members receive personalized offers, and members see their level in the app and by email.

That is the confirmed part. Everything below is how a small shop can borrow the idea.

## 1. Tell People Where They Stand

Starbucks shows members their level in the app and by email, and progress toward the next level in the app or online account. People do more for a goal they can see.

**Copy it:** a 10-stamp card is a level system. Send a monthly email: "You're 3 visits from a free drink." If your POS or loyalty tool exports balances, merge the number into the email. If not, a simple "Collect 8 stamps by 30 November" reminder works. See [coffee shop loyalty ideas without an app](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app).

## 2. Use Expiry Honestly

Green Stars expire after six months, but monthly activity extends them. The lever is deadline plus easy action.

**Copy it:** put an end date on every offer, and say what to do: "Free cookie with any drink, this Friday only." Never invent fake urgency. Customers who feel tricked unsubscribe.

## 3. Send a Birthday Treat

Starbucks gives Green members a free birthday beverage or food item and gives higher levels longer to redeem it.

**Copy it:** ask for a birthday month (not the year) on your sign-up form, then automate a "Happy birthday, a coffee is on us this month" email. Most email tools can send on a date field. Make the treat cheap to you and generous to them.

## 4. Segment Before You Personalize

Green members receive personalized offers. You cannot run Starbucks' systems, but you can run three groups:

| Segment | What defines it | Message |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New | Joined in last 14 days | Welcome and first-visit offer |
| Regulars | Visited in last 30 days | New menu items, loyalty progress |
| Lapsed | No visit in 45 to 90 days (my suggestion) | A reason to come back |

Add a one-question tag at sign-up: "What's your usual order?" Our guides to [coffee shop email segmentation](/blog/coffee-shop-email-segmentation) and [welcome email series](/blog/welcome-email-series-local-business-5-email-template) show the setup.

## 5. Make the Email a Reason to Walk In

An email that drives foot traffic has three things: one offer, a deadline and your address and hours. "Show this email for a free cookie with any drink this Friday" is trackable. Keep your hours consistent with your Google listing, as a mismatch costs you the visit.

## 6. Build the List at the Counter

Starbucks' members come through its app. You have a till. Put a QR code on the counter and the receipt, tell the barista to say one sentence ("Want a free drink on your birthday? Scan here"), and offer something small and real in return. Our [list-building guide](/blog/coffee-shop-email-list-building) has more ideas.

## 7. Protect Your Sender Reputation

This is the least glamorous tactic and the one that decides whether emails arrive at all.

- **Law.** The FTC's [CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) lists the basics: truthful header information, non-deceptive subject lines, a clear ad disclosure, a valid postal address, a clear opt-out, and honouring opt-outs within 10 business days. The FTC says each violating email can cost up to $53,088. If you email in the UK, EU or Australia, local rules (such as GDPR, PECR or the Spam Act) differ and you should check them.
- **Gmail rules.** Google's [sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) say all senders need SPF or DKIM authentication. Anyone sending more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail needs SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and one-click unsubscribe, with spam rates kept below 0.30%. A small café will be nowhere near 5,000 a day, but your email tool should handle authentication for you, so switch it on.
- **Never buy a list.** That is my advice, and it is also the fastest way to push spam complaints up.

## The Math

Here is what a small, three-automation programme might earn in a month. Every figure is an **assumption**; the Starbucks facts above are not inputs. Replace each cell with your own numbers after one month.

| | Welcome | Win-back | Birthday |

| People reached per month (assumption) | 30 new sign-ups | 200 lapsed | 33 birthdays (1,000 list x 40% with a birthday month / 12) |
| Redemption rate (assumption) | 20% | 5% | 25% |
| Visits generated | 6 | 10 | 8 |
| Average ticket (assumption) | $9 | $9 | $9 |
| Revenue | $54 | $90 | $72 |

Total: 24 visits and $216 in revenue. Subtract the cost of the offers (assume $1.50 each, about $36) and the email tool (assume $20 a month), and the net is about $160 a month. That is modest on purpose. The point is that it runs without you, and each cell is a lever. Doubling the win-back redemption rate adds $90. A weak offer or a dirty list can make every number in this table zero.

## Do This Today

1. Put a sign-up QR code on the counter and receipt with a one-line reason to join (a birthday treat or first-visit offer).
2. Add two fields to your sign-up form: birthday month and usual order.
3. Write and automate one welcome email: thanks, your best-seller, one offer with a date, address and hours.
4. Create a "lapsed" segment of customers who have not visited in 45 to 90 days, and draft one win-back email with a deadline.
5. Confirm your email tool has your postal address and an unsubscribe link in every message, and that its authentication is switched on.

## FAQ

**Q: Does Starbucks publish its email open rates?**
A: Not that I could find. It publishes loyalty figures such as active members and the share of spend from Rewards members, but not email metrics. Be wary of any article that quotes them as fact.

**Q: Is email marketing worth it for a café with a small list?**
A: It can be, because a small list with a usual-order tag lets you send relevant messages. The math table above shows a modest return that depends on your redemption rates, so test and measure.

**Q: Do I need an app to run a loyalty programme?**
A: No. A stamp card, a POS loyalty feature or a spreadsheet plus a monthly email covers the idea. See our [SMS marketing guide for coffee shops](/blog/sms-marketing-for-coffee-shops-how-to-start) for adding texts.

**Q: What is a good open rate?**
A: I'm not quoting a number because sources disagree widely by year and method, and some email apps load images automatically, which inflates opens. Compare your own emails with each other and track visits and redemptions.

**Q: How do I grow the list?**
A: Ask at the counter, put a QR code on receipts, and offer a specific reason to join. Never buy a list.
