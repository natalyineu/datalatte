Starbucks uses email to keep customers coming back: personalized offers based on what people buy, loyalty reminders tied to its rewards program, seasonal drink launches and timely nudges that drive store visits. You cannot match its data scale, but a small coffee shop can copy the principles with a simple email tool and your point-of-sale data. This guide is based on the tactics visible in Starbucks' public emails and marketing, not on any internal figures, so treat the specifics as observed patterns rather than official statistics.

The main lesson is that Starbucks does not send the same email to everyone. It sends relevant messages to specific groups at the right time. That is something independent coffee shops can do too.

## 1. Personalized Offers Based on Purchase History

Large brands use what customers buy to decide what to send. A customer who always orders cold brew may get a cold-brew offer; someone who has not visited lately may get a nudge to return.

**How you can do it:**

- Capture email addresses at the register or through online ordering, and tag customers by what they buy if your POS allows it.
- Use an email tool like Mailchimp, Klaviyo or a POS-integrated option to send different messages to different groups.
- Add a personal greeting and a recommendation that matches their usual order.

For the full setup, see our [Email Marketing Strategy for Small Business playbook](/blog/email-marketing-strategy-for-small-business-the-step-by-step-playbook).

## 2. Loyalty Program Engagement

Starbucks' rewards program is closely tied to its emails and app, with messages about points, status and bonus offers. The tactics that carry over to a small shop are clear value, a deadline and one clear call to action.

You do not need an app. A simple "Buy 5, get the 6th free" card, tracked in your POS or a spreadsheet, plus a monthly email reminding members of their progress, can work well. See [coffee shop loyalty program ideas without an app](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app).

## 3. Seasonal and Timely Offers

Seasonal drink launches are a signature of Starbucks' marketing. Your shop can do the same on a smaller scale:

- Plan a seasonal calendar (autumn drinks, winter specials, summer iced menu) and schedule emails ahead of time
- Announce new menu items to your list first
- React to local events and weather where it makes sense, such as a cold snap or a local festival

For more on following customers across channels, read about [cross-channel retargeting](/blog/cross-channel-retargeting-how-to-follow-customers-across-every-platform).

## 4. Driving Store Visits

Email can drive foot traffic when it includes something to do in the store:

- A limited-time in-store offer ("Show this email for a free cookie with any drink this Friday")
- Specific times and your address, with a link to directions
- A reminder for people who have not visited in a while

Keep your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) up to date so your hours and location match what your emails say.

## 5. Mobile-Friendly Design

Most people read email on their phones, so design for that:

- Short copy, one main message, a large button
- Good photos of your drinks or space, compressed so they load fast
- Subject lines that are short and readable on a phone screen

Send yourself a test and open it on your phone before every send.

## 6. Timing and Frequency

Do not email just because you have a list. Look at your own open and click data to find when your customers are most responsive, then test send times. Set up a simple re-engagement email for subscribers who have stopped opening, and remove people who never engage so they do not hurt your deliverability.

## 7. Segmentation

Segmentation is the idea behind most of the tactics above. Useful groups for a coffee shop:

- New subscribers (welcome series)
- Regulars (loyalty and new menu items)
- Lapsed customers who have not visited in a while
- Customers who prefer particular drinks or products

Most email platforms let you build these segments from tags or purchase data. For a deeper look, see [email marketing for coffee shops](/blog/email-marketing-for-coffee-shops).

<Callout type="tip">Start with two segments, regulars and lapsed customers, and one automation: a welcome email for new subscribers. Add more once those are working.</Callout>

## What Email Costs

Many email tools have free or low-cost entry plans that cover a small coffee shop's list, but pricing changes with list size and features, so check each provider's current pricing page. Compare what you actually need (automations, segmentation, SMS) rather than paying for a large platform you will not use.

## FAQ

**Q: Is email marketing worth it for a coffee shop with a small list?**
A: Yes. A small list with purchase information can perform well because you can send relevant messages to the right people. Relevance matters more than list size.

**Q: What is a good open rate for a local business?**
A: Open rates vary by industry and are less reliable now that some email apps pre-load images, so look at clicks and visits as well. Compare yourself against your own past emails rather than a generic benchmark.

**Q: Should I buy an email list?**
A: No. Bought lists tend to perform poorly, harm your sender reputation and can break privacy laws such as GDPR and CAN-SPAM if people have not opted in. Grow your list from customers who sign up.

**Q: How do I get more people to sign up?**
A: Ask at the register and on your online ordering page, and give a clear reason to sign up, such as seasonal menu news, member-only offers or a saved favorite order. Test different offers to see what works for your customers.

**Q: What is the one email I should send first?**
A: A win-back message to customers who have not visited in a while, or a welcome email for new subscribers. Keep it short, with one link and one clear offer or reminder.
