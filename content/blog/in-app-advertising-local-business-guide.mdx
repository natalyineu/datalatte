In-app ads are ads shown inside other companies' mobile apps. A local business rarely buys them directly: they usually appear as part of Google Display campaigns or Meta placements, and search ads are cheaper per lead in LocaliQ's 2026 data. For example, display leads averaged $115.58 in Beauty and Personal Care against $39.25 on search. Use in-app mainly for retargeting and awareness, after search ads are working.

No source cited here publishes costs for in-app placements alone. The figures below are for display campaigns in general, which include apps among other places.

## How Does In-App Advertising Work?

Apps sell ad space through ad platforms. AdMob, Google's platform for app publishers, lets developers add ad units in formats such as banners, interstitials shown at natural breaks, rewarded ads, native ads and app-open ads. That is the supply side, aimed at app owners, not at advertisers.

As an advertiser you buy through a network. Google says Display campaigns reach people across 35 million websites and apps and on YouTube and Gmail. Meta's help center says its Audience Network shows ads in third-party apps, and that you can opt out by editing placements. You target people, not specific apps.

## What Does Display Advertising Cost?

LocaliQ's September 2026 display benchmarks (7,074 campaigns, April 2025 to June 2026) report medians by category. They do not separate in-app from website placements.

| Restaurants and Food | 0.12% | $4.18 | $97.51 |

The report gives no CPM. For comparison, LocaliQ's 2026 search report lists a Beauty and Personal Care cost per lead of $39.25 and Sports and Recreation of $44.26.

## Which Is Better, In-App or Search? A Worked Example

The budget is an **assumption**; the benchmark figures come from LocaliQ.

| Item | Display (Beauty) | Search (Beauty) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $500 (assumption) | $500 (assumption) |
| Cost per lead | $115.58 | $39.25 |
| Leads | about 4 | about 13 |

Search produces roughly three times as many leads at these averages. That does not make display useless, because display builds awareness and retargets visitors, which the "lead" metric undervalues. But for a first campaign, search wins. ## Who Should Use In-App Advertising?

Consider it if you already get website visits, have a clear offer, and can follow up with people who visited but did not book. Google lists data segments that remind people who previously visited your website or app. Optimized targeting is the default in Display campaigns, and new campaigns enter a learning phase of about five to seven days.

Skip it if you have no tracking, a budget below what search needs, or an offer that only works for people searching right now, such as emergency repairs. ## What Should You Check Before You Start?

- Is conversion tracking working for calls, forms or bookings?
- Is the location set to the area customers can reach?
- Are you running search ads for people ready to buy first?
- Can you review where ads appeared, and exclude poor placements?

Poor placements and accidental clicks are common risks with app inventory, but no published rate exists in the sources cited for local advertisers, so do not assume a figure. Check placement reports and cost per lead after the first two weeks.

## FAQ

### What is in-app advertising?
Ads shown inside mobile apps, bought through networks such as Google Display or Meta's Audience Network.

### Is in-app advertising worth it for a local business?
Usually after search ads. LocaliQ's display data shows higher cost per lead than search in the categories listed.

### Can I choose which apps show my ad?
You mainly choose audiences and locations. Google and Meta also give placement controls; check your account for the current options.

### Can I turn off in-app placements on Meta?
Meta's help center says you can opt out of Audience Network by editing placements.
