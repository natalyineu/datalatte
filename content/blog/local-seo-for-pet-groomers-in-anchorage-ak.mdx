## What makes a pet groomer appear in the Anchorage map pack?

[Adwave states that the top three businesses in the map pack are "pulled directly from their GBP listings"](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business), so the profile is the starting point. The website, reviews and citations then reinforce it.

Ranking weight estimates vary. Adwave cites a 2025 benchmark that puts Google Business Profile signals at 32% of local pack ranking weight, and a [separate Adwave article](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit) does not explain the method behind that number. Another Adwave article says analysis of millions of profiles shows the primary category is the number one factor for the Local Pack, without naming the source. A BrightLocal survey cited by Ahrefs found 36% of SEOs ranked the profile as the most important factor, but that data is from 2021.

Treat these figures as directional. What they agree on is that the profile matters, so the rest of this guide focuses on profile data first.

## How do you check Anchorage competitors without local benchmark data?

The sources here contain no Anchorage-specific competition, population or search volume figures, so this guide does not state any. You can build your own baseline from the live results. [Adwave recommends reviewing the top 3-5 businesses](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) that appear in local map results for a service search to see which categories they use.

1. Search your core service plus "Anchorage" in a private browser window and list the top three to five results.
2. Note each competitor's primary category and any secondary categories.
3. Record each competitor's review count, star rating and approximate photo count.
4. Compare your profile against that list and fix the largest gap first.

Repeat the check every few weeks, since the local pack changes over time.

## How should the Google Business Profile be set up?

Google describes its [Business Profile as free to set up](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/), so cost is not a barrier. Start with the category. Choose a primary category that matches the service customers book most, then add secondary categories only where they describe something you actually offer, such as mobile grooming.

Write the business description in the 750 characters the profile allows, according to [Adwave's beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Lead with the service and the Anchorage neighborhood you serve, then add one clear differentiator. Keep the wording natural. A description that reads like a keyword list is harder for customers to trust.

Make sure hours, phone number and address are correct. Adwave says businesses should make these [100% accurate](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) on the profile.

Mobile groomers need one extra setting. Adwave notes that a business that works from home or serves customers at their location can [hide its street address and is then designated a Service Area Business](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Set your service area around the neighborhoods you actually visit rather than a generic radius.

## How many photos and posts does the profile need?

Adwave recommends that a profile have [at least 15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), and says a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls. Its beginner guide recommends [at least 10](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), so the two recommendations differ. Aim for 15 as a working target and use real photos of your own dogs, your space and your mobile van if you run one.

Posts need to be approved before they appear. Google says a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is [Live](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), and Pending or Not approved posts do not show. Adwave advises [at least one new post a week](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) to keep the profile active. Plan seasonal offers and new services in advance so the weekly cadence holds. The fuller profile routine is covered in the [Google Business Profile optimization guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers).

## How many reviews does a pet groomer need?

Google states that reviews [show next to a business's Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), so every review is visible to shoppers as well as to ranking systems. The most specific figure in the sources comes from a third-party analysis of [2 million Google Business Profile pages](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business), which found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. That analysis is secondary and shows correlation, not a guaranteed result.

Use 200 as a long-term benchmark rather than a launch target. The practical goal is steady review growth. Ask customers after each visit and keep the request simple. The worked example below shows how long the gap takes to close at a realistic pace.

## Worked example: closing a review gap in Anchorage

The following calculation uses assumed inputs for one hypothetical groomer. It is an illustration, not a sourced result.

Assumption inputs: 40 reviews today, 3 new reviews per week, target of 200 reviews from the benchmark above, 6 photos today, target of 15 photos.

Review timeline: (200 - 40) / 3 = 53.3 weeks, so roughly 54 weeks at 3 reviews per week. At 6 reviews per week (assumption), the same gap closes in about 27 weeks.

Photo gap: 15 - 6 = 9 photos to upload.

Post schedule: at one post per week, the same 54-week window needs about 54 approved posts.

Use this checklist to run the plan:

1. Enter your current review count, weekly review pace and photo count into a spreadsheet.
2. Calculate the weeks needed to reach 200 reviews at your pace, and change the pace to see how the timeline moves.
3. Upload the missing photos in the first two weeks.
4. Set a calendar reminder for one approved post per week.
5. Review the numbers monthly and adjust the request process if the pace falls behind.

## Which citations need matching NAP details?

Citations are mentions of your business name, address and phone number on other sites. [Adwave states that the NAP must be exactly the same](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses) on your website, your Google Business Profile, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places. Small differences count. The [same Adwave beginner guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) says the address must match right down to the comma and whether you write "St." or "Street."

Apple Maps deserves a separate check. Adwave's Apple Business Connect article reports an [unattributed 88% vs 12% split](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring) between Google Maps and Apple Maps use, so verify the figure before relying on it. The same article describes Profile Views in Apple Business Connect as the count of people who saw your business card in Apple Maps, which is a useful measure to track.

The sources here do not rank which local chamber, association or niche directories matter most in Anchorage, so add them only after the main directories are consistent.

## How should the website support the profile?

Your website should repeat the exact NAP details used on the profile. Create a location or contact page that lists the same name, address and phone number, and add a [Google Maps embed on that page](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) so visitors can find the location. The sources here do not quantify any ranking effect from the embed, so treat it as a usability step.

If you offer several services, give each its own page with a clear title, such as a page for mobile grooming and another for a specific service. Each page should name the service and Anchorage in plain language. Mobile groomers should describe the areas they serve on the website as well as in the profile, so the two sources match.

## Should a pet groomer also run paid ads?

Paid ads are a separate channel from local SEO, but they share the same profile data. Google states that [location asset data in Google Ads is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), and that successful corrections made there flow into linked Google Ads accounts. When Business Profile options do not work, advertisers can select up to 10 locations from Google Maps, and those locations must belong to the advertiser.

A [Marketing 360 case study](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/) describes a mobile pet groomer that advertised on Facebook and Google, which is a secondary example of mixing channels. For a comparison of the two platforms, see [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for local businesses](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins). Start with the profile, then decide whether paid spend adds enough bookings to justify it.

## Which metrics show progress?

[Adwave lists six local search metrics](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent) worth tracking: Google Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift over time. Record them monthly, alongside your review count and photo count from the worked example.

Expect a lag between profile changes and movement in these numbers. Compare the same months year over year where possible, because seasonal demand can shift results. The profile routine and the matching citation checks give you the inputs, and these metrics show whether they are working. ## FAQ

### Is Anchorage a low-competition market for pet groomers?

This guide has no sourced Anchorage competition data, so it cannot confirm that. Use the competitor check above on the live map for your own service search.

### Does a mobile pet groomer need to show a street address?

Not necessarily. Adwave states that a business serving customers at their location can hide its street address and be designated a Service Area Business, so set the service area to match the neighborhoods you cover.

### How many reviews are enough to compete in the local pack?

A third-party analysis cited by Adwave associates over 200 Google reviews with a higher likelihood of appearing in the local pack. That is a correlation from one analysis, not a guarantee, so track your own pace against it.

### Should I add the Google Maps embed to my website?

It is an easy step that helps visitors find your location. The sources here do not quantify any ranking benefit, so do not treat it as a substitute for profile accuracy and reviews.
