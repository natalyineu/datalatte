SparkToro reports that 68.01% of US Google searches ended without a click in the first four months of 2026, up from 60.45% in 2024. Run the arithmetic and the share of searches that produce any click fell from about 39.6 per 100 to about 32.0, a drop of roughly 19%. That sounds like a disaster for your website. For a local business the loss is usually smaller than it looks, because the person who "didn't click" may have rung you or tapped Directions from the results page.

This guide is for local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: coffee shops, salons, groomers, studios and clinics. It covers what the data actually says, what Google confirms about AI features, how to measure the actions that replace clicks, five fixes in order, and the math on what you lose and what you can win back.

## What the Data Says (and What It Doesn't)

Treat every zero-click figure as a rough direction, because each study measures differently.

- **SparkToro, 2026:** [TechWyse's summary of the study](https://www.techwyse.com/news/reports-data/google-zero-click-searches-68-percent-2026) gives 68.01% for January to April 2026, from Similarweb clickstream data, assuming two-thirds of searches are on mobile. It excludes searches in Google's mobile app, covers the US only, and notes that the 2024 number came from a different data provider, so the exact size of the rise is uncertain. The study did not isolate AI Overviews as the cause. This is a second-hand account.
- **SparkToro, 2024:** The original [SparkToro and Datos study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study-for-every-1000-us-google-searches-only-374-clicks-go-to-the-open-web-in-the-eu-its-360/) found about 58.5% of US searches ended without a click, and 360 clicks per 1,000 US searches went to the open web. It lists caveats, including that iPhones and iPads have minimal coverage.
- **Pew Research, 2025:** In [Pew's analysis](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) of the browsing of 900 US adults in March 2025, users clicked a traditional result link in 8% of visits with an AI summary against 15% without one. They clicked a link inside the summary in 1% of visits.

None of these numbers are local-specific. Local searches such as "groomer near me" are answered by the map pack and the Business Profile panel, where the action is a call or a tap, not a website visit.

## What Google Says About AI Features

Google's [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary", and that no special schema.org structured data is needed. A page has to be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet, and meeting that does not guarantee inclusion. So the winning approach is the boring one: accurate profile, clear pages, real reviews. Our guides on [Google AI Mode and local SEO](/blog/google-ai-mode-local-seo-2026) and [using AI for local SEO](/blog/ai-for-local-seo-how-to-use-it) cover the AI side without hype.

## Measure Actions, Not Just Visits

If you only watch website sessions, zero-click looks like a decline. Google's [Business Profile performance help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094) lists the metrics that matter:

- **Calls:** how often someone tapped the call button (you need a phone number on the profile).
- **Directions:** requests, adjusted for multiple taps, cancellations and spam.
- **Website clicks:** taps on your site link.
- **Messages:** counted as conversations, not individual messages.
- **Bookings:** only counted when they run through a booking provider.

Only metrics that apply to your business appear. Views are counted once per person per day and cover your profile only. Add a tracked phone number if you need more detail; see call tracking for local business. Compare each month with the same month last year, because local demand is seasonal.

## Five Fixes, in Order

### 1. Make the Business Profile do the selling

Google's [local ranking tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) say local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that complete, detailed information helps relevance. Check these:

1. Primary and secondary categories match what you really do.
2. Hours are correct, including holidays.
3. Phone number and booking link are present so people can act in one tap.
4. Services and products have short descriptions and "from" prices.
5. Photos are real and recent: exterior, interior, team, work.
6. Posts and your own Questions and Answers cover offers and common queries.

Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) goes item by item.

### 2. Earn reviews and reply to them

Google says prominence depends partly on how many reviews you have, that positive ratings can help local ranking, and that helpful replies can help you stand out. Ask every happy customer with the direct review link or QR code from your profile, and never offer a reward for a review. Reply to each one with a specific detail. Our [Google Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) has templates.

### 3. Write pages that answer the question first

Whether your page is quoted in a snippet or summary is not something you control, but clear pages give you the best chance. Open with the answer, use headings that match real questions ("How much does a dog groom cost in [town]?", "Do you take walk-ins?"), keep answers short with prices and policies, and use lists or tables for steps and comparisons. Structured data helps search engines read your details, though Google says AI features do not need any special markup; our [local business schema guide](/blog/local-business-schema-markup-rich-snippets-google) explains the basics.

### 4. Keep your details identical everywhere

Name, address and phone should match across your website, Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Facebook, Yelp and directories. Pick one format, fix duplicates and close dead listings. This is our advice, not a Google ranking rule.

### 5. Give people a reason to click anyway

Some tasks still need your site: booking, menus, price lists, detailed services. Make that click worth it with a fast mobile booking page, a current price page, original local content that a summary cannot replace, and an email sign-up so a visit becomes a relationship. See [email list building strategies](/blog/email-list-building-strategies).

## The Math

All inputs are assumptions except the percentages quoted from SparkToro. Replace them with your own Search Console, Business Profile and booking data.

| Input | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website visits from Google per month | 200 | Assumption |
| Drop in searches with a click, 2024 to 2026 | about 19% | Arithmetic from SparkToro: (39.55 - 31.99) / 39.55; panels differ, so use as an upper-end illustration |
| Visits lost | 38 | 200 x 19% |
| Visit to booking rate | 3% | Assumption |
| Value of a booking | $60 | Assumption |
| Revenue lost from fewer website visits | about $68 a month | 38 x 3% x $60 |
| Business Profile views per month | 1,500 | Assumption |
| Share of views that call, ask for directions or click | 10% (150 actions) | Assumption |
| Actions that become customers | 25% (about 38 customers) | Assumption |
| Revenue from profile actions | about $2,250 a month | 37.5 x $60 |
| Effect of a more complete profile | 10% to 12% action rate (assumption, not a benchmark) | Adds 30 actions, about 7.5 customers, about $450 a month |

How to read it: the fall in website clicks costs about $68 a month in this example, while a two-point lift in profile actions is worth about $450. Your numbers will differ, particularly if your site sells things online, where lost clicks hurt more. The takeaway is where to spend your hour: on the profile and on answering the phone, not on chasing a click that may never come.

Keep an eye on the second row of costs too. A call that goes to voicemail is a lost customer. If 1 in 4 profile calls goes unanswered, fix the phone before you fix anything on Google.

## A Simple Monthly Routine

1. Check Performance for calls, directions, website clicks and messages.
2. Add new photos and one post.
3. Reply to every new review.
4. Check hours and special-day changes.
5. Update one page on your site with a clearer answer or new detail.
6. Every quarter, check your listings for consistency.

## Examples by Business Type

- **Coffee shop:** keep hours, menu photos and dine-in or takeaway attributes accurate so a searcher decides in seconds.
- **Hair salon:** add a booking link, list services with starting prices and refresh recent work photos.
- **Pet groomer:** answer vaccination and breed questions in profile Q&A and on an FAQ page.
- **Fitness studio:** show the timetable, trial offer and a clear book-a-class button.

## Do This Today

1. Open your Business Profile Performance report and write down last month's calls, directions, website clicks and messages.
2. Check categories, hours, phone number and booking link, and fix anything wrong.
3. Add five new real photos and answer every unreplied review.
4. Rewrite the first sentence of your two most important pages to answer the main question directly.
5. Set a monthly 20-minute reminder to compare actions with the same month last year and ask new customers how they found you.

## FAQ

**Q: Are zero-click searches bad for local businesses?**
A: Not necessarily. If people call, ask for directions or book from your listing, you still gain customers. The risk is relying on website traffic as your only measure.

**Q: How many Google searches end without a click?**
A: SparkToro reports 68.01% of US searches in January to April 2026, up from 60.45% in 2024, using different data providers. Treat the trend as clear and the exact size as uncertain.

**Q: Do I need special optimization for AI Overviews?**
A: Google says no additional requirements or special optimizations are necessary beyond being indexed and eligible to show with a snippet.

**Q: How do I appear in the local pack?**
A: Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Complete your profile, collect real reviews and keep your details accurate.

**Q: Should I still invest in my website?**
A: Yes. It supports bookings, trust and email capture, and it is where Google reads your details.
