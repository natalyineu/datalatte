A zero-click search is one where the person gets what they need on Google's results page and never visits a website. For a local business, the answer is to make sure the thing they see is you: a complete Google Business Profile, strong reviews, accurate details and content that Google can quote, so they call, get directions or book without ever clicking through.

It is widely reported that a large share of Google searches end without a click to any website, and features like local packs, featured snippets and AI-generated summaries have made that more common. For a local business this is not always bad news. Someone searching "coffee shop open now" often wants an address and hours, not an article. If your listing gives them that and they walk in, you won.

## What zero-click looks like for a local business

Common zero-click moments:

- **The local pack:** the map and three listings that show up for searches like "hair salon near me".
- **Your Business Profile panel:** the box showing your hours, phone number, photos, reviews and directions button.
- **Featured snippets and "People also ask":** short answers pulled from web pages.
- **AI summaries:** overviews that combine information from several sources.
- **Direct answers:** hours, phone numbers and prices shown without a click.

In each case, the person may call, tap "Directions" or message you straight from Google. Your job is to win those actions.

## Shift how you measure success

If you only measure website visits, zero-click can look like a decline. Add these measures instead:

- **Calls** from your Business Profile.
- **Direction requests** and **website clicks**.
- **Bookings and messages** from your listing.
- **Profile views** by search type (discovery versus direct).
- **Walk-in or phone questions:** "How did you find us?"

Business Profile's Performance report shows many of these, and a simple call-tracking number can add more detail. See our [call tracking guide](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) for options. Keep in mind that numbers vary by season and business, so compare month against the same month a year ago.

## Step 1: Make your Business Profile do the selling

For zero-click searches, your profile is your storefront. Complete it:

1. **Accurate primary and secondary categories.**
2. **Correct hours,** including holiday hours, updated promptly.
3. **Phone number and booking link,** so people can act in one tap.
4. **Services and products** with short, clear descriptions and prices where possible.
5. **Real photos,** such as your exterior, interior, team and work. Add new ones regularly.
6. **Posts** for offers and events.
7. **Questions and answers:** add your own most common questions and answer them.

Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) goes through each step. If you have no time, our [Google Business Profile service](/services/google-business-profile) can manage it.

## Step 2: Earn reviews and respond to them

Reviews appear directly in results and influence whether someone picks you without clicking. Ask every happy customer, make it easy with a short link or QR code and reply to each review in a friendly, specific way. Do not offer rewards for reviews, as that breaks Google's rules.

Recent, detailed reviews that mention your services help you appear for those terms. If a topic is repeated, such as "best flat white" or "gentle with nervous dogs", it can influence what shows up for related searches. See [Google Business Profile reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews).

## Step 3: Write content that can be quoted

Featured snippets and AI summaries draw from pages that answer questions clearly. You can help by structuring your own site:

- **Answer the question in the first sentence,** then explain.
- **Use headings that match real questions:** "How much does a dog groom cost in [town]?" or "Do you take walk-ins?"
- **Keep answers short and factual,** with details such as price ranges and policies.
- **Add an FAQ section** to key pages such as services and contact.
- **Use lists and tables** for steps, comparisons and prices.

Where you can, add structured data (schema) such as LocalBusiness and FAQ markup so search engines can read your details more easily. Our guide to [local business schema markup](/blog/local-business-schema-markup-rich-snippets-google) explains how. Markup does not guarantee a snippet, but it helps clarity.

For more on AI-generated summaries, read about [Google AI Overviews and local SEO](/blog/google-ai-overviews-local-business-seo).

## Step 4: Keep your details consistent everywhere

Search engines and AI tools gather facts from many sources. If your name, address and phone number differ between your website, Business Profile, Facebook, Apple Maps, Yelp and directories, you create confusion, and may lose visibility.

- Choose one exact format for your name, address and phone number.
- Update every major listing: Google, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Facebook, Yelp and industry directories.
- Fix duplicates and closed listings.

## Step 5: Give people a reason to click anyway

Some searches still need a visit to your site, especially bookings, menus, price lists and detailed services. Make the click worthwhile:

- A clear **booking page** that loads quickly on mobile.
- A **menu or price page** that is current and readable.
- **Original content,** such as a local guide or comparison, that a summary cannot replace.
- **Email sign-up,** so a visit is the start of a relationship. See [email list building strategies](/blog/email-list-building-strategies).

Your page titles and meta descriptions also affect whether people click. Write them for humans: state what you offer, where, and one reason to choose you.

## A simple monthly routine

1. Check Performance for calls, directions and clicks.
2. Add new photos and one post.
3. Reply to new reviews.
4. Check your hours and any special-day changes.
5. Update one page on your site with a clearer answer or new detail.
6. Review your listings for consistency every quarter.

## Quick checklist

- Business Profile is verified, with correct categories, hours and phone.
- Booking or order link is added and works.
- At least ten recent, real photos are uploaded.
- Reviews are answered within a few days.
- Key pages open with a direct answer and include an FAQ.
- Name, address and phone match across all major listings.
- You track calls, direction requests and bookings monthly.

If you can tick every item, you are well placed to benefit when searchers get their answer without a click. If not, start at the top of the list.

## Examples by business type

- **Coffee shop:** keep hours, menu photos and a "dine-in, takeaway" attribute accurate, so a searcher can decide in seconds.
- **Hair salon:** add a booking link, list services with starting prices and keep recent work photos fresh.
- **Pet groomer:** answer vaccination and breed questions in your profile Q&A and on your FAQ page.
- **Fitness studio:** show the class timetable, trial offer and a clear "book a class" button.

## FAQ

**Q: Are zero-click searches bad for local businesses?**

A: Not necessarily. If people call, ask for directions or book from your listing, you still gain customers. The risk is only if you rely on website traffic as your single measure.

**Q: How do I appear in the local pack?**

A: Local results typically depend on relevance, distance and prominence. Complete your Business Profile, collect real reviews, choose the right categories and keep your information consistent.

**Q: Can I stop Google showing my information without a click?**

A: No, and you would not want to for most local businesses. Instead, make sure what Google shows is accurate and persuasive.

**Q: Should I still invest in my website?**

A: Yes. Your website supports bookings, trust and content that search engines draw from. It also gives you a place to capture email addresses.
