To start SMS marketing for a coffee shop, collect written opt-ins at the counter, pick a texting platform that handles opt-outs, and send one short offer for your slowest hour. Raw sending is cheap: Twilio lists $0.0083 per US message segment plus a carrier fee of $0.0035 to $0.005, so a 500-person list costs about $6 per send. The hard part is consent, not price.

This guide covers the United States first, with notes for the UK and Australia. Prices and rules were checked on 25 October 2026 and can change. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Does SMS Marketing Cost a Coffee Shop?

Twilio's [US pricing page](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/us) lists $0.0083 per outbound segment for long codes, toll-free and short codes. Carrier fees are added: $0.0035 on AT&T, $0.0045 on T-Mobile and $0.005 on Verizon. A message to an AT&T customer costs about $0.0118. Twilio says registration fees apply to US business texting (A2P 10DLC) but the page does not state the amounts.

Twilio is a sending service, not a ready-made marketing tool. Marketing platforms charge their own monthly fees and credits, and those prices were not verified for this guide. For Australia, Twilio lists $0.0515 per outbound segment (currency not stated on the page).

## How Long Can a Text Be Before It Costs More?

A plain-text segment holds 160 characters, according to Twilio's [character limit page](https://www.twilio.com/docs/glossary/what-sms-character-limit). Longer messages are split into segments of 153 characters each, and you pay per segment. One emoji or curly quotation mark switches the whole message to a different encoding that fits only 70 characters.

For a coffee shop that means: keep offers under 160 characters, skip emoji if you want to stay in one segment, and check the text in a segment calculator before sending. Twilio recommends keeping messages under 320 characters.

## What Consent Do You Need Before Texting Customers?

In the US, marketing texts sent with automated systems need prior express written consent, under [47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-64/section-64.1200). The consent must be a written agreement signed by the person, electronic signatures included, and it must say they agree to automated marketing texts and that signing is not a condition of buying anything.

This is not legal advice, and a lawyer or your texting platform's compliance team should confirm your sign-up wording. A paper sheet with a phone number is weak evidence. A form or keyword opt-in that records the date and wording is stronger.

## How Do You Handle Opt-Outs?

The same rule says people may revoke consent by any reasonable method. Replies of stop, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel or unsubscribe count as revocation. You must honor requests within a reasonable time, not more than ten business days, and may send one confirmation text that contains no marketing.

Do not make one method the only way to opt out. Most platforms handle STOP replies automatically, so check that yours does before you send anything.

## What Are the Rules in the UK and Australia?

The UK's Information Commissioner says marketing texts generally need specific consent, such as a ticked opt-in box. A soft opt-in lets you market similar products to your own past customers if you gave a clear opt-out when collecting their details and in every message. See the [ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/).

In Australia, [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/online/promoting-your-business-by-email-or-text-messages) says to get express or inferred permission, keep a record of who agreed, when and how, and process unsubscribes within 5 working days. Branded SMS needs a registered sender ID or it shows as "Unverified."

## How Do You Build the List?

Ask at the moment of purchase, with one clear benefit. Use a counter QR code or a keyword to a number, and show the sign-up wording: who you are, how often you will text, that message and data rates may apply, and how to stop. Offer a small reward only if your platform and law allow it. Our [coffee shop email list guide](/blog/coffee-shop-email-list-building) uses the same sign-up principles.

No published figure for what share of customers will opt in could be verified, so measure your own sign-up rate per week at the counter.

## Which Texts Fill Slow Hours?

Send a short offer for a specific window, close to the time. Examples to adapt, each under 160 characters:

- "Quiet Tuesday: show this text for a free cookie with any large coffee, 2-4pm today. Reply STOP to opt out."
- "New oat latte on the menu. First one is half price until Friday. Reply STOP to opt out."
- "Your loyalty stamp card is 1 away from a free drink. Stop in this week."

Name your shop in the sign-up and, where rules require, in the message. Two to four texts a month is a sensible starting point, but no benchmark for the best frequency could be verified. Watch your opt-out rate and cut back if it climbs. Pair texts with your [loyalty program](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app) so the offer has a reason to exist.

## Should You Choose SMS or Email?

Texts cost money per message, while email costs little per send. Email suits longer content, and SMS suits a single time-bound offer. ## FAQ

### Is SMS marketing legal for a coffee shop?
Yes, with consent. In the US, automated marketing texts need prior express written consent under 47 CFR 64.1200. The UK and Australia require consent too.

### How much does it cost to send a text?
Twilio lists $0.0083 per US segment plus a carrier fee of $0.0035 to $0.005. Platform and registration fees are extra and vary.

### How long can a text be?
160 plain characters in one segment. Emoji or curly quotes cut that to 70 per segment.

### How often should I text customers?
No verified benchmark exists. Start with a few texts a month and watch opt-outs.

### Do I have to honor STOP replies?
Yes. Under US rules, replies like stop, quit and unsubscribe revoke consent, and you have at most ten business days to act.
