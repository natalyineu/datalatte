DOOH (digital out-of-home) advertising puts your salon's name and offer on digital screens near where clients walk, ride or wait. Programmatic DOOH is bought by CPM, cost per thousand impressions. StackAdapt states CPMs range from $2 to $15 depending on screen type and campaign scope. Minimum budgets are not consistently published, so get a written quote before you commit.

This guide is for salons and barbershops that want a local awareness channel beyond search and social. It compares DOOH with the paid search and Facebook benchmarks, and the worked example is labelled as an assumption. Nothing here is a salon case result.

## What Is DOOH, and Is It Big Enough to Matter?

The Out of Home Advertising Association of America reports US out-of-home ad spend grew 3.6% to $9.46 billion in 2025. DOOH was 36% of ad sales, programmatic DOOH was 30% of DOOH, and DOOH grew 10.5%. By format, transit grew 9.8%, street furniture 5.6%, billboards 2.3% and place-based 0.2%.

Those are industry totals, not a promise for a single salon. They do show that buying screens by the hour through a platform is a mainstream channel, not a niche one.

## Which Screens Suit a Salon?

Salons depend on proximity, so choose screens clients actually pass. The IAB's July 2025 measurement guide says exterior moving screens, such as bus wraps, are built for reach but can struggle with short dwell times and inconsistent viewability. Interior moving screens, such as taxis and transit, give longer dwell time and make attribution easier.

| Screen type | Audience | Fit for a salon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Street-level or pedestrian screens | People walking near your block | Walk-ins and local awareness |
| Transit and bus-stop screens | Riders waiting | Longer message, QR code possible |
| Roadside digital billboards | Drivers | Name and location only |
| Venue screens (gyms, malls, offices) | People already at a venue | Targeting by routine |

Match the screen to where your clients travel. Ask your next ten new clients how they get to the salon.

## How Can You Target and Schedule It?

A UK IAB guide to DOOH for small businesses lists three controls: context (rail, retail, roadside), time of day down to the hour, and geography, down to a business 100 meters from a screen. It also says most digital ads show for about 10 seconds, so the headline must read at a glance.

For a salon, that means running ads when clients plan appointments, such as lunch and early evening, and ending them when you are closed. Keep one message: your name, one service or offer and the location. Add a QR code only on screens where people stand still.

## What Does DOOH Cost Compared With Search and Facebook?

DOOH is priced per thousand impressions, while search and Facebook are priced per click, so compare on cost per booking, not per impression. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks for Beauty and Personal Care show Google Ads at $4.62 per click, 10.35% conversion and $39.25 per lead. On Facebook the sector shows $0.50 per click for traffic campaigns, and $50.91 per lead on lead campaigns.

The UK IAB guide, dated October 2020 and priced in pounds, says many media owners set a minimum order of about 2,500 to 5,000 pounds for a burst of a few weeks. That is old and British, so treat it as context. US platforms read for this guide did not publish minimums.

## How Do You Measure Whether It Worked?

The IAB guide separates impression types: "opportunity to see" counts people in a screen's exposure zone, while "likelihood to see" requires them to be there when the screen is active and viewable. Neither confirms a client noticed your ad.

For outcomes, the IAB recommends incrementality testing, such as synthetic control or matched market tests. A salon can do a simple version: compare new-client bookings in the target area with a similar area without ads, over the same weeks. Add a booking question: "How did you hear about us?" Small salons may not see a measurable lift, so keep expectations modest.

## The Math: A $500 Test Against Google Ads

| | DOOH test | Google Ads (benchmark) |

On these inputs DOOH looks cheaper per booking, but the 1 in 10,000 rate is invented and could be far lower. No source publishes a DOOH booking rate for salons. A real test with a comparison area will tell you which row you are in. Impressions are estimates, so treat them cautiously.

## What Should You Do First?

1. Pick one booking-friendly offer, such as a first-cut price.
2. List five screens within a short walk or drive of the salon.
3. Ask each vendor for minimum spend, self-serve access, formats and reporting in writing.
4. Set a comparison area and a start date.

## FAQ

### How much does DOOH cost for a salon?

StackAdapt states DOOH CPMs range from $2 to $15. Total cost depends on screens, hours and any minimum order, which are not published for US platforms read here.

### Is DOOH better than Google Ads for a salon?

It does a different job. Google captures people searching now, while DOOH builds awareness near the salon. Compare them on cost per booking.

### Can a single-chair salon use DOOH?

Possibly, if screens are close and the minimum fits. A small salon may struggle to detect a lift, so use a short test.

### How do I know if people saw my ad?

You cannot know exactly. IAB's guide says impressions are estimates, and outcomes come from bookings compared with a control area. For salon-specific social ads, see our [Facebook ads guide for hair salons](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons).
