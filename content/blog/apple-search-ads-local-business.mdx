Apple Search Ads, now called Apple Ads, puts your app in App Store results. A local business with its own app can pick Basic, where you pay per install at a cost you choose with a cap of $10,000 (US) per app per month, or Advanced, where you pay per tap with no monthly cap. It only suits you if the app is the product: a booking, ordering or loyalty app. A shop without an app should skip it.

Apple says over 850 million people visit the App Store every week. Apple publishes no average cost per tap or per install, so this guide shows how to work out your own break-even point.

## Who Should Use Apple Ads?

Use it if you own an app that a local customer would download for a reason: ordering ahead, booking, a loyalty card, a class schedule. If customers simply book by phone or through a third-party marketplace, the ad has nowhere to send them. A business that has not yet built an app should start with Google Business Profile and local search; see our [app marketing guide for local businesses](/blog/app-marketing-for-local-business).

Apple Ads is not a way to promote a shop's location. It promotes an app listing. Check your listing first: screenshots, a short description and ratings influence whether a tap becomes an install.

## What Is the Difference Between Basic and Advanced?

Apple publishes a side-by-side comparison. These are its figures.

| | Basic | Advanced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pricing | Cost per install, at a cost you choose | Cost per tap, up to a maximum you set |
| Monthly cap | Up to $10,000 (US) per app | None; control spend with a daily budget |
| Where ads show | App Store search results only | Today tab, Search tab, search results, product pages |
| Targeting | No keywords or audience settings; Apple matches users | You choose keywords; audience refinements available |
| Apps | Up to 50 | Unlimited |
| Reporting | Quick-view dashboard | Detailed reports |

Basic is built for developers who want simplicity. Advanced gives control over keywords and bids, and it is where you work out your cost per install. Apple does not list a minimum budget or a minimum bid on the pages checked.

## Can You Target Customers Near Your Business?

Partly. Advanced lets you refine location within some countries, including the US, UK, Canada and Australia. Apple's help page gives the example of an app that serves the area around San Francisco, but it does not describe radius or DMA targeting, so test what the account offers. Location refinements cannot be set in campaigns that run in more than one country or region.

You can also pick customer types: all users, new users, returning users, or users of your other apps. Age and gender refinements exclude people who have Personalized Ads turned off, so they shrink your reach. Compare this with a [Google app campaign](/blog/google-app-campaigns-local-business), which reaches users beyond the App Store.

## How Do You Work Out If Apple Ads Pays Off?

Apple does not publish typical costs, so use your own figures. Cost per install in Advanced is cost per tap divided by the share of taps that become installs. Then compare that to what an installed customer is worth.

All inputs below are **assumptions** for a salon with a booking app.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Cost per tap | $1.50 |
| Taps that become installs | 50% |
| Cost per install | $3.00 |
| Installs that book a first visit | 20% |
| Cost per first booking | $15.00 |
| Average first-visit revenue | $60 |
| Gross margin on that visit | 50% = $30 |

Under these assumptions each booking costs $15 and earns $30 in margin, so the campaign breaks even on the first visit and any repeat visit is profit. If only 5% of installs booked, the cost per booking would be $60 and the campaign would lose money. Your install-to-booking rate matters more than the tap price. Track it before scaling.

Open your app analytics and find what share of new installs book or order within 14 days. That single number tells you what a tap is worth.

## How Do You Start a Small Test?

Start with Basic if you want the simplest path: set your goal and the cost you will pay per install, and Apple handles placement. Move to Advanced if you need keyword control, such as your brand name and the service plus city. Search for your own app name first, because competitors may bid on it.

Run the test for a few weeks and record installs, first bookings and cost. Do not compare with web ads without counting the install step. For the web side, see how a [mobile-first loyalty program works without an app](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app), which may fit better if install rates are low.

## FAQ

### How much does Apple Search Ads cost?
Apple lists no average price. Basic charges per install at a cost you choose, up to $10,000 (US) per app per month. Advanced charges per tap up to a maximum you set, with no monthly cap.

### Is it still called Apple Search Ads?
Apple now brands the product Apple Ads, and its older searchads.apple.com help pages redirect to ads.apple.com. Many guides still use the old name.

### Do I need an app to advertise?
Yes. Apple Ads promotes apps in the App Store. A business without an app cannot use it to promote a location.

### Can I target a city?
Advanced supports location refinement in some countries, including the US. The page checked does not state radius or DMA options, so confirm in the account.

### Which should a small business pick, Basic or Advanced?
Basic if you want simple automatic management. Advanced if you need keywords, audience settings and detailed reports.
