A hardware store should start Google Ads with a small, local Search campaign, call and direction goals, and a long list of negative keywords. Click costs vary a lot. WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks list $8.33 per click for Home and Home Improvement and $4.14 for Shopping, Collectibles and Gifts. Neither category is hardware stores exactly, so use them as a range, not a promise.

This guide is for independent hardware and home-supply stores in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers DIY customers and trade buyers such as contractors. Every figure comes from a source at the end. Illustrative examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Does Google Ads Cost for a Hardware Store?

The benchmarks show a wide spread. For all industries the average is $5.42 per click, a 6.64% click-through rate and $66.69 per lead. Home and Home Improvement sits above that at $8.33 per click and $90.92 per lead, with a conversion rate of 8.05%. Shopping, Collectibles and Gifts is cheaper per click at $4.14, with 4.01% conversion.

The closest category depends on who you target. A store chasing homeowners buying screws and paint behaves more like retail. A store courting contractors and tradespeople behaves more like home services, with higher clicks and higher baskets. Treat your own account data as the real benchmark after the first month.

## Who Should You Target First: DIY Shoppers or Pros?

Pick one lane at the start, because the keywords, landing pages and offers differ. DIY shoppers search for a product or a quick fix, and want to know you have it in stock and are close. Trade buyers want a counter account, bulk pricing, and a reliable pickup time.

- **DIY lane:** emergency and project searches near the store, such as a product plus "near me" phrases, with a landing page showing stock, hours and directions.
- **Pro lane:** a "contractor supply" or trade-account offer, with a call button as the main action.

## How Do You Keep Ads Local?

Location settings are your main cost control. Google's help says radius targeting lets you show ads to customers within a certain distance from your business, with a radius of at least 1 km. It also recommends broad geo targeting ("Presence or interest") as a best practice for Search, but notes "Presence" fits when you only want people in specific locations.

For a store that people drive to, check the setting and test "Presence" if you see clicks from places you cannot serve. Then add negative keywords. Google explains that they exclude search terms and that they do not match close variants, so add singular and plural forms yourself.

## What Search Terms Should You Block?

A hardware store attracts a lot of unprofitable traffic: job seekers, store-brand comparisons, how-to videos, and online-only shoppers. Review your Search terms report weekly and add negatives. Ideas to start with:

- Jobs and careers words, such as "hiring" or "salary"
- Product types you do not stock
- Free and DIY-video intent, such as "how to" if you have no guide to show

Do this early. A pound or dollar saved on junk clicks is worth the same as one earned. Keep a list in a spreadsheet and note what each negative blocked.

## Can You Show Your Stock on Google?

Yes, if your country is supported. Google Merchant Center describes local inventory ads as a way to show in-store product and price availability hosted on Google, with pickup options such as pickup today or later. In-store inventory data must reach Merchant Center, so you need a product feed from your point-of-sale system. Availability is limited to certain countries, so check Google's list.

This suits hardware because shoppers often want a specific item today. For setup basics, read our [Google Business Profile 25-point checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist). A well-kept profile with hours and photos supports every ad.

## Should You Track Phone Calls?

Yes, for trade accounts especially. Google says clicks on a call asset on mobile cost the same as headline clicks, and that you only pay when the forwarding number shown in the ad is clicked. For conversion tracking, you set the minimum call length that counts, in seconds. Google gives an example advertiser who chose two minutes, so pick a length that matches a real inquiry, not a wrong number.

Pair calls with your in-store system. Ask counter staff to note when a customer says they saw you on Google, and compare to the report.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a hardware store spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no standard figure. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks show clicks of $4.14 to $8.33 across the nearest categories, so even $600 a month buys only roughly 70 to 145 clicks. Start small and scale once you know your cost per sale.

**Q: Is it better to target DIY customers or contractors?**
A: Trade accounts usually have larger baskets, which helps when click costs are high. DIY traffic works better when stock is visible and the shop is close.

**Q: How do I stop wasted clicks?**
A: Use radius targeting, review the Search terms report weekly, and add negative keywords. Negatives do not match close variants, so add plurals yourself.

**Q: Can I advertise my in-store stock?**
A: Local inventory ads can show in-store availability and price, if your country is supported and your inventory reaches Merchant Center.

**Q: Do calls count as conversions?**
A: They can. You set the minimum call length that counts, so choose one that reflects a real customer inquiry.
