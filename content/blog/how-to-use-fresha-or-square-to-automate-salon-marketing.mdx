Fresha and Square Appointments can both support automated salon messages, but the costs depend on your plan and add-ons. Fresha's paid plans start at $3 per team member, according to a comparison article, and Fresha also offers a free basic plan. Square Loyalty includes a 30-day free trial, and its built-in CRM tools are free. Marketing message prices change, so confirm the live price in each dashboard before you commit, and measure booked appointments rather than clicks.

## What Can Fresha and Square Automate for a Salon?

Both platforms handle more than appointment times. Fresha Deposits lets a business take a customizable amount in advance to secure a booking, and Fresha states that online bookings are not confirmed unless a payment is made first ([Salon Today, 2022 guide](https://www.salontoday.com/articles/the-2022-salon-technology-guide), secondary). Square Appointments can offer Afterpay buy now, pay later, and Salon Today notes that owners receive their full earnings from each sale immediately under that option.

Square also connects to hardware. Omlitt integrates with Square so a salon can hold credit cards for appointments and take payments through a connected Square reader ([Salon Today](https://www.salontoday.com/articles/the-2022-salon-technology-guide), secondary). Booksy, a rival platform, automatically prompts clients to leave a review after their appointment ([Booksy salon marketing guide](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), vendor). That review prompt is a useful test case: check whether your platform sends it without manual work.

## What Does Each Platform Cost to Start?

Fresha's paid options start at $3 for the team tier, and a free basic plan is available, according to a 2026 comparison article ([Zoca](https://zoca.com/post/6-best-software-to-get-more-salon-clients-comparison-reviews), secondary). Fresha's calendar and appointment management is subscription-free in the 2022 guide cited above, so confirm whether that still applies to the plan you choose.

Square Loyalty offers a free trial lasting 30 days, and Square says its built-in CRM tools that Square Loyalty integrates with are free ([Square Loyalty](https://squareup.com/us/en/software/loyalty), primary). A loyalty trial is not the same as a marketing message budget, so check how message sends are billed after the trial ends.

Add-on costs are easy to miss. One agency's terms list hosting, phone, SMS, email, registration, AI usage, premium automation, integration and third-party software costs as separate from its monthly fee ([TailWerks](https://tailwerks.com/industries/pet-grooming-marketing-agency/), vendor example). Ask whether each automation you plan to use is included in your plan or billed separately.

## Which Automations Should You Switch On First?

Start with messages tied directly to a visit, because they need the least campaign planning. Treat each step as a test, and keep it only if the booked appointments justify it.

1. Require a deposit or payment before a booking is confirmed, if your platform offers it, as Fresha Deposits does.
2. Send a review request after each appointment, using the automatic prompt your platform provides.
3. Add a Reserve with Google booking option, which Booksy says lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds (vendor claim).
4. Prioritise rebooking messages for regular clients. Lutily cites Kitomba data saying regular clients make up about 40% of the typical client base but account for 70% of all appointments ([Lutily](https://lutily.com/blog/rebook-before-they-leave), secondary).
5. Offer early access to holiday slots to your highest spenders, a tactic Booksy suggests for its top 10% of spenders ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), vendor).

Set up the messages in this order and check each one for two weeks before adding the next.

## How Do You Measure Whether a Marketing Message Pays?

Do not judge a campaign by clicks alone. The Rozhair advertising guide advises judging a search test by search terms, qualified calls or forms, consultations booked and booked appointments instead ([Rozhair](https://rozhair.com/blogs/education/hair-salon-advertising-ideas), secondary). It also recommends a weekly scorecard with advertising spend, qualified inquiries, consultations booked, new-client appointments completed, revenue from those appointments, and second appointments booked.

Trafft lists click-through rate, cost per click, appointments booked and return on ad spend as metrics for salon ads ([Trafft](https://trafft.com/salon-ads/), vendor). For paid traffic that feeds your booking page, the [Meta ads guide for hair salons](/blog/using-meta-ads-for-hair-salon-marketing) explains how to connect those numbers to bookings.

One agency case study reports that a New York salon's Google Ads cost $5.40 per booking action on $1.6K of monthly spend between April 18 and May 17, 2023 ([ARC](https://arccreativeco.com/what-a-salon-booking-costs-on-google-ads/), agency data). ARC defines a booking action as a booking-widget open or a call over 60 seconds. That is an engagement signal, not a booked appointment, so it cannot replace the scorecard above.

## What Might the Numbers Look Like? A Worked Example

The inputs below are **assumptions** for a solo stylist, except the $5.40 cost per booking action, which comes from the ARC case above. Replace every assumption with your own figures.

| Scenario | Booking actions | Appointments | Revenue | Net after $1,000 ad spend |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10% of actions book (assumption) | 185 | 18 | $1,080 | $80 |
| 25% of actions book (assumption) | 185 | 46 | $2,760 | $1,760 |
| 40% of actions book (assumption) | 185 | 74 | $4,440 | $3,440 |

Inputs: monthly ad budget $1,000 (assumption); cost per booking action $5.40 (ARC, sourced); average service price $60 (assumption). Booking actions = $1,000 divided by $5.40, about 185. Appointments = actions multiplied by the booking rate. Revenue = appointments multiplied by $60. These figures exclude platform fees, message costs, staff time and product costs, so subtract those before deciding.

The spread between rows is the point. A tool that adds a few percent to your booking rate can change the result more than a small change in message price.

## Where Does Automation Go Wrong?

Automation can make a salon look busy while the client experience gets worse. A SOCi guide to AI agents for marketing lists three main risks: lack of context in sensitive situations, inconsistent brand voice without training, and over-automation ([SOCi](https://www.soci.ai/blog/ai-agents-for-multi-location-marketing-5-tasks-you-can-fully-automate-today/), vendor). Those risks apply to salon messages too. A rebooking reminder sent to a client who recently had a difficult visit does damage that a template cannot judge.

Review your messages each quarter, and remove any automation that does not lead to a booked appointment. The [AI-powered email marketing guide for hair salons](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-for-hair-salons) covers how to keep AI-written messages on-brand.

## FAQ

### Is Fresha or Square cheaper for salon marketing?
The sourced figures do not allow a clean comparison of marketing message costs. Fresha's paid plans start at $3 for the team tier, and Square Loyalty offers a 30-day free trial. Compare the live per-message prices in each dashboard before choosing.

### Do booking deposits reduce no-shows?
Fresha says online bookings are not confirmed unless a payment is made first. The sourced material does not measure how much this reduces no-shows, so record your no-show rate for a month before and after you turn deposits on.

### Is a booking link the same as an integration?
No. A booking link is different from an integration that can read or change appointments, so check whether your tool connects to your calendar before you rely on it.

### What should I track first?
Use a weekly scorecard covering spend, qualified inquiries, consultations booked, new-client appointments completed, revenue, and second appointments booked. Clicks and views are diagnostic only.
