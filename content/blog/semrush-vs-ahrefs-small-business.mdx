The sticker price of the main Semrush plan is $139 a month. The main Ahrefs plan is $129. Over a year that is roughly $1,550 to $1,670 before add-ons, for a tool a single-location barber, café or groomer may open twice a month. This guide compares what you would actually pay, what each tool is good at, and which cheaper routes, including a $30 local plan and a free Ahrefs account, get a local business most of the value.

Prices below were read from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026. They change, and Semrush's page did not state its currency (the figures carry dollar signs), so check the live page for your country. This is written for owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada.

## The Short Answer

- **Most single-location local businesses:** neither of the big plans yet. Start with Google Search Console, your Business Profile insights, and Ahrefs' free account.
- **If you want local map-pack rank tracking and listings:** look at Semrush's separate Local toolkit, from $30 a month per location on annual billing, before the $139 plan.
- **If you plan to publish content and chase links:** Ahrefs is built for backlink and content research; Semrush is broader.

## The Cheaper Doors Into Each Tool

### Semrush Local: priced per location

[Semrush's Local toolkit](https://www.semrush.com/pricing/local/) is a separate product from the SEO plans. Its page lists:

- **Base, $30 a month per location (billed annually):** Google Business Profile management with an AI agent, AI post generation and publishing, review auto-replies, automated photo posting, and Map Rank Tracker with 375 credits.
- **Pro, $60 a month per location (billed annually):** everything in Base plus listing management, review management and generation, advanced Business Profile management, and 1,225 Map Rank Tracker credits.

The page shows only annual billing prices, so a monthly price may be higher. If your business lives and dies by the map pack, $360 to $720 a year is a far cheaper way in than the $139-a-month SEO plan.

### Ahrefs Free: more than you'd expect

What used to be called Webmaster Tools is now [Ahrefs Free](https://ahrefs.com/webmaster-tools). Ahrefs describes it as free website analytics, technical audit and SEO metrics for websites you verify. Limits per verified site, per Ahrefs' page: unlimited verified sites, a Site Audit that scans for 170+ technical issues with 5,000 crawl credits a month, and Site Explorer showing up to 1,000 keywords and 1,000 backlinks at once. Ahrefs also points to its $29 Starter plan for extended access.

For a 10-page site, that covers most of what a small business gets from a site audit.

## Where Each Tool Is Strong for Local Work

**Semrush** has more breadth. Its SEO plans do keyword research, competitor ad research and content checks, and its Local toolkit adds map rank tracking and listings. If you also run Google Ads, competitor keyword research is useful; our [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads) uses that kind of research when building campaigns.

**Ahrefs** has a reputation for link data, and its Site Explorer shows which sites link to you or to competitors. On local specifically, Ahrefs' own [local SEO guide](https://ahrefs.com/blog/local-seo/) describes its Rank Tracker as tracking regular organic results by country, state, city and postal code, and it recommends a separate tool (Grid My Business) for map pack tracking. In other words, Ahrefs itself does not claim to track the local pack. See our [Ahrefs local SEO guide](/blog/ahrefs-local-seo-guide) for how to work around that.

Be careful with claims like "Ahrefs has the largest link index". We did not verify a current third-party comparison, so we do not repeat it here.

Rankings for "plumber near me" or "barber in [town]" depend mostly on a complete Business Profile, reviews, consistent listings, a decent page per service and area, and a few relevant local links. ## The Math

How many new customers does a tool need to bring in to pay for itself? The prices are from the vendors' pages above. The customer value and the "tool influenced" share are **assumptions**; replace them with your own numbers.

| | Ahrefs Starter | Semrush Local Base | Semrush Local Pro | Ahrefs Lite | Semrush SEO (monthly billing) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Price per month | $29 | $30 (annual billing) | $60 (annual billing) | $129 | $139 |
| Cost per year | $348 | $360 | $720 | $1,548 | $1,668 |
| Profit per new customer (assumption) | $150 | $150 | $150 | $150 | $150 |
| New customers needed to break even | 2.3 | 2.4 | 4.8 | 10.3 | 11.1 |
| Per month | 0.2 | 0.2 | 0.4 | 0.9 | 0.9 |

How to read it: at $150 profit per new customer (a salon regular, a groomer's repeat client), the $139 plan must bring about 11 extra customers a year that you would not have won otherwise. That is plausible for a business publishing content for local search; it is a stretch for a café with a good Business Profile and no blog. Note it also ignores your time. If the profit per customer is $30, multiply every break-even figure by five.

## A Sensible Way to Test

1. Verify your site in Ahrefs Free and run a Site Audit. Fix errors that affect the pages people use to book.
2. Decide on one measurable job: track the map pack for 10 keywords, find 20 content ideas, or list 10 competitor backlinks.
3. If you need map tracking, start with the cheapest route to it (Semrush Local Base) before the full SEO plan.
4. Use Semrush's seven-day trial on an SEO plan only when you have the task list ready, so the week is not wasted.
5. Cancel or downgrade if you are not logging in weekly.

## Do This Today

1. Write down your profit per new customer and how many extra customers a year would justify a tool. The table above shows the break-even for each price.
2. Verify your website in Search Console and in Ahrefs Free, and run the Site Audit.
3. Check your Business Profile is complete, using our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).
4. List the 10 keywords you want to win locally, and check where you rank today by searching them manually in a private window.
5. Only if the answer is "I need ongoing map-pack tracking", try Semrush Local or a trial, with a one-month review date in your calendar.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Semrush or Ahrefs better for local SEO?**
A: For local rank tracking and listings, Semrush has dedicated products (Local Base at $30 a month per location and Local Pro at $60, on annual billing, per its pricing page). Ahrefs' own guide describes its Rank Tracker as organic-only and recommends a separate tool for the map pack. Ahrefs is the one to look at for backlink research.

**Q: Do I need either one?**
A: Not at the start. Search Console, Business Profile insights, GA4 and Ahrefs Free cover the basics. A paid plan helps once you do regular keyword research, competitor analysis or content planning.

**Q: Is there a free trial?**
A: Semrush's pricing page offers a seven-day free trial on its SEO, Starter and Pro+ plans and a limited free account. We could not confirm a current Ahrefs trial, so check its page.

**Q: Can I use both?**
A: You can, but for most small businesses the extra value does not cover the second bill. Pick one paid tool and use free ones for the rest.

**Q: Will prices change?**
A: Yes. Treat the figures above as October 2026 prices and check the vendor pages before you buy. Semrush's page did not confirm the currency.
