For a single-location business, neither Semrush nor Ahrefs is the default first purchase. Ahrefs lists Starter at $29 a month, Lite at $129, Standard at $249 and Advanced at $449, per its pricing page checked in November 2026. Those tiers were built for consultants and in-house teams tracking many projects. A local business usually needs rank checks in one city, a look at its Google profile, and a short list of pages to fix. This rewrite does not quote Semrush prices, because its pricing page was not among the checked sources.

Annual billing is the main discount. Ahrefs says it saves up to 17% when paid yearly, so the Lite tier could drop by at most about $21.93 a month on a $129 base. Extra users cost more on top of every tier, and Lite includes only one user. Prices change, so confirm the live figure before buying.

## What do Ahrefs plans include for a small business?

Lite is the tier most small businesses would test first. It includes 750 tracked keywords, 5 non-verified projects with unlimited verified projects, 100,000 crawl credits and 150 checks on AI prompts. It also lists Rank Tracker, Site Audit, Competitive Analysis and a GBP Monitor marked as beta, which is the only local-specific item on the plan list.

Starter at $29 a month is positioned for checking what people search and spying on competitors. The pricing page does not itemise its limits, so a buyer should test whether the feature set answers the questions on hand before paying the higher Lite price.

The table below uses only figures from the pricing page. Starter is left out because its limits are not published there.

| Plan | Price per month | Tracked keywords | Projects | Users included |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lite | $129 | 750 | 5 non-verified, unlimited verified | 1 |
| Standard | $249 | 2,000 | 20 non-verified, unlimited verified | 1 |
| Advanced | $449 | 5,000 | 50 non-verified, unlimited verified | 1 |

Standard roughly doubles the keyword allowance and adds two years of history. A single café or dental practice rarely needs that depth, so the step up is only worth it when several locations or clients share the account.

## Which Ahrefs features help with local SEO?

Ahrefs describes its local work in terms of the Google Business Profile. Its homepage says the Local SEO module helps owners optimise a profile to attract more local customers and rank in local results, and it links to a GBP Monitor. The pricing page lists GBP Monitor as a beta feature on Lite and above.

Local rankings depend heavily on the profile, reviews and location pages. Ahrefs's own local guide says many people use search engines to find nearby businesses, and cites Google figures that 30% of mobile searches relate to location and 28% of nearby searches lead to a purchase. Those figures are secondary, as quoted in Ahrefs's guide, and not opened directly from Google.

Ahrefs is stronger on links and competitor research than on review management. A local owner who wants to know which directories or local partners link to rivals will find that useful. A business whose main gap is reviews and listing accuracy may get more from a dedicated local tool. ## Does a free Ahrefs option cover a local site?

Ahrefs Free is listed on the pricing page with the line "Get Ahrefs data on your site and fix what matters." That makes it the obvious first step for a site owner who only needs data about their own domain, rather than a full competitor study.

Free access is the right test when the question is narrow: which pages are underperforming, and what technical issues block them. Once the list grows to competitors, multiple cities or several clients, the paid tiers become relevant. The page does not state the Free tier's exact limits, so check them on the live page before relying on them for a monthly report.

Choosing between tiers is a budget question, not a feature contest. The next section works through one example.

## What does the maths look like before subscribing?

**Worked example, with assumed inputs.** Assume a café owner spends 2 hours a month checking rankings by hand and values that time at $30 an hour, which is $60 a month. Assume Ahrefs Lite at $129 a month, billed monthly. The gap is $69 a month. Lite pays for itself only if it replaces other paid checks or surfaces one fix worth at least $69 a month in extra sales.

Those inputs are assumptions. Replace the hours, the hourly value and the named fix with real figures before deciding. If the café owner saves 1 hour, the break-even gap grows to $99 a month, and the case gets weaker.

A simpler test runs for one month. Write down three questions the tool must answer, such as which five pages to rewrite or which competitor gained listings. Pay monthly rather than annually. If the answers do not change a decision by renewal, cancel. ## FAQ

### Is Semrush or Ahrefs better for a local business?

For most single-location businesses, neither is the first purchase. Ahrefs is a better fit when link research and competitor analysis drive the work, and Starter at $29 a month is the cheapest paid entry point on its pricing page. Semrush should be compared on its own live pricing before choosing.

### What is the cheapest way to use Ahrefs?

Ahrefs Free covers data on your own site, and Starter lists at $29 a month. Starter suits short, specific question lists. Ongoing competitor tracking usually pushes buyers to Lite at $129 a month, so keep Starter for a defined test.

### Can a small business share one Ahrefs account?

Lite includes one user, and extra users cost $40 a month each on that plan according to the pricing page. Sharing a login informally risks lockouts and messy billing. A freelancer reporting monthly may need one seat, but a team should price seats into the comparison from the start.

### When is a paid SEO suite worth it?

A suite is worth paying for when it replaces several separate tools, such as rank tracking, link research and reporting for multiple locations or clients. A business that logs in twice a month to check five keywords should stay on free tools.
