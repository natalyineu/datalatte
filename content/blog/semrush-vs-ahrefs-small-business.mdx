For most small and local businesses, Semrush is the safer all-round choice and Ahrefs is the better pick if your priority is backlink research and clean, fast reporting. Neither is essential on day one. If you run a single-location business, free tools plus a paid plan on one of these two is usually plenty.

Both platforms are professional-grade tools built mainly for agencies and in-house SEO teams, and both are priced accordingly. So the real question for a small business is not "which is better in general?" but "which one will I actually use, and do I need either of them yet?" Here is how to decide.

## The short comparison

Pricing and features change often, so treat the figures below as rough ranges and check each vendor's current pricing page.

| Factor | Semrush | Ahrefs |
|---|---|---|
| Typical entry price | Roughly $120 to $140 per month for the base plan | Roughly $100 to $130 per month for the base plan |
| Best known for | All-in-one toolkit: keywords, ads, content, social, local | Backlink index, site audits, keyword and content research |
| Local SEO features | Listing management and local rank tracking add-ons | Limited dedicated local tools |
| Learning curve | More tools to learn | Cleaner, more focused interface |
| Free options | Limited free account | Free Ahrefs Webmaster Tools for your own site |
| Best for | Owners who want one platform for SEO, content and PPC research | Owners focused on content and links |

The practical pattern we see: Semrush has more breadth, Ahrefs has more depth in link data. For a barber, a coffee shop or a pet groomer, breadth matters more than depth.

## What small businesses actually need from an SEO tool

Local businesses win on a short list of things:

1. A complete, active Google Business Profile.
2. Consistent name, address and phone details across directories.
3. A decent website with a page per service and location.
4. Reviews, and responses to them.
5. A handful of relevant local links.

Only some of that happens inside an SEO suite. Profile and review management happens in Google itself, and your website happens in your CMS. A suite helps with keyword research, competitor checks, site audits and tracking rankings. That is useful, but it is a supporting role.

## Where Semrush tends to win

- **Local and listing tools.** Semrush offers local SEO features, such as listing management and map rank tracking, typically as paid add-ons. If you want one dashboard for citations and local rankings, this is a plus.
- **Keyword research with intent.** Its Keyword Magic Tool is easy to filter by question, intent and difficulty.
- **Competitor ad research.** If you also run Google Ads, you can see which keywords nearby competitors bid on. Our [Google Ads keyword guides](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-coffee-shops-in-2026) show how that research feeds a campaign.
- **Content helpers.** Writing assistants, topic research and on-page checks are built in.
- **Position tracking by location.** You can track rankings for a specific city or postcode.

## Where Ahrefs tends to win

- **Backlink data.** Ahrefs is widely regarded as having one of the largest link indexes. If you want to see who links to a competing salon or which local sites might link to you, it is excellent.
- **Site Audit and clarity.** The interface is clean, and reports are easy to read.
- **Content Explorer.** Helpful for finding what has worked for similar topics.
- **Free Webmaster Tools.** Verify your own site and get a free site audit and backlink view, a good low-cost starting point.

Ahrefs is lighter on dedicated local features, so you may pair it with a separate local rank tracker or manual checks. We cover that in our [Ahrefs local SEO guide](/blog/ahrefs-local-seo-guide).

## The real costs

Subscription price is only part of it. Consider:

- **Plan limits.** Base plans cap projects, tracked keywords and reports, which is fine for one business but can pinch if you manage several locations.
- **Add-ons.** Local, AI and extra seats can raise the bill.
- **Your time.** A tool you open once a quarter is expensive at any price.
- **Annual discounts.** Both typically discount annual billing, but do not commit before a trial or a monthly test.

A realistic budget for a small business that really uses one of these is roughly $100 to $150 per month. If that feels heavy relative to your marketing spend, you probably do not need it yet.

## Cheaper or free alternatives to try first

Before paying, make good use of what is free:

- **Google Business Profile Insights** for how people find and contact you.
- **Google Search Console** for the queries your site already appears for.
- **Google Analytics 4** for traffic and conversions.
- **Google Keyword Planner** (inside a Google Ads account) for rough search volumes.
- **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools** for site audits on your own domain.
- Budget local SEO tools, which we compare in our [best local SEO audit tool roundup](/blog/best-local-seo-audit-tool) and [AI SEO tools for small business](/blog/ai-seo-tools-for-small-business).

## How to choose: a quick decision guide

Choose **Semrush** if:
- You want keyword research, competitor ad insight and local tools in one place.
- You run both SEO and Google Ads.
- You plan to publish regular content and want built-in guidance.

Choose **Ahrefs** if:
- You want deep backlink and competitor link research.
- You prefer a simpler interface and strong site audits.
- You mostly need content and link ideas rather than listings.

Choose **neither yet** if:
- You have one location and fewer than a few dozen pages on your site.
- You have not finished your Google Business Profile, reviews and website basics.
- You will not review reports at least monthly.

<Callout type="coffee">Buying an enterprise SEO suite before you have fixed your Google Business Profile is like buying a commercial espresso machine before you have beans. Start with the basics, then upgrade the kit.</Callout>

## A sensible way to test

1. Pick one tool and use a free trial or one month on a monthly plan (check current trial terms).
2. Set a clear goal: for example, find 20 keywords, audit your site, and list 10 competitor backlinks.
3. Export what you need.
4. Cancel or downgrade if you are not using it weekly.

Run a [local SEO audit](/blog/local-seo-audit-checklist-small-business) alongside it, because tool output without a checklist tends to turn into a long to-do list nobody finishes.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Semrush or Ahrefs better for local SEO?**
A: Semrush has more dedicated local features, such as listing management and local rank tracking add-ons. Ahrefs is stronger on backlinks. For purely local work, Semrush usually fits better, though neither replaces a well-optimised Google Business Profile.

**Q: Can I use both?**
A: You can, but most small businesses will not get enough extra value to justify paying for both. Pick one and use free tools for the rest.

**Q: Do I need an SEO tool at all?**
A: Not always. Google Search Console, Google Analytics and Google Business Profile Insights cover the essentials. A paid tool helps when you are doing active keyword research, competitor analysis or regular content planning.

**Q: How much do these tools cost?**
A: Entry plans are typically around $100 to $140 per month, with higher tiers costing considerably more. Prices change, so check the current pricing pages.
