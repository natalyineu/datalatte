To improve local SEO, start with your Google Business Profile: verify it, fill in every field, keep the name honest, and collect genuine reviews. Google says local results rest on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. You control the first and third; distance is set by where the searcher stands. The 15 steps below are grouped by those factors.

Google does not publish a weight for each factor, and nothing here guarantees a position. The steps come from Google's own help pages and one consumer survey, all listed at the end.

## What Do Google's Three Local Ranking Factors Mean?

Google's help page defines them in one line each. Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for. Distance is how far each business is from the searcher. Prominence is how well-known a business is.

You cannot move closer to every customer, so the work sits in relevance (accurate categories and details) and prominence (reviews, a real website, a profile people use). Each step below says which factor it supports.

## Which Steps Improve Relevance?

1. **Claim and verify the profile.** Google says verification tells it you are authorized to represent the business, so the profile is more likely to show in results.
2. **Complete every field.** Google states that businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results.
3. **Use your real name.** Google's guidelines say the name should be the one used consistently on your storefront, website and stationery. Do not add taglines, hours, phone numbers or service descriptions, which can lead to suspension.
4. **Pick the fewest, most specific categories.** Google advises categories that complete the phrase "This business is a", not a list of services.
5. **Set regular and special hours.** Hours, including special hours for holidays, are on Google's list of details to keep current.
6. **Add photos and video.** Google says photos and videos show customers what you offer and tell the story of your business.
7. **Keep name, address and phone the same everywhere.** This is a practice, not a Google-stated rule: a mismatch between profile and website makes it harder to confirm which business is which.

## Which Steps Build Prominence?

8. **Ask for reviews with Google's link or QR code.** In Business Profile, choose Read Reviews, then Get more reviews, and copy the link or save the QR code (desktop only). Details are in our [guide to getting more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop).
9. **Never pay for reviews.** Google's policy calls incentives, including discounts and free goods, prohibited, and says merchants may not selectively solicit positive reviews.
10. **Reply to reviews.** Google says replying shows you value feedback.
11. **Post updates, offers and events.** Posts show on Search and Maps; offers need a title and dates. See our [Business Profile post types](/blog/google-my-business-post-types-for-local-seo).
12. **Add LocalBusiness structured data to your site.** Google requires name and address, and recommends hours, phone and URL. It does not guarantee any rich result.
13. **Give each location a page and a clear service list.** A practice, not a Google rule; it helps the profile link point somewhere specific.
14. **Track calls, direction requests and bookings.** See [local SEO KPIs](/blog/how-to-measure-local-seo-success-kpis) for what to watch.
15. **Review the profile monthly.** Hours, photos, offers and unanswered reviews go stale quickly.

## How Many Reviews Do You Need?

BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and only 9% will use one with five or fewer. It also found 78% were asked by a business to leave a review in the past 12 months. The page gives two different follow-through figures (65% and 83%), so no response rate is quoted here.

That makes a steady flow of requests more useful than a one-time push. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) lists what else to check.

## FAQ

### How long does local SEO take to work?

Google publishes no timeline. Changes to a profile can appear quickly, while reviews and prominence build over months. Track calls and direction requests monthly.

### Do I need a website for local SEO?

Google's profile guidance does not require one, but a website gives the profile a destination and lets you add structured data. Posts and buttons can also link to a booking page.

### Does paying for reviews help?

No. Google's policy bans incentivized reviews and they can be removed.

### Is structured data required?

No. Google says it does not guarantee that features using structured data will appear.

### How often should I update my profile?

Check monthly, and update hours before every holiday.
