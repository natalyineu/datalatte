To improve local SEO, start with your Google Business Profile. Verify it, complete every field, keep the business name accurate, and collect genuine reviews over time. Google says verification tells it you are authorized to represent the business, and that businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results. Google does not publish weights for its ranking factors, so nothing below guarantees a position. The 15 steps are grouped by what they support.

## Why Does a Complete Profile Matter First?

Google's local ranking help page treats the profile itself as the starting point. It says verification makes a listing more likely to appear in search results, and that complete, accurate information has the same effect in local results. ## Which Steps Improve Relevance and Accuracy?

1. Claim and verify the profile. Verification tells Google you are authorized to represent the business, so the profile is more likely to show in search results.
2. Complete every field. Grubhub recommends including as much information as possible, including an address, phone number, website URL, operating hours and photos. [Grubhub restaurant SEO guide](https://get.grubhub.com/blog/restaurant-seo/)
3. Use the name customers see on your sign and website. Keep it free of taglines and extra keywords. This is a practice, not a Google-stated rule.
4. Pick the most specific primary category rather than a list of services. This is also a practice.
5. Set regular hours and special hours for holidays. Operating hours are on Grubhub's list of details to include.
6. Add photos and video. Google's insights are built partly from photos, reviews and local search trends. [Google Business Profile Help: Understand your performance and insights](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en)
7. Keep name, address and phone number identical everywhere they appear. A mismatch between the profile and the website makes it harder to confirm which business is which.

Google's insights, according to its help page, are created from public, location-based information such as reviews, photos and local search trends. That means the details you publish feed what Google can see. For a field-by-field walkthrough, the [Google Business Profile optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers the same ground.

## Which Steps Build Prominence?

8. Ask customers for reviews directly. Google says reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search. [Google Business Profile Help: Get reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)
9. Never pay for reviews. A Guardian reporter who took fake-review work described scam channels offering up to 14 reviews per session, initially paid at $5 each. The reporter earned $30 from several hours of work. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/mar/27/paid-write-fake-google-reviews-scammer-cryptocurrency) (secondary)
10. Reply to reviews in your own words. This is a practice that keeps the review feed active.
11. Publish posts about offers and events. Google says a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live; Pending and Not approved posts do not show. [Google Business Profile Help: Create and manage posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169)
12. Earn mentions in local media. Coverage from local outlets is a practical way to become better known, which is what prominence measures. See [how to get local press coverage for your small business](/blog/how-to-get-local-press-coverage-for-your-small-business).

## How Do Reviews Affect Local Visibility?

Google defines prominence as how well-known a business is, and says prominent places are more likely to show up in search results. It adds that prominence also draws on how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has. [Google Business Profile Help: Tips to improve your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)

Reviews therefore do two jobs. They appear beside the listing, and they count toward prominence. Mailchimp describes Google profile reviews as especially influential in local search ranking and visibility, although it does not quantify the effect. [Mailchimp Local Marketing Tips](https://mailchimp.com/resources/local-marketing/) (secondary)

Steady requests work better than a single push. Industry guides for specific niches show the same pattern, such as this [coffee shop Google Business Profile guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization), which covers how to make review requests part of the visit.

## What Should You Track Each Month?

13. Give each location its own page and a clear service list. This is a practice that helps the profile link to a specific destination.
14. Track calls, direction requests and booking clicks in your Business Profile Insights, and compare them month to month.
15. Review the profile monthly. Indelible Think advises checking Google Analytics, Google Search Console and Google Business Insights once a month. [Indelible Think](https://indeliblethink.co.uk/how-liverpool-businesses-can-improve-seo/) (secondary)

Monthly checks catch stale hours, unanswered reviews and posts that were never approved. They also show whether steps 8 and 11 are producing measurable activity.

## A Worked Example: Reviews Over Three Months

The figures below are **assumptions** for a salon, not benchmarks. Google does not publish a review target, so 40 is a planning figure you can change.

| Item | Assumption or result |
| --- | --- |
| Reviews today | 18 (assumption) |
| Planning target | 40 (assumption) |
| Reviews still needed | 22 |
| Clients visiting per month | 120 (assumption) |
| Share of clients who leave a review after being asked | 10% (assumption) |
| New reviews per month | 120 × 10% = 12 |
| Months to reach 40 | 22 ÷ 12 ≈ 1.8, about 2 months |
| Same plan at a 5% response rate | 6 per month, 22 ÷ 6 ≈ 3.7, about 4 months |

The calculation shows why measurement matters. Replace the 10% and 5% with your own rate after the first month of requests. Weeks 1 and 2 cover steps 1 to 7, weeks 3 and 4 cover steps 8 to 12, and steps 13 to 15 run every month after that.

## FAQ

### How long does local SEO take to show results?

Google's SEO guidance says you should generally wait a few weeks to assess whether your work had beneficial effects in Search results. Reviews and prominence build over months, so track trends rather than single weeks.

### Do I need a website for local SEO?

Google's tips page says prominence draws on how many websites link to a business. A website that others link to supports the profile. Check Google's live help page for current guidance on what else it considers.

### Is structured data required?

No. Google's LocalBusiness structured data guidance says non-critical issues flagged by the Rich Results Test are not required for rich results eligibility, although fixing them can improve structured data quality.

### Can I offer incentives for reviews?

Avoid it. Paid review schemes carry real risk, and the Guardian reporter's experience shows how fake-review work is marketed. Ask for honest reviews and reply to all of them.

### How often should I update my profile?

Check it monthly, and update hours before every holiday.
