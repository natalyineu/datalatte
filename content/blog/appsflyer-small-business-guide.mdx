A small business needs AppsFlyer only if it has its own mobile app and pays for ads that drive people to install it. AppsFlyer's pay-as-you-go Growth plan charges $0.07 per attributed conversion after a Welcome Package of 12,000 free conversions in the first 12 months, according to its [pricing page](https://www.appsflyer.com/pricing/). Local businesses such as cafes, salons and gyms rarely need it. They need booking, call and direction tracking, which is a different job.

## What Does AppsFlyer Do?

AppsFlyer is a mobile attribution platform. Attribution connects an app install, and the actions that follow it, to the ad campaign that prompted it. That lets you see which campaign, network or channel produced each user rather than guessing from total downloads.

The tool is built for apps. It reads install and in-app event data from the phone, so it only makes sense when your product lives in an app store. A website visitor who fills in a booking form is outside its scope, which is why the pricing page frames the Zero plan around owned media for apps.

## Does a Small Business Need Mobile Attribution?

Usually not. Attribution answers one question: which paid campaign brought this app install? That matters when you spend on several networks to promote an app. It matters much less if customers book through your website, phone calls or a Google Business Profile listing.

You are more likely to need it if you run a loyalty or ordering app, advertise it on more than one network, and want to compare what each install cost. If the app has a small base of users who arrived by word of mouth, the tool will tell you very little. Paying for a measurement layer before paid acquisition exists is the most common mistake here.

Before signing up, check three things: whether you spend on app-install ads, how many networks you use, and whether a developer can add an SDK. If the answer to the first is no, the other two do not matter.

## How Much Does AppsFlyer Cost?

The pricing page lists three plans, and only two have a published price.

The Zero plan is free but is described as a simple tool for owned-media activities, with the note "Not suitable for apps with paid activities". That rules it out for any business running app-install ads. The Growth plan is pay-as-you-go at $0.07 per conversion after the Welcome Package. The Enterprise plan is custom and requires a demo request to get a quote.

Zero and Growth both include the Welcome Package: 12,000 free conversions within the first 12 months, core marketing analytics, and 30 days of access to select premium add-ons. The allowance is tied to that first year, so budget from month 13 onward as if no free conversions remain. The page does not publish Enterprise prices, so any figure above the Growth rate is a sales conversation rather than a number you can budget from the website.

Secondary sources also report that premium features such as Protect360 and Data Locker are sold separately. [Airbridge's pricing article](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/appsflyer-pricing-small-apps) (secondary) says AppsFlyer does not publish add-on prices, so you would need a quote before totalling your costs.

## What Would Growth Cost a Local App?

All inputs below are assumptions for illustration. Only the $0.07 rate and the 12,000 allowance come from AppsFlyer's pricing page.

Take a gym chain with a booking app. Assume it drives 2,000 attributed installs per month from paid campaigns. Use these steps to estimate cost:

1. Multiply monthly paid installs by 12 to get year-one conversions: 2,000 × 12 = 24,000 (assumption).
2. Subtract the Welcome Package allowance: 24,000 − 12,000 = 12,000 billable conversions in year one.
3. Multiply by the Growth rate: 12,000 × $0.07 = $840 in year one.
4. From year two, with no free allowance: 24,000 × $0.07 = $1,680 per year, or $140 per month.

The year-one figure assumes every free conversion is used inside the first 12 months. Add any premium add-on quotes on top, and count any in-app purchases or bookings if you plan to bill them as conversions, since those raise volume.

## What Should a Small Business Do Instead?

If you have an app but only one paid channel, the ad platform's own install reporting is the cheapest starting point. It will not match installs to in-app events across networks, but it shows cost per install for a single source, which is enough for a small budget.

If your main goal is bookings, calls or walk-ins, attribution is the wrong tool. Track those with call tracking, booking-form events and your point-of-sale or scheduling software. ## How Hard Is Setup?

Setup is developer work. The vendor's SDK is added to the app, in-app events are defined, and each ad network is connected. That work runs on both iOS and Android, so it can cost more than the subscription if the app was built by an agency or contractor.

The pricing page does not give setup steps or hours. Ask your developer for an estimate before committing, and confirm whether the 30-day premium add-on trial is long enough to test the features you need. If the app is still in design, decide on attribution before launch, because event naming is hard to change afterwards.

## FAQ

### Is AppsFlyer free?

The Zero plan is free, but it is for owned-media apps and is not suitable for apps running paid activities. The Growth plan includes 12,000 free conversions in the first 12 months, then charges $0.07 per conversion.

### What counts as a conversion?

AppsFlyer's pricing page describes conversions as installs and actions that your campaigns drove and AppsFlyer measured. Organic installs it does not attribute are not counted.

### Do I need AppsFlyer if I only run Google Ads?

Not necessarily. If your only paid channel is one network and your app is small, that network's own install reporting may be enough. Consider AppsFlyer when you advertise across several networks and need to compare them on one basis.

### Can I use AppsFlyer for a website?

AppsFlyer is built for mobile app attribution. Its pricing page frames both plans around apps, so website conversions such as bookings or contact forms are outside what it is designed to measure.
