Local businesses can buy ads around Amazon's Thursday Night Football (TNF) in three ways: through Amazon's own advertising team, through Amazon DSP via a partner agency, or through a programmatic DSP that has access to Prime Video inventory. Live NFL inventory is premium, so costs are typically far higher than ordinary streaming ads.

A local business generally needs a meaningful monthly budget and a clear game-day reason to advertise. Check current pricing and minimums with Amazon Ads or your partner before committing.

The question is not whether the ad can run. It is whether the cost per customer beats a Google search ad that costs a fraction of it. The worked example below shows how to test that before you spend a dollar.

## What Is Thursday Night Football on Prime Video?

Prime Video is the exclusive streaming home of Thursday Night Football in the US, and 2026 is its fifth season ([Cord Cutters News](https://cordcuttersnews.com/prime-video-unveils-thursday-night-football-schedule-for-the-2026-nfl-season/), second-hand, checked Oct 2026). Sources differ on the exact 2026 game count, so confirm the schedule with your provider. Week 1 is a Netflix game, outside Amazon's package. For advertisers that means:

- Viewers watch through Prime Video rather than a standard broadcast channel.
- Audiences are large by streaming standards. Check Amazon's and Nielsen's latest published figures, since averages change by season and matchup.
- Amazon DSP lists Thursday Night Football among the inventory it can buy, alongside Prime Video, Twitch and Fire TV ([Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/amazon-dsp), checked Oct 2026), and Amazon has first-party shopping and viewing signals for targeting.

## How Do You Buy Ads Around Thursday Night Football?

### Option 1: Amazon Ads directly

Amazon's advertising team can manage sports campaigns for larger advertisers. Minimum commitments for live sports are typically high, so this route rarely fits a single-location business.

### Option 2: Amazon DSP through a partner

Amazon DSP is Amazon's programmatic platform and has offered both self-service and managed-service options ([Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/amazon-dsp), checked Oct 2026). Access terms differ, and agencies that are Amazon Advertising partners may offer lower thresholds than going direct. Ask any partner for current minimum spend, fees and whether live sports inventory is available on your budget.

### Option 3: A programmatic DSP

Some programmatic platforms can buy Amazon streaming inventory alongside other CTV supply, which suits smaller budgets. You usually will not get Amazon's full audience data, but you can still target by geography and sometimes by content category. Platforms to ask about include StackAdapt, The Trade Desk and Simpli.fi; see our [StackAdapt review for local business](/blog/stackadapt-review-local-business-programmatic).

Ask each provider these four questions:

1. Is live TNF inventory actually available to me, or only general Prime Video ads?
2. What is the minimum budget and the CPM range for live sports?
3. Can I target by DMA or ZIP code?
4. What reporting will I receive, and can I get household-level or store-visit measurement?

## What Does TNF Advertising Cost?

Live sports CTV is among the most expensive streaming inventory, and prices vary by game, season, platform and buying method. No reliable single figure is available, so request a written quote with the CPM and the minimum spend.

To budget:

- Decide how many impressions you want in your market, divide by 1,000 and multiply by the quoted CPM.
- Divide impressions by the frequency you want per household to get reach. Four impressions across 25,000 households beats one impression across 100,000.
- Add the cost of a 15 or 30-second video creative if you do not already have one.

<Callout type="warning">If your budget is only a few hundred dollars a month, live NFL inventory is probably not the best use of it. Capture existing demand with Google Ads and your Google Business Profile first.</Callout>

## What Would You Need to Break Even on TNF Ads?

Example only. Every number below is an assumption, not an Amazon price, not a market average and not a client result. Swap in the CPM from your written quote and your own ticket size.

Assumptions: budget $3,000, quoted CPM $30, target frequency 4, average customer spend $40, Google Ads click cost $3 and 5% of clicks becoming customers.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions bought | $3,000 / $30 x 1,000 | 100,000 |
| Households reached | 100,000 / 4 | 25,000 |
| Customers needed to break even on revenue | $3,000 / $40 | 75 |
| Share of households that must buy | 75 / 25,000 | 0.3% |
| If 0.1% of households buy | 25,000 x 0.001 | 25 customers, $120 each |
| Same $3,000 in Google Ads | $3,000 / $3 = 1,000 clicks x 5% | 50 customers, $60 each |

So under these assumptions the TNF test needs three times the response rate you would need to match the search budget, because 0.3% of households must act. That does not make TNF a bad idea. It means it is a brand and game-day bet, and you should price it that way. If the table says you need more response than you can believe, do not buy.

## What Targeting Options Are Available?

Amazon's advantage is its shopping and viewing data. Through Amazon DSP, advertisers can often target audiences by product interest categories and combine them with location. Examples of how that could apply:

| Business | Possible targeting angle |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Pizza or wings restaurant | Food delivery or grocery shoppers in your DMA |
| Auto repair shop | Car parts and accessories shoppers nearby |
| Pet groomer | Pet product buyers in your area |
| Gym or fitness studio | Fitness equipment and supplement shoppers |
| Home services | Home improvement shoppers |

Available audiences and minimums change, so confirm exactly what your buying route supports. Tight neighborhood-level targeting on CTV tends to waste impressions, so these campaigns work best for businesses serving a metro area.

## How Do You Set Up a TNF Campaign?

1. **Choose your window.** TNF runs for part of the NFL season. Pick the full season or a few key weeks.
2. **Pick your buying route** by budget: a programmatic DSP for smaller tests, a partner agency for mid-sized budgets, Amazon directly for large ones.
3. **Set targeting.** Geography first, then any audience layers your provider supports. Limit to connected TV devices.
4. **Make a short creative.** A 15-second spot with one local offer and one call to action. Viewers are watching the game, not reading.
5. **Add tracking.** Use a unique URL, promo code or QR code (for example, "tnf" at checkout) so you can see response from game nights.

## Are TNF Ads Worth It for a Local Business?

**Likely a fit when:**

- You have a game-day tie-in, such as a restaurant, bar, delivery or sports-adjacent service.
- You serve a metro area, not a few blocks.
- You can afford a meaningful test and already have a video spot.

**Probably not a fit when:**

- Your monthly ad budget is small.
- You serve a very small radius.
- You sell B2B services.

## What Are Cheaper Ways to Reach Football Fans?

If live TNF inventory is out of reach, you can still reach the same fans around game day:

- **Local sports bars, partner promotions or sponsorships.** A game-day special promoted through a venue or a local league can cost far less.
- **Geo-targeted social and search ads** that run Thursday afternoons and evenings with a "watch party" or takeout offer.
- **Other CTV and streaming placements** not tied to live games, which usually cost less per thousand impressions.
- **Email and SMS** to your own list with a game-day offer, which costs almost nothing.

## How Do You Measure Whether It Worked?

TV-style campaigns are harder to measure than search ads, so plan measurement before you launch:

- Compare sales and foot traffic on Thursdays in your test market with previous weeks.
- Use a unique URL, QR code or promo code tied to the campaign.
- Watch branded search volume in Google Search Console and calls to your business.
- Ask customers how they heard about you.

Decide in advance what result would justify a repeat. Write the number down before the first game.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I advertise on Amazon Thursday Night Football?**
A: Through Amazon Ads directly, through Amazon DSP via a partner, or through a programmatic platform with Prime Video access. Ask about live sports availability and minimums, since these vary.

**Q: How much does a Thursday Night Football ad cost?**
A: Live NFL inventory is premium and prices vary by game, platform and buying method. Request a written quote with CPM and minimum spend before planning.

**Q: Can I target only TNF games?**
A: It depends on the buying route. Direct and managed buys offer the most control, while programmatic platforms may give you sports content adjacency rather than guaranteed TNF placement.

**Q: Do I need to sell on Amazon to advertise on Prime Video?**
A: No. Advertisers use Amazon DSP and similar routes to buy streaming ad inventory without having a seller account.

**Q: What video file do I need?**
A: Each provider publishes its own video specifications (format, resolution, length, audio). Ask for them before you produce the creative.
