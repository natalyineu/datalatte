Wolt ads let a small restaurant buy visibility inside the app and pay only when a customer completes an order. Wolt says its sponsored listings place a venue in top spots across the discovery page and search results, and that advertisers pay for completed orders, not clicks ([Wolt Luxembourg merchant page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/lux/wolt-ads/restaurants-and-stores)). For a restaurant competing with chains, the lever is rarely a bigger budget. It is a clear listing, a focused local audience, and a promo that gives a customer a reason to choose you. This guide covers the formats, a launch checklist and a worked budget example with labelled assumptions.

## How can Wolt ads help a small restaurant compete with chains?

Chains win on brand recognition and scale. A single restaurant usually cannot match that, but it can win the moment a nearby customer opens the app and is deciding where to order. Wolt's pages describe its ads as a way to be seen at that decision point, through placement on the discovery page and in search results ([Wolt Azerbaijan page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/aze/wolt-ads)).

The payment model matters more for a small operator than the reach claims. Wolt's merchant page states that you only pay for completed sales placed through your ads ([Wolt Luxembourg merchant page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/lux/wolt-ads/restaurants-and-stores)). That turns advertising into a cost per order rather than a fixed guess about awareness, which makes it easier to stop a campaign that is not working.

Wolt's own figures are vendor-stated and come from its wider network, not from a single restaurant. Treat them as direction, then check your own order data.

## Which Wolt ad format fits a small budget?

Wolt's merchant pages list three formats that matter for a restaurant: sponsored listings, promos and banners ([Wolt Luxembourg merchant page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/lux/wolt-ads/restaurants-and-stores)). Sponsored listings are the core order driver because they sit where a customer is already choosing. Promos are ready-made offers that give a customer a reason to press buy. Banners are for brand recognition and repeat exposure.

Wolt recommends pairing sponsored listings with promos for what it calls full-funnel results, and it describes that combination as fully pay-per-order ([Wolt Luxembourg merchant page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/lux/wolt-ads/restaurants-and-stores)). For a small budget, start with one sponsored listing and one promo. Add banners only when you have order data showing which items and times convert.

Wolt also lists off-app media across platforms such as Meta, TikTok and Google, and offline activity such as branded vehicles and sampling ([Wolt Azerbaijan page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/aze/wolt-ads)). Those formats are aimed at larger brands. A single restaurant should not buy them before the in-app basics work.

## How do you launch a Wolt campaign step by step?

The Wolt Luxembourg merchant page gives two launch figures in different sections. One says you can go live with "just 5 clicks", and another says to "launch your ad in 6 clicks" ([Wolt Luxembourg merchant page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/lux/wolt-ads/restaurants-and-stores)). Either way, the setup is short. The work is in preparing what the customer sees.

1. Confirm that Wolt operates in your city and that you have an active merchant account.
2. Open the Merchant Portal and choose the format: sponsored listing first, then a promo.
3. Set a daily or total budget you can afford to lose during a test period.
4. Check your listing photos, opening hours and menu before launch, since the ad sends customers to that page.
5. Pair the sponsored listing with one promo that has a clear reason to order now.
6. Review real-time reporting in the portal and compare completed orders against spend ([Wolt Luxembourg merchant page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/lux/wolt-ads/restaurants-and-stores)).
7. Decide on continuation only after you have enough orders to judge the cost per order.

Automation can cut setup time for large operators. A Smartly case study on Wolt reports that automated creative and campaign management saved the brand 10 hours per week ([Smartly case study](https://www.smartly.io/resources/wolt)). That is a secondary source describing a large, multi-market setup, so do not expect the same saving at one location.

## Should you fix your listing before spending on ads?

Yes. A sponsored placement sends a customer to your listing, so the ad is only as good as the page it leads to. If the photos are dated, the menu is out of date or the hours are wrong, the customer leaves before ordering and the spend is wasted.

Check the listing as a first-time customer would. Confirm the top items, the price points and the delivery time. Then read the most recent reviews and reply to any that mention a fixable problem. The sources here do not give a rating threshold, so set your own standard and keep the listing consistent for the whole test period.

Use the same care with promos. A promo should be easy to understand in one line. If the offer needs explanation, it will not carry a sponsored placement.

## FAQ

### Does Wolt charge for clicks on sponsored listings?

Wolt's merchant page says sponsored listings are pay-per-order, and that you only pay for completed orders, not clicks ([Wolt Luxembourg merchant page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/lux/wolt-ads/restaurants-and-stores)). Check your own account terms before launch, since terms can change by market.

### How quickly can I see results?

Wolt's sources say you can track results in real time in the Merchant Portal ([Wolt Luxembourg merchant page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/lux/wolt-ads/restaurants-and-stores)). They do not give a typical time to meaningful results. Judge a campaign only after you have enough completed orders to compare against spend.

### Should I start with banners or sponsored listings?

Start with sponsored listings, because they are the format tied to order decisions and are paid per completed order. Add promos to test a specific offer. Banners are described by Wolt as a way to build recognition over repeat exposure, which suits a restaurant with an established customer base ([Wolt Luxembourg merchant page](https://merchant.wolt.com/en/lux/wolt-ads/restaurants-and-stores)).

### Do I need a large team to run Wolt ads?

Wolt's pages describe a launch in 5 or 6 clicks, so setup itself is light. The sources do not say how much ongoing staff time a single restaurant needs. Plan for regular checks of your listing, promo and order numbers, and assign one person to own that review.
