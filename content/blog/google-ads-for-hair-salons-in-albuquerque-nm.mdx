## How many people search for a hair salon near them?

Google Ads keyword data captured in April 2026 estimated about 1.5 million U.S. searches per month for "hair salon near me" and about 110,000 for "hair stylist near me", as reported by the salon product brand RŌZ in its advertising guide. These are national U.S. figures, not Albuquerque numbers. Use them to confirm that the category has demand, not to forecast local clicks.

These searches come from people who are already looking to book, which is the audience a local salon wants. RŌZ, quoting Google, says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. For a paid campaign, relevance comes from your keywords and ad text, distance from the radius you choose, and prominence from your profile and reviews.

RŌZ's comparison table lists a Google Business Profile and Google reviews as free. Set those up before you spend on clicks, because a paid ad that sends a searcher to an empty or unreviewed profile wastes its budget.

## Which keywords should an Albuquerque salon bid on?

Start with searches that name the service, the stylist or the place. Trafft's salon advertising guide gives "spa near me", "hair salon near me" and "nail salon in [city]" as examples of how people search, which is the pattern to copy with Albuquerque in place of the city.

| Theme | Example phrase | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Near me | "hair salon near me" | Strong local intent, high national volume |
| Stylist near me | "hair stylist near me" | Matches a stylist-led search |
| Service and city | "hair salon Albuquerque" | Clear location match |
| Specialty and city | "balayage Albuquerque" | Specific service, planned visit |
| Neighborhood | "hair salon Nob Hill" | Narrow, so test before scaling |

Use the [best Google Ad keywords for hair salons](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026) as a starting set, then check volumes and costs for Albuquerque in Keyword Planner. Add negative keywords for job seekers, courses and do-it-yourself searches so the budget goes to people who might book. For a comparable city breakdown, the [Phoenix salon guide](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-in-phoenix-az) shows how the same keyword groups can be organised by market.

## How wide should the ad radius be?

A 2025 salon guide from CIIM recommends a local targeting radius of about 5 to 10 km around the salon, which is roughly 3 to 6 miles. That is a starting range from a single guide, not a rule for Albuquerque. Salons in dense central areas and salons on the edge of town will see different travel patterns.

Set the radius from your own booking records. Look at where recent clients live, then draw the radius to cover most of them. If the search terms report shows clicks from places your clients never travel to, narrow the radius. If clients from just outside the radius book regularly, widen it slightly and check again after a few weeks.

## Where should the ads send clicks?

A click is only useful if it reaches a page that makes booking easy. Sunny Storm, a Google Ads agency writing about salons, says that if a salon gets 100 visitors and no bookings, there is probably a problem with the landing page. The [common Google Ads mistakes for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-mistakes-hair-salons-make-and-how-to-fix-them) guide covers the usual causes, from slow pages to missing prices.

Booksy's salon marketing guide says Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search or Maps in under 30 seconds. If your booking software supports it, connect it so the path from search to booked is short. Keep the offer on the landing page matching the ad. Trafft's examples include a new-client offer, and Zeely gives "$20 off first color service" as an example of a new-client offer promoted each month.

## What does a month of ads cost? A worked example

The inputs below are assumptions, not benchmarks. Replace them with your own prices, click costs and rebooking rate.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | Assumption | $600 |
| Average cost per click | Assumption | $4.00 |
| Clicks | $600 / $4.00 | 150 |
| Calls or booking requests | 150 x 8% (assumption) | 12 |
| Requests that book and attend | 12 x 50% (assumption) | 6 |
| Cost per new client | $600 / 6 | $100 |
| First-visit spend | Assumption | $85 |
| Annual spend per client | 4 visits x $85 (assumption) | $340 |

At these inputs, a first visit ($85) does not cover the $100 cost per new client, but four visits ($340) do. The $340 is revenue, not profit, so subtract product and labour costs before deciding. If your rebooking rate is lower than four visits a year, the campaign needs a higher booking rate or a lower cost per click to pay back within the year.

## What should an Albuquerque salon track?

Set up conversion tracking for calls and booking requests before the campaign goes live. The [Google Analytics guide for hair salons](/blog/google-analytics-for-hair-salons) explains how to connect those actions to your reports, so you can see which keywords and areas produce bookings rather than just clicks.

When you hire help, ask for the full cost breakdown. ARC, which reports salon booking costs from one New York salon, notes that its figures come from one salon in one market and that yours will differ. Treat any agency figure the same way and test it against your own bookings.

## FAQ

### How much does Google Ads cost for an Albuquerque hair salon?

No Albuquerque-specific salon price is published in this guide. Set the budget from the worked example and your own cost per call or booking request.

### How wide should the targeting radius be?

A 2025 salon guide recommends about 5 to 10 km, roughly 3 to 6 miles. Adjust the radius using where your recent clients live and which areas produce bookings.

### Are ads better than a Google Business Profile?

They work together. RŌZ lists a Google Business Profile as free, so set up and review your profile first, then use ads to reach searchers who compare options.

### How long before I know if the campaign works?

No standard timeline applies to every salon. Compare bookings against cost over several weeks, and change one thing at a time so you can see what caused the result.
