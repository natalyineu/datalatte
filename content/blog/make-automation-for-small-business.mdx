Make's free plan gives you 1,000 credits a month. A "new lead to spreadsheet, text me, email them" scenario uses about 4 credits per lead if it starts from a webhook, so 100 leads cost around 400 credits. But set the same scenario to check a spreadsheet every 15 minutes and it burns roughly 2,880 credits a month doing nothing but looking. The tool is cheap. The trigger you choose decides whether it stays cheap.

This guide is for local owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada who want to know what Make (formerly Integromat) does, what it costs on the pricing page today, and which five automations are worth an afternoon. Prices below are in US dollars as shown on Make's pricing page; check it for your currency and any tax.

## What You Are Actually Paying For

Make bills in credits. According to Make's [credits help page](https://help.make.com/credits), for non-AI apps one operation equals one credit, and some AI modules use more. Its [operations help page](https://help.make.com/operations) defines an operation as one module run, with non-trigger modules running once per bundle (item) they receive, and a trigger that checks for data counting as one operation per check.

Here is the plan table from [Make's pricing page](https://www.make.com/en/pricing) at the time of writing:

| Plan | Price per month | Credits | Shortest schedule | Active scenarios | Log history |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | up to 1,000 | 15 minutes | 2 | 7 days |
| Core | $12 | 10,000 | 1 minute | Unlimited | 30 days |
| Pro | $21 | 10,000 | 1 minute | Unlimited | 30 days |
| Teams | $38 | 10,000 | 1 minute | Unlimited | 30 days |

The page lists 10,000 credits as the base volume for the paid plans and shows larger tiers on a slider without prices. It says annual billing saves "15% or more" but gives no per-plan annual price. Core adds unlimited active scenarios, minute-level scheduling and Make API access; priority execution appears under Pro. For a single-location business, Free or Core is almost always the right place to start.

<Callout type="coffee" title="The Bundle Trap">
Make's help page gives an example: a trigger returns 10 form responses, followed by three modules. That is 1 operation for the trigger plus 10 for each of the other three, so 31 operations in one run. Anything that loops over a list multiplies your usage. Filter early, and process only what you need.
</Callout>

## Five Automations Worth Building

Each of these is a common pattern, not a promise of results. They work with whatever form, booking and email tools you already use.

### 1. New enquiry: save it, alert you, thank them

Trigger: a webhook from your website form (Make's Custom webhook module gives you a URL to paste into the form tool). Steps: add a row to Google Sheets, send yourself an email or Slack message, send the lead an acknowledgement ("We got your message and will reply within one working day"). That is 4 operations per lead.

Why it matters: people who enquire usually contact several businesses. Replying in minutes is a habit you can build, and the alert is what builds it.

### 2. Booking confirmation and reminder

Trigger: a new booking in Calendly, Acuity, Square Appointments or Google Calendar. Steps: send a confirmation, schedule a reminder, send a "how was your visit?" message with your review link. For the reasoning behind reminders, see our guide to [reducing no-shows](/blog/reduce-no-shows-appointment-business).

### 3. Review alert with a drafted reply

Trigger: a new Google review arriving via a review tool or connector you already use. Steps: post it to Slack or email, optionally send the text to an AI module to draft a reply, save the draft for you to edit. A human sends the reply. Never auto-publish AI replies to a complaint.

### 4. Weekly numbers email

Trigger: a schedule, Monday 8 a.m. Steps: pull last week's figures from Google Analytics, Google Ads or Meta Ads into a sheet, then email yourself a summary. This one runs about once a week, so it uses almost no credits, but it saves you logging into three dashboards.

### 5. New customer to email list (with consent)

Trigger: a new customer in your booking or POS system. Steps: add them to Mailchimp, Brevo or similar with a tag for the service they bought, only where they agreed to marketing, and send a welcome email. In the UK, EU, Canada and Australia that means consent; in the US, CAN-SPAM and, for texts, TCPA rules apply. Ask a professional if unsure.

## Build the First One: Lead Alert in 8 Steps

1. Create a Make account and a new scenario.
2. Add a **Webhooks > Custom webhook** module and copy its URL.
3. Paste that URL into your website form's webhook or integration setting.
4. Submit one test form, then click **Run once** in Make so it learns the field names.
5. Add **Google Sheets > Add a row** and map name, email, phone, message and a timestamp.
6. Add an email or Slack module that alerts you with the name and message.
7. Add a second email module to the lead with the acknowledgement text.
8. Switch the scenario on, submit another real test, and check the run history the next day.

Budget two or three hours the first time. Name each module clearly ("Lead to Sheet", "Alert me") so you can read the history later.

## Habits That Prevent Silent Failures

- **Add error notifications.** A scenario that stops quietly means lost leads. Route errors to an email.
- **Use instant triggers where you can.** Polling triggers check on a schedule, and Make's help page counts each check as one operation.
- **Duplicate before editing.** Test changes on a copy of a live scenario.
- **Send only needed fields.** Fewer fields means less personal data moved between tools.
- **Write down what each scenario does.** Three lines in a doc, with the owner's name.

## The Math

All volumes below are **assumptions** for a small business. The plan prices and credit rules come from Make's pages above. The polling figure is an inference: Make's help page counts a trigger check as one operation, and the table applies that to every check, including ones that find nothing. Make's page does not address the empty-check case or webhook triggers explicitly, so confirm on your own scenario's history.

| Scenario | Trigger | Runs per month (assumption) | Operations per run | Credits per month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lead alert | Webhook | 100 leads | 4 (assumed 1 for trigger) | 400 |
| Lead alert, polling a sheet every 15 min | Schedule | 2,880 checks (96 a day x 30) | 1 per check, plus 3 per new lead | about 3,180 |
| Booking reminders | Webhook | 200 bookings | 3 | 600 |
| Weekly report | Schedule | 4 | 6 | 24 |
| Review alert | Webhook | 20 reviews | 3 | 60 |

Add up the webhook-based set (lead alert, reminders, weekly report, review alert): 400 + 600 + 24 + 60 = 1,084 credits. That is just over the free allowance, so you would either trim a scenario or move to Core at $12 for 10,000 credits. The polling version of the lead alert alone would exceed the free tier by itself.

What does $12 a month buy? If you value the time at, say, $30 an hour (assumption) and the automations save you 2 hours a month of copying leads and sending reminders by hand, that is $60 of time for $12. If they save 20 minutes, it is break-even. The honest test is whether you will still be using it in three months.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Make the same as Integromat?**
A: Yes. Make is the renamed Integromat. The platform is the same idea: visual scenarios made of modules.

**Q: Is Make free?**
A: There is a Free plan at $0 with up to 1,000 credits a month, 2 active scenarios and a 15-minute minimum interval, per Make's pricing page.

**Q: Do I need to code?**
A: No for most small-business scenarios, which are drag-and-drop. Knowing what a field and a webhook are helps.

**Q: Which automation first?**
A: The lead alert. It is short, useful the same day, and teaches you triggers and field mapping.

**Q: Why did I run out of credits so fast?**
A: Usually a polling trigger checking too often, or a loop over many items. Each module runs once per item, so lists multiply usage.
