Make (formerly Integromat) is a visual automation platform that connects the apps your small business already uses, so that tasks such as saving new leads to a spreadsheet, sending booking follow-ups or alerting you to new reviews happen on their own. It is a good fit if you want more flexibility than simple one-step tools offer and you are willing to spend a few hours learning how it works.

This guide explains how Make works, shows practical automations for local businesses, covers pricing in broad terms and lists the mistakes to avoid.

## How Make works in plain English

Make uses **scenarios**. A scenario is a flowchart you build on a canvas:

- **Trigger:** the event that starts it, such as "new form submission" or "new row in Google Sheets".
- **Modules:** steps that do something, such as "create a contact", "send an email" or "add a calendar event".
- **Routers and filters:** logic that sends data down different paths ("if the enquiry is about weddings, do this").
- **Data mapping:** you drag values, like a name or email, from one step into the next.

Scenarios can run on a schedule (for example every 15 minutes), on instant webhooks or when something changes in a connected app. Make shows each run's history, which makes it easier to see what went wrong.

## Make vs Zapier, in short

Both tools connect apps. Make tends to give you more control over data and branching at a lower cost per operation, while Zapier is usually quicker to start with and has a very large app library. ## Pricing in broad terms

Make's pricing is based on **operations** (each module run counts) rather than on the number of automations. As of this writing, there is a free tier with a limited number of operations, and paid plans that start at roughly the price of a few coffees per month and rise with volume. Features such as shorter run intervals, more active scenarios and extended logs differ by plan. Prices and limits change, so check Make's pricing page and estimate your usage first.

How to estimate: count how many times a scenario will run per month and how many modules it has. A scenario with five modules that runs 200 times uses about 1,000 operations.

## Six automations that suit local businesses

### 1. New enquiry to CRM and instant alert

Trigger: a contact form, Facebook lead ad or Instagram DM tool submits a new lead.
Steps: add the lead to a Google Sheet or CRM such as HubSpot (free tier), send yourself a text or Slack message, and email the lead a confirmation.
Why it helps: speed matters with enquiries. Responding in minutes rather than hours generally improves your chances of winning the booking.

### 2. Booking confirmation and reminder flow

Trigger: a new booking in Calendly, Acuity, Square Appointments or Google Calendar.
Steps: send a confirmation, schedule a reminder email or SMS (through Twilio, for example) and add a "how was your visit?" message after the appointment.
Why it helps: reminders reduce no-shows, and follow-ups encourage rebooking and reviews. See [reducing no-shows](/blog/reduce-no-shows-appointment-business).

### 3. Review alert and reply drafting

Trigger: a new Google review (through a connector or review-management tool).
Steps: post the review into Slack or email, optionally send it to an AI model to draft a reply, and save the draft for a human to edit and publish.
Why it helps: you never miss a review. Keep a human in the loop for sending replies.

### 4. Weekly marketing report

Trigger: a schedule, such as Monday morning.
Steps: pull numbers from Google Analytics, Google Ads or Meta Ads, add them to a Google Sheet and send yourself a summary email.
Why it helps: you check performance consistently without logging into several dashboards.

### 5. Social content pipeline

Trigger: a new row in a content-calendar sheet marked "approved".
Steps: post to Facebook Pages or Instagram through supported connectors, or send to a scheduler such as Buffer, then mark the row as published.
Why it helps: planning stays in one place, and publishing is consistent. Check each platform's current API rules, since they change.

### 6. Email list growth

Trigger: a new customer in your booking or POS system.
Steps: add them to Mailchimp, Brevo or Klaviyo with tags such as service type, only where you have consent, and send a welcome email.
Why it helps: your list builds itself from real customers.

## Step-by-step: build your first scenario

We suggest starting with the lead-alert automation, because it is simple and useful.

1. **Create an account** and a new scenario.
2. **Choose the trigger.** If your form tool has a Make connector, select it. Otherwise use a **Custom webhook** and paste the webhook URL into your form's settings.
3. **Run once** to capture a sample submission so Make can see the fields.
4. **Add a Google Sheets module** and map name, email, phone and message to columns.
5. **Add an email or Slack module** to alert you, mapping the name and message into the text.
6. **Add a second email module** to send the lead an acknowledgement. Keep it honest: "We received your message and will reply within one working day."
7. **Test** with a real submission and check every step.
8. **Switch on scheduling** and let it run.
9. **Check the history** after a few days to confirm there are no errors.

Plan on a couple of hours the first time, less for later scenarios.

## Good habits that save you pain

- **Name everything.** Give scenarios and modules clear names such as "Lead form to Sheet and Slack".
- **Add error handling.** Use Make's error-handler routes or notifications so that a failed run does not go unnoticed.
- **Watch operations.** Looping through large lists can burn your allowance quickly.
- **Limit data.** Send only the fields you need, especially with personal data.
- **Document it.** Keep a short note of what each scenario does and who owns it.
- **Test changes on a copy.** Duplicate a scenario before you edit one that is live.

## Privacy and consent

If you automate emails or SMS, you must have a lawful basis. That generally means consent in the UK, EU, Canada and Australia, and compliance with CAN-SPAM, TCPA and carrier rules in the US. Include unsubscribe options, and avoid moving customer data into tools you have not vetted. If in doubt, ask a professional.

## When Make is not the right tool

- Your needs are one simple connection: native integrations or Zapier might be quicker.
- You need heavy custom logic or very high volume: consider a developer-built solution.
- Nobody on your team has time to maintain it: broken automations can silently fail, so consider a managed service.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Make the same as Integromat?**
A: Yes. Integromat was renamed Make, and the platform has since added features, though the core idea is the same.

**Q: Is Make free?**
A: There is a free tier with limited operations, suitable for testing and light use. Heavier use needs a paid plan.

**Q: Do I need to know how to code?**
A: No. Most small-business scenarios use drag-and-drop modules. A basic understanding of data fields and webhooks helps, and some advanced steps use simple formulas.

**Q: Which automation should I build first?**
A: A new-lead alert that saves the enquiry and notifies you. It is simple, valuable and a good way to learn.
