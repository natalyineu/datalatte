Yes, a small business can technically buy streaming TV ads that run on Prime Video, but for most single-location shops the spend is hard to justify. The practical test is simple: divide your monthly budget by your average customer value, then ask whether a streaming audience can deliver that many customers at a believable cost. Amazon's published minimums and recommended budgets are the first thing to confirm, since this guide could not verify them from the sources it checked. A worked example below uses a $10,000 test budget, with every input labelled as an assumption.

This guide is for owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada who have heard that TV ads are now open to them through Prime Video and want to know what that means for a café, salon, gym or home service company. Dollar figures are US dollars. Confirm local availability and pricing with the seller in your country.

## Can a small business actually buy Prime Video ads?

Prime Video inventory is sold through Amazon's advertising products, including its demand-side platform (DSP). Amazon has described both self-service and managed-service routes, and a third-party DSP or agency can also buy streaming inventory. The entry points differ in minimum spend, fees and how much help you get setting up creative and measurement.

The catch for a small business is the minimum. A managed service usually comes with a larger commitment than a self-serve buy, and a self-serve buy still needs a finished video spot, tracking and enough budget to reach people more than once. Check the current minimums on [Amazon's streaming TV ads page](https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/streaming-tv-ads) and get them in writing for your country.

Fees on top of media are the second thing to ask about. A low media minimum can still produce a total cost that is higher than the headline number suggests.

## How much does a Prime Video test need to break even?

Break-even is the number that decides the test. With a $10,000 budget and an assumed average first-year customer value of $120, you need 84 new customers to cover the spend on revenue alone ($10,000 / $120 = 83.3, rounded up).

Converting that into households depends on the CPM and frequency you are quoted. The example below assumes a $40 CPM and an average frequency of 4, so the $10,000 buys 250,000 impressions and reaches about 62,500 households.

| Assumption-based scenario | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Impressions ($10,000 / $40 CPM x 1,000) | 250,000 | 250,000 | 250,000 |
| Households reached (impressions / frequency 4) | 62,500 | 62,500 | 62,500 |
| Share of households that become customers (assumption) | 0.05% | 0.13% | 0.30% |
| New customers | 31 | 81 | 188 |
| Cost per customer | $323 | $123 | $53 |
| Revenue at $120 each | $3,720 | $9,720 | $22,560 |

The base case sits just under break-even. To cover $10,000 at $120 each you need about 0.134% of households to buy, or roughly 1 in 744. If your honest estimate is far lower, the test will lose money on direct response and can only be justified as awareness spending.

Compare that with a search budget using the same $10,000. Using the all-industry average search CPC of $2.69 from the benchmark below and an assumed 4% conversion rate, the same money buys about 3,717 clicks and roughly 149 customers at about $67 each. The search figures are assumptions too, so replace them with your own account data before deciding.

## Is there any local targeting on Prime Video?

Local targeting is the feature most small businesses care about, and it is the one this guide could not confirm. Ask any seller whether Prime Video delivery can be limited to your state, metro or ZIP codes, and at what budget. Do not assume a neighbourhood radius, because a streaming buy is usually planned around audiences and regions rather than a shop's trading area.

If a seller offers location-specific versions of one commercial, get the terms, creative requirements and minimums in writing before you commit budget.

Multi-location businesses should also ask about reporting by location. Without location-level data, a regional buy can look successful even when individual branches see no change.

## Who should test it, and who should skip it?

A Prime Video test is more reasonable for some businesses than others. The pattern is consistent: the case is strongest where the audience matches the TV-watching habits of your customers and where the budget can absorb a test without hurting the core marketing plan.

- Product sellers who already sell on Amazon and can see sales move on the marketplace itself.
- Multi-location businesses or franchisees with a budget of $10,000 or more and a finished video spot.
- Brands whose products sell well outside a local service area.

Skip it if:

- You run a single-location service business with a monthly budget under about $2,000, where the response needed to break even is hard to believe.
- You have no way to track results, such as a promo code, QR code or dedicated URL.
- Your customers come from search, referrals or local listings, and you cannot yet show that streaming viewers are your buyers.

Home service companies that need lead tracking should first sort out call and job logging. A tool like [Jobber or ServiceTitan](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business) can show which channel brought in each booked job, which makes any later TV test measurable.

## What should you do this week?

Start with the cheapest tests and the measurement, not the media buy. This sequence costs little and tells you whether a streaming test is worth pricing at all.

1. Write your break-even number: monthly budget divided by average customer value, then convert it to households using the CPM and frequency from your quote.
2. Ask your first 20 customers whether they watch Prime Video and whether they would notice an ad. If most say no, the test is probably not worth the budget.
3. Confirm you have a 15 or 30-second video spot. If not, price the production before pricing media.
4. Email the seller four questions: current minimums in your country, all fees on top of media, whether delivery can be limited geographically at your budget, and what reporting you will receive.
5. Set up tracking first with a promo code, a QR code and a dedicated URL in Google Analytics.
6. Price a search test for the same weeks so you have a like-for-like comparison. A [Google Ads campaign](/services/google-ads) is the usual benchmark for local direct response.

## FAQ

### Is there a minimum spend for Prime Video ads?

Minimums are published on Amazon's streaming TV ads page, but this guide could not confirm them from its sources. Terms vary by country and change over time, so get the current minimum for your country in writing before budgeting.

### Can I target only people near my business?

Not confirmed here. Ask the seller whether delivery can be limited to your state, metro or ZIP codes, and what budget that requires. Do not assume a radius around your shop.

### Do I need to sell on Amazon to advertise on Prime Video?

Not necessarily. Advertising access depends on the buying route, so confirm eligibility with the seller. Amazon sellers can see extra value because they can track sales on the marketplace.

### Is Prime Video better than Google or Meta for a local business?

For direct response from a local area, search and social usually offer more precise targeting at lower cost. Prime Video works best as a reach or awareness tool, and it needs measurement before you commit. The [Google Ads service](/services/google-ads) is a common starting point for local direct response.

### How long should a test run?

Long enough to reach your planned frequency across the audience and to see whether branded search, calls or sales respond. Decide in advance what number counts as success.
