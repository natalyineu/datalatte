The best Google Ads keywords for a florist are urgent, local, buy-now searches: same-day delivery, "florist near me", funeral and sympathy flowers, and wedding florist enquiries. Start them on phrase or exact match, add negatives for jobs, DIY and wholesale, and check the Search terms report weekly. Google does not publish a florist-specific cost per click, so this page gives no CPC. Pull your own estimate from Keyword Planner.

## Which keyword themes work best for florists?

Group keywords by what the searcher needs and how soon. Urgent and occasion-based themes usually justify the highest bids because the order is close. The table lists themes and sample searches. The sample phrases are examples to test, not keywords with proven volume.

| Theme | Example searches | Buyer need | Start with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same-day delivery | same day flower delivery, flowers delivered today | Urgent order | Exact and phrase |
| Near me | florist near me, flower shop open now | Local, ready to buy | Exact and phrase |
| Sympathy and funeral | funeral flowers delivery, sympathy flowers | Time-bound, higher order value | Phrase |
| Wedding | wedding florist, wedding bouquet | Enquiry, longer sales cycle | Phrase |
| Occasion | birthday flowers delivery, anniversary flowers | Planned gift | Phrase |
| Holiday | Mother's Day flowers, Valentine's Day flowers | Seasonal peak | Phrase, short flights |
| Brand and shop name | your shop name | Already knows you | Exact |
| Subscription | flower subscription, weekly flowers | Repeat revenue | Phrase |

Keep each theme in its own ad group so the ad can repeat the searcher's words. A funeral ad and a Valentine's ad should never share a group.

## Why do "near me" and delivery keywords matter?

Searchers who type a location or an urgent need are usually closer to buying than those who type a single word such as "flowers". That is a reasonable working assumption, not a published conversion rate, so test it in your own account. Google gives no florist conversion benchmark on its help pages.

Use the location settings to enforce "local". Google's help says location targeting can cover areas within a country or a radius around a location, and it warns that very small targets may show your ads only intermittently or not at all ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10835274?hl=en)). Pick a radius that matches your real delivery zone, then compare it with what the account reports.

## Which match type should a florist start with?

Start with phrase and exact match on your highest-intent themes. Google defines broad match as the default match type, "the most comprehensive", and says exact match "gives you the most steering over who views your ad" but reaches fewer searches ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529)).

A new keyword with no match type is treated as broad. Add quotation marks for phrase match and square brackets for exact match yourself. Move a keyword toward broad only once the Search terms report shows that the matches it brings in are relevant.

<Callout type="tip">Check the Search terms report once a week during the first month. It shows what people actually typed, and it is where most new negative keywords come from.</Callout>

## What negative keywords should every florist add?

Negative keywords stop your ads showing on searches you do not want. Google also warns that too many can reduce your reach ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972)). A starter list for a florist:

- Training and work: jobs, hiring, salary, course, school, certification
- DIY and supplies: diy, how to, tutorial, wholesale, bulk, supplier
- Wrong product: artificial, silk, plastic, seeds, plants if you do not sell them
- Wrong place: cities and states outside your delivery area

Negative keywords have two quirks. Google says they do not match close variants, so you must add singular, plural and misspelled forms yourself. Their match types also differ: with broad match negatives, the ad is blocked only when the search contains all the negative terms, in any order. ## When should a florist raise bids?

Raise budgets before the dates that matter, not during them. The NRF's 2026 Mother's Day survey of 7,877 US adults found that 75% of shoppers planned to buy flowers, with $3.2 billion of planned flower spending ([NRF](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/mother-s-day-spending-expected-to-hit-record-38-billion)). For Valentine's Day 2025, the NRF reported 40% of shoppers planned flowers and $2.9 billion in spending ([NRF](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/nrf-survey-valentine-s-day-spending-reaches-record-27-5-billion)).

These are national survey figures of planned purchases, not your local demand. Use them as a reminder to start holiday campaigns early, set a daily cap, and compare your own week-over-week clicks. ## What does a simple budget look like? (worked example)

Every number below is an **assumption** for illustration. It is not a benchmark.

- Assumption: daily budget of $20 for a delivery campaign
- Assumption: average cost per click of $2.00
- Assumption: 3% of clicks become orders
- Assumption: average order of $75

At $20 a day, the campaign buys about 10 clicks. Three percent of 10 clicks is 0.3 orders a day, or about 9 orders in a 30-day month. Spend is $600 and revenue is $675. Before the cost of flowers and delivery, that is barely break-even. If the real cost per click is $3, the same budget buys about 7 clicks and loses money.

The lesson is the formula: clicks = budget divided by CPC; orders = clicks times conversion rate. Replace each assumption with your own account numbers after two to four weeks. A [$10-a-day budget](/blog/is-10-a-day-enough-for-google-ads-complete-guide-for-2026) gives even less data, so keep the keyword list short.

## What should you check in Keyword Planner?

Use Keyword Planner for estimates, with the caveat Google gives itself: campaign performance depends on many factors, and keywords with very low search volumes may not be forecastable ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243)). Treat any volume or bid it shows as a range to test, then let your own conversion data decide.

## FAQ

### What are the best Google Ads keywords for florists?

Same-day delivery, "florist near me", funeral and sympathy flowers, wedding florist, and your own shop name. Start on phrase or exact match and review the Search terms report weekly.

### How much does a click cost for florist keywords?

No official florist-specific figure is published by Google. Keyword Planner shows an estimate for your location, and your own account shows the real cost after a few weeks.

### Should a florist use broad match?

Broad match is Google's default and reaches the most searches, but gives you the least control. Use phrase and exact match first, then widen only if the matched searches are relevant.

### What negative keywords should a florist use?

Jobs, courses, DIY, wholesale, supplier, artificial and silk flowers, and cities you do not serve. Add singular and plural forms yourself, because negatives do not match close variants.

### When should a florist run holiday campaigns?

Before the peak. The NRF found that 75% of Mother's Day shoppers planned to buy flowers in 2026, so start the campaign early and cap daily spend.
