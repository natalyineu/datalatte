Here is the number that should shape your Instagram plan. If you spend five hours a week on it and $150 a month on boosts, and each cover leaves you about $12 after food and direct costs (both my assumptions), Instagram has to bring in roughly 48 covers a month, or about 19 bookings, just to break even. Most restaurant owners never work that out. They post, count likes and hope.

This guide is for independent restaurants, cafes and bars in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers how to set up the profile, what to post, how to track bookings, and the math. One honest caveat first: Instagram is rarely where a diner makes the final decision.

## Where Instagram Fits in the Diner's Decision

BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (1,002 US adults, run through SurveyMonkey) found that 54% of consumers visit a business's website after reading positive reviews, and 24% visit its social media channels. So Instagram is often the second or third checkpoint: someone finds you on Google Maps, sees good reviews, then taps through to check whether the food looks good and the room looks right. BrightLocal sells review software, and this is a US panel, so read it as a direction, not a forecast.

Reach is real, though. Pew Research Center's social media fact sheet (survey fielded February to June 2025) says 50% of US adults use Instagram, rising to 80% of 18 to 29 year olds and 62% of 30 to 49 year olds. If your guests are in those age bands, they are on the app.

What follows from this: your Instagram has to work for two audiences. One is the stranger who finds a Reel. The other, probably bigger, is the person who already found you elsewhere and is checking you out.

## Fix the Profile Before You Post Anything

Your profile is the landing page for every Reel and tag. Work through this list:

- **Switch to a professional account.** It unlocks insights, contact options and ad tools. It is free.
- **Name field with a search phrase.** "Casa Verde | Italian Restaurant Leeds" beats "Casa Verde". Your name and bio text are what Instagram search can read.
- **Bio in one line.** Cuisine, area, and one reason to come ("wood-fired pizza, open till 11, dogs welcome").
- **One link.** Point it at your booking page, or your ordering page if takeaway is your main business. If you use a link-in-bio page, keep it to three or four buttons.
- **Action button.** Instagram offers Reserve and Order buttons through partner services (reservation partners named in third-party coverage include OpenTable, Resy and Tock; ordering partners include Grubhub and ChowNow). Instagram's own Help Center was not readable by our tools, so check Edit profile, then Contact options, in your app to see what your country offers. Per that second-hand coverage, you need an existing partner account and only one button shows at a time.
- **Highlights.** Menu, Find Us, Parking, Events, Reviews, Private Dining. These answer the questions people DM you.
- **Match Google.** Name, address, opening hours and booking link should be identical to your Google Business Profile. People compare them. See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and the [Google profile guide for restaurants](/blog/google-my-business-for-restaurants).

## What to Post: Four Buckets, One Rhythm

| Bucket | Examples | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | A cheese pull, a pour, plating, the pass at 7pm | Create appetite |
| People | The baker at 5am, your longest-serving server | Build trust |
| Place | Patio at golden hour, the bar, the private room | Set expectations |
| Offers and news | New menu, event night, holiday hours | Trigger a booking |

A sustainable rhythm for a small team: two Reels, two feed posts or carousels, and Stories on service days. This is my suggestion, not an Instagram rule. A rhythm you can keep for a year beats a burst you abandon in three weeks.

Dash Social's food and beverage benchmark (2026 edition, covering activity from 1 July to 31 December 2025 on 3,363 Instagram handles with at least 1,000 followers) says views were up 44% while engagement rate fell from 3.3% to 2.5%, and that Reels outperformed static formats. The report gives a target range of 2.0% to 2.5%, but it also lists a 0.4% industry average without explaining the gap, and it does not define engagement rate on the page we could open. Brand accounts also differ from a single local restaurant. Use it to justify trying Reels, not to grade yourself.

## Phone Photography That Sells Food

- Shoot near a window, with the flash off.
- Wipe the plate rim before every shot.
- Shoot flat dishes (pizza, bowls) from above and tall ones (burgers, drinks) from a low angle.
- Show texture: a cut-open dish, steam, a drizzle.
- Edit lightly. Over-filtered food sets expectations the plate cannot meet. Google's own [photo guidelines for Business Profiles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862) make the same point: sharp, well lit, with no significant alterations or excessive filters, and accurately showing what the business looks like.

## Reels: The Cheapest Way to Reach Strangers

Reels are the format Instagram pushes to people who do not follow you yet. Good restaurant ideas:

- A 10 to 20 second plating or pour with natural sound
- The opening routine, from lights on to first order
- How the signature dish is made
- A walk through the dining room before service
- A first-bite reaction from a guest who agreed to be filmed

Show the most appetising frame in the first second, add captions (many people watch muted) and end with one action: "Book via the link in bio". Our [Instagram Reels for business guide](/blog/instagram-reels-for-business) covers editing and formats. Reel length limits have changed over time, with some sources reporting up to three minutes; check what your app allows, but a short clip is usually enough for food.

## Make Location Work for You

- Tag your location on every post, Reel and Story.
- Put the area and cuisine in the caption in plain words: "Sunday roast in Headingley".
- Use a few specific hashtags. Several sources quote Instagram's head Adam Mosseri as saying hashtags are not a reach tool and are mainly useful for search. A reported five-hashtag cap was, per Social Media Today's February 2024 update, called a glitch by Instagram, so do not build a strategy on either rule.
- Reply to tags and comments quickly. A reply is free social proof.

## Guests, Creators and Disclosure

Make it easy for guests to tag you: put your handle on menus, table cards and takeaway bags, and create one corner or dish worth photographing. Run any monthly "best tagged photo" prize under Instagram's current promotion rules for your country.

If you invite a local food creator for a free meal, disclosure matters. The US Federal Trade Commission's guide for influencers says a disclosure is needed when there is a financial, employment, personal or family relationship with a brand, that free or discounted products count, and that the disclosure should sit with the endorsement and be hard to miss (in a video, inside the video itself, not just the description). It suggests clear wording such as "ad" or "sponsored" and warns against vague tags like "collab". The FTC's page puts the responsibility on the influencer, but a restaurant that hosts creators should still brief them. UK, Australian and Canadian rules differ in detail, so check your local advertising regulator. Our [restaurant influencer guide](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-restaurants) has a brief template.

## Paid Promotion: Only After the Basics Work

Boosting a Reel that already performed organically is the lowest-risk way to start. Target a radius around your restaurant, run it for a few days around a specific event or quiet night, and judge it on bookings, not reach. Minimum boost budgets and radius options are set by Meta and change, so confirm them in the boost screen in your own app (one third-party tool says boosts start around $1 a day, which is second-hand). ## Track Bookings, Not Likes

1. **Tag your bio link.** Google Analytics' help page on manual tagging says to always use `utm_source`, `utm_medium` and `utm_campaign`. For example: `?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio_link`.
2. **Ask at booking.** Add "How did you hear about us?" with Instagram as an option, and ask walk-ins at the door for a month.
3. **Watch insights that predict action:** profile visits, link taps, saves and shares. Saves and shares usually say more than likes.
4. **Do a monthly review.** Which five posts drove link taps? Make more like those.

## The Math

All inputs below are assumptions for a neighbourhood restaurant. Replace them with your own numbers.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Staff time: 5 hrs/week x 4.3 weeks x $20/hr (assumption) | $430 | $430 | $430 |
| Boost budget (assumption) | $150 | $150 | $150 |
| Monthly cost | $580 | $580 | $580 |
| Bio-link taps per month (assumption) | 150 | 300 | 600 |
| Tap to booking rate (assumption) | 5% | 8% | 10% |
| Bookings | 7.5 | 24 | 60 |
| Covers per booking (assumption) | 2.5 | 2.5 | 2.5 |
| Extra covers | 19 | 60 | 150 |
| Contribution per cover after food and direct costs (assumption) | $12 | $12 | $12 |
| Contribution | $225 | $720 | $1,800 |
| Net after Instagram costs | -$355 | +$140 | +$1,220 |

Break-even is $580 divided by $12, about 48 covers, or roughly 19 bookings at 2.5 covers each. Two things move it fastest. Count only extra covers: if a regular would have come anyway, the booking is not Instagram's. And raise the tap-to-booking rate by fixing the booking page: if it loads slowly on a phone, no content fixes that.

## Common Mistakes

Posting only discounts, leaving DMs unanswered (people ask "do you have a table at 8?"), skipping the call to action on Reels, vanishing for weeks after a burst, and buying followers, who never book a table.

## Do This Today

1. Switch to a professional account, set your name field to "Name | Cuisine Area", and check the address, hours and link match your Google profile.
2. Open Edit profile, then Contact options, and see which Reserve or Order action buttons your country offers.
3. Add UTM parameters to your bio link and a "How did you hear about us?" field to your booking flow.
4. Film three 15-second Reels this week (plating, opening routine, signature dish) and schedule them across seven days.
5. Work out your own break-even: monthly Instagram cost divided by contribution per cover, then write the number on a sticky note by the till.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should a restaurant post on Instagram?**
A: There is no official figure. Two to four feed posts or Reels a week plus Stories on service days is a workable rhythm for most small teams, as long as you can sustain it for months.

**Q: Do I need a professional photographer?**
A: Not to start. Natural light, a clean plate and a modern phone are enough. A professional shoot for your hero dishes and dining room once or twice a year can still pay off.

**Q: Are hashtags worth using?**
A: Modestly. A few specific tags for cuisine and neighbourhood help categorise posts, but captions with plain place and cuisine words, a location tag and strong content matter more.

**Q: Instagram or Google Business Profile first?**
A: Google first if you must choose, because it is where people search for a table nearby. BrightLocal's 2026 survey also suggests diners often check social media only after reading reviews. Do both, and keep them consistent.

**Q: How do I turn followers into reservations?**
A: Make the next step obvious: one booking link, a link sticker on Stories, a call to action in every Reel and fast replies to table requests by DM.
