Programmatic display usually costs about $1 to $5 per 1,000 impressions (CPM) on open exchanges, $5 to $15 on private marketplaces, and up to $30 for guaranteed deals, according to one 2025 benchmark roundup. That is a secondary source, and platform fees come on top. The CPM is only the starting point: what you pay per lead depends on click rate and conversion rate.

This guide explains each price, what to check before you buy, and a worked example for a local business.

## What Does CPM Mean in Programmatic Advertising?

CPM is the price of 1,000 ad impressions. The cost of a campaign is impressions divided by 1,000, multiplied by the CPM. A $3 CPM and a $1,000 budget buy about 333,000 impressions.

An impression is not a visit. Nobody has to click, and some impressions may never be seen on screen, so a low CPM is not automatically a good deal. Compare cost per click and cost per lead as well.

## What Are Typical Programmatic CPMs in 2026?

No single official rate card exists, and the numbers below come from a secondary roundup. [OwlClaw's display benchmarks](https://owlclaw.com/benchmarks/display-ads-benchmarks/) list these ranges and attribute them to DoubleVerify and IAB 2025 data, which have not been independently confirmed.

| Inventory type | Reported CPM |
| --- | --- |
| Programmatic open exchange | $1 to $4 |
| Google Display Network | $2 to $5 |
| Private marketplace (PMP) | $5 to $15 |
| Programmatic guaranteed | $10 to $30 |

The same page gives different ranges in different sections, so treat these as rough. Cheaper open-exchange inventory can include lower-quality sites, which is why private marketplaces cost more.

## What Does Meta Advertising Cost Per 1,000 Impressions?

Meta is not programmatic in the exchange sense, but many owners compare it with display. [AdManage.ai](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-cpm-benchmarks-by-industry) reports a global median CPM of about $19.81 for January 2025 to January 2026, drawn from over $3 billion of Facebook spend. Its closest local categories were home services at about $9.84 and local real estate at about $5.13 (2025).

That is a vendor analysis, not Meta's own data. Your CPM will differ by city, audience and season.

## What Fees Come on Top of CPM?

Platform fees are the part owners most often miss. Google says its Display & Video 360 fee is [calculated as a percentage of media spend](https://support.google.com/displayvideo/answer/7622449?hl=en), with a higher rate for open auction and private auction deals and a lower one for YouTube, Demand Gen and programmatic guaranteed deals. The page publishes no figure, so ask for the rate in writing.

AdRoll's [pricing page](https://www.adroll.com/pricing) says it has no minimum spend and prices dynamically on CPM, but lists no rates. Agencies and managed services add their own fees. Always ask whether a quoted CPM includes all fees.

## What Does It Cost in Practice? A Worked Example

Everything below is an **assumption**: a $1,000 monthly budget at a $3 CPM, on a landing page that books appointments.

| Step | Strong result | Weak result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impressions | 333,333 | 333,333 |
| Click rate | 0.5% | 0.2% |
| Clicks | 1,667 | 667 |
| Cost per click | $0.60 | $1.50 |
| Landing page conversion | 2% | 1% |
| Leads | 33 | 6.7 |
| Cost per lead | $30 | $150 |

The same CPM produced a five times difference in cost per lead. For comparison, LocaliQ's [2026 search benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/) show an all-industry average of $5.42 per click, an 8.18% conversion rate and $66.69 per lead, so $1,000 would buy about 15 leads. Search clicks cost more, but the people clicking are already looking.

## How Much Should a Local Business Budget?

Start small and decide in advance what result counts as success. A reasonable test is one month, one audience around your service area, one offer and one landing page. Work out your break-even cost per lead before you spend.

Do not move past the test until you can track leads from the ads. If you cannot measure bookings, spend the money on search or your Business Profile first. The [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows how to split a budget across channels.

## When Is Programmatic Not Worth It?

Skip it for now if your budget is under a few hundred dollars a month, if you have no tracking, or if your customers search for you by name or service anyway. Programmatic works best for awareness and retargeting once search is already covered.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does programmatic advertising cost?**
A: Reported display CPMs are about $1 to $5 on open exchanges and $5 to $15 on private marketplaces, per a secondary 2025 roundup. Add platform and agency fees.

**Q: What is a good CPM?**
A: There is no universal answer. A low CPM can still produce expensive leads, so judge by cost per lead.

**Q: Is programmatic cheaper than Meta or Google Ads?**
A: Per impression, often yes. Per lead, it depends on click and conversion rates. In the example above, cost per lead ranged from $30 to $150.

**Q: Is there a minimum budget?**
A: AdRoll says it has no minimum spend. Others, including managed services, may set one, so confirm before you commit.

**Q: Does a CPM include fees?**
A: Not always. Google's Display & Video 360 fee is a percentage of media spend, so ask whether quoted prices include it.
