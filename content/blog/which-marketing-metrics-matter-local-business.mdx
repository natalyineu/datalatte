A local business should track the actions that lead to a sale: calls, bookings, direction requests and form submissions, plus the cost of each. LocaliQ's 2026 Google Ads benchmarks give a cost per lead of $30.57 for Restaurants and Food, $31.50 for Animals and Pets, $39.25 for Beauty and Personal Care and $67.36 for Health and Fitness. Likes, followers and impressions can sit in the background.

This guide lists the metrics worth checking weekly, where each comes from, how to turn them into a cost per customer and which numbers to ignore.

## Which Metrics Should You Check Every Week?

Start with four: calls, bookings or orders, direction requests and website actions. Google Business Profile reports calls (taps on the call button, which needs a phone number on the profile), directions, website clicks and, if you use a booking provider, completed bookings. Pick a date range at the top of the report.

Then add what you spend and what each result cost. Free sources come first because they cost nothing: your Business Profile, your booking software and your POS. Our [Google Business Profile analytics guide](/blog/google-my-business-analytics) shows where to find them.

## What Is a Conversion?

A conversion is an action that matters to your business. Google Ads says conversion tracking records actions such as purchases, sign-ups and phone calls, and lists website, app, phone call and offline types. Google Analytics 4 calls these key events: events that measure an action particularly important to the success of your business.

Decide what counts before you spend. For a salon it is a booking; for a café, an order or a visit with a code; for a groomer, a call or booking request. Everything else is secondary.

## What Does a Lead Cost in Your Industry?

LocaliQ's 2026 report, updated 1 June 2026, shows the all-industry average cost per lead on Google Ads as $66.69, with a conversion rate of 8.18%. The table lists the closest categories to common local businesses.

| Industry (LocaliQ name) | CPC | Conversion rate | Cost per lead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Restaurants and Food | $2.05 | 8.05% | $30.57 |
| Animals and Pets | $4.06 | 16.22% | $31.50 |
| Beauty and Personal Care | $4.62 | 10.35% | $39.25 |
| Health and Fitness | $6.17 | 6.94% | $67.36 |

These are lead costs, not cost per paying customer. A lead may be a call or a form, and not every lead buys.

## How Do You Work Out Cost per Customer?

Divide spend by new paying customers, not by clicks. If you spent $600 and won 8 new clients, cost per client is $75. Compare that with what a client is worth over a few visits, and set the ad budget with our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide). This takes one extra column in a spreadsheet: the number of leads that became customers.

ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend, and it only means something if the revenue is tracked. For local shops with walk-in sales, ask new customers how they found you and log the answer at the till.

## Which Metrics Can You Mostly Ignore?

Open rates, because Mailchimp warns Apple's Mail Privacy Protection can affect their accuracy. Use clicks and orders instead. Follower counts, likes and raw impressions do not tell you whether anyone bought. They can help you see whether content is reaching people, but they should not drive decisions.

For email, Klaviyo's 2026 data shows a Food and Beverage order rate of 1.16% for campaigns and 1.79% for flows. That is a more useful number than an open rate. See [coffee shop analytics and tracking](/blog/coffee-shop-analytics-and-tracking) for a setup walkthrough.

## The Math: What Is a Salon's Cost per New Client?

All inputs are **assumptions** for an illustrative salon, built on the LocaliQ Beauty and Personal Care figures.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend | $400 |
| Cost per lead (LocaliQ benchmark) | $39.25 |
| Leads | about 10 |
| Leads that book (assumption) | 60% |
| New clients | 6 |
| Cost per new client | about $67 |
| First-visit spend (assumption) | $85 |

Six new clients at $85 is $510 of first-visit revenue against $400 of spend. Keep the real conversion from lead to booking in your own sheet, because it is the number most likely to differ from the benchmark.

## FAQ

### How many metrics should I track?
Four to six. Calls, bookings, directions, website actions, spend and cost per customer cover most local businesses.

### What is a good cost per lead?
It depends on the industry. LocaliQ shows $30.57 to $67.36 across four local categories, against $66.69 for all industries. Compare against what a customer is worth to you.

### Do I need Google Analytics?
It helps if you have a website. Mark booking or order events as key events so you can count them.

### Are social media followers a metric that matters?
They show reach, not sales. Track what followers do, such as bookings from a link.
