A Danish small business should start with a complete Google Business Profile, run Danish-language Google Search ads in a tight local area, and use Facebook and Instagram for retargeting and local awareness. DataReportal's Digital 2026 report counts 5.95 million internet users in Denmark (99.0% of the population), so the audience is online. Google does not publish Danish click prices by industry, so budget from your own test data.

This guide is for cafes, salons, studios and similar businesses in Copenhagen, Aarhus, Odense and Aalborg. Every figure comes from a source listed at the end. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## Which Platforms Reach Danish Customers?

DataReportal's late-2025 ad reach figures are the best public guide. Ad reach is Meta's or the platform's estimate of people your ads can reach. It is not the same as monthly active users, and DataReportal warns against treating it as such.

| Platform | Ad reach in Denmark | Share of population | Change on previous year |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube | 4.74M | 78.9% | +1.1% |
| LinkedIn | 3.80M members | 63.2% | +5.6% |
| Facebook | 3.45M | 57.4% | -4.2% |
| Snapchat | 2.87M | 47.7% | -3.0% |
| Instagram | 2.65M | 44.1% | -1.9% |
| TikTok | 1.49M (adults 18+) | 30.6% of adults | +8.0% |

LinkedIn counts registered members, so it is not directly comparable with the rest. Facebook and Instagram reach both fell slightly, while TikTok grew. For most local businesses, Facebook and Instagram still give the widest reach per krone, which you can confirm in the ad tool's audience estimate.

## What Does Google Ads Cost in Denmark?

Google does not publish Danish averages by industry, and earlier versions of this article listed per-industry DKK ranges that had no source. They were removed. The figures LocaliQ publishes are for the United States, so treat them as a rough scale only: across all industries a click averages $5.42 and a lead $66.69 (2026).

Use your own account instead. Run a small campaign for 30 days, record cost per click and cost per booking, and scale from there. Our [Google Ads for European businesses](/blog/google-ads-for-european-businesses) guide covers multi-market setup, and the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) covers how to split spend.

For local intent, use neighbourhood terms in Danish such as "frisør Nørrebro" or "café Vesterbro". Google's help says radius targeting needs at least 1 km, and a very small radius can mean ads show only intermittently.

## Should Your Ads Be in Danish?

Yes for local customers, and write the landing page in Danish too. Google's help says language settings let your ads run in the languages your customers speak. Secondary reporting (Techwyse, August 2026) says Google is removing the manual language setting from Search campaigns from late September 2026 and will match on the language of your ads and landing page instead. That makes a Danish ad pointing to a Danish page the safest setup.

No published study compares English and Danish ad performance in Denmark, so claims that English ads underperform are not backed here. If you serve tourists in central Copenhagen, test a separate English campaign and compare cost per booking.

## How Do You Rank on Google Maps in Denmark?

Google says local results rely on relevance, distance and prominence, and that you cannot pay for a better local ranking. Reviews support prominence. Google lets businesses ask customers for reviews with a link or QR code, and prohibits offering discounts or goods in exchange.

Complete your profile in Danish, add accurate categories, services, hours and photos, and reply to reviews. For wider European tips see our [Google Business Profile guide for Europe](/blog/google-business-profile-optimisation-europe).

## What Do Danish Consent Rules Mean for Marketing?

Two rules matter most. For email and text marketing to consumers, the Danish business portal says electronic marketing is only permitted with the consumer's explicit consent. For tracking cookies, the GDPR (Recital 32) says silence and pre-ticked boxes are not consent, and Article 7 says withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it.

Datatilsynet and Digitaliseringsstyrelsen issued joint cookie guidance in May 2025 (secondary report: Dansk Erhverv), which was not available to check directly. Read it before you configure a banner. This article is not legal advice. For help with a specific campaign, the Consumer Ombudsman offers preliminary opinions. Our [GDPR website compliance guide](/blog/local-business-website-gdpr-compliance) covers the basics.

## FAQ

### Do I need to market entirely in Danish?

For local customers, use Danish in ads and on the landing page. No published test shows by how much English underperforms, so test if you serve tourists.

### Is Facebook still worth using in Denmark?

DataReportal shows Facebook ad reach of 3.45M, down 4.2% on the previous year, so it still reaches a large share of people. Use the audience estimate in your own account to check your target group.

### Do I need double opt-in for email?

The sources we opened say explicit consent is required for consumer email marketing. No source checked for this article requires double opt-in specifically, so ask a Danish adviser about your list process.

### Can I offer a discount for Google reviews?

No. Google prohibits offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews. You may ask customers for reviews with a link or QR code.
