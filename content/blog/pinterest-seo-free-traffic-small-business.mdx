Pinterest SEO means writing your Pin titles, Pin descriptions, board names and landing pages so they match what people type into Pinterest search. Pinterest says Pins "last forever" and can resurface seasonally, so one well-labelled Pin can send visitors to your site for months without ad spend. It works best for visual, planned purchases (hair colour, pet grooming styles, home projects) and poorly for urgent "near me" searches.

## Does Pinterest SEO work for local businesses?

Pinterest reported 640 million global monthly active users for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, up 11% year on year, in its [Q2 2026 results filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001506293/000150629326000102/q2-26xpressrelease.htm). That is a large audience, but it is global and browsing-led. People search Pinterest for ideas, then decide later.

That shapes who benefits:

- **Good fit:** salons, groomers, photographers, studios, home-service firms with before-and-after work, anyone with a blog or portfolio.
- **Weak fit:** emergency services and anything bought within hours of a search.

For the budget side of the same decision, read our [Pinterest worth-it guide for local businesses](/blog/pinterest-marketing-for-local-businesses-worth-it). This article covers the free, organic side only.

## Which Pinterest SEO fields should you fill in?

Pinterest's own advice is to fill in every Pin field, every time, because some fields feed its recommendation systems and are never shown to viewers. In its [audience-building guide](https://business.pinterest.com/blog/how-to-build-audience-pinterest/) it says to always include a Pin title and description with relevant keywords, add topics when offered, and give every board a clear title and description.

Use this checklist per Pin:

1. **Title:** the phrase a customer would type ("curtain bangs for round faces"), not your business slogan.
2. **Description:** one or two plain sentences that repeat the phrase naturally and say what the Pin links to.
3. **Topics and board:** pick the most specific option offered.
4. **Destination link:** a page that matches the Pin text and loads in under 4 seconds.
5. **Board names:** descriptive, such as "Winter hair colour ideas", not "My work".

Pinterest's [distribution help article](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pin-performance-and-distribution) also says Pin images, text and keywords should match the landing page, and that broken links and blurry images hurt distribution.

## How do you find keywords for Pinterest?

Start inside Pinterest. Type a seed phrase into the search bar and note the suggestions that appear below it; they come from what people actually search. Pinterest also suggests combining broad and specific terms (for example "bedroom" with "tropical ceiling fan") so a Pin can appear in more searches.

A simple method for a service business:

| Step | Example for a pet groomer |
| --- | --- |
| Seed phrase | dog grooming |
| Specific variant from search suggestions | goldendoodle haircut styles |
| Problem phrase | how to stop matting between grooms |
| Pin title | Goldendoodle haircut styles: 6 cuts owners ask for |

Pinterest Trends, which Pinterest recommends for timely topics, helps you plan seasonal Pins ahead of the season rather than during it.

## How often should you pin, and what image works?

Pinterest says to create new original Pins at least once a week and not to focus on a fixed number beyond that. Its [Pin-creation page](https://business.pinterest.com/en/Pinterest-board-strategies-and-tools) also recommends adding a link to every Pin and using the built-in scheduler.

Avoid re-saving the same Pin repeatedly: Pinterest warns that duplicate saving may be flagged as spam.

On image size, Pinterest's official pages we checked give no dimensions. A [Pinterest community post](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/about-creating-pins/2757) describes a vertical 2:3 ratio, with 1000 by 1500 pixels as a common target. Treat that as community guidance, not an official rule.

Two practical points: put readable text on the image, since many viewers never open the description, and publish article Pins from blog posts so one post yields several Pins.

## Should you use Rich Pins?

For blog content, yes. Per [Pinterest's Rich Pins help](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/rich-pins), article Rich Pins need Open Graph or Schema.org markup in the page head, show the headline, description and author, and require a text-based page (listicles and image-heavy pages do not qualify). Data syncs automatically, which can take up to 24 hours. Product Rich Pins also require accurate price and stock.

If your site runs on a builder, check whether it already outputs Open Graph tags before assuming you need a developer.

## What does a 90-day test look like?

This is a worked example built on assumptions, not a forecast. A salon owner commits 30 minutes a week:

- **Week 1:** claim the site, set up 4 boards named after searches, create 8 Pins from existing photos and pages.
- **Weeks 2 to 12:** publish 1 to 2 new original Pins a week, each tied to a page on the site.
- **Day 90:** open Pinterest analytics and compare outbound clicks and enquiries against the hours spent.

Set the pass mark before you start. For example: "at least one booking per month traceable to Pinterest, or we stop." That number is a choice, not a benchmark. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a Pinterest business account?**
A: Rich Pins and analytics are tied to business features, and the Rich Pins process requires site markup, so a business account is the practical choice.

**Q: How long until Pinterest SEO shows results?**
A: Pinterest publishes no timeline. Because it says there is no set engagement window and Pins can resurface seasonally, plan for a multi-month test rather than weeks.

**Q: Should I use hashtags?**
A: Pinterest's official pages we reviewed focus on titles, descriptions, topics and board names, so put effort there first.

**Q: Does Pinterest work for B2B or professional services?**
A: It can if you have visual or educational content, such as checklists and guides, but it is rarely the first channel to test.

**Q: What should I measure?**
A: Outbound clicks to your site, then enquiries or bookings from those visits. Saves and impressions alone do not pay bills.
