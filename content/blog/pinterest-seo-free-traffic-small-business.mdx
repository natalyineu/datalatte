Pinterest SEO means writing Pin titles, descriptions, board names and landing pages so they match the phrases people type into Pinterest search. It suits visual, planned purchases such as hair colour, grooming styles or home projects better than urgent needs. ## Who uses Pinterest, and how big is the audience?

Pew Research Center's 2026 fact sheet draws on a national survey of 5,511 U.S. adults, conducted January 30 to June 18, 2026, with a margin of error of plus or minus 1.8 percentage points. It reports YouTube at 84% of adults, Facebook at 70% and Instagram at 51%, according to the [Pew social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/).

The 2026 platform table in that fact sheet does not include a Pinterest figure, so this source gives no current Pinterest usage number. The data also covers U.S. adults only. It does not show how many people in the UK, Australia or Canada use Pinterest, so check current platform data for your own country before assuming reach.

Reach alone does not decide fit. A large audience that browses for ideas will not help a business whose customers need someone today. Pinterest tends to suit services people plan in advance and can show visually, such as salons, groomers and renovation firms.

## How do you match Pins to what people search for?

Start with the phrase a customer would type, not the business slogan. A pet groomer might use "goldendoodle haircut styles" as the Pin title and "how to stop matting between grooms" as a problem phrase in the description. The aim is a Pin that reads like the question the customer is already asking.

Use this checklist for each Pin:

1. Write the title as the search phrase, in plain words.
2. Write a one- or two-sentence description that repeats the phrase naturally and says what the linked page covers.
3. Choose the most specific topic or board available, and name the board after a search rather than after the business.
4. Link to one page on the site that matches the Pin's text and image.
5. Open the link after posting to confirm it works.

Confirm the platform's current field rules in its help centre before publishing, because this guide cannot verify them.

## Does the landing page matter as much as the Pin?

It matters more. A Pin that sends people to a thin or broken page wastes the visit, whatever the title says. Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says a site benefits when its pages are organised so search engines and users understand the topic, and it recommends descriptive URLs with words users might look for. The same logic applies to the page a Pin links to.

The same guide says a crawler should see a page much as a user does, so important content should not be hidden in ways that stop it loading. If pages show different information by location, check what Google sees from its crawler's location, which is generally the US. The URL Inspection Tool in Search Console shows how Google sees a given page.

## How do you run a fair 90-day test?

Meta's [guidance on A/B testing for Meta Ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669) covers its own ads, not Pinterest, but its principles transfer. It says a test compares two versions by changing a variable such as the image, text or audience, and recommends using the same budget for both versions. It also advises against informal testing, such as switching ad sets on and off by hand, because that can produce overlapping audiences and unreliable results.

Apply the same discipline to Pins. Change one thing, such as the title format, and compare results over equal periods. Keep the time spent on each approach the same, so the comparison stays fair.

For a small business, a practical test looks like this:

- Set up four boards named after search phrases in week one.
- Publish one or two new original Pins a week, each linked to a page on the site.
- Keep a simple log of which Pins link to which pages, so enquiries can be traced later.
- On day 90, compare outbound clicks and enquiries against the hours spent.

Write the pass mark before starting, for example "at least one booking a month traceable to Pinterest, or stop the test." Choose a number that matters to the business, not one copied from someone else.

## Who should skip Pinterest SEO?

Businesses whose customers need help within hours, such as emergency repairs or same-day services, should usually test search and local listings first. Pinterest rewards planning, so it offers less when a customer is searching urgently.

Businesses with no photos, no portfolio and no pages worth linking to should also wait. A Pin without a useful landing page sends people away. Build the page first, then run the test.

## FAQ

### Do I need a Pinterest business account?

A business account gives access to analytics that show outbound clicks from Pins. Confirm the current account options in Pinterest's help centre, since this guide cannot verify them.

### How long until Pinterest SEO shows results?

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says changes can take a few hours or several months to show, and that waiting a few weeks is usually sensible before judging them. Plan for a multi-month test rather than a few days.

### Should I use hashtags on Pins?

The sources behind this guide do not cover Pinterest's hashtag guidance, so test it separately, changing one variable at a time. Focus first on titles, descriptions and the landing page.

### What should I measure?

Measure outbound clicks to the site, then enquiries and bookings traced to those visits. Saves and impressions alone do not show whether Pinterest is paying off.
