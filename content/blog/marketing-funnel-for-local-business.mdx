A marketing funnel for a local business has four stages: be found, earn trust, get the booking, and bring customers back. Start with the stage that leaks most. Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read online reviews. So a complete profile and a steady flow of reviews are usually the first fixes.

This guide uses simple stages rather than the long awareness-interest-desire-action model, because a local shop rarely needs five steps. Numbers come from cited sources, and the worked example is labelled as an assumption.

## What Are the Stages of a Local Marketing Funnel?

| Stage | Question the customer asks | Where you work on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Be found | Who is near me? | Google Business Profile, local SEO, ads |
| Earn trust | Are they good? | Reviews, photos, website proof |
| Book | How do I get started? | Call, booking link, form |
| Come back | Why return or refer? | Email, SMS, reminders, loyalty |

Each stage has one number to watch: profile views, review count, bookings and repeat visits. If you cannot measure a stage, start by setting up a simple count.

## Stage 1: How Do Local Customers Find You?

Google's help says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance depends on how fully the profile describes the business. Distance is measured from the searcher. Prominence reflects how well known you are, partly through reviews and ratings.

That gives a checklist: complete every profile field, pick the right category, add hours and photos, and keep your name, address and phone number consistent. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) lists the fields. Paid search can sit on top, but fix the free profile first.

## Stage 2: How Do You Earn Trust?

BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found 97% read online reviews, and 41% always read them when browsing local businesses, up from 29% a year earlier. It also found consumers use six different review sites on average, and that Google's share as a review source fell from 83% in 2025 to 71%.

So ask for reviews on more than one site, reply to them, and show them on your website. The survey is US-based, so treat the figures as directional for other countries. For a process, see [Google Business Profile reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) and [how to automate review requests](/blog/how-to-automate-google-review-requests).

## Stage 3: How Do You Turn Interest Into a Booking?

Remove steps. A visitor who wants a haircut, a table or a quote should be able to call, tap a booking link or fill a short form from the first screen. Put the same details on the profile, the website and any ad.

For people who look but do not book, [retargeting ads for local business](/blog/retargeting-ads-for-local-business) can bring them back. Keep the offer clear and the booking path short, and expect some visitors to need a second visit.

## Stage 4: How Do You Bring Customers Back?

Harvard Business Review says acquiring a new customer is "anywhere from five to 25 times more expensive than retaining an existing one." The article does not name the study behind that range, so read it as a general rule, not a measured figure for your trade.

Use what you collect at the booking: an email or phone number, with permission. Send a reminder, a seasonal note or a review request. A short sequence beats a monthly blast for most small shops. See [email marketing for coffee shops](/blog/email-marketing-for-coffee-shops) for an example of a light, repeatable approach.

## Worked Example: Where Is the Leak?

Inputs are **assumptions** for a salon: 1,000 profile views a month, 5% tap to call or book, 40% of those become appointments, 30% of customers return within 3 months.

| Step | Assumption | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profile views | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| Tap to call or book | 5% | 50 |
| Become appointments | 40% | 20 |
| Return within 3 months | 30% | 6 |

Improving one stage helps differently. A better profile that lifts taps from 5% to 7% adds 20 contacts and 8 appointments. Lifting the return rate from 30% to 40% adds 2 repeat customers. Test the biggest leak first, which is usually the earliest stage with the lowest conversion, and use your own numbers.

## How Do You Choose the First Fix?

Pick the stage where you have the least information. If you do not know how many people view your profile, start there. If you get views but few calls, work on trust and the booking path. If customers book once and vanish, add follow-up.

Do not build all four stages at once. A single working loop, such as profile to booking to review request, will teach you more than a large plan. A related walkthrough is our [sales funnel guide for local businesses](/blog/marketing-funnel-for-local-business).

## FAQ

### What is a marketing funnel for a local business?

It is the path from someone finding you to becoming a repeat customer. A simple version has four stages: be found, earn trust, book, return.

### Which stage should I fix first?

The one with the weakest measured result. For many local businesses that is the Google Business Profile and reviews.

### How many reviews do I need?

No source gives a target. Google says prominence is partly based on the number of reviews and ratings, and BrightLocal found 97% of consumers read them.

### Is retention cheaper than finding new customers?

HBR says five to 25 times cheaper but does not name its study. Measure your own cost per new and per returning customer.

### Do I need paid ads in the funnel?

Not always. Ads add reach at the first and third stages, but a free profile, reviews and follow-up can carry a small business.
