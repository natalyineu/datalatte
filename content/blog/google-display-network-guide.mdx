The Google Display Network (GDN) is worth it for most local businesses as a remarketing add-on, not as the main channel. It shows image ads to people who already visited your website or appear on your customer list, while they browse other sites, apps, YouTube and Gmail. Google says Display can reach across 35 million websites and apps, but reach is not the goal. Display works when it brings back people who already know you, and wastes money when it buys cold strangers. Run search first, then test Display with a fixed budget and a clear break-even number.

This guide covers who Display suits, the jobs worth paying for, a setup checklist, and a $300-a-month test with the maths shown.

## What is the Google Display Network for a local business?

Google's [Display help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190) describes Display campaigns as visually engaging ads shown across the Google Display Network, which reaches people as they browse websites, apps and Google properties such as YouTube and Gmail. Display campaigns use AI for bidding, targeting and creative, and the default ad format is responsive, so you upload images, logos and headlines, and Google assembles them to fit each placement.

Display click costs are far lower than search, but fewer people click. WordStream's [benchmark page](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks) (a secondary, third-party source) gives all-industry averages of $2.69 per search click and $0.63 per display click, with display CTR at 0.46% against 3.17% for search. Display shows an ad to someone reading a recipe or watching a video. Search shows one to someone who just typed "pilates classes near me". The intent is different, so the job is different.

| | Search ads | Display ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shown when | Someone types a query | Someone browses sites, apps, YouTube or Gmail |
| Intent | High, they asked | Low, you interrupted |
| Format | Text | Images and responsive ads |
| Average cost per click | Higher ($2.69 all-industry) | Lower ($0.63 all-industry) |
| Best job | Capturing demand | Reminding people who already know you |

## Which Display jobs are worth paying for?

Remarketing to your own visitors is the strongest use for a local business. Someone looks at your prices or booking page, leaves, and then sees a reminder while reading other sites. Google's Display page describes targeting that spans people with demonstrated interests through to customers about to buy, but for a salon or clinic the most useful audience is the person who already came close to booking. Set the tag first and let visitors accumulate before expecting results.

Existing customers are a second option. You can upload a customer list so Google can show ads to those people when they are signed in. Only use emails that customers gave you with consent, and keep the offer specific: a new class timetable, a seasonal menu, or a "we have missed you" booking discount. For a list-building workflow, see the comparison of [marketing automation tools for small business](/blog/best-marketing-automation-tools-small-business).

Local awareness, such as announcing a new venue, is the weakest job. You pay to show ads to people who did not ask for them. If you test it, cap the budget and judge it by calls and visits, not impressions. ## How do you set up a local Display campaign?

Start with tracking, not ads. Install the Google tag on every page and create conversions for calls, form fills and bookings. Without them you are buying impressions with no way to judge cost per enquiry. Check that each conversion fires in a test before launch.

Next, set location tightly. Choose the areas or radius your customers actually travel from. A single-site cafe gains nothing from a country-wide setting, and paying for distant readers only inflates the bill. Keep the audience simple too: location plus one audience, such as website visitors. Stacking many filters can shrink the audience until Google has too little to serve.

For creative, upload several images of your real space, products or team, in both landscape and square shapes, with several headlines. Google's default responsive format picks combinations, so variety helps. Make sure the ad promise matches the page it lands on. If the ad offers a free first class, the landing page should lead with that sign-up.

Set bidding to match your goal. Google recommends Smart Bidding strategies based on your advertising goals, though you can choose manual bidding instead. If you use Target CPA, Google's help page notes you can pay for conversions rather than clicks. Then set a frequency cap in the campaign settings so one person does not see the same ad every day.

Finally, review placements after the first week or two. Exclude sites, apps or videos that look irrelevant to your customers, such as games. Exclude past converters from remarketing too, so you stop paying to remind people who already booked.

## How much would a $300 test return?

The table below is a worked example. Every input is an **assumption**, chosen for illustration rather than sourced: the Base cost per click of $0.60 sits near WordStream's $0.63 all-industry display average, and the click-to-enquiry and enquiry-to-customer rates are placeholders. Replace them with your own numbers after four weeks.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |

| Monthly budget (assumption) | $300 | $300 | $300 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $0.80 | $0.60 | $0.45 |
| Clicks | 375 | 500 | 667 |
| Click to enquiry rate (assumption) | 0.5% | 1.0% | 2.0% |
| Enquiries | 1.9 | 5.0 | 13.3 |
| Cost per enquiry | $160 | $60 | $22.50 |
| Enquiry to customer rate (assumption) | 25% | 25% | 25% |
| New customers | 0.5 | 1.25 | 3.3 |
| Ad cost per customer | $640 | $240 | $90 |

Your break-even is simple: maximum cost per enquiry equals customer value multiplied by close rate. Assume a customer is worth $400 to you (assumption) and you close 25% of enquiries (assumption). The ceiling is $100 per enquiry. Base at $60 passes, and Tough at $160 fails.

Two cautions apply. View-through conversions, where someone saw an ad and converted later, can claim credit for sales that would have happened anyway, so judge on click-based conversions first. Second, a remarketing audience already knows you, so its enquiry rate should beat cold Display. If it does not, the creative or landing page is the problem.

## What mistakes waste a Display budget?

- Running Display with no conversion tracking.
- Letting automatic placements run for a month without checking where the money went.
- Targeting a radius far larger than anyone will travel to your door.
- Using one generic stock photo for every audience.
- Expecting search-level conversion rates from a reminder channel.
- Judging the campaign after four days instead of four weeks.

## What should you do this week?

1. Confirm the Google tag is on every page and that call, form and booking conversions fire in a test.
2. Count last month's website visitors. If you have only a few hundred, fix search and your Google Business Profile before adding Display.
3. Write down the most you would pay per enquiry, using the break-even formula above.
4. Launch one small remarketing campaign with tight location, one audience, one offer, both image shapes and a frequency cap.
5. Set two reminders: day seven to review placements and add exclusions, and day 28 to compare cost per enquiry with your search campaign.

## FAQ

### Is the Google Display Network worth it for a small local business?

Often as a remarketing add-on, rarely as the main channel. Google describes Display as ads shown while people browse, so intent is lower than search. Run search and a complete Google Business Profile first, then add Display to bring back your own visitors.

### How much should I spend on Display?

There is no fixed figure. The $300 test above buys roughly 375 to 667 clicks at the assumed prices of $0.80 to $0.45 per click. Size the budget to your audience, because a small remarketing list cannot absorb a large spend.

### Can I target only people in my town?

Yes. Set the location to the areas or radius your customers actually travel from. Keep the radius realistic, since paying for distant readers inflates cost without adding customers.

### How do I stop Display chasing people forever?

Set a frequency cap in the campaign settings, exclude past converters from remarketing, and refresh the creative every month or two so the same ad does not run indefinitely.
