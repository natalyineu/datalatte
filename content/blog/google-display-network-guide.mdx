The Google Display Network (GDN) is a collection of websites, apps, YouTube and Gmail placements where Google can show your image and text ads. For most local businesses it works best for remarketing to people who already visited your site, not for finding brand new customers, and it rarely replaces search ads.

Display ads reach people while they read, watch or browse, rather than when they search for you. That makes them cheaper per click than search typically, but also lower in intent. Used thoughtfully, GDN is a good reminder channel. Used carelessly, it burns budget on irrelevant placements. Here is how to decide and how to run it.

## Search vs. display in plain terms

| | Search ads | Display ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shown when | Someone searches a keyword | Someone browses sites, apps or videos |
| Intent | High (they asked) | Lower (you interrupt) |
| Format | Text | Images, responsive ads, sometimes video |
| Typical cost per click | Higher | Lower |
| Best for | Capturing demand | Reminding and building awareness |

If you have not yet set up search ads, start there. Our [Google Ads beginner guide](/blog/google-ads-for-beginners-first-campaign-step-by-step) explains the basics. Add display once you have steady website traffic to remarket to.

## Where the network shows your ads

Google says its Display Network covers a very large number of sites and apps worldwide, reaching most internet users. Placements include news and blog sites, Gmail promotions, YouTube and mobile apps. You can choose broad targeting or restrict ads to specific placements.

In practice, many Display placements are low quality (mobile games, accidental clicks), so you need to monitor and exclude them.

## Good uses for local businesses

1. **Remarketing**: Show ads to people who visited your site or key pages, such as a booking page, but did not convert. This is usually the strongest GDN use.
2. **Customer match**: Upload an email list (with consent) to show ads to existing customers, for example a new menu, class timetable or seasonal offer.
3. **Local awareness**: Target a radius around your location to introduce a new business or promote an event.
4. **Seasonal campaigns**: Mother's Day, back to school, holiday gift cards, new-year fitness.

If your business is not searched for much (a new type of service), display can help seed awareness. ## Targeting options you can use

- **Location**: Radius or specific postcodes. Choose "presence" rather than "interest" so you reach people actually in your area.
- **Audience segments**: Affinity (long-term interests), in-market (people researching a product category) and life events.
- **Your data**: Website visitors, app users, YouTube viewers and customer lists.
- **Content targeting**: Keywords, topics and placements to choose where ads appear.
- **Demographics**: Age, gender, parental status and household income in supported countries.

Layering too many filters can leave an audience too small to serve ads. Begin with location plus one audience.

## Step-by-step setup

1. **Install tracking**: Add the Google tag and set up conversions for calls, form fills and bookings first. Our [conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) walks through it.
2. **Build audiences**: Create remarketing lists in Google Ads (the data manager) for all visitors, key page visitors and converters. Lists need a minimum size to serve.
3. **Create a campaign**: Choose your goal (leads, sales or website traffic). For smaller accounts, a Display remarketing campaign or a Performance Max campaign may be offered depending on your account and goal.
4. **Set location and language** and limit by schedule if relevant.
5. **Upload assets**: Responsive display ads use several headlines, descriptions, images and a logo that Google assembles automatically.
6. **Set budget and bidding**: Start with a modest daily amount and either maximise conversions or a manual cost-per-click cap for control.
7. **Exclude placements**: Add exclusions for mobile app categories, and review the placement report after a week or two.

## Creative that gets noticed

- One clear message per ad, such as "Book your free first class" or "New spring menu".
- Real photos of your business rather than abstract stock images.
- Your logo and name visible, so people recognise you.
- A strong call to action on the headline or button.
- Several image sizes, including square and landscape, so ads fit different placements.
- Avoid text-heavy images, which can limit delivery and look cluttered.

Match the ad to the landing page. If the ad says "Free class", the page should show the free class sign-up above the fold. See our advice on [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices).

## Budget: what is realistic

Costs vary by country, industry and audience. Display clicks are typically cheaper than search clicks, and many small remarketing campaigns can be tested with a small daily budget over several weeks. A rough working rule is to keep display at a fraction of your total ad spend while you learn, and to scale only if results justify it.

Do not judge success by clicks or impressions. Track:

- Conversions (calls, bookings, form leads)
- View-through conversions, but treat them cautiously, since someone may have converted anyway
- Cost per conversion compared with your search campaigns

If you want help sizing a budget, see our guide to [Google Ads budgets for small business](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business).

## Frequency and fatigue

Remarketing can annoy people if the same ad follows them everywhere. Set a frequency cap and refresh creative every month or two. Exclude people who already converted so you do not keep advertising to existing customers, unless that is the point of a customer-match campaign.

## Common mistakes

- Running display with no conversion tracking
- Leaving placements unchecked, so budget goes to junk apps
- Targeting an area that is too large
- Using the same generic image for every audience
- Expecting display to deliver search-level conversion rates
- Judging after a few days with too little data

## FAQ

**Q: Is the Google Display Network worth it for a small local business?**
A: Often as a remarketing and awareness add-on, rarely as the primary channel. Search ads and a strong Google Business Profile usually come first.

**Q: How much should I spend on display ads?**
A: Start small and scale if you see real conversions. The right number depends on the size of your remarketing audience, your margins and how many leads you can handle.

**Q: Can I target only people in my town?**
A: Yes. Use location targeting with a radius or specific areas, and set it to people present in or regularly in that location.

**Q: What is the difference between Display and Performance Max?**
A: Display is one channel type you control directly. Performance Max uses automation across Search, Display, YouTube, Maps and more. It can work well, but gives you less control and needs good conversion data.
