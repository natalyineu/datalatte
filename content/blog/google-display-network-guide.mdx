Google says Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps. Your local business, meanwhile, might get 1,500 website visitors a month. The smart question is not "how do I reach 35 million placements?" but "how do I get in front of my own 1,500 visitors again, cheaply, before they book somewhere else?" That is where the Google Display Network (GDN) earns its place, and it is why it usually sits behind search ads rather than in front of them.

This guide is for owners of local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: cafes, salons, studios, clinics, groomers. It covers what Display is, the two or three jobs worth giving it, the settings that stop budget leaking, and a $300-a-month test with the math laid out so you can see when it works and when it does not.

## What Display Actually Is

Google's [Display help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190) describes Display campaigns as visual ads shown as people browse websites and apps, plus Google properties such as YouTube and Gmail, across what it calls 35 million websites and apps. The default format is the responsive display ad: you upload images, headlines, logos and descriptions, and Google assembles them to fit the space available.

Benchmarks for Display are thin and mostly old. An agency write-up from [Thomas Marsden](https://www.thomasmarsden.com.au/blog/192/) (dated February 2020) lists these all-industry Display averages, crediting WordStream for the first two:

Treat those as the shape of the thing, not current prices. The figures are second-hand (WordStream's original was not available to check) and about six years old. The pattern is still useful: Display clicks cost a fraction of search clicks and a far smaller share of people click. Display shows an ad to someone who was reading a recipe or playing a game. Search shows one to someone who just typed "pilates classes near me". Different intent, different job.

| | Search ads | Display ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shown when | Someone types a query | Someone browses a site, app, YouTube or Gmail |
| Intent | High, they asked | Low, you interrupted |
| Format | Text | Images, responsive ads |
| Cost per click | Higher | Typically lower |
| Best job | Capturing demand | Reminding people who already know you |

## The Three Jobs Worth Giving Display

**1. Remarketing to your own visitors.** Google's [audience targeting page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497941) lists "your data segments" (formerly remarketing) as audiences that have interacted with your business, including website and app visitors and Customer Match lists. For a local business this is the strongest use: someone looked at your price list or booking page, left, and now sees a reminder for a week or two. Our [remarketing setup guide](/blog/google-ads-remarketing-setup-local-businesses) walks through the tag and list build.

On list size, a PPC News Feed report dated [24 December 2025](https://ppcnewsfeed.com/ppc-news/2025-12/google-lowers-audience-size-limit-to-100-across-all-campaign-types/) says Google lowered the minimum audience size to 100 users across Search, Display and YouTube, citing an update to Google's Audience Segments help page. That is a secondary report, so check the number in your own account. If your list is tiny, the practical fix is time: let the tag collect visitors for a few weeks before launching.

**2. Customer Match for existing customers.** Google's [Customer Match help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6379332) says you upload a customer data file and target the people in it who are signed in to a Google account, across Search, YouTube, Gmail and Display. Memberships expire after 540 days, and the list needs at least 100 members added or updated in that window to stay eligible. Only upload emails you collected with consent. Good uses: a new class timetable, a seasonal menu, a "we have missed you" offer.

**3. Local awareness for something new.** A new venue or an event can justify a radius campaign. This is the weakest of the three, because you are paying to be seen by strangers who did not ask. Cap the budget and judge it by calls and visits, not impressions.

## Set It Up Step by Step

### 1. Tracking before ads

Install the Google tag and create conversions for calls, form fills and bookings. Without this you are buying impressions. The steps are in our conversion tracking guide.

### 2. Location: choose who you reach

Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722043) describes two options. "Presence or interest" also reaches people who have shown interest in your target location, and Google recommends it as a best practice for Search. "Presence" limits reach to people physically in the location, and Google suggests it when you only want to reach users there. For a local Display campaign I recommend Presence, which is my advice rather than a Google rule. A cafe has no use for someone in another country who once read about your town.

### 3. Audience: one layer, not five

Start with location plus one audience, such as your website visitors. Google's audience page also describes affinity segments (interests and habits) and in-market segments (people researching a purchase). Stack too many filters and you can end up with an audience too small to serve.

### 4. Assets that meet the spec

Google's [responsive display ad requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7005917) say:

- 1 to 5 short headlines, 30 characters or fewer each
- One long headline of 90 characters or fewer
- 1 to 5 descriptions
- A required business name
- Landscape images (1.91:1, recommended 1200 x 628 px) and square images (1:1, recommended 1200 x 1200 px), up to 5120 KB each
- A square or 4:1 landscape logo

Upload both image shapes, using real photos of your space, product or team. Match the ad to the page it lands on. If the ad says "Free first class", the page should open with that sign-up. See our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices).

### 5. Frequency: do not stalk people

Per Google's [frequency capping help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6034106), you can only add a cap after the campaign exists: Campaigns, Settings, select the Display campaign, Additional settings, Frequency management. You can leave it to Google's optimisation or set your own cap at campaign, ad group or ad level, and only viewable impressions count. Read our [retargeting best practices](/blog/retargeting-best-practices-10-rules-to-avoid-annoying-your-audience) for sensible limits.

### 6. Exclude junk placements

Google's [exclusions help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2454012) shows where: Audiences, keywords and content, then Content, then Exclusions, then Edit exclusions. You can exclude at campaign, ad group or account level, and by URL, app ID, video ID, category or content label. It also gives the syntax for single apps (`mobileapp::1-` plus the iTunes ID for iPhone, `mobileapp::2-` plus the package name for Android). Check the placements report after the first week or two and exclude anything that looks like a game or a site that is clearly not your customer. Whether to exclude whole app categories is a judgement call: it cuts accidental taps, but also removes some real traffic.

<Callout type="coffee" title="Remove Your Customers From the Audience">
Once someone books, they should stop seeing "Book now" ads. Exclude converters from your remarketing campaign, or you will pay to remind people who already came. The exception is a deliberate Customer Match campaign for repeat visits.
</Callout>

## The Math

Here is a $300 monthly Display remarketing test. Every input is an **assumption** for illustration. The Base CPC is chosen to sit close to the $0.58 older average above; the conversion rates and close rate are placeholders, since no current, trustworthy Display figures for local services could be verified. Swap in your own numbers after four weeks.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $300 | $300 | $300 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $0.80 | $0.60 | $0.45 |
| Clicks | 375 | 500 | 667 |
| Click to enquiry rate (assumption) | 0.5% | 1.0% | 2.0% |
| Enquiries | 1.9 | 5 | 13.3 |
| Cost per enquiry | $160 | $60 | $22.50 |
| Enquiry to customer rate (assumption) | 25% | 25% | 25% |
| New customers | 0.5 | 1.25 | 3.3 |
| Ad cost per customer | $640 | $240 | $90 |

Read it like this. At Base, the test only makes sense if one customer is worth well over $240 to you, or if those enquiries would otherwise have gone to a competitor. A dental implant consult or a salon colour client with a long lifetime value can clear that bar. A $4 latte cannot. Your break-even is simple: maximum cost per enquiry = value of a customer x close rate. At a $400 customer value and 25% close rate, that is $100 per enquiry, so Base passes and Tough fails.

Two cautions. First, view-through conversions (someone saw an ad, did not click, converted later) can claim credit for sales that would have happened anyway, so judge on click-based conversions first. Second, remarketing audiences are made of people who already know you, so the enquiry rate should beat cold display. If it does not, the creative or the landing page is the problem, not the network.

## Common Mistakes

- Running Display with no conversion tracking.
- Letting auto-placements run for a month without checking where the money went.
- Targeting a radius far larger than anyone will travel.
- One generic stock photo for every audience.
- Expecting search-level conversion rates from a reminder channel.
- Judging after four days. Give it four weeks to collect clicks.

## Do This Today

1. Check your Google tag is installed on every page and that call, form and booking conversions are firing. Start building a website-visitors audience if you have not already.
2. Count your last month of website visitors. If it is under a few hundred, delay Display and fix search and your Google Business Profile first.
3. Write down the maximum you would pay per enquiry using the break-even formula above.
4. Launch one small remarketing campaign: Presence targeting, one audience, one offer, both image shapes, and a frequency cap set after the campaign is created.
5. Put a diary note for seven days out to read the placements report and add exclusions, and another for day 28 to compare cost per enquiry with your search campaign.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the Google Display Network worth it for a small local business?**
A: Often as a remarketing add-on, rarely as the main channel. Google describes Display as ads shown while people browse, so intent is lower than search. Run search and a complete Google Business Profile first, then add Display to bring back your own visitors.

**Q: How much should I spend on Display?**
A: There is no official figure. A test of $300 a month, as in the table, buys roughly 400 to 650 clicks if your clicks cost $0.45 to $0.80 (assumed prices). Size it to your audience: a tiny remarketing list cannot absorb a big budget.

**Q: Can I target only people in my town?**
A: Yes. Use a radius or specific areas and choose Presence, per Google's location targeting help, to reach people physically in those locations.

**Q: What is the difference between Display and Performance Max?**
A: Display is one channel you control directly. Performance Max runs across Google's channels with more automation and less control, and works best with solid conversion data. Prove a simple campaign first.

**Q: How do I stop Display chasing people forever?**
A: Set a frequency cap after the campaign is created, exclude past converters, and refresh the creative every month or two.
