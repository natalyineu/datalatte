Direct mail can work for a local business when you mail a tight area with one clear offer and count the redemptions. USPS Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM) Retail postage is currently $0.26 per piece for flats up to 3.3 oz, with a minimum of 200 pieces and no mailing permit. Printing and design cost extra, and USPS does not publish a response rate for your trade.

This guide is for local businesses in the US. The postage prices are USPS prices; readers in the UK, Australia and Canada need their own postal service's rates. Every figure comes from a source at the end, and worked examples are labelled as assumptions.

## How Much Does Direct Mail Cost for a Local Business?

Postage is the part USPS publishes. Printing, design and any list purchase are priced by your printer, and no source gives a standard figure, so get two quotes for the size you plan to mail.

| Option | Postage per piece | Needs a list? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EDDM Retail | $0.26 | No, mail goes to every address on chosen routes | 200 to 5,000 pieces per ZIP Code per day, no permit |
| EDDM through a BMEU | as low as $0.259 | No | Business Customer Gateway account and bulk permit |
| Stamped First-Class postcard | $0.65 | Yes, you supply names and addresses | USPS Notice 123 retail price |

USPS lists these on its [Every Door Direct Mail page](https://www.usps.com/business/every-door-direct-mail.htm) and in [Notice 123](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/Notice123.htm). Prices change, and USPS describes them only as "currently", so confirm in the EDDM tool before you budget.

## How Does Every Door Direct Mail Work?

EDDM Retail lets you pick postal carrier routes in an online tool, print your piece and drop it at the Post Office. USPS says the pieces are addressed "Postal Customer" and delivered to every address on your selected routes. You do not need a mailing list or a permit.

You can filter routes by age, income or household size using U.S. Census data, according to USPS. That is route-level data, not data about your customers, so treat it as a rough filter.

The order steps USPS lists are: create a USPS.com account, select routes, design and print, choose a drop-off date and payment, print the forms, bundle the mail and deliver it. Bundles are stacks of 50 to 100 pieces, no higher than 6 inches.

## Is Direct Mail Better Than Digital Ads?

No source settles this for local businesses, and response claims on vendor blogs are usually secondary retellings. The honest comparison is cost per customer, measured in your own area. Our [direct mail vs digital ads comparison](/blog/direct-mail-vs-digital-ads-local-business-2026) walks through that test.

In practice, mail suits businesses with a fixed service area and a repeat product, such as dentists, restaurants, salons and home services. It suits a new-resident or grand-opening push. It suits weakly when your offer needs a long explanation or your customers are spread across a city.

## Which Offer Should You Mail?

One offer, one deadline, one way to respond. The mailer should answer three questions in a few seconds: who you are, what is in it for the reader, and what to do next.

- A time-limited first-visit offer with a code or a QR code that goes to a page only mailer recipients see
- A grand-opening or seasonal event with a date
- A "bring this card" item that staff can count at the till

Avoid claims such as "voted best" unless you can source them. Put your address, phone number and hours on the piece, because the card may sit on a counter for weeks.

## How Do You Measure Results?

Track redemptions by a unique code, a dedicated phone number or a QR code that opens a landing page used only for the mailer. Count responses weekly for a month after delivery, since some people keep the piece.

Then compare mailed routes with a similar unmailed route if you can. A redemption is not always a new customer, because existing customers also have mail. Ask at the till whether it is their first visit.

## What Does a 2,000-Piece Mailing Return? A Worked Example

Every input is an **assumption**, except the $0.26 postage from USPS. Replace each with your quote and your numbers.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |

| Print and design total | $600 | $600 | $600 |

| Response rate (assumption) | 0.2% | 0.5% | 1% |
| Responses | 4 | 10 | 20 |
| Share who are new customers (assumption) | 50% | 60% | 70% |
| New customers | 2 | 6 | 14 |
| Cost per new customer | $560 | $187 | $80 |

The response rates are placeholders, not benchmarks. What matters is the break-even: divide total cost by the profit you earn on a new customer over their lifetime. With $1,120 spent and $300 lifetime profit per customer (assumption), you need about 4 new customers. At the Base rate here, the mailing returns roughly $1,800 against $1,120.

## Can You Combine Mail With Email?

Yes, and it is cheap. If your offer page captures an email, the mailer feeds the list you own, and later messages cost almost nothing to send. ## FAQ

### How much does direct mail cost per piece?

USPS lists EDDM Retail postage at $0.26 per piece for flats up to 3.3 oz. Printing and design are extra and vary by printer. A stamped First-Class postcard is $0.65 under USPS Notice 123.

### Do I need a mailing list for EDDM?

No. USPS says EDDM Retail pieces are addressed "Postal Customer" and delivered to every address on the routes you pick.

### What is the minimum order?

USPS says EDDM Retail requires at least 200 and up to 5,000 pieces per day per ZIP Code.

### What response rate should I expect?

No verified benchmark exists for your trade. Vendor blogs quote figures that retell an industry report; we could not check that report, so we do not use them. Run a 200-piece test.

### How long should I wait before judging a mailing?

At least four weeks after delivery, since recipients may keep the piece, and compare redemptions with your cost per new customer.
