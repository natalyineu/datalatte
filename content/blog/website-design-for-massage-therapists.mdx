A massage therapist website should show your services and prices, your license and approach, your location, and a booking button that works on a phone. Booking software is the main cost: published 2026 plans run from $16 a month for a basic scheduler to $59 to $99 a month for a practice-management tool such as Jane.

This guide covers independent therapists and small clinics. Prices were checked on 31 October 2026 on the vendors' own pages. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Pages Does a Massage Therapist Website Need?

Five pages cover most solo practices.

| Page | Include | Main button |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Who you help (for example sports recovery or prenatal), area, one clear offer | Book now |
| Services | Each treatment, length, price, who it suits | Book this treatment |
| About | License, training, approach | Book now |
| FAQ | First visit, clothing, cancellation, payment | Book now |
| Contact | Address, parking, hours, map | Book now |

Put the price next to each treatment. Visitors compare you with nearby practices before they call. Our guide to [FAQ pages](/blog/website-faq-page-local-business) shows how to answer first-visit questions without a phone call.

## Which Online Booking Tool Should You Use?

Pick based on whether you need only a calendar or full client records.

| Tool | Published price (31 Oct 2026) | Notes from the vendor page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acuity Starter | $16/mo annual, $20/mo monthly | 1 calendar, email reminders, intake forms, online payments |
| Acuity Standard | $27/mo annual, $34/mo monthly | Up to 6 calendars, text reminders, memberships and packages |
| Acuity Premium | $55/mo annual, $69/mo monthly | Adds HIPAA BAA signing |
| Jane Balance | $59/mo | One practitioner profile, capped at 20 appointments per month |
| Jane Practice | $79/mo | One full-time practitioner |
| Jane Thrive | $99/mo | Adds advanced scheduling and retention tools |

Every Jane plan lists a custom-branded online booking site. Jane's insurance billing is a paid add-on at $20 a month plus a per-practitioner fee, and Jane Websites is $59 a month per clinic. A solo therapist who takes cash or card only can usually start with a scheduler; a clinic that bills insurance may need the larger tool.

## Does HIPAA Apply to Your Website?

It depends on how you bill. The US Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services says providers who submit HIPAA transactions, like claims, electronically are covered. If you only take direct payment and never send claims electronically, you may not be a covered entity, but this is a legal question for your adviser, not a rule of thumb. HHS pages returned an error when checked, so this article relies on CMS only.

If you are covered, the booking and intake tools you pick need to support it. Acuity lists HIPAA BAA signing only on its Premium plan. Ask any vendor in writing before putting health questions into a form.

## What Should You Say About Licensing?

State rules differ. AMTA's state regulation page lists 46 states and the District of Columbia with massage practice laws and the year each was enacted. Check your state board for the exact wording on how a license number or title must appear in advertising, and put it in your footer and About page.

## How Fast and Mobile-Friendly Should It Be?

Google's published Core Web Vitals targets are Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint of 200 ms or less and Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less, measured at the 75th percentile. Google also indexes the mobile version and says it should hold the same content as the desktop one. Calm, large photos are common on massage sites, so compress them. See our [speed guide](/blog/website-speed-optimization-small-business) and [mobile-first guide](/blog/mobile-optimization-local-business).

For accessibility, WCAG contrast and readable fonts matter more on soft, low-contrast spa palettes; see our [accessibility guide](/blog/website-accessibility-small-business).

## Worked Example: What Does Booking Software Cost Per Booking?

All inputs are **assumptions**.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Tool | Acuity Standard, $27/mo annual |
| Bookings per month through the site | 40 |
| Average treatment price | $90 |
| Software cost per booking | $27 / 40 = $0.68 |
| Monthly booked revenue | 40 x $90 = $3,600 |

At 40 bookings the software costs under 1% of booked revenue. If you used Jane Practice at $79 instead, the cost per booking would be about $1.98, still low. Card processing fees are separate and not included here.

## FAQ

### How much does a massage therapist website cost?

Tools range from about $16 a month for a scheduler to $99 a month for Jane Thrive. Site builders such as Squarespace list plans from $19 a month on annual billing; see our [personal trainer site guide](/blog/website-design-for-personal-trainers) for the builder price table. A domain and photos are extra.

### Do I need online booking?

It is not mandatory, but it lets clients book outside your working hours. No independent benchmark was found for how much it raises bookings, so test it against your own calls.

### Do I need to be HIPAA compliant?

Only if you are a covered entity, for example if you send insurance claims electronically. Ask your adviser.

### Where do I show my license?
