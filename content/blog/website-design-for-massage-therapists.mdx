## What Pages Does a Massage Therapist Website Need?

Five pages cover most solo practices. Each one should answer a question a new client would otherwise ask by phone: what you offer, who it suits, what it costs, how it works, and where to find you.

| Page | What to include | Main button |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Who you help, your area, one clear offer | Book now |
| Services | Each treatment, its length, its price, who it suits | Book this treatment |
| About | Your license, training and approach | Book now |
| FAQ | First visit, what to wear, cancellation and payment rules | Book now |
| Contact | Address, parking, hours and a map | Book now |

Put the price next to each treatment rather than behind a call-to-quote. The button should appear above the fold on a phone, so a visitor does not need to scroll to find it. The [above-the-fold guide](/blog/above-the-fold-design-local-business) covers placement, and the [local business website checklist](/blog/local-business-website-checklist) lists the other basics. For the contact page, a map embed helps people find the room; see the [Google Maps embed guide](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

## Does Online Booking Reduce Missed Appointments?

A study of a medical practice and hospital, cited by Adwave, reported that online scheduling cut unused appointments from 22.7% to 10.3%. The same study reported never-booked appointments falling from 8.6% to 1.6%. These are medical settings, not massage practices, so treat them as a direction rather than a forecast for your own clinic.

Awareness is a separate problem. In a large UK dataset cited by Adwave, 45.11% of patients who tried to book knew their practice offered online booking, but only 15.61% used it. If the button is hard to find, clients will call instead.

Track your own numbers before and after you change the site. Count calls, online bookings and no-shows for four weeks, then repeat the count after the new booking page goes live.

## Should You Check HIPAA and Licensing First?

Whether HIPAA applies depends on how you bill and what health information you collect. This guide does not state that rule, because the sources used here do not cover it. Ask your legal or tax adviser before you collect health details in any online form, and ask each vendor in writing how it handles that data.

Licensing rules also vary. Check your state or regional board for how your license number and title may appear in advertising, then place that wording on the About page and in the footer. If you are in the UK, data protection rules for intake forms are covered in the [GDPR guide for local business websites](/blog/local-business-website-gdpr-compliance).

## How Should You Track Calls and Bookings?

Each tap-to-call link and booking link should log an event in your analytics, according to Adwave's [mobile-first standard](https://adwave.com/resources/mobile-first-website-design) (secondary). Without that event, you cannot tell which page produced the booking.

Call tracking needs care. Google's help page says that if your site uses Dynamic Number Insertion or third-party call tracking, its automated system cannot verify the phone number by scanning the code. Google also says its tag cannot verify a domain if it is installed through a tag manager such as Google Tag Manager, so check the setup steps on the [Google Ads call tracking help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) before you rely on the data.

Google says businesses can [enable 24/7 online booking from their Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/). If you already have a profile, check whether that option applies to your practice.

## Worked Example: What Does Booking Software Cost per Booking?

All inputs below are assumptions except where marked. Use your own figures.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Booking plan | $16 per month (assumption: Acuity starting price from the 2025 table) |
| Bookings per month through the site | 40 (assumption) |
| Average treatment price | $90 (assumption) |
| Software cost per booking | $16 / 40 = $0.40 |
| Monthly booked revenue | 40 x $90 = $3,600 |
| Software as share of booked revenue | $16 / $3,600 = 0.44% |

At 40 bookings, the software adds about 40 cents per booking. If your volume stayed under the 50-booking free allowance listed for GoConverso in the 2025 table, the monthly software cost would be $0 on that plan (assumption: you choose that plan). Card processing fees are separate and are not included here.

## FAQ

### How much does a massage therapist website cost?

Booking tools in the 2025 comparison start between $0 and $16 a month, with some paid plans at $10. Your domain, photos and any designer fees are extra. Check the live pricing page of each tool before you commit.

### Do I need online booking?

It is not required, but a 2025 Zippia statistic cited by Adwave found 67% of customers prefer businesses that offer it. The same source reports that 40% of appointments are booked outside standard business hours. Test it against your own call volume.

### Do I need to be HIPAA compliant?

That depends on how you bill and what health data you collect. Ask a qualified adviser and confirm each vendor's data handling in writing before you use an intake form.

### Can I track calls from my website?

Yes, but check the setup. Google says its automated verification cannot confirm a number that uses Dynamic Number Insertion or third-party call tracking. The [Google Ads call tracking help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) lists the steps.

### Where do I show my license?

On the About page and in the footer, using the wording your state or regional board requires.
