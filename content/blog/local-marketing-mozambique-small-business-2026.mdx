A small business in Mozambique should split its marketing between phone contact, printed material and a small amount of Facebook advertising. The most recent national figures in the sources come from a 2017 survey, which found 30% mobile phone penetration and 10% internet penetration. Most customers are therefore hard to reach with online ads alone. Test each channel with a small budget, count enquiries, and then shift money toward what works.

The sources for this guide are limited. They include a 2017 survey published by [Research ICT Africa](https://researchictafrica.net/research/ict-in-mozambique/) and the services page of a local agency, [Lugela Digital](https://lugela.com/). ## How Many People Can You Reach Online?

The After Access Survey of 2017, covering 16 countries in the Global South, found Mozambique had among the lowest mobile phone and internet penetration rates, at 30% and 10%. The [Research ICT Africa policy paper](https://researchictafrica.net/research/ict-in-mozambique/) reports that smartphone penetration stood at 7%, which helps explain the low internet figure.

Among respondents who were offline, 76% said they did not have a device to access the internet, and 14% said they did not know how to use it. These are survey responses, not a count of the whole population.

These numbers are old, so treat them as a direction rather than a current count. Until newer data for your own city is available, assume that phone ownership is well ahead of internet use.

## Why Do Phones Not Mean Online Customers?

Phones and data are not the same thing. The paper reports that 64% of people without smartphones said smartphones were too expensive, and 78% of those without a mobile phone said they could not afford one.

Customers with basic phones can take a call or read a text, but many will never see a Facebook ad. The same paper shows a gender gap: more than half of men (50%) have mobile phones, against 32% of women. A message that only reaches the Facebook feed may miss a large share of women customers. Calls and in-person contact should sit alongside any online work.

## Which Channels Suit a Local Business?

The table below is a planning aid built from the constraints above. It is not a set of measured results.

| Channel | Reaches | What it needs | How to measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Facebook ads to a phone or WhatsApp number | Urban smartphone users | Smartphone, some data, a clear offer | Enquiries per 1,000 MZN (assumption: track weekly) |
| Printed signs and flyers | Walk-in and offline customers | A shop front or market stall | Visits or calls tagged by flyer code |
| Referrals and calls | Rural and offline customers | Someone to call or ask | Calls logged per week |
| Basic website | Customers who search on a computer or smartphone | Hosting, a page, a contact number | Form or call count |

Rural customers are the group most affected by the access gap. The same survey notes that very few rural households have telecom access, so voice calls, signs and word of mouth will usually matter more there than online ads.

## How Do You Split a Small Test Budget? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions** for planning. No ad prices are used.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly test budget | 5,000 MZN |
| Facebook ads to a WhatsApp or phone number (60%) | 3,000 MZN |
| Printed signs and flyers (25%) | 1,250 MZN |
| Calls and visits follow-up (15%) | 750 MZN |
| Enquiries from Facebook in one month | 10 |
| Test length | 4 weeks |

Cost per enquiry from Facebook is 3,000 divided by 10, which gives 300 MZN. Compare this with the value of one sale to the business. If one sale is worth well above 300 MZN, the channel may be worth keeping. If the cost per sale is higher, cut the Facebook share and test a different offer.

Use the same method for the other channels, and record the channel each enquiry came from. After four weeks, move money toward the channel with the lowest cost per enquiry, and keep a small test running on the others.

## Does Data Cost Affect Video Ads?

The Research ICT Africa paper reports that fierce pricing competition among operators has resulted in low data prices. It ranks Mozambique third among 49 African countries on 1GB data price, above Ghana, Kenya and South Africa. Low prices help customers use data, but the paper also notes that operators have not yet earned significant revenue from data, so the market is still changing.

Short videos use more data than text ads. Keep them short, add captions for viewers with the sound off, and test a file size that loads quickly on older phones.

## How Can Offline Marketing Work Better?

Offline marketing does not need a large budget. Printed signs at a shop front, a clear phone number on every flyer and a short offer that customers can pass on by word of mouth all work without data.

The paper's figures on financial inclusion also matter. Access to formal banking is extremely limited, particularly in rural areas, so cash remains important. Signs should state whether cash is accepted, and the business should ask customers which payment method they prefer before assuming.

Pair offline work with simple follow-up. Call back enquiries within a day, and log the source of each one. ## FAQ

### Is Facebook the best platform in Mozambique?

The sources do not give current platform reach for Mozambique, so no platform can be ranked with confidence. Test Facebook ads to a phone number on a small budget, and compare the cost per enquiry with signs and calls.

### Can offline marketing still work?

Yes. In the 2017 survey, 90% of respondents were offline, so signs, referrals and calls can reach people that online ads cannot.

### Are there verified ad prices for Mozambique?

None could be verified from the sources. Use your own ad account data to estimate costs.

### Should a small business accept mobile payments?

Ask customers first. The sources show that formal financial access is limited, particularly in rural areas, so cash remains important. Show every accepted payment method on your signs.
