The best Google Ads keywords for a day spa are service-led, local searches such as "massage near me", "facial [city]" and "couples massage", run on phrase or exact match with negatives that block hot tub, job and training searches. No source publishes a spa-only click price. The nearest benchmark, LocaliQ's 2026 Beauty and Personal Care average, is $4.62 per click and $39.25 per lead.

This guide groups spa keywords by intent, lists negatives and shows a budget example. The keyword examples are starting ideas to check in Google Ads Keyword Planner, not search-volume claims. The budget example is labelled as an assumption.

## Which Keyword Groups Should a Spa Start With?

Group keywords by what the searcher wants, because each group needs its own ad text and landing page.

| Group | Example keywords | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core services | "massage near me", "facial near me", "day spa [city]" | Highest booking intent |
| Specific treatments | "deep tissue massage", "hydrafacial", "body wrap" | Match the page to the treatment |
| Occasions | "couples massage", "bridal spa day", "spa day for two" | Higher ticket, plan ahead |
| Gifts | "spa gift certificate", "spa gift card" | Seasonal peaks around holidays |
| Brand | [your spa name] | Protects your name |

Swap in your city or neighborhood as a second pass. Only list treatments you actually offer, and check the estimated bid for each in Keyword Planner. Google says the estimated top of page bid is based on Quality Score and competition and is not guaranteed.

## Which Match Types Should a Spa Use?

Google's help describes broad match as the default and most comprehensive, phrase match as searches that include your keyword's meaning, and exact match as the most control with the fewest searches. It says it is critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match.

For a small budget, start with phrase and exact on your core and treatment groups. Add broad match later if you have conversion data and use Smart Bidding. That sequence is a recommendation, not a Google rule.

## What Should a Spa Block With Negative Keywords?

The word "spa" is shared with hot tubs and pool equipment, so expect irrelevant searches. Google says negative keywords do not match close variants, so add singular and plural forms yourself.

Starting negatives, to refine from your search terms report:

- Equipment: `hot tub`, `spa tub`, `pool`, `parts`, `cover`, `filter`
- Jobs and training: `jobs`, `hiring`, `salary`, `school`, `course`, `license`
- Do-it-yourself: `diy`, `at home`, `recipe`
- Price shoppers, if you do not discount: `free`, `cheap`

Read the search terms report weekly for the first month, since new unwanted phrases will appear.

## How Much Does a Click or a Lead Cost?

LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks list Beauty and Personal Care at $4.62 per click, a 6.75% click-through rate, a 10.35% conversion rate and $39.25 per lead. For all industries the figures are $5.42 per click, 6.64% and $66.69 per lead. Neither list has a spa row, so your account will give the true figure within a few weeks.

Spa treatments usually cost more than a haircut, so the value of a booking can be higher. That makes lead quality, not only lead cost, the number to watch. ## FAQ

### What is the average CPC for spa keywords?

No spa-only figure was found. LocaliQ's 2026 Beauty and Personal Care average is $4.62 per click, the nearest category. Check your own estimates in Keyword Planner.

### Should I bid on the word "spa" alone?

Usually not. It is broad and attracts hot tub and equipment searches. Pair it with a service, like "day spa" or "massage", and add negatives.

### How much should a spa spend on Google Ads?

No standard figure is published. Work from the number of bookings you want, your booking rate and cost per lead, as in the example above.

### Do gift certificates work as a keyword group?

They can, especially before holidays, but no source here gives their performance. Track them as a separate group and compare cost per sale.
