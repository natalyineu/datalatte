Google Ads works well for pet boarding because people search for boarding right when they are planning a trip, so you can reach them at the moment they need a kennel. The recipe: bid on specific local keywords like "dog boarding near me", raise bids ahead of holidays and school breaks, show real availability in your ads, and track calls and bookings so you know what is working. Costs vary by city, so start small and scale only what books.

## Why Most Pet Boarding Ads Miss the Mark

Peak season for boarding follows travel: summer vacations, school breaks, holiday weekends and sudden trips. Many owners switch on ads in a rush, bid on broad terms like "pet services", send clicks to a generic homepage, and never check which searches actually bring bookings. The fixes below are mostly about being specific and about timing.

## Step 1: Target Travel-Driven Searches

Boarding demand is tied to trip planning, so build keywords around that intent rather than pet emergencies alone.

<Callout type="tip">Use phrase match and exact match first. Think about when travelers search: weeks ahead for holidays, but also last minute when plans change.</Callout>

Starter keyword ideas (replace with your city or neighborhood):
- "dog boarding near me"
- "pet boarding [City]"
- "last minute dog boarding [City]"
- "holiday pet boarding [City]"
- "cat boarding [City]" (if you offer it)

Create separate ad groups for dogs and cats, and for any special services such as luxury suites or medical boarding, so each ad matches the search. Our guides on [Google Ads for dog daycare](/blog/google-ads-for-dog-daycare) and [Google Ads for pet groomers](/blog/google-ads-for-pet-groomers-complete-2026-guide) show how nearby pet categories structure keywords.

## Step 2: Set a Budget That Fills Kennels

Budget depends on your city, how competitive "boarding" keywords are, and how many kennels you need to fill. Rather than copying a number, work backwards:

1. What is a booking worth to you (average stay length times nightly rate)?
2. How many extra bookings do you need in peak weeks?
3. What cost per booking can you afford while staying profitable?

Use Google's Keyword Planner to see estimated costs per click for your area, then set a daily budget that gets at least a few clicks a day. Many small facilities start modestly and raise spend only in the weeks before peak periods.

<Callout type="warning">Avoid broad terms like "pet services". They attract grooming, sitting and vet searches that will never book a kennel.</Callout>

A sensible split to begin with:
- Most of the budget on exact and phrase match boarding keywords.
- Search network only at first, which keeps you on actual searches.
- A smaller share for remarketing to website visitors who did not book.

## Step 3: Write Ads That Show Availability

Ads that mention real availability, specific services and a clear action tend to earn better clicks. Never claim scarcity you do not have.

**Example ad**
```
Headline: Dog Boarding in [City] | Book Your Dates
Description: Spaces open for upcoming holidays. Daily updates,
staff on site, tours available. Call or book online today.
```

**Holiday example**
```
Headline: Holiday Pet Boarding [City] | Reserve Early
Description: Holiday dates fill up. Secure your pet's stay with
daily photo updates. Check availability online.
```

Add call, location and sitelink extensions so people can phone, get directions or jump straight to your booking page. Make sure your landing page shows prices or a clear "from" rate, vaccination requirements, and a simple way to request dates.

## Step 4: Seasonal Bidding for High-Demand Periods

Plan bids around your calendar. Increase budgets and bids a few weeks before major travel periods such as summer holidays, Thanksgiving and Christmas, and ease off during quiet stretches. In Google Ads you can use ad schedules and bid adjustments for day of week and time of day, and you can pause or reduce campaigns for dates when you are already full.

<Callout type="coffee">When your kennels are full for a date range, pause those ads. Paying for clicks you cannot serve is the fastest way to waste budget.</Callout>

## Step 5: Track Calls and Bookings

Many boarding enquiries arrive by phone. Set up call extensions and call tracking, and track form submissions and booking-page visits as conversions. Our guide to [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) explains the setup. Without this, you cannot tell whether a campaign is working. Also make sure your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) is complete, since many searchers check reviews and photos before calling.

## Step 6: Add Negative Keywords Early

Review the search terms report every week. Common negatives for boarding include: free, cheap, jobs, training, grooming, volunteer, adopt, shelter, rescue, and DIY. Add "daycare" or "sitting" only if you do not offer those services. See our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) for a fuller list.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should I spend on Google Ads for my pet boarding facility?**
A: It depends on your city and competition. A practical approach is to start with a modest daily budget, measure cost per booking over a few weeks, then increase spend ahead of peak periods if that cost is profitable. Use Keyword Planner for local click cost estimates.

**Q: Can I run Google Ads myself, or do I need an agency?**
A: You can, if you can commit time each week to review search terms, adjust bids, test ad copy and add negative keywords. If you cannot, hire someone who understands local service businesses.

**Q: How long until I see bookings from Google Ads?**
A: Often within the first few weeks, but allow a couple of weeks for the system to learn before judging. Avoid changing budgets and keywords every few days. If there are no enquiries after several weeks, check keywords, landing page and call tracking.

**Q: Is Google Ads better than Facebook Ads for pet boarding?**
A: Often yes as a first step, because Google reaches people actively searching for boarding. Meta ads are better for awareness and retargeting. Our [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads comparison](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins) explains when to use each.

**Q: What if I have a small facility with only a few kennels?**
A: Keep the radius tight, focus on a handful of specific keywords, and set a small daily budget. Your aim is to fill a limited number of kennels, not to maximize clicks. A well-tuned Google Business Profile adds free visibility.
