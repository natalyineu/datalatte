Here is a number to anchor on. If a click costs you $3.50, 8 in 100 clicks become an enquiry, and 35 in 100 enquiries book a stay, a $800 month buys about 6 bookings at roughly $125 each. Those three inputs are my assumptions, not benchmarks, and changing any one of them swings the answer between about $55 and $400. That is the real picture of boarding ads: a funnel with three dials, plus a calendar that makes the same ad worth ten times more in December than in February.

This guide is for boarding kennels, pet hotels and home-based boarders in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers keywords, ad copy, seasonal bidding, call tracking and the math. Dollar figures are US-based; use your own local prices for pounds or Australian and Canadian dollars.

## What Benchmarks Exist (and Where They Run Out)

There is no public Google Ads benchmark for pet boarding specifically, and you should distrust anyone quoting one. The closest data is for veterinary advertising. A July 2026 roundup by [Webtonic on veterinary Google Ads](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/veterinary-google-ads-statistics) (updated October 2026) lists, from named third parties:

- New patient and general vet keywords: CPC of $2.00 to $4.50 (attributed to RYN Digital and CUFinder).
- Emergency vet keywords: $5.00 to $7.00.
- Vaccines and wellness: $1.50 to $3.00.
- Brand-name searches: $0.50 to $1.50.

Webtonic itself notes that most of its benchmarks come from 2023 to 2025, that several headline ranges have no named source, and that its own CTR history is inconsistent. So treat this as one adjacent industry's rough shape: broad service terms cost a few dollars, emergencies cost more, your own name costs the least. Boarding keywords sit somewhere in that range in most markets, but the only number that counts is the one in your own account.

For pricing context, Care.com's [dog boarding cost guide](https://www.care.com/c/how-much-does-dog-boarding-cost/) (updated August 2026) says US dog boarding averages $40 to $60 a night, with traditional kennels at $40 to $60 and veterinary boarding at $60 to $75. It does not cite a source for those rates, so treat them as a consumer-guide estimate. Use your own rate card in the math below.

## Why Most Boarding Campaigns Waste Money

Boarding demand follows travel: summer, school breaks, Thanksgiving, Christmas, long weekends and last-minute trips. Many owners switch ads on in a panic, bid on broad terms like "pet services", send clicks to a homepage and never look at the search terms report. Three fixes cover most of the damage: specific keywords, a calendar-driven budget and real conversion tracking.

## Step 1: Keywords Built Around Trips

Google's [match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) defines exact match as the most control with the least reach, phrase match as in-between, and broad match as the default with the widest reach. It also says it is critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match. With a small boarding budget and no conversion history, start with phrase and exact.

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core dog boarding | "dog boarding near me", [dog boarding your town], "dog kennel your town" | Phrase + exact |
| Holiday and dates | "holiday dog boarding your town", "christmas dog boarding", "thanksgiving pet boarding" | Phrase |
| Last minute | "last minute dog boarding", "emergency pet boarding your town" | Phrase + exact |
| Cats | "cat boarding your town", "cattery near me" | Phrase + exact |
| Special care | "senior dog boarding", "boarding for anxious dogs", "dog boarding with vet care" | Phrase |
| Your brand | [your business name] | Exact |

Use "cattery" and "kennel" for UK and Australian searchers, where those words are more common. Keep each ad group to a few tightly related keywords so the ad can echo the search.

### Negative keywords

Add on day one: `jobs`, `career`, `training`, `grooming`, `volunteer`, `adopt`, `shelter`, `rescue`, `diy`, `how to`, `course`, `franchise`, `for sale`. Add `sitter`, `daycare` or `walker` only if you do not offer those services. Then read the search terms report weekly. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) shows how to build lists.

## Step 2: Campaign Setup

- **Campaign type:** Search only, with Display and the search partner network off at first. Keep one campaign per location.
- **Location:** A radius around your facility based on where your current clients live. Pull your last 50 bookings and check their postcodes or zip codes. People often travel further for boarding than for grooming, so your radius may be wider than you expect.
- **Keyword research:** Google's [Keyword Planner help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243) says it gives search volume estimates and average bid estimates, refreshed with seasonality, and that forecasts are estimates affected by your bid, budget, ad quality and targeting. Use it to compare ideas, not to promise results.

## Step 3: Ads That Match the Search

Google's [responsive search ad rules](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791) allow up to 15 headlines of 30 characters and up to 4 descriptions of 90 characters. Fill them. Examples that fit:

- Headlines: `Dog Boarding in [Town]`, `Holiday Spots Now Open`, `Daily Photo Updates`, `Staff On Site Overnight`, `Book a Free Tour`
- Descriptions: `Small kennel, daily updates and a vet nearby. Check dates and book online today.`

Never claim scarcity you do not have. "Holiday dates fill up" is fine when it is true and ruinous when it is not, because your own regulars will notice.

Add a call asset, a location asset linked to your Business Profile, and sitelinks (Prices, Vaccination Rules, Book a Tour, Reviews). Send clicks to a dedicated landing page that shows your nightly rate or a "from" price, vaccination requirements, what a day looks like, three real photos, reviews and a date request form. The [landing page best practices guide](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) covers the layout.

## Step 4: A Calendar-Driven Budget

Boarding is seasonal, so your ad plan should be too. This part is our advice, not a Google rule:

1. List your 5 to 8 peak windows (summer break, Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year, school half terms, Easter, long weekends).
2. Start ramping spend 4 to 6 weeks before each window, because owners book ahead.
3. Pause or cut ads for dates you are full. Paying for clicks you cannot serve is pure waste.
4. In quiet weeks, run a lower daily budget on exact match and brand only.

Google's [ad schedule help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6372644) describes ad schedule bid adjustments as a way to raise or lower bids on specific days and times, with your average daily budget staying the same. Use them to match your phone hours, so you bid up when someone can pick up. Check the current interface, because bidding strategy affects which adjustments apply.

## Step 5: Track Calls and Bookings

Many boarding enquiries arrive by phone, so call tracking is the difference between knowing and guessing. Google's [website call conversions help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) says you set a minimum call duration so only longer calls count as conversions, and that a forwarding number can replace your number on your site. It also warns that the default tag tracks one number per page and that Google Tag Manager is not recommended if you use dynamic number insertion or third-party call tracking. See our conversion tracking setup guide and call tracking for local business.

Set up three conversions: ad call, landing page call and date-request form. Then close the loop manually. When a stay is booked, note where it came from. Ad platforms count enquiries; only your booking system knows what paid.

## The Math

Take an $800 peak-season month. Every row below is an **assumption** for illustration, not a benchmark. The CPC range is chosen to sit inside the adjacent vet figures above, and the booking value is a plain stay of 4 nights at $50 (inside Care.com's $40 to $60 range).

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $800 | $800 | $800 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $5.00 | $3.50 | $2.50 |
| Clicks | 160 | 229 | 320 |
| Click to enquiry (assumption) | 5% | 8% | 10% |
| Enquiries | 8 | 18 | 32 |
| Enquiry to booking (assumption) | 25% | 35% | 45% |
| Bookings | 2 | 6.4 | 14.4 |
| Ad cost per booking | $400 | $125 | $56 |
| Revenue per booking (4 nights x $50, assumption) | $200 | $200 | $200 |
| Revenue from the month's bookings | $400 | $1,280 | $2,880 |

How to read it: in the Tough case you spend $800 to earn $400 of first-stay revenue, which loses money unless those clients return. In the Base case you roughly book your spend back 1.6 times over on first stays alone. Revenue is not profit, so subtract food, staff and laundry.

Two takeaways:

- **Break-even click price** = revenue per booking x enquiry rate x booking rate. In the Base case that is $200 x 0.08 x 0.35 = $5.60. Bid above it on average and you lose money on first stays.
- **Holiday stays change everything.** A 7-night stay at an assumed $60 a night is $420, which more than doubles your break-even click price to about $11.76 at the same rates. That is the maths behind bidding harder before Christmas.

Also count repeat business. A client who boards twice a year is worth more than their first booking, but use your own retention data, not a guess.

## Optimise Weekly

1. Read the search terms report and add negatives.
2. Pause keywords with more than about twice your target cost per enquiry and no enquiries.
3. Rotate in one new headline every two weeks.
4. Check calls by hour and match your ad schedule to when someone answers.
5. Raise the budget by 20% or so only on campaigns that hit your target cost per booking.

Pair ads with a complete [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist), since owners check reviews and photos before they call. For neighbouring pet services, see [Google Ads for dog daycare](/blog/google-ads-for-dog-daycare) and Google Ads for pet groomers.

## Do This Today

1. Write down your rate card, average stay length and the most you will pay for one new booking. Calculate your break-even CPC with the formula above.
2. List your next 12 months of peak windows and mark when each ramp should start (4 to 6 weeks before).
3. Build one Search campaign for one location with the six ad groups above, phrase and exact match, and the negative list.
4. Make a dedicated landing page with price, vaccination rules, photos, reviews and a date request form, then set up call and form conversions before switching ads on.
5. Set a rule that every enquiry gets a reply within an hour, and book a weekly 20-minute slot for the search terms report.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a pet boarding facility spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official figure. Work backwards from your break-even click price and the number of bookings you want in peak weeks. At an assumed $3.50 click and the funnel above, $800 buys about 229 clicks, but your own numbers will differ.

**Q: Can I run Google Ads myself, or do I need an agency?**
A: You can, if you commit time each week to the search terms report, ad copy tests and call tracking. If you cannot, hire someone who understands local service businesses and who shows you your cost per booking, not just clicks.

**Q: How long until I see bookings?**
A: Enquiries can come within days. Give it four to six weeks, my rule of thumb rather than a Google figure, before judging, and start well before a peak window rather than in it.

**Q: Is Google Ads better than Facebook ads for boarding?**
A: Google reaches people already searching for a kennel, so it is usually the first channel to test. Meta suits reminders and retargeting. See our [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads comparison](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins).

**Q: What if I only have a few kennels?**
A: Keep the radius tight, use a handful of exact and phrase keywords, and pause ads when dates are full. Your goal is full kennels, not maximum clicks.
