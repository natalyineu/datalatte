A Charlotte hair salon ranks in Google Maps by getting into the local pack, which [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today) defines as the top three business listings in local search results. ## What decides who makes the Charlotte map pack?

No Charlotte-specific ranking data is published in the sources used here, so treat the national figures below as directional. A 2025 multi-location benchmark, as cited by [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit), reports that Google Business Profile signals make up 32% of local pack ranking weight. In a BrightLocal survey covered by [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), 36% of SEOs rated GBP as the most important local pack ranking factor in 2021, up from 23% in 2013.

Adwave also states that the primary GBP category is the number one ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, though it does not name the analysis behind that claim. Read that as a vendor view. The practical takeaway is that the profile itself drives much of the result, so it is the first place to work.

## Which Google Business Profile settings matter most?

Work through the profile in this order:

1. Check your primary category against the top three to five businesses in local map results for your main service search, as [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) recommends. Use the category they use if it fits your services.
2. Write a business description of up to 750 characters, which [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) states Google allows. Name your service, neighbourhood and specialty in plain sentences.
3. Upload at least 15 high-quality photos, the figure [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) recommends. Include colour work, the interior and the stylists.
4. Publish one post a week. Google shows a post publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, per [Google's help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), so check the status after publishing.
5. Keep hours, phone number and address accurate. Adwave says they should be 100% accurate.
6. Seed the Q&A section. Google launched the feature in 2017, and [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a) reports that AI-driven answers draw on profile data, website content and reviews.

## How do reviews and citations affect the pack?

Google states that reviews appear next to a business's profile in Maps and Search, as noted in its [review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122). That makes review count and content visible to every searcher, not just the algorithm.

A third-party analysis of 2 million GBP pages, reported by [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business), found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. Read the threshold as a correlation, not a guarantee. Reviews that mention specific services, such as balayage or colour correction, give the profile more descriptive text to match.

Citations matter for consistency. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses) says your name, address and phone must be exactly the same on your website, your GBP, and directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places. The same guide from [Adwave's map pack beginner page](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) notes that even punctuation counts, including "St." versus "Street." Fix existing listings before adding new ones.

## Worked example: how long will review growth take?

The calculation below uses assumed inputs. Replace them with your own counts.

Assumed current review count: 60 (assumption). Target: 200, the mark cited above. Assumed new reviews per month: 6 (assumption). Gap: 200 minus 60 equals 140 reviews. Time to target: 140 divided by 6 equals about 23.3 months.

If the salon raises the pace to 12 reviews a month (assumption), the gap closes in about 11.7 months. The lesson is that pace drives timing, so a simple request after each visit matters more than a one-off campaign. ## Where do Apple Maps, Reserve with Google and Google Ads fit?

Apple Business Connect is worth checking. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring) reports that in a maps-only comparison that excludes web search, Apple has a 7% share and is growing. That page also cites Apple Maps Actions for connecting booking services and Profile Views for tracking. Its Google-versus-Apple usage split has no named source, so do not rely on it.

Booking inside search is a separate channel. [Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing) says Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds. Set it up if your booking software supports it.

Paid search can sit alongside the profile. Google's [location asset guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en) says Ads location data comes directly from Google Maps and GBP, so corrections made there flow into linked accounts. Multi-location salons can link up to 10 locations. For paid setup, see the [complete Google Ads guide for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide) and the [keyword list for hair salons](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026).

## Which metrics show the map pack is working?

[Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent) names six local search metrics worth tracking: GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time. Record a baseline before you change anything, then review the same figures monthly.

Track your rank for the core keywords by checking the map pack in a private browser window, which gives a free, if rough, reading. ## FAQ

### Is Google Business Profile free for a Charlotte salon?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up on Google Search and Maps, as stated on its [Business Profile page](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/).

### How many reviews does a salon need to appear in the local pack?

No fixed number is guaranteed. A 2-million-profile analysis reported by [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business) found businesses with over 200 reviews are significantly more likely to appear. Use it as a target, not a promise.

### Can a salon manage Google Business Profile messages from a computer?

Yes. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on) says GBP messages can be managed from the dashboard on desktop, not only in the Maps mobile app.

### Should a salon rely on Apple Maps as well as Google?

Adwave reports that Apple Business Connect is growing in a maps-only comparison, so claiming the listing is a low-cost step. Keep its details identical to your Google profile and your other directories.
