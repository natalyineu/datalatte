A general contractor gets the most from three things: a complete Google Business Profile set up as a service-area business, a steady flow of recent reviews, and a small paid test on Google Search or Local Services Ads. Paid clicks are not cheap. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks show $8.33 per click and $90.92 per lead for Home and Home Improvement, so test with a fixed budget before scaling.

This guide covers residential and light commercial contractors in the US. Every figure has a source at the end. Examples built on guesses are labelled as assumptions.

## How Much Do Google Ads Cost a General Contractor?

LocaliQ does not publish a category called general contractors. The closest is Home and Home Improvement: $8.33 per click, 6.47% click-through rate, 8.05% conversion rate and $90.92 per lead. The all-industry averages are $5.42 per click and $66.69 per lead, so contractors pay more than the typical advertiser.

A lead here means a form fill or call, not a signed contract. Many of those leads will be small jobs, wrong areas or price shoppers. Your own account data after one or two months is a better guide than any benchmark. For keyword ideas, see [best Google Ads keywords for contractors](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-contractors-in-2026).

## Can General Contractors Use Local Services Ads?

Yes. Google's Local Services Ads help page lists "General contractor" among the home service categories, next to kitchen remodeling, bathroom remodeling, roofers and painters. To go live you submit a license, or business registration if no license is required, and complete billing and a budget. Some categories also need reviews first.

Google also offers a Google Verified badge for businesses that pass its screening. The help page does not list the exact checks, so read Google's screening article before you apply. What a lead costs in your city is not published on those pages, and it varies by area. Compare it with your Search campaign CPL.

## How Should a Contractor Set Up a Google Business Profile?

If customers never visit an office, set up a service-area profile. Google's guidelines say service-area businesses should hide the business address from customers, that service areas should generally stay within about two hours' driving time of your base, and that virtual offices are not eligible unless staffed during business hours.

Use your real business name, not keywords or a tagline. Google says taglines, phone numbers and location details do not belong in the name. Descriptions cannot contain links. Our [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) walks through the remaining fields, and [Business Profile reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) covers how to ask.

## How Many Reviews Does a Contractor Need?

BrightLocal's 2026 survey found that 68% of consumers will only use a business rated 4 stars or higher, 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and 74% only consider reviews from the last three months. 89% expect owners to reply to reviews.

Ask every finished job for a review with a Google link or QR code. Google allows this, but it prohibits offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews. The FTC's 2024 rule also bans buying or selling fake reviews. Reply to each review with specifics, since half of consumers say they are unlikely to choose a business that uses templated responses.

## What Do Referrals and Lead Marketplaces Add?

No published source we could verify gives conversion rates for contractor referrals, so treat them as a channel to test, not a promise. Past clients, architects and suppliers who already know your work are the natural source. A short message after project completion that asks for a name of one person who may need work is enough.

Marketplaces charge for leads that several contractors may receive at once. Compare the economics in [Angi vs HomeAdvisor for contractors](/blog/angi-ads-vs-homeadvisor-contractors) and [Houzz ads for contractors](/blog/houzz-ads-home-services-contractors). Track each source separately, or you will not know which one paid.

## FAQ

### How much should a general contractor spend on marketing?

No source gives a reliable percentage for contractors. Start from the cost per signed job you can afford. At LocaliQ's $90.92 average cost per lead, $1,500 a month buys roughly 16 leads on average, so size the budget to your sales capacity.

### Are Local Services Ads better than Google Search ads?

They are different. Local Services Ads list a Google Verified badge and are available for the General contractor category. Search ads give more control over keywords and landing pages. Many contractors run both and compare cost per signed job.

### Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

A profile gets you found on maps, but a site gives proof of work, service pages and a place to track form fills. Our [guide to Google Ads for contractors](/blog/google-ads-for-contractors) shows how landing pages affect results.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?

No. Google lists incentives for reviews as prohibited content, and the FTC says you cannot condition an incentive on a review being positive.
