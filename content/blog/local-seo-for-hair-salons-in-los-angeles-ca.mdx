A hair salon in Los Angeles ranks in the Google Maps local pack by keeping its Google Business Profile (GBP) complete, accurate, and active. The local pack is the top three business listings in local search results, as [Adwave defines it](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today), and those listings are [pulled directly from their GBP entries](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business). Google describes a Business Profile as [free to set up](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/). Profile work comes first, with reviews, citations, and your website supporting it.

## Why does the Google Business Profile decide map pack placement?

Adwave reports that GBP signals were [reported to represent 32% of local pack ranking weight in 2025](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit), citing a multi-location benchmark. Treat that as a secondary figure, since the underlying study is not named on the page. The same Adwave source says the primary category is the top ranking factor for entering the local pack, though it also does not name its analysis.

Your profile is also what customers see first. Google states that reviews [show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en). Booksy says that Reserve with Google lets clients [book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), which turns a map view into a booking.

New profiles can move quickly. Adwave's own estimate is that a business [may start ranking for its own name in the Map Pack a few weeks after verification](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). That is a vendor estimate, not a guarantee, but it means the first month of profile work is worth doing carefully.

## How should a salon fill out each profile field?

Work through these steps in order:

1. Choose the primary category by checking the [top 3-5 businesses in local map results](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) for your main service search, and use the category they use.
2. Write a description of up to 750 characters, as [Google Business Profile allows 750 characters](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Lead with your service and Los Angeles neighbourhood, and avoid keyword stuffing.
3. Make your hours, phone number, and address [100% accurate](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers).
4. Upload at least [15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), such as colour work, stylists at work, and the interior.
5. Publish at least one post a week. Posts appear publicly only when their status is [Live](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), so check the status after you publish.
6. Seed the Q&A section with the questions you hear most. Google [launched Q&A in Maps in 2017](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a), and it remains a place where customers look for answers.

If you work from home or travel to clients, you can hide your street address and [become a Service Area Business](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack).

## Which keywords should a Los Angeles salon target?

Build keywords from the service, the city, and the neighbourhood, such as "balayage Los Angeles" on a dedicated service page. The [keyword list for hair salons](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026) gives a starting set for paid search. This article does not list search volumes, so check current figures in a keyword tool before you commit to a set.

Place neighbourhood names in your title tags, service pages, and GBP description. Customers in a city as large as Los Angeles often search by area, so a salon in one neighbourhood should name that area rather than only the city. Keep each page focused on one service so the content matches the search.

For the paid side of search, the [complete Google Ads guide for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide) covers campaign setup. Use the same service and location wording in your ads and your profile so the two match.

## How many reviews and photos does a salon need?

Adwave reports a third-party analysis by Blogging Wizard of 2 million Google Business Profile pages. It found that [businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business). This is a correlation from a secondary source, so use 200 as a target to work toward rather than a guaranteed result.

Review growth depends on how often you ask. A steady pace beats a burst followed by silence, so set up a follow-up message after each appointment. A market comparison can show what review counts look like locally. The [Denver hair salon review benchmarks](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026) show one example of how counts spread across a city.

Ask customers to name the service they booked. Their wording gives prospects the same language they search with, and it makes your replies more specific when you respond.

## Which citations must match your name, address, and phone number?

Citations are mentions of your business name, address, and phone number (NAP) on other sites. Adwave states that [the NAP must be exactly the same](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses) on your website, your GBP, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places. It also says the match must include [punctuation and whether "St." or "Street" is used](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack).

Start with an audit of existing listings before you add new ones. Adding more directories with mismatched details creates more conflicts to fix later. Apple Business Connect is another listing to claim. Adwave notes that it reports [Profile Views](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), which counts how many people saw your business card in Apple Maps.

If you run several locations or Google Ads, note that [location asset data is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en). Corrections you make there flow into linked Google Ads accounts. An embedded map on your contact page is also a [simple addition to your website](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

## What does a review-request plan look like in practice?

The worked example below uses assumption-labelled inputs for a single salon. Replace them with your own appointment numbers.

| Input or result | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Completed appointments per month | 80 | assumption |
| Clients who leave a review after one follow-up | 25% | assumption |
| Reviews gained per month | 20 | calculated (80 x 25%) |
| Months to reach 200 reviews from zero | 10 | calculated (200 / 20) |
| Clients who leave a review after an improved follow-up | 40% | assumption |
| Reviews gained per month at 40% | 32 | calculated (80 x 40%) |
| Months to reach 200 reviews at 40% | 6.25 | calculated (200 / 32) |

At 20 reviews a month, the salon adds roughly five reviews a week, so a weekly review of the follow-up process is worth scheduling. Improving the follow-up message cuts the time to 200 reviews from ten months to about six. The result depends on your own numbers, so track the actual response rate for a month before you plan around it.

## How do you track whether the profile is working?

Adwave lists six local search metrics it considers useful: [GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent). Record these each month so you can see whether photos, posts, and reviews are moving the numbers.

Check your rankings by searching your main keywords yourself, ideally in a private browser window, and noting where your salon appears. Compare the results with the metrics above rather than relying on one signal alone.

If you want an outside view of where your profile stands, a [free audit](/free-audit) can show the gaps, and the [local SEO service](/services/local-seo) covers ongoing work on the profile, citations, and reviews.

## FAQ

### Does a Google Business Profile cost anything?

Google describes its Business Profile as [free to set up](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) on Search and Maps. You can create and manage it without paying for the listing itself.

### How many photos should a salon upload?

Adwave recommends [at least 15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Another Adwave guide suggests [at least 10](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), so 15 is a practical target for a salon with a varied set of work to show.

### How soon can a new salon rank for its own name?

Adwave estimates that you may start ranking for your own name [a few weeks after verifying your profile](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). This is the vendor's estimate and timing varies.

### Do Google Business Profile posts appear right away?

Not always. Google reviews each post, and a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is [Live](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en). Check the status after you publish, and keep a weekly posting schedule.

### Can a salon that works from home hide its address?

Yes. The profile lets you [hide your physical street address](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), and the business is then designated a Service Area Business.
