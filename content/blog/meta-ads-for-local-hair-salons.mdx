The closest published benchmarks are Facebook cost-per-click figures of about $0.70 for traffic campaigns and about $1.92 for lead campaigns, both across all industries rather than beauty alone. Treat them as a starting point. Before you spend, work out what one booked client is worth and whether the budget covers it. ## What does a Meta ad cost per click and per lead for a salon?

No salon-specific Meta cost benchmark appears in the sources reviewed for this guide, so start from the all-industry averages. AdWave, citing WordStream and LocalIQ, puts the average cost per click for Facebook traffic campaigns at around $0.70 and for leads campaigns at around $1.92. Lead campaigns show the higher click cost because they ask people to share contact details, not just visit a page.

Use these numbers as a sanity check, not a budget promise. After the first two weeks, compare your own Ads Manager cost per click and cost per lead with the benchmark. If your salon pays far more per lead, rerun the break-even table below with your real figure before you scale spend.

## Which clients should a salon target on Meta?

Bookeo advises salons to target women aged 21 to 55 who live nearby and show interest in hair care and beauty. Its article also says consumers aged 45 to 54 make up 21.9% of the market, and that consumers aged 55 and older account for 30% of market share and spend the most on hair care services. The article does not cite a source for the 30% figure, so treat it as the vendor's claim.

Booksy gives a seasonal example: a "Bridal Styling" ad shown to newly engaged women within a 10-mile radius of the salon. That idea works best when your service menu includes bridal packages with a clear booking step. Booksy's example is a template, not a result, so test the radius against where your existing clients actually live.

## How do you check whether a Meta campaign pays back?

Break-even tells you when revenue covers total costs. Start with the ad spend, then work out how many leads become bookings and what each booking brings in. The table below uses the $1.92 benchmark, which is an all-industry figure. Every input marked assumption should be replaced with your own salon data.

| Line | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $300 (assumption) |
| Cost per lead | $1.92 (AdWave benchmark, all industries) |
| Leads | 156 (300 divided by 1.92) |
| Share of leads that book | 1 in 3 (assumption) |
| Bookings | 52 |
| Cost per booking | $5.77 |
| First-visit ticket | $90 (assumption) |
| First-visit revenue | $4,680 |
| Gross margin | 60% (assumption) |
| Break-even return on ad spend | 1.67:1 (1 divided by 0.60) |
| Return on ad spend at 1 in 3 | 15.6:1 |

Return on ad spend depends on margin. AdWave gives an example where a high-margin software company might thrive on 3:1, while a low-margin e-commerce store might need 10:1 just to break even. A salon's margin sits between those cases, so check it against your own price list.

At a lower conversion rate, the result changes quickly. If only 1 in 10 leads books (assumption), you get 16 bookings, $1,440 in first-visit revenue and a return of 4.8:1. Under these inputs the campaign clears break-even even on the first visit alone, which is why the true lead cost matters more than the headline number.

Cost lists should also include more than ad spend. A loyalty-program cost guide from Loopy Loyalty recommends including software, rewards, staff time, setup, promotion and tools before working out the break-even point. Apply the same list to a salon campaign, including creative time and front-desk follow-up.

## What creative should a salon test first?

Booksy says high-impact "Before and After" videos capture attention in the first two seconds, which is the window a salon ad has to earn a view. Its guide also recommends 15-second clips that explain at-home hair maintenance or how to use a specific styling tool. Both are vendor recommendations rather than measured results, so test them against your own cost per lead.

One practical approach is to run two short videos with the same offer and the same audience for two weeks. Keep the booking link identical so the only variable is the creative. If you work with local creators for extra reach, the guide on [influencer marketing for hair salons](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-hair-salons-how-to-get-free-bookings-from-local-creator) covers how to set up those partnerships.

## What should happen after the click?

A paid lead is only useful if the booking step is fast. Booksy states that Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds. Set that up alongside your ad campaign, and check how the listing works with your existing [Google Business Profile posts](/blog/hair-salon-marketing-google-my-business-posts).

Reviews and follow-up matter too. Booksy says its software automatically prompts clients to leave a review after an appointment, and its guide claims over 90% of consumers check reviews before trying a new stylist, without a third-party source. On email, the Booksy page says email returns an average of £38 for every £1 spent in the beauty industry, while the Brenton Way guide cites Litmus and Constant Contact benchmarks of $36 to $42 returned per $1. Both are useful reference points, but neither is a promise for your salon. ## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Meta ads?

The sources reviewed for this guide do not give a Meta budget range for salons. The worked example uses $300 per month as an assumption. Set your budget by running the break-even table with your own lead cost, booking rate and margin.

### Is $1.92 per lead a realistic target for a salon?

It is an all-industry average from AdWave, citing WordStream and LocalIQ. Salon results depend on the offer, the audience and the booking path, so compare it with your own Ads Manager data after the first few weeks.

### Does Meta or Google work better for salon ads?

Bookeo argues that Facebook exposure can come before a client searches for a salon, but the conversion figure it gives has no source. Google ads reach people already searching, so the two channels do different jobs. For a comparison of paid search for salons, see the [Google Ads service page](/services/google-ads).

### What is the first number to check?

Cost per booked client. It shows whether the ad budget covers the cost of each appointment, which is the figure the break-even table is built to test.
