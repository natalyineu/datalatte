Google Ads can bring more customers into a cafe if you run a tightly local campaign: target people searching "coffee near me" or "laptop friendly cafe" within a short radius, set a modest daily budget, and send clicks to a page with your address, hours and menu. It is not the cheapest channel for cafes, so it works best for specific goals such as opening week, new locations or weekday gaps.

This guide shows which campaign types suit a cafe, how to target remote workers versus coffee lovers, realistic budgets, and the mistakes that waste money.

## Should a cafe use Google Ads at all?

Before paying for clicks, check the free basics:

- Your Google Business Profile is complete, accurate and active. For many cafes this brings most of the "near me" traffic at no cost. See our [coffee shop Google Business Profile guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization).
- You have recent photos, a menu link and plenty of genuine reviews.
- Your website, if you have one, loads quickly on a phone.

Ads add most when you have a clear reason to pay: launching a new cafe, promoting events or catering, pushing a new product, or reaching a specific group like remote workers who do not know you exist yet.

## Campaign types that fit a cafe

| Campaign type | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Search ads | Show text ads when people search "coffee shop near me" or similar | Capturing high-intent customers |
| Performance Max with store goals | Uses Google's automation across Search, Maps, YouTube and Display | Cafes with good assets and tracking |
| Local campaigns or Maps promotion | Promote your location across Google properties | Visibility and store visits |
| Display or YouTube | Visual reminders across the web | Awareness; usually secondary |
| Call or catering search | Targets queries like "office coffee delivery" | Catering and wholesale |

Google changes campaign names and features often, so check what is currently offered in your account. For most single-location cafes, start with Search ads, add Performance Max later once you have conversion data.

## Two audiences, two messages

### Remote workers and students

They search for terms like "cafe with wifi", "laptop friendly coffee shop" or "quiet cafe to work". What they need to know: wifi, power sockets, seating and noise level, and opening hours.

Example ad angle: "Wi-Fi, power outlets and good espresso. Open 7 am to 6 pm." Send them to a page that shows seating photos and your wifi policy.

### Coffee lovers

They look for "specialty coffee near me", "best flat white" or "single origin coffee". They want to know what is distinctive: your roaster, brewing methods and house-made treats.

Example ad angle: "Locally roasted single origin, brewed to order." Send them to the menu or your story page.

Keep these messages in separate ad groups so the headline matches what the searcher typed.

## Step-by-step setup

### Step 1: Define the goal

Choose one primary action: get directions, call, order online, or book a table. Set it up as a conversion in Google Ads and Google Analytics. Without this, you will not know whether ads work. Our [conversion tracking setup guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) walks through it.

### Step 2: Link your Google Business Profile

Linking your profile lets you add location assets (address, directions button) and call assets to your ads. This is one of the most valuable extensions for a cafe. Our [Google Ads extensions guide](/blog/google-ads-extensions-guide) explains the options.

### Step 3: Choose keywords and match types

Start narrow and local:

- "coffee shop near me"
- "cafe [neighbourhood]"
- "laptop friendly cafe [city]"
- "specialty coffee [city]"
- "brunch near me" if you serve it

Use phrase and exact match to keep control. Add negative keywords for terms you do not want, such as "jobs", "recipes", "instant coffee", "beans wholesale" (unless you sell them) and competitor names you do not wish to appear for. See our guide to [negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).

### Step 4: Target a tight radius

Set location targeting to people in or regularly in your area, and use a radius that reflects how far people walk or drive for coffee. In dense cities this might be one mile or less; in suburbs, a few miles. Exclude areas where nobody would reasonably visit.

### Step 5: Use ad scheduling

Show ads during hours you are open, plus a bit earlier for commuters planning their route. Consider higher bids for the morning rush if that is your busiest time.

### Step 6: Write ads that fit

Include: your neighbourhood, one distinctive feature, opening hours and a clear action. Write several headlines and descriptions so Google can test combinations. Keep claims honest, since Google disapproves of exaggerated or unsupported statements.

### Step 7: Set the budget

Cafe clicks in local markets often cost somewhere from under a dollar to a few dollars each, depending on the city and competition. A reasonable starting point is about $10 to $20 a day, or roughly $300 to $600 a month. Run it for four to six weeks before judging. For more on budgeting, see our [Google Ads budget guide for small businesses](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business).

## Landing pages that convert

Where you send clicks matters as much as the ad. A good cafe landing page:

- Shows your address, map, hours and phone number at the top.
- Loads quickly on mobile.
- Has real photos, not stock pictures.
- Offers one clear action, such as "Get directions" or "Order ahead".
- Mentions what the ad promised, like wifi or oat milk.

If you do not have a website, link to a simple one-page site rather than a social profile.

## How to measure success

Track:

- **Direction requests, calls and store visits** reported in Google Ads and your Business Profile.
- **Cost per action** (for example cost per direction request).
- **Impression share and search terms**, to see what people actually typed.
- **Redemptions of an ad-only offer**, such as "free cookie with your first latte, mention this ad".

Review the search terms report weekly at first. Add negatives for irrelevant queries and expand on those that bring real visits.

## Common mistakes

- Running broad targeting across a whole city for a single-location cafe.
- Letting Google auto-expand keywords without checking search terms.
- Sending clicks to a homepage that buries the address.
- Not tracking directions or calls.
- Giving up after a few days without enough data.

## A four-week launch checklist

- Week 1: link your Business Profile, set conversions for calls and directions, and build one tight Search campaign.
- Week 2: read the search terms report and add negative keywords.
- Week 3: test a second ad variation focused on the other audience, remote workers or coffee lovers.
- Week 4: compare cost per direction request or call, then decide whether to raise, hold or pause the budget.

If the numbers look poor, check the landing page and your listing before blaming the ads.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a cafe spend on Google Ads?**
A: Many cafes start around $300 to $600 a month and adjust based on results. Smaller test budgets are possible, but very low spend can make it hard to learn.

**Q: Are Google Ads better than Instagram ads for a cafe?**
A: They serve different needs. Google reaches people actively searching, while Instagram helps with visual discovery. Cafes often benefit from both, starting with whichever matches their immediate goal.

**Q: Can I advertise to remote workers specifically?**
A: Yes, through keywords like "laptop friendly cafe" and messaging about wifi, power sockets and seating. Audience signals can help in automated campaigns.

**Q: How soon will I see results?**
A: Impressions and clicks start within a day or two, but give it several weeks to see patterns in visits and cost per action.
