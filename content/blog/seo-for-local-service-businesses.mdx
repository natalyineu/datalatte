Google ranks local results mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and says you cannot pay for a better local ranking. For a service-area business such as a plumber, cleaner or electrician, SEO therefore comes down to seven jobs: a complete Business Profile with a hidden home address, accurate service areas (up to 20), service pages, consistent business details, reviews, a fast mobile site and tracking.

This guide is for owners who travel to customers and do not run a shopfront. It uses Google's own help pages, and says plainly where no number is published, such as how many reviews win a ranking. The worked example uses assumptions.

## How Does Google Rank Local Service Businesses?

Google's help page says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well a profile matches the search. Distance is how far the business is from the searcher, which you cannot change. Prominence is how well-known the business is, and Google counts links from websites and the number of reviews, noting that more reviews and positive ratings can help.

Two limits follow. You cannot pay for a better local ranking, and you cannot move closer to the customer. What you control is the completeness of your profile, your reviews and the quality of your website. For the wider checklist, read our [15 steps to improve local SEO](/blog/how-to-improve-local-seo-for-your-small-business-15-steps-that-work-in-2026).

## How Should a Service-Area Business Set Up Its Profile?

Google says a service-area business visits or delivers to customers but does not serve them at its address, and that you should hide the address from customers. Its example is a plumber working from a home address. You can have one profile for the whole area.

In the profile, choose Edit profile, Location, then Service area. You can add up to 20 service areas defined by cities, postal codes or other areas, but you cannot use a radius. Google suggests keeping the whole area within roughly two hours of driving time from your base. Edits can take up to 48 hours to show. A business with a staffed storefront can show its address and a service area; see the [profile optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## What Pages Does the Website Need?

Build one page per main service, such as drain cleaning, and a Service Areas page listing the places you work. Each service page should say what is included, how you price it (a range or starting price if you can), how fast you can come and how to book. Put the phone number at the top.

Do not copy one page for 30 towns. Google's spam policy describes creating many pages that funnel people to one page, or substantially similar pages, as doorway abuse. Write a town page only if it holds facts that differ for that town. Add [LocalBusiness structured data](/blog/schema-markup-local-business-website): Google requires name and address for eligibility and recommends telephone, URL, hours and geo.

## Why Do Reviews and Consistent Details Matter?

Google names the number of reviews and the ratings as parts of prominence. Ask each customer for a review after the job, by text with a direct link, and answer every review. Do not offer rewards for reviews: Google's Maps policy bars incentives and selective solicitation. Our [review strategy guide](/blog/google-business-reviews-strategy) covers the process.

Keep your business name, phone and website the same everywhere they appear. A mismatch between the profile, the website and directories gives search engines conflicting signals. See [NAP consistency](/blog/nap-consistency-local-seo) for a checking routine. No verified source gives a review count needed to rank, so compare yourself with the three businesses above you in Maps and match them.

## A Worked Example: Is an SEO Month Worth It?

All inputs are **assumptions** for a cleaning company.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly profile and website calls before work | 12 |
| Calls after 3 months of work | 20 |
| Share of calls that become jobs | 40% |
| Average job value | $150 |
| Extra jobs per month | 8 x 0.4 = 3.2 |
| Extra revenue per month | about $480 |

If the work costs $400 a month, the extra revenue of $480 covers it with little margin, so the case rests on repeat customers. If calls only rise to 15, extra revenue is about $180 and it does not pay. Track calls with a separate number or call tracking, and use [KPIs to measure local SEO success](/blog/how-to-measure-local-seo-success-kpis).

## FAQ

### Do I need a physical address for local SEO?

No. A service-area business can hide its address and list service areas instead. Google says a business serving customers at its address should keep it.

### How many areas can I list?

Up to 20 service areas, defined by cities, postal codes or other areas. A radius is not allowed.

### How long does local SEO take?

No source gives a reliable timeline. Profile edits can show within 48 hours, but rankings depend on competition. Measure monthly.

### Should I pay for ads instead?

Ads and local SEO answer different needs. Ads buy visibility now at a cost per click, and SEO builds a profile and site you keep. Compare both with your own numbers.
