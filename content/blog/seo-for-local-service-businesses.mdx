## What Does Google Expect From a Local Service Business?

Google's guidelines for local businesses ask for accuracy more than tricks. Google says a business that has a physical location customers can visit, or that travels to customers where they are, can create a [Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177). To keep that profile from being suspended, the business must avoid prohibited content, reflect its business accurately and follow the product guidelines in that article.

The sources reviewed here do not publish a ranking formula or a number of reviews needed to rank. This playbook does not invent one. It focuses on what Google asks you to do, and on what you can measure yourself. Ranking results change with competition in your area, so a checklist of accurate details is more reliable than a guessed target.

## How Should a Service-Area Business Set Up Its Profile?

Google asks for one profile per business, because more than one can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search. Choose the fewest categories that describe your core business. A plumber should pick the plumbing category, not a long list of loosely related services, since the guidelines ask for the fewest categories that describe the overall core business.

Make sure your address and service area are accurate and precise. The business description should explain your services and history in plain language. It should not carry promotions, prices or sale offers, and it should not contain links, since Google says no links of any type are allowed there. Contact details such as phone and email are allowed on your own profile.

If you list a service area, name the real places you cover. Avoid adding towns you rarely visit, because the profile should match how the business works in the real world. ## What Should the Website Do for Search?

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says sites that follow Search Essentials are more likely to show up in Google's results, though no site is guaranteed a place. For a service business, that means the site should be easy to crawl, read and understand. Google says it should see a page the same way an average user does. If key parts of the page, such as CSS and JavaScript, are hidden from the crawler, Google may not understand the page.

Descriptive URLs help too. Google says words in the URL can help users understand a result before they click, so an address that names the service, such as a drain cleaning page, is more useful than a code-style address. Check your pages with the URL Inspection Tool in Search Console, and search for your domain with the site: operator to confirm that pages are indexed.

A practical approach is one clear page per main service, with the phone number and booking method easy to find. This is advice for your business, not a rule Google sets. Google's [LocalBusiness structured data guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) is the place to check which markup fits your site.

## Why Do Consistent Business Details Matter?

Google asks that your name reflect the real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website and stationery, and as customers know it. For a van-based business, that usually means the same name on your vehicles, invoices, website and profile. Google's guidance also rejects extra terms in the name, such as marketing taglines, phone numbers or store codes.

Consistency reduces confusion for customers and for Google. If your profile says one name and your website says another, the mismatch is easy to spot and easy to fix. Create a single master sheet with your name, phone number, service area and hours, and check every listing against it each quarter. Remove any extra profile you find for the same business, since Google asks for one per business.

## How Long Before Changes Show in Search?

Google's SEO Starter Guide says changes can take effect within a few hours, while others can take several months. The guide suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change had a beneficial effect. Not every change produces a visible result, so the right approach is to iterate and measure over time.

This timing matters for budgeting. A profile update may show quickly, but a website improvement may need a month or more before you read the numbers. Judge the work over a full quarter, and compare it with calls and jobs rather than with one day's search position. ## A Worked Example: Is a Monthly SEO Budget Worth It?

All inputs below are **assumptions** for a cleaning company, not reported figures.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly calls from profile and website, before work | 12 |
| Monthly calls after 3 months of work | 20 |
| Share of calls that become jobs | 40% |
| Average job value | $150 |
| Extra calls per month | 20 - 12 = 8 |
| Extra jobs per month | 8 x 0.4 = 3.2 |
| Extra revenue per month | 3.2 x $150 = $480 |
| SEO cost per month | $400 |
| Net result per month | $480 - $400 = $80 |

The break-even point is about 6.7 extra calls a month, because $400 divided by $60 of profit per call (0.4 x $150) equals 6.7. If calls rise only to 15, extra revenue is about $180 and the work loses about $220 a month. Track calls with a separate number or call tracking, so you know which calls came from search.

## What Is a Step Checklist for Local SEO?

Use this order for your first quarter. Each step is a task you can finish and check.

1. Confirm one Business Profile exists and matches your real-world name.
2. Pick the fewest categories that describe your core service.
3. Enter your address and service area accurately, and remove any area you do not serve.
4. Remove links, prices and promotions from the business description.
5. Check each service page with the URL Inspection Tool and confirm it is indexed with the site: operator.
6. Compare name, phone and hours across your website, profile and directories, and fix mismatches.
7. Log calls and jobs each month, and review after three months.

If you want to run paid search alongside this plan, see [Google Ads for service businesses](/blog/google-ads-for-service-businesses). Paid and organic results can run together, and the call log from step 7 tells you which one brings the jobs.

## FAQ

### Do I need a physical address for local SEO?

Not necessarily. Google's guidelines say a business that has a location customers can visit, or that travels to customers, can create a profile. Whatever you choose, keep your address and service area accurate and precise.

### How many profiles should I create?

Google says there should be only one profile per business. More than one can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search.

### How long until local SEO works?

Google says some changes take effect within a few hours and others take several months. It suggests waiting a few weeks before judging a change, so measure over at least one quarter.

### Should I pay for ads instead?

Ads and organic local SEO serve different needs. Ads can bring calls while you build organic presence, and organic work builds assets you keep. Compare both with your own call and job numbers before choosing.
