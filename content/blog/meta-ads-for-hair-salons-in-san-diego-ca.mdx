## How much should a San Diego salon spend on Meta ads?

No source reviewed gives a San Diego-specific Meta rate, so the figures here are national planning bands. Adwave lists a standalone Meta buy at $300 to $1,000 a month. Bookeo advises a higher starting point of $750 to $1,500 a month for Facebook and Instagram ad spend, which fits a salon testing paid social for the first time.

A practical approach is to start at the lower end, run one campaign long enough to read the results, and move budget only after bookings show a pattern. Adwave notes that [Meta takes weeks to optimise](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-small-business-2026), so judging a campaign after a few days can mislead. For a broader comparison of paid social options, read [Facebook ads for hair salons](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons).

## Which ad formats and posting rhythm work for salons?

Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, which says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram. For a salon, that usually means short clips of a cut, colour or blow-dry filmed in the salon, with real staff and clients who have agreed to appear.

Bookeo says most salon owners find three to four Instagram posts and one to two Facebook posts a week sustainable. That cadence gives the ads a steady pool of content to draw from. Stories are a temporary format, according to [Associated Hair Professionals](https://www.associatedhairprofessionals.com/updates/blog-posts/power-social-media-your-hairstyling-brand), so use them for same-week openings rather than permanent service menus. The organic side of this calendar is covered in [Instagram marketing for hair salons](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-hair-salons).

## How do you target clients within a local radius?

Bookeo advises setting a 5 to 10 mile radius around the salon for Facebook and Instagram ads. In San Diego, the right radius depends on how far clients travel for a visit. A salon in a dense neighbourhood may do best with a tighter circle than one in a spread-out area, so test two radii and compare the booking rate from each.

Bookeo also points to peak follower activity in the Insights tab, which requires a business account. Schedule posts and ad flights around the hours your followers are online. [Meta's apps reach an average of 3.56 billion daily active people](https://adwave.com/resources/facebook-advertising-for-small-business), so the real constraint for a salon is local targeting, not audience size.

Campaigns can be built around traffic or reservations across Facebook, Instagram or both, as [The Fork Manager's guide to paid ads](https://www.theforkmanager.com/en/blog/introduction-paid-ads-restaurants) explains for restaurants. A salon can apply the same logic and set the goal to booking-page traffic.

## How do you set up the account before launching?

Meta Ads Manager is the central tool for running ads across Instagram and Facebook, according to [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising). [Turrboo](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media) notes it also covers Messenger. Because Instagram is owned by Meta, Instagram ads run through the same system, and one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook or both, per [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business).

Before launch, confirm the salon has a business account on both platforms, since the Insights data depends on it. [Sascom247](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/) reports that Meta's Blueprint certification covers Facebook and Instagram advertising at no cost, so the person running ads can train before spending money on a campaign.

## How do you measure whether ads bring bookings?

Count bookings and new clients, not impressions or reach. Install the Meta Pixel on the booking confirmation page, configure a booking event, and add UTM parameters to every ad link so analytics can separate Meta traffic from other sources. Without that setup, Ads Manager shows reach but cannot show which ad produced a booking.

Allow the campaign time to settle before judging it, then compare the cost per booking with what the salon pays for a new client through other channels. Once bookings arrive, a follow-up sequence can support repeat visits, as outlined in [AI-powered email marketing for hair salons](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-for-hair-salons).

## Which mistakes waste a salon's Meta budget?

Several habits drain budgets without producing bookings:

- Running one ad for months without refreshing the creative as results decline.
- Using generic stock imagery instead of footage of the salon's real work.
- Ignoring the radius and targeting a wide area the salon cannot serve.
- Judging campaigns by reach rather than bookings.
- Launching without a business account, which removes the Insights data needed to schedule posts.

A useful check is to review the ad set every few weeks against the cost-per-booking figure. For a fuller playbook on running campaigns across both platforms, see [using Meta ads for hair salon marketing](/blog/using-meta-ads-for-hair-salon-marketing).

## FAQ

### How much does a hair salon need to spend on Meta ads?

Adwave lists a standalone Meta buy at $300 to $1,000 a month. Bookeo suggests starting Facebook and Instagram ad spend at $750 to $1,500 monthly. Start within the range the salon can sustain for several weeks, then adjust based on bookings.

### What radius should Facebook and Instagram ads use around a salon?

Bookeo advises a 5 to 10 mile radius around the salon. Test two radii in dense and suburban areas and compare the booking rate from each before settling on one.
