Maryland had about 696,710 small businesses in the SBA's 2025 profile, 99.6% of the state's businesses.

This guide is for coffee shops, studios, salons, groomers and service firms in Baltimore, Bethesda, Annapolis, Frederick and other Maryland towns. Cost figures are US-wide benchmarks, because none of the sources checked publishes Maryland-specific click costs. Anything unverified is marked.

## What Marketing Works for a Small Business in Maryland?

Rank channels by how directly they reach someone ready to buy. Search ads and Google Maps reach people who are already looking. Meta ads and email reach people who are not yet looking or who already know you.

| Channel | Best for | Benchmark (US-wide, 2026) | Start with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Business Profile | Every local business | Free | Complete profile, real photos, review replies |
| Google Ads Search | Services people search for | CPC $2.05 (Restaurants) to $8.33 (Home improvement) | $300 to $600 per month test |
| Meta lead ads | Studios, salons, offers | Lead CPL $27.11 (Fitness), $50.91 (Beauty) | One offer, small radius |
| Email and SMS | Repeat customers | Cost of the tool | Collect addresses at checkout |

All CPC and CPL figures come from LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark pages and describe the whole US. None of the sources checked for this guide publishes Maryland-specific click costs.

## What Does the Maryland Market Look Like?

BLS lists Maryland's September 2026 unemployment rate at 3.9% (preliminary, seasonally adjusted), with 2,794.3 thousand nonfarm jobs, of which 271.7K are in leisure and hospitality. Leisure and hospitality jobs rose 1.3% over 12 months.

The SBA Office of Advocacy's 2025 Maryland profile lists 696,710 small businesses (99.6% of Maryland businesses) and 1.2 million small-business employees (47.9% of employees), per a search summary. The underlying data are mostly from 2022 Census sources. The profile PDF returned 403, so treat the figures as secondary and approximate.

## How Do You Keep Ads Local in Maryland?

Set a radius around the business and exclude places you cannot serve. Google's help page says radius targeting needs at least 1 km. It recommends broad "Presence or interest" targeting as the best practice for Search, and says presence-only targeting makes more sense if you want to avoid reaching people outside your target areas.

For a Baltimore shop, a 2 to 4 mile radius is a sensible first test, which is an assumption to check against your own customer addresses. Outside the Baltimore and Washington corridors, drive distances grow, so your radius and customer base will differ.

Add negative keywords weekly from the Search terms report: jobs, free, DIY and categories you do not offer. Google notes that negatives do not match close variants, so add plurals yourself.

## How Do You Rank on Google Maps in Maryland?

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. It also states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Practical steps, in order:

- Fill every field: categories, hours, services, service area and website.
- Upload real photos and refresh them each season.
- Reply to every review, good or bad.
- Keep name, address and phone identical on your website and other listings.

Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) goes through each field.

## Can You Offer a Discount for a Google Review?

No. Google's contributed content policy says merchants may not offer incentives, "such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services", in exchange for posting a review. You may ask customers for a review. You may not reward them for one.

Ask at the right moment with a direct link, for example right after a visit or pickup. See [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop) for scripts.

## Do Meta Ads Work for Maryland Small Businesses?

They can, if you sell something visual or have a clear first-visit offer. LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmarks list lead-campaign results of $27.11 per lead for Health and Fitness and $50.91 for Beauty and Personal Care, with $1.84 and $2.97 average CPC. Traffic campaigns across all industries average $0.60 per click.

Use Meta for people who are not searching yet. Run one offer, one small radius and a booking link. Track cost per booked visit, not likes.

## Where Can Maryland Owners Get Free Advice?

The Maryland Small Business Development Center describes its core service as no-cost confidential consulting, by appointment, through a request form. Its site lists six regional offices (Corridor, Eastern, Northern, Southern, Western and Baltimore) plus a lead center, and specialty programs such as Retail and Restaurant. Check each specialty program for fees.

## What Mistakes Do Maryland Owners Make?

- **Targeting the whole state.** If customers come from 10 miles away, statewide ads waste budget.
- **Stopping in slow months.** Compare cost per booking monthly, then lower spend instead of pausing.
- **Not tracking calls.** Phone calls are often the real conversion for service firms. Set a minimum call length that counts, so wrong numbers do not inflate results.
- **Spreading thin.** Test one channel for 60 days before adding a second. See our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).

Google Business Profile and local SEO first, then Google Ads for high-intent searches, then Meta and email to bring people back. This order is a recommendation, not a ranked study.
