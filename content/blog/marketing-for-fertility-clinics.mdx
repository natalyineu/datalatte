## What Rules Apply to Fertility Clinic Advertising?

Start with each platform. Google's healthcare and medicines policy page says Google "doesn't allow the promotion of products and services related to birth control, fertility" in certain locations, and that many healthcare items are allowed only with limitations or certification. The page does not tell you which rule applies to your clinic, so read it and test an ad account before you plan a budget.

Meta publishes its own health and wellness advertising standards on its transparency site. Check them for the wording that applies to medical treatments and personal attributes before building audiences or creative. Policies change, so re-read them every few months.

## How Does HIPAA Affect Marketing?

HIPAA's definition of marketing is in 45 CFR 164.501. It says marketing means a communication about a product or service that encourages recipients to purchase or use it. Exceptions include communications for treatment of an individual, and describing a health-related product or service your own practice provides, unless a third party pays you for the communication.

Because patient data and ad platforms can mix badly, ask a privacy lawyer before uploading patient lists, using tracking pixels on appointment pages or posting patient stories. This guide cannot say which uses are allowed in your case.

## How Should a Clinic Talk About Success Rates?

CDC publishes ART success rates for reporting US clinics, with 2022 the latest year on its page. It cautions that average chances may not reflect actual success for an individual or couple, and that chances vary with age, diagnosis, prior pregnancy history and procedures used.

Use the same care. Show the year and source of any figure, avoid comparing yourself to a competitor's different patient mix, and link to the CDC page. Do not write that a patient "will" conceive. A page that explains the process, costs and next steps will serve a worried reader better than a number.

## How Can a Clinic Handle Reviews Safely?

Ask for reviews with a Google link or QR code. Google prohibits incentives in exchange for reviews, and the FTC's 2024 rule bans buying or selling fake reviews. Never write replies that confirm treatment details. A neutral line such as an invitation to call the clinic is enough.

For bad or fake reviews, see [how to deal with fake Google reviews](/blog/how-to-deal-with-fake-google-reviews). Fill in your profile completely using the [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist), and use the real clinic name, as Google's guidelines require.

## FAQ

### Can fertility clinics advertise on Google?

Google lists fertility among items it does not allow in certain locations, and many healthcare items need certification. Check the policy page and your Google Ads account for your location.

### Is an appointment reminder email marketing under HIPAA?

The HIPAA definition excludes communications for treatment of an individual. Ask counsel how it applies to your messages, especially if a third party pays you.

### Should I publish success rates?

If you do, cite CDC or your reported data with the year, and add that average chances may not reflect an individual's result.

### Can I reply to reviews from patients?

Yes, but do not confirm that the reviewer is a patient or mention treatment. Invite them to call instead.
