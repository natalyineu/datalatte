Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram advertising) work for local businesses when you target a tight radius around your location, run a simple offer to a small audience, and measure bookings or calls rather than likes. Most local businesses can start with roughly $5 to $15 a day, and give it four to six weeks before judging results.

This guide walks through the setup in the order we would do it for a coffee shop, salon, pet groomer or fitness studio: account foundations, audience, offer, creative, budget, and measurement.

## What Meta Ads Are Good For (and What They Are Not)

Google Ads catches people who are already searching for "dog groomer near me". Meta Ads work differently: you interrupt someone who is scrolling and show them something relevant. That makes Meta strong for:

- **Awareness in your neighborhood:** new openings, new services, seasonal menus.
- **Offers with a clear hook:** a first-visit discount, a free trial class, a limited-time package.
- **Retargeting:** showing ads to people who visited your website or engaged with your Instagram profile.
- **Filling slow periods:** Tuesday-morning appointments, off-peak class times.

Meta is usually weaker for high-urgency needs ("emergency plumber"), where Google search intent wins. Many local businesses end up using both: Google for people ready to buy now, Meta for building demand and staying visible. If you are deciding between them, our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business) covers the search side.

## Step 1: Set Up the Foundations

Before spending anything, get these in place. Skipping this step is the most common reason local campaigns underperform.

1. **A Facebook Page and Instagram professional account**, connected in Meta Business Suite. Fill in address, hours, phone and a clear profile photo.
2. **A Meta Business portfolio** (formerly Business Manager) so the ad account belongs to the business, not to a personal profile.
3. **The Meta Pixel and/or Conversions API** on your website, so Meta can see who books or buys. Our [Facebook Pixel setup guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-setup-guide) walks through it.
4. **A landing destination that works on a phone:** a booking page, a simple offer page, or a click-to-message button. If your website loads slowly or buries the booking button, fix that before paying for traffic.

## Step 2: Define a Local Audience

Local audiences are small, and that is fine. In Ads Manager you set the location first, then optionally narrow it.

- **Location:** choose "people living in this location" and drop a pin on your business. A radius of roughly 3 to 8 miles (5 to 13 km) suits most urban businesses; rural businesses may need 15 miles or more. Think about how far your best existing customers actually travel.
- **Age and gender:** only narrow these if your service genuinely skews. A barbershop may reasonably target adult men; a coffee shop usually should not.
- **Interests:** use sparingly. Meta's targeting has become broader over the years, and its delivery system often finds the right people on its own when the creative is clear. Start broad inside your radius and let the ad do the filtering.
- **Custom audiences:** upload your email or phone list, or build an audience of people who engaged with your Instagram or visited your site. These tend to be your cheapest, best-performing audiences. See our guide to [Facebook custom audiences](/blog/facebook-custom-audiences) for the mechanics.

## Step 3: Pick the Right Campaign Objective

Meta asks you to choose an objective. For local businesses, the practical options are:

| Goal | Objective to try | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Phone calls or bookings from your website | Sales or Leads (with a website conversion) | Salons, groomers, studios with online booking |
| Enquiries without a website | Leads (instant form) or Engagement with messages | Service businesses, trial class sign-ups |
| Conversations on WhatsApp, Messenger or Instagram | Engagement, "messages" destination | Businesses that book by DM |
| Foot traffic and local reach | Awareness or Traffic | Openings, events, new menus |

Avoid the "boost post" button as your main tool. It is quick, but it gives you much less control over audience, placement and conversion tracking.

## Step 4: Build an Offer People Can Act On

The offer matters more than any targeting setting. Weak local ads say "We are the best salon in town". Strong ones say who the offer is for, what they get, and what to do next.

- **Coffee shop:** free pastry with your first coffee this week, within a short walk of the shop.
- **Hair salon:** a first-visit color consultation with a clear price range and booking link.
- **Pet groomer:** a first-groom welcome price for new dogs, with a note about the breeds you handle.
- **Fitness studio:** a one-week trial or a free intro class with limited spots.

Keep the barrier low for a first purchase and make the next step obvious: one button, one page, one action.

## Step 5: Create Simple, Honest Creative

You do not need a studio. Real photos and short vertical video shot on a phone usually beat polished stock images for local businesses, because people want to see the actual place and the actual people.

- Show the result: a finished haircut, a groomed dog, a latte on your own counter, a class in progress.
- Put the offer and location in the first line of text.
- Use a vertical 9:16 video for Stories and Reels, and a square or 4:5 image for the feed.
- Run two or three variations per ad set so you can see which message resonates. More than that spreads a small budget too thin.

Our guide to [Facebook ads creative](/blog/facebook-ads-creative-guide) goes deeper on formats and hooks.

## Step 6: Set a Realistic Budget

There is no universal number, because costs vary by country, industry and season. As a rough planning range:

- **Testing phase:** about $5 to $15 per day for one campaign, run for at least two to four weeks.
- **Scaling phase:** increase gradually, around 20 percent at a time, once you have a steady flow of leads or bookings at a cost you can afford.

A useful way to set the ceiling: work out the average value of a new customer over their first few months, decide what fraction of that you can spend to acquire them, and work backwards. Our [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) has a worked method.

## Step 7: Measure What Matters

Likes and reach feel good, but they do not pay rent. Track:

- **Cost per lead or booking:** ad spend divided by the number of enquiries or appointments you can trace to the ad.
- **Booking quality:** did those people show up, and did they come back?
- **Call and message volume:** if you use phone or DM bookings, ask every new customer "how did you hear about us?" and log the answer.
- **Frequency:** if the same people see your ad many times a week and results drop, refresh the creative.

Change one thing at a time, and give each change several days before judging it. Meta's system needs a learning period, and small local budgets produce noisy results over short windows.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Targeting a huge radius because it feels like more reach, then paying to show ads to people who will never visit.
- Sending ad clicks to a homepage instead of a specific offer or booking page.
- Changing budgets, audiences and creative every day, so nothing ever stabilises.
- Ignoring messages and comments. A fast reply often decides whether a lead becomes a booking.
- Running ads with no tracking, then guessing what worked.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a local business spend on Meta Ads per month?**
A: Many local businesses start between roughly $150 and $450 a month for testing. The right figure depends on your average customer value and how competitive your area is. Start small, measure cost per booking, and scale only what is working.

**Q: Do Meta Ads work better than Google Ads for local businesses?**
A: Neither is universally better. Google tends to capture people actively searching, while Meta builds awareness and fills slower periods. Many local businesses use Google for high-intent searches and Meta for offers and retargeting.

**Q: How long before I see results?**
A: You may see enquiries within days, but expect to spend four to six weeks testing offers and creative before you have a reliable picture of your cost per booking.

**Q: Do I need a website to run Meta Ads?**
A: Not strictly. You can use instant lead forms or click-to-message ads. A simple website or booking page with the Meta Pixel installed does give you better tracking and usually better results.
