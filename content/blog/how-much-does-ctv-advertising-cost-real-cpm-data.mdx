CTV advertising is sold mostly by CPM, the cost per 1,000 ad views. Published industry estimates put typical CPMs at roughly $15 to $45 across the market, with premium platforms such as Roku and Hulu quoted at $20 to $60 ([Vibe, Roku advertising cost](https://www.vibe.co/blog/roku-advertising-cost), checked Oct 2026). That is well above what most social and display buyers expect to pay. For a local business, a first test usually starts at around $500 in total, but there is no official price list, so always ask for a current quote.

Picture a Tuesday night. A household is halfway through a cooking show and your 30-second ad for the corner coffee shop plays. At a $35 CPM, that one view cost you 3.5 cents. Fine. Now multiply: $600 buys about 17,000 of those views, and the only question that matters is how many of them turn into people at your counter. If you cannot answer that before you spend, do not spend.

The figures here are estimates from ad-tech vendors, not rates published by the streaming platforms. The sections below show how CTV is priced and how to turn a CPM into a cost per customer.

## What is CTV advertising?

**CTV (Connected TV)** means smart TVs, streaming devices such as Roku, Fire TV and Apple TV, and game consoles that stream video over the internet. Unlike traditional TV, CTV allows digital targeting and reporting. To see how it differs from OTT, read [CTV vs OTT: what's the difference](/blog/ctv-vs-ott-whats-the-difference-and-which-should-you-buy). In short, CTV is the screen, and OTT is the content delivered over the internet.

## What CTV CPMs look like

Vendor benchmarks for 2026 describe a blended market CPM of roughly $26, with most campaigns landing between $25 and $35 and the wider market spanning about $15 to $45 depending on inventory ([AdWave, average CTV CPM](https://adwave.com/resources/average-ctv-cpm-q2-2026), checked Oct 2026). The same Vibe roundup estimates Hulu at $20 to $60 and YouTube at $20 to $25, and notes that Roku does not publish fixed rates.

Treat every number as a planning range. Your CPM depends on platform, targeting, season and how you buy.

## How to turn a budget into views

To estimate reach, divide your budget by the CPM and multiply by 1,000. The table uses example CPMs inside the range above.

| Budget | If the CPM is $25 | If the CPM is $35 | If the CPM is $45 |
|---|---|---|---|
| $500 | about 20,000 views | about 14,300 views | about 11,100 views |
| $1,000 | about 40,000 views | about 28,600 views | about 22,200 views |
| $2,500 | about 100,000 views | about 71,400 views | about 55,600 views |

These are arithmetic examples, not predictions. "Views" are impressions, not unique people, so one household can account for several.

## What affects CTV ad pricing

1. **Platform and inventory.** Premium streaming services and live sport generally cost more than free ad-supported or FAST channels. Prices change often.
2. **Targeting.** Narrow location, demographic or audience targeting typically raises CPM because fewer impressions qualify. Tight local targeting also means smaller reach, so check the estimated audience size.
3. **Ad format and length.** Common formats are 15- and 30-second video ads. Cost can differ by placement and format.
4. **Buying model.** Direct deals offer predictable placements but can have higher minimums. Programmatic buying, which uses real-time bidding, is more flexible. See [real-time bidding explained](/blog/real-time-bidding-explained-for-non-technical-business-owners).
5. **Seasonality.** Prices can rise in busy periods such as the end of the year and big sports events.

For a platform-by-platform view, see [CTV advertising platforms compared](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026).

## Minimum budgets

Platforms and ad partners set their own minimums. Vibe reports that Roku does not list a minimum publicly, but that campaigns typically start around $500 in total through Roku Ads Manager ([Vibe](https://www.vibe.co/blog/roku-advertising-cost), checked Oct 2026). Managed services and direct deals often require more. Ask each vendor for its current minimum in writing before you plan.

## How much should you spend on CTV?

There is no universal figure. Fund search first: [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and your Google Business Profile capture people already looking for you. CTV is an awareness layer on top of that, not a replacement.

<Callout type="coffee">Rule of thumb: if you cannot afford to run CTV for several weeks with enough frequency to be remembered, spend that money on search ads first and come back to CTV when your basics are working.</Callout>

## The math: from CPM to break-even customers

Example only. The $600 budget, the $35 CPM, the 5,000 households and the customer values are assumptions to show the arithmetic. They are not a real campaign or a market statistic. Swap in your own numbers.

- Views = 600 / 35 x 1,000 = about 17,100 (call it 17,000).
- If those views reach about 5,000 households, each sees the ad about 3 to 4 times (17,100 / 5,000 = 3.4).

Now the question that decides it: how many new customers do you need to cover the $600?

| What a new customer is worth to you (assumed) | New customers to break even ($600 / value) | Share of the 5,000 households |
|---|---|---|
| $15 (one first visit) | 40 | 0.8% |
| $60 (a few visits) | 10 | 0.2% |
| $150 (a regular for a year) | 4 | 0.08% |

If you only count first visits, you need 40 new customers from one month of TV. That is hard for a coffee shop. If you know regulars are worth $150, 4 is plausible. Your lifetime value is the whole case for or against CTV. Work it out first.

## Do this before you buy

1. Write down what a new customer is worth over 12 months. One number.
2. Divide your test budget (say $500) by that number. That is your break-even customer count.
3. Set up a promo code and a QR code to a landing page before the ad runs.
4. Ask each of your next 20 new customers how they heard about you.
5. Set a review date 6 weeks out and a stop rule.

## How to keep CTV costs under control

- **Use tight local targeting.** Limit delivery to the area your customers travel from. Extra targeting layers add cost, so test whether they pay off.
- **Cap frequency.** Do not let one household see your ad dozens of times in a week.
- **Test creative.** Compare 15-second and 30-second versions. Show your name and web address, with one call to action.
- **Track conversions.** Promo code, QR code, landing page. If you cannot prove a return, you cannot justify the spend.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does CTV advertising cost per month for a small business?**
A: It depends on the platform, targeting and minimum spend. Vendor reports describe tests starting around $500 on self-serve tools such as Roku Ads Manager, while managed services typically need more. Request current quotes from a few vendors.

**Q: What is a good CPM for CTV ads?**
A: Vendor estimates put typical CTV CPMs at about $15 to $45, with some premium inventory higher. A "good" CPM is one that delivers customers at a cost per customer you can afford, so judge results by that, not CPM alone.

**Q: Can I run CTV ads on a small budget?**
A: Yes, but a small budget buys fewer views, so it works best with tight local targeting or retargeting people who have already visited your website. Expect to run it for several weeks before judging.

**Q: How do I know whether a CTV viewer visited my business?**
A: Use a unique promo code, a QR code to a landing page you control, a tracked phone number and a "how did you hear about us?" question. Watching branded search volume in Google Search Console can also show a lift.

**Q: Is CTV better than Google Ads for local businesses?**
A: They do different jobs. Google Ads captures people actively searching, while CTV builds awareness. For most local businesses, Google Ads and a strong Google Business Profile come first.
