Pest control is an emergency-driven search category: someone sees a wasp nest or a bed bug and types a query within minutes. That urgency is the reason to advertise on Google, and also the reason clicks can be pricey. This guide uses published benchmarks, clearly labelled, and a worked example so you can check whether the numbers work for your company.

## What the numbers say (and do not say)

The most reliable anchor is WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark, summarized by [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/what-are-good-google-ads-benchmarks/546995/): across industries, search CPC averages $5.42, conversion rate 8.18% and cost per lead $66.69. The summary does not list a pest control line, so those are not pest control numbers.

For pest-specific figures we found only agency write-ups. [Webtonic's roundup](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/pest-control-google-ads-statistics) quotes these, each attributed to the named company and unverified by us:

- Pest control CPC of $6-$28 overall, with bed bug emergencies at $20-$28, termites $18-$25, general pest $6-$12 and mosquito or tick seasonal work $8-$15 (Blue Grid Media).
- Google Search cost per lead of $90.92 for pest control (attributed to WordStream/LocaliQ 2025 via another agency).
- Local Services Ads cost per lead of $20-$30 (Cube Creative).

Use them as planning ranges, then replace them with your own account data after 3-4 weeks.

## Worked example (assumption, not a benchmark)

A single-technician company with a $1,500 monthly budget targets general pest and termite keywords. All inputs are illustrative assumptions.

| Input | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Average CPC | $12 |
| Click to lead rate | 6% |
| Cost per lead | $12 / 0.06 = $200 |
| Leads who book a job | 40% |
| Cost per booked customer | $200 / 0.40 = $500 |
| First-year value of a customer (assumption) | $600 gross profit with some on a recurring plan |

On these numbers, $1,500 buys about 125 clicks, 7-8 leads and 3 booked jobs, so the campaign roughly breaks even in year one and relies on recurring plans for profit. If you cannot sell a recurring plan, a $500 cost per customer is hard to justify. If your CPC is $7 instead, the same budget doubles the leads. This is why keyword selection matters more than budget size.

## Decision guide: which keywords to buy

1. **Start with high-intent, narrow terms** such as "bed bug exterminator near me" or "termite inspection [your city]". Higher CPC, but the searcher is ready to book.
2. **Treat seasonal terms separately** (mosquito, tick, wasps) so you can raise budgets only when demand peaks.
3. **Block research traffic** with negative keywords like "how to kill", "diy" and "jobs". See our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).
4. **Use call-focused setup.** Urgent searchers phone; read our [call campaign guide](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns-service-businesses).
5. **Test Local Services Ads** alongside search. They charge per lead rather than per click; agencies report $20-$70 per lead for pest control, but Google's own pricing varies by market, so confirm in your area.

## Landing page and tracking checklist

- A tap-to-call button above the fold and a short form (name, phone, pest type).
- Service area stated clearly; ads should not send out-of-area clicks.
- Call tracking and conversion tracking set up before spending. Agencies cite a dedicated landing page converting at 6-12% versus 1-3% for a homepage (MarketingLTB, via Webtonic), which is plausible but second-hand.
- Review your [Quality Score](/blog/google-ads-quality-score-guide) so relevant ads pay less per click.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does pest control Google Ads cost per click?**

Agency-reported ranges are $6-$28 depending on the pest and urgency. The cross-industry 2026 average is $5.42, so pest control tends to run above it.

**How much should I budget per month?**

Enough for at least 30-50 clicks a month in your highest-intent ad group. At $12 per click that is $360-$600, before seasonal increases.

**Are Local Services Ads better than search ads?**

They can be cheaper per lead, but they give you less control over keywords. Many companies run both and compare cost per booked job.

**How do I know the campaign is working?**

Track cost per booked customer, not clicks. Compare it with the value of a customer over 12 months.
