That urgency is the reason to advertise on Google, and also the reason clicks can be pricey. ## What Do the Benchmarks Say About Pest Control CPC?

The most reliable anchor is [WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/). Across all industries, search CPC averages $5.42 and cost per lead $66.69. The closest category, Home and Home Improvement, runs $8.33 per click and $90.92 per lead, with an 8.05% conversion rate. The page has no pest control line, so none of these are pest control numbers.

For pest-specific figures, only agency write-ups turned up. [Webtonic's roundup](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/pest-control-google-ads-statistics) is a secondary source that quotes these, each attributed to the named company and not verified by us at the original:

- Pest control CPC of $6-$28 overall, with bed bug emergencies at $20-$28, termites $18-$25, general pest $6-$12 and mosquito or tick seasonal work $8-$15 (Blue Grid Media 2026; the per-pest rows carry no separate attribution).
- Local Services Ads cost per lead of $20-$30 (Cube Creative 2026).

Use them as planning ranges, then replace them with your own account data after 3-4 weeks.

## What Does Cost Per Customer Look Like? A Worked Example

A single-technician company with a $1,500 monthly budget targets general pest and termite keywords. All inputs are **assumption** values, not benchmarks and not a client result.

| Input | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Average CPC | $12 |
| Click to lead rate | 6% |
| Cost per lead | $12 / 0.06 = $200 |
| Leads who book a job | 40% |
| Cost per booked customer | $200 / 0.40 = $500 |
| First-year value of a customer (assumption) | $600 gross profit with some on a recurring plan |

On these numbers, $1,500 buys about 125 clicks, 7-8 leads and 3 booked jobs, so the campaign roughly breaks even in year one and relies on recurring plans for profit. If you cannot sell a recurring plan, a $500 cost per customer is hard to justify. If your CPC is $7 instead, the same budget doubles the leads. This is why keyword selection matters more than budget size.

## Which Keywords Should a Pest Control Company Buy?

1. **Start with high-intent, narrow terms** such as "bed bug exterminator near me" or "termite inspection [your city]". Higher CPC, but the searcher is ready to book.
2. **Treat seasonal terms separately** (mosquito, tick, wasps) so you can raise budgets only when demand peaks.
3. **Block research traffic** with negative keywords like "how to kill", "diy" and "jobs". See our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).
4. **Use call-focused setup.** Urgent searchers phone; read our [call campaign guide](/blog/google-ads-call-only-campaigns-service-businesses).
5. **Test Local Services Ads** alongside search. They charge per lead rather than per click; one agency (Cube Creative, via Webtonic) reports $20-$30 per lead for pest control, but Google's own pricing varies by market, so confirm in your area.

## What Should the Landing Page and Tracking Include?

- A tap-to-call button above the fold and a short form (name, phone, pest type).
- Service area stated clearly; ads should not send out-of-area clicks.
- Call tracking and conversion tracking set up before spending. One agency source (MarketingLTB, via Webtonic) cites 6-12% for a dedicated landing page versus 1-3% for a homepage. That is second-hand, so test it yourself.
- Review your [Quality Score](/blog/google-ads-quality-score-guide) so relevant ads pay less per click.

## FAQ

**How much does pest control Google Ads cost per click?**

Agency-reported ranges are $6-$28 depending on the pest and urgency (secondary source). The cross-industry 2026 average is $5.42 and Home and Home Improvement is $8.33, so pest control can run above both.

**How much should I budget per month?**

A rule of thumb, not a benchmark: enough for 30-50 clicks a month in your highest-intent ad group. At the $12 assumption above that is $360-$600, before seasonal increases.

**Are Local Services Ads better than search ads?**

They can be cheaper per lead, but they give you less control over keywords. Many companies run both and compare cost per booked job.

**How do I know the campaign is working?**

Track cost per booked customer, not clicks. Compare it with the value of a customer over 12 months.
