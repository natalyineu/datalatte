## Why Does Quality Score Matter for Pest Control Ads?

Google describes [Quality Score](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118) as a diagnostic tool measured from 1 to 10 at the keyword level. A higher score means your ad and landing page are more relevant and useful to someone searching that keyword, compared with other advertisers.

It is built from three components: expected clickthrough rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Each is rated "Above average," "Average" or "Below average," based on advertisers whose ads showed for the same search over the last 90 days.

Google also states that Quality Score is not a key performance indicator and is not an input in the ad auction. Treat it as a checklist for fixing ads and pages. If a component shows Average or Below average, Google notes it may point to an opportunity to improve ads, landing pages or keyword selection. For a parallel look at urgent-search campaigns, see [Google Ads for urgent care](/blog/google-ads-for-urgent-care), where the same logic applies to emergency queries.

## What Does a Worked Cost-Per-Customer Example Look Like?

Consider a single-technician company testing general pest and termite keywords. Every input below is an assumption for illustration, not a benchmark or a client result. Replace each one with your own account data before making a budget decision.

| Step | Input or calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly budget | Assumption | $1,500 |
| Average CPC | Assumption | $12 |
| Clicks bought | $1,500 / $12 | 125 |
| Click to lead rate | Assumption | 6% |
| Leads | 125 x 0.06 | 7.5 |
| Cost per lead | $1,500 / 7.5 | $200 |
| Lead to booked job rate | Assumption | 40% |
| Booked jobs | 7.5 x 0.40 | 3 |
| Cost per booked customer | $1,500 / 3 | $500 |
| First-year gross profit per customer | Assumption | $600 |
| Year-one gross profit | 3 x $600 | $1,800 |

On these inputs, the account returns $1,800 gross profit on $1,500 spend, a $300 margin before overhead and staff time. If first-year gross profit is only $400 per customer, the same three jobs return $1,200 and the campaign loses money in year one.

Bid cost moves the result as much as budget. If CPC is $7 instead of $12, the same $1,500 buys about 214 clicks, roughly 70% more. Keyword choice decides whether that extra traffic books jobs.

## Which Keywords Should a Pest Control Company Buy?

1. Start with narrow, high-intent terms such as "bed bug exterminator near me" or "termite inspection" plus your city. These usually cost more per click, but the searcher is ready to book.
2. Put seasonal terms such as mosquito, tick or wasp removal in separate ad groups. Raise their budgets only when demand peaks in your area.
3. Add negative keywords for research intent, such as "how to kill", "diy" and "jobs". Review the search terms report regularly and add new exclusions as they appear.
4. Send each ad group to a matching page. A bed bug ad should land on a bed bug service page, not a generic homepage, which supports the relevance that Quality Score measures.

The same keyword-list approach works across local niches. A parallel example is in the [barbershop keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-barbershops-in-2026).

## How Much Should a Pest Control Account Spend Per Month?

A practical planning heuristic is enough budget to buy 30 to 50 clicks a month in your highest-intent ad group. This is a rule of thumb, not a published benchmark. At the $12 assumed CPC above, that means roughly $360 to $600 a month before any seasonal increase.

Start with the ad group that earns the most booked jobs in your own data, then add budget to seasonal groups once they prove they convert. Adding budget to broad groups first usually inflates cost per customer without adding qualified calls.

## What Should the Landing Page Include?

Put a tap-to-call button above the fold and keep the form short, asking only for name, phone number and pest type. State your service area clearly so that out-of-area clicks are not paid for. Set up call tracking and conversion tracking before spending, so you measure booked jobs rather than clicks.

Tools for tracking leads and automating follow-up are compared in [AI tools for small business marketing in 2026](/blog/ai-tools-for-small-business-marketing-2026). Whichever you choose, check that each booked job traces back to the keyword and ad group that produced it.

Local Services Ads work differently from search ads, so confirm how they are priced and reviewed in your market before moving budget across. Compare cost per booked job for both channels over the same period.

If you want help building a campaign around cost per booked job, see [Google Ads services](/services/google-ads).

## FAQ

### How much does pest control Google Ads cost per click?

This guide does not cite a verified pest control CPC. The $12 figure used above is an assumption for illustration only. Use your own account data from your first full reporting period.

### How much should I budget per month?

A rule of thumb is enough budget for 30 to 50 clicks a month in your highest-intent ad group. At a $12 assumed CPC, that is about $360 to $600 before seasonal increases.

### What does Quality Score measure?

Quality Score is a keyword-level diagnostic on a 1 to 10 scale. It is based on expected clickthrough rate, ad relevance and landing page experience, each compared with advertisers showing for the same search over 90 days.

### Does Quality Score affect what I pay per click?

Google states that Quality Score is not an input in the ad auction. It is a diagnostic tool, so use it to find ads and pages to improve rather than as a success metric.

### How do I know the campaign is working?

Track cost per booked customer, not clicks, and compare it with the gross profit a customer generates over 12 months.
