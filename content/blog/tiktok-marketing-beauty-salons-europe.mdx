## Is TikTok Worth It for a Beauty Salon in Europe?

The platform's scale is clear, but scale is not the same as salon bookings. The sources used for this guide give no salon-level TikTok conversion rate and no European breakdown of TikTok users. That means the first three months are a test. Post on a steady schedule, record where each new client heard about you, and decide from that record rather than from view counts.

Bookeo also makes a related claim without giving a source: a client who has seen a brand on Facebook 5 to 10 times before searching "hair salon near me" converts at 2 to 3 times the rate of a cold searcher. That figure is about Facebook, not TikTok. It does suggest that repeated exposure matters, which is a reason to post consistently rather than once.

## What Should a Beauty Salon Post on TikTok?

Start with the before and after reveal. Booksy says high-impact before and after videos capture attention in the first two seconds, so show the result early and explain the process afterwards. Use the same camera position each time so viewers can compare the two frames easily.

Short tips work well too. Booksy recommends clips of about 15 seconds that explain how to maintain a colour at home or how to use a specific styling tool. These clips also give people a reason to visit the salon for a service.

Video testimonials from clients who have agreed to appear are another option. BonusQR claims that video testimonials can increase conversion by over 80% compared with text-only reviews, but it gives no source for this figure, so treat it as a hypothesis to test.

A regular contest can also keep followers engaged. BonusQR suggests making contests part of the marketing calendar, quarterly for example. Filming can be batched: One of One Group, a London agency, says a month of salon content can be shot in a single salon visit. Film vertically on a phone, in one spot with good light.

## Do You Need Client Consent to Film Clients?

Yes. Ask before you film, and say where the video will be posted, including TikTok. Write the answer in the booking notes, so the team can see it. If a client changes their mind, remove the clip from every account where it appears.

Data protection rules differ across the UK and EU countries, so check current guidance from your national data protection authority before you publish client footage. Keep the rule simple for staff: no client appears in a video without a recorded yes. Staff should also know to avoid filming clients who are not in a position to say no, such as someone still in the chair with foils or a clinical treatment in progress.

## How Do You Turn TikTok Views Into Bookings?

Make booking a single step from the video. Booksy says Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds. Set that up alongside the link in your bio, so a viewer who searches for your salon can book in the same visit. Our guide to [Google Business Profile posts for hair salons](/blog/hair-salon-marketing-google-my-business-posts) covers the local profile.

Reviews matter as well. Booksy states that over 90% of consumers check reviews before booking a new stylist, but gives no source for the figure. Booksy also says its software prompts clients to leave a review after their appointment. If your booking software does the same, switch it on and ask happy clients directly.

Capture contacts too. Booksy says email returns an average of £38 for every £1 spent in the beauty industry, a vendor claim with no source given. A new-client offer can encourage sign-ups, such as the example Booksy gives of 20% off a new client's first visit. ## What Does a TikTok Month Cost? A Worked Example

Every input below is an assumption for illustration. None comes from the sources, so replace them with your own prices and hours.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Posts per month | 12 (3 a week) |
| Filming and editing per post | 60 minutes |
| Staff time value per hour | £30 |
| Value of a new client's first visit | £60 |
| One-week ad test | £15 a day for 7 days (£105) |

## How Do You Measure TikTok Results?

Track three things each month: followers, reach and enquiries. One of One Group says its monthly report covers these measures and treats enquiries as the most important. Add a question to your booking form, "How did you hear about us?", with TikTok as an option, so bookings can be matched to the platform.

Compare the monthly enquiry count with the cost in the worked example. If enquiries stay flat for three months while you post steadily, change the format or stop paying for ads. ## FAQ

### Can a salon use TikTok without showing its face?

Yes. Film hands, hair and finished results. Any client who appears in a clip still needs to agree to be filmed.

### How long should a TikTok video be?

Booksy suggests clips of about 15 seconds to explain at-home maintenance or a styling tool. Its guide also says before and after reveals capture attention in the first two seconds, so start with the result.

### Do reviews still matter if clients find me on TikTok?

Booksy says over 90% of consumers check reviews before booking a new stylist, a figure with no source given. Keep your Google profile current and add a booking link through Reserve with Google.

### Are TikTok ads worth it for a small salon?

The sources here give no TikTok ad cost or conversion rate for salons. Promote a post only after it has done well without paid support, set a fixed test budget, and stop if the test brings no bookings.

### How many people use TikTok in Europe?

This guide has no verified European figure. Bookeo gives over 1 billion monthly active users worldwide. Check TikTok's own published data for your country before planning a regional campaign.
