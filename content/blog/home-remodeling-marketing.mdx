Paid clicks are costly in this trade. ## What Does a Remodeling Lead Cost in Paid Search?

Vendor guides give different cost points, and they measure different things. Ruban KT's [Google Ads guide for construction companies](https://www.rubankt.com/google-ads-for-construction-companies/) says keywords such as "home renovation contractor" or "commercial builders near me" can cost $8–$15 per click. The [2026 PPC guide from Paid Media World](https://paidmediaworld.com/google-ads-for-construction-companies/) lists average CPC for high-end kitchen and home remodeling in Tier 1 metros at $25.00–$65.00.

Cost per lead is a separate measure. [Contractor Marketing Pros](https://contractormarketingpros.net/blog/best-digital-advertising-platforms-for-contractors/) states that for most contractor industries, cost per lead ranges from $25 to $150 depending on service type and competition. The same page, citing WordStream benchmarks, reports that home services companies average a 4.4% click-through rate on search ads.

A cheap click is not automatically a cheap lead. iCatch's [comparison of Google Ads and Yelp Ads](https://www.icatchgroup.com/google-ads-vs-yelp-ads/) gives a hypothetical case: a $40 click that consistently produces $6,000 remodeling projects can be more profitable than $6 clicks that never produce qualified leads.

## Which Lead Sources Fit a Remodeling Business?

Lead marketplaces charge in different ways. Thumbtack's opportunity leads are paid for only when the potential customer responds to the contractor's offer, and registration is free. [HomeAdvisor](https://onlinevisibilitypros.com/internet-marketing-for-contractors/homeadvisor-vs-thumbtack-vs-angis/) is pay-per-lead, requires an annual membership fee to be listed, and charges a lead fee even when the client does not choose the contractor. Its Market Match leads are shared with other providers in the area, while Exact Match leads are exclusive.

Lead generation tools are marketing automation tools that find people interested in a business and send them personalised messages, as [Encompass](https://360encompass.com/automated-marketing-tools/) describes them. A channel summary citing Neil Patel's marketing stats says organic search, local listings and paid search together generate 86% of local business leads, with organic at 31%, local listings and maps at 28%, and paid search at 27%. That summary is secondary, so use it as direction rather than a plan.

Print and direct mail can reach homeowners near a finished job, and [direct mail marketing for local businesses](/blog/direct-mail-marketing-local) covers how to run it. Home shows and open houses are another option, covered in [event marketing for local businesses](/blog/event-marketing-for-local-business).

## How Should a Remodeler Split a Paid Budget?

Lead Nurture Close recommends three allocations for contractor ad budgets: 30% to Facebook awareness ads for broad reach, 50% to Google Display and Search for keyword-driven clicks and high-intent prospects, and 20% shared across both platforms to re-engage interested users. The table below applies that split to an assumed budget.

| Allocation (vendor split) | Share | Assumed monthly amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facebook awareness ads | 30% | $900 |
| Google Display and Search | 50% | $1,500 |
| Retargeting, shared across both | 20% | $600 |
| Total | 100% | $3,000 |

The $3,000 monthly budget is an assumption. Coverage needs can grow quickly. Admanage's [Facebook ads guide for construction companies](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-for-construction-companies) says covering a mid-size remodeling company's market across service lines, cities, seasonal offers and form variations can require 60–100+ active test variants. A small firm working one trade in one town needs far fewer, so start with a handful of ads and one page per service.

## How Fast Should You Respond to Remodeling Leads?

LeadSync's [Facebook ads guide for contractors](https://leadsync.me/blog/facebook-ads-for-contractors/) analysed 58 contractor and home services lead ads from the public Meta Ad Library. It states that the odds of qualifying a lead are 21 times lower when a contractor calls at 30 minutes instead of 5 minutes. The article gives no source for that figure, so treat it as a benchmark to test with your own call logs.

Timing matters on the other end too. Admanage cites a Houzz study, as reported by Woodworking Network, which says major renovation decisions often take months. That is why it recommends nurturing leads for 14–90 days. A fast first reply and a scheduled follow-up sequence cover both points. Drafting those messages is covered in [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing), though every draft should be reviewed before it is sent.

## What Does a Signed Job Cost? A Worked Example

The example uses the illustrative inputs from Admanage's remodeling case, plus one assumed cost per lead. It shows the method, not a result.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project value | $35,000 | Admanage illustration |
| Gross margin | 35% | Admanage illustration |
| Gross profit per job | $12,250 | $35,000 × 35% |
| Raw-lead-to-close rate | 5% | Admanage illustration |
| Cost per lead | $100 | Assumption, inside the $25–$150 range |
| Ad cost per signed job | $2,000 | $100 ÷ 5% |
| Gross profit to ad cost | about 6.1 to 1 | $12,250 ÷ $2,000 |

If the close rate falls to 2.5% (assumption), ad cost per signed job doubles to $4,000, which still sits well below the $12,250 gross profit. The result holds only if your real close rate and cost per lead match these inputs, so track both from your own estimates and compare them with the channel data above.

## What Consent Rules Apply to Texting Leads?

Text follow-ups help with nurturing, but consent comes first. Textdrip's [10DLC registration guide](https://textdrip.com/blog/10dlc-registration-best-practices) states that a business must obtain the recipient's explicit consent before sending messages, and that sending without consent violates compliance rules and can lead to penalties. Capture consent on the enquiry form, record it, and send only the messages the person agreed to.

Licensing and trade rules differ by state, province and country. Check your regulator before advertising work you are not licensed to perform, and state your licence details where your jurisdiction expects them.

## FAQ

### How much does a remodeler pay per click?

Vendor figures differ by keyword and market. Ruban KT cites $8–$15 for terms such as "home renovation contractor," and Paid Media World lists $25.00–$65.00 average CPC for high-end kitchen and home remodeling in Tier 1 metros. Check live platform pricing for your area.

### Should I use Thumbtack or HomeAdvisor?

It depends on how you want to pay. Thumbtack charges only when the customer responds to your offer. HomeAdvisor charges a lead fee plus an annual membership, and it charges even when the client does not choose you. Compare total cost per signed job before committing.

### How quickly should I call a new lead?

LeadSync reports that the odds of qualifying a lead are 21 times lower at 30 minutes than at 5 minutes. The article does not attribute that figure, so test it against your own call data and aim for the shortest reply your team can manage.

### Can I text remodeling leads?

Only with explicit consent. Textdrip's guidance says sending without it violates compliance rules and can lead to penalties, so record consent at the enquiry stage.
