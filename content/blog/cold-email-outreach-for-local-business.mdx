Cold email can win local B2B work (supplying offices, gyms, salons or venues near a business) when it is legal, specific and short. In the United States the CAN-SPAM Act sets the floor: accurate sender details, an honest subject line, a physical postal address and a working opt-out honoured promptly, per the FTC's compliance guide checked in November 2026.

Local cold outreach differs from mass B2B blasting. The list is a few hundred named businesses within delivery or service distance, and the sender can plausibly visit. That smallness is the strategy: relevance replaces volume, and one reply is worth more than a thousand impressions.

## What does the law require for cold email?

The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide requires accurate header information, a non-deceptive subject line, identification of the message as an ad where applicable, a valid physical postal address, and a clear opt-out that the sender honours within 10 business days. Buying an email list does not transfer compliance; the sender remains responsible.

Other jurisdictions differ. The UK's ICO guidance treats business contacts differently from consumers but still requires identification and opt-out, and Canada's CASL generally requires consent before commercial email. A local business emailing only nearby firms should confirm its own jurisdiction's rules; this article describes the US floor, not legal advice.

Purchased or scraped consumer lists are where local senders get hurt. Spam complaints damage the domain every legitimate invoice and booking confirmation travels on. ## What should a local cold email say?

Three short parts. First, one sentence proving the recipient was chosen deliberately: their location, service or a visible gap the sender can genuinely fill. Second, the offer in one line with a concrete, low-effort next step. Third, a one-line opt-out that works without replying angrily.

Length is a feature. A phone-readable email under 75 words respects the reader and signals a real neighbour rather than a sequence. Attachments and image-heavy templates read as bulk mail and trigger filters built to catch exactly that. [Email marketing for yoga studios](/blog/email-marketing-for-yoga-studios) shows the same brevity standard for permission-based sends.

**Assumption example.** Assume a caterer emails 120 nearby offices with a specific lunch offer and 2% reply, producing about 2 to 3 conversations. If one becomes a $600 monthly order, the campaign returns far more than its 3 hours of list-building and writing. The reply rate is an assumption for the arithmetic; the only honest source is the sender's own first campaign.

## How does a sender protect deliverability?

Use a separate subdomain or a carefully warmed secondary domain for outreach so complaints cannot scorch the main domain's invoices and receipts. Authenticate with SPF, DKIM and DMARC before the first send; unauthenticated bulk-looking mail fails filters regardless of content quality.

Start small and watch bounces. A bounce rate above a few percent signals a rotten list, and continuing burns the domain for weeks. Remove unsubscribes and bounces immediately, not at the end of the campaign. Volume should grow only while complaint signals stay near zero.

One campaign, one audience, one offer. Blasting every service at every business type teaches filters and readers the same lesson. ## FAQ

### Is cold email legal for a local business?

In the US, commercial email is permitted under CAN-SPAM when it carries accurate sender details, an honest subject, a physical postal address and a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days, per the FTC guide checked in November 2026. Other countries are stricter, notably Canada. This is general information, not legal advice.

### How many cold emails should a local business send?

Start with 50 to 120 highly specific recipients, not thousands. Local B2B value sits in relevance: named businesses inside real service distance with a reason to talk. Watch bounces and complaints on the first batch before any increase. A separate sending subdomain protects the main domain while testing.

### What reply rate should cold outreach expect?

No sourced benchmark applies to a specific town and trade, and published averages mix industries beyond recognition. Measure the first campaign against its own cost: hours spent versus conversations started. The assumption example above uses 2% purely to show arithmetic. A specific, neighbourly offer to 120 firms can beat a generic blast to 5,000.

### Should cold email come from the main business domain?

Prefer a secondary domain or subdomain dedicated to outreach. Spam complaints and bounces from cold sends can damage the reputation of the domain carrying invoices, booking confirmations and password resets. Authenticate the outreach domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, warm it with small batches, and keep permission-based marketing on the main domain.
