A local business should run Instagram ads from a clear goal (calls, bookings, store visits), a tight radius around the business, one offer and a $300 test. Pew Research's 2026 survey found 51% of US adults use Instagram, rising to 79% of those aged 18 to 29, so the audience is there but skews young. Meta ads are bought in an auction, so the only real price is the one your own account produces.

This guide walks through goals, targeting, creative, budget and measurement, and compares Instagram with Google Ads. For price benchmarks, see our [Instagram ads cost breakdown](/blog/how-much-do-instagram-ads-cost-2026-pricing-breakdown).

## Who Is on Instagram in Your Market?

Pew's social media fact sheet shows Instagram use by US adults at 47% in 2023, 50% in 2024 and 2025, and 51% in 2026. By age in 2026 it is 79% for ages 18 to 29, 64% for 30 to 49, 41% for 50 to 64 and 21% for 65 and over. Pew notes a survey-mode change from 2023, so compare earlier years with care.

For a local business, that means Instagram reaches younger adults well and older customers less. A salon, cafe or gym with customers under 50 fits. A business that sells mainly to retirees may do better on Facebook or Google. Pew's same fact sheet puts Facebook at 70% of adults in 2026.

## What Goal Should the Ads Have?

Pick one result before you open Ads Manager. Common choices for a local business:

- **Calls or messages** from nearby people
- **Bookings or form fills** on your website
- **Store traffic**, sending people to your site or map page

Meta groups its campaign objectives by awareness, traffic, engagement and leads, and is consolidating the list, so the labels in your account may differ from older guides. Choose the objective closest to your result. LocaliQ's Facebook benchmarks show why: traffic campaigns have cheap clicks ($0.60 across industries) but no reported leads, while lead campaigns average $27.39 per lead.

## How Do You Target Local People?

Start with location. Set a radius around your address that matches how far customers travel, using your last 50 customers' postcodes as evidence. Keep interests broad and let the creative filter.

If you have an email list or phone list and permission to use it, you can upload it to build a custom audience and exclude current customers from new-customer offers. Meta's menus change, so look in the audiences section of Ads Manager. Our [audience targeting guide](/blog/audience-targeting-for-local-ads) covers how to choose between broad and narrow audiences.

Special categories such as housing, employment and credit have stricter location rules, so check Meta's policy if you advertise those.

## What Creative Works for a Local Business?

No published study isolates what wins for local advertisers, so test it. Use one clear offer, a real photo of your product, space or team, and one action to take. Build separate versions for Feed, Stories and Reels because the formats are cropped differently.

Practical steps:

1. Write one sentence that says what you sell, where and what to do next.
2. Make three images or short videos of the real thing.
3. Run them as separate ads in one ad set and let results show the winner.

## How Much Should You Budget to Test?

An illustration, with every input an **assumption** rather than a forecast:

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget | $300 for 30 days |
| Cost per lead | $27.39 (LocaliQ all-industry Facebook lead average) |
| Leads | about 11 |
| Lead to customer | 30% |
| New customers | about 3 |
| Break-even | a new customer is worth at least $91 |

Replace the cost per lead with your own figure after the first week. A beauty business should expect nearer the Beauty and Personal Care benchmark of $50.91, which would give about 6 leads for the same money.

## Instagram Ads or Google Ads: Which Is Better for a Local Business?

They answer different needs. Google says a Smart Search campaign shows your ad on Search and Maps when people look for businesses like yours, and you pay when someone clicks. Instagram shows ads to people who were not searching, so you create the want.

| | Instagram ads | Google Search or Smart campaign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer state | Browsing | Searching now |
| Strength | Visual products, awareness, offers | Urgent, high-intent needs |
| Typical test | Local radius, one offer | Keyword themes, call button |
| Public cost data | LocaliQ Facebook benchmarks | LocaliQ search benchmarks |

Use Google first for urgent services such as plumbers, and Instagram first for visual, repeat-visit businesses. Many run both. Our [Instagram versus Facebook ads guide](/blog/instagram-ads-vs-facebook-ads) covers the placements.

## What Should You Measure?

Count outcomes, not reach. Track cost per lead, cost per booking, the share of leads who become customers and how many customers say they saw the ad. Add "How did you hear about us?" to your booking form. Review weekly and change one thing at a time. If you have no leads after a month of steady spend, check the offer and the landing page before raising the budget.

## FAQ

### Do Instagram ads work for local businesses?

They can, particularly for businesses whose customers are under 50 and respond to visuals. No public source proves results for a specific trade, so test with a small budget and measure cost per customer.

### How much should a local business spend on Instagram ads?

A $300 test for 30 days is a reasonable starting point, an assumption rather than a rule. Scale only after you know your own cost per lead and per customer.

### Do I need a Facebook page to run Instagram ads?

Meta runs both apps through one ad system, so most advertisers manage them from the same account. Check the current requirements in Ads Manager, since they change.

### Can I run Instagram ads without a website?

You can aim ads at calls or messages, but a simple booking or contact page makes results easier to track.
