Run the numbers on a typical coffee shop test. Spend $600 a month at a $2.05 click, convert 8% of visitors into a call or directions request, and assume 4 in 10 of those people actually walk in. That is about 9 new customers, or roughly $64 in ads per new customer. If a new regular is worth about $58 in margin over six months (my assumption), you are slightly under water. Nudge any one input and you swing to a profit or a bigger loss. That is why coffee shop ads need setup discipline, not just a credit card.

This is the in-depth setup and budget guide. For a shorter overview of whether ads suit your shop, read our [Google Ads for coffee shops primer](/blog/google-ads-for-coffee-shops). Here we go step by step through targeting, keywords, bidding, tracking and what to spend. It is written for single-location cafes in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Dollar figures are US benchmarks; run the same math in your own currency.

## What Clicks Cost: One Benchmark, Handled Carefully

Three cautions. LocaliQ (formerly WordStream) sells ad management, so it has an interest in this topic. "Restaurants & Food" mixes full-service restaurants, takeaways and cafes, and a "conversion" can mean anything from a form to a call. And averages hide your market: a specialty cafe next to a university in a big city will not pay the same as one in a small town. Use $2.05 as a planning figure, not a promise, and replace it with real data after two weeks.

## Before You Start: Is the Profile Ready?

Ads send people to your Business Profile as often as to your website, since location assets display your address, hours and a directions button. If your photos, hours and menu link are thin, fix that first with our [coffee shop profile guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization). Ads on top of a half-empty profile waste budget.

## Step 1: Count the Right Things Before Spending

Set up tracking before you turn ads on. For a cafe, the valuable actions are rarely form fills. They are:

- **Calls** from the ad and from your website
- **Direction requests**
- **Online orders or menu clicks**, if you offer them

Google's help page on local actions conversions explains that these are counted when someone taps Call or Directions "while or after engaging with your ads", with menu views and order clicks counted for businesses that have those options. The default click-through window is 30 days. The page says these are available in the "All conversions" column, so do not be surprised if your main Conversions column looks empty; segment by conversion action to see them. Our conversion tracking walkthrough covers website events and call tracking.

Be honest about what a "conversion" is. A directions tap is not a latte sold. In the math below we apply a show-up rate for that reason.

## Step 2: Targeting a Radius People Really Walk or Drive

Google lets you target a radius around a location and exclude areas you do not want, according to its location targeting help. Choose the radius from your own customers: ask staff to note where regulars live or work for a week, or check your loyalty app. As a starting point (my rule of thumb, not Google's), try about one mile in dense city areas and two to five miles in suburbs and small towns, and widen only if the data supports it.

In the campaign settings, I recommend selecting presence (people in or regularly in your area) rather than also including people merely interested in the area. That is my advice, and it stops tourists researching a trip from burning your budget. Confirm which options your campaign type shows.

## Step 3: Keywords That Match Coffee Intent

Google defines the three match types in its keyword match help: broad match can show on related searches, phrase match on searches that include the meaning of your keyword, and exact match on searches with the same meaning or intent, giving the most control and the fewest searches. Google also says broad match should be used with Smart Bidding. With a small budget, start with phrase and exact.

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic | "coffee shop near me", "cafe open now" | Phrase + exact |
| Neighbourhood | "coffee shop [neighbourhood]", "cafe [town]" | Phrase + exact |
| Product | "oat milk latte near me", "cold brew near me", "pour over coffee [town]" | Phrase |
| Need-based | "cafe with wifi [neighbourhood]", "quiet cafe to work", "dog friendly cafe" | Phrase |
| Occasion | "coffee catering [town]", "cafe for meetings" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your shop name] | Exact |

Keep each group to a handful of closely related terms. Our [keyword list for coffee shops](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-coffee-shops-in-2026) has more ideas.

**Negative keywords on day one:** `jobs`, `hiring`, `recipe`, `how to make`, `machine`, `grinder`, `wholesale`, `franchise`, `pods`, `instant`, `starbucks` (unless you want to conquest) and `beans` if you do not sell them. Review the search terms report weekly. The negative keywords guide shows list-level setup.

## Step 4: Ads That Mirror the Search

Google's responsive search ad requirements allow up to 15 headlines (3 minimum, 30 characters each) and up to 4 descriptions (2 minimum, 90 characters each). Use them. Pin only what you must; Google's page says pinning reduces the combinations the system can build.

Headline ideas: `Specialty Coffee on Main Street`, `Open 7am | Oat Milk Available`, `Fresh Cold Brew in [Town]`, `Wifi, Plugs and Quiet Corners`, `Order Ahead or Walk In`.

Description idea: `Locally roasted espresso, fresh pastries and a friendly welcome. Open 7am to 5pm daily. Find us on Main Street.`

Add a location asset, a call asset and sitelinks to Menu, Order and Hours. Avoid claims you cannot prove, such as "best coffee in town".

## Step 5: Budget and Bidding in Stages

Start modest and tight. A working plan for a first month:

| Stage | Setup | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1 to 2 | Maximize clicks with a maximum CPC limit near your planning CPC | Gathers data under a cost cap |
| Weeks 3 to 6 | Review search terms, conversions, hours | Cut waste before automating |
| After steady conversions | Test Maximize conversions or Target CPA | Lets Google optimise toward actions |

Google's Maximize clicks help says it aims to get as many clicks as possible within your budget, that a maximum CPC limit sets a cap, and that a limit set too low may stop the strategy reaching its goal. It also notes the strategy does not factor in differences between mobile clicks. Its Maximize conversions page says the strategy will try to fully spend your daily budget, so watch spend closely if you switch. Google does not give a conversion-count threshold on the pages we could read, so treat "after steady conversions" as a judgement call. Our bid strategy comparison goes deeper.

**Use your opening hours.** Google's ad schedule help says you can adjust bids for specific days and times, and that average daily budget stays the same. The same page states these adjustments are available with Maximize clicks. Run it by your own hourly data: if most calls come 7am to 11am and Saturday mornings, raise bids there.

## Common Mistakes

- **Radius too wide.** A neighbourhood cafe rarely needs ten miles.
- **Only bidding "coffee near me".** Chains fight hardest on that. Add product, neighbourhood and need terms.
- **No negatives.** Ads show for recipes, machines and jobs.
- **Landing on a slow page.** Most local searches are on phones. Show hours, map, menu and a tap-to-call button above the fold.
- **Judging on clicks.** Track calls and directions, then ask new customers how they found you.
- **No target price.** Decide in advance what a new regular is worth to you, as in the math below.

## The Math

All rows marked (assumption) are mine. The Base CPC and conversion rate are taken from the LocaliQ restaurant figures above; the Tough and Strong columns are plausible swings, not benchmarks.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per click | $3.00 (assumption) | $2.05 (LocaliQ) | $1.50 (assumption) |
| Clicks | 200 | 293 | 400 |
| Click to call/directions rate | 4% (assumption) | 8.05% (LocaliQ) | 10% (assumption) |
| Calls and direction requests | 8 | 23.6 | 40 |
| Share who actually visit (assumption) | 30% | 40% | 50% |
| New customers | 2.4 | 9.4 | 20 |
| Ad cost per new customer | $250 | $64 | $30 |
| Margin per new customer over 6 months (assumption) | $58.50 | $58.50 | $58.50 |
| Margin from new customers | $140 | $552 | $1,170 |
| Net after ad spend | -$460 | -$48 | +$570 |

The $58.50 comes from an assumed 15 visits at a $6 average ticket and 65% margin (15 x $6 x 0.65). Check yours against your till data and loyalty app.

Two practical results:

- **Break-even click price.** Maximum CPC = margin per new customer x action rate x show-up rate. At the Base inputs: $58.50 x 0.0805 x 0.40 = $1.88. That is below the $2.05 benchmark, so a shop with a $6 ticket and no repeat-visit engine struggles on generic terms. A catering order, a higher-margin food menu or a loyalty programme changes the answer.
- **The lever is repeat visits.** Doubling visits per new customer from 15 to 30 doubles margin per customer to $117, which flips Base to roughly +$500. Ads fill the door; your loyalty scheme makes the customer worth buying.

If your ad offers a free first coffee, subtract its cost from the margin per new customer before you trust the table. Offers raise the show-up rate, but they eat the margin that pays for the ad.

## Do This Today

1. Write down your average ticket, margin and how many times a new customer visits in six months. Use the break-even CPC formula above.
2. Check your Business Profile: hours, photos, menu and order link. Fix gaps first.
3. Set up call and direction tracking, and confirm where local actions show in your reporting ("All conversions").
4. Build one campaign with a tight radius, six small ad groups, phrase and exact match, and the negative list above.
5. Put a 20-minute weekly slot in your calendar to read the search terms report and the hour-of-day numbers.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a coffee shop spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official number. Start with an amount you can lose while you learn, such as $300 to $600 a month, and work out the break-even click price. See [how much a coffee shop should spend](/blog/how-much-should-a-coffee-shop-spend-on-google-ads) for budget ranges.

**Q: Do I need Performance Max or Smart Bidding?**
A: Not at first. Start with Search and a capped manual or Maximize clicks setup to learn what a conversion costs. Test automation once actions are steady.

**Q: Which actions should I count as conversions?**
A: Calls, directions requests and online orders. Segment "All conversions" by conversion action to see the Google-hosted local actions.

**Q: Can I run ads only during opening hours?**
A: Yes, through ad scheduling. Google says you can raise or lower bids for specific days and times without changing your daily budget.

**Q: Are Google Ads better than Facebook or Instagram ads for a cafe?**
A: They catch different moments. Google meets people searching now; Meta suits events and offers. Most cafes start with Google, then add Meta.
