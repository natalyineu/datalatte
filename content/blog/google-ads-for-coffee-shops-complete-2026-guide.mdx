Google Ads for a coffee shop pays only when a new customer is worth more than the ads it took to win them. Take a $600 month at a $2.05 click, an 8.05% action rate and an assumed 40% of those people walking in. That is about 9 new customers, or roughly $64 in ads each. If a new regular is worth $58.50 in margin over six months (an assumption), the test is slightly under water. Setup discipline decides which side of zero you land on.

This is the step-by-step setup and budget guide. For a shorter overview of whether ads suit your shop, read the [Google Ads for coffee shops primer](/blog/google-ads-for-coffee-shops). It is written for single-location cafés in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Dollar figures are US benchmarks; run the same math in your own currency.

## Is Your Google Business Profile Ready First?

Fix the profile before paying for clicks. Ads that use location assets send people to your address and hours, and a half-empty profile wastes the budget. Check photos, hours, menu link and order link using our [coffee shop Business Profile guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization). Then move on to tracking.

## What Should a Coffee Shop Track as a Conversion?

Set up tracking before turning ads on. For a café the valuable actions are rarely form fills:

- **Calls** from the ad and from the website
- **Direction requests**
- **Online orders or menu clicks**, if you offer them

Google's [local actions help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9013908) says local actions are counted when a user performs an action specific to your physical store, such as clicking Directions on a Business Profile after seeing an ad. The default click-through window is 30 days, and the results appear in the "All conversions" column, so the main Conversions column may look empty.

For calls from a website, Google's [call conversion help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) says a call counts only if it lasts longer than a minimum length you set, and the default tag tracks one number per page. A directions tap is not a latte sold, which is why the math below applies a show-up rate.

## How Wide Should the Targeting Radius Be?

Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) says you can target a radius around a location and exclude areas, and that targeting is best effort with no guarantee of 100% accuracy. Choose the radius from your own customers: ask staff to note where regulars live or work for a week, or check loyalty data.

As a starting rule of thumb (not a Google rule), try about one mile in dense city areas and two to five miles in suburbs and small towns. Widen only if the data supports it. Review the location settings your campaign type shows for who sees your ads.

## Which Keywords Work for a Coffee Shop?

Google's [match type help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) says broad match can show on related searches, phrase match on searches that carry the meaning of your keyword, and exact match on searches with the same meaning or intent, with the most control and fewest searches. It also says "It's critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match." With a small budget, start with phrase and exact.

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic | "coffee shop near me", "cafe open now" | Phrase + exact |
| Neighbourhood | "coffee shop [neighbourhood]", "cafe [town]" | Phrase + exact |
| Product | "oat milk latte near me", "cold brew near me" | Phrase |
| Need-based | "cafe with wifi [neighbourhood]", "dog friendly cafe" | Phrase |
| Occasion | "coffee catering [town]", "cafe for meetings" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your shop name] | Exact |

Keep each group to a handful of closely related terms. Our [coffee shop keyword list](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-coffee-shops-in-2026) has more ideas.

Add negatives on day one: `jobs`, `hiring`, `recipe`, `how to make`, `machine`, `grinder`, `wholesale`, `franchise`, `pods`, `instant`. Google's [negative keyword help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972) says negatives do not match close variants, so add plurals separately. Read the search terms report weekly.

## How Do You Write Ads That Mirror the Search?

Google's [responsive search ad requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791) allow 3 to 15 headlines of up to 30 characters and 2 to 4 descriptions of up to 90 characters. The page says pinning an asset makes it show only in that position and is not recommended for most advertisers, so pin sparingly.

Headline ideas: `Specialty Coffee on Main Street`, `Open 7am | Oat Milk Available`, `Fresh Cold Brew in [Town]`, `Order Ahead or Walk In`. Add a location asset, a call asset and sitelinks to menu, order and hours. Google's [assets help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7331111) says call assets can be scheduled to show only when you can take calls, and that extra assets appear only when predicted to improve performance and the ad meets a minimum Ad Rank. Avoid unprovable claims such as "best coffee in town".

## How Should a Coffee Shop Set Budget and Bidding?

Start modest and capped. A working plan for the first weeks:

| Stage | Setup | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1 to 2 | Maximize clicks with a maximum CPC limit near your planning CPC | Gathers data under a cost cap |
| Weeks 3 to 6 | Review search terms, conversions and hours | Cut waste before automating |
| After steady conversions | Test Maximize conversions or Target CPA | Lets Google optimise toward actions |

Google's [Maximize clicks help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268626) says a maximum CPC limit caps bids, that a limit set too low may stop the strategy reaching its goal, and that it does not account for differences between mobile and other clicks. Its [Maximize conversions page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7381968) says the strategy will try to fully spend your average daily budget and requires conversion tracking. It gives no conversion-count threshold, so "steady conversions" is a judgement call.

Use your opening hours. Google's [ad schedule help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6372644) says schedule bid adjustments are available with Maximize clicks and that your average daily budget does not change. If most actions arrive 7am to 11am and Saturday mornings, raise bids there.

## What Mistakes Waste a Coffee Shop's Ad Budget?

- **Radius too wide.** A neighbourhood café rarely needs ten miles.
- **Only bidding "coffee near me".** Add product, neighbourhood and need terms.
- **No negatives.** Ads show for recipes, machines and jobs.
- **A slow landing page.** Show hours, map, menu and a tap-to-call button above the fold.
- **Judging on clicks.** Track calls and directions, then ask new customers how they found you.
- **No target price.** Decide in advance what a new regular is worth.

## What Does a $600 Month Return? A Worked Example

Every row marked (assumption) is illustrative. Base CPC and action rate use the LocaliQ restaurant figures above; the Tough and Strong columns are plausible swings, not benchmarks. LocaliQ's rate is a general conversion rate, so applying it to calls and directions is itself an assumption.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per click | $3.00 (assumption) | $2.05 (LocaliQ) | $1.50 (assumption) |
| Clicks | 200 | 293 | 400 |
| Click to call or directions | 4% (assumption) | 8.05% (LocaliQ) | 10% (assumption) |
| Calls and direction requests | 8 | 23.6 | 40 |
| Share who actually visit | 30% (assumption) | 40% (assumption) | 50% (assumption) |
| New customers | 2.4 | 9.4 | 20 |
| Ad cost per new customer | $250 | $64 | $30 |
| Margin per new customer, 6 months | $58.50 | $58.50 | $58.50 |
| Net after ad spend | -$460 | -$48 | +$570 |

The $58.50 assumes 15 visits at a $6 ticket and 65% margin (15 x $6 x 0.65). Check yours against till and loyalty data.

- **Break-even click price** = margin per new customer x action rate x show-up rate. At Base: $58.50 x 0.0805 x 0.40 = $1.88, below the $2.05 benchmark. A shop with a $6 ticket and no repeat-visit engine struggles on generic terms.
- **Repeat visits are the lever.** Doubling visits from 15 to 30 doubles margin per customer to $117 and turns Base into roughly +$500.

## Do This Today

1. Write down your average ticket, margin and visits per new customer over six months, then compute break-even CPC.
2. Check your Business Profile: hours, photos, menu and order link.
3. Set up call and direction tracking and find it under "All conversions".
4. Build one campaign with a tight radius, six small ad groups, phrase and exact match and the negative list.
5. Block 20 minutes weekly for the search terms report and hour-of-day numbers.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a coffee shop spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official number. Start with an amount you can afford to lose while learning, such as $300 to $600 a month, and work out the break-even click price. See [how much a coffee shop should spend on Google Ads](/blog/how-much-should-a-coffee-shop-spend-on-google-ads).

**Q: Do I need Performance Max or Smart Bidding?**
A: Not at first. Start with Search and capped Maximize clicks, then test automation once actions are steady.

**Q: Which actions should I count?**
A: Calls, directions requests and online orders. Local actions appear in the "All conversions" column.

**Q: Can I run ads only during opening hours?**
A: You can adjust bids by day and time with Maximize clicks without changing your average daily budget, per Google's ad schedule help.

**Q: Are Google Ads better than Facebook or Instagram ads for a café?**
A: They catch different moments. Google meets people searching now; Meta suits events and offers. Most cafés start with Google.
