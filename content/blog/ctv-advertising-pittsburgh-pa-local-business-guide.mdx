Connected TV (CTV) advertising lets a Pittsburgh local business place video ads in front of households watching streaming services, with targeting by ZIP code and demographics. CTV builds brand familiarity rather than capturing immediate clicks, so it works best alongside search. This guide covers how CTV differs from Google Ads, which platforms to compare, how to set targeting, a worked budget with labelled assumptions, and how to measure results over 60 to 90 days.

## How does CTV differ from Google Ads for a Pittsburgh business?

Search ads reach people who already typed a need into Google, while CTV reaches households during shows they chose to watch. The two channels answer different questions. Search captures intent; CTV creates the recognition that makes a name come to mind before the next search.

Google's own documentation describes Display campaigns as a way to reach people across millions of websites, apps, and Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail. The Display Network reaches [35 million websites and apps](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190), which shows how far display formats extend beyond search results. CTV is a separate inventory type, so Display benchmarks should not be read as CTV performance.

Benchmarks from WordStream help set expectations for paid search. Its all-industry averages were a [3.17% search click-through rate and a $2.69 search cost per click](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks), with display averages lower on both measures. Those figures are useful for judging search campaigns, not for forecasting a CTV campaign.

## Which streaming platforms should a Pittsburgh business compare?

Most Pittsburgh local businesses should compare platforms on three points: whether the audience fits the business, whether ZIP-code targeting is available in the buying tool, and what the minimum spend and ad formats require. Platform names and ad tiers change often, so confirm current terms in each platform's own ad console before committing budget.

| Platform | Where it may fit | What to verify before buying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hulu | Broad household reach and a common starting point | Ad manager access, minimum spend, ZIP targeting options |
| Peacock | Sports and live event viewing | Live-event inventory and whether it matches your audience |
| Tubi | Free ad-supported viewing with broad audiences | Targeting depth compared with paid tiers |
| Amazon Prime Video | Ad-supported tier with shopping-related signals | Eligibility, audience age profile, and targeting controls |
| Paramount+ with Pluto TV | Wide general reach across ad-supported inventory | Whether inventory is bought directly or through a programmatic partner |

Programmatic platforms such as StackAdapt and The Trade Desk can buy across several streaming services from one dashboard. They usually carry higher minimums than a single platform's ad manager, so they suit businesses that have already tested CTV once. ## How should ZIP-code and daypart targeting be set up?

ZIP-code targeting is the main lever for a local business. Start with the ZIP codes where existing customers live, which can be checked against past order addresses, booking records, or loyalty sign-ups. Add a radius around the business only if those ZIP codes do not capture enough of the customer base.

Demographic layers can narrow the audience further. A fitness studio might target adults in the age ranges and household income bands that match its membership prices. A family salon might prioritise households with children. Each added layer reduces the audience, so test one narrow setting at a time.

Daypart choices affect cost and creative fit. Evening viewing usually commands the highest demand, while morning or weekend slots may be cheaper to buy. A breakfast café can use a morning daypart for a creative that shows the menu. Ask the platform for current rates in each daypart before assuming a discount, since pricing differs by inventory and season.

## What does a worked CTV budget look like?

The example below uses assumed inputs, not published rates. Replace them with figures from your own platform quote before planning spend.

- Monthly budget: $500 (assumption)
- Assumed CPM, meaning cost per 1,000 impressions: $25 (assumption)
- Impressions purchased: $500 ÷ $25 × 1,000 = 20,000 (calculated)
- Assumed frequency, meaning ad views per household per month: 4 (assumption)
- Estimated unique households reached: 20,000 ÷ 4 = 5,000 (calculated)

If a quote shows a higher CPM, the same budget reaches fewer impressions and households. Recalculate before approving a larger flight. A 4-week trial at a lower budget can test whether the creative and ZIP selection are working before a three-month commitment.

## What steps should a local business follow to launch?

A practical launch follows a short sequence. Each step creates a checkpoint before more budget is committed.

1. Export the ZIP codes of your last 100 to 200 customers, where records allow, and rank them by count.
2. Choose a platform and confirm minimum spend, ZIP targeting, and ad specifications in its help pages.
3. Build a 15-second creative with the brand name early, the offer in the middle, and the location or phone number at the end.
4. Run a 4-week trial and record impressions, reach, and cost against your assumed CPM.
5. Keep the ZIP codes that show strong branded search or direct visits, and drop the rest.

## How do you measure whether CTV is working?

CTV does not produce click data that matches a search campaign, so measurement has to look at signals around the ad. Track branded search volume in Google Business Profile insights or Google Ads, direct website visits during flight dates, and new-customer answers to a consistent "how did you hear about us?" question. Compare these against the weeks before the campaign.

Give the campaign 60 to 90 days before judging it. Branded search on paid channels can also be tracked through [Microsoft Advertising for local businesses](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business), which helps show whether a rise in brand queries is spread across more than one search engine. ## How do CTV and search ads work together?

CTV and search serve different stages of the same decision. The television ad makes the business familiar; a search ad captures the person who later needs that service and types the name or category. Running both lets the search ad benefit from the recognition CTV builds.

Keep the message consistent across channels. If the CTV creative promotes a January membership offer, the search ad should lead to a landing page with the same offer. ## FAQ

### Is CTV advertising worth it for a small business in Pittsburgh?

It can be, if the business has a local customer base and can wait 60 to 90 days for brand effects to show. Start with a short trial, measure branded search and direct visits, and scale only if those signals move.

### What is the difference between CTV and Display advertising?

CTV places video ads in streaming shows and films on TV screens. Display ads run on websites, apps, and Google-owned properties, and the Google Display Network is one example. The two use different inventory and should be measured separately.

### How much should a Pittsburgh business budget for a first CTV campaign?

The budget depends on the platform's quoted CPM, the ZIP codes chosen, and the frequency target. The worked example above uses a $500 monthly budget with assumed rates. Use your own quote to replace those numbers before committing.

### Can CTV replace Google Ads?

No. CTV builds recognition, while Google Ads captures people who are searching now. Most local businesses use search for immediate demand and treat CTV as a complement, especially in the weeks before a busy season.

### Do I need a programmatic platform to run CTV?

Not always. Some platforms offer self-serve ad managers with ZIP targeting. Programmatic tools add access to more streaming services but usually require higher minimums and more setup.
