Google Ads can bring in teeth whitening clients by showing your service to people searching "teeth whitening near me" at the moment they are ready to book. Start with a tight local radius, a clear offer, compliant wording and a landing page that makes booking easy, then track calls and bookings to see what each client costs.

Teeth whitening is a well-known, searched-for cosmetic treatment, which makes it a good fit for search ads. It is also competitive and regulated, so setup matters. This guide covers the regulatory points to check, how to build the campaign and how to measure results.

## First, check what you are allowed to offer and advertise

Rules for teeth whitening differ by country and sometimes by region, so confirm yours before you spend a cent:

- In the UK and EU, cosmetic whitening using higher-strength peroxide-based products is generally limited to registered dental professionals, and non-dentists face tight restrictions. Check your regulator's guidance.
- In the US, many states allow non-dentist whitening in some forms, while others treat it as the practice of dentistry. Check your state dental board.
- In Australia and Canada, rules also vary by state or province.
- Dental advertising is often regulated by a professional body. Claims about results, "best" or "safest" and before-and-after images may be restricted.

Google also has policies on healthcare and medicines advertising, and may require certifications in some countries. Review Google's current advertising policies and the rules for your region. This article is general guidance, not legal advice.

## Who searches for teeth whitening

People searching for whitening typically fall into a few groups:

- Someone with an event coming up, like a wedding, holiday or job interview
- People comparing in-chair treatment with home kits
- Regular patients who want a cosmetic add-on
- People researching safety, cost and sensitivity

Your ads and landing page should speak to the group you most want, such as event-driven clients or existing dental patients.

## Choose keywords by intent

| Intent | Example searches | Comments |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready to book | "teeth whitening near me", "teeth whitening [town]", "book teeth whitening" | Highest value |
| Comparison | "laser vs LED teeth whitening", "dentist vs home whitening" | Good for content, lower for ads |
| Price | "teeth whitening cost", "how much is teeth whitening" | Show your pricing clearly |
| Product | "whitening strips", "whitening kit" | Often shoppers, not service clients |
| Brand | Your business name | Cheap and worth protecting |

Use phrase and exact match for core terms, and consider whether broad match makes sense once you have conversion data.

## Build a negative keyword list

Whitening searches attract many people who will never book. Common negatives include "DIY", "home remedy", "baking soda", "charcoal", "strips" (unless you sell them), "kit", "cheap", "free", "jobs", "course", "training", "supplies" and "wholesale". Check the search terms report weekly at first and keep adding to the list.

## Structure the campaign

1. **Campaign**: Search, with location targeting set to a radius or specific postcodes that matches how far clients will travel.
2. **Ad groups**: Separate groups for in-chair whitening, laser whitening, whitening for events and brand terms.
3. **Responsive search ads**: Provide multiple headlines and descriptions mentioning the treatment, the location, the offer and a call to action.
4. **Assets**: Call, location, sitelinks (Pricing, Before You Book, FAQ) and callouts (Same-day appointments, Fully qualified, Free consultation).
5. **Schedule**: Show ads when your phone or online booking is staffed, unless you have 24 hour online booking.

## Write compliant, useful ads

Focus on clear facts rather than dramatic claims:

- State the service and place: "Teeth Whitening in [Town] | Book Online".
- Mention qualified staff or the registered clinic, if that is true.
- Offer something concrete: a free shade consultation, evening appointments, finance options or an event package.
- Avoid unsupported promises ("guaranteed X shades whiter") and absolute claims.
- Do not use before and after images in ads where regulations restrict them.

## Landing page essentials

Send ad clicks to a dedicated page rather than a generic home page. Include:

- A headline that matches the search ("Teeth Whitening in [Town]")
- What the treatment involves, how long it takes and what to expect afterwards
- Honest information on sensitivity and who it is not suitable for
- Clear prices or "from" prices, which typically reduces tyre-kickers
- Qualifications and registrations
- Reviews from real clients
- A simple booking form or online scheduler, plus a tap-to-call number
- An FAQ answering safety, results and aftercare (see our [FAQ page guide](/blog/website-faq-page-local-business))

Test the page on a phone, since most local searches happen there. Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) provide a checklist.

## Budget and bidding

Cosmetic dental keywords can be among the pricier local search terms, and costs vary widely by city. A reasonable approach:

- Estimate what a whitening client is worth, including follow-up treatments and ongoing visits.
- Decide the most you would pay to win a new client.
- Start with a daily budget that lets you collect real data over several weeks. Many small clinics begin in the range of $20 to $50 a day or the local equivalent, then adjust.
- Use manual or conversion-based bidding as you gather data.

See our guide to [Google Ads budgets for small business](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business) for a method to work back from client value.

## Track calls and bookings

You need to know which clicks became clients. Set up:

- Conversion tracking for form submissions and online bookings
- Call tracking for calls from ads and from your landing page (see [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business))
- Google Business Profile call and direction metrics
- A "How did you hear about us?" question at booking

Follow the guide to [conversion tracking setup](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup). Be careful with privacy: avoid sending health-related personal data to advertising platforms, and follow the rules that apply to your region and profession.

## Support your ads with your free local presence

Ads work better when the rest of your presence is credible:

- Keep your Google Business Profile accurate, with photos of your clinic and team
- Collect reviews, and reply professionally while respecting patient confidentiality
- Keep your website fast and clear
- Follow up on enquiries quickly

## Optimise on a routine

- **Weekly**: Review search terms, add negatives, check calls and bookings.
- **Fortnightly**: Test one new headline or offer.
- **Monthly**: Review cost per booking, compare devices and times, and decide on budget.
- **Seasonally**: Plan for wedding season, holidays and January, when interest in cosmetic treatments typically rises.

## FAQ

**Q: Are teeth whitening ads allowed on Google?**
A: Generally yes, but advertising rules for healthcare and cosmetic dental services vary by country. Check Google's policies and your professional regulator, and avoid prohibited claims or imagery.

**Q: How much do Google Ads cost for teeth whitening?**
A: Costs per click vary by location and competition. Test with a modest budget for several weeks, then judge by cost per booking rather than per click.

**Q: What keywords work best for teeth whitening?**
A: Local, booking-intent searches such as "teeth whitening near me" or "teeth whitening in [town]" tend to convert best. Use negative keywords to filter out DIY and product searches.

**Q: Should I send people to my home page?**
A: No. A dedicated whitening page with price, process, FAQs and a booking button generally converts better.
