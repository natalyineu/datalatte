LocaliQ's healthcare benchmarks put the median General Dentistry click at $7.03. At that price a $1,500 month buys about 213 clicks. If 7.74% of clicks become enquiries (LocaliQ's General Dentistry conversion rate) and 55% of enquiries turn up and pay, you spend about $165 in ads for each whitening client. The 55% is my assumption, not a benchmark, and it moves the answer more than anything else on this page. Drop it to 40% and the cost per client jumps to about $230.

This guide is for dental practices and cosmetic whitening studios in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Whitening is different from most local services because in some places who may legally do it is the first question, and your ads inherit that. We cover the rules we could verify, a Search campaign setup, tracking that keeps health details out of ad tools, and the math. It is general information, not legal advice.

## First Question: Are You Allowed to Offer It?

Do not spend a cent before you can answer this. What we could verify:

**United Kingdom.** The General Dental Council's [tooth whitening position statement](https://www.gdc-uk.org/docs/default-source/what-is-the-legal-position/tooth-whitening-position-statement.pdf) (dated 11 July 2016) says:

- Cosmetic whitening products that contain or release more than 6% hydrogen peroxide may not be supplied or administered.
- Products between 0.1% and 6% should not be made directly available to the consumer, other than through treatment by a registered dentist, dental hygienist, dental therapist or clinical dental technician working to a dentist's prescription. Breach is a criminal offence, enforced by Trading Standards.
- Products below 0.1% remain freely available.
- Anyone not registered with the GDC is not entitled to provide tooth whitening, because it falls within the practice of dentistry, and the GDC says it will prosecute illegal providers.

The charity Oral Health Foundation's [tooth whitening and the law page](https://www.dentalhealth.org/tooth-whitening-and-the-law) (updated February 2020) says the same in plain terms: over-the-counter kits, including internet-bought ones, can legally contain only up to 0.1%, and the patient must be over 18. These are older documents, and we have not checked whether the rules have changed since. Confirm with the GDC before you launch.

**United States.** We could not find one national rule, and we will not guess. What we can say, from the [Supreme Court's 2015 decision](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/13-534) in North Carolina State Board of Dental Examiners v. FTC, is that the question has been fought over. The North Carolina board sent at least 47 cease-and-desist letters to non-dentist whitening providers from 2006, the FTC challenged that, and the Court held that a board controlled by practising dentists can claim antitrust immunity only if the state actively supervises it. The case was about competition law, not about whether a given state allows non-dentist whitening. That is set by each state, so read your state dental board's rules.

**Australia and Canada.** We have not verified the rules. Check your state, territory or provincial dental regulator.

If you are a dentist, this section is mostly reassurance. If you run a non-dental studio, it is the whole business plan.

## Google's Policies: What Applies and What Is Unclear

### Health is a sensitive interest category

Google's [personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) lists Health among the sensitive interest categories and says advertisers in them "are unable to use advertiser-curated audiences". The [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855) page names the unsupported tools: Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion and lookalike segments. In-market segments, affinity audiences, life events and location targeting remain available.

Does teeth whitening count as Health? The page does not mention dental care. It lists "invasive medical procedures, including cosmetic surgery, surgical procedures, or injections", and whitening is none of those. So Google does not say either way. Our practical reading, not Google's: plan a Search-only account that does not depend on remarketing, and treat any audience tool you do get as a bonus. Keyword targeting is not an audience, so your main tool is untouched.

### Do not upload client records as conversions

Google's [customer data policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7475709) list "health or medical information, like purchases of medical services" as sensitive and say you may not upload conversion information related to sensitive categories. Whether a whitening payment counts is again unstated. The safe habit, and the one our health-sector guides recommend, is to count on-site events (a booking form submit, a call over a set length) and never push patient or treatment records from your practice software into Google Ads. For the full version of this reasoning see [Google Ads for pediatricians](/blog/google-ads-for-pediatricians) and [Google Ads for mental health professionals](/blog/google-ads-for-mental-health-professionals).

### Claims

Google's [unreliable claims policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936857) bars claims that "entice the user with an improbable result (even if this result is possible)". It says testimonials claiming specific results need a visible disclaimer that results are not guaranteed and can vary. It does not address before-and-after photos specifically. So "up to 8 shades whiter in 45 minutes" is the line most likely to cause trouble, and "Free shade check with a registered dentist" is not.

In the UK, the advertising regulator's [teeth whitening advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/teeth-whitening.html) goes further. It warns that whitening by non-dental professionals could be illegal and tells clinics to check with the GDC. It says efficacy claims need relevant evidence, reports that the ASA upheld a complaint against a "whiten teeth by three shades" claim the advertiser could not substantiate, and says before-and-after images must not be exaggerated or digitally altered. Advice from CAP and the ASA is guidance and does not bind the ASA itself.

If you sell take-home kits in the US, the FTC's [Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) says health claims need "competent and reliable scientific evidence", and that the same legal principles apply to "any health-related product". Whether a cosmetic whitening kit is a health-related product is for your lawyer; the safe course is to claim only what you can show.

## Pick the Campaign and Build It

**Start with a Search campaign.** Whitening is a searched-for treatment, and Search catches "teeth whitening near me" when someone is ready. Keep one campaign per location.

### Location

Pull your last 50 whitening clients and map their postcodes or zip codes, then draw a radius that covers most of them. For a tight radius, consider presence-only targeting, which is my recommendation rather than a Google rule. Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) explains the options.

### Keywords by intent

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready to book | "teeth whitening near me", [teeth whitening your town], "book teeth whitening" | Phrase + exact |
| Professional | "dentist teeth whitening", "laser teeth whitening", "in chair whitening" | Phrase |
| Event | "teeth whitening before wedding", "whitening package" | Phrase |
| Price | "teeth whitening cost your town" | Phrase (show prices) |
| Brand | [your practice name] | Exact |

Negatives from day one: `diy`, `home remedy`, `baking soda`, `charcoal`, `strips`, `kit`, `pen`, `toothpaste`, `jobs`, `course`, `training`, `supplies`, `wholesale`, `free`, `cheap`, `pdf`. Add `kit` and `strips` only if you do not sell them. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/negative-keywords-google-ads) covers lists and levels.

### Ads

Use the full set of responsive search ad fields. Examples that stick to verifiable facts: `Teeth Whitening in [Town]`, `Provided by Registered Dentists` (only if true), `Free Shade Consultation`, `Evening Appointments`, `Prices Shown, Book Online`. Add a call asset, a location asset, and sitelinks for Prices, Before You Book and FAQ.

## Landing Page and Tracking

Send clicks to a dedicated page: headline matching the search, how the treatment works, how long it takes, who it is not suitable for, honest price (or "from" price), your qualifications or registration, real reviews and a booking button with a tap-to-call number. See [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices).

Track three conversions: booking form submits on a thank-you page with no health details in the URL; calls from the ad; calls from the page. Google's [website call conversions help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) says a call counts only if it lasts longer than a minimum length you set (it gives no default), and that the default tag tracks one number per page. See [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) and the [conversion tracking setup guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup).

Then ask every new client how they found you. That log is your real data and stays in your own systems.

## The Math

A $1,500 monthly budget. Only the Base CPC and click-to-enquiry rate come from LocaliQ's General Dentistry medians; everything else is an **assumption**. Replace the assumptions after one month.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget | $1,500 | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Cost per click | $9.00 (assumption) | $7.03 (LocaliQ) | $5.00 (assumption) |
| Clicks | 167 | 213 | 300 |
| Click to enquiry | 4% (assumption) | 7.74% (LocaliQ) | 10% (assumption) |
| Enquiries | 6.7 | 16.5 | 30 |
| Enquiry to paid treatment (assumption) | 40% | 55% | 70% |
| Whitening clients | 2.7 | 9.1 | 21 |
| Ad cost per client | $562 | $165 | $71 |
| Treatment price (assumption) | $400 | $400 | $400 |
| Ad cost as share of first treatment price | 141% | 41% | 18% |

How to use it:

- **Your maximum click price.** Max CPC = most you will pay per client x enquiry rate x paid rate. If you will pay $150 per client, at 7.74% and 55% your ceiling is $150 x 0.0774 x 0.55 = $6.39 a click. Above that you lose money on average.
- **Whitening is often an entry treatment.** If a whitening client also books hygiene visits or other work, their value is higher than the $400. That is your data to supply, not ours, and Strong is better than any benchmark here, so do not plan around it.
- **The enquiry-to-treatment rate is a front-desk problem.** Answering within minutes and offering a booking link moves it without touching Google.

Also work out your capacity. If whitening needs a chair for an hour, 21 extra clients in a month is a staffing question.

## Review Weekly

Each week read the search terms report and add negatives, pause keywords that spent about twice your target cost per enquiry with none, and check which hours calls go unanswered. Every two weeks test one new headline or offer. Seasonal demand (weddings, holidays) is common sense, but we have no verified data on it, so check your own account history before you scale. A complete Business Profile and recent reviews help here too. ## Do This Today

1. Confirm in writing who may legally offer whitening where you operate (GDC in the UK, your state dental board in the US, your regulator elsewhere).
2. Map your last 50 whitening clients and set your radius from it.
3. Work out the most you will pay for one client and compute your maximum CPC with the formula above.
4. Build one Search campaign with the five ad groups, the negative list and no dependence on remarketing.
5. Set up form and call conversions before turning ads on, and add "how did you find us" to your booking form.

## FAQ

**Q: Are teeth whitening ads allowed on Google?**
A: Google's policies we checked do not ban them, but they do not mention dental whitening by name either. The bigger risk is legal: in the UK, GDC says only registered dental professionals may provide whitening. Check your own regulator.

**Q: Can I use remarketing for whitening?**
A: Unclear. Google's Health category restricts remarketing-style audiences and does not say whether whitening is included. Plan without it.

**Q: How much should I spend per month?**
A: There is no official figure. At LocaliQ's $7.03 median General Dentistry click, $1,500 buys about 213 clicks and $500 about 71. Work backwards from what a client is worth to you.

**Q: Can I show before-and-after photos?**
A: Google's unreliable claims policy does not address them specifically. The UK ASA says they must not be exaggerated or digitally altered. Check your regulator first.

**Q: How long before ads bring in clients?**
A: Calls can start within days. Give it four to six weeks, my rule of thumb, before judging cost per client.
