## Who Uses Snapchat, and Does It Match Gym Buyers?

Pew's 2025 survey, fielded February 5 to June 18, shows Snapchat use of 26% among all US adults, 58% among ages 18 to 29 and 31% among ages 30 to 49. For comparison, Instagram stood at 50% overall, 80% for ages 18 to 29 and 62% for ages 30 to 49 ([Pew Research Center: Social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)).

That tells you two things. Snapchat is a young-adult channel. And Instagram reaches a larger share of both age groups above, so Snapchat is a supplement, not a replacement.

The fit with fitness is real. The Health and Fitness Association reports 81 million Americans were members of a fitness facility in 2025, 26.1% of the population aged six and older, and that Gen Z adults aged 18 to 24 had the highest membership penetration of any age group at 35.5% ([Health and Fitness Association](https://www.healthandfitness.org/?p=5212)).

Younger adults both use Snapchat and join gyms. Whether they join yours depends on price, location and offer.

## What Does Snapchat Require You to Spend?

Snap's developer documentation sets the minimum daily budget for an ad squad at 5,000,000 micro-currency units, which is 5 units of currency, in every supported currency ([Snap Marketing API: Ad squads](https://developers.snap.com/api/marketing-api/Ads-API/ad-squads)). It also lists a minimum bid of 10,000 micro-currency units.

Treat 5 USD a day as a floor, not a plan. A single ad squad at that level spends about 150 USD in a 30-day month, which is a thin sample for a studio that needs trial signups.

Compare with search. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks show a median cost per lead of 67.36 USD for Health and Fitness search ads, with a 6.94% conversion rate, against 66.69 USD across all industries ([LocaliQ search advertising benchmarks](https://www.localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/)). Search reaches people who are already looking for a gym. That is the bar your Snapchat cost per trial has to beat or justify.

## How Do You Target People Near Your Studio?

Snap's targeting documentation supports point-radius targeting. The radius is set in meters, with a minimum of 96 meters and a maximum of 100,000 meters, which is roughly 0.06 to 62 miles. Units can be meters, kilometers, feet or miles, and a targeting spec can hold up to 500 circles ([Snap Marketing API: Targeting](https://developers.snap.com/api/marketing-api/Ads-API/targeting)). Location categories are a separate option, with a proximity setting around the chosen category.

The same page has an easy-to-miss detail. The minimum age field accepts 13 to 35, and "if min_age is left out, targeting uses 13 as the minimum age." A gym ad should not default to teens. Set the minimum age to 18, or higher if your membership terms require it.

A sensible first setup:

1. Draw circles around your studio and any nearby offices or campuses you can reach.
2. Set age 18 to 34, or the range of your best current members.
3. Keep a single offer: a free class, a week pass or a low-cost trial.
4. Send people to a page where the trial can be booked in under a minute on a phone.

## Worked Example: What Is a Trial Worth?

All figures are **assumptions** for illustration. Replace them with your own.

- Revenue per member: 60 USD a month.
- Average stay: 6 months.
- Contribution margin: 50%, so each member is worth 180 USD.
- Trial signups that become members: 30%.
- Monthly Snapchat spend: 450 USD (15 USD a day).

Break-even cost per member is 180 USD. With a 30% conversion, break-even cost per trial is 54 USD.

| Cost per trial signup | Trials from 450 USD | Members | Contribution | Net |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25 USD | 18 | 5.4 | 972 USD | +522 USD |
| 50 USD | 9 | 2.7 | 486 USD | +36 USD |
| 100 USD | 4.5 | 1.35 | 243 USD | -207 USD |

How to read it: at 50 USD per trial the test barely pays. At 100 USD it loses money. The trial-to-member rate does most of the work in this table. A studio that converts 15% of trials has a break-even cost per trial of 27 USD, so follow-up after the trial matters as much as the ad.

<Callout type="warning" title="Stop Rule">
Set your break-even cost per trial before launch, and pause if the first 450 USD lands above it. Snap's documentation does not publish a number of days or conversions a campaign needs to settle.
</Callout>

## How Do You Measure Walk-Ins From Snapchat?

Use three checks, in this order:

1. **A dedicated trial link or page.** Anyone who books there came from the ad.
2. **A code at the front desk.** Ask new members how they heard about you, and log it.
3. **Booking counts by week.** Compare weeks with and without the campaign.

None of these is perfect. Snap's pages used here do not document an in-store visit measure, so do not rely on one. Keep the numbers in a simple sheet and look at cost per trial and cost per member monthly.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the minimum budget for Snapchat ads?**
A: Snap's developer documentation sets a minimum daily budget of 5 USD for an ad squad. That is a technical floor. Your real minimum is whatever produces enough trial signups to judge.

**Q: Can I target a few blocks around my gym?**
A: Yes. Snap's targeting documentation allows radius targeting from 96 meters up to 100,000 meters, with up to 500 circles per targeting spec.

**Q: Will my ads show to teenagers?**
A: They can if you do not set an age. Snap's documentation says the minimum age defaults to 13 when left out. Set 18 or higher.

**Q: How many young adults use Snapchat?**
A: Pew found 58% of US adults aged 18 to 29 and 31% of those aged 30 to 49 use Snapchat, in a survey fielded February to June 2025.

**Q: Should a small studio start with Snapchat?**
A: Usually not first. Search ads and a complete Google Business Profile reach people already looking. Test Snapchat once those are working and you have a clear trial offer.
