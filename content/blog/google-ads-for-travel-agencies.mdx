For a travel agency, Google Ads works best when you bid on people who want help planning a specific trip (for example "honeymoon planner near me" or "Italy tour packages") rather than on broad destination searches. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put Travel search ads at $2.14 per click and $44.70 per lead, so a lead is cheap and the real question is how many leads become paid bookings.

This guide covers what to bid on, how to set the account up, what rules apply to agencies and a worked break-even example.

## What Does Google Ads Cost for a Travel Agency?

LocaliQ's [2026 search benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/) show Travel at a 9.32% click-through rate, a $2.14 cost per click, a 5.83% conversion rate and a $44.70 cost per lead. The all-industry averages are $5.42 per click and $66.69 per lead.

These figures combine Google and Microsoft Ads data from thousands of campaigns, so your own numbers will differ by destination, season and competition. A lead is usually an enquiry, not a sale. For general pricing see the [Google Ads cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-google-ads-cost-for-small-businesses-real-2026-pricing-breakdown).

## Which Keywords Should a Travel Agency Bid On?

Start with searches that show planning or booking intent, and keep them in separate ad groups.

1. **Service intent:** travel agent near me, honeymoon planner, group trip organiser.
2. **Trip plus agent:** Italy tour packages, Disney vacation planner, cruise booking agent.
3. **Your own name:** protect it with a small budget.

Avoid broad research terms such as "cheap flights" or "best beaches" until you are tracking results, since these attract people who are not looking for an agent. Add negative keywords such as jobs, courses, free and DIY. Our guide to [negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) shows how, and the [quality score guide](/blog/google-ads-quality-score-guide) explains how relevant ads lower costs.

## How Should You Set Up the Campaign?

A simple structure beats a complicated one for a small agency.

1. One Search campaign per trip type (honeymoons, families, groups), each with its own budget.
2. Location targeting set to the areas you can serve or sell to.
3. Ad copy that names the trip, the planning service and one proof point you can back up, such as years in business or a certification.
4. A landing page for each trip type with a single enquiry form and a phone number.
5. Conversion tracking on form submissions and calls before any money is spent.

Google's [conversion help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883?hl=en) describes tracking calls made from your website with Google forwarding numbers, counted when a call lasts longer than a minimum length you set. Without tracking, you cannot tell which keywords bring bookings.

## What Rules Apply to Travel Sellers?

Some US states regulate sellers of travel. California's Attorney General says all sellers of travel must [register](https://oag.ca.gov/travel) and display the registration number on all advertising. Other states, including Florida, Hawaii and Washington, are commonly listed as having similar laws, but the sources found were older blog posts and attorney pages, so check each state's official site.

If you sell to residents of those states, confirm your obligations before running ads there. This is not legal advice.

## What Does It Cost in Practice? A Worked Example

Everything below is an **assumption**: a $1,500 monthly budget at LocaliQ's Travel cost per lead of $44.70, a $300 commission per booking, and two closing rates.

| Step | 15% of leads book | 10% of leads book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Leads | about 33.6 | about 33.6 |
| Bookings | about 5.0 | about 3.4 |
| Cost per booking | about $298 | about $446 |
| Commission at $300 | about $1,512 | about $1,008 |
| Result | about break-even | loss of about $492 |

At a 15% close rate you roughly break even on the first sale, so the campaign pays only if clients rebook or refer others. At 10% it loses money. Your profit per booking and your close rate decide whether ads work, so measure both before scaling. Replace each assumption with your own.

## Should You Use Performance Max?

Performance Max can add reach, but it hands more control to Google. Google's help says search themes (up to 50 per asset group) can guide it, and brand exclusions apply only to Search, Shopping and YouTube search inventory.

For a small agency, begin with Search so you can see queries and costs, and consider Performance Max later once conversions are reliable. The comparison in [Performance Max vs standard Google Ads](/blog/performance-max-vs-standard-google-ads-which-should-you-run) walks through the trade-offs.

## How Does Your Google Business Profile Help?

Local searches such as travel agent near me show map results as well as ads. A complete profile with accurate hours, services, photos and reviews helps you appear there without paying per click. Follow the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist), and see the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) for deciding how much goes to ads and how much to the profile.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do Google Ads cost for a travel agency?**
A: LocaliQ's 2026 Travel benchmark is $2.14 per click and $44.70 per lead. Monthly spend depends on how many leads you want.

**Q: What is a good conversion rate for travel ads?**
A: LocaliQ reports a 5.83% average for Travel in 2026. Treat it as a guide, not a target.

**Q: Do travel agencies need to register to advertise?**
A: In California, the Attorney General says all sellers of travel must register and show the number in advertising. Other states have similar laws, so check yours.

**Q: Should I use broad destination keywords?**
A: Not at first. They draw researchers, not buyers. Start with agent, planner and package terms.

A: Yes. Google supports website call conversions with forwarding numbers, counted when a call exceeds a minimum length you set.
