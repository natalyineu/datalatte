Most local businesses do not need a mobile ad network. If you have no app, run Google Search ads with call and location settings, plus Meta placements limited to your area. App-install networks such as Apple Ads and Google App campaigns only work if you have a published app. Neither Apple nor Google publishes a typical cost per install, so any figure you read is a guess.

This guide covers what each network sells, how it charges and who it fits. The previous version quoted CPI and CPM ranges and called AdMob an advertiser platform. I could not verify the ranges, and AdMob serves app publishers, so both are removed.

## Do You Need an App First?

Ask this first, because it splits the options. Apple Ads promotes an app in the App Store. Google App campaigns require the app to be published on Google Play or the Apple App Store and available for download. If you do not have an app, skip both.

Most single-location cafes, salons and studios are better served by a booking link and a fast site; see [mobile optimization for local business websites](/blog/mobile-optimization-local-business). The broader plan is in our [mobile marketing guide](/blog/mobile-marketing-for-local-business).

## What Does Apple Ads Sell?

Apple lists four placements: the Today tab, the Search tab, search results and product pages. Pricing is cost per tap: you pay when someone taps your ad and set the maximum per tap, which you can change any time. The page says eligible developers get a 100 USD credit, subject to terms.

The page I opened does not state a minimum spend, a bid floor or typical tap costs, and it does not explain differences between Basic and Advanced. It does not say what an install costs, so I give no CPI. Apple Ads suits a business whose app is the product, such as a booking or loyalty app.

## What Are Google App Campaigns?

Google says App campaigns can run across Google Search and search partners, Google Play, YouTube, the Google Display Network (Gmail, other apps and mobile sites) and Discover. You provide text, a starting bid and budget, languages and locations, plus images, videos or HTML5 assets. Google can also use your app store listing.

Campaign types are installs, engagement and pre-registration. Google optimizes toward actions such as installs or in-app purchases. No CPI is stated on the page.

## What About AdMob and Display?

AdMob is for app publishers who earn money by showing ads inside their apps, based on how Google's own help page describes banner placement and ad serving. It is not where a local business buys ads. To reach people inside other apps and on mobile sites, use Google display or YouTube through your Google Ads account, with location targeting.

Google Ads radius targeting needs a radius of at least 1 km, per Google Ads Help. If you only serve people in the area, consider the "Presence" setting rather than "Presence or interest."

## Can Meta Ads Reach People in Other Apps?

Yes, through Audience Network. Meta's help center, as summarized by search, says advertisers extend campaigns to websites and apps beyond Facebook, with native, banner, interstitial and rewarded video formats. In Ads Manager you select an eligible objective and use Edit Placements at the ad set level.

Caveat: the Meta help pages returned no readable text to my fetch, so I relied on search snippets. Check the placement list in your own Ads Manager and review placement reports to see where your money went.

## Which Network Fits Which Business?

| Situation | Better fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No app, customers within a few miles | Google Search with call asset and radius | Intent and location control |
| No app, want visuals and reach | Meta ads with chosen placements | Local audience, image or video |
| App is the product | Apple Ads, Google App campaigns | Both promote apps |
| Streaming TV reach | See the [CTV platforms comparison](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026) | Different channel |

## Worked Example: Splitting $400 a Month

This is an **assumption**, not a result, for a salon with no app.

| Item | Monthly | Purpose |

| Google Search, radius around the salon, call asset | $250 | Bookings and calls |

No mobile ad network gets budget here, because the salon has no app. Compare channels on cost per booking you can count. More on splitting spend is in our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).

## FAQ

### What is the best mobile ad network for a local business?
For a business without an app, Google Search ads. Mobile networks that sell installs only help if you have an app.

### How much does an app install cost?
Neither Apple nor Google publishes a typical cost per install on the pages I opened, so I will not give a number.

### Is AdMob for advertisers?
No. It monetizes apps for publishers. Advertisers buy through Google Ads.

### Is Apple Ads the same as Apple Search Ads?
Apple's page uses the name Apple Ads and describes the same App Store placements and cost-per-tap pricing.
