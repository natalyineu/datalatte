## How Much Should a Fitness Studio in Albuquerque Spend on Google Ads?

The general benchmark is $1,000–$2,000 a month for a well-optimized fitness campaign. The size-based guidance is more specific. A small independent studio with under 200 members is pointed to $600–$1,200 a month, and a mid-size gym with 200–600 members to $1,500–$2,500 ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026)).

These are starting ranges, not rules. A studio with full classes and a waitlist may need less to fill open spots. A newer studio building its member base may need the upper end to generate enough data for the platform to learn from.

Budget also has to fit the wider marketing plan. Mariana Tek says many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). Checking your Google Ads line against that share shows whether the number is realistic for your revenue.

## Which Keywords Should an Albuquerque Studio Bid On First?

Start with exact match on your three to five highest-converting terms, such as "gym near me", "fitness studio Albuquerque", "yoga classes near me", or "personal trainer Albuquerque". Exact match gives you the most control over where the budget goes, which matters most while the campaign is new.

Phrase match works well for service plus neighborhood combinations. Add neighborhood names such as Nob Hill, Old Town, or North Valley to keyword variants and ad copy, so residents see their own area in the ad. Pull current search volume and cost estimates from Google Keyword Planner, since local keyword costs are not covered by the benchmarks in this guide.

For more keyword options, see the [best Google Ads keywords for fitness studios](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-fitness-studios-in-2026) guide. Hold off on broad match for generic terms like "gym" until the campaign has enough conversion data to judge them.

## Which Negative Keywords Stop Wasted Clicks?

Negative keywords block searches that rarely turn into members. Review the search terms report regularly and add new exclusions as they appear. Common categories to exclude:

- Job seekers: "jobs", "hiring", "career", "fitness studio salary"
- DIY and self-training: "how to", "course", "certification"
- Out-of-area searches: town names outside your service radius
- Irrelevant intent: "franchise", "wholesale", "supplier"

Radius targeting around your address also limits spend on people unlikely to travel to your studio. Set the radius based on where your current members actually live.

## Should Google Business Profile Come Before Google Ads?

Both should run in parallel, but the profile needs to be ready before ad traffic arrives. The Association of Fitness Studios describes Google Business Profile as a free tool that shows business hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews, and photos directly in search results ([Association of Fitness Studios](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google), secondary). Its Map Pack also lists local businesses for queries such as "personal training studios near me".

Reviews matter on both sides of the funnel. Mariana Tek notes that Google review volume can start influencing visibility within a few weeks, while local SEO and Google reviews show a typical payoff of 6 to 12 months ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). Ads deliver traffic fast; organic local visibility takes longer to build.

Work through the [fitness studio Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization) checklist before scaling spend.

## What Should Happen After Someone Clicks?

A paid click is wasted if the lead goes cold. Mariana Tek advises studios to follow up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence, since interest fades fast after a pop-up ends ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). The same logic applies to trial sign-ups from ads.

Automation helps keep that response time consistent, even when classes run late or staff are teaching. The [marketing automation for local fitness studios](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-fitness-studios) guide covers how to set that up, and the [email marketing for fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-fitness-studio) guide covers what to send.

Mariana Tek also describes three to five short-form videos per week as a realistic pace for most studios, which helps keep your organic presence active while ads run.

## How Do You Track Whether Ads Are Bringing Members?

Set up conversion tracking before the first click. The minimum set for a studio is:

1. Phone calls from ads, using a Google call tracking number
2. Completed trial or booking forms on your website
3. Direction requests from your Google Business Profile
4. Clicks on "book now" or contact buttons as a micro-conversion

Record cost per trial sign-up and cost per member each week from launch. That lets you compare your own numbers against the targets in the worked example, rather than relying on a benchmark alone.

<Callout type="tip">
Check cost per member weekly from the first month. If it stays above the $150–$200 benchmark ceiling after 60 days of data, adjust keywords and negatives before raising the budget.
</Callout>
