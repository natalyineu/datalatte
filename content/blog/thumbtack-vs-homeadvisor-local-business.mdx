Thumbtack and HomeAdvisor both charge you for leads, not for jobs you win. HomeAdvisor's parent company says pros pay fees for consumer matches whether or not they end up doing the work. Neither platform publishes a full price list, so the useful comparison is lead type, billing rules and your own cost per signed job.

This guide is for US home-service pros such as handymen, cleaners and landscapers. The sources are an SEC filing, plus a 2022 third-party comparison that is marked as secondary below. Check current terms with each platform before you spend.

## How Do Thumbtack and HomeAdvisor Charge You?

HomeAdvisor is owned by Angi Inc., which says it operates the brands Angi, Angie's List, HomeAdvisor and Handy. Its annual report says lead revenue comes from fees pros pay for consumer matches "regardless of whether the Pro ultimately provides the requested service" ([Angi Inc. Form 10-K, FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000011/angi-20251231.htm)).

The same filing says pros in 2025 used full-priced leads within a monthly budget, or discounted leads in a subscription package. It also says Angi Inc. sells membership subscriptions to approved pros.

A 2022 comparison by Online Visibility Pros (secondary) says Thumbtack registration is free and you pay when a customer asks for a bid. The same source says HomeAdvisor adds an annual membership fee. That was 2022, so ask both platforms what applies today.

## What Is the Difference Between Shared and Exclusive Leads?

The 2022 comparison describes two HomeAdvisor lead types. Market Match leads are shared with other providers in your area. Exact Match leads go to you alone and cost more. It also says HomeAdvisor charges the lead fee even if the customer does not pick you.

On Thumbtack, the same source describes exact match leads, which are charged automatically when a job fits your targeting, and opportunity leads. For opportunity leads you see customers requesting quotes, contact the ones you want, and pay only if the customer responds. It also describes Instant Match, which sends a bid for you.

<Callout type="warning" title="Check the terms in writing">
Lead types and fees change. Ask each sales rep which lead types you will get, what is charged when a customer does not reply, and whether credits exist for bad leads.
</Callout>

## Why Does Thumbtack Lead Price Change?

The same secondary source says the price of a Thumbtack lead depends on factors such as job size and the number of interested providers nearby. When many contractors compete for the same jobs, prices go up. HomeAdvisor is described the same way: industry, job type, project size and region all move the price.

For a single number, that means you should not trust any blog table of "average" prices, including ones that quote a range for each platform. Run a small test in your own ZIP codes and record what each lead really cost. Our [Thumbtack lead cost guide](/blog/thumbtack-leads-cost-what-you-pay-per-job-2026) shows which settings you control.

## How Do You Compare Them by Cost Per Signed Job?

Lead price alone misleads. What matters is total spend divided by jobs won. All numbers below are an **assumption** for illustration, not Thumbtack or HomeAdvisor pricing.

| Assumption | Shared lead | Exclusive lead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Price per lead | $50 | $90 |
| Leads bought | 20 | about 11 |
| Close rate | 10% | 20% |
| Jobs won | 2 | about 2.2 |
| Cost per signed job | $500 | about $450 |

In this example the lead that costs more wins, because fewer competitors means a higher close rate. If your real close rates were equal, the cheaper lead would win. Your own spreadsheet decides, not the platform's marketing.

## How Do You Run a Fair Test?

1. **Split the budget evenly** between platforms for at least 30 days.
2. **Reply with the same script** and speed on both, so the platform is the only variable.
3. **Log every lead:** date, source, price, time to reply, outcome and job value.
4. **Calculate cost per signed job** at the end, not cost per lead.
5. **Ask for credits** on duplicate or invalid leads, and note the answer.

Speed matters on any shared lead. If you cannot reply within minutes, a pay-per-lead platform will cost more per job than the sticker price suggests. For a wider lead mix, see our [lead generation guide for local business](/blog/lead-generation-for-local-business).

## Which Should You Choose?

- **Larger jobs, quick replies:** an exclusive lead can justify a higher fee if it lifts your close rate.
- **Small jobs, tight margin:** a lead fee can exceed the profit on a $400 service call, so use a cap or skip the platform.
- **New to paid leads:** start with the platform that lets you set a spending cap and pause leads.

## FAQ

### Is Thumbtack or HomeAdvisor cheaper?

No source gives a reliable price for both. Prices vary by job, size, region and competition. Compare cost per signed job from a 30-day test.

### Do you pay if the customer does not hire you?

Yes on both, in general. Angi Inc.'s filing says HomeAdvisor-type fees apply regardless of whether you do the work. A secondary 2022 source says Thumbtack opportunity leads are charged only if the customer responds.

### Can you use both at once?

Yes. Many pros split a small budget to compare. Keep the reply script and tracking identical.

### How long should a test last?

Run at least 30 days, longer if your trade is seasonal, and judge on signed jobs, not lead counts.
