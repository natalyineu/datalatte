Text reminders do cut missed appointments, but not by 40%. A 2016 BMJ Open meta-analysis of 21 studies found that patients who got electronic text notifications missed 15% of appointments versus 21% without them, a risk ratio of 0.75. A medical practice should send one or two short texts, keep health details out of them, and get a patient's consent and opt-out handling right before it automates anything.

This guide is for small US medical, dental and allied-health practices. Rules differ by country, so the legal parts cover the US only. It is not legal advice. Every figure comes from a source at the end, and the worked example is labelled as an assumption.

## Do Text Reminders Actually Reduce Medical No-Shows?

Yes, on average, and the size depends on your starting point. The BMJ Open review pooled 21 studies with 8,345 patients who received text notifications and 7,731 who did not. Non-attendance fell from 21% to 15%. The abstract also says voice notifications appeared more effective than text, so a phone-call reminder for patients who do not read texts is worth testing.

Timing and count matter too. At Kaiser Permanente Washington, high-risk patients were randomly given one text reminder two business days ahead or two reminders, two and three days ahead. The second text cut no-shows by about 7% in primary care (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.89 to 0.96) and about 11% in mental health (RR 0.89, 95% CI 0.86 to 0.93). Those are relative reductions, not percentage points.

## What Should the Message Say?

Short and plain: practice name, date, time, and a way to confirm, cancel or call. A large Israeli randomized study (Clalit Health Services, 161,587 appointments analysed) tested nine wordings of the same reminder. The control no-show rate was 21.1%. The best wording, an emotional-guilt message, brought it to 14.2%, and the appointment-cost message to 15.3%. Standard, personal-request and professional-figure wordings did not differ significantly from control.

Two cautions. The authors note the design cannot prove which psychological effect caused the difference. And the guilt and cost messages also raised advance cancellations (26.3% and 27.4% versus 17.2%), which is useful only if you can refill the freed slot. Test your own wording instead of copying theirs.

| Element | Include | Leave out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Practice name | Diagnosis, test names, medication |
| When and where | Date, time, location | Reason for visit |
| Action | Reply C to confirm, call number to change | Links to promotions or offers |
| Opt-out | "Reply STOP to stop texts" | Anything that needs a login to read |

## What Do HIPAA and the TCPA Require?

This is the US picture as written in the federal rules. Under the HIPAA privacy rule, communications for a patient's treatment are excluded from the definition of marketing, unless the provider is paid by a third party to send them. Providers must also honour reasonable requests to be contacted by alternative means or locations (45 CFR 164.522). Plain SMS is not encrypted, so keep the message minimal and record how each patient asked to be contacted.

The TCPA adds a second layer. Under 47 CFR 64.1200, a healthcare-provider exemption for texts to a number the patient provided carries conditions: the text must not be charged to the patient or counted against plan limits, must stick to listed purposes such as appointment reminders, must contain no marketing, must be 160 characters or fewer, and is limited to one message a day and three a week per patient. Each message needs an easy opt-out, honoured immediately. Many practices do not meet the free-to-recipient condition, so they rely on written consent instead. Ask your compliance adviser which route applies to you.

## How Much Do SMS Reminders Cost?

Sending is cheap; software and registration are the bigger line. Twilio's US page, current as of October 2026, lists $0.0083 per 160-character segment plus a carrier fee of roughly $0.0035 to $0.005 depending on the carrier. Registration for business texting (10DLC) carries separate onboarding fees that the page does not itemise. Scheduling or practice-management software that bundles reminders will price differently, and many do not publish rates.

No published source gives an average cost of a no-show that fits every specialty, so use your own: average visit revenue times the share of slots that stay empty. If your practice management system tracks it, pull last quarter's missed-visit count.

## Worked Example: What Could Reminders Save?

| Messages per appointment | 2 |
| Cost per message, with carrier fee | $0.015 |

At a 25% reduction, 30 fewer visits are missed, which is about $4,200 in recovered revenue if each freed slot is filled. Messaging costs 800 x 2 x $0.015 = $24. Software, registration and staff time are extra and not included. Not every missed slot refills, so treat $4,200 as an upper bound and measure your own before-and-after rate for 8 to 12 weeks.

## Which Setup Steps Come First?

1. Add a consent and preferred-contact field to intake and record it.
2. Confirm the mobile number belongs to the patient before the first text.
3. Choose one or two send times, for example 3 days and 1 day before.
4. Add reply handling: confirm, cancel and STOP.
5. Offer a call or voice reminder for patients who do not reply.
6. Compare no-show rate before and after, by visit type.

## FAQ

### Do SMS reminders reduce no-shows by 40%?

Not according to the evidence cited here. The 21-study BMJ Open meta-analysis found 21% down to 15%, a risk ratio of 0.75, about a 25% relative reduction. Results vary by clinic and baseline.

### Are text appointment reminders allowed under HIPAA?

Treatment communications fall outside HIPAA's definition of marketing, so reminders are generally permitted. Keep content minimal, since SMS is not encrypted, and honour patients' requests for other contact methods. Check your policy with a compliance adviser.

### How many reminders should I send?

The Kaiser Permanente Washington trial found two reminders beat one for high-risk patients. More than that is untested here, and the TCPA exemption caps messages at one per day and three per week.

### Is a phone call better than a text?

The BMJ Open abstract says voice notifications appeared more effective than text. A call costs more staff time, so try it on patients who do not respond to texts.

### Can I add a promotion to a reminder?

Not if you rely on the TCPA healthcare exemption, which bars marketing content in those messages. Send promotions separately, with proper consent.
