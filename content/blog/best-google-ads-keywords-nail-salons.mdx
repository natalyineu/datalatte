## The Keyword Categories That Drive Nail Salon Bookings

Not all nail salon keywords are equal. Here's how to think about them:

**Tier 1 — High intent, ready to book now:**
These people have their card out. Target these first.

People who know what they want.

| Keyword | Avg CPC | Use Case |
|---|---|---|

**Tier 3 — Research phase, lower intent:**
Can work if your landing page converts well, but expect lower conversion rates.

- "how much do acrylic nails cost"

<Callout type="tip">
Start with Tier 1 only. Once you're profitable and understand your conversion rate, layer in Tier 2. Tier 3 keywords need a dedicated landing page to convert well — don't send them to your homepage.
</Callout>

## Location-Based Keywords: The High-Value Setup

The most profitable nail salon keyword structure combines service + location:

```
[service] + [city]
[service] + [neighbourhood]
[service] + "near me"
```

Examples for a salon in Austin:
- "nail salon austin"
- "nail salon south congress austin"
- "nail salon near me" (Austin targeting set in campaign settings)
- "gel nails austin tx"
- "acrylic nails east austin"

Neighbourhood keywords are often cheaper than city-wide keywords and convert better because the searcher is closer to you.

## Match Types: Don't Make the Most Common Mistake

Most nail salons running their own Google Ads make one of two mistakes:
1. **Broad match everything** — you spend budget on irrelevant searches like "nail gun" or "nail care products"
2. **Exact match only** — you miss valuable variations and keep campaigns too small to get data

The right starting structure:

**Use Phrase Match for:**
- "nail salon [city]" variants
- Service keywords with location modifiers

**Use Exact Match for:**
- Your highest-converting keywords once you know what they are
- Branded keywords (your salon name)

**Avoid Broad Match until:**
- You have 90+ days of conversion data
- You've built a solid negative keyword list
- You have $50+/day budget to absorb the variability

## Negative Keywords: Add These From Day One

This is where most nail salon ad campaigns lose money. Add these negatives before your first ad runs:

**Products to exclude:**
- nail polish
- nail tools
- nail supplies
- nail press on
- fake nails (the stick-on kind)
- nail care products
- nail drill

**DIY searches:**
- how to do
- DIY
- at home
- tutorial
- YouTube

**Jobs:**
- nail technician jobs
- nail salon hiring
- nail tech career
- nail training

**Price comparison (low intent):**
- cheap nails
- free nails
- nail salon prices (unless you're running a promotions campaign)

## Campaign Structure That Works

For a single-location nail salon with a $30–60/day budget:

**Campaign 1: Location + Service (Priority)**
- Ad Groups: Near Me | City Name | Neighbourhood
- Keywords: Tier 1 list above
- Budget: 60% of total

**Campaign 2: Service-Specific**
- Ad Groups: Gel | Acrylic | Pedicure | Nail Art
- Keywords: Tier 2 list
- Budget: 30% of total

**Campaign 3: Branded**
- Keywords: Your salon name + variations
- Budget: 10% of total (low CPC, protects your name from competitors)

## What to Put on Your Landing Page

The ad is only half the equation. A great nail salon landing page includes:
- Your salon name, address, and phone number above the fold
- A clear "Book Now" button that goes directly to your booking system
- Photos of actual work done at your salon (not stock photos)
- Your top 3–5 Google reviews
- A short list of services with prices
- Your hours, especially weekend availability

If your landing page is just your homepage, conversion rates drop by 40–60%. Build a dedicated page for your Google Ads traffic.

## Realistic ROI Expectations

A well-run nail salon Google Ads campaign with:
- $40/day budget
- Good keyword targeting
- Optimized landing page

Should generate:
- 15–30 clicks/day at $1.50–3 avg CPC
- 2–4 booking form submissions/day
- 1–2 confirmed bookings/day

At $60 average booking value, that's $60–120 daily revenue from $40 spend. A 1.5–3x ROAS is typical for nail salons in competitive markets.
