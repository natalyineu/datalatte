A Nextdoor Business Page is free. To claim it, go to business.nextdoor.com/local, log in, search for your business by name and location, and claim the listing. If it does not exist, create it. You then verify by phone or by uploading business documents. Until you are verified, you cannot post to the neighborhood newsfeed.

This guide covers claiming, verification, privacy settings, what to put on the page and how to collect recommendations. Every claim comes from a Nextdoor page listed in Sources. Where Nextdoor's own pages disagree, I say so.

## How Do You Claim a Nextdoor Business Page?

Nextdoor describes the steps like this. Go to business.nextdoor.com/local and choose the free sign-up or claim option. Create an account or log in; use the same email and password to manage the page. Search for your business by name and location. If it does not appear, use the link at the bottom of the results to create a new Business Page.

A listing may already exist. Nextdoor says business information can come from third-party public data or from members who wrote recommendations or comments. You must be an owner, employee or other authorized representative to claim a page.

## How Do You Verify Your Business?

Nextdoor's page-management FAQ gives two routes. If you claimed an existing page that has a phone number on file, you can verify by phone call or text message, and you can do it later in account settings. If you created a new page or no phone option appeared, you submit documents.

Accepted documents include a government-issued business license or permit, a business tax or VAT registration certificate, a business bank statement with the account number redacted, and a business credit report. Some items are US only, such as an IRS EIN letter. Switch to your business profile, open Settings and choose "Submit verification materials."

Verification matters. Nextdoor says unverified businesses cannot post into the newsfeed.

## What Can a Free Page Do?

Per Nextdoor's Business Page description, you can add contact details, photos of your team and work, and a business story. You can publish posts with updates, advice, offers and announcements, collect recommendations and "Faves," and answer customer messages. The same page mentions selling gift cards and sharing take-out links.

Casual services such as dog walking or babysitting are treated differently. Nextdoor says these need official business documents before posting to the newsfeed. Otherwise members can use the "Neighbor Made" or "Neighbor Services" options from a personal account.

Two free-post figures exist. One Nextdoor page says "unlimited free posts"; the page-management FAQ says verified businesses get two free monthly Business Posts. Check your own dashboard before planning a posting schedule. The pages do not publish hours, a services list or analytics features, so do not count on those.

## Who Can See Your Page?

Under Settings and Privacy you choose one of three levels. "Nearby Neighbors" shows the page only to neighbors who live near your business. "Nextdoor Members" shows it to anyone on Nextdoor. "Public" also shows it to people who find it through web search, including people without a Nextdoor profile.

A shop that wants search traffic should pick Public. A business that only wants its immediate streets, such as a home service, may prefer Nearby Neighbors.

## How Do You Get Recommendations?

Recommendations come from address-verified neighbors, and Nextdoor's guide says they appear on your page and in the main newsfeed. Written recommendations do not raise your Fave count; Faves are separate. Neighbors can use your unique link, found under the Page tab, but must sign in first.

Nextdoor's guide suggests a post with your link, replying to every recommendation from the Page tab, a storefront poster, a website banner, and an email request 7 to 30 days after delivery. It cites that businesses with one recommendation see 3x more page views and 4x more messages. That is Nextdoor's own claim. ## Worked Example: A First-Week Plan

These are **assumptions**, not results. A bakery with a Google profile already live wants to open a Nextdoor page.

| Day | Task | Assumed time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Claim the page, start phone verification | 20 min |
| 2 | Upload 5 photos, add contact details and a short story | 30 min |
| 3 | Set privacy to Public | 5 min |
| 4 | Post a neighborhood hello with your recommendation link | 15 min |
| 5 | Add the link to your email footer and put a poster at the till | 20 min |

That is about 90 minutes and no ad spend. Copy the business name, address and phone from your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) so the details match.

## FAQ

### Is a Nextdoor Business Page really free?
Yes. Nextdoor describes the Business Page as free. Paid ads are a separate product.

### Can I just use my personal account?
Personal accounts do not have Business Page features. You can link a personal and a business account to switch between them, but the email must match. Do not link a personal account if employees will share the business login.

### Why can't I post to the newsfeed?
Nextdoor says unverified businesses cannot post to the newsfeed. Finish phone or document verification.

### Can I delete a Business Page?
The pages I opened do not explain removal. Nextdoor points to its Help Center for that, so I cannot give steps.

### How many users does Nextdoor have?
The Business Page does not publish a user count, so this guide gives none.
