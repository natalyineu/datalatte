A laser tag arena should run a small local Search campaign aimed at group and party searches, count calls and booking requests as conversions, and show ads mainly when staff can answer. No laser-tag-specific click price is published. The closest public benchmark is Arts & Entertainment in WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 data: $1.63 per click and $26.84 per lead, both well under the all-industry average.

This guide is for single-site arenas and indoor entertainment venues in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Benchmarks are US figures. Examples marked as assumptions are illustrations, not results.

## What Does Google Ads Cost for a Laser Tag Arena?

LocaliQ's [2026 search advertising benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/) (page last updated June 1, 2026) list Arts & Entertainment at $1.63 per click, a 12.75% click-through rate, a 5.91% conversion rate and $26.84 per lead. The all-industry averages on the same page are $5.42, 6.64%, 8.18% and $66.69.

Treat these as a planning range. LocaliQ sells ad management, the page gives no sample size, and the category covers theatres, museums and attractions as well as arcades. A "lead" can be a call, a form or a booking request. Replace the benchmark with your own numbers after the first month.

## Who Is the Customer: Parties, Teams or Walk-Ins?

Each audience searches differently, so give each its own ad group and landing page. Parents search for birthday party venues weeks ahead. Office managers look for team-building outings. Friends and families search "laser tag near me" on the day.

Party and group bookings are worth more per enquiry than a single session, so start there. A landing page should show group sizes, prices or a clear "from" price, an age guide, and one booking button or phone number. ## Which Keywords and Negatives Should You Use?

Start with phrase and exact match around the way groups search:

| Ad group | Example keywords |
| --- | --- |
| Birthday parties | "laser tag birthday party", "kids party venue [town]" |
| Corporate | "team building [town]", "corporate event venue [town]" |
| Walk-in | "laser tag near me", "things to do [town] indoors" |
| Brand | your venue name (exact) |

Add negatives from day one, such as `jobs`, `hiring`, `gun`, `airsoft`, `paintball`, `game download`, `vest` and `equipment`. Google's help says negative keywords do not match close variants, so add plurals separately. Our [negative keyword guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) shows how to read the search terms report.

## When Should the Ads Run?

Ad scheduling is the cheapest control you have. Google's [ad scheduling help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404244) says you can limit ads to certain days or hours, for example when you can respond to enquiries, and raise or lower bids for particular times. Campaigns run all day by default.

For an arena, test showing ads on the days when booking enquiries are answered quickly, and lower bids when the desk is closed. Do not assume which days convert best. Check the schedule report after a few weeks and adjust from your own data.

## How Should You Set Up Location and Tracking?

Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722043) says radius targeting must be at least 1 km and that very small radii may show ads only intermittently. It also recommends "Presence or interest" for Search and says "Presence" fits when you only want people physically in the chosen locations.

Parents plan parties from home, so test both settings. For tracking, Google's [call conversion help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6100664) says you set a minimum call length for calls from ads and calls to your website number. Choose a length that matches a real booking enquiry. Also count booking-form submissions and direction requests, and make sure the [Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) shows correct hours.

## The Math: What Does $500 a Month Buy?

All inputs are **assumptions** built on the Arts & Entertainment benchmark, and a "conversion" is an enquiry, not a booking.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $500 |
| CPC (benchmark) | $1.63 |
| Clicks | about 307 |
| Conversion rate (benchmark) | 5.91% |
| Enquiries | about 18 |
| Cost per enquiry | about $27.50 |
| Share that book (assumption) | 25% |
| Bookings | about 4.5 |
| Cost per booking | about $110 |

If a party booking is worth $300 in revenue (assumption), four to five bookings return roughly $1,350 against $500 of ads, before staffing and costs. If the booking rate is 10%, you get about two bookings and the test is roughly break-even on revenue. Fast replies to enquiries move the booking rate more than any bid change.

## FAQ

### How much should a laser tag arena spend on Google Ads?

No standard figure exists. At the benchmark $1.63 per click, $500 a month buys roughly 300 clicks. Start with that, track enquiries, and scale only once you know your cost per booking. The [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) helps size the total.

### What conversion rate should I expect?

LocaliQ lists 5.91% for Arts & Entertainment in 2026. Your rate depends on the landing page, price clarity and how fast you answer.

### Should I target parents or companies?

Run both as separate ad groups. Parties are frequent and seasonal, while corporate bookings are rarer but larger. Check your own enquiry data before shifting budget.

### Do call ads work for a venue?

They can. Google lets you set the minimum call length that counts, so pick one that reflects a real enquiry and not a wrong number.
