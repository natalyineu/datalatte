If your average groom is $75, a $20 a month email plan pays for itself with 1 extra booking a month, and even a $100 plan needs only 2. That is the whole case for email in a grooming business: your clients already know you, their dog needs you again every few weeks, and a short message at the right moment fills a slot. The hard part is not the software. It is knowing which Mailchimp plan you actually need, which three emails to build first, and what you are allowed to send.

This guide is for solo groomers and small salons in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It does not repeat the template swipe files in our pet groomer email templates article or the general approach in email marketing for pet groomers. Instead it covers Mailchimp specifically: the plan limits that decide what you can automate, a three-email setup, the legal basics, and a break-even table. Dollar figures are US dollars and Mailchimp changes pricing often, so confirm your own tier on its pricing page.

## What Mailchimp's Plans Actually Allow

According to [Mailchimp's pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/), the differences that matter to a groomer are these:

  values="250|500|4|200"

  subs="250 contacts, 1 audience, 1 seat|500 a month or 250 a day|Per flow, scheduling included|Up to 200 flows, 5 audiences"

In plain terms:

- **Free:** up to 250 contacts, 500 sends a month or 250 a day. The page lists automations and scheduling as not included, and says sending pauses if you exceed the contact or send limit. Good for learning the tool and sending a one-off newsletter.
- **Essentials:** scheduling, plus flows limited to four steps. The page showed it from $13 a month as a first-12-month rate. Enough for one short rebooking flow.
- **Standard:** up to 200 flows and custom templates you can send. The page showed it from $20 a month as a first-12-month rate.
- **Premium:** listed from $350 a month. Not for a grooming business.

Two caveats. The prices shown are promotional first-year rates and the page does not give exact prices for every contact tier, so use Mailchimp's calculator at your list size. And the page's description of send limits does not fully match its own feature table (for example, Standard's "up to 6,000 emails per month" versus a multiple of your contact count), so plan around the lower figure until you see your own account.

Mailchimp's [flows help page](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-customer-journeys/) adds that Essentials plans allow up to four steps per flow, Standard and higher allow up to 200, and example triggers include a contact signing up or being added to a group. Steps include send email, time delay, tag and untag, and update contact. That is enough to build everything below.

### One billing trap

Contacts you no longer email can still cost you. Mailchimp's [archive help page](https://mailchimp.com/help/archive-unarchive-contacts/) says archived contacts do not count toward your billing tier. If you have a few hundred old addresses from clients who moved away, archive them before you choose a plan.

## The Three Emails Worth Building

Skip the monthly newsletter at first. For a groomer, three messages do most of the work.

### 1. The "due for a groom" nudge

This is your highest-value email. The goal is a booking before the coat gets matted. Send it a set number of weeks after the last visit, where the number is your own typical gap for that client (for example 5 weeks for a client who normally books every 6). Grooming frequency depends on breed and coat, so use your booking history, not a generic figure.

- **Subject:** "Biscuit is due, here are Saturday's open slots"
- **Body:** two sentences, the dog's name, three actual open times, one booking button.
- **Not in the body:** your life story, a link to every service, a discount.

How you trigger it depends on your booking system. Mailchimp's flow page does not list a "weeks since last visit" trigger. Practical options (my suggestions, not Mailchimp features): tag clients with a "groomed this month" tag from your booking tool, or export last-visit dates monthly and send the nudge to clients whose last visit was 5 or more weeks ago. Check what your booking app syncs to Mailchimp before you pay for a plan. Mailchimp's [integrations page](https://mailchimp.com/integrations/) has a Booking and Scheduling category; whether your specific app is listed there is something to check in the live directory.

### 2. The new-client welcome

A new client is the most attentive they will ever be. Trigger on sign-up and send one email with what to expect: drop-off and pick-up times, vaccination requirements if you have them, what to bring, and your cancellation policy in one sentence. This also cuts no-shows, because the rules arrive in writing. Our [Google Business Profile guide for groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers) covers the other half of that first impression.

### 3. The quiet-client win-back

For anyone who has not booked in roughly twice their normal gap, send one honest message: "We have not seen Biscuit since March. Want us to hold a spot?" Send it once, then stop. The permission rules in our [customer reactivation guide](/blog/customer-reactivation-campaigns) matter most here, because these are the clients whose consent may have lapsed.

Which plan covers this? One welcome flow and one nudge flow fits within Essentials' four-step limit (trigger, delay, send, tag). If you want a branch, such as skipping the nudge if the client already rebooked, you need Standard's conditional splits.

## What the Benchmarks Say, and Why to Ignore Open Rates

Mailchimp's [benchmark page](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-marketing-benchmarks/) lists averages by industry. It has no pet or grooming category, and its data was last updated in December 2023. For all users it shows an average open rate of 35.63% and click rate of 2.62%, and for ecommerce 29.81% and 1.74%. Mailchimp itself notes open-rate accuracy may be affected by Apple's Mail Privacy Protection.

So do not chase an open rate. Count what only you can: replies, bookings that mention the email, and clicks on the booking button. A list of 200 clients where 5 book from one nudge is a better result than any benchmark.

## The Rules Before You Hit Send

This is general information, not legal advice.

- **US:** the FTC's [CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) requires accurate header and subject lines, a clear ad disclosure, your valid postal address, a clear way to opt out, and that you honor opt-outs within 10 business days. It says each violating email can be penalized up to $53,088. Mailchimp adds your address and an unsubscribe link in its footer, but you are still responsible for what you send.
- **UK:** the ICO's [email marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/) says you generally need consent to market to individuals. The main exception, the soft opt-in, applies to existing customers who bought something similar from you, provided you offered a simple opt-out when you collected their details and in every message. It does not cover prospects or bought lists. Sole traders are treated as individuals.
- **Australia and Canada:** both have consent-based laws that are stricter than the US rules. Our [reactivation guide](/blog/customer-reactivation-campaigns) walks through the permission buckets, including Canada's two-year window for existing relationships.

The safe habit everywhere: collect the email with a clear tick box at booking, send only grooming-related messages, and never import a list you bought.

## The Math

Example only. Every number below is an **assumption** except Mailchimp's plan limits and the first-year prices quoted above. Replace them with your own.

Assumptions: 250 contacts, average groom $75, gross margin after supplies and time of 60% ($45 per booking), 60 clients overdue at any time.

| Scenario | Plan cost per month | Overdue clients emailed | Booking rate (assumed) | Bookings | Extra gross profit | Net after plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cautious | $20 | 60 | 4% | 2.4 | $108 | $88 |
| Base | $20 | 60 | 8% | 4.8 | $216 | $196 |
| Strong | $20 | 60 | 12% | 7.2 | $324 | $304 |
| Free plan, manual send | $0 | 60 | 4% | 2.4 | $108 | $108 |

How to read it: at $45 profit per booking, a $20 plan breaks even at about 0.4 extra bookings a month. The real cost is your time, around an hour a month at these volumes. The Free row shows why you can start manually before paying: you lose scheduling and automations, not the ability to send.

Also work out your own break-even: plan cost divided by profit per booking. If a client stays for 12 more months at one groom every 6 weeks, a single reactivated client is worth roughly 8 grooms, or about $600 in revenue at $75. That is an assumption-based illustration, not a promise.

## Do This Today

1. Count your contacts, archive anyone who has left the area or bounced, and confirm every address came from a client who gave it to you.
2. Open [Mailchimp's pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/) and price your list size on Free, Essentials and Standard. Pick Free if you only send manually.
3. Add a sign-up tick box to your booking form or front desk iPad, with one line saying what you will send.
4. Write the "due for a groom" email: dog's name, three open slots, one button. Send it to 10 overdue clients this week.
5. Log every booking that comes from it for 30 days, then fill in the table above with your real rate.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Mailchimp's free plan enough for a pet groomer?**
A: For up to 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, yes for manual newsletters. Mailchimp's pricing page lists automations and scheduling as not included on Free, so a hands-off rebooking flow needs Essentials or higher.

**Q: Which Mailchimp plan do I need for a rebooking automation?**
A: Essentials allows flows of up to four steps, which fits a simple trigger, delay and send. Standard adds conditional splits and up to 200 flows if you want branches.

**Q: How often should I email clients?**
A: There is no verified rule. Tie it to the dog's grooming cycle: one message when they are due, one when they are overdue, then stop. Fewer, better-timed emails beat a fixed weekly schedule.

**Q: Do I need a lawyer to send marketing emails?**
A: Not for routine messages to clients who gave you their address, but each country has rules on consent and opt-out (CAN-SPAM in the US, PECR in the UK, and consent laws in Australia and Canada). Check with an adviser before a large or unusual send.

**Q: Should I use email or text to remind clients?**
A: Use your booking system for appointment reminders, and email for rebooking nudges and win-backs. Mailchimp lists SMS as an add-on for Standard and higher, so texting inside Mailchimp costs extra.
