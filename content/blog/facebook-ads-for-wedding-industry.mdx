Facebook and Instagram ads work well for wedding businesses when you target engaged couples in your service area with beautiful, specific creative and send them to a simple enquiry form, then follow up quickly. The audience is easy to describe, the purchase is high value and visual platforms suit weddings, but the sales cycle is long, so patience and follow-up decide results.

This guide covers who it suits, how to set up campaigns in Meta Ads Manager, what to show couples and how to measure success without relying on invented benchmarks.

## Why Meta suits wedding businesses

- **Visual product.** Venues, flowers, cakes, photography and styling all sell through images and video.
- **Planning happens on mobile.** Couples browse Instagram and Facebook for ideas long before they contact a vendor.
- **High order value.** Even one extra booking can justify a modest ad budget, which makes careful tracking worthwhile.
- **Local intent.** Most couples choose vendors within travelling distance of the venue or home town, so geographic targeting is effective.

The caveat is timing. Many couples enquire months or even more than a year before the wedding, and most compare several vendors. Your ads need to build familiarity, not only chase immediate enquiries.

## Step 1: Set up the basics

Before spending anything:

1. **Business Manager and Page.** Create or verify a Facebook Page and Meta Business account, and connect your Instagram profile.
2. **Meta Pixel and Conversions API.** Install the pixel on your site, ideally with Conversions API, and set up a conversion event such as "Lead" for enquiry submissions. Your website platform or a tag manager usually offers integration.
3. **A dedicated landing page.** A page just for the offer, with strong photos, a short form, pricing guidance and clear contact details. Avoid sending people to a busy homepage.
4. **Response process.** Decide who responds to enquiries and how fast. Quick replies matter, since couples often message several vendors at once.

## Step 2: Choose a campaign objective

For most wedding vendors:

- **Leads** (instant forms or website forms) if you want enquiries.
- **Sales or Conversions** optimised for a Lead event if you have a pixel and a landing page.
- **Awareness or Engagement** only if you have a clear plan for retargeting later.

Instant forms are quick for couples on mobile, but they can generate lower-quality leads because they are so easy to submit. Add a qualifying question, such as wedding date or approximate guest count, to filter out casual enquiries.

## Step 3: Target engaged couples

Meta regularly changes its targeting options, so check what Ads Manager offers today. Approaches that typically work:

- **Location.** Use a radius around your business or the specific towns you serve. Weddings often involve travelling couples, so consider a wider area for venues.
- **Age range.** Start broad, such as late twenties to mid-thirties, then adjust based on your actual customers. Do not exclude people on assumptions.
- **Interests and behaviours.** Options related to weddings, engagement and bridal topics may be available, for example wedding planning interests. Some life-event targeting options, such as recently engaged, have been available in the past; availability varies by region and over time.
- **Lookalike audiences.** Upload your past clients' emails (with proper consent and following Meta's rules) and let Meta find similar people.
- **Retargeting.** Show ads to people who visited your site, watched your videos or engaged with your Instagram, which is often the most efficient group.
- **Broad targeting with strong creative.** Meta's delivery system has become better at finding relevant people, so some advertisers use fewer restrictions and let the creative do the filtering.

Avoid anything that implies you know sensitive facts about a person. Write ads that speak to a situation, such as "Planning a spring wedding near [town]?", rather than "You're engaged!"

## Step 4: Create ads that couples save and share

**Images and video.**

- Use real work: your venue at golden hour, actual bouquets, finished photo albums or real tastings.
- Show detail and atmosphere, not only wide shots.
- Short vertical videos (15 to 30 seconds) work well in Reels and Stories placements: a venue walkthrough, a time-lapse of setting up a table, a client reaction with permission.
- Get written permission before using clients' photos and faces.

**Copy that helps.**

- Start with the couple's concern: "Looking for a barn venue within an hour of [city]?"
- Add one or two concrete facts: capacity, location, what's included, starting prices if you are comfortable sharing them.
- End with one clear action: "Book a viewing" or "Check your date".

**Testing.** Run two to four variations of image or video and primary text. Let each gather enough results before changing anything, and change one element at a time so you learn from each test. Our [Facebook ads creative guide](/blog/facebook-ads-creative-guide) has more ideas.

## Step 5: Budget and bidding

There is no single right budget. Consider:

- **Start modestly.** A small local business might begin around $10 to $30 a day (or the local equivalent) per campaign and adjust after a few weeks of data.
- **Run long enough.** Meta needs time to learn; avoid changing budgets or targeting every day.
- **Match budget to margin.** If one booking is worth several thousand dollars, you can afford to pay more per enquiry than a coffee shop could, as long as enough enquiries turn into bookings.
- **Seasonality.** Enquiries often rise after the holidays and around engagement peaks, though patterns vary by region. Check your own booking history.

## Step 6: Follow up like it matters

Ads create enquiries. Follow-up creates bookings.

- Respond quickly, ideally the same day, with a personal message rather than a template.
- Offer a clear next step: a call, a viewing, a consultation.
- Send helpful content, such as a pricing guide, sample timeline or FAQ.
- Add leads to an email sequence and nurture them over weeks and months. Our [email and SMS marketing service](/services/email-sms) can help.
- Record how each enquiry progressed, so you know which ads produce real bookings.

## Step 7: Measure what matters

Track:

- **Cost per enquiry** (Meta's cost per lead)
- **Enquiry to consultation rate**
- **Consultation to booking rate**
- **Cost per booking** and revenue per booking

Meta's reporting shows the first metric; you will need your own CRM or spreadsheet for the others. Early in a campaign, avoid judging by clicks or likes alone. Ask each couple how they found you and compare answers with your data.

Because the sales cycle is long, review results in monthly blocks rather than daily snapshots.

## Common mistakes

- Boosting posts instead of using Ads Manager campaigns with clear objectives
- Sending traffic to a generic homepage
- Weak or slow follow-up
- Using stock images that do not represent your work
- Targeting too narrowly and starving the algorithm
- Ignoring Meta's advertising policies and special categories rules, which can limit targeting for some industries; check the latest guidelines

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a wedding business spend on Facebook ads?**
A: Many small vendors begin with roughly $300 to $900 a month and scale if booking numbers justify it. The right figure depends on your average booking value and capacity.

**Q: Can I target recently engaged people?**
A: Meta has offered options related to engagement and weddings in some regions, but availability changes. Check Ads Manager, and combine it with interest, location and retargeting audiences.

**Q: Are Instagram Reels worth it for wedding ads?**
A: Often yes, because short video shows atmosphere and personality. Test them alongside images.

**Q: How long before I see bookings?**
A: Enquiries can arrive within days, but bookings often take weeks or months because couples compare vendors. Plan for a full season.
