No source publishes a Google Ads budget for cat groomers. The workable method is to work backwards from the bookings you want. WordStream by LocaliQ's June 2026 benchmarks list Animals & Pets at $4.06 per click, 16.22% conversion rate and $31.50 per lead. At that average, 10 leads costs about $315 a month. It is an industry-wide average, not a cat-grooming figure.

Cat grooming is a small search niche in most towns, so your own account data will replace these averages quickly. Benchmarks were read from LocaliQ and Google's help on 2026-11-01. Example inputs are labelled as assumptions.

## What Do Google Ads Cost in Animals & Pets?

LocaliQ's table compares the category with all industries:

| Category | Avg. CPC | Avg. CTR | Avg. conversion rate | Avg. cost per lead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All industries | $5.42 | 6.64% | 8.18% | $66.69 |
| Animals & Pets | $4.06 | 7.49% | 16.22% | $31.50 |
| Beauty & Personal Care | $4.62 | 6.75% | 10.35% | $39.25 |

The page has no separate category for pet services or cat groomers, so Animals & Pets is the closest match. It also mixes vets, shops and breeders with groomers. A conversion on LocaliQ's page is whatever the advertiser counted, such as a call or form, not a paid appointment.

## How Do You Calculate the Budget?

Work from customers back to spend.

1. Decide how many new cat clients you want per month.
2. Estimate the share of leads that book. This is your number; track it.
3. Leads needed = clients divided by booking share.
4. Monthly budget = leads needed times cost per lead.
5. Daily budget = monthly budget divided by 30.4.

The 30.4 comes from Google: its help says to divide a monthly budget by 30.4 to get the average daily budget. You can change the average daily budget at any time and as often as you like, so you are not locked in. Google's page excerpt did not state how far a single day can exceed the average, so check your campaign's budget notes.

## A Worked Example: Six New Clients a Month

All inputs are **assumptions** except the cost per lead.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| New cat clients wanted per month | 6 |
| Share of leads that book (assumption) | 40% |
| Leads needed | 15 |
| Cost per lead (LocaliQ average) | $31.50 |
| Monthly budget | about $473 |
| Average daily budget | about $15.50 |
| Cost per new client | about $79 |

If the average click is $4.06, $473 buys about 116 clicks. At 16.22% conversion that would be about 19 leads, a little above the 15 needed, so the plan has some room. Real results can be far lower in a small market where few people search for cat groomers.

Compare $79 with what a client is worth. If a cat client rebooks four times a year at a price of your choosing, the first-year value can cover the acquisition cost. Use your own price list, not an average.

## What Should a Small Cat Groomer Start With?

A budget below the example is possible but yields thin data. At $200 a month and $4.06 per click you get about 49 clicks, and at 16.22% that is about 8 conversions on paper. That is enough to see whether searchers contact you, not enough to judge profit.

Keep the campaign local, review search terms weekly and add negatives for jobs, training courses and dog-only terms. ## FAQ

### What is the average Google Ads budget for a cat groomer?

None is published. LocaliQ gives Animals & Pets averages of $4.06 per click and $31.50 per lead, which you can multiply by the leads you want.

### Is $200 a month enough?

It can show whether leads arrive: about 49 clicks at the average CPC. It is a test, not a growth budget.

### How do I convert a monthly budget to a daily one?

Google says to divide the monthly figure by 30.4.

### Do cat clients cost more than dog clients?

No source compares them. Do not assume a difference.

### Should I run ads at all?

If your profile has few reviews or your booking page is weak, fix those first. Ads send people to what you already have.
