A real estate agent's website should work as proof and a place to check you out, not as a copy of the portals. Referrals are the main route: NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers reports that 43% of buyers found their agent through a referral and 18% used an agent they had worked with before. Those people will search your name before they call, so the site must show your reviews, recent sales, areas and a simple way to contact you.

## What do buyers and sellers do before they contact you?

The NAR summary says most home searches began online, and 88% of buyers still bought through an agent. A person given your name by a friend will look you up. BrightLocal's 2026 survey says 54% of consumers visit a business's website after reading positive reviews, so reviews and the site work together.

The NAR figures cover the whole US market and the 12 months to June 2025. The UK, Australia and Canada work differently, so check your own market's data.

## Which pages does an agent site need?

| Page | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Who you help, your area, a contact button, 2-3 reviews |
| About | Your background, licence details and brokerage, as your jurisdiction requires |
| Neighbourhoods | One page per area you serve, with real local information |
| Buyers and sellers | Your process, fees you can state, what happens first |
| Results | Recent sales you may publicly share, with permission |
| Contact | Phone, form, and your reply time |

Link your [about page](/blog/website-about-page-local-business) to your reviews. Our [guide to Facebook ads for real estate](/blog/facebook-ads-for-real-estate) shows where to send paid traffic.

## What should a lead form ask?

Ask as little as you can: name, phone or email and what they want (buy, sell, valuation). Every extra field costs responses; test it with your own data. See our [contact form guidance](/blog/website-conversion-rate-optimization). State how and when you will reply, and keep personal data handling in line with your local law.

## How do you tell Google what you are?

Google's LocalBusiness structured data guide asks for the most specific subtype possible. Schema.org defines RealEstateAgent as a LocalBusiness type, so use it with name, address, phone and hours, and test it in the Rich Results Test. Keep details identical to your Google Business Profile. See our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-for-local-business).

## Should you build your own listings search?

That depends on your MLS or listing-data rules, which differ by market and were not researched here. If you cannot display listings, link to the official search and put your effort into local content and proof. Check your broker's and MLS's display rules before publishing listing data.

## How fast should it load?

Property photos are heavy. Google rates LCP within 2.5 seconds and INP of 200 ms or less as good. Compress photos, set image dimensions, and never lazy-load the first-screen image. See our [speed guide](/blog/website-speed-optimization-small-business).

## Worked example: what would one extra lead be worth?

All inputs are **assumptions**; commission varies widely and is not sourced here.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly website visitors | 900 |
| Visitors who send a form or call | 1% |
| Leads that become clients | 10% |
| Net commission per client | $6,000 |

Leads: 900 x 1% = 9 a month. Clients: 9 x 10% = 0.9 a month. Revenue: 0.9 x $6,000 = $5,400 a month before your costs. Raising the contact rate to 1.5% gives 13.5 leads and 1.35 clients, or $8,100. The contact-rate lift is a guess; your own form and call logs will show what is real. Closing time in real estate is long, so judge over several months.

## FAQ

### Do agents still need a website?

NAR data says referrals drive agent choice, and referred clients tend to look you up first. A site gives them somewhere to verify you.

### What matters more, listings or content?

For a solo agent, proof and local knowledge matter more than a copy of the portals. Use your site to show reviews, sales you can share and neighbourhood guides.

### How many neighbourhood pages should I build?

Only the areas you serve and can write about with real information. Thin pages repeat the same text and add little.
