## What Should a Small Business Track About Competitors?

Track things that change what you do this month: new services and prices, new ads and offers, new locations, and review themes. Ignore the rest. A salon owner who learns that a rival started advertising bridal packages can respond within days. A full social media audit rarely pays off at that scale.

Pick three to five competitors you actually lose customers to. Record each one's website, Google Business Profile link and social pages in a single note. A short list is easier to keep current than a long one.

Check that each rival's directory listings are accurate and consistent, because they affect how customers find them. The [local citation building guide](/blog/local-citation-building-small-business-guide) explains how listings work.

## How Do You Set Up Free Alerts?

Google Alerts is the simplest starting point. Enter a topic, open the alert options, and adjust the region, language, sources and number of results. Create one alert per competitor name and one for your own business name. Add a competitor's owner name only if that name is already public.

Alerts only catch what Google indexes, so private posts and many social updates will not appear. Check the current frequency settings on Google's live help page, because the options change over time. Common business names return unrelated results, so add the city name to narrow the matches.

## How Do You Check Competitor Ads?

The Google Ads Transparency Center lets you search for an advertiser and review the ads it runs. Search each competitor once a month and save screenshots of new ads. Note the offer, the landing page and the wording. This shows what a rival pays to promote, which is often more useful than what it says about itself.

Confirm which formats and regions the tool covers on Google's live help page, since coverage changes. Meta's ad library works differently, so check its help pages directly. Before you compare spending, read the [local ad spend forecast for 2026](/blog/local-ad-spend-forecast-2026-small-business) to set realistic expectations for your area.

## Can AI Read Competitor Reviews for You?

Yes, as a summarizer. Copy a competitor's recent public reviews into an AI assistant and ask for the three most common praises and complaints, each with a short quote. Then read a sample yourself, because an assistant can misread sarcasm or confuse two businesses with similar names.

Collect reviews by hand or with tools that respect each platform's terms. Automated scraping can break those terms, so check them before automating anything. For a few reviews, manual copying is the safe route. Use what you learn on your own profile: reply to repeated complaints publicly and fix the underlying issue where you can.

## What Does the Routine Cost? A Worked Example

The figures in this example are **assumptions**. A pet groomer tracks four competitors, and the minutes below are planning inputs, not measurements.

| Task | Minutes per week |
| --- | --- |
| Read Google Alerts emails | 5 |
| Check Ads Transparency Center (one competitor in rotation) | 5 |
| Skim new competitor reviews | 10 |
| AI summary of the notes | 5 |
| Decide one action and write it down | 5 |
| Total | 30 |

At 30 minutes a week over four weeks, the routine takes about 120 minutes a month (assumption). The output should be one decision, such as matching a rival's new-client offer, adding a service page, or answering a repeated complaint on your profile.

## FAQ

### Is it legal to look at competitors' ads and reviews?
Reading public pages is common practice. Automated collection can break a platform's terms, so read those terms before automating anything. This is general information, not legal advice.

### Do I need paid competitive intelligence software?
No. The three free sources cover the core routine. Paid tools make more sense when you need history, alerts or summaries across many competitors.

### How often should I check?
A weekly pass for alerts and reviews and a monthly pass for ads is a reasonable start for a small local business. Adjust it to how quickly your local market changes.

### Can AI do the whole thing automatically?
It can summarize material you provide. It should not be the only reader of reviews or ads, because it can misread context or confuse businesses with similar names.
