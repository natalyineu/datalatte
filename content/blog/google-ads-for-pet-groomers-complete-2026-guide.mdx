Pet groomers can use Google Ads to get more bookings by bidding on local, high-intent searches such as "dog grooming near me," targeting a tight radius around the shop, and tracking calls and bookings rather than clicks. Plan on a testing budget you can sustain for about 90 days, because the first weeks are mostly learning what works in your area.

This guide walks through how to:
- Set up a local-first campaign structure
- Write ad copy that earns clicks from pet owners
- Choose keywords and negative keywords
- Set a realistic budget and scale it carefully
- Track the metrics that show whether ads are actually filling your calendar

## Why Google Ads Works for Pet Groomers

Pet grooming is a local, competitive service with steady demand. When someone searches "pet groomer near me," they are usually ready to book, which is exactly the moment search ads capture.

Google Ads lets you:
- Target people within a set radius of your shop
- Show ads at the times you can actually take bookings
- Compete with larger chains by focusing on specific local services they overlook

Results vary a lot by city, season and how strong your website and reviews are, so treat any benchmark you read, including the ones here, as a starting point.

## Step 1: Set Up a Local-First Campaign Structure

Start simple. Begin with a manual or conversion-focused bidding strategy and move to Smart Bidding such as Target CPA only once you have enough conversion data for Google to learn from.

Demand for grooming is seasonal in many areas. Shedding seasons, holidays and summer often bring spikes, so check Google Trends and your own booking history to plan your budget around busy periods.

| Setting | Suggested starting point |
|---------|--------------------------|
| Location | Tight radius around your shop, adjusted to how far clients really travel |
| Ad schedule | Hours when you can answer the phone and take bookings |
| Devices | Mobile-first, since many searches happen on phones |
| Language | Match the languages your clients speak, and test a second language if relevant |

## Step 2: Write Ad Copy That Converts

Pet owners are emotionally invested, so lead with trust and convenience.

**Headline ideas to test:**
- "Dog Grooming in [City] | Book Online"
- "Gentle Senior Dog Grooming | [Neighborhood]"
- "New Client Offer on Your First Groom"

**Description best practices:**
- Mention one or two specialties, such as handling curly coats or anxious pets
- Include a clear call to action and, where true, an offer or booking deadline
- Add real social proof, such as your review rating

Only claim what is true. If you mention certifications or "fear-free" practices, make sure they apply to your shop.

## Step 3: Master Local Keyword Targeting

**Primary keyword ideas:**
- "[City] pet groomer"
- "mobile pet grooming near me"
- "cat grooming [City]"

**Long-tail opportunities:**
- "hypoallergenic dog grooming [City]"
- "pet groomers that take [breed]"
- "low stress grooming for senior dogs"

For more ideas see our list of [Google Ads keywords for pet groomers](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-pet-groomers-in-2026).

**Negative keywords:** Add terms like "free," "wholesale," "jobs," "course" and "school" to avoid irrelevant clicks. Review your search terms report regularly and add new negatives.

## Step 4: Budget Wisely

There is no universal right budget, because costs per click vary by market. Check Keyword Planner in your account for current estimates. A reasonable approach:

- Pick a monthly budget you can sustain for 60 to 90 days, even if early results are slow
- Avoid very small budgets that cannot collect enough data to learn from
- Work backward from what a new client is worth to estimate what you can afford to pay per booking

**Scaling approach:**
- Weeks 1 and 2: test a few ad variations
- Weeks 3 and 4: shift more budget to the best performers
- Month 3 onward: increase spend gradually on keywords that bring booked appointments

For deeper budget guidance, read [How Much Should a Pet Groomer Spend on Google Ads?](/blog/how-much-should-a-pet-groomer-spend-on-google-ads).

## Step 5: Use Ad Extensions

Extensions (now called assets) make your ad larger and more useful:

1. **Call assets** let people phone straight from the ad
2. **Sitelinks** such as "Book Online," "Gift Cards" or "Senior Pet Care"
3. **Callouts** such as "Mobile Grooming" or "Cat Friendly"
4. **Structured snippets** listing your services
5. **Location assets** linked to your Google Business Profile

Seasonal callouts, such as winter coat care or summer de-shedding, can keep your ads fresh.

## Track the Metrics That Matter

1. **Cost per booking:** the most important number. Compare it with what a client is worth.
2. **Booked calls and form submissions:** count only conversations that become appointments, not every call.
3. **Conversion rate by device:** phones and desktops often behave differently.
4. **Search terms report:** find wasted spend and add negatives.
5. **Impression share:** shows whether budget or bids are limiting your reach.

## 2026 Trends Worth Watching

1. **Retargeting:** remind people who viewed your pricing or booking page but did not book.
2. **Video:** short grooming clips can work on YouTube and Performance Max placements.
3. **AI-assisted campaign types:** useful for some accounts, but monitor them closely and keep your negatives and conversion tracking tight.
4. **Local profile strength:** your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) reviews and photos help every ad perform better.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should I spend on Google Ads if I'm a new pet grooming business?**
A: Many groomers start with a few hundred dollars a month and a 90-day test, but the right number depends on your city's click costs. Avoid budgets so small that Google cannot gather enough data. After 90 days, judge by cost per booked appointment compared with what a client is worth.

**Q: How long until I see results from Google Ads?**
A: You may see inquiries within days if tracking, keywords and your landing page are set up well, but predictable results usually take several weeks to a couple of months. Avoid changing everything after the first two weeks.

**Q: Can I outrank PetSmart and other national chains?**
A: You do not need to outspend them. Chains often bid on broad terms, so you can compete by targeting specific, local, service-based keywords such as a breed or a specialty service, where intent is high and competition may be lower.

**Q: Should I use Google Ads or Yelp Ads for my pet grooming business?**
A: Start with Google Ads, which gives you more control over keywords, budgets and targeting. Once Google is working, you can test Yelp with a small budget if your area has active Yelp users. Results vary a lot by city, so test before committing.

**Q: My ads are running but I'm only getting a couple of clicks a day. What's wrong?**
A: Usually one of two things: the budget is too low for your market's click costs, or the keywords are too narrow to trigger often. Check impression share and the search terms report, then raise the budget or broaden to neighborhood-level and related service terms.

**Q: How do I track calls that do not book online?**
A: Use Google's call reporting or a call tracking service, and tag only calls that lead to appointments as conversions. Price shoppers and existing clients can inflate call counts if you treat every call as a win.
