A local business can start Snapchat ads at a $5 daily minimum, but Snap itself recommends at least $20 to $50 a day so a campaign can finish its first learning period. Snapchat reaches mostly younger adults: Pew's 2026 survey found 56% of US adults aged 18 to 29 use it, falling to 13% at ages 50 to 64. It suits a business whose customers are under 40, such as a salon, a café near a campus or a studio.

## Who Uses Snapchat in the US?

Pew's 2026 social media fact sheet gives usage by age. Among US adults, 56% of those aged 18 to 29 use Snapchat, 33% of those aged 30 to 49, 13% of those aged 50 to 64 and 4% of those 65 and older. Compare that with TikTok: 65%, 45%, 31% and 11% for the same age bands.

So Snapchat is narrow but deep with younger people. If your regulars are 45 and up, Facebook or Google search will reach more of them. See [Snapchat vs TikTok ads](/blog/snapchat-vs-tiktok-ads-which-is-better-local) for a side-by-side.

## What Does Snapchat Charge?

Snap's pricing page says the minimum spend per day is $5 and recommends at least $20 to $50 per day. It also describes an exploration phase right after launch that typically lasts 4 days or more, depending on objective, while the system learns who responds. The page names no CPM or CPC averages and advises trying different bid strategies with suggested bids.

That means you cannot forecast clicks from a public benchmark. Your first campaign is how you learn your own cost. Our [Snapchat ads cost guide](/blog/snapchat-ads-cost-local-business) tracks what we can verify.

## How Do You Target Locally?

Snap's targeting page lists standard targeting by location and demographics, such as age, gender and language. It also offers lookalike audiences built from your customer list or Pixel audience. Specific radius settings and location categories are not described on that page, so check them inside Ads Manager before planning a campaign around a one-mile circle.

Keep the audience wide enough to deliver. A tiny audience in a small town can stall in the learning phase.

## What Should a $200 Test Look Like?

This is an **assumption**, using Snap's published minimum and recommendation.

| | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Total test budget (assumption) | $200 |
| Daily spend (within Snap's $20-50 range) | $20 |
| Days of delivery | 10 |
| Exploration phase (Snap: 4 days or more) | about 4 days, $80 |
| Days of optimised delivery | about 6, $120 |

Judge results after day 10, not day 2, because the first four days are learning. Pick one offer, such as a student discount or a first-visit price, and one call to action. Track the offer with a code or a dedicated booking link so you can match sign-ups to the ad. Without that, you only count taps.

## What Creative Fits the App?

Snapchat is full-screen vertical video. Use footage shot on a phone: a barista pouring a drink, a trim from start to finish, a class starting. Show the place and the price in the first seconds. Skip stock footage. Repurpose clips you already post to [TikTok](/blog/tiktok-ads-for-small-business-complete-beginners-guide-2026) or Instagram.

## FAQ

### How much does a Snapchat ad cost?

Snap's minimum is $5 per day, with $20 to $50 per day recommended. Average CPM or CPC is not published on the pages checked, so run a small test to learn your own cost.

### Is Snapchat good for local businesses?

It can be if your customers are under 40. Pew found 56% of US adults 18 to 29 use it, but only 13% of those 50 to 64.

### How long before I judge a campaign?

Snap says the exploration phase typically lasts 4 days or more. Judge after at least a week of spend.

### Should I choose Snapchat or TikTok?

TikTok reaches more adults at every age band in Pew's data. Snapchat is stronger only for the youngest group. Test one first.

### Do I need the Snap Pixel?

For online bookings, yes, because it lets you measure sign-ups and build lookalike audiences. A phone-only business can use a code at checkout instead.
