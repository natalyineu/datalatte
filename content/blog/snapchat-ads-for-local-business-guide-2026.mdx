## Is Snapchat Worth Testing for a Local Business?

Snapchat's reach among all US adults is 26%, according to the [Pew Research Center social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/). That is well below YouTube, Facebook and Instagram in the same survey. The fact sheet's text available for this rewrite reports the overall figure, not a breakdown by age, so do not assume Snapchat's share among a specific age group without checking.

The practical question is whether the regulars are the people Snapchat reaches. A salon with a mostly student clientele has a different case from a plumber serving homeowners. If the customers are mainly older, a channel with broader reach, such as Facebook or Google, is usually the better first test. Pew's survey covers only US adults, so these figures do not describe Canada, the UK or Australia.

## What Does Snapchat Advertising Cost?

The Snapchat pricing page was not verified for this rewrite, so this guide does not state a minimum spend, a recommended daily budget or average CPM or CPC. Any figure read elsewhere, including older guides, should be confirmed inside the Ads Manager account before setting a budget.

The first campaign is the only reliable way to learn local cost. Set a fixed test budget, run one offer, and record cost per booking or cost per sign-up. A public average from another country or another industry will not show what a single café or studio pays in its own postcode.

## How Do You Target Locally?

Targeting controls differ by platform and change over time, and the Snapchat targeting page was not part of this rewrite's sources. Check location, age and language options directly in Ads Manager before planning a campaign around a specific radius.

Keep the audience wide enough to deliver. A very small audience in a small town can take longer to get enough impressions, which makes early results hard to read. A broader area with a clear offer is usually easier to judge than a tight radius that never fills.

Judge results after the full 10 days, not after the first two. Early days are noisy because the platform is still finding who responds. Pick one offer, such as a first-visit discount, and one call to action, such as booking a slot. A dedicated booking link or discount code ties each sign-up to the ad. Without that, only taps get counted.

## How Do You Judge the Result?

Meta's guidance on A/B testing warns against informal tests, such as switching ad sets on and off by hand, because overlapping audiences produce [unreliable results](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669). The same logic applies on Snapchat: change one variable at a time and keep the budget equal across versions. That guidance comes from Meta, not Snap, so check whether Snapchat's own tools offer a comparable test before relying on it.

A simple checklist keeps the review honest:

1. Write the hypothesis before launch, for example "a first-visit offer will get more bookings than a generic service ad."
2. Change only the offer or the creative, not both.
3. Use a booking link or code unique to the ad.
4. Review cost per booking after day 10, not after day 2.
5. Decide in advance what result would count as a pass, and what to do if it fails.

For help drafting the offer and ad copy, the [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) guide includes prompt templates that can be adapted. Keep the final wording short and specific to the location.

## What Creative Fits Snapchat?

Snapchat is a full-screen vertical video app, so creative should work at phone size with the sound on or off. Footage shot on a phone usually fits better than polished stock video: a barista pouring a drink, a class starting, a before-and-after from a service.

Show the place and the offer in the first few seconds. If the viewer has to wait to learn what is being sold, most will swipe past. Clips already posted to other short-video platforms can often be cut down and reused, which keeps production cost near zero during a test.

## How Can You Keep Track of Results?

Set up a simple tracking sheet before the campaign starts. Record the date, daily spend, total spend, number of bookings or sign-ups by code, and any walk-ins that mention the ad. Compare the cost per booking against the margin on the service. If the cost per booking is higher than what a customer is worth, the test has failed, even if the ad got many views.

Note anything that changed during the test, such as a price change or a holiday closure, so the ad does not get blamed for a shift the business caused. ## FAQ

### How much does a Snapchat ad cost?

This rewrite did not verify Snapchat's published pricing, so no minimum or average cost is stated here. Check current minimums and recommended budgets inside Ads Manager, then run a small test to learn the actual cost per booking.

### Is Snapchat good for local businesses?

It can be, if the customers are young and the offer suits short video. Pew's 2026 survey found 26% of US adults use Snapchat overall, so check whether the customers are among them before spending.

### How long should a Snapchat test run?

Judge the test after about 10 days of delivery, not after the first two. The early days are often used by the platform to learn who responds, so early numbers can mislead.

### Should Snapchat or another platform come first?

Pew's 2026 figures show YouTube, Facebook and Instagram each reach more US adults than Snapchat. Start with the platform the customers already use, and test Snapchat only if the audience is clearly younger.

### Is a tracking pixel required?

For online bookings, a pixel or a unique booking link helps match sign-ups to ads. A phone-only business can use a discount code given on the call, which works without any technical setup.
