To optimise a Google Business Profile in Europe, use your real-world business name and address, pick as few categories as possible, set accurate hours and special hours, verify the profile, and ask customers for genuine reviews. Google applies the same published guidelines across countries. Verification options depend on your business type and region, and postcard codes usually arrive within 14 days.

This guide is for small businesses in the UK and the EU. Google publishes no country-by-country ranking weights, so none are claimed here. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## Who Can Have a Business Profile?

Google says you can create a profile if your business has a physical location customers can visit, or travels to customers. The name should reflect your real-world business name, and unnecessary information in it is not permitted. The address must be your actual location, and P.O. boxes or mailboxes at remote locations are not acceptable.

Service-area businesses, such as mobile groomers or plumbers, should have one profile for the central office with a designated service area. This rule applies in every country.

## How Does Verification Work Across Countries?

Google's help says the verification options available to you depend on your business type, public information, region or business hours. Google decides the method automatically and you cannot change it. Options include phone or text, email, video recording, live video call and postcard, and none is available for every business.

Postcard codes usually arrive within 14 days and expire after 30. Requesting a new code can make the process longer, so wait before you re-request. If you want the faster route, check whether video verification is offered, which Google recommends when you are eligible.

## Which Categories and Hours Should You Set?

Google tells businesses to use as few categories as possible to describe the core business. Choose the primary category with care. Our [categories guide](/blog/google-business-profile-categories-guide) shows how to choose.

Set regular customer-facing hours, and use special hours for holidays or other temporary periods. National holidays differ across Europe, so set them per location rather than copying from another country. Photos and videos can take 24 to 48 hours to show after upload, per Google.

## What Decides Your Local Ranking?

Google names three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search, and complete, detailed information helps. Distance is how far you are from the searcher. Prominence is how well known you are, including how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have.

Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. It does not say how much weight each factor has. For ranking beyond the profile, read our [Europe Maps ranking guide](/blog/google-maps-ranking-tips-europe).

## What Do UK Review Rules Mean for You?

The UK's Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act introduced a banned practice on fake reviews, and GOV.UK states the unfair commercial practices provisions came into force on 6 April 2025. The CMA has published separate fake reviews guidance. For a small business the practical rule is simple: only ask for genuine reviews.

Google's own policy already prohibits offering incentives such as free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews. EU-level review rules are not covered here, so check your national consumer authority.

## FAQ

### Does Google Business Profile work the same across Europe?
The published guidelines are the same. Verification options depend on business type, public information, region and hours.

### How long does postcard verification take?
Most codes arrive within 14 days. They expire after 30.

### Can I choose how to verify?
No. Google decides automatically and you cannot change it.

### Are there UK rules on fake reviews?
Yes. The DMCC Act introduced a banned practice on fake reviews, in force from 6 April 2025 for unfair commercial practices provisions.

### How many categories should I use?
As few as possible to describe your core business.
