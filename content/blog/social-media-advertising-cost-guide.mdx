## Why Do Published Ad Prices Differ So Much?

Cost depends on the platform, the ad objective, the audience and the industry. A click on a broad awareness campaign and a click on a lead form are priced differently, and a neighbourhood audience can cost more or less than a national one. Any single average quoted without those details is hard to use.

The sources checked here do not give a verified average cost per click for Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn or Pinterest. Earlier versions of this guide quoted benchmark prices, but those figures could not be traced to a source in this review, so they have been removed. Check current prices inside your own ad account, where the auction shows what your audience actually costs.

## What Does Each Ad Type Cost to Run?

Adobe's guide describes the main formats: photo ads, video ads, carousel ads, story ads and influencer ads. It notes that short videos under one minute are generally more impactful, and that video can appear in search results. Format choice affects production cost more than media cost, so a simple photo ad is often the cheapest place to start.

Story ads suit time-sensitive offers, such as a limited promotion running for a few days. Carousels let one ad show several services, which can help a local business that offers more than one thing. The format should match the action the viewer is expected to take.

## How Do You Size a First Test Budget?

Start with what one customer is worth in profit, not in revenue. Decide the most you could pay for a customer and still break even, then work backwards to the cost you can afford per lead or per booking. That ceiling, not an industry average, should drive the budget.

A test needs enough results to judge. Very small daily budgets collect so few clicks that one good or bad day decides the outcome. Set a budget that can run for several weeks, and compare results between ad variations aimed at the same audience.

## What Does a $500 Month Look Like?

Every input below is an assumption to replace with your own figures. The purpose is to show the arithmetic, not to predict results for any business.

| Input or result | Value | Label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $500 | Assumption |
| Average cost per click | $1.00 | Assumption |
| Clicks bought | 500 | Budget divided by cost per click |
| Click-to-enquiry rate | 5% | Assumption |
| Enquiries | 25 | Clicks multiplied by rate |
| Cost per enquiry | $20 | Budget divided by enquiries |
| Enquiries that become customers | 1 in 3, so 8 | Assumption |
| Profit per customer | $120 | Assumption |
| Gross profit from customers | $960 | 8 multiplied by $120 |
| Net result after ad spend | $460 | $960 minus $500 |

If the click-to-enquiry rate falls to 2%, the same budget produces 10 enquiries and 3 customers, a gross profit of $360 and a net loss of $140. The test is therefore sensitive to the landing page and follow-up process, not only to the ad. Measure those before scaling spend.

## Which Tools Help Track Ad Spend?

Keep ad spend in the same records as the rest of the business. Exporting monthly ad invoices into bookkeeping lets you compare spend with the bookings and sales it produced. The [FreshBooks and QuickBooks comparison for small business](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business) covers which accounting tool suits a local team's workflow.

If several people post or reply to ads, a scheduling tool keeps activity organised. The [Hootsuite and Sprout Social comparison for local businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) explains the trade-offs. Tool subscriptions are a separate cost from ad media and should be budgeted on their own line.

## How Does Social Compare With Other Local Channels?

Social ads reach people based on interests and location, while search ads reach people already looking for a service. Television is a different kind of channel with its own costs and audience. Canadian businesses weighing television against digital can read the [CTV advertising guide for Canadian small businesses](/blog/ctv-advertising-canada-small-business-guide) before committing a budget to one medium.

Compare channels on cost per customer, not cost per impression or click. A cheap channel that produces no bookings is more expensive than it looks.

## What Steps Come Before the First Spend?

1. Write the profit earned from one new customer.
2. Set the highest cost per customer that will be accepted.
3. Choose one objective, such as enquiries or bookings, that matches the action wanted.
4. Set conversion tracking so bookings and calls are recorded before the first dollar is spent.
5. Run a fixed budget for several weeks with one audience and two ad variations.
6. Review cost per customer, not likes or clicks, and only then raise spend.

## FAQ

### How much should a small business spend on social media ads?

No verified standard figure exists in the sources checked. Start with an amount that can be afforded if it is lost, set a cost-per-customer ceiling, and increase spend only after results meet it.

### Do ads on different platforms cost the same?

Not necessarily. Prices vary by platform, objective and audience. The sources reviewed do not publish verified averages, so compare costs inside your own account.

### Are short videos better for ads?

Adobe notes that short videos under one minute are generally more impactful. Test a short version against a static image before assuming either wins.

### How do I know whether my ads work?

Count bookings, calls, form enquiries and sales, not likes. Set up conversion tracking before spending so results are recorded from the first day.

### Should I use a platform minimum budget as my budget?

No. A platform minimum is the lowest amount it will accept, not the amount that produces a useful test. Size the budget from a cost-per-customer ceiling instead.
