If you sell products online, whether a pet boutique, a coffee roaster or a salon retail line, the title and description you submit decide which searches your products can match. Google publishes exact rules for both in Merchant Center, and most weak listings break at least one. This guide turns those rules into a checklist.

All figures in this article come from Google's Merchant Center help pages: [title requirements](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324415) and [description requirements](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324468). Google does not publish a conversion lift for following them, so treat the rules as eligibility and relevance guidance, not a promise of more sales.

## What does Google require in product titles?

- Titles can be 1 to 150 characters. Google recommends using the full 150, because the title is what gets matched to a shopper's query.
- Users usually notice only the first 70 or fewer characters, so put the most important details first.
- Include the product name, brand if it differentiates the product, and specific details such as size or color. Details with no dedicated attribute, such as flavor, belong in the title.
- Do not include promotional text: price, sale dates, shipping details or your company name. Do not use all caps, symbols, HTML tags or extra spaces.

## What does Google require in product descriptions?

- Descriptions can be 1 to 5,000 characters, and every product needs one. Missing descriptions trigger a warning and may limit performance.
- List the most important details in the first 160 to 500 characters, since shoppers must click to read more.
- The description should match the title and the landing page, and cover the whole product, including bundle contents.
- Do not include other products or comparisons, links to your store, category references, promotional text, or your business history and policies.

## What does a before and after listing look like?

This worked example is an **assumption**: an invented dog shampoo, not a real listing. The product details are made up, so write only what is true of your product.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Title | SALE!!! Best Dog Shampoo - Free Shipping - Paws & Co | Oatmeal Dog Shampoo for Sensitive Skin, Fragrance Free, 16 oz Bottle, Hypoallergenic |
| Problems | Promotional text, caps, shipping, store name, no product detail | None of those; leads with product type and the detail that sets it apart |
| First 70 characters | SALE!!! Best Dog Shampoo - Free Shipping - Paws & Co | Oatmeal Dog Shampoo for Sensitive Skin, Fragrance Free, 16 oz Bo |

Description before: "Our groomers love it! See also our conditioner. Buy now, free shipping over $50." This breaks several of Google's rules (references to other products, promotional text, business claims).

Description after: "Gentle oatmeal shampoo for dogs with sensitive or dry skin. Fragrance free and soap free. Suitable for puppies over 8 weeks. 16 oz bottle with pump. Lathers easily and rinses clean. Ingredients: oatmeal, aloe, glycerin."

The product facts are in the first 160 characters, and they are statements you must be able to back up on the product page. The ingredient list and age claim are assumptions for the example.

## How do you optimize a product listing? A checklist

1. Put the product type and the differentiator in the first 70 characters.
2. Use the variant attributes (size, color) in their own fields and in the title.
3. Remove promotions and shipping from titles and descriptions; use the dedicated attributes.
4. Make the landing page match the listing exactly.
5. Rewrite your ten best sellers first.
6. Recheck any AI-generated copy. Google states AI-generated titles and descriptions must be submitted in the structured_title and structured_description attributes.

## FAQ

### How long should a product title be?
Google allows up to 150 characters and recommends using them, with the key details in the first 70.

### How long should a product description be?
Up to 5,000 characters, but the first 160 to 500 characters carry the most important details.

### Can I put "free shipping" or the sale price in the title?
No. Google says not to include price, sale dates, shipping or your company name in titles or descriptions.

### Do I need unique descriptions for every product?
Google requires a description for each product and says it should describe the whole product, so copied manufacturer text for different variants is a weak approach.
