OTT (over-the-top) advertising means video ads inside streaming services, delivered over the internet instead of cable or broadcast. A local business buys it by CPM, the price per 1,000 ad views. Vendor benchmarks for Q2 2026 put the market at roughly $15 to $45 per 1,000 views, with most campaigns at $25 to $35 ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/average-ctv-cpm-q2-2026)). Amazon says its streaming TV ads have no minimum spend, and Roku campaigns typically start around $500 in total (secondary source).

These are estimates, not published rate cards. Streaming platforms do not list fixed prices, so ask for a current quote before you plan a budget.

## Why does OTT matter for a local business?

Because your customers are already there. Nielsen's June 2026 Gauge, as reported by MediaPost, put streaming at 48.5% of total US TV viewing, ahead of broadcast at 19.8% and cable at 19.5% ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/417308/streaming-climbs-to-485-says-last-lame-duck-edi.html), secondary source for Nielsen data).

Self-serve tools have also lowered the entry point. A shop no longer needs a TV sales rep and a five-figure commitment to try it. Whether it pays for you depends on what a new customer is worth, which the worked example below shows.

## What is the difference between OTT and CTV?

OTT is the delivery method: content and ads sent over the internet. CTV is the screen: a smart TV, Roku, Fire TV, Apple TV or game console. An OTT ad can play on a phone, a laptop or a TV. A CTV ad always plays on a TV. For local buying the two terms are often used together, and our guide on [CTV vs OTT advertising](/blog/ctv-vs-ott-advertising-explained) covers the split in more detail.

## How much does OTT advertising cost?

AdWave's Q2 2026 benchmark gives these ranges per 1,000 impressions. All are vendor estimates, and the page describes them as benchmarks, not fixed rate cards ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/average-ctv-cpm-q2-2026)).

| Inventory type | Typical CPM (estimate) |
|---|---|
| Free ad-supported streaming (Tubi, Pluto, Roku Channel) | about $15 to $25 |
| Standard programmatic on premium services | about $25 to $45 |
| Premium direct deals and live sports | about $45 to $65 |
| Addressable, first-party targeted buys | about $45 to $85 |

Vibe estimates Roku at $20 to $60 per 1,000 impressions with an average near $30, and says Roku does not publish fixed rates ([Vibe](https://www.vibe.co/blog/roku-advertising-cost), a competing seller, so treat as secondary). The same page says there is no official minimum, but most campaigns start around $500 in total.

Tighter geographic targeting usually raises the CPM because fewer impressions qualify. See our breakdown of [what CTV advertising costs](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data).

## Where can a small business actually buy OTT ads?

Three routes exist, and they differ in minimums and control.

- **Amazon Ads streaming TV.** Amazon calls it a self-service product with no minimum spend, billed by CPM, running on Prime Video, Twitch and Amazon Publisher Direct. For small and medium businesses it suggests testing with a daily budget of $150 ([Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/streaming-tv-ads)). The guide lists targeting by content interests and in-market categories and does not mention location targeting, so check whether you can limit delivery to your area.
- **Roku and other CTV self-serve platforms.** Roku does not publish a minimum. Third-party sources put a typical start near $500.
- **YouTube on TV screens.** Google's help page says the TV masthead is available only on a reservation basis through a Google sales representative. Non-skippable in-stream and bumper ads can also appear on YouTube TV, depending on eligibility ([Google Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2375464)).

Hulu has a self-serve Ad Manager, but the only minimum figure found dates from its 2020 launch and Hulu's own page could not be opened, so check its advertising site for current terms. For a side-by-side look, read [CTV advertising platforms compared](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026).

## How do you target a local audience on OTT?

Vibe says Roku viewers can be targeted by location, age, interests or streaming behaviour ([Vibe](https://www.vibe.co/blog/roku-advertising-cost)). In practice, a local shop wants three things set before launch.

1. A geographic limit that matches where customers travel from, such as a city or set of ZIP codes.
2. A frequency cap, so one household does not see the ad dozens of times a week.
3. Dayparting if the platform offers it, so the budget runs when your customers are awake.

Availability of each control differs by platform and buying route. Confirm it in writing with the seller.

## The math: what does $600 buy, and what must it return?

Everything below is an **assumption** used to show the arithmetic. It is not a real campaign or a market statistic.

- Assumption: $600 monthly budget and a $30 CPM, the midpoint of the $25 to $35 range most campaigns land in.
- Views: 600 / 30 x 1,000 = 20,000 ad views.
- Assumption: the targeting reaches about 6,000 households, so each sees the ad about 3.3 times.

| Assumed value of one new customer | New customers needed to cover $600 |
|---|---|
| $20 (one first visit) | 30 |
| $80 (a few visits) | 8 |
| $250 (a regular for a year) | 3 |

If you can only count a single $20 visit, you need 30 new customers from one month of streaming ads, which is hard for most local shops. If a regular is worth $250, three is plausible. Your own customer value decides whether OTT makes sense.

## How do you measure whether it worked?

OTT viewers rarely click. Use methods that do not rely on a click:

- A unique promo code or QR code on the ad that points to a page you control.
- A tracked phone number used only in the ad.
- A "how did you hear about us?" question for every new customer during the test.
- Branded search volume in Google Search Console before, during and after the flight.

Set a review date and a stop rule before you spend. Six weeks is a reasonable first window, but that is a judgment, not a platform rule.

## Should you start with OTT or with search ads?

Fund search first. [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and your Google Business Profile reach people who already want what you sell. OTT builds awareness on top of that foundation. If a small budget cannot run for several weeks with enough repetition to be remembered, spend it on search and revisit OTT later. ## FAQ

### How much does OTT advertising cost for a local business?
There is no published price list. Vendor benchmarks for Q2 2026 put CPMs at about $15 to $45 across the market. Amazon has no minimum spend and suggests a $150 daily test budget, and Roku campaigns typically start near $500 in total according to a vendor source.

### Is OTT the same as connected TV?
Not exactly. OTT is the delivery method over the internet, and CTV is the TV screen it plays on. OTT also includes phones and laptops.

### Can I target only people in my city?
Some platforms offer location targeting, including Roku according to Vibe. Amazon's streaming TV guide does not mention it. Confirm the controls with each seller before you buy.

### Do OTT ads work for small budgets?
A small budget buys few views. At a $30 CPM, $600 buys about 20,000 views (an assumption-based calculation). It works best with tight local targeting and a clear way to track results.

### How do I know if someone came in because of my ad?
Use a promo code, a QR code to a landing page, a tracked number and a direct question to new customers. No method catches every visit.
