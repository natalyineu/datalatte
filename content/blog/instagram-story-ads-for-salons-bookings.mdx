A salon should run Instagram Story ads as a small, local test with one offer, one vertical creative and a direct booking link. Count bookings, not taps. In WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 Meta benchmarks, Beauty and Personal Care lead campaigns averaged $50.91 per lead, against $27.39 for all industries. So set expectations from real numbers, not from claims that Stories "double bookings".

This guide is for salons, barbershops and spas in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. The benchmarks cover Facebook and Instagram advertising together, not Stories alone. Illustrative figures are labelled as assumptions.

## What Are Instagram Story Ads?

Story ads are paid vertical ads shown in the Stories placement between people's Stories. You do not buy them separately. In Meta's Ads Manager you choose an objective, then choose placements. Meta's Help Center describes Stories as one of several Instagram placements (alongside Feed, Explore, Reels and others) and recommends a 9:16 vertical format.

If you supply media that is not vertical, Meta's help pages say it may reformat it, for example into a full-screen template. Upload a real vertical file so you control how it looks. Exact size limits change, so check the Stories design requirements page and the Ads Manager preview before you publish. Keep faces, prices and buttons away from the top and bottom edges, where the profile icon and call-to-action sit. Meta's ad specs for vertical Reels images, for example, ask advertisers to keep roughly the top 14%, bottom 35% and the sides 6% free of text and logos.

## What Do Beauty Ads Cost on Meta?

LocaliQ's 2026 figures separate traffic campaigns (clicks to a page) from lead campaigns (form fills). For Beauty and Personal Care, traffic campaigns averaged a 2.73% click-through rate and $0.50 per click. Lead campaigns averaged a 1.35% click-through rate, $2.97 per click, a 5.63% conversion rate and $50.91 per lead. Health and Fitness lead campaigns averaged $27.11 per lead, and the all-industry average was $27.39.

These are averages across placements and advertisers. Your cost depends on your offer, area and creative.

## How Should a Salon Set Up the Campaign?

**Pick one goal.** For a booking link, a traffic or conversions style objective fits. For a form inside Instagram, a lead objective fits. Meta says it is rolling out a new set of six campaign objectives, so the labels you see in Ads Manager may differ from older guides.

**Keep the audience local.** Set a location around the salon you can realistically serve. Let Meta find people inside it before you stack on narrow interests.

**Make one clear offer.** A first-visit price, a seasonal service or a gap-filling slot works better than "Book now" with nothing behind it. State the price or deal in the first lines.

**Send people to a working booking page.** Open it on a phone. If booking takes more than a few taps, fix that before spending on ads. Our [Facebook ads guide for hair salons](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons) covers campaign setup in more detail.

## What Should the Story Creative Show?

Four formats are worth testing. These are ideas to test, not proven winners:

1. **Before and after** of a service you actually did, with the client's permission.
2. **Limited offer** with the price, dates and number of slots.
3. **Stylist introduction** that shows who the client will meet.
4. **Real client review** read out or shown on screen, with permission. Do not invent or edit reviews. The US Federal Trade Commission bans fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones, when the business knew or should have known they were fake.

Use short text, a clear next step and sound that still works with the volume off. Film on a phone in good light.

## How Do You Track Whether Story Ads Bring Bookings?

Put a unique tag on the booking link for the campaign, and ask new clients how they found you. Check whether your booking software shows the traffic source. Without this you only see taps.

Track four numbers: spend, clicks or leads, bookings, and the revenue from those bookings. Cost per booking is the one that decides whether to continue. A click is not a client.

At benchmark prices, $105 buys about two leads and about one booking. That does not pay back on the first visit alone, so repeat visits matter. The honest conclusion is that a one-week test tells you little. Run it long enough to see at least 10 to 20 leads before judging, and compare against cheaper routes such as referrals and your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile).

## FAQ

### Do I need a lot of followers to run Story ads?

No. Paid ads reach people chosen by your targeting, not only your followers. Ad performance depends on the offer, creative and audience.

### How much should a salon spend to test Story ads?

There is no official number. LocaliQ's averages suggest roughly $51 per lead for beauty lead campaigns, so a $105 week buys only a couple of leads. Set a budget you can afford to lose, and run the test long enough to see results.

### Do Story ads work without a website?

Meta offers lead forms and messaging objectives, so you can collect contacts without a site. A direct booking link usually removes steps for the client.

### What size should the video be?

Meta recommends a vertical 9:16 format for Stories. Check the current Stories design requirements and the Ads Manager preview for exact limits.

### Why did my earlier Instagram ads fail?

Common causes are a vague offer, a slow booking page, a wrong audience area or no tracking. Review each before spending more.
