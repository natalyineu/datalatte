Most local businesses should start with a standard Search campaign and add Performance Max later. Search lets you choose keywords and match types, so you see which searches you pay for. Performance Max runs across Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps from one campaign, and Google's AI picks placements. It can add reach, but you give up keyword-level control. Google positions it as a complement to Search, not a replacement.

This guide compares the two using Google's help pages and LocaliQ's 2026 Search benchmarks. No source reviewed for this guide publishes Performance Max averages, so none are quoted. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Is the Difference Between Performance Max and Search Campaigns?

A Search campaign shows text ads on Google results when a search matches your keywords. Performance Max is a goal-based campaign type that runs ads across all of Google's inventory. You supply assets and goals, and Google combines them and decides where to show them.

| | Search campaign | Performance Max |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where ads show | Google Search results | Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail, Maps |
| What you supply | Keywords, ads | Assets (text, images, video), goals |
| Keyword control | Broad, phrase, exact match | Search themes, negative keywords |
| Audience control | Targeting settings | Audience signals as hints |
| Query reporting | Search terms report | Search terms report (added 2025) |
| Best first use | Intent to buy now | Extra reach once tracking is solid |

The control gap is the main trade-off. Google says exact match gives the most control, and that Performance Max shows ads based on goals, not a keyword list.

## Which One Gives You More Control?

Search does. Google's match type page says broader match types reach more searches while narrower types give more control over who sees your ads. You can also add negative keywords at several match types to block irrelevant searches. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) shows what to block.

Performance Max has added controls. Google's August 2025 update lists campaign-level negative keywords for Search and Shopping inventory, shared negative keyword lists, search terms reporting for all Performance Max campaigns, and up to 50 search themes per asset group. Audience signals help the AI learn but are not hard limits.

## How Do They Work Together?

Google states that Search campaigns containing an exact match keyword are prioritised to serve over Performance Max. Search themes have the same priority as phrase and broad match keywords. So a running Search campaign with exact match keywords for your core terms keeps those searches under your control.

That makes a two-campaign setup reasonable. Keep Search on your core local terms, such as "service plus city". Let Performance Max try wider reach. Check for overlap in the search terms report. If you want the setup steps, read [how to set up Performance Max](/blog/how-to-set-up-performance-max-campaigns-step-by-step).

## What About Reporting and Cost?

On reporting, Search shows search terms with cost, so you can see what each query cost. Google's 2025 update says Performance Max now has search terms reporting for all campaigns and full asset stats, and a channel performance report in beta. Beta features may not reach every account, so check yours.

On cost, LocaliQ's 2026 Search benchmarks give an all-industry average of $5.42 per click, 6.64% click-through, 8.18% conversion rate and $66.69 per lead. These are Search averages, not a promise for your account, and they are not Performance Max numbers. Our [Google Ads hardware stores guide](/blog/google-ads-for-hardware-stores) shows how to read them by category.

## When Should a Local Business Use Each?

Use Search first when:

- People already search for what you sell, such as "emergency plumber" or "dog groomer near me".
- You need to see exact queries and block bad ones.
- Your budget is small and every click must count.

Add Performance Max when:

- Conversion tracking is accurate and counts real bookings or calls.
- You have images, a logo and short copy ready. Google requires headlines, long headlines, descriptions, a landscape and square image and a logo.
- You want store visits or local actions across Maps and other surfaces.

## What Would a Fair Test Look Like?

The figures below are **assumptions**. A salon has $1,200 a month. It puts $900 into Search and $300 into Performance Max, both counting booking-form submissions and calls as conversions. The assumption for Search uses LocaliQ's $66.69 average cost per lead to estimate roughly 13 leads ($900 divided by $66.69). Performance Max has no published benchmark, so the test sets its own.

Judge both after 1 to 2 conversion cycles, as Google suggests for performance to stabilise. If the Performance Max cost per booking is no worse than Search, keep it. If it is clearly worse, pause it and move the money back. The point is that both campaigns count the same conversion, so they can be compared fairly.

## FAQ

### Is Performance Max better than Search campaigns?

Neither is better in general. Search gives keyword control and clear query data. Performance Max adds reach across more Google surfaces. Google calls it a complement to Search.

### Can I run both at the same time?

Yes. Google says Search campaigns with an exact match keyword are prioritised over Performance Max for that query, so your core terms stay in Search.

### Does Performance Max show search terms?

Google says search terms reporting is available for all Performance Max campaigns. The older insights did not include cost, so check what your account shows.

### What is a good cost per click?

LocaliQ's 2026 all-industry Search average is $5.42 per click. That is not a Performance Max figure. Your own data after a test is the benchmark that matters.

### How long should I test before deciding?

Google says performance can take 1 to 2 conversion cycles to stabilise, and recommends at least 30 days for store goals. Match the window to how long your customers take to book.
