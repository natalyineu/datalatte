A hair salon can publish three kinds of Google Business Profile posts: Update, Offer and Event. An Update carries text, a photo or video and an optional button such as Book. An Offer needs a title and dates and gets a "View Offer" button automatically. An Event needs a title with start and end dates. Google archives posts older than 6 months unless you set a date range.

Google's ranking guidance does not name posts as a ranking input. Treat them as a free shop window on your profile, not as a ranking trick. This guide uses only Google's own help pages, and every example figure is labelled as an assumption.

## What Can a Salon Post on Google?

Google lists three post types, and each has its own required fields.

| Post type | Required | Optional | Button |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Update | Text, photo or video | Action button with a link | Book, Order online, Learn more |
| Offer | Title, dates and times | Description, photo, coupon code, link, terms | "View Offer", added automatically |
| Event | Title, start and end dates and times | Description, photo, video, button | Action button |

If an event has no start or end time, Google shows it as lasting 24 hours on the event dates. Posts can be scheduled: turn on "Schedule this post" and pick a date, or use the "Repeats" menu.

## Where Do Salon Posts Appear?

On mobile, posts appear in the Updates or Overview tab of your profile in Search and Maps. On desktop they appear in the "From the owner" section. So a post is seen by people who are already looking at your profile, which makes it a conversion tool more than a discovery tool.

For single-location food and drink businesses in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, Google also shows featured posts on mobile. That feature is for food and drink, so a hair salon should not expect it.

Because people reach the posts from your profile, the button matters. Link it to your booking page, not your home page. See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) for the rest of the profile.

## What Should a Hair Salon Post About?

Google does not publish topic data by industry, so these are suggestions rather than tested results. Match each post type to something a client could act on this week.

- **Update:** a finished colour or cut with a photo you have the client's permission to use, a new stylist, or a reminder that you are open late on Thursdays.
- **Offer:** a limited-time price on a service, with a clear end date, for example a gloss add-on for the month.
- **Event:** a bridal hair trial day, a product launch evening, or a charity cut day.

Keep each post about one thing. Add a booking button on every Update and Event.

## What Will Google Reject?

Posts get a status: Live, Pending or Not approved. A Not approved post is not shown and the profile marks which content policy it broke. Google's help page names several causes:

- A phone number in the description that cannot be verified as connected to the business
- Misspellings, extra characters, text with no value, or auto-generated or distracting content
- Offensive language or images, or links to unsafe sites
- Posts about products in regulated categories, such as alcohol, tobacco, pharmaceuticals or health and medical devices. Posts about the business itself are allowed.

For a salon, the regulated-product rule matters if you sell hair-loss or medical-style treatments. Describe your service, not the product claims.

## How Do You Measure Whether Posts Work?

Google's performance report shows only the metrics that apply to your profile, such as calls, directions, website clicks, messages and bookings. A person is counted once per day for views, even across devices. Posts are not reported as a separate metric on that help page, so measure by what follows: add a UTM tag to the button link and watch bookings in your booking tool.

## Worked Example: A Monthly Posting Plan

All inputs below are **assumptions** for a salon with 4 stylists, not benchmarks.

| Week | Post | Type | Time to prepare (assumption) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Photo of a recent colour result with Book button | Update | 10 minutes |
| 2 | Late-opening evening notice | Update | 5 minutes |
| 3 | Month-end gloss add-on, dates set | Offer | 10 minutes |
| 4 | Bridal trial day, if one is planned | Event | 15 minutes |

That is about 40 minutes a month. Check at the end of the month whether bookings with your UTM tag came in. If none did, change the button link or the offer before changing the plan.

## FAQ

### How often should a hair salon post on Google?

Google gives no required frequency. Posts older than 6 months are archived unless you set a date range, so one or two a month keeps the Updates tab current without much work.

### Do Google posts help a salon rank higher?

Google's local ranking guidance names relevance, distance and prominence and does not list posts. Use posts to convert profile visitors, not to move rankings.

### Can I put my phone number in a post?

Google says a phone number in the description may lead to rejection if it cannot be verified as connected to your business. Use the button and your profile's phone field instead.

### Can I schedule posts in advance?

Yes. When you add a post, turn on "Schedule this post" and pick a date and time, or use the "Repeats" menu.

### What happens when a post expires?

Posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set. Offers and Events use the dates you give them.
