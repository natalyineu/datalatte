## What Post Types Can a Salon Use?

Google Business Profile posts come in a few forms, and the two with the most fixed rules are Offer and Event posts. An Offer post automatically includes a "View Offer" button, so the offer title should state the deal clearly. An Event post should have both a start and an end time. If you leave out the end time, Google shows the event as lasting 24 hours on the event dates, according to [Google's posts help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en).

Offer ideas need to be specific. A vendor guide for salon marketing suggests a new-client offer such as "$20 off first color service", promoted monthly ([Zeely, secondary](https://zeely.ai/blog/hair-salon-advertising-ideas/)). Use the same structure for your own offer: one service, one price, one end date.

## What Should a Salon Post About?

Google does not publish topic data by industry, so the ideas below are suggestions, not tested results. Pick topics a client could act on within a week, such as a recent colour result, a new stylist joining, or a gloss add-on running until month end.

Keep each post about one thing. Use a photo where you can, because a guide to Google Business Profile automation recommends a photo for both Offer and Event posts ([dmly, secondary](https://dmly.io/google-business-profile-automation-guide/)). Only use images of clients who have given permission to be shown.

Link the button to your booking page rather than your home page. A client who sees a post has already looked at your profile, so the next step should be booking, not browsing.

## Why Does Google Reject Some Posts?

Google reviews each post after it is published. Only posts with a Live status are public and show on Search and Maps, so a post that is pending or rejected will not reach anyone. Google's help page gives one specific cause that salon owners often miss: a phone number in the post description may lead to rejection if Google cannot verify it as connected to your business.

Use your profile's phone field and the post button for contact details instead. Check Google's current content guidelines before publishing any health-related or product claims, because the rules can change and the posts help page is the authoritative source.

## How Long Does a Salon Post Stay Visible?

A post older than 6 months is archived unless you set a date range. For an Offer or Event, the end date you enter controls when it stops, so set dates on purpose. For a standard post, plan to republish something fresh each month so the Updates section does not look stale.

Posts also cannot be edited once published. According to dmly, you have to delete the post and publish a new one to change it ([dmly, secondary](https://dmly.io/google-business-profile-automation-guide/)). Proofread wording, dates and button links before you publish.

## How Do You Tell If Posts Bring Bookings?

Add a UTM tag to every post button link, then compare the bookings in your booking tool with the posts you published that month. Google's Performance tab reports on profile activity, and dmly lists it as one of five tabs on a connected profile ([dmly, secondary](https://dmly.io/google-business-profile-automation-guide/)). Read the Performance tab for calls and website clicks, but rely on your own booking data for the bookings figure.

## FAQ

### How often should a hair salon post on Google?

Google gives no required frequency. Because posts older than 6 months are archived unless you set a date range, one or two posts a month keeps the Updates section current without much work.

### Can I put my phone number in a post?

Avoid it. Google says a post with a phone number in the description may be rejected if the number cannot be verified as connected to your business. Use the button and your profile's phone field instead.

### Can I schedule posts in advance?

Google has no scheduled posting feature of its own, so scheduled posts are held outside Google until they publish. A third-party tool is needed for that, and Google's API quota may need a request before automated posting works ([dmly, secondary](https://dmly.io/google-business-profile-automation-guide/)).

### What happens to a post after 6 months?

Google archives posts older than 6 months unless you set a date range. Offer and Event posts follow the dates you enter for them.

### Can I edit a post after publishing?

No. According to dmly, you need to delete the post and publish a new one to change it ([dmly, secondary](https://dmly.io/google-business-profile-automation-guide/)).
