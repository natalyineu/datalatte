Choose one primary category that describes your core service as specifically as the dropdown allows, then add two or three extras only for services you deliver today. Check the wording against the phrases customers type and against the top local competitors in Google Maps. The primary category carries the most weight. Adwave's analysis of millions of profiles says it is the top ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, although the analysis source is not named.

## Why does the primary category carry so much weight?

Adwave's article on [choosing Google Business Profile categories](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) states that in-depth analysis of millions of profiles shows the primary category is the #1 ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack. Treat that as a vendor's reading of the data rather than a Google statement, but it is a clear reason to get the primary category right first.

Other signals still count. Adwave's article on [Google AI Overviews for local business](https://adwave.com/resources/google-ai-overviews-local-business) says weak performance on simple local searches points back to profile accuracy, reviews, categories, and proximity. The category is one lever among several, so fix it alongside the rest of the profile.

The vendor figure above is a claim, not a forecast. Adwave says a well-ranking profile can generate 20-50+ leads per month for active contractor categories. That number is for contractors, so a café or groomer should use it only as a sense of what a strong profile can aim for.

## How many extra categories should you add?

Google's help page on [editing your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617) says you can select up to 9 more categories in addition to the primary category. Keepers Digital describes the same structure, with one primary and up to nine secondary categories, which helps a business appear in searches for related services. The [Keepers Digital comparison of Yelp and Google](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/) makes that point in its section on categories.

The cap is not a target. Adwave recommends choosing a small number of additional categories, usually 2-3, that represent other major services you offer. Use that as the working range and add more only when each one describes a real service.

Multi-location businesses face a stricter rule. The [Cheers guide on Google Business Profile and AI visibility](https://www.cheers.tech/geo-academy/is-google-business-profile-enough-for-ai-visibility) (secondary, summarising Google's guidelines for chains) says locations that provide the same service should generally keep consistent names and categories. For a single shop, the same principle means using one set of categories everywhere. Check that your other directory listings match, using the steps in the [local citation building guide](/blog/local-citation-building-small-business-guide).

## How do you find the wording customers and Google use?

Start with the words customers type, not the words you use inside the business. Work through these steps:

1. Write down five phrases a customer would type, such as "dog groomer near me" or "mobile pet grooming".
2. Type each phrase into the category field of your profile and screenshot the suggestions. Adwave notes these come from a predefined list of over 4,000 categories.
3. Search your main phrase in Google Maps on a phone, standing near your service area, and open the top three to five profiles.
4. Note the category each of those profiles uses. Adwave recommends this comparison specifically.
5. Pick the most specific option that matches where most of your revenue comes from.

Use competitors as a check, not a template. If the top profiles all use a category you had never considered and it truly fits your work, consider it. If it does not describe what you do, skip it, even if it ranks for competitors.

## Which extras are honest, and which cause problems?

Add an extra category only if you deliver that service today and a customer could book or buy it this week. A groomer with a van that does on-site visits passes that test. A groomer who once sold calming treats does not, and neither does a café that hosted one wedding.

Categories also control features. Google's help page on [editing your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617) describes a car dealership inventory feature as a pilot available only for select business categories and regions. The same logic applies to other profile features: a category or region can determine what you see. After any category change, check the buttons and features on your profile, not just your rankings.

If you also run paid search, the category settings in your profile do not replace your ad account setup. The [Google Ads setup guide for small businesses](/blog/how-do-i-set-up-google-ads-for-my-small-business-complete-guide-for-2026) covers that side separately.

## How do you measure whether a change helped?

Before editing, record searches, calls, and direction requests for the last 28 days from your profile's performance report. Change one thing only, such as the primary category or the extras, and note the date. Do not change the name at the same time, or you will not know what caused any difference.

Compare the same 28-day window afterwards. Four to six weeks is a practical waiting period suggested here, not a Google figure. Seasonality can distort results, so compare against the same period a year earlier if you have that data. Then check how your profile appears for your top three phrases from a phone in your area.

## What should you do this week?

1. Screenshot the category suggestions for your five customer phrases.
2. Note the primary category of the top three profiles in Google Maps for your main phrase.
3. Record the last 28 days of searches, calls, and direction requests.
4. Set the most specific accurate primary category, remove any extras you cannot deliver today, and add two or three real extras.
5. Set a reminder to compare the same metrics in six weeks, and check your other directory listings for matching categories.

## FAQ

### How many categories can I choose?

Google's help page on [editing your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617) says you can select up to 9 more categories in addition to the primary. Adwave suggests choosing a small number, usually 2-3, that represent other major services you offer. Only add a category when you deliver that service.

### Does the primary category affect rankings?

Adwave's analysis states that the primary category is the #1 ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, but it does not name the study behind that claim. Adwave's AI overviews article also says that profile accuracy, reviews, categories, and proximity all affect local performance. Treat the category as important, not as the only factor.

### What if the category I want is not in the dropdown?

Adwave notes that category suggestions come from a predefined list of over 4,000 options. Try different wording, such as the phrases customers use, and pick the closest accurate option. Describe the rest of what you offer in your services and description.

### Can changing categories remove profile features?

Yes, in some cases. Google's help page describes at least one feature, the car dealership inventory pilot, as available only for select business categories and regions. Check your profile's buttons and features after any change.
