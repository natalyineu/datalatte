To choose the right Google Business Profile categories, pick the most specific category that describes your main service as your primary category, then add a small number of accurate secondary categories for other things you genuinely offer. Categories are one of the strongest signals you control for which searches your profile appears in, so they deserve a careful half hour.

This guide shows you how to find the best options, what to avoid and how to review your choices over time, with examples for coffee shops, salons, pet groomers and fitness studios.

## Why categories matter

Google uses your categories to understand what your business is and which searches it is relevant for. They also influence which features appear on your profile, such as booking buttons, menu links or product sections, depending on the category and country.

Three practical effects:

- **Matching searches.** A "dog groomer" and a "pet store" may both be near the searcher, but only one matches a grooming search well.
- **Filters and attributes.** Some attributes (for example, "wheelchair accessible entrance" or "online appointments") are available only for certain categories.
- **Competitor comparison.** Choosing categories like the businesses that rank above you is a quick way to see what Google considers relevant.

Category choice is not a magic switch. Distance, reviews, website quality and consistent listings also matter. But it is among the cheapest things to fix.

## Primary versus secondary categories

- **Primary category:** a single choice that should describe your core business as customers would. It tends to carry the most weight.
- **Secondary categories:** additional descriptors for other real services. Google allows multiple (up to nine at the time of writing, though this may change), but more is not better.

A simple rule: your primary should answer "What is this place?" and secondaries should answer "What else do you do as a real, ongoing service?"

## Step 1: Start from how customers search

Write down the phrases customers use, not the terms you prefer. For example:

- Coffee shop: "coffee shop near me", "cafe", "espresso bar"
- Salon: "hair salon", "balayage", "barber"
- Pet groomer: "dog groomer", "mobile pet grooming"
- Fitness studio: "yoga studio", "pilates studio", "personal trainer"

Then compare against Google's list of categories. You can browse the official list in the Google Business Profile help centre, or type into the category field of your profile and see the suggestions.

## Step 2: Study competitors

Search for your main keyword in Google Maps and open the top profiles. To see their categories, you can:

- Look at the category shown under the business name and star rating in Maps (this is generally the primary category).
- Use a browser extension such as GMB Everywhere or PlePer, or a local SEO platform like BrightLocal or Whitespark, which can reveal secondary categories. Tool availability and pricing change, so check current options.

Note which categories the top three to five competitors use. Do not copy blindly. Use the pattern as a sanity check.

## Step 3: Choose your primary category

Pick the most specific category that is accurate for your core revenue-generating service.

| Business | Possible primary | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Independent café | Coffee shop or Cafe | Matches the main search; choose based on how you operate |
| Hair studio | Hair salon | Specific; "Beauty salon" is broader |
| Men's cuts | Barber shop | More relevant to men's grooming searches |
| Dog grooming shop | Pet groomer | More relevant than "Pet store" if grooming is the main service |
| Boutique pilates space | Pilates studio | Specific beats "Gym" |

Category names differ slightly between countries and may change over time, so choose from what Google currently offers for your location.

If you hesitate between two, ask: "If I could only have one label on the front window, which would bring the right customers?"

## Step 4: Add secondary categories

Add a secondary category only if:

- You provide that service regularly
- Customers could reasonably book or buy it from you
- It is available as a category in Google's list

Examples:

- A coffee shop that also sells whole-bean coffee and bakes in-house might add "Coffee store" and "Bakery", if those are real, substantial parts of the business.
- A hair salon offering bridal styling and extensions might add "Wedding service" or "Hair extension technician" if available and appropriate.
- A dog groomer that also provides mobile service might add "Mobile pet groomer" if they truly operate a van.
- A studio that runs yoga and pilates might use "Yoga studio" as the primary and "Pilates studio" as a secondary.

Aim for a handful rather than the maximum. Over-broad categories can dilute relevance.

## Step 5: Avoid common mistakes

- **Adding categories you do not offer** in hope of ranking for them. This can lead to poor customer experiences and may breach Google's guidelines.
- **Picking a category that is too broad**, such as "Store" or "Health" when a precise option exists.
- **Changing the primary category too often.** Make a considered change, then leave it for a few weeks before judging results.
- **Using the business name to carry keywords.** Your name should match your real-world name; keyword stuffing can get a profile suspended.
- **Forgetting that categories drive features.** If a booking or menu option disappears after a change, you may have changed to a category that does not support it.

## Step 6: Align services, attributes and website

Categories work best alongside matching details:

- **Services section:** list the specific services under each category with short descriptions and prices if possible.
- **Attributes:** tick relevant options, such as dog-friendly, outdoor seating, women-led or online booking, where they are accurate.
- **Website:** make sure your site has pages for each major service and mentions the same terms. See our [local SEO for pet groomers](/blog/local-seo-for-pet-groomers) article for an example of aligning pages and listings.
- **Other listings:** match your category choices on Apple Maps, Bing Places and Yelp, since mismatched descriptions can confuse customers.

## Step 7: Review and test

Set a reminder to revisit categories every six months, or when you add or drop a service. Before and after any change:

1. Note your current Performance numbers: searches, calls, direction requests.
2. Record how your profile appears for your top three keywords, ideally from a phone in the area you serve.
3. Wait several weeks, then compare.

Do not expect instant leaps, and make only one major change at a time so that you can tell what helped.

## Seasonal and changing businesses

If your offer changes, for example a café that turns into a café-and-wine-bar in the evening, only add categories that are consistently true. Seasonal pop-ups are better handled with posts, hours and services than with temporary category changes.

## FAQ

**Q: How many categories should I choose?**
A: One primary plus a few accurate secondaries. Quality and accuracy matter more than quantity, even if Google allows more.

**Q: Can I create my own category?**
A: No. You must pick from Google's predefined list, though you can describe unusual services in your business description and services section.

**Q: Will changing my primary category hurt my ranking?**
A: It can change which searches you appear for, sometimes temporarily. Make the change only if the new category describes your business better, and monitor the results.

**Q: Why can't I find the category I want?**
A: The list varies by country and is updated periodically. Choose the closest accurate option and use your description and services to add detail.
