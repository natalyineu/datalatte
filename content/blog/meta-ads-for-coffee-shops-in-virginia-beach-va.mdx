In 2024, 14.3 million visitors came to Virginia Beach and spent $1.09 billion on food and beverages alone, according to a Tourism Economics report for the city's tourism office. It is tempting to read that as a reason to aim every Meta ad at the Oceanfront. The arithmetic later in this guide suggests the opposite for a coffee shop: a visitor who stops once is expensive to win, while a local who makes your café a morning habit can pay back a small ad budget in a few visits.

This guide is for independent café owners in Virginia Beach. It uses public data to describe who lives where, how big the visitor and military populations are, how many beverage businesses the city has, and what the calendar looks like. Then it runs the numbers on a $450 test. For the step-by-step campaign setup, offers and tracking, read our [Meta Ads for coffee shops complete guide](/blog/meta-ads-for-coffee-shops-complete-guide). Because three in four workers drive alone, many customers pass a café on the way to somewhere else, so parking and which side of the road you sit on may matter as much as the menu. Test that against your own morning sales.

### The military factor

ACS counts residents who are serving in the armed forces. In 2024, 23,321 Virginia Beach residents aged 16 and over were in the Armed Forces, about 6.3% of the 368,717 people in that age group. The ACS five-year estimates (2020 to 2024) also show that 52,520 civilian adults, 15.7% of the 334,536 civilian adults 18 and over, are veterans. These counts do not include the many sailors and families who live in neighboring cities, and they come from a survey with margins of error, so treat them as a sizeable-but-approximate share of your audience.

Why this matters for a café: new arrivals do not yet have a regular coffee place. The ACS data do not say how often people move in, so use your own customer data to see whether newcomer messaging works. If it does, a "new to Virginia Beach" angle can be tested with a Meta audience restricted to a few ZIP codes near the base gates you serve.

## The Visitor Economy in Numbers

Tourism Economics' 2024 report for Virginia Beach (published October 2025) found:

- **14.3 million visitors**, up 1.9% on 2023, spending $2.6 billion, with a total economic impact of $3.9 billion.
- **Food and beverage was the largest spending category**: $1.09 billion, or 42.6% of visitor spending.
- **Day visitors were 58.8% of volume** (8.42 million) and overnight visitors 5.89 million.
- **Spending per visitor was $178 on average**: $112 for a day visitor and $273 for an overnight visitor. That is spending across lodging, food, recreation and retail, not coffee.
- **Visitor spending supports 56% of food and beverage jobs** and, in total, 34,076 jobs, about one in five jobs in the city.

The implication for a café owner is double-edged. The visitor economy is a large part of why Virginia Beach has hospitality businesses, and a café near the Oceanfront draws on it. But a day visitor who is here for a few hours has less time and fewer reasons to come back, which affects how much you should pay to reach them (see The Math).

## How Many Beverage Businesses Are There?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages covers businesses subject to unemployment insurance. For the City of Virginia Beach, NAICS 722515 (Snack and Nonalcoholic Beverage Bars, the code that includes coffee shops along with juice, tea and similar counters), 2025 annual averages for private employers are:

- **114 establishments**, up from 93 in 2019, an increase of about 23%.
- **1,387 employees**, up from 1,162.
- **$23,532 average annual pay**, up from $16,291 in 2019.

That is a count of establishments in the category, not a count of independent cafés, and chain locations are included. It shows a growing field, so a plain "new coffee shop" ad has more to compete with than it did in 2019.

## What the Benchmarks Say

LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmarks (page updated September 23, 2026), compiled with WordStream, list Restaurants & Food traffic campaigns at an average click-through rate of 2.68% and a cost per click of $0.45. LocaliQ sells ad management, the page does not describe its sample, and the benchmarks are national, so there is no verified Virginia Beach figure. A click is also not a coffee.

On campaign objectives, Flighted's explainer says Meta now organises campaigns into six objectives (Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App Promotion and Sales) and that older objectives including Store Traffic are no longer available for new campaigns. It describes Traffic as sending people to a website, landing page, Instagram profile or app, and does not mention physical locations. So a café with no online ordering should plan to send clicks to a landing page with an offer, and count redemptions in the shop. Our complete guide covers how.

## A Split Audience Plan

Run the two audiences as separate ad sets so you can compare them:

1. **Residents.** Two or three ZIP codes where your regulars already live or work, using the postcodes of your last 50 loyalty customers. Offer: a weekday-morning deal that starts a routine.
2. **Visitors.** A small area around the café during the months you know are busy. Offer: a specific, in-the-moment item, such as an iced drink or a beach-day pastry, that someone can use today.

Test one change at a time and review weekly. Short vertical video of your counter and your drinks works as an ad format on Meta; our [coffee shop TikTok guide](/blog/coffee-shop-tiktok-marketing-guide) covers the content side.

## The Math

A $450 monthly Meta traffic test, split into three customer types. Rows marked assumption are illustrations, not benchmarks. The $0.45 click is LocaliQ's Restaurants & Food average.

| | Resident | Newcomer who builds a habit | Visitor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $450 | $450 | $450 |
| Cost per click (LocaliQ) | $0.45 | $0.45 | $0.45 |
| Clicks | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| Click to offer claim (assumption) | 6% | 6% | 6% |
| Claims | 60 | 60 | 60 |
| Claim to redemption (assumption) | 40% | 40% | 25% |
| New customers | 24 | 24 | 15 |
| Ad cost per new customer | $18.75 | $18.75 | $30.00 |
| Margin per visit at a $7 ticket and 65% margin (assumption) | $4.55 | $4.55 | $4.55 |
| Cost of the free item (assumption) | $1.20 | $1.20 | $1.20 |
| Visits in the next 90 days (assumption) | 3 | 5 | 1.2 |
| Net result after 90 days | -$151 | +$67 | -$386 |
| Visits needed to break even | 4.4 | 4.4 | 6.9 |

How to read it: break-even visits equal (ad cost per customer plus the free item) divided by margin per visit. Under these assumptions, the visitor never reaches break-even because they do not return, and the resident only does if you can move them from 3 visits to about 4.5. That is why a loyalty card, an email at the first visit and a reason to come back matter more than the click price. If your ticket is higher (brunch, for example) the break-even visit count falls, so re-run the table with your own figures.

<Callout type="coffee" title="Count Redemptions, Not Clicks">
Give each ad a different phrase for staff to hear, such as "the beach-day pastry" or "the morning pour", and keep a tally by the till. At the end of each week, compare redemptions by audience. Cost per redeemed customer is the number that decides where the next dollar goes.
</Callout>

## Do This Today

1. Work out your margin per visit and how many visits a typical new regular makes in 90 days. That gives you break-even visits.
2. Pull the postcodes of your last 50 loyalty customers and mark them against the ZIP table above.
3. Build one landing page with one offer and a visible address, and a different redemption phrase for the resident and visitor ad sets.
4. Launch two ad sets, residents and visitors, each with a small budget, and log claims and redemptions weekly.
5. Set up a loyalty card or email capture at the first visit, so a one-time customer has a reason to return. See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) to make sure the search side is ready.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do Meta ads cost for a coffee shop in Virginia Beach?**
A: There is no verified Virginia Beach figure. LocaliQ's 2026 national benchmark for Restaurants & Food traffic campaigns is $0.45 per click, so $450 buys roughly 1,000 clicks. The cost per customer depends on how many clicks become redeemed offers.

**Q: Should I target tourists or locals?**
A: Test both as separate ad sets. In the illustrative math above, locals and newcomers who return pay back faster than visitors who stop once, because the visitor makes fewer repeat visits. The result depends on your assumptions, so replace them with yours.

**Q: Does the military community matter for a café?**
A: Census data show 23,321 Virginia Beach residents in the armed forces in 2024. A "new to the area" offer targeted to nearby ZIP codes is worth a test, but the data does not show how often people move, so measure it.

**Q: Which Meta objective should I use?**
A: Traffic or Leads sending people to an offer page is the usual start for a shop with no online orders, since Store Traffic is no longer available for new campaigns according to Flighted's explainer.

**Q: Is Meta better than Google Ads for a café?**
A: Google reaches people searching for coffee now; Meta reaches people nearby who might not be. See our [Google Ads for coffee shops guide](/blog/google-ads-for-coffee-shops-complete-2026-guide) and the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) to split the budget.
