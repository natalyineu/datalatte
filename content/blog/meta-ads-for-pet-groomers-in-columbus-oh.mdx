## Do Facebook and Instagram ads need separate campaigns?

No. Instagram ads run through the same system as Facebook ads. [AdWave explains](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business) that this is because Instagram is owned by Meta, and one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook, or both. The [Meta Ads Manager](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising) is the central place to run ads across both apps.

Placement settings still matter. [Turrboo's guide to paid social](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media) notes that Meta Ads Manager covers Facebook, Instagram, and Messenger. For a local service, start with Instagram and Facebook placements and review the results by placement after the first few weeks. [Bons and Frazer](https://bonsandfrazer.com/insights/facebook-ads-on-a-budget/) add that Meta's platform also runs on the Audience Network, which is a separate placement you can exclude if it does not bring bookings.

If you want more detail on how the two Meta apps compare for small businesses, read [Instagram ads vs Facebook ads](/blog/instagram-ads-vs-facebook-ads) before you split your budget.

## How much should a pet groomer spend on Meta Ads?

Published budgets vary by business type, so use them as reference points rather than targets. [AdWave's veterinary guide](https://adwave.com/resources/veterinary-advertising) says most veterinary practices can run effective Meta campaigns for $300 to $1,000 per month. [AdWave's channel selection page](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels) puts a standalone Meta buy at often $300–$1,000 per month. A brand-building plan for restaurants is listed at $300–$1,500+ per month in [AdWave's restaurant guide](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-restaurants).

A grooming business is closer to the veterinary range than to a full-scale brand campaign. A test budget of $300–$600 per month lets you compare two or three ad sets without spreading the money too thin. Increase spend only after one ad set produces bookings at a cost you can accept.

## Which ad formats work best for groomers?

Video gets more interaction than still images. [AdWave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners) as saying short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram. For a groomer, that usually means short clips filmed in your salon or mobile van, showing a dog before and after the groom.

Keep the first three seconds clear. Show the dog and the result, then add one line of text with your city and the booking action. Still images can work as a second test, but treat them as a control rather than the main format.

If you want to see how Reels fit into a service-business plan, the [fitness studio Instagram Reels guide](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-instagram-reels) covers the same format logic for appointment-based businesses.

## Who should you target around Columbus?

[Barklead's groomer guide](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers) suggests targeting users within a 10–15 mile radius of the groomer's location and pet owners aged 25–60 who are likely to have disposable income. Those are the most specific targeting ranges in the sources, so start there and narrow only if the data shows a clear reason.

Set the radius around your actual Columbus address, not the whole metro area. A mobile groomer should draw the radius around its service zone. Once you have past clients, use them as a custom audience. [AdWave lists a typical Meta retargeting CPM of $5–15](https://adwave.com/resources/retargeting-ads-small-business), which is in the same range as cold traffic, so retargeting is an efficient use of spend for repeat-visit services.

For a Columbus-specific view of search demand, see [Google Ads for pet groomers in Columbus](/blog/google-ads-for-pet-groomers-in-columbus-oh). Meta and search often reach different people at different stages.

## What offer should the ad make?

A first-visit discount is the offer most often used in groomer campaigns. [Barklead's sample ad copy](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers) offers 10% off a customer's first grooming session, with a call to book today. Use a concrete offer with an end date, and make sure the booking page shows the same price.

Write the ad for the owner, not the dog. Name the breed or coat type in the body text, then state the service and the offer in one sentence. Test two offers, such as a discount and a free bath add-on, rather than changing the creative every week.

## How do you track whether ads bring bookings?

Set up tracking before you spend. Install the Meta Pixel on your booking confirmation page and your contact form, and set a Lead event for form submissions. Add UTM parameters to each ad link so your analytics tool can separate Meta visits from other traffic. Meta's own help pages explain how to verify the Pixel is firing, and those steps change over time, so check the live help center when you set up.

Compare cost per booking, not likes or reach. If a $600 budget produces five bookings in a month, that is a $120 cost per booking, and you can compare that figure with the assumed ticket value in the table above.

## Where can you learn Meta Ads for free?

Meta offers free training that you can finish before you launch. [A guide to digital marketing skills](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/) notes that Meta Blueprint covers Facebook and Instagram advertising at no cost and includes certification on completion. Spend a few hours on the basics of audiences, placements, and budgets before you increase spend.

Pair the training with a simple test plan. Run one campaign for four weeks, change one variable at a time, and write down the result. That habit matters more than any single benchmark in this article.

## FAQ

### How much does Meta Ads cost for a pet groomer?

Published benchmarks suggest a CPM of $5–15 and a cost per click of $1–3. Barklead recommends starting at $10–20 per day. A test budget of $300–600 per month is enough to compare two or three ad sets.

### Should a groomer run ads on Facebook or Instagram?

Both run from the same Meta Ads Manager, and one campaign can serve both. Start with Instagram and Facebook placements, then compare results by placement after several weeks. Exclude placements that do not bring bookings.

### Do short videos work better than photos for groomers?

AdWave cites Meta's 2025 report saying short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images. Test video first, and keep a photo ad as a control.

### Can I target past clients with Meta Ads?

Yes. Past clients can be used as a custom audience for retargeting. AdWave lists a typical retargeting CPM of $5–15, so repeat-visit services can use retargeting as a steady part of the plan.
