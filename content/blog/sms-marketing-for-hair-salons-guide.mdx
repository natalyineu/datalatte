A hair salon gets the most from SMS by using it for a few jobs: appointment reminders, filling a cancelled slot, rebooking nudges and handling replies. A UK salon-marketing consultant recommends reminders 24 to 48 hours before the visit, and says blanket texting to every client is expensive and often irritating.

This guide covers what to send and how to measure it. ## What Should a Hair Salon Text Clients?

Lockhart-Meyer, a UK salon-marketing consultancy, says SMS works best because it is instant and personal. Its recommended uses are reducing no-shows, flash offers to a small group of selected clients (occasionally), and reminders for promotional events.

That gives four jobs for your list:

- **Reminders** for booked visits.
- **Gap fills** when a slot opens at short notice.
- **Rebooking nudges** to clients who are due back.
- **Replies:** confirming, rescheduling and answering questions.

Anything else, such as general promotions to everyone, should go to email or social.

## When Should You Send an Appointment Reminder?

The same consultant says a reminder 24 to 48 hours before suits most salons, and prefers 48 hours. The reasoning is that a day before is late if the client has forgotten and needs to rearrange.

Another vendor, SalonIQ, also recommends two days before and suggests asking the client to reply "yes" to confirm. That is a vendor's advice from 2019, so test the timing against your own no-show log.

## How Do You Fill a Cancelled Slot?

Send a flash offer late, because urgency helps. Lockhart-Meyer says last-minute texts create scarcity and make a phone call more likely, and that on same-day cancellations you should text as soon as you know about the gap.

Send it to the few clients who fit the service and the time, not to the whole list. Name the service, the time, the way to book and the price or offer. Put a code in it so you can count results.

## How Do You Keep Texts Short and Personal?

Use the client's first name and get to the point. The consultant advises staying near 160 characters, avoiding text-speak and keeping spelling and grammar as clean as your other branding. Tell the client exactly what is on offer, how to take it up and what to do next.

Example for a gap fill (illustrative wording):

"Hi Jess, a colour slot has opened tomorrow at 2pm. Book on our link or reply YES. Code GAP1."

## How Do You Handle Replies and Opt-Outs?

One-way texts leave replies unread. Bella Booking, a salon software vendor, describes a shared two-way inbox where YES or CONFIRM can confirm an appointment and STOP ends all SMS to that client, with a separate opt-out for marketing only. That is one vendor's design, but the principle holds: someone must read replies, and STOP must always work.

Clean the list too. Lockhart-Meyer warns that texting old numbers or clients who have moved away wastes money, so check delivery reports and update your database.

## FAQ

### How many texts per month is too many?

No source gives a fixed number. The consultant warns that overuse is what gives SMS a bad name, so send only when you have a specific reason.

### Should I text all clients about a promotion?

Usually not. Target the clients who fit the service, and use email for general news. See our [SMS vs email guide](/blog/sms-vs-email-marketing-local-business-which-is-better).

### How do I measure results?

Put a unique code in every offer and count redemptions, plus no-show rates before and after reminders.

### Do I need consent to text clients?

Yes in most countries. Rules differ by country, so check our [US rules guide](/blog/sms-marketing-for-hair-salons) or your local regulator before sending.
