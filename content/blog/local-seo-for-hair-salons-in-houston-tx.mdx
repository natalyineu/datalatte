## What does a Google Business Profile for a Houston salon need?

Start with eligibility. Google says a business can create a Business Profile if it has a physical location customers can visit, or if it travels to customers where they are. [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) also say there should be only one profile per business, because duplicates can cause problems with how information displays on Google Maps and Search.

The guidelines ask that a business be represented as it is recognised in the real world, across signage, stationery and other branding. The address or service area should be accurate and precise, and the fewest number of categories that describe the core business should be chosen. For most salons that means one primary category such as "Hair salon". Secondary categories make sense only when each one describes a core service actually offered.

The service area setting depends on how the salon operates. For a salon where clients come to the chair, the physical address is the anchor. For mobile or at-home services, the service area should match the places the business actually travels to.

## How should a salon name and describe its profile?

The business name must match the name customers use in the real world. Google's guidelines state that details such as hours, phone numbers, promotional taglines and store codes are not permitted in the name, and that including unnecessary information could lead to suspension of the profile. The table below is a quick check before editing a listing.

| Element | Not acceptable | Acceptable |
|---|---|---|
| Name with hours | Bella Hair Co. Open Sundays | Bella Hair Co. |
| Name with tagline | Bella Hair Co. Houston's Best Balayage | Bella Hair Co. |
| Name with phone number | Bella Hair Co. 713-555-0100 | Bella Hair Co. |
| Name with legal term | Bella Hair Co. LLC (without signage proof) | Bella Hair Co. |
| Description with a link | Book at bellahair.com | Book through the website button |
| Description with a promotion | Everything 50% off this month | Balayage, cuts and colour correction in Houston |

The example names are illustrative. The rules come from [Google's guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), which say the description should focus on useful information about services, not prices, sales or links.

Write the description for a client deciding whether to book. Name the services offered, the neighbourhood served and what sets the salon apart, in plain language. Avoid keyword lists, since they read poorly and the guidelines require content that is relevant and useful.

## How long do local SEO changes take to show up?

Google's SEO Starter Guide says every change takes some time to be reflected on Google's end. Some take effect within hours, while others can take several months. The guide advises waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change had a beneficial effect in search results.

The guide also notes that not every change produces a noticeable result. If results are unsatisfactory and the business strategy allows it, a different change can be tested. A fixed checklist, applied consistently and reviewed monthly, usually works better than many edits made in one afternoon.

Keep a dated log of every edit to the name, categories, photos and description. When the profile moves, the log makes it possible to connect the movement to a specific action.

## Which citations and reviews should a salon manage first?

Citations are mentions of the business name, address and phone number on other websites. Consistency matters most. If the same salon appears with different addresses or phone formats across directories, search engines and customers receive conflicting information. Correct existing listings before adding new ones.

Reviews follow the same logic. Responses should be professional and factual. Google's guidelines say published content should highlight what makes the business unique and should not include private or confidential information, so a reply should never mention a client's personal details.

For listings that cannot be fully controlled, check the basics: that the name, address and hours match the website and the profile, and that any change is made at the source rather than on a single listing.

## How should the salon website support the profile?

The website should reinforce the profile rather than compete with it. Google's SEO Starter Guide says a site should be seen by Google the same way an average user sees it. If important components such as CSS or JavaScript are hidden, Google may not understand the pages, which can keep them out of results or limit how well they rank.

The [URL Inspection Tool in Search Console](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9012289) shows how Google sees a page. The guide also suggests using the site: operator in Google to confirm that pages are indexed. If pages show different information depending on the visitor's location, the guide notes that Google's crawler generally sees the US version, so that version should be checked.

Give each major service its own page with the service name and Houston location in the heading. Add a map to the contact page, as explained in [this guide to embedding a Google Map](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website). Google documents [LocalBusiness structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) for describing a business's name, address and hours. Use it only when those details match the profile exactly.

## What does a 90-day worked example return?

This example uses assumptions, not sourced data. Each input is labelled so the owner can replace it with real numbers.

- Assumption: 2 hours per week on profile upkeep, photos, posts and review replies.
- Assumption: 13 weeks in the 90-day window, so 26 hours in total.
- Assumption: average client ticket of $90 for a cut and colour appointment.
- Assumption: 8 extra bookings per month attributed to the map listing after the first 90 days.

The monthly booking value is 8 bookings multiplied by $90, which equals $720. Over twelve months at that rate, the figure would be $8,640. The time cost is 26 hours across the quarter, or about 8.7 hours a month. Dividing $720 by 8.7 hours gives roughly $83 of booking value per hour of profile work.

Treat this as a planning tool, not a forecast. The real figure depends on how many of those bookings would have come through other channels anyway. Record a baseline before starting so the 90-day result can be compared fairly.

## What should a salon measure each month?

Set up measurement before the first edit. [Google Search Console](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) tracks impressions and clicks from organic search by page. The Business Profile's own insights allow month-to-month comparison of views and actions. Check map position for the main services from a private browser window, and record results in the same format each time.

A monthly routine can be short. Review the profile against the checklist, check citations for inconsistencies, count new reviews and reply to them, and note any change in the metrics. ## FAQ

### Can a Houston salon have more than one Google Business Profile?

Google's guidelines say there should be only one profile per business. Multiple profiles for the same salon can cause problems with how information displays on Google Maps and Search, so duplicates should be consolidated rather than added to.

### Can hours or a phone number go in the salon name?

No. Google's guidelines say hours, phone numbers and marketing taglines are not permitted in the name, and that unnecessary information could result in suspension. Keep the name as customers know it, and place hours and phone numbers in their own fields.

### How long should a change be given before judging it?

Google's SEO Starter Guide advises waiting a few weeks before assessing whether a change had a beneficial effect. Some changes take effect within hours and others take several months, so a dated log of edits is useful.

### Should a salon add many categories?

Google's guidelines recommend choosing the fewest categories that describe the overall core business. Start with one accurate primary category and add secondary ones only when they clearly describe services actually offered.

### Is local SEO enough without paid ads?

Local SEO and paid ads serve different purposes and can run together. The [Google Ads guide for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide) explains how paid search fits alongside the profile.
