The best Google Ads keywords for hair salons combine a service with a place: "balayage [city]", "hair salon near me", "women's haircut [city]", "bridal hair [city]" and "curly hair specialist near me". These searchers are close to booking, so they usually convert better than broad terms like "hair" or "hairstyles". Pair them with phrase and exact match, a strong negative keyword list and tight location targeting.

Click costs and search volumes change by city and season. Check Google Keyword Planner and your own Search Terms report for real numbers in your area.

## Why keyword choice matters for salons

People searching "hairstyles for curly hair" are looking for inspiration. People searching "curly hair specialist near me" are looking for an appointment. Your budget should go to the second group first.

Your keyword strategy should include:
1. **Geo-targeted keywords** (service plus city or neighbourhood)
2. **Service-specific and long-tail phrases**
3. **A negative keyword list** that screens out jobs, DIY and supply searches

## High-intent keywords to start with

| Keyword idea | Why it works |
|---|---|
| "hair salon near me" | Broad local intent, high volume and high competition |
| "hair salon [city]" | Clear location, easy to target |
| "balayage [city]" | Specific high-value service |
| "women's haircut [city]" | Core service |
| "book hair appointment [city]" | Booking intent |
| "hair colourist [neighbourhood]" | Very local, often less competitive |
| "hair extensions [city]" | High-ticket service |
| "bridal hair [city]" | Event bookings, plan ahead |
| "curly hair specialist near me" | Niche that customers search by name |
| "affordable hair salon [city]" | Price intent, bid only if it fits your pricing |
| "same-day haircut near me" | Urgency, good for mobile |

Look up each one in Keyword Planner to see volume and the suggested bid range for your area, then start with the handful that match services you most want to sell.

## Long-tail keywords for local salons

Longer phrases have lower volume but clearer intent and are often cheaper. Ideas:

- "organic hair salon in [city]"
- "best hair colourist for balayage in [neighbourhood]"
- "Japanese straightening near me"
- "keratin treatment near me"
- "hair salon with wheelchair access [city]"
- "express men's haircut [city]"
- "hair salon open late [city]"

To find more, check Google's autocomplete and the "Searches related to" box at the bottom of results, and use your Search Terms report once ads are running. Voice-style queries such as "where can I get a keratin treatment today" are worth reading for ideas, though results vary.

## Location keywords and targeting

A simple formula is **service plus place plus qualifier**:

- "hair salon in [city] with parking"
- "women's haircuts near [landmark]"
- "hair salon [neighbourhood]"

Also set your campaign location targeting to the area your clients actually come from, often a radius of a few miles around the salon. Review the Locations report after a month and adjust. If you serve a neighbouring suburb, you can add it with a separate bid.

## Seasonal keywords

| New Year | "new year new hair", "hair colour refresh" |

Use Google Trends for your region to see when searches rise.

## Match types, negatives and bids

**Match types.** Start with phrase match for most keywords and exact match for the terms you most want to win. Hold off on broad match until you have conversion tracking and a thorough negative list.

**Negative keywords.** Start with these themes:
- DIY and learning: "diy", "at home", "how to", "tutorial", "what is"
- Jobs and training: "jobs", "career", "salary", "school", "course"
- Supplies: "wholesale", "supplies", "kit", "tools"
- Services you do not offer: "dog grooming", "nail salon", "barber" (if you do not serve men)
- Price-only searches such as "free" if they do not fit your offer

Check the Search Terms report every week or two and add new negatives. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) explains how.

**Bids.** A lot of salon searches happen on phones. Make sure ads show a call button, your page loads quickly on mobile, and consider a modest mobile bid adjustment once you have enough data to see which device converts better.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**1. Paying for brand clicks without checking value.** If your Google Business Profile and website already rank first for your name, a brand campaign may add little. But it can protect you if competitors bid on your name. Keep brand and non-brand campaigns separate and test. See [Google Ads brand campaigns](/blog/google-ads-brand-campaigns).

**2. Forgetting negatives.** DIY, job and supply searches can quietly drain budget.

**3. Using broad match without guardrails.** It can show your ads for unrelated searches. Start with phrase and exact match.

**4. Ignoring mobile.** People often search "hair salon near me" while out and about. Use call assets and a fast, simple booking page.

**5. Running ads when nobody can answer or book.** Align ad schedules with your opening hours, or make sure online booking is available around the clock and say so in the ad.

## How to structure your campaigns

- **Service campaigns:** separate cuts and styles, colour, treatments and bridal or events, so ad text and landing pages match the search
- **Local campaign:** "near me" and neighbourhood keywords with call and location assets
- **Optional brand campaign:** your salon name, kept separate

For each service, send traffic to a page about that service with prices or starting prices and a clear booking button, not just your homepage.

## Google Business Profile and local services

Your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) is often the first thing people see on the map. Choose the right primary category, add secondary categories that fit, upload recent photos, connect online booking if you can, and reply to reviews. Strong profiles help ads and organic results work together.

Google Local Services Ads are only available for certain business types and countries. Check Google's current list to see whether your category and location are eligible before planning around them.

## FAQ

**What are the best Google Ads keywords for hair salons?**
Specific service-plus-location phrases: "balayage [city]", "hair salon near me", "women's haircut [city]", "bridal hair [city]" and "hair colourist near me". Broad terms like "hair salon" attract many clicks but often convert less well unless your location targeting is tight.

**How much should a hair salon spend on Google Ads?**
It depends on your city, competition and the value of a new client. Many salons start with a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month. Use the formula in our budget guide and track cost per booking.

**What negative keywords should hair salons use?**
Start with free, DIY, how to, at home, school, course, jobs, career, wholesale and supplies, plus any service you do not offer. Add more from your Search Terms report.

**Which match type works best for a hair salon?**
Phrase match is a safe starting point, with exact match for your best terms once you have data. Avoid broad match until your negative list and conversion tracking are solid.

**How long does it take for Google Ads to work for a hair salon?**
You can see clicks within hours and enquiries within days, but allow several weeks of data before making big changes. Give the system time to learn from your conversions.
