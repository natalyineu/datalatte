The best Google Ads keywords for hair salons combine a service with a place: "balayage [city]", "hair salon near me", "women's haircut [city]", "bridal hair [city]" and "curly hair specialist near me". These searchers are closer to booking than people typing broad terms like "hair" or "hairstyles".

Pair them with phrase and exact match, a strong negative keyword list and tight location targeting. Google says exact match gives you the most steering over who sees your ad but reaches fewer searches, while broad match has the most reach and the least control ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529), read 3 Nov 2026).

Click costs and search volumes change by city and season, and Google does not publish one figure for salons. Check Keyword Planner and your own Search Terms report for numbers in your area.

## Why does keyword choice matter for a salon?

"Hairstyles for curly hair" is inspiration. "Curly hair specialist near me" is an appointment. Your budget goes to the second group first. That means:

1. **Geo-targeted keywords:** service plus city or neighbourhood.
2. **Service-specific and long-tail phrases.**
3. **A negative keyword list** that screens out jobs, DIY and supply searches.

A tight list will not fix a thin profile, but a loose one burns budget faster than anything else. The comparison below shows the profiles your ad competes against in seven US cities.

## Which long-tail keywords are worth testing?

Longer phrases have lower volume but clearer intent and are often cheaper. Test: "organic hair salon in [city]", "best hair colourist for balayage in [neighbourhood]", "Japanese straightening near me", "keratin treatment near me", "hair salon with wheelchair access [city]", "express men's haircut [city]" and "hair salon open late [city]".

To find more, use Google's autocomplete, the "Searches related to" box at the bottom of results, and your Search Terms report once ads run. Conversational queries such as "where can I get a keratin treatment today" are worth reading for ideas.

## How should you use location keywords and targeting?

A simple formula: **service plus place plus qualifier**, for example "hair salon in [city] with parking", "women's haircuts near [landmark]" or "hair salon [neighbourhood]". Set campaign location targeting to where your clients actually come from, often a radius of a few miles. Review the Locations report after a month. If you serve a neighbouring suburb, add it with a separate bid.

## What match types, negatives and bids work for salons?

**Match types.** Google says broad match can show your ad on searches that do not contain the direct meaning of your keywords, phrase match reaches more searches than exact and fewer than broad, and exact match gives the most steering ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529), read 3 Nov 2026). Start with phrase match for most keywords and exact match for the terms you most want to win. Hold off on broad until you have conversion tracking and a thorough negative list.

**Negative keywords.** Start with these themes:
- DIY and learning: "diy", "at home", "how to", "tutorial", "what is"
- Jobs and training: "jobs", "career", "salary", "school", "course"
- Supplies: "wholesale", "supplies", "kit", "tools"
- Services you do not offer: "dog grooming", "nail salon", "barber" (if you do not serve men)
- Price-only searches such as "free" if they do not fit your offer

Negative keywords let you exclude search terms from your campaigns, and Google notes they do not match close variants, so list singular and plural forms yourself ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972), read 3 Nov 2026). The search terms report shows the actual terms that triggered your ad and is a source of negative ideas ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7102466), read 3 Nov 2026). The [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) explains how.

**Bids.** A lot of salon searches happen on phones. Show a call button, make the page load fast, and consider a modest mobile bid adjustment once you have enough data to see which device converts better.

## Which keyword mistakes waste salon budget?

1. **Paying for brand clicks without checking value.** If your profile and site already rank first for your name, a brand campaign may add little. It can still protect you if competitors bid on your name. Keep brand and non-brand separate.
2. **No negatives.** DIY, job and supply searches quietly drain budget.
3. **Broad match without guardrails.** It shows ads for unrelated searches.
4. **Ignoring mobile.** Use call assets and a fast, simple booking page.
5. **Running ads when nobody can answer or book.** Match ad schedules to opening hours, or offer round-the-clock online booking and say so.

## How should you structure the campaigns?

- **Service campaigns:** separate cuts and styles, colour, treatments and bridal or events, so ad text and landing pages match the search.
- **Local campaign:** "near me" and neighbourhood keywords with call and location assets.
- **Optional brand campaign:** your salon name, kept separate.

Send each service to a page about that service with prices or starting prices and a clear booking button, not your homepage.

## Do Google Business Profile and Local Services Ads matter here?

Your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) is often the first thing people see on the map. Choose the right primary category, add fitting secondary ones, upload recent photos, connect online booking if you can, and reply to reviews.

Google's Local Services help says the ads are available only for certain service categories and lists "Hair Salon" among them, but it also says the Google Verified badge is unavailable for beauty. Eligibility depends on your area, so check [Google's Local Services help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841) before planning around them (read 3 Nov 2026).

## FAQ

### What are the best Google Ads keywords for hair salons?

Specific service-plus-location phrases: "balayage [city]", "hair salon near me", "women's haircut [city]", "bridal hair [city]" and "hair colourist near me". Broad terms like "hair salon" attract many clicks but often convert less well unless your location targeting is tight.

### How much should a hair salon spend on Google Ads?

It depends on your city, competition and the value of a new client. Use the formula in the budget guide and track cost per booking.

### What negative keywords should hair salons use?

Start with free, DIY, how to, at home, school, course, jobs, career, wholesale and supplies, plus any service you do not offer. Add more from your Search Terms report.

### Which match type works best for a hair salon?

Phrase match is a safe starting point, with exact match for your best terms once you have data. Hold off on broad match until your negative list and conversion tracking are solid.

### How long does it take for Google Ads to work for a hair salon?

You can see clicks within hours and enquiries within days, but allow several weeks of data before making big changes. Give the system time to learn from your conversions.
