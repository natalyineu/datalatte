The best Google Ads keywords for hair salons combine a service with a place: "balayage [city]", "hair salon near me", "women's haircut [city]", "bridal hair [city]" and "curly hair specialist near me". These searchers are closer to booking than people typing broad terms like "hair" or "hairstyles". Pair them with phrase and exact match, a strong negative keyword list and tight location targeting. Google describes exact match as the option that gives you the most control over who sees your ad, with phrase match reaching more searches and broad match the most ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529), checked Oct 2026).

Picture the search results. Someone types "balayage Austin" on her phone. Your ad sits next to salons that look like this: in the 50 Austin hair salons we pulled from Google Maps in Oct 2026, the median had 359 reviews and 32 of them had more than 200. That is the bar. A tight keyword list will not rescue a thin profile, but a loose one will burn your budget faster than anything else.

Click costs and search volumes change by city and season. Check Google Keyword Planner and your own Search Terms report for real numbers in your area.

## Why keyword choice matters

"Hairstyles for curly hair" is inspiration. "Curly hair specialist near me" is an appointment. Your budget goes to the second group first. That means:

1. **Geo-targeted keywords:** service plus city or neighbourhood.
2. **Service-specific and long-tail phrases.**
3. **A negative keyword list** that screens out jobs, DIY and supply searches.

## The math: specific beats broad

**Example only: every figure is an assumption, not a market statistic or a client result. Swap in your own.** Budget $630 a month at an assumed $3.50 per click gives 180 clicks. Assume an average new-client visit of $120 for both. The only difference is the keyword type.

| | "balayage [city]" | "hairstyles" |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks ($630 / $3.50) | 180 | 180 |
| Share who book (assumed) | 5% | 1% |
| Bookings | 9 | 1.8 |
| Cost per booking | $630 / 9 = $70 | $630 / 1.8 = $350 |
| Revenue at $120 | $1,080 | $216 |

Same budget, five times the bookings. The 5% and 1% are guesses chosen to show the effect of intent, not benchmarks. Your own data from 30 days of clicks will tell you the real split.

## Long-tail keywords

Longer phrases have lower volume but clearer intent and are often cheaper. Test: "organic hair salon in [city]", "best hair colourist for balayage in [neighbourhood]", "Japanese straightening near me", "keratin treatment near me", "hair salon with wheelchair access [city]", "express men's haircut [city]" and "hair salon open late [city]".

To find more, use Google's autocomplete, the "Searches related to" box at the bottom of results, and your Search Terms report once ads run. Conversational queries such as "where can I get a keratin treatment today" are worth reading for ideas.

## Location keywords and targeting

A simple formula: **service plus place plus qualifier**, for example "hair salon in [city] with parking", "women's haircuts near [landmark]" or "hair salon [neighbourhood]". Set campaign location targeting to where your clients actually come from, often a radius of a few miles. Review the Locations report after a month. If you serve a neighbouring suburb, add it with a separate bid.

## Match types, negatives and bids

**Match types.** Google says broad match can show your ad on searches that do not contain the direct meaning of your keyword ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529), checked Oct 2026). Start with phrase match for most keywords and exact match for the terms you most want to win. Hold off on broad until you have conversion tracking and a thorough negative list.

**Negative keywords.** Start with these themes:
- DIY and learning: "diy", "at home", "how to", "tutorial", "what is"
- Jobs and training: "jobs", "career", "salary", "school", "course"
- Supplies: "wholesale", "supplies", "kit", "tools"
- Services you do not offer: "dog grooming", "nail salon", "barber" (if you do not serve men)
- Price-only searches such as "free" if they do not fit your offer

Negative keywords stop your ads showing on the searches you exclude ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972), checked Oct 2026), and the search terms report is a good source of negative ideas ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7102466), checked Oct 2026). Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) explains how.

**Bids.** A lot of salon searches happen on phones. Show a call button, make the page load fast, and consider a modest mobile bid adjustment once you have enough data to see which device converts better.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Paying for brand clicks without checking value.** If your profile and site already rank first for your name, a brand campaign may add little. It can still protect you if competitors bid on your name. Keep brand and non-brand separate. See [Google Ads brand campaigns](/blog/google-ads-brand-campaigns).
2. **No negatives.** DIY, job and supply searches quietly drain budget.
3. **Broad match without guardrails.** It shows ads for unrelated searches.
4. **Ignoring mobile.** Use call assets and a fast, simple booking page.
5. **Running ads when nobody can answer or book.** Match ad schedules to opening hours, or offer round-the-clock online booking and say so.

More in [Google Ads mistakes hair salons make](/blog/google-ads-mistakes-hair-salons-make-and-how-to-fix-them).

## Campaign structure

- **Service campaigns:** separate cuts and styles, colour, treatments and bridal or events, so ad text and landing pages match the search.
- **Local campaign:** "near me" and neighbourhood keywords with call and location assets.
- **Optional brand campaign:** your salon name, kept separate.

Send each service to a page about that service with prices or starting prices and a clear booking button, not your homepage.

## Google Business Profile and Local Services Ads

Your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) is often the first thing people see on the map. Choose the right primary category, add fitting secondary ones, upload recent photos, connect online booking if you can, and reply to reviews.

Google Local Services Ads are only available in certain countries and categories. Hair salon appears as a beauty service there, but beauty eligibility has been limited by location and requires licences and insurance, so check [Google's Local Services help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841) for your area before planning around them (checked Oct 2026).

## FAQ

**Q: What are the best Google Ads keywords for hair salons?**
A: Specific service-plus-location phrases: "balayage [city]", "hair salon near me", "women's haircut [city]", "bridal hair [city]" and "hair colourist near me". Broad terms like "hair salon" attract many clicks but often convert less well unless your location targeting is tight.

**Q: How much should a hair salon spend on Google Ads?**
A: It depends on your city, competition and the value of a new client. Use the formula in our budget guide and track cost per booking.

**Q: What negative keywords should hair salons use?**
A: Start with free, DIY, how to, at home, school, course, jobs, career, wholesale and supplies, plus any service you do not offer. Add more from your Search Terms report.

**Q: Which match type works best for a hair salon?**
A: Phrase match is a safe starting point, with exact match for your best terms once you have data. Hold off on broad match until your negative list and conversion tracking are solid.

**Q: How long does it take for Google Ads to work for a hair salon?**
A: You can see clicks within hours and enquiries within days, but allow several weeks of data before making big changes. Give the system time to learn from your conversions.
