## What Can AI Write for a Salon's Email List?

AI tools are strong at wording and weak at facts about your business. Useful jobs include drafting a monthly newsletter from a list of services and openings you supply, producing several subject line options for one email, turning a rebooking rule into a message, and rewriting copy in a warmer or shorter tone.

Do not let the tool invent offers, prices, stylist names or availability. Paste the real facts in and instruct it to use only those. The AI's job is wording the messages, while you decide who receives them and what they offer. ## Which Rebooking Emails Are Worth Automating?

Booksy's US blog recommends automated SMS or email rebooking reminders for clients who have not rebooked in the salon, sent 48 hours later to catch them. Its UK ideas page goes further: if a regular client has not booked within 12 weeks, the system can send a friendly "come back and see us" incentive. These are vendor recommendations, so test the timing on your own clients before relying on it.

BonusQR, another vendor, recommends an automated feedback email within 24 hours of a service, with a link to rebook. The article gives no source for that timing, so treat it as a starting point rather than proof. ## Why Should Loyal Clients Get Their Own Segment?

Booksy's data, cited in its US blog, says engaged loyalty members visit salons 2.4 times more often. That is a vendor claim about its own platform, so use it to justify a loyalty segment rather than to forecast your revenue. A segment is simply a group of clients with a shared pattern, such as recent visitors or those who have been loyal for a long time, and it lets the AI draft a different message for each group.

Bookeo's guide adds that consumers aged 55 and older account for 30% of the market share and spend the most on hair care services. Your own booking records will show whether that holds locally. Check those records before you segment by age, and only use fields your clients have consented to share. ## What Must Every Salon Email Include Before It Sends?

Legal duties apply even when a model wrote the text. Check that the From name and address identify your business, that the subject line matches the content, that the message is clearly marked as a promotion, that it includes a valid postal address, and that it explains how to opt out. Honor opt-outs promptly, and confirm that the unsubscribe link works before each campaign.

Treat the legal responsibility as yours, even when a tool or agency sends the email for you. Read the regulator's current guidance on commercial email for your country, because consent and marketing rules differ between the US, UK, Canada and Australia. The UK consent rules are covered in the [UK email and SMS guide for salons](/blog/email-sms-marketing-uk-salons).

## What Would a 200-Client Rebooking Campaign Bring In?

All inputs below are assumptions, not benchmarks:

- Consented clients on the list: 200 (assumption)
- Campaigns per month: 2 (assumption)
- Share of recipients who book after a campaign: 3% (assumption)
- Average service price: $70 (assumption)
- Tool cost: $0 on a free plan (assumption)

Each campaign produces 200 × 3% = 6 bookings. Two campaigns make 12 bookings a month, and 12 × $70 = $840 a month before costs. At a lower 1% response rate, each campaign brings 2 bookings, so 4 a month, or $280. The 3% rate is invented for illustration and your real rate may be lower. Some clients would have booked anyway, so measure with a booking code or the source field in your booking system. Treat the $38 return from Booksy as a vendor claim, not a target for your salon.

## How Do You Run an AI-Assisted Email Routine?

1. Collect email addresses with consent at booking, with a line saying you will send offers and news.
2. Split the list into segments, such as recent visitors and clients who have not booked for 12 weeks.
3. Give the AI your real offers, dates and prices, and ask for one draft per segment.
4. Edit each draft for names, prices, dates and spelling.
5. Check the legal items, add your postal address and opt-out link, send yourself a test, then send.
6. Remove opt-outs straight away and log which bookings each campaign brings in.

Keep a short log so each new campaign can improve on the last. For software options that combine drafting with sending, see the [AI-powered email marketing software guide](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-software).

## FAQ

### Is AI email marketing allowed for salons?

Yes, if each email meets the commercial email rules in your country, including honest headers, a postal address and a working opt-out. The tool that wrote the text does not change those duties.

### Can AI send emails on its own?

Some tools can. This guide recommends human approval of every send, because a wrong price or offer sent to the whole list is hard to undo.

### How often should a salon email its clients?

The sources reviewed do not set a number. Watch unsubscribes and replies, and adjust frequency based on your own results.

### Should I buy an email list?

No. Use only people who gave consent at booking or sign-up. A purchased list raises the risk of complaints and can break consent rules.
