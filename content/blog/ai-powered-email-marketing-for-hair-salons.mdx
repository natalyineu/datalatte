AI can write a hair salon's email drafts, subject lines and rebooking reminders in minutes, but you still approve every send. The legal duties do not change because a model wrote the text. Under the US CAN-SPAM Act each non-compliant email can carry penalties of up to $53,088, and Gmail asks all senders to keep their spam rate under 0.3%. So use AI for wording and keep control of lists, offers and unsubscribes.

## What Can AI Do for a Salon's Email Marketing?

AI tools are good at wording and weak at facts about your business. Useful jobs:

- Draft a monthly newsletter from a list of services and openings you provide.
- Write five subject line options for one email, so you can pick one.
- Turn a rebooking rule into a message, such as "six weeks since your colour".
- Rewrite your copy in a warmer or shorter tone.

Do not let it invent offers, prices, stylist names or availability. Paste those facts in and tell it to use only them. Our [AI automation for hair salons](/blog/ai-automation-for-hair-salons) page covers the booking side.

## What Does the Law Require in Every Email?

The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide lists eight requirements for commercial email. The ones salons miss most often:

| Requirement | In plain words |
| --- | --- |
| No false header information | The From name and address must identify you |
| No deceptive subject lines | The subject must match the content |
| Identify the message as an ad | Make clear it is a promotion |
| Tell recipients where you are | Include a valid postal address |
| Explain how to opt out | A clear way to stop all marketing email |
| Honor opt-outs | Within 10 business days; the opt-out must work for at least 30 days after sending |

Hiring a tool or agency does not transfer responsibility: the FTC says to monitor what others do on your behalf. Penalties are up to $53,088 for each separate violating email.

## Do You Need to Worry About Gmail and Inbox Rules?

Yes. Since February 1, 2024, Google asks all senders to set up SPF or DKIM, use TLS and keep the spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%. Best practice is below 0.10%. Senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail also need SPF, DKIM, DMARC and one-click unsubscribe.

A salon with a few hundred clients is far below the bulk threshold, but the 0.3% spam line still applies. The best protection is a list of people who asked to hear from you, such as those who joined at booking. ## Which Email Tool Fits a Small Salon?

Mailchimp's pricing page lists a Free plan with a limit of 250 contacts and a maximum of 500 emails a month or 250 a day. It says generative AI is not included on Free or Essentials and comes at no additional cost on Standard and Premium. The paid prices on that page are quoted for 10,000 or more contacts with a first-year discount, so they do not describe a small salon list and are not used here.

## How Do You Set Up a Simple AI-Assisted Routine?

1. Collect emails with consent at booking, with a line saying you will send offers and news.
2. Split the list by last visit date.
3. Ask the AI for one draft per segment, giving it your real offer and dates.
4. Edit the draft: check prices, names, dates, and spelling.
5. Add your postal address and an unsubscribe link, send a test to yourself, then send.
6. Remove anyone who opts out right away, well inside the 10-day limit.

Keep a note of what you sent and the bookings that followed, so next month's email can improve on it. For a chat-based option, see [AI chatbots for hair salons](/blog/ai-powered-chatbots-for-hair-salons).

## Worked Example: A 200-Client Rebooking Email

All inputs below are **assumptions**, not benchmarks.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| List size | 200 consented clients |
| Emails per month | 2 campaigns, so 400 sends |
| Plan | Mailchimp Free (limit 250 contacts, 500 emails a month) |
| Clients who book after an email | 3% per campaign |
| Average service price | $70 |

Two campaigns send 400 emails, within the 500 monthly limit, so the tool costs $0. At 3%, each campaign brings about 6 bookings, 12 a month, worth about $840 at $70. The 3% rate is invented for illustration. Your rate may be far lower, and some of those clients would have booked anyway. Measure with a booking code or the booking system's source field.

## FAQ

### Is AI email marketing legal for salons?

Yes, if each email follows CAN-SPAM: honest headers and subject lines, a postal address, a working opt-out honored within 10 business days. The tool that wrote the text does not matter.

### Can AI send emails by itself?

Some tools can, but this guide advises human approval of each send. A wrong price or offer to the whole list is hard to undo.

### How often should a salon email its clients?

No source reviewed sets a number. Watch unsubscribes and spam complaints, and keep the Gmail spam rate under 0.3%.

### Do I need a paid email plan?

Not to start. Mailchimp's Free plan allows 250 contacts and 500 emails a month. Larger lists need a paid plan.

### Should I buy an email list?

No. Consent-based lists protect your spam rate, and a bought list risks complaints. See our [AI agents for hair salons](/blog/ai-agents-for-hair-salons) guide for safer automation.
