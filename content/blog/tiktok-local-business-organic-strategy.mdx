A local business can grow on TikTok without ads by posting short videos that show real work for people in its area, then judging them by bookings and visits, not follower counts. TikTok says follower count is not a direct factor in what it recommends, so a new account can reach people. No source guarantees a follower number, and 10,000 followers is not a useful goal for a business whose customers live within a few miles.

This guide covers what drives reach on TikTok, a posting plan, and a worked example of the break-even maths. Figures are sourced at the end; examples are labelled assumptions.

## Who Is on TikTok?

Pew Research's 2026 survey of 5,511 US adults (fieldwork January 30 to June 18, 2026) found 31% use TikTok. Use is much higher among younger adults: 65% of those aged 18 to 29 and 45% of those aged 30 to 49. Compare YouTube at 84% and Instagram at 51% of all adults.

Earlier Pew reports gave a higher share of adults, so check the current fact sheet before quoting a figure. The practical point is the same: TikTok suits businesses whose customers are under 50, such as salons, cafés, studios and pet services. For businesses serving older customers, Facebook and Google may bring more.

## How Does TikTok Decide Who Sees a Video?

TikTok's newsroom explains the For You feed. It is ranked using user interactions (likes, shares, follows, comments), video information (captions, sounds, hashtags) and device and account settings, which carry lower weight. It states that "neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors," though a larger account may get more views because more people already follow it.

TikTok also says finishing a longer video is a stronger signal than weaker ones. That is a 2020 statement, so treat details as likely but not guaranteed to be current. The takeaway for a local business: each video is judged on how people respond to it, so a small account with a good video can be seen.

## What Should a Local Business Post?

Show the work and the place, with a clear local cue. Examples by business type:

| Business | Video idea |
| --- | --- |
| Hair salon | A colour transformation, with the service name and the town in the caption |
| Café | One drink made in a single take, with the price on screen |
| Pet groomer | A before-and-after with the dog's owner's permission |
| Fitness studio | A 20-second class clip or a coach explaining one move |

Rules that keep it simple: ask permission before filming customers, put the town or neighbourhood in the caption, and start with the most interesting second. ## How Often Should You Post?

No source we opened proves an ideal posting frequency, so pick a number you can keep for 12 weeks. Three videos a week is a common starting point, not a tested rule. Review the results every two weeks: which videos kept people watching, which got shares, and which led to a message or booking. Do more of those.

Consistency beats volume. If you can only manage one a week, do one a week. For a view on when to add paid support, see [TikTok organic vs paid](/blog/tiktok-organic-vs-paid-strategy) and [using TikTok for local businesses](/blog/using-tiktok-for-local-businesses).

## Is It Worth It? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions** for a hair salon. They are not benchmarks.

| Input | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Videos per week | 3 |
| Hours per video | 1 |
| Hours per month (4.33 weeks) | about 13 |
| Value of time | $20 per hour |
| Cost of time per month | about $260 |
| Margin per new client visit | $35 |
| New bookings needed to break even | 8 ($260 / $35 = 7.4, rounded up) |

On these inputs the salon needs about eight extra bookings a month from TikTok, roughly two a week, to cover the time. That may be realistic for a salon with strong before-and-after content and unrealistic for one that gets no local views. Ask every new client how they found you and count TikTok mentions for 12 weeks before deciding.

## FAQ

### Can I really get 10,000 followers without ads?

No source guarantees it. TikTok says follower count is not a direct recommendation factor, which helps new accounts, but results vary and followers anywhere in the world do not pay your rent.

### Do hashtags matter?

TikTok lists hashtags, captions and sounds as video information used for recommendations. No source proves a specific number of hashtags works best.

### Do I need many followers to get views?

TikTok says follower count is not a direct factor in recommendations, although a larger account may get more views because more people already follow it.

### How long until I see results?

No verified timeline exists. Judge over at least 12 weeks and by bookings, not views.

### Should I run ads too?

Only after an organic video performs. See our budget guide on TikTok organic vs paid.
