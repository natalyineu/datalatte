A/B testing means showing two versions of one thing to similar visitors and comparing results. Most local business websites do not get enough visits to test small changes. In the sample size calculator by Evan Miller, a page converting at 10.2% needs 2,545 visitors per version to reliably detect a rise to 13.2%. A site with a few hundred visits a month should test big changes, or test ads instead of pages.

This guide explains what to test, how much traffic you need, which tools are still available since Google Optimize closed, and how to test ads on Google and Meta.

## How Much Traffic Do You Need for an A/B Test?

More than most local sites have. The sample size depends on your current conversion rate and the smallest lift you want to detect. The calculator's own example shows 2,545 visitors per version for a 10.2% baseline and a 13.2% target. Two versions means about 5,090 visitors in total.

If your page gets 400 visits a month, that test would take over a year. The options: test a bigger change (a different offer or layout, not button color), test on a page with more traffic, or run the test on ads, where you control spend and impressions.

## What Should a Local Business Test First?

Test things that change the decision, not decoration. Ideas in rough order of impact:

- The offer: free estimate vs fixed price, trial class vs intro pass
- The main action: phone call, online booking or form
- The headline: service plus area vs a slogan
- Proof near the button: review count, years in business (only if true)

Change one thing at a time so you know what caused the change. Our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) and landing page examples that convert list candidates. ## Which Tools Can You Use Since Google Optimize Closed?

Google Optimize and Optimize 360 were discontinued on 30 September 2023, and running experiments ended that day. Google's help page points to third-party tools and says it is working on integrations with AB Tasty, Optimizely and VWO, and that its APIs are public so other providers can connect to Google Analytics.

Pricing for those tools was not checked for this article, so confirm it on each vendor's site. For a very small site, a manual test also works: run version A for four weeks, version B for the next four weeks, and compare. This is weaker, because seasons and promotions can change results, but it is better than guessing.

## How Do You Test Ads Instead?

Ad platforms split traffic for you. Meta's Business Help Center says an A/B test duplicates your ad budget when you copy an existing object, or divides your budget when you compare two. Meta recommends the same budget for both versions for a fair comparison. Its best-practices page recommends tests of at least 7 days, notes tests can run at most 30 days, and warns that shorter tests may be inconclusive. If a result has low confidence, Meta suggests running longer or with a higher budget.

Start with one variable: image, headline or audience. Our guide to [Meta ads for local business](/blog/do-facebook-ads-actually-work-for-local-businesses-data-backed-answer) shows what results to expect.

Worked example, all inputs are assumptions: a gym runs two ads for 14 days with $100 each. Ad A gets 20 trial sign-ups and ad B gets 26. The gap is six sign-ups, which could be chance. The gym keeps both running another 14 days. Only if B stays ahead does it move the budget.

## How Do You Read the Result?

Wait until the planned end date. Do not stop when one version leads early. Check that the lead holds across the whole period, including weekends. Record what you tested, the dates, the numbers and what you decided, so you do not repeat the test.

If the difference is small and the sample is small, treat the result as a hint. Run a second test with a bigger change. For tracking setup see our [Google Analytics 4 guide](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business).

## FAQ

### How long should an A/B test run?

For Meta ads, the best-practices page recommends at least 7 days and allows up to 30. For website tests, run long enough to reach the sample size from a calculator and cover full weeks.

### Can I A/B test with a small website?

Only large changes. With low traffic, small lifts are not detectable in a reasonable time. Test ads or collect more traffic first.

### Is Google Optimize still available?

No. It was discontinued on 30 September 2023.

### What is a good conversion rate?

This article does not give a benchmark because the sources checked here did not provide a local business figure. Compare against your own earlier months.

### Can I change two things at once?

You can, but then you cannot tell which change worked. For small sites, one big change is easier to read.
