Facebook Ads can produce real estate leads on a small budget if you choose a lead-focused objective, spend enough per day to read the results, keep targeting local, and measure cost per appointment instead of cost per click. A Facebook advertising vendor recommends starting Meta lead generation campaigns at $20–$50 per day. A UK agency puts the working floor for lead generation at £10–£12 a day per ad set. Below that range, many small accounts never gather enough leads to judge their ads.

## What daily budget should a real estate agent set for Facebook lead ads?

Start inside the range that practitioners use for lead generation. [LeadSync's Facebook Ads guide for contractors](https://leadsync.me/blog/facebook-ads-for-contractors/) advises starting a Meta lead generation campaign with $20–$50 per day. For UK campaigns, [Bons and Frazer](https://bonsandfrazer.com/insights/facebook-ads-on-a-budget/) recommends at least £10–£12 a day per ad set, so the platform has enough leads to show which ads work.

These figures are starting points, not guarantees. Set the budget per campaign and keep the number of ad sets small. Splitting a small budget across many ad sets leaves each one with too little data to compare creative or audiences.

## Which campaign objective should you choose in Ads Manager?

Ads Manager asks for a campaign objective before anything else. One agency guide lists the options as brand awareness, increase traffic, lead generation, conversions and sales, as of 2022 ([Impossible](https://www.impossible.sg/a-guide-to-facebook-advertising-in-singapore-how-to-get-started/), secondary). Check the current list on Meta's help pages, since platform menus change.

For listing enquiries and appointment bookings, lead generation is the natural fit. Brand awareness can help build familiarity, but it does not collect contact details, so you need a follow-up plan for anyone who engages with it.

## How tight should your targeting radius be?

A hyperlocal marketing guide suggests a slightly wider radius of 1–5 miles for services such as real estate or event venues ([Geotargetly](https://geotargetly.com/blog/what-is-hyperlocal-marketing), secondary). Use that as a starting range, then test a tighter and a wider radius against the same creative to see which one brings in enquiries at a sensible cost.

Avoid interest-only audiences such as "Real Estate" applied across a whole metro area. Combine location with homeowner or life-event signals where Facebook offers them, and check the options available in your country, since targeting menus differ between the US, UK, Australia and Canada.

<Callout type="tip">Test one variable at a time: radius, audience or creative. Changing all three at once makes it impossible to tell which change produced the result.</Callout>

## What do benchmark click and cost numbers mean for local lead ads?

[Adverge's 2026 Facebook Ads benchmarks](https://advergemedia.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2026) report local lead-generation click-through rates of 0.7–1.2%, cost per click of $1.00–$2.30 and CPM of $10–$27 across its managed accounts. These are averages for local lead campaigns, not for real estate alone, so use them as a sanity check rather than a target.

If your click-through rate sits well below 0.7%, look at creative relevance and audience overlap first. If your cost per click is far above $2.30, the audience is probably too broad. Search costs in Google run differently, so compare channels using the [best Google ad keywords guide for real estate agents](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-real-estate-agents-in-2026) before moving budget between them.

## What does a $900 monthly budget return? A worked example

The example below uses assumed inputs to show how the numbers connect. None of the inputs except the daily spend range is a sourced benchmark. Replace each one with your own figures from the last 90 days.

| Input | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Daily budget | $30 per day, about $900 per month | assumption (inside the $20–$50 vendor range) |
| Cost per lead | $25 | assumption |
| Lead-to-appointment rate | 20% | assumption |
| Appointments needed per closed deal | 1 in 8 | assumption |
| Average commission per closed deal | $6,000 | assumption |

Start with leads: $900 divided by $25 gives 36 leads. At a 20% appointment rate, that is 7.2 appointments, so cost per appointment is $900 divided by 7.2, or $125. At one closed deal per eight appointments, the month produces 0.9 deals, worth about $5,400 in commission. Return on ad spend is $5,400 divided by $900, or 6.0x.

One month of data is noisy. Re-run the calculation each quarter, and replace the cost per lead with your actual figure as soon as you have it.

## Which ad formats and creative should real estate agents test?

Start with real photos from your current listings, your neighbourhood or a short walk-through of a property you represent. Stock images of homes look like every other agent's ads, which makes them easy to scroll past. Test a single image, a carousel of two or three angles, and a short video, using the same audience and budget for each.

Video is often the easiest format to show a neighbourhood's character. The [video marketing guide for real estate](/blog/video-marketing-for-real-estate) covers scripting and filming for agents who have not used video before.

## How do you track leads and cost per appointment?

Install the Meta pixel on your website and set standard events for form submissions and bookings. If your calendar tool supports it, fire a scheduling event when a viewing is booked, so the platform can connect spend to outcomes. Check Meta's current help pages for the setup steps, because event names and options change.

Divide total spend by booked appointments to get cost per appointment. That number tells you more than cost per lead, because a cheap lead who never replies has no value to you. Speed of follow-up matters too: a fast, personal reply is usually the difference between a lead and a viewing.

Use a simple nurture sequence for leads who are not ready yet. The [email marketing guide for real estate](/blog/email-marketing-for-real-estate) explains how to send market updates to people who are not ready to move, and [AI email marketing tools](/blog/ai-email-marketing-tools) lists options for automating that follow-up.

## What checklist keeps your ad budget from being wasted?

1. Confirm the objective is lead generation and the pixel or lead form is firing before you spend the first dollar.
2. Set the daily budget at or above the range your region's guidance supports, so each ad set can gather data.
3. Limit each campaign to one or two ad sets and keep the audience definitions written down.
4. Check results every 24–48 hours during the first week, looking at cost per lead, click-through rate and frequency.
5. Pause any ad set that spends a set amount without a lead. The threshold is your choice; $50 is an assumption, not a benchmark.
6. Compare cost per appointment, not cost per lead, before deciding which audience gets more budget.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I use Google Ads instead of Facebook Ads?

Google Ads usually needs a larger commitment. [Focus Digital's 2025 analysis](https://focus-digital.co/minimum-google-ads-budget-by-industry-2025-guidelines/) puts the minimum viable monthly Google Ads budget for real estate at $3,800, because the market is competitive in dense regions. Facebook lead ads can start with a smaller daily budget, but people searching on Google often show stronger intent. Many agents run both, with Facebook building awareness and Google catching active searches.

### Can I target people who are likely to sell their home?

Facebook does not offer a "likely to sell" setting. Instead, check the homeowner, life-event and location options available in your country's Ads Manager, and layer them with your own website visitor data through the pixel. Build lookalike audiences from past seller clients only once your list is large enough for the platform to use.

### How long should I run a test before judging it?

Judge a test after it has spent enough to produce several leads, not after a set number of days. The UK budget guidance treats the £10–£12 daily floor as the amount needed to gather enough leads to judge an ad ([Bons and Frazer](https://bonsandfrazer.com/insights/facebook-ads-on-a-budget/)). If an ad set has spent that much without a lead, change the creative or audience before adding more money.

### Do I need a website to run real estate lead ads?

Not always. Lead forms can collect contact details inside Facebook, which removes a step for the person enquiring. You still need a reliable follow-up process, and you will lose website data for retargeting. Check the current lead ad options on Meta's help pages, because form features change.
