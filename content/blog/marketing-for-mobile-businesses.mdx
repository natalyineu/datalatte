A mobile business, one that travels to customers, should list itself on Google as a service-area business: hide the street address, name the cities or postal codes you cover, and target ads to those same places. Google allows up to 20 service areas, kept within about 2 hours of driving. Then spend ad money only where your route actually goes.

This guide covers vans, mobile groomers, detailers, repair trades and similar firms. The cost figure used is the closest category benchmark from WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 report, which is a US average.

## How Do You Set Up a Mobile Business on Google?

Google defines a service-area business as one that visits or delivers to customers but does not serve them at its business address. Its examples include plumbers and cleaning services. If that is you, remove your address from the profile and enter only your service area.

Two limits matter. You can have up to 20 service areas, and the overall area should stay within roughly 2 hours of driving from your base. You cannot define the area as a radius; use cities, postal codes or other area types.

If customers also visit you at a place with permanent signage, you are a hybrid business and may show the address. Age-restricted products and services, such as alcohol, cannabis or weapons, must have a storefront.

## How Should You Target Ads for a Mobile Business?

Match ad locations to your real route. Google Ads lets you target regions, cities or postal codes depending on the country, and add the country or state to avoid same-named places. Radius targeting needs at least 1 km. Very small radii can show ads intermittently.

Google recommends "Presence or interest" as the default for Search and says "Presence" fits when you only want people physically in the chosen places. You can also exclude locations. For dense areas, add postal codes you want to skip.

| Setting | What it does | Mobile business use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service areas on profile | Shows where you work | List up to 20 |
| Postal code or city targeting | Limits ad reach | Mirror your route |
| Location exclusions | Blocks places | Skip areas outside your drive limit |
| Ad schedule | Chooses days and hours | Show ads when you can answer |

## How Do Reviews and Calls Fit In?

Customers never see your premises, so reviews carry more weight. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read reviews and 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20. Ask every customer on the spot, while the van is still in the driveway.

Calls matter too. Google says clicks on a call asset cost the same as headline clicks. Answer or return calls quickly, and use an ad schedule so ads stop when you are off the road. See the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## FAQ

### Should a mobile business show its address on Google?
Not if customers never visit it. Google says to remove the address and list service areas.

### How many areas can I list?
Up to 20, within roughly 2 hours of driving.

### Can I set a radius around my base?
Not on the profile. You can use radius targeting in Google Ads, with a minimum of 1 km.

### Do mobile businesses need ads?
Not always. A complete profile and reviews can bring calls. Ads help fill gaps in the schedule.

### What does a mobile lead cost?
No mobile-specific figure is published in these sources. Animals and Pets averages $31.50 per lead; your trade may differ.
