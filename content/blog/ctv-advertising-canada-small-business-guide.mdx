A Canadian small business can run CTV (connected TV) advertising by buying streaming TV ads through a self-serve or managed programmatic platform, targeting viewers by postal code or radius, and starting with a modest test budget. Prices are quoted per thousand impressions (CPM) and vary by platform, province and season, so get current quotes before you commit.

Streaming has become a major way Canadians watch TV, and several services now offer ad-supported options. That means a neighbourhood coffee shop in Vancouver or a hair salon in Toronto can place video ads on the big screen in a way that used to be reserved for large brands. This guide explains how the market works and which approaches suit local businesses. Platform availability, ad tiers and pricing change often, so confirm details with your provider.

## The Canadian Streaming Landscape

Understanding where Canadians watch is the first step to spending wisely. Local businesses usually do not buy directly from each streaming service. Instead, they buy through a demand-side platform (DSP) or a managed service that has access to many apps and channels.

Types of inventory you may encounter include:

- **Broadcaster streaming apps:** Canadian broadcasters offer ad-supported streaming, and some are reachable through programmatic platforms. These often attract a distinctly Canadian audience.
- **Global streaming services with ad tiers:** Large services such as Amazon Prime Video, Netflix and Disney+ offer ad-supported plans in Canada. Access for small local buyers is often limited or goes through specific partners, so ask what is actually available.
- **FAST channels and free streaming:** Free ad-supported streaming TV services tend to be the most affordable way to test CTV, since CPMs are often lower than premium subscription services.

Ask any provider exactly which apps and channels your ads would run on, what geographic targeting is available and what the current CPMs are.

## Regional Considerations

Canada's regions differ, so local targeting matters.

**Ontario** is the largest market, and competition from large advertisers can push prices up. The upside is that you can often target specific postal-code areas in dense cities such as Toronto and Ottawa.

**Quebec** deserves special attention. French-language audiences are sometimes underserved by English-first advertisers, so French creative can be an opportunity, and it may face less competition. If your business serves Francophone customers, make sure your ads are written in natural, local French rather than machine-translated copy.

**British Columbia** has strong streaming adoption in metro areas such as Vancouver and Victoria, which can make it a good place to test.

**The Prairies and Atlantic Canada** often have lower competition for impressions and strong local business density, which can make modest budgets go further, though audience sizes are smaller.

## Targeting Options

Canadian CTV platforms and DSPs offer several levers beyond basic geography. Availability depends on the provider.

- **Postal-code or radius targeting:** The most important option for local businesses. Canadian postal-code prefixes cover small areas, which makes them useful for neighbourhood campaigns.
- **Language targeting:** English or French, which is critical in Quebec, New Brunswick and parts of Ontario.
- **Household income and interest segments:** Often available through programmatic partners, though accuracy varies.
- **Device targeting:** Smart TVs, streaming sticks and consoles. Make sure your video looks polished on a large screen.
- **Dayparting:** Many small businesses overlook scheduling. Evening and weekend viewing are typically heavier, so test restricting your ads to those windows and compare results.
- **CTV-to-digital retargeting:** Some platforms can match a household's TV exposure to follow-up display or social ads. Ask about it, and check privacy and consent requirements.

## Budget Guidance

CTV usually has a lower entry point than business owners expect, though minimums vary by provider. A sensible way to plan:

| Stage | Goal | Approach |
|-------|------|----------|
| Test | Learn what works | A modest monthly budget for a single local area, for at least four to six weeks |
| Established | Build consistent reach | A larger budget with frequency caps so households see your ad a few times per week |
| Multi-market | Expand | Separate creative and budgets for each city or region |

Avoid budgets so small that very few households see your ad more than once. Ask your provider how many impressions your budget buys and what frequency that implies.

## Common Mistakes in the Canadian Market

- **Using US-only creative.** References to zip codes, US pricing or American offers feel off to Canadian viewers. Version your creative for Canada, using Canadian spellings and local references.
- **Ignoring Quebec.** If you serve French-speaking customers, French-language creative may be worth testing.
- **Targeting too broadly.** A very large radius reaches people who will never visit. In dense cities, a few kilometres is often more realistic.
- **Ignoring seasonality.** January, back-to-school and pre-holiday periods are common peaks for local businesses, but check your own data.
- **Not measuring.** CTV is hard to measure directly, so set up promo codes, a dedicated landing page and call tracking before you launch.

## How to Measure Results

Clicks are naturally low on CTV, so combine several signals:

- Google Business Profile views, calls and direction requests
- Website sessions and branded search trends
- A unique promo code, phone number or landing page URL for the campaign
- "How did you hear about us?" on bookings and intake forms

## FAQ

**Q: How do I buy CTV ads in Canada without a large agency?**
A: Several programmatic platforms offer self-serve or managed options for smaller advertisers, and many Canadian digital agencies offer managed CTV buying. Compare minimum spends, targeting options and reporting before choosing.

**Q: Do I need French-language creative to run in Quebec?**
A: Rules and expectations can vary, so check requirements for your business type. For local Quebec businesses, French-language creative is strongly recommended because it connects better with Francophone audiences.

**Q: What video length works best for CTV in Canada?**
A: Fifteen-second ads are a common starting point because they are easy to watch fully and focus on one message. Use 30 seconds only if you need to explain a more complex offer.

**Q: Can I track foot traffic from CTV campaigns?**
A: Not directly. You can measure correlated lift through Google Business Profile views, calls and branded search. Some providers model store visits from device data, but treat those as estimates.

**Q: Is CTV better than Google Ads or Meta Ads for a local business?**
A: They do different jobs. Search ads capture people ready to buy now, while CTV builds awareness. Most local businesses should get [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and their local profile working first, then add CTV.
