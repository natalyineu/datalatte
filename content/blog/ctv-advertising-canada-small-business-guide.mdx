## How Big Is the Online Audience in Canada?

DataReportal's Digital 2026 report for Canada counts 40.2 million people in October 2025 and 38.2 million internet users at the end of 2025, which is 95.1% of the population. Social media user identities reached 33.0 million, or 82.1%.

YouTube's ad reach is also 33.0 million, which is 82.1% of the population and 86.3% of internet users. DataReportal says this is advertising reach, not monthly active users. The report has no TV or streaming figures, so it cannot tell you how many Canadians watch streaming TV.

## What Counts as CTV, and What Can a Small Business Actually Buy?

CTV means video ads on a TV screen delivered over the internet, not through a cable box schedule. For a small Canadian advertiser, the documented routes are these:

- **YouTube on the Google TV network.** Google says the network lets advertisers extend YouTube video campaigns to TV screens, with in-feed ads on the Google TV homescreen and Masthead inventory.
- **A broadcaster's self-serve tool.** Rogers Sports & Media runs RSM Ad Manager, built by DanAds.
- **Programmatic platforms and agencies.** Prices, minimums and inventory are set by the seller and are not published by the sources opened for this guide.

No published Canadian CTV rate card was found, so any CPM you read elsewhere should be treated as unverified until a seller quotes it to you.

## Is the Google TV Network Available in Canada?

Yes. Google Ads Help lists Canada among 28 countries: AT, AU, BE, BR, CA, CH, DE, DK, ES, FI, FR, GB, ID, IE, IN, IT, JP, KR, MX, NL, NO, NZ, PH, SE, TH, TW, US and VN.

Per the same page, in-feed ads on the Google TV homescreen run as part of Video Reach campaigns. They are eligible when an "Efficient reach" campaign is opted in to the In-feed format and the Google TV network. Audience segment and location targeting are supported. Creative must be 1920 x 1080 pixels at 16:9. Duplicated campaigns must be opted in again by hand.

## Can You Use Google's Reach Planner or YouTube TV in Canada?

Not for CTV planning. Google's Reach Planner help page says connected TV data in Reach Planner is available to all users in one country, the United States. It lists Canada for linear TV data, which is a different thing.

YouTube TV ads are also US-only. Google's help page says "Currently, YouTube TV is only available in the United States." A US guide that recommends YouTube TV or Reach Planner CTV forecasts does not apply to a business in Toronto or Calgary.

## What Does Rogers Self-Serve Offer?

On 16 November 2022 Rogers Sports & Media announced RSM Ad Manager, developed by DanAds. The release says advertisers can buy across its digital properties starting with budgets as low as $350. It does not name the currency. ExchangeWire reported USD 350, so treat the currency as unconfirmed.

The release names Sportsnet and SN NOW, Citytv, FX, TUBI, MLB.com, NHL.com and Véro. It speaks of "digital properties" and "streaming platforms" and does not describe ad formats or targeting in detail. The article is nearly four years old, so check that the minimum, the properties and the CTV option still apply before you plan around them.

## Which Advertising Rules Apply to a Streaming TV Ad?

Canada's main framework is self-regulatory. Ad Standards administers the Canadian Code of Advertising Standards, accepts consumer complaints and offers fee-based preclearance for six categories, including children's advertising, food and health products.

Two points matter for local advertisers, both from a Gowling WLG article of 27 August 2026 (a law firm, so a secondary source):

- The Broadcast Code for Advertising to Children covers ads aimed at children under 12, and those ads need a clearance number from Ad Standards before airing.
- Quebec's Consumer Protection Act prohibits commercial advertising directed at children under 13, with very limited exceptions.

The statute and CRTC rules were not opened for this guide. Check Quebec's law and any CRTC conditions directly if you advertise to families.

## Worked Example: Testing a Small Budget

The inputs here are **assumptions** for planning, not benchmarks. A bakery in Winnipeg sets aside CAD 1,000 for one test month.

| Step | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget | CAD 1,000 for 4 weeks |
| Goal | New customers who show a coupon code or say they saw the ad |
| Break-even customer value | CAD 60 gross margin over six months |
| Customers needed to break even | 1,000 / 60 = about 17 |
| Decision rule | Under 8 coded redemptions, do not scale |

Set the numbers from your own margin. Whether the seller can deliver 17 customers for that money is something only a quote and a test will show, since no public Canadian CTV cost data was found.

## What Should You Ask a CTV Seller Before You Pay?

Ask in writing. The answers decide whether the buy fits a small business:

1. What is the minimum spend and in which currency?
2. Which apps and devices will the ad run on, and can you see a placement report?
3. How is location set: postal code, radius, city or province?
4. Is the ad skippable, and how is a view counted?
5. How will you measure results: coupon codes, a dedicated phone number, a QR code or a lift in branded searches?

If a seller cannot answer the second question, the buy is not ready.

## FAQ

### Can a small business in Canada run CTV ads?

Yes. Google lists Canada for the Google TV network, and Rogers launched a self-serve platform with budgets from $350 in 2022. Confirm current minimums with the seller.

### How much does CTV advertising cost in Canada?

No public price data was found for this guide. Costs depend on the seller, the audience and the season. Get a written quote and a test plan rather than relying on a quoted CPM from another country.

### Is YouTube TV available in Canada?

No. Google's help page says YouTube TV is only available in the United States. YouTube ads on TV screens through the Google TV network are a separate product.

### Do I need clearance for a TV ad?

Only for children's advertising on broadcast, which needs an Ad Standards clearance number according to Gowling. Other ads should follow the Canadian Code. Quebec has a separate under-13 advertising ban.
