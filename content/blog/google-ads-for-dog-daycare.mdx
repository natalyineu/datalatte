To run Google Ads for a dog daycare, build a Search campaign around high-intent phrases like "dog daycare near me", limit it to the neighborhoods you realistically serve, send clicks to a page with clear pricing and a booking form, and track every call and form as a conversion. A test budget in the low hundreds of dollars a month is a reasonable start in many markets. Below is a step-by-step setup you can follow.

Regular clients are the foundation of a daycare, and Google Ads reaches dog owners at the moment they search for care, which word of mouth alone cannot do. Results vary by city, competition and how good your landing page is, so use the numbers in this guide as planning aids, not promises.

## Step 1: Keyword Research and Campaign Setup

Start with phrases that show a need for the service you actually sell:

- "dog daycare near me"
- "dog daycare [city or neighborhood]"
- "doggy daycare"
- "dog boarding [city]" (only if you offer boarding)
- "puppy daycare" or "dog day care for anxious dogs" if that is a niche you serve

Check estimated volume and cost per click in Google's Keyword Planner, which gives estimates for your area. Costs differ widely between a small town and a large metro, so use your own numbers.

Add negative keywords such as "jobs", "careers", "franchise", "training", "course", "free" and "cheap" if they do not match your offer. See our guide to [negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).

Create a Search campaign, set the target location to the areas around your facility (most owners will not drive far for daily drop-off), and choose a goal such as leads, calls or form submissions.

| Campaign setting | Suggested starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Campaign type | Search |
| Location | The neighborhoods or radius clients can realistically reach |
| Bidding | Manual CPC or Maximize Clicks with a cap at first, move to conversion-based bidding once you have data |
| Ad groups | Daycare, boarding, puppy daycare (one theme per group) |
| Extensions / assets | Call, location, sitelinks, price or promotion |

## Step 2: Ad Copy and Landing Pages

Good ad copy answers the questions a worried dog owner has: Is it close? Is it safe? What does it cost? Can I book easily?

- **Headline:** Lead with the service and place, for example "Dog Daycare in [Neighborhood]".
- **Description:** Mention what sets you apart, such as staff supervision, play-group sizes, webcams or evaluation process, only if they are true.
- **Call to action:** "Book a Trial Day" or "Request a Tour" works better than a vague "Learn More".

Your landing page should repeat the same promise and make the next step obvious:

- A matching headline and photos of your actual facility and dogs
- Prices or price ranges, hours and vaccination requirements
- Reviews and a short description of the temperament evaluation
- A short booking or inquiry form and a click-to-call button on mobile

If you do not yet have a website, a simple one-page site with a booking button is usually a better destination than a social profile. See our [website service](/services/website) for options.

## Step 3: Budget and Expectations

There is no universal right budget. Cost per click in pet services can be modest in some markets and high in others, so estimate before committing. A simple method:

1. Decide what a new regular client is worth over a few months.
2. Estimate the share of clicks that will turn into inquiries and the share of inquiries that become clients.
3. Work backward to the most you can pay per click and per client.

For example, if a client typically spends a few hundred dollars a month and stays for several months, you can afford a meaningful cost per new client. Your own pricing and retention matter more than any benchmark. Start with a test budget you are comfortable with, and expect the first weeks to be about learning, not profit. ## Step 4: Track and Optimize

Track calls from ads, call extensions, form submissions and booking clicks as conversions. Then review weekly:

- **Search terms:** add negatives for irrelevant searches and notice phrases worth adding as keywords.
- **Click-through rate:** if it is low, revise headlines to include the neighborhood or an offer.
- **Conversion rate:** if clicks are fine but inquiries are weak, improve the landing page.
- **Cost per booking:** compare against client lifetime value rather than clicking cost alone.
- **Day and time:** check whether weekday morning searches convert better than evenings or weekends, and adjust your schedule and bids accordingly.

## Common Challenges and Fixes

- **High cost per click:** narrow the keywords to specific, high-intent phrases and tighten the location, rather than bidding broadly.
- **Low click-through rate:** add the neighborhood or a clear offer to headlines.
- **Clicks but few bookings:** check price clarity, form length, mobile speed and whether your phone is answered during business hours.
- **Bookings but poor retention:** advertising cannot fix service quality, so look at the first-day experience and follow-up.

<Callout type="warning">Do not send ad traffic to a Google Business Profile or Instagram page only. A dedicated page with a booking path nearly always converts better.</Callout>

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a new dog daycare budget for Google Ads?**
A: Start with an amount you can sustain for at least a month or two, enough to collect real clicks and conversions. Costs are higher in expensive metros, so check Keyword Planner for your area before choosing.

**Q: Can I rely on Yelp and word of mouth instead?**
A: You can, and referrals are valuable. Google Ads adds a way to reach people actively searching who have never heard of you, and you can measure the cost per new client.

**Q: What if someone clicks my ad but does not book?**
A: That is normal. Many visitors compare several options. Improve the page with prices, photos, reviews and a simple form, and measure cost per booking rather than expecting every click to convert.

**Q: Should I run ads on weekends?**
A: Test it. Many drop-offs are on weekdays, but weekend searches can still be valuable depending on your area. Review performance by day and time after a few weeks.

**Q: Do I need a website to run Google Ads?**
A: Technically you can send traffic elsewhere, but a simple page with pricing and a booking form generally converts better and gives you something to measure.

**Q: How do I know if my ads are working?**
A: Track cost per new client and whether those clients return. If clicks are high and bookings low, look at the landing page and follow-up speed first.
