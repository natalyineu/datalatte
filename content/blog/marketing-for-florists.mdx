A florist's demand arrives in short peaks, so the timing of advertising matters as much as the amount. AdWave's seasonal guidance recommends starting the pre-season ramp 8-12 weeks before peak and running off-season advertising at 20-30% of peak levels. For independent florists, the same vendor reports that CTV campaigns can run $300-$1,500 a month with a 2-3x peak surge. This plan sets a timeline, a budget split and a reserve you can check against your own order data before you spend.

## How far ahead of a florist's peak should advertising start?

AdWave's seasonal guidance treats the pre-season ramp as the first of four phases, starting 8-12 weeks before peak ([AdWave, advertising for seasonal businesses](https://adwave.com/resources/advertising-seasonal-businesses)). Its TV calendar takes a shorter view for home services, advising campaigns to start 4-6 weeks before peak ([AdWave, seasonal TV advertising calendar](https://adwave.com/resources/seasonal-tv-advertising-calendar)). Those guides cover other industries, so treat them as a range. A florist with two peaks a year can start earlier and tighten the schedule in the final weeks.

Awareness and conversion are different jobs. AdWave's tourism guidance says to start awareness-building at least 90 days before peak and raise conversion messaging 30-60 days out, when buyers make final decisions ([AdWave, tourism marketing for small cities](https://adwave.com/resources/tourism-marketing-small-cities)). Universal Ads puts the familiarity window for holiday shoppers at about a month before they start checking out ([Universal Ads](https://www.universalads.com/blog/tis-the-season-to-stream-why-advertising-early-wins-holiday-shoppers)). For a florist, that suggests quiet-month visibility, then order-focused messages in the last month.

Retail gifting follows the same pattern. AdWave describes November to December as the peak e-commerce period ([AdWave, TV advertising for ecommerce](https://adwave.com/resources/tv-advertising-ecommerce-guide)), so a florist selling online should count back from late autumn.

## What does a florist's CTV budget look like?

AdWave says CTV platforms let independent florists run campaigns for $300-$1,500 monthly, with a 2-3x peak surge ([AdWave, florist advertising](https://adwave.com/resources/florist-advertising)). The surge figure is a vendor claim, not a measured result across shops, so test it on your own orders.

A simple way to plan is a base rate plus a peak multiplier. Assumption: you start at $300 a month. If the 2-3x surge applies to spend in peak weeks, that base becomes $600-$900. Confirm the platform's current minimums and reporting on its live help pages before you commit.

Florists in markets outside the core four can compare local set-ups; for example, the [New Zealand local marketing guide](/blog/local-marketing-new-zealand-small-business-guide) covers small-business conditions there.

## How much budget should stay active in the off-season?

AdWave recommends running off-season advertising at 20-30% of peak levels, and its planning table puts off-season spend at 20-30% of peak budget ([AdWave, advertising for seasonal businesses](https://adwave.com/resources/advertising-seasonal-businesses)). The reason is continuity: a shop that disappears for months has to rebuild its visibility before each peak.

AdWave also gives a hypothetical example in which a $100 slow-season TV campaign might deliver the same reach as a $150 campaign during peak ([AdWave, slow season marketing strategies](https://adwave.com/resources/slow-season-marketing-strategies)). The page states this is an illustration, not measured data, so use it as a reason to test off-season reach rather than as a forecast.

## How would a $6,000 annual budget split across the year?

The inputs below are assumptions, not results. The ratios come from AdWave's illustrative four-phase budget for a single-peak business, which allocates 35% to the pre-season ramp and 15% to the off-season ([AdWave, advertising for seasonal businesses](https://adwave.com/resources/advertising-seasonal-businesses)).

Assumptions: a single-location florist, a $6,000 annual ad budget, $20,000 in peak-month revenue, a $600 peak-month ad spend, a three-month ramp and six off-season months.

Pre-season ramp: 35% of $6,000 is $2,100, or about $700 a month over three months.

Off-season: 15% of $6,000 is $900, or $150 a month over six months. That sits inside 20-30% of the assumed $600 peak month, which is $120-$180.

Peak weeks and post-peak work: the remaining 50%, or $3,000. The vendor's table does not split this part, so divide it to match your own calendar.

Reserve: AdWave suggests moving 3-5% of peak-month revenue into a marketing reserve to pre-fund the ramp, saying this works for most businesses ([AdWave, advertising for seasonal businesses](https://adwave.com/resources/advertising-seasonal-businesses)). On $20,000, that is $600-$1,000 set aside from peak revenue, separate from the ad budget.

This arithmetic applies vendor ratios to hypothetical numbers. Replace the inputs with your own revenue and margin before you spend.

## What should a florist check before each peak?

Work through this list about three months before each peak.

1. Mark the peak weeks and count back 8-12 weeks to set the ramp start date.
2. Set the reserve at 3-5% of the previous peak month's revenue.
3. Check your Google Business Profile for accurate hours, categories and recent photos, and confirm current rules on Google's live help pages.
4. Publish pre-order and cut-off times on the page where people order.
5. Keep the off-season spend at 20-30% of your assumed peak level so the shop stays visible.
6. After the peak, record spend and orders by channel, and cut anything that did not cover its cost.

## FAQ

### Is CTV worth it for a small florist?

AdWave puts independent florist CTV campaigns at $300-$1,500 a month, with a reported 2-3x peak surge. Start at the low end, track orders that name the campaign, and scale only if the cost per order stays below your margin on a typical order.

### How early should a florist start advertising before a peak?

AdWave recommends starting the pre-season ramp 8-12 weeks before peak. Its TV guidance for home services uses 4-6 weeks, so a shorter lead time is a valid fallback if budget is tight.

### How much should I spend in the quiet months?

AdWave advises 20-30% of peak levels. Keeping a small, steady presence helps the shop stay visible before the next peak.

### Should I hold money back for the next peak?

AdWave suggests setting aside 3-5% of peak-month revenue in a reserve to pre-fund the ramp.

### Where do I check my Google Business Profile rules?

Use Google's live help pages for current guidance on profile setup and local ranking. Rules change, so confirm them before you rely on them.
