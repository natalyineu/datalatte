## Is Instagram Worth Advertising on for a Local Hair Salon?

Instagram is worth testing when your clients are local, visual and likely to book a service after seeing a result. The [Pew data](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) shows Instagram usage rising from 47% in 2023 to 51% in 2026, which means the platform's audience is growing rather than shrinking. That matters for a salon whose work is easy to show in photos and short videos.

Instagram is a weaker fit when your services are hard to picture or when your nearest clients are outside a realistic drive. A salon that offers only general cuts with no signature look will struggle to stand out in a feed. Start with the service that already brings your best repeat clients, then build an ad around it.

## Which Offer Should a Salon Test First?

Test a specific, time-limited offer before testing creative styles. "Haircuts" tells a viewer nothing, while "$20 trim on Wednesdays for your neighbourhood" gives a reason to book now. The offer is the variable most likely to change whether an ad produces bookings, so it should be tested first.

Keep the offer tied to a single booking action. Send the ad to one booking page or phone number, and make sure that page shows the same offer the ad promised. A mismatch between the ad and the landing page is one of the most common reasons paid traffic fails to convert, and it is a setup problem you can fix in an afternoon.

## How Should You Split a Budget for an A/B Test?

Meta's own guidance is to run structured tests rather than switching ads on and off by hand. Meta's [A/B testing help page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669) explains that each version is shown to a separate segment so nobody sees both, and it says to use the same budget for both versions so the comparison is fair. Manual on-and-off testing can produce overlapping audiences and unreliable results.

For a salon, this means changing one variable at a time, such as the offer, the image or the audience area, and giving both versions equal spend. Run the test long enough to gather a meaningful number of bookings before you decide. Judging a winner after two days of low volume usually reflects noise, not a real difference.

| Variable to test | Version A | Version B | What to compare |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offer | $20 student trim | Free gloss with a first cut | Cost per booking |
| Visual | Before and after photo | 15-second stylist video | Cost per booking |
| Audience | 5-mile radius | 10-mile radius | Bookings per dollar |
| Call to action | Book online | Call the salon | Completed appointments |

Change only one row at a time. If you change the offer and the photo together, you will not know which change caused the result.

## What Does a Worked Budget Look Like for a Salon?

The numbers below are assumptions for illustration, not benchmarks. Assume a $20 daily budget, split equally across two ad sets for a 30-day test, so $10 per day for each version. Assume an average service ticket of $60 and a cost per booking of $30 after the test settles.

Under those assumptions, total spend is $600 over 30 days ($20 × 30). At $30 per booking, that produces 20 bookings. At a $60 average ticket, the revenue from those bookings would be $1,200. The return is therefore about $600 of revenue per $600 of spend before product and labour costs, which is not profitable on its own. This is why the test should aim for a lower cost per booking, a higher average ticket, or repeat visits that add value after the first appointment.

Use your own real figures in place of these assumptions. If your cost per booking climbs above your average ticket, the ad is losing money on its first visit and needs a new offer or audience before more spend.

## Which Ad Formats Should Salons Start With?

Start with the format that shows the result most clearly. For many salons, that is a single photo of a finished style with the offer in the caption, or a short vertical video of a stylist at work. Use a carousel when you want to show several services, such as colour, cut and treatment, in one ad.

Keep the same person or style across two or three ads so repeat viewers recognise your salon. The format choice should follow the test, not a general rule. Choose the version that gets more bookings per dollar in your own account, and retire the one that does not.

## How Do You Know When to Stop an Ad?

Stop an ad when its cost per booking stays above what you can afford for several days in a row, not after a single bad afternoon. A clear rule is to compare cost per booking with your average ticket and set a ceiling before the test begins. Check the numbers at least weekly and write the ceiling down.

Watch for audience fatigue as well. If the same people see the same ad many times, results usually decline and cost rises. Refresh the image or offer before that happens. Any specific fatigue threshold you use should be your own assumption, tested against your results, rather than a rule taken from a general benchmark.

## FAQ

### How long should an Instagram ad test run for a salon?

Run the test long enough to collect a meaningful number of bookings for each version before comparing them. Very short tests tend to reflect chance. Meta's testing guidance focuses on equal budgets and clean audience splits rather than a fixed number of days, so judge the result by bookings and cost per booking, not by the calendar alone.

### Do I need a large budget to test Instagram ads?

No. The test works with a small daily budget as long as both versions get the same spend. The worked example above uses $20 a day as an assumption. A smaller budget means fewer bookings per week, so the test takes longer to reach a clear answer.

### Should I use a phone number or an online booking link?

Use whichever action your clients already take. Track which one produces completed appointments, not just clicks. If you use one booking path for one ad and a different path for another, make sure the test compares them on the same basis.

### Does Instagram advertising replace local search marketing?

No. Paid Instagram ads reach people while they scroll, while local search reaches people who are already looking for a salon. Many salons use both. The [hair salon local SEO guide](/blog/hair-salon-local-seo-guide) covers the search side, and the [Google Ads guide for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide) covers paid search.

### How do I know if my ad is attracting the right clients?

Compare the services booked with the services promoted. If the ad promotes a $20 trim but most bookings are for colour, the offer may be drawing the wrong audience or the landing page may be pushing a different service. Adjust the offer or audience so the ad and the booking match.
