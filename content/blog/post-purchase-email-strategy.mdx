A post-purchase email strategy for a local business can be three automatic emails: a thank-you with aftercare the day after the visit, a review request a few days later, and a rebooking or reorder nudge timed to your normal repeat cycle. Klaviyo's 2026 benchmarks show why automation is worth the setup: flows made up 5.3% of email sends but nearly 41% of email revenue. These are ecommerce numbers, so treat them as direction, not a promise.

## What is a post-purchase email sequence?

It is a short series of automatic emails triggered by a purchase or appointment, not by a date. Because it fires on a customer action, each email arrives while the experience is fresh.

Klaviyo's benchmark page compares triggered flows with one-off campaigns: flows had a 5.58% average click rate versus 1.69% for campaigns, and Klaviyo says flow revenue per recipient is nearly 18 times higher ([Klaviyo email benchmarks](https://www.klaviyo.com/marketing-resources/email-benchmarks-by-industry)). Klaviyo's page gives no figures for post-purchase flows alone, so these are all-flow averages from online stores.

## Which 3 emails should you send?

Keep it to three. Timing below is a starting recommendation, not a tested rule.

| Email | When | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Thank-you plus aftercare | Within 24 hours | Confirm the experience, give one useful tip |
| 2. Review request | 3 to 5 days later | Ask for honest feedback with a direct link |
| 3. Rebook or reorder | Around your normal repeat cycle | One clear next step |

For a salon, repeat cycle might be six weeks; for a café with a loyalty offer, a week or two. Use your own booking data to set it. If you also sell online, pair this with an [abandoned cart sequence](/blog/abandoned-cart-email-strategy).

## How do you ask for reviews without breaking Google's rules?

Ask every customer, not only happy ones. Google's policy lets merchants solicit reviews that reflect a genuine experience, but bans offering incentives such as payment, discounts or free goods, and bans selectively soliciting positive reviews ([Google Business Profile policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114)). It also says not to pressure customers to review while they are on the premises or ask for specific content.

So email 2 should go to the whole list, include a plain link, and offer no reward. Reviews matter to buyers: BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found 97% read online reviews, 41% always read them, and 74% look for reviews written in the last three months ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)). That recency figure is the reason to ask continually. Our [guide to getting Google reviews for a coffee shop](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop) has wording to start from.

## What does a worked example look like?

This is an assumption-based example, not a result. A pet groomer serves 120 clients a month and has an email address for 80 of them.

- Email 1 and 2 go to those 80 clients each month.
- Assumed: 10% leave a review, which is 8 new reviews a month. Replace with your own rate after two months.
- Email 3 goes out 5 weeks after the visit with a rebooking link.
- Assumed: 6 of 80 rebook because of the email, and each visit is worth $60 gross profit.

That is 6 x $60 = $360 a month against a free or low-cost email plan and about three hours to set up (assumed). If your numbers come in at half this, the sequence still pays; if they come in at near zero, test the subject line and send time before changing the offer.

## How do you set it up without a big tool?

Any email tool with automation works: pick one that can trigger from a purchase or booking. Connect it to your booking or point-of-sale system, or add the client to a list after each visit.

Checklist:

1. Collect an email at checkout or booking, with permission.
2. Write the three emails in plain text with your name on them.
3. Set the triggers and delays from the table above.
4. Review open and click rates after 60 days and edit one email at a time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a post-purchase sequence worth it with a small list?**
Automation costs nothing per send once built, so yes. Even 50 recipients a month will tell you which email gets clicks.

**Should I put a discount in the sequence?**
Not in the review email, because Google bans incentives for reviews. A discount in the rebook email is allowed, but test it against a version without one.

**How many emails is too many?**
There is no verified universal number. Start with three and watch unsubscribe and complaint rates in your email tool.

**Do I need a service like Klaviyo?**
No. Klaviyo's data is used here as a benchmark. Any tool that triggers emails from a purchase will do.
