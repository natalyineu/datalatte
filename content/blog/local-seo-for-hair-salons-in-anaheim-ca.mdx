Local SEO for a hair salon in Anaheim, CA mostly comes down to one listing: the Google Business Profile (GBP). The Google Maps local pack is the top three business listings in local search results ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today), secondary). Those three listings are pulled directly from GBP data ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business), secondary). A salon that wants that spot needs a complete profile, a steady stream of photos, posts and reviews, and business details that match everywhere they appear online.

## How does the Google Maps local pack decide which salons appear?

Google builds the local pack from business listings, so the first job is making sure your listing is claimed, free and accurate. Google describes its Business Profile as a free tool for managing your presence across Search and Maps ([Keepers Digital](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/), secondary; [Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/), primary).

Adwave's analysis states that the primary GBP category is the number one ranking factor for getting into the local pack, though it does not name the source of that analysis ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones), secondary). Adwave also suggests checking the categories used by the top three to five businesses in your local results for your service search.

Ranking reports also vary. Ahrefs cites a BrightLocal survey in which 36% of SEOs in 2021 named GBP as the most important ranking factor for the map pack ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), secondary). That is an opinion survey, not a measurement of ranking weight, so treat it as direction rather than a formula.

## Which Google Business Profile fields should a salon fill in first?

Start with the fields that are easiest to get wrong. Hours, phone number and address must be accurate, and Adwave recommends making them 100% correct ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Then work through the rest in this order:

1. Confirm the primary category matches what the top local results use for your service.
2. Add secondary categories only where they describe services you actually offer.
3. Write a business description of up to 750 characters that names your service and neighbourhood plainly ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).
4. List each core service, such as balayage, keratin treatment or men's cuts, as its own service item.
5. If you work from home or travel to clients, hide the street address and set a service area. The same guide says this creates a Service Area Business listing.
6. Turn on messaging if you can answer it quickly. Adwave notes that GBP messages can be managed from a desktop dashboard as well as the mobile app ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on), secondary).

Google also lets businesses seed Q&A content. Google launched the feature in Maps in 2017 ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a), secondary). Write your five most common client questions and answer them yourself.

## How many photos and posts does a salon profile need?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, and says a robust gallery gets more clicks and calls ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Useful photos for a salon include finished colour work, stylists at work, the reception area and the product display. Replace older images as your work changes.

Posts are the second content lever. Adwave advises at least one new Google Post a week and describes posts as free listings that appear in Search and Maps ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Google reviews each post after publishing, and a post shows publicly only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), primary).

Check posts after you submit them. A post that stays Pending is not doing any work for the profile.

## Which citation sites should a salon check?

Citations are listings of your name, address and phone number (NAP) on other websites. Adwave says the NAP must be exactly the same on your website, your GBP and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses), secondary). Salon-specific booking platforms, including Booksy, are also worth checking, since clients often find salons there.

The matching rule is strict. Adwave notes that the NAP on your website must match the GBP listing right down to punctuation, and whether you write "St." or "Street" ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary). Pick one format and use it everywhere.

Apple's maps matter too. Apple Business Connect reports Profile Views, defined as how many people saw your business card in Apple Maps ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), secondary). Adwave also reports that 88% of people use Google Maps and 12% use Apple Maps, but the page does not name a source, so use these figures only as rough context.

## How many reviews does a salon need to compete?

Adwave cites an analysis of 2 million GBP pages by a third party, Blogging Wizard, which found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business), secondary). It is a correlation in a large sample, so use 200 as a goal, not a guarantee.

Reviews also show next to your profile in Maps and Search, so they affect what a client sees before they click ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), primary).

Here is a worked example. All inputs are assumptions for illustration, not data about any specific salon.

- Current review count (assumption): 80
- New reviews per month from a follow-up message after each visit (assumption): 6
- Target count from the benchmark: 200

Months to reach 200 = (200 − 80) ÷ 6 = 20 months.

If the follow-up process brings in 12 reviews a month (assumption), the same gap closes in 10 months. The calculation shows why the follow-up process matters more than a single review push.

## Does local SEO work alongside Google Ads?

Local SEO and paid search cover different parts of the same search results. Local SEO builds the free listing, while paid search places ads above and beside it. A salon can use both, and the GBP data feeds directly into ads. Google's location asset data is sourced from Google Maps and GBP, and corrections there flow into linked Google Ads accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), primary).

For budget and keyword decisions on the paid side, read the [complete Google Ads guide for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide) and the [keyword list for hair salons in 2026](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026). If your reviews are weak, the [Denver review benchmarks](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026) give a useful comparison point for review counts in a similar market.

## How should a salon track local SEO progress?

Adwave lists six local search metrics it calls the useful ones: GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent), secondary). Record these monthly in a simple spreadsheet, so you can see whether changes to photos, posts and reviews are moving anything.

Check your map position with a private browser window for your top keywords, and note the date. Compare results against the same keywords at the same time of month, since local results change.

## FAQ

### How long does it take for local SEO changes to show?

Changes to a GBP can take time to appear, and results vary by market. Track GBP views and clicks monthly and compare against your own baseline rather than a fixed timeline. The Booksy guide makes no timing claim for GBP changes, so set your own review date.

### Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up on Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/), primary).

### Should a salon use Apple Maps as well?

Yes, where possible. Adwave advises keeping your NAP identical on Apple Maps and other directories, and Apple Business Connect provides Profile Views to track activity ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), secondary).

### What is the fastest first step?

Verify that your hours, phone number and address are correct on your GBP, then match them on every citation.
