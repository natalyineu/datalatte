To run Google Ads across several European markets, build one campaign per country and language pair, with ads and a landing page written in that market's language and a budget set for each campaign. Start with one market, measure your real cost per click and cost per lead there, then copy the structure to the next. Keeping each market's data separate stops one country's results from hiding another's. The figures below come from Google's help content and two secondary reports. ## How should you structure campaigns across markets?

One campaign per country and language pair is the simplest structure to read. For example, "Germany - German" and "France - French" each get their own budget, location targeting and landing page, so results are never blended. Multi-location PPC is generally described as strategy tailored to each locale rather than one setup for everything ([seoplus+](https://seoplus.com/paid-ads/the-ultimate-guide-to-multi-location-ppc-advertising-on-google-ads/), secondary).

Other splits are possible. Dall lists three common approaches, and the right one depends on the offer and the audience.

| Structure | Best when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| By country | Each country has its own budget, USP such as free shipping or a guarantee, or product range | Needs a budget and reporting set up for each country |
| By language | Several countries share one language and you want one campaign | Can miss some audiences in each country |
| By area within a country | You should only reach specific cities, regions or postal codes | More campaigns to manage |

Within each campaign, group ad groups by service so the ad text can match search terms closely. Dall notes that keyword phrasing varies by market, so the same offer may be searched for in different ways.

## Do ads and landing pages need to be in each language?

In most cases, yes. Lianlian Global advises that ads written in a buyer's native language convert better than English copy, and it recommends having a native speaker proofread anything produced by machine translation. Google Translate covers more than 130 languages, but it is not always accurate, so treat its output as a first draft ([Lianlian Global](https://www.lianlianglobal.com/post-us/5-tips-for-using-google-ads-to-expand-your-ecommerce-businesses-into-international-markets), secondary).

Google's Quality Score guidance explains why the landing page matters. Its landing page experience component measures how relevant and useful the page is to people who click the ad ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118)). A French ad that lands on an English page is a relevance gap. The same help page says Quality Score is a diagnostic tool and not an input in the ad auction, so use it to find problems rather than as a score to chase.

## What should be ready on each landing page?

Dall recommends three things before launch: translated pages, prices in the local currency, and a local domain extension, which it says builds user trust ([Dall](https://www.dall-int.com/google-ads-campaigns-on-foreign-markets/), secondary). Keyword research also needs to happen per market, because direct translation of search terms often misses how locals actually search.

Use this checklist before spending in a new market:

1. Check market fit with Google's free Market Finder tool, which scans a website and suggests markets based on data ([Lianlian Global](https://www.lianlianglobal.com/post-us/5-tips-for-using-google-ads-to-expand-your-ecommerce-businesses-into-international-markets), secondary).
2. Run Keyword Planner for the target country and write down the local search terms.
3. Translate and proofread the ads and landing page, with accents and punctuation checked.
4. Show prices in the local currency on the page.
5. Confirm that the landing page matches the ad's language and offer.

For a service business that needs help with account basics, the [Google Ads for service businesses](/blog/google-ads-for-service-businesses) guide covers the setup steps.

## Which bidding and measurement settings suit a new market?

Lianlian Global suggests using Smart Bidding, also called automated bidding, when entering a new market. Google's algorithm sets bids at auction time, which saves management time while the advertiser learns. Once the market is understood, bid control can become more hands-on. The same source advises checking time zones when setting ad schedules, and notes that language and location can be targeted together ([Lianlian Global](https://www.lianlianglobal.com/post-us/5-tips-for-using-google-ads-to-expand-your-ecommerce-businesses-into-international-markets), secondary).

Quality Score data can be thin in a new market. Google shows a dash when there are not enough exact searches over the last 90 days. Its guidance is to use Keyword Planner to find higher-volume terms and to broaden match types so keywords enter more auctions ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118)). For tracking conversions across markets, the [Google Analytics 4 guide for small business](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business) explains the measurement setup.

## What does a two-market test cost per lead?

All inputs below are **assumptions**. They are not benchmarks for any country.

| Input (assumption) | Market A | Market B |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly test budget | EUR 400 | EUR 400 |
| Cost per click | EUR 1.50 | EUR 3.00 |
| Clicks (budget / CPC) | about 267 | about 133 |
| Conversion rate | 4% | 4% |
| Leads (clicks x rate) | about 10.7 | about 5.3 |
| Cost per lead (budget / leads) | about EUR 37 | about EUR 75 |

With the same budget and conversion rate, the market with the higher click price returns half the leads. If a lead is assumed to be worth EUR 50 to the business, only Market A is profitable at these inputs. Market B would need a higher conversion rate or a lower click price to break even.

If a market falls under the digital tax that Dall describes (UK, Austria or Turkey), add 2 to 5% to its click cost as a sensitivity check before setting the budget ([Dall](https://www.dall-int.com/google-ads-campaigns-on-foreign-markets/), secondary). Replace every assumed value with the first month of real data. ## FAQ

### Can I run one campaign for all of Europe?

You can, but it blends markets, budgets and languages into one report. One campaign per country and language gives clearer results and separate budgets. Shared-language campaigns across countries are an option, though they can miss some audiences.

### Do I need to translate landing pages as well as ads?

Yes, where possible. Google rates how relevant and useful a landing page is to the people who click, so a page in the same language as the ad is the safer choice.

### Does Quality Score change what I pay?

Not directly. Google says Quality Score is not an input in the ad auction. It is a diagnostic that shows whether an ad, keyword or landing page needs work.

### Should I use Smart Bidding in a new market?

It can help while the market is being learned, since the algorithm sets bids at auction time. Check results against cost per lead, and move to manual control once the market is understood.
