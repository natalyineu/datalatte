To run Google Ads across several European markets, build one campaign per market, with ads and a landing page in that market's language, a daily budget you set per campaign, and a consent setup that passes EEA visitors' choices to Google. Start with one market, learn your cost per lead there, then copy the structure to the next.

## How Should You Structure a Multi-Market Campaign?

Use one campaign per country and language pair, for example "Germany - German" and "France - French". That gives each market its own budget, its own location targeting and its own landing page, so results are never blended. It also stops one market from spending another's budget.

Give campaigns a naming pattern such as market, language, service. Keep ad groups themed by service so your ad text can match search terms closely. Google's Quality Score guidance says that for weak ad relevance you should match ad text more directly to search terms and split ad groups by theme.

For the budget split across countries, see our [European local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide-europe).

## Do You Need Ads and Landing Pages in Each Language?

Yes, as a rule. Google's help says language settings let you show ads in the languages your customers speak. Secondary reporting (Techwyse, August 2026) says Google is removing the manual campaign-level language setting for Search and AI Max for Search from late September 2026, and will rely on the language of your ads and landing pages plus its own signals about the languages a user understands.

That makes the ad text and landing page the language signal. A French ad that lands on an English page is a mismatch Google's quality guidance warns against: its landing page experience rating looks at how relevant and useful the page is to the person who clicked. Quality Score is a diagnostic. Google states it is not an input in the auction.

No published study measures how much a language mismatch raises costs, so test it.

## What Consent Rules Apply to European Visitors?

Google's EU user consent policy applies to the European Economic Area. If you use Google tags on EEA traffic and want measurement or ad personalization, you must collect consent and pass the signals to Google. Google's consent mode added two parameters: `ad_user_data` and `ad_personalization`.

Google's help gives these cases:

- With a banner and consent mode and no personalized ads, no action is needed.
- With a Google-certified consent platform and personalized ads, the platform should update itself.
- With your own banner, implement consent mode v2.
- Without a banner or consent mode, set both up.

Blocking Google tags until a visitor clicks the banner can cause data loss. This is not legal advice. Our [GDPR website compliance guide](/blog/local-business-website-gdpr-compliance) covers the website side.

## How Do You Handle Currency Across Countries?

Google says an account's currency is permanently set at creation because it determines how you are billed. To bill in another currency you need a new account. So decide the billing currency before you launch.

Conversion values are reported in the account's billing currency. Google's help is less clear on conversion tracking when your customers pay in a different currency, so confirm your conversion actions show the right value in a test purchase. For stores, check that your shop plugin passes the order value and currency code.

If your business bills in euro, pounds and kroner, test one order in each currency before you spend.

## How Much Should You Test Per Market?

No source sets a standard test budget. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks (United States, all industries) show a $5.42 click, a 6.64% click-through rate and $66.69 per lead, which gives a scale, not a European forecast.

Earlier versions of this article advised $300 to $500 per market and a rule of 100 clicks and 5 conversions by day 60. Those numbers had no source and are now only an **assumption** you can adjust.

## FAQ

### Can I run one campaign for all of Europe?

You can, but you would blend markets, budgets and languages in one report. One campaign per country and language gives clearer results and separate budgets.

### Do I need to translate landing pages as well as ads?

Google rates how relevant and useful your landing page is to people who click, so a page in the ad's language is the safer choice.

### Which currency should I set up?

Choose it when you create the account. Google says it cannot be changed later.

### Do I need a cookie banner for Google Ads in Europe?

If you use Google tags on EEA traffic and want measurement or personalization, Google's policy requires you to collect consent and pass the signals. Ask a data protection adviser for your setup.

### Can a service business in one city advertise to European visitors?

You can target people in your service area or who show interest in it, but you cannot serve customers outside it. Check location options in Google's location targeting help before choosing "Presence or interest".
