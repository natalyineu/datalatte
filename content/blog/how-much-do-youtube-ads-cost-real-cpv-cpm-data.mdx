**How much do YouTube ads cost?** YouTube ads are priced by auction, so there is no fixed rate. Skippable in-stream ads are commonly charged per view (CPV), often somewhere in the range of a few cents to under a dollar, while impression-based ads are charged per 1,000 views (CPM), often in the low double digits. For a local business, a test budget of a few hundred dollars a month is enough to learn what your market costs.

Your real price depends on your location, audience, ad format and creative. The ranges in this guide are general guidance rather than guaranteed benchmarks, so use Google Ads' own forecasts for your area before you commit budget.

## YouTube Ads Pricing: CPM vs. CPV

YouTube ads are primarily priced using two models:

- **CPM (cost per thousand impressions)**: You pay for every 1,000 times your ad is shown.
- **CPV (cost per view)**: On skippable in-stream ads, you generally pay when someone watches around 30 seconds (or the full ad if it is shorter) or interacts with it, such as clicking. Formats and billing rules change, so confirm in Google Ads.

For small businesses promoting local services like haircuts, massages or personal training, view-based bidding can be a sensible starting point because you are not paying for people who skip immediately.

### Typical ranges to plan with

| Metric | Typical planning range | Notes |
|--------|------------------------|-------|
| CPV (skippable in-stream) | From a few cents to under a dollar | Local, niche audiences and weak creative push you higher |
| CPM | Often low-to-mid double digits | Competitive cities and narrow targeting cost more |
| Starting monthly test budget | A few hundred dollars | Enough to see early data, not enough to dominate a market |

These are broad ranges. Your numbers can be higher at the start, before you have data and tested creative.

## How CPM and CPV relate

CPM and CPV are two ways of measuring the same spend. A simple illustration with made-up round numbers: if you spend $500 and your ad is shown 25,000 times, your effective CPM is $20. If 1,000 of those impressions turn into paid views, your effective CPV is $0.50. Fewer people watching for the same money means a higher cost per view, which is why creative quality matters as much as budget.

### Four factors that affect YouTube ad cost

1. **Location and competition.** Big, competitive metro areas typically cost more than smaller markets.
2. **Ad length and format.** Skippable in-stream, bumper and discovery formats are priced differently, so match the format to your goal.
3. **Audience targeting.** Very narrow audiences can cost more per impression; broad ones can waste money on people who will never visit.
4. **Bid strategy.** Manual bidding gives more control, while automated strategies can lead to less predictable costs until you have conversion data.

## How to set up YouTube ads that can pay for themselves

### 1. Start with skippable in-stream ads

Because you generally pay when someone watches a meaningful part of the ad or interacts, this format tends to be efficient for small budgets. For a deeper look, see our explainer on [YouTube ad formats](/blog/youtube-ad-formats-explained-skippable-non-skippable-bumper-discovery).

### 2. Use targeting to reach likely customers

YouTube lets you target by:

- **Location**: Run ads only in your city or a radius around your business.
- **Interests and topics**: For example, people interested in hair care or fitness training.
- **Demographics**: Age and other attributes where available.

A local salon might start with a modest radius around the shop and an age range that matches its clients, then refine from results.

### 3. Create ads that lead to action

If your ad does not lead to a call, visit or booking, you are paying for attention only.

- **Show your team** and your actual work.
- **Show the service**: a short clip of a stylist at work beats generic stock footage and music.
- **Include a clear call to action**: "Book online now" or "Call us today."
- **Hook viewers early**, since viewers can skip after about five seconds.

For ideas on turning attention into repeat business, see our guide to [email marketing ideas for small businesses](/blog/email-marketing-ideas-for-small-businesses-20-campaigns-that-drive-revenue-2026).

### 4. Test, track and optimize

YouTube Ads is not a set-it-and-forget-it channel. Test two or three versions of your creative, give the campaign time to exit its learning phase, then judge by cost per booking, not just views. More tips are in [YouTube ads best practices for local businesses](/blog/youtube-ads-best-practices-10-tips-for-local-businesses).

## A simple budget example

The following is a hypothetical calculation to show the method; replace the inputs with your own numbers.

| Step | Example |
|------|---------|
| Monthly budget | $300 |
| Assumed CPV | $0.30 |
| Paid views | about 1,000 |

If your average client is worth more than the cost per booking, the channel can pay off. If not, change the creative, offer or targeting before adding spend.

## YouTube vs. other platforms

If you are deciding where to advertise, compare YouTube with Google Search, Meta Ads and TikTok.

| Platform | Cost level | Best for |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| YouTube | Moderate CPM, low CPV possible | Video storytelling, awareness |
| Google Search | Pay per click, varies a lot by keyword | Immediate bookings from search intent |
| Meta Ads | Moderate | Targeted ads, retargeting |
| TikTok | Moderate | Younger audiences, short-form video |

For services like salons or fitness studios, YouTube and Meta are both worth testing, but Search usually captures the most ready-to-book customers. ## FAQ

**Q: Can I run YouTube ads with a $200 budget?**
A: Yes, but expect limited reach. At $200 a month you will get a small number of paid views, which is enough to test a creative and offer, not to dominate a market. Track cost per lead and scale only if the numbers work.

**Q: Should I run YouTube ads or Google Search ads?**
A: They solve different problems. Search captures people already looking for your service, while YouTube builds awareness. Most local businesses start with [Google Ads search campaigns](/services/google-ads) and add YouTube once they have a baseline.

**Q: How long does it take for YouTube ads to work?**
A: You will see delivery data within a day or two, but plan on a few weeks for meaningful results while the campaign learns. Avoid heavy changes in the first week.

**Q: Can I target people near my shop?**
A: Yes, you can target by location or radius, though very small areas may not deliver enough impressions. Test different radii against your real customer travel distance.

**Q: Do I need a professional video?**
A: Not necessarily. A clear, well-lit phone video with good audio and an honest message often works for local services. Fix the creative before increasing spend.
