Running Google Ads for a coffee shop is different from running ads for most businesses. People don't plan their coffee stops weeks in advance — they search when they're nearby, tired, and want coffee now. This means your ads need to be fast, local, and specific.

The good news: Google Ads for coffee shops can be highly profitable with a modest budget. The bad news: most coffee shops set theirs up wrong and waste money on people who'll never walk through the door.

This guide gives you the exact setup that works.

---

## Should Your Coffee Shop Run Google Ads?

Before we get into setup, be honest about whether paid search makes sense for your situation:

**Google Ads make sense if:**
- You're in a competitive area with similar cafés nearby
- You're new and need foot traffic before organic rankings kick in
- You have a differentiator (specialty coffee, great wifi, outdoor seating) worth advertising
- You can track whether ad spend turns into revenue (ideally through bookings or online orders)

**Google Ads may not make sense if:**
- You're the only café in a 5-block radius (organic will get you found)
- Your average ticket is under $8 (harder to make numbers work)
- You don't have a website or a Google Business Profile set up yet

**Minimum viable budget for Google Ads:** $20–30/day. Below this, you won't get enough data to optimise. Most coffee shops see returns at $30–60/day.

## Campaign Structure for a Coffee Shop

**Campaign 1: Proximity Search (40% of budget)**
- Ad Groups: Near Me | Open Now | City Name
- Keywords: Tier 1 list
- Radius: 2–3 miles from your location (use location targeting in Settings)
- Bidding: Maximise Clicks to start, switch to Target CPA after 50+ conversions

**Campaign 2: Experience Keywords (30% of budget)**
- Ad Groups: WiFi/Work | Outdoor Seating | Dog Friendly | Specialty Coffee
- Keywords: Tier 2 based on your specific differentiators
- Only include the features you actually have

**Campaign 3: Competitor Conquesting (10% of budget)**
- Bid on your closest competitors' names
- Note: you can bid on competitor names but you cannot use them in ad copy
- Keep bids low — this is opportunistic, not core strategy

**Campaign 4: Branded (20% of budget)**
- Your café name + variations
- Very low CPC, protects you from competitors bidding on your name
- Always run this once you have any brand awareness

## Writing Coffee Shop Ads That Get Clicks

Google's text ads have limited space. Every character matters.

**What to include:**
- Your location or neighbourhood
- Your key differentiator
- A time signal when relevant ("Open 6am–8pm", "Walk-ins welcome")
- A call to action

**Ad examples that work:**

*Headline 1:* Specialty Coffee in [Neighbourhood]
*Headline 2:* Fresh Espresso, Great WiFi, Open 6am
*Headline 3:* 2 Min Walk from [Landmark]
*Description:* Independent coffee shop serving single-origin espresso and pour-overs. Dog-friendly patio. Find us on [Street Name].

*Headline 1:* Coffee Shop Near You — Open Now
*Headline 2:* Quiet Workspace + Strong Coffee
*Headline 3:* [City]'s Best Flat White

**What not to do:**
- Keyword stuffing in headlines ("Coffee Shop Coffee Shop Coffee Shop")
- Generic descriptions ("We serve great coffee in a welcoming environment")
- No location information

<Callout type="tip">
Use Google's "Ad Strength" indicator but don't optimise purely for it. An ad with "Poor" strength that includes your specific differentiator often outperforms a "Good" strength generic ad.
</Callout>

## Location Extensions: Non-Negotiable for Coffee Shops

Enable location extensions in every campaign. These show your address, distance from the searcher, and a click-to-call button directly in the ad.

For coffee shops, location extensions typically increase CTR by 10–20%. They're free to add and require connecting your Google Business Profile.

Also enable:
- **Call extensions** — direct phone number in the ad
- **Promotion extensions** — if you have a current offer (happy hour, loyalty card)
- **Structured snippets** — list your features (WiFi, Outdoor Seating, Specialty Coffee)

## What to Expect: 90-Day Google Ads Timeline

**Days 1–30: Learning phase**
- Google's algorithm is still learning which clicks convert
- Expect higher CPCs and lower performance than steady state
- Don't make major changes — let the campaigns gather data
- Check: impression share, CTR, search terms report (add negatives weekly)

**Days 31–60: Optimisation phase**
- You should have enough data to identify which keywords drive walk-ins
- Pause underperforming keywords (below average CTR, zero conversions)
- Increase bids on your top-performing terms
- Test 2–3 new ad variants

**Days 61–90: Scaling phase**
- If cost per customer visit is acceptable, increase budget 20–30%
- Add new ad groups based on what's working
- Consider adding a retargeting campaign (website visitors who didn't call)

## Tracking: The Part Most Coffee Shops Skip

Without tracking, you're flying blind. At minimum, set up:

**Google Ads conversion tracking for:**
- Phone calls from ads (enable "Calls from ads" — this is automatic)
- Website clicks to "Get Directions" button
- Clicks on your phone number on mobile

**What you cannot easily track:**
- Walk-ins who saw the ad but didn't click anything (use a simple "How did you hear about us?" question at the counter for the first few weeks)

If you have online ordering or booking, set up conversion tracking on the order completion page. This makes Google Ads optimization dramatically more effective.
