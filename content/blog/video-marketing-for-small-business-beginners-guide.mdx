A small business can start video marketing with a phone, natural light and one repeatable format, such as a 30-second tour of a service or product. Pick one or two platforms where your customers already are. Pew Research's 2026 survey finds 84% of US adults use YouTube, 70% Facebook, 51% Instagram and 31% TikTok.

This guide covers what to film, what the platforms and Google allow, and how to test results without spending money. Where a number is an example, it is labelled as an assumption.

## Which Platform Should You Start With?

Match the platform to your customers and to what you can post weekly. Pew's reach figures show where adults are, not where your local customers are, so check your own audience.

| Platform | US adult use (Pew 2026) | Format limit from the platform's help page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | 84% | Shorts can be up to 3 minutes; maximum resolution 1080p |
| Facebook | 70% | Not verified for this guide |
| Instagram | 51% | Reels limits not verified: Meta's help page returned an error |
| TikTok | 31% | Not verified for this guide |
| Google Business Profile | Not a social platform | Videos up to 30 seconds, up to 75 MB, 720p or higher |

If you are choosing one, start where you already have followers or reviews, and add your Business Profile, since a short video there is seen by people searching for you. Our [Business Profile photos and videos guide](/blog/google-my-business-photos-and-videos) shows what to upload.

## What Videos Should a Small Business Make?

Start with formats that answer questions customers already ask:

- A 20 to 30 second tour of the space or the service in action
- One question from a customer answered in one take
- A short before and after, with the customer's permission
- A staff introduction: who they are and what they do
- A seasonal offer or event reminder

Film vertical for phones. Keep the first two seconds clear: say who you are and what you are showing. Add captions, since many people watch without sound. ## What Equipment Do You Need?

A recent phone, a window for light and a way to hold the phone steady are enough. Clean the lens, face the light and film short clips you can cut together. Use the editing tool built into your phone or a free app. Check any app's export limits and watermark rules before you commit.

Music needs care. Business accounts on some platforms have a smaller licensed library than personal accounts, and the Meta help pages could not be loaded to confirm current terms. Use platform-provided business tracks or music you have the rights to.

## How Do You Plan 30 Days of Video?

Pick a rhythm you can keep. Quality of idea beats volume.

| Week | Post | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tour or intro video | Show who you are |
| 2 | Answer a common question | Build trust |
| 3 | Behind the scenes of a service | Show process |
| 4 | Offer or event reminder | Drive a visit or booking |

Put the same videos on the platform where you posted, on your website's relevant page and, if under 30 seconds, on your Business Profile. See our [video content strategy guide](/blog/video-content-strategy-small-business) for a longer plan.

## How Do You Know If It Is Working?

Pick one action per video: a booking, a call, a coupon redemption or a direction request. Views and likes show interest, not sales. Give each video a unique link or offer code. Paid promotion is optional; see our [Facebook video ads guide](/blog/facebook-video-ads-guide) if you want to boost a winner. No source verified for this guide gives a benchmark conversion rate for local video, so compare against your own baseline.

## Worked Example: Is a Weekly Video Worth Your Time?

All inputs are **assumptions**.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Time per video | 90 minutes including filming and posting |
| Videos per month | 4 |
| Monthly time | 6 hours |
| Value of your time | $30 an hour = $180 a month |
| New customers you can trace to video | 3 a month |
| Value per customer | $70 |
| Monthly return | $210 |

At those numbers video returns $210 on $180 of time. If it traces only 1 customer, it loses money in the short term, so judge it over three months and track which format brings enquiries.

## FAQ

### Do I have to be on camera?

No. Film your hands, product, space or team. Voiceover and captions work.

### How long should my videos be?

Platform limits are caps, not targets. Google allows 30 seconds on Business Profile; YouTube Shorts allow up to 3 minutes. Keep videos as short as the idea needs.

### How often should I post?

Choose a schedule you can keep for three months. No verified benchmark exists for the ideal number.

### Do I need to spend on ads?

Not to start. Organic posting costs only time. Test paid promotion once a format works.

### Which platform is best for a local business?

The one your customers use. Pew's figures give a starting point, not an answer.
