Groupon's US merchant pricing page says there are no upfront fees. You pay a commission only on each voucher that a customer redeems, and the rate is agreed before the campaign launches. The page gives illustrative splits on a $50 voucher: a 20% commission leaves you $40, 30% leaves $35 and 40% leaves $30. Whether that is profitable depends on what the service costs you to deliver.

This guide is based on Groupon's US merchant pricing page, opened on 24 October 2026. Groupon's UK merchant pages returned access errors (HTTP 403) to our fetcher, so no UK terms are used. Your contract, not this article, sets your actual rate.

## How Does Groupon Charge Merchants?

The page describes a single fee: a commission on each redeemed voucher. It lists no charge for listing, setup, impressions or clicks. It says there is no universal rate and that the commission depends on your industry, the discount, the deal structure and the campaign goals. It also calls the commonly quoted 50% figure a myth, so do not assume any number until you read your contract.

Your rate appears in the Groupon contract before anything goes live. That is the moment to model the deal, not after launch. The page does not give a standard rate card, so ask for the exact percentage in writing and for any rules that apply to repeat or extended campaigns.

## When Do You Actually Get Paid?

Groupon holds the customer's payment until the voucher is redeemed or refunded. Redemption triggers the payout. Vouchers that expire unredeemed are refunded to the customer by Groupon, and the page says you are not billed for them.

That has a cash-flow effect. You do not earn anything on a voucher until the customer shows up, and a customer who never comes costs you nothing but the chair or slot you held for them.

Payments process weekly on Wednesdays. Invoices from the last five business days of a month are paid on the first business day of the next month. ACH deposits arrive 3 to 5 business days after processing, and mailed checks take 5 to 7 business days. If you redeemed a voucher that is later refunded, your share is deducted from your next payment.

## What Can Reduce Your Payout?

Two adjustments are worth modelling. First, if Groupon features your deal in a Promotional Program, the page says your payout can be reduced, "never by more than 20% of the Net Remittance Amount." Ask whether your deal is in any such program and how it affects your net.

Second, the page does not mention card processing fees at all. Do not assume none apply. Ask Groupon in writing whether any processing or other deductions come out of your net.

The page also gives no further contract terms beyond the agreed commission. Terms such as exclusivity, redemption rules, expiry and limits on number of vouchers should be read in the contract before signing.

## What Should You Model Before You Say Yes?

Work from the voucher price, not the original price. Subtract the commission to get your net per redemption. Then subtract the real cost of delivering the service: labour, products, and the opportunity cost of the slot if a full-price customer could have taken it.

Four numbers decide the outcome:

- Net per redemption (voucher price minus commission)
- Delivery cost per visit
- Redemption rate (unredeemed vouchers earn nothing but also cost nothing)
- Share of customers who come back at full price

## Worked Example: A $40 Voucher for an $80 Service

All inputs are **assumptions**, chosen for round numbers. They are not Groupon figures. A salon sells an $80 service as a $40 voucher. Groupon's commission is 30%. The service costs $22 in labour and products. Of 100 vouchers sold, 80 are redeemed and 20 expire and are refunded to buyers.

| | Per redemption | 80 redemptions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voucher price | $40.00 | $3,200 |
| Commission (30%) | $12.00 | $960 |
| Net payout | $28.00 | $2,240 |
| Delivery cost | $22.00 | $1,760 |
| Profit | $6.00 | $480 |

Three variations show where the risk sits:

1. Promotional Program at the maximum 20% reduction: net falls to $22.40 per redemption, profit drops to $0.40, or $32 across 80 redemptions.
2. Commission rises to 40%: net is $24, profit $2 per redemption, $160 in total.
3. Repeat business: if 15 of the 80 return once at the full $80 price, each repeat visit earns $58 after the $22 cost, adding $870.

The break-even commission on this example is 45%, because the $40 voucher must leave at least $22. The deal makes sense only if the repeat customers are real. ## Who Should Use Groupon and Who Should Skip It?

Use this rule:

1. High margin, spare capacity: services whose break-even commission (1 minus delivery cost divided by voucher price) sits well above your contract rate, and slots that would otherwise sit empty.
2. Thin margin or full calendar: skip it. At a break-even commission of 45% in the example, one program deduction can erase the profit.
3. You can capture the customer: if you collect an email address or a review from each new client, the deal buys a list as well as a visit. Pair it with [a plan to build your email list](/blog/email-list-building-strategies).
4. You depend on cash this month: payments come only after redemption and on a weekly schedule, so a deal sold today may not pay for weeks.

<Callout type="tip" title="Do This Today">
Write down the delivery cost of the service you would discount and divide it by the voucher price. Subtract the result from 1. That is your break-even commission; if it is under 40%, the top of Groupon's example range loses money.
</Callout>

## FAQ

### Does Groupon charge to list my business?

The US pricing page says there are no upfront fees and no charge for listing, setup, impressions or clicks.

### What commission does Groupon take?

The rate is agreed before the campaign launches and depends on industry, discount, deal structure and goals. The page shows 20%, 30% and 40% only as examples.

### What happens to vouchers that are never used?

The page says they are refunded to the customer by Groupon and you are not billed for them.

### How often does Groupon pay?

Payments process weekly on Wednesdays, with a separate rule for invoices in the last five business days of a month.

### Can Groupon reduce what I receive?

Yes, if your deal is in a Promotional Program, by up to 20% of the Net Remittance Amount, according to the page.
