The five SEO steps that matter most for a pet groomer are: complete your Google Business Profile, earn and answer reviews, make your website say what you groom and where, keep your name and address identical everywhere, and add LocalBusiness structured data. Google says local results rest on relevance, distance and prominence, and that you cannot pay for a better local ranking.

This guide is for groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Each figure comes from a source listed at the end, and the worked example is labelled as an assumption.

## How Does Google Rank Local Pet Groomers?

Google's [Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) names three factors. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Distance is how far you are from the person searching. Prominence is how well known you are, which depends partly on links from other websites and on how many reviews you have.

You cannot change distance. You can improve relevance and prominence. Google also states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking, so be wary of anyone who sells one. The five tips below each work on relevance or prominence.

## 1. What Should Your Google Business Profile Include?

Fill in every field Google offers, and follow its [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177):

- **Name:** use your real-world business name. Do not add taglines, hours, phone numbers or service descriptions.
- **Category:** choose the fewest categories that describe your core business, as specifically as possible.
- **Address:** a mobile groomer with no shopfront is a service-area business. Hide the address if you do not serve customers there, and keep the service area within roughly two hours of driving from your base.
- **One profile per location:** duplicates are not allowed.

## 2. How Do Reviews Help You Rank and Book?

Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking, and that replying to reviews can help you stand out. Reviews also decide the booking. BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found that 97% read local reviews, 68% require four stars or more, and 31% require 4.5 or more, up from 17% a year earlier.

Recency counts too: 74% only consider reviews from the last three months. Google's [review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) says you can send a review link or QR code, and that offering discounts or free services for reviews is strictly prohibited. Keep replies short, specific and not promotional, and respond promptly.

## 3. What Should Your Grooming Website Say?

Search engines match pages to searches, so each service needs words that match how owners search. Give the site a clear page for each service you sell, such as dog grooming, cat grooming or mobile grooming, and name the town or neighborhood you serve in plain language. Show prices or a price range if you can, hours, and a booking link or phone number.

No keyword tool figures are quoted here because search volumes differ by town. Check Google Search Console after a few weeks to see which searches already show your site, then write pages for the ones you are missing. Our [local SEO optimization guide for groomers](/blog/pet-groomer-local-seo-optimization) covers page structure in more detail.

## 4. Why Does Consistent Business Information Matter?

Use the same business name, address and phone number on your website, your profile and other directories such as Yelp and Bing Places. Mismatches make it harder for search engines to be sure which business is yours. This is a common-sense practice, not a ranking factor Google has published a figure for.

Claim the free listings that groomers actually use, and fix any duplicate. If you moved or changed your number, update every listing the same week. A short spreadsheet of each listing, its login and last check date saves time later.

## 5. Should You Add Structured Data?

Structured data tells search engines the facts on your page in a fixed format. Google's [LocalBusiness documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business), updated 8 September 2026, lists name and address as required. It recommends geo coordinates, opening hours, telephone, URL and price range.

All of Google's examples use JSON-LD. Many website builders and plugins add this for you, so check before writing code. Markup does not guarantee a special result in search, but it removes guesswork about your hours and location.

## FAQ

### How long does local SEO take for a pet groomer?
No source publishes a standard timeline. Profile edits can show quickly, while reviews and links build over months. Track calls and direction requests monthly in your profile.

### Do I need a website, or is a Google Business Profile enough?
A profile can bring bookings alone, but a website gives you service pages, structured data and links. Google lists links from other websites as part of prominence.

### Can I offer a discount for a Google review?
No. Google says offering free or discounted services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. Ask, give a link or QR code, and thank people.

### Can a mobile groomer have a profile?
Yes, as a service-area business with the address hidden if you do not serve customers there. Keep the service area realistic.

### Can I pay Google to rank higher in local results?
No. Google states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Paid ads are separate and are labelled as ads.
