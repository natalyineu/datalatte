A fitness studio can use Meta's AI tools to draft ad variations and handle delivery, but it should keep control of three things: the location, the offer and the tracking of who actually joins. Most adults in the US already use Facebook, so the audience is there, according to Pew Research Center's 2026 survey. Meta's automation can reduce the production work, but it cannot know which leads turned up for a trial class. Start with one campaign, one offer and a clear count of joins.

This guide is written for boutique studios, gyms and personal trainers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Pew's figures describe US adults, not studios, and the worked example below uses assumptions throughout.

## What Campaign Should a Studio Start With?

Start with one leads or messages campaign for one offer, such as a free trial class or a first week of unlimited classes. Testing one offer keeps the results readable. If three offers run at once, you cannot tell which one brought the people who joined.

Pew's survey, run from 30 January to 18 June 2026 with 5,511 US adults, found that 70% of adults say they use Facebook and 51% use Instagram ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)). Those shares describe the whole country, so they show Facebook is a broad channel, not how your neighbourhood responds. Set the campaign goal to match how people book: a lead form for trial sign-ups, or a booking link you can track.

## Which Parts Should AI Handle?

AI tools are best used for production: generating copy options, resizing images and building variations of the same offer. Google describes a similar approach on its Display campaigns, where AI selects the best combination of headlines and images from the assets you supply, and you can choose how much AI to use for bidding, creatives and targeting ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)). Meta's tools work on the same principle, so the job for a studio is to supply good photos, clear offers and honest copy.

Use AI to draft variations of your own photos and copy, then review every one before it runs. A variation that promises a result the studio cannot prove should be removed, even if the tool wrote it well. ## What Should You Still Control?

Three settings stay with the studio: location, minimum age and the offer itself. Location matters most. Someone 30 miles away will not attend a trial class, so a tight radius around the door is usually the sensible starting point. This is a judgement call, not a Meta rule, and the right radius depends on how far members travel to the building.

Meta's own testing guidance points to the same discipline. Its A/B testing documentation says a test should use the same budget for both versions, and that audiences are split so that nobody sees both versions ([Meta Business Help Centre](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669)). Informal testing, such as switching ad sets on and off by hand, can overlap audiences and produce unreliable results. Test one variable at a time, such as the image or the headline, and write down the hypothesis before starting.

## How Do You Write Copy That Converts?

Name the offer, the place and the next step. A line such as "Free trial class at [studio name], [neighbourhood]. Book a time" is specific and gives the reader something to do. A slogan does not tell anyone what happens next. Ask an AI tool for ten headline options, then cut any claim that cannot be supported, especially promised weight loss or transformation. Describe the class, the coach and the time, and let people decide whether it suits them.

Test two images against one offer. Photos of the actual room and coaches, with permission from the people shown, tend to show what a member would experience more accurately than stock images. Whether real photos outperform stock for a given studio is an open question, so run the test and let the bookings decide. The [Facebook ads A/B test guidance](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669) gives a clean way to run it.

## How Do You Measure and Scale?

Count trial bookings, show-ups and joins, not clicks. Meta's pixel and lead forms stop at the form submission. The front desk is the only place that records who walked in and who signed a membership. Keep a simple sheet with four columns: lead date, booked, attended, joined. Once a month, divide the ad spend by the number of joins to get cost per new member.

Scale the budget only after a month of data, and only if cost per member stays within what the membership fee can recover. ## What Does a Month Cost? A Worked Example

Every input in this table except the platform's own tracking is an **assumption**. Replace them with the studio's figures before deciding anything.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | Assumption | $600 |
| Cost per lead | Assumption | $25 |
| Leads | $600 / $25 | 24 |
| Leads who attend a trial (assumption) | 40% | about 10 |
| Trial attendees who join (assumption) | 30% | 3 |
| Cost per new member | $600 / 3 | $200 |
| Monthly membership fee (assumption) | Assumption | $120 |
| Months of fees to recover ad cost | $200 / $120 | about 1.7 |

If only 15% of trial attendees join, the number of new members falls to about 1.5 and cost per member doubles to about $400, which takes more than three months of fees to recover. Retention and the trial-to-join rate often matter as much as the ad itself, so measure them alongside the cost per lead.

## FAQ

### Does Facebook still reach enough people for a local studio?

Pew's 2026 survey found 70% of US adults use Facebook, so it is a broad channel. Whether it reaches enough of a local catchment depends on the radius and the audience, which only the studio's own campaign data can show.

### Does AI make Facebook ads cheaper for a studio?

Nothing in the sources reviewed here measures that for fitness studios. Treat AI as a way to produce variations faster. Judge it on cost per joined member over at least a month.

### Should I let Meta choose my audience?

Meta can explore interests, but the location and minimum age should be set by the studio. A tight radius around the studio is the most important control.

### How long should I test before judging results?

Run a test with a fixed budget on both versions and keep it running long enough to see trial attendance and joins, not only clicks. A month is a practical minimum for a studio with modest volume.
