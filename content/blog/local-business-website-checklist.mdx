A local business website needs six things done well: clear contact details, a page for each service, a fast mobile experience, proof such as reviews, markup that tells Google who and where you are, and basic accessibility and legal pages. This checklist has 30 items in those six groups. Where Google or the W3C publish a target, it is quoted with a source; the rest is practical advice and is labelled that way.

Work through it with your phone in hand. You can finish a first pass in under an hour.

## What Must Visitors Find in the First Few Seconds?

Visitors arrive from a map listing or a search and want to know three things: what you do, where you are, and how to reach you. Check each item below on your home page, on a phone.

1. Your business name and what you do, in plain words, at the top.
2. Your town or service area in the first screen.
3. A tap-to-call phone number.
4. Opening hours that match your Google Business Profile.
5. One clear main action, such as Book, Call or Get a quote.

Our guide on [getting more phone calls from your website](/blog/how-to-get-more-phone-calls-from-your-website) covers the call button in more depth.

## Does Your Site Work Well on a Phone?

Google uses the mobile version of your site for indexing and ranking, and says the mobile version should have the same content, headings, alt text, structured data, titles and meta descriptions as the desktop version. It also says not to lazy-load primary content that needs a tap or swipe to appear.

6. Same content on mobile and desktop.
7. Text readable without zooming and buttons easy to tap.
8. No pop-up covering the phone number or the main button.
9. Forms usable with a phone keyboard.
10. Phone numbers and addresses as real links, not images.

## How Fast Should the Site Load?

Google's web.dev gives targets, measured at the 75th percentile of page loads: largest contentful paint (LCP) within 2.5 seconds, interaction to next paint (INP) of 200 milliseconds or less, and cumulative layout shift (CLS) of 0.1 or less. Test with PageSpeed Insights or the Core Web Vitals report in Search Console.

11. LCP within 2.5 seconds on mobile.
12. INP of 200 ms or less.
13. CLS of 0.1 or less.
14. Photos sized to the screen, not uploaded at camera size.
15. No unused tracking scripts or chat widgets.

For fixes, see [website speed optimization for small business](/blog/website-speed-optimization-small-business).

## Does Google Understand Who and Where You Are?

Google says each page should have a distinct, descriptive and concise title element, and that it may rewrite a title that does not reflect the page. Its LocalBusiness structured data guide lists name and address as required properties, and recommends opening hours, telephone, URL and geo coordinates.

16. A unique title for every page, with the service and town.
17. A short meta description written for people.
18. One page per main service, not one page for everything.
19. LocalBusiness markup with name, address, phone and hours.
20. Name, address and phone identical to your Business Profile.

See [schema markup for local business websites](/blog/schema-markup-local-business-website) for the setup. Google's guidelines say a business name should reflect the real-world name, without added keywords.

## Does the Site Give Reasons to Trust You?

BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey of 1,002 US adults found 97% read online reviews, 68% require at least 4 stars and 74% only consider reviews from the last three months. Show recent, real reviews, and never invent them.

21. Recent reviews with the source named.
22. Photos of your own premises, team or work.
23. Prices or a price range where you can give one.
24. An About page with who runs the business.
25. A short FAQ answering the questions customers ask by phone.

The post on [trust signals](/blog/trust-signals-website) shows how.

## Is the Site Accessible, Secure and Legal?

W3C recommends WCAG 2.2, which organizes requirements under four principles: perceivable, operable, understandable and robust, at levels A, AA and AAA. Legal requirements depend on your country; if you serve EU or UK customers, check consent and privacy rules.

26. Text with enough contrast and alt text on images.
27. Every form field has a label.
28. HTTPS on every page.
29. A privacy page and, where required, cookie consent.
30. A working contact form that sends to an inbox someone reads.

Read [website accessibility for small business](/blog/website-accessibility-small-business) and [GDPR compliance for local sites](/blog/local-business-website-gdpr-compliance).

## FAQ

### What pages does a local business website need?

At minimum: home, one page per main service, about, contact, and a privacy page. A pricing or FAQ page helps if customers ask about cost or policies.

### How often should I review my site?

Check hours, phone and address whenever anything changes, and run the full checklist at least once a year. No source sets a fixed schedule.

### Do I need schema markup?

It is optional, not required to rank. Google lists name and address as the required properties for LocalBusiness markup to be eligible for rich results.

### What are the Core Web Vitals targets?

LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP of 200 milliseconds or less and CLS of 0.1 or less, at the 75th percentile, according to Google's web.dev.

### Is a website still needed if I have a Business Profile?

A profile shows hours and reviews, but a site holds your services, prices and proof.
