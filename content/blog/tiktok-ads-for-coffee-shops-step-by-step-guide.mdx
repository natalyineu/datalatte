Start with the number most guides skip. TikTok's [budget help page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/budget) says a daily ad group budget must exceed $20 and a campaign budget must exceed $50. So a two-week test at the minimum is more than $280 before you learn anything, and that is for a shop that may net a few hundred dollars a day. The question is not "is TikTok good?" but "can this test earn back its $280?" The math below shows when it can.

This guide is for café owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. TikTok's minimums are stated in US dollars on that page and it does not say how they change in other currencies, so check your own Ads Manager. Everything below is a plan for one disciplined test: one offer, one video, a tracked outcome.

## Is Your Customer on TikTok?

The Pew Research Center's [2025 social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) (5,022 US adults surveyed Feb. 5 to June 18, 2025) reports the share who ever use TikTok by age:

This is US data, so it will differ in the UK, Australia and Canada. The point is the slope: if your regulars are mostly commuters over 50, TikTok will reach fewer of them than Google will, and your [Google Business Profile](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization) and a [Google Ads campaign](/blog/coffee-shop-google-ads-guide) will do more. If you serve students, remote workers and under-40s, TikTok is a fair test. ## Step 1: Set Up the Accounts

1. Create a TikTok account for the shop with your name, logo and a bio that says what you are and where: "Specialty coffee and pastries in [Neighborhood]. Open daily 7 to 3."
2. Open a TikTok Ads Manager account and finish the business and billing steps it requests.
3. Post three to five real organic videos first. When someone taps your profile after an ad, they should find a living account, not an empty one.

## Step 2: Pick the Ad Format

The format that suits most cafés is a **Spark Ad**, which runs an organic post as an ad. TikTok's [Spark Ads page](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/spark-ads) says you can use posts from your own account, that all views, comments, shares, likes and follows gained from boosting the video are credited to the original organic post, and that the ad's display name and text come from the post and cannot be edited during ad creation. That last point matters: write the final caption before you boost.

Standard in-feed video ads in Ads Manager give you more control over the caption and call to action. Larger full-screen placements exist, but skip them until you have a result from the basic format.

## Step 3: Targeting, and One Correction

Many guides tell you to target "a radius around your shop". TikTok's [location targeting page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/location-targeting?lang=en) lists these levels: city, country or region, county, DMA regions, province, state, urban district and zip code (postal code), and for Canada only the first three characters of the postal code. It does not mention radius targeting. It also says targeting uses signals to find people "located in or regularly in the selected location" and does not guarantee delivery or accuracy.

So the practical approach is:

- Choose the zip or postal codes within walking or short driving distance of the shop, rather than a whole city.
- Check the options in your own account, since TikTok says the available choices depend on where your Ads Manager account is registered. See its [location options by market](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/placements-available-locations?lang=en) page.
- Expect some waste. A zip code is a blunt tool, and people who live nearby may never walk past you.

For the audience, match your real customers using your POS or loyalty data rather than assuming. Start broad within the locations and let the creative do the filtering.

## Step 4: Make the Video

Your ad is competing with entertainment, so it has to feel like a video someone would choose to watch.

- **Open on the action.** Steam wand, pour, first sip. Not your logo.
- **Show one offer.** "Show this video for a free cookie with any drink" gives you something to count.
- **Add captions.** Many people watch without sound.
- **Film vertically in natural light.** Steady phone footage is fine.
- **Use licensed audio only.** TikTok's [Commercial Music Library page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library) says businesses cannot use the general music library for commercial usage, and that the Commercial Music Library has about 1 million songs, free for businesses. A trending sound that is not in that library is not safe for an ad.

For creative ideas, see our [Reels ideas](/blog/coffee-shop-instagram-reels-ideas-that-get-views) and the [coffee shop TikTok marketing guide](/blog/coffee-shop-tiktok-marketing-guide); good Reels usually translate well.

## Step 5: Budget the Test

TikTok's budget rules, per its [help page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/budget):

- Daily budgets: more than $50 at campaign level, more than $20 at ad group level.
- Lifetime budgets: campaign more than $50; ad group at least the number of days in the run (including past days) times $20. A 31-day run needs at least $620.
- The campaign budget should not be lower than the ad group budget, or delivery can suffer.
- You can change the amount after launch but cannot switch between daily and lifetime budgets once live.

A realistic test is one campaign, one ad group, two or three videos, 14 days at just over $20 a day: about $280 to $300. For typical cost levels, see our [TikTok ad cost benchmarks](/blog/how-much-do-tiktok-ads-cost-real-2026-pricing-benchmarks).

<Callout type="warning" title="Do Not Judge By Views">
A video can get 50,000 views and zero redemptions. Decide before launch what counts: a redeemed code, a staff-tallied "saw it on TikTok" at the till, or a lift in new loyalty-card sign-ups.
</Callout>

## Step 6: Track Real Visits

TikTok will report impressions and clicks. You need walk-ins.

- **A unique offer** staff can ring in as its own POS button, such as "TikTok cookie".
- **A question at the till:** "Where did you hear about us?" with a tally sheet.
- **A before and after:** compare new-customer counts in the test weeks to your usual weeks, knowing that weather and school terms will blur it.

This is imperfect, but it is far better than views. If you run an online order page, use the TikTok pixel and a conversion objective to count orders directly.

## The Math

Take the $280 test (14 days at $20). Every row marked "assumption" is my estimate for illustration, not a TikTok or industry benchmark for local cafés. The one published reference I used is Lebesgue's [TikTok benchmarks page](https://lebesgue.io/tiktok-ads/tiktok-ads-benchmarks), which reports a $4.80 average CPM and a 0.61% CTR, both from its ecommerce data. Café and local-radius numbers will differ.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Test budget (assumption) | $280 | $280 | $280 |
| Cost per 1,000 views (assumption) | $8.00 | $4.80 | $4.00 |
| Impressions | 35,000 | 58,333 | 70,000 |
| Click-through rate (assumption) | 0.4% | 0.61% | 1.0% |
| Clicks | 140 | 356 | 700 |
| Click to redeemed offer (assumption) | 2% | 5% | 8% |
| Redemptions | 2.8 | 17.8 | 56 |
| Cost per redemption | $100 | $15.73 | $5.00 |
| Visits from each redeemer in the next 90 days (assumption) | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Average ticket (assumption) | $6 | $6 | $6 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 70% | 70% | 70% |
| Gross profit per redeemer | $16.80 | $16.80 | $16.80 |
| Gross profit returned per $1 of ads | $0.17 | $1.07 | $3.36 |

How to read it: Base roughly breaks even, Tough loses most of the budget, and Strong is a good result that you should not plan around. Redemptions will include existing regulars who would have visited anyway, so the true new-customer return is lower than the table shows. That is why the 90-day visits row carries so much weight: a single free cookie rarely pays for itself, but a new regular might.

The break-even cost per redemption is visits x ticket x margin: 4 x $6 x 0.70 = $16.80. If your test comes in below that and the redeemers are mostly new faces, scale slowly. If it is above that, change the video or the offer before you spend more.

## Step 7: Put the Results Next to Your Other Channels

TikTok should sit beside, not replace, the channels that catch people already searching: an optimised [Business Profile](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization), [Google Ads for "coffee near me"](/blog/coffee-shop-google-ads-guide), and [email](/blog/email-marketing-for-coffee-shops-build-1000-loyal-regulars-in-90-days) to bring new customers back. A [loyalty card without an app](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app) turns a first visit into a habit. ## Do This Today

1. Check who your customers are: pull age groups from your loyalty data or tally the next 100 customers by eye.
2. Decide one offer you can ring in as its own POS button, and brief your staff on the "where did you hear about us?" question.
3. Film three vertical 15 to 25 second videos of the drink, the pour and the space, using audio from the Commercial Music Library.
4. Set up Ads Manager with one campaign, one ad group, zip codes near the shop and a daily budget just over $20 for 14 days.
5. After day 14, divide spend by redemptions and compare it to your break-even figure before deciding whether to continue.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I really target a one-mile radius?**
A: TikTok's location targeting page lists city, county, DMA, state and zip or postal code levels and does not mention a radius. Check the options in your own account, and use nearby zip codes if no radius appears.

**Q: How much does a TikTok test cost at minimum?**
A: TikTok requires more than $20 a day at ad group level and more than $50 at campaign level. A 14-day test at about $20 a day is roughly $280 to $300 for the ad group.

**Q: My customers are older. Is TikTok worth it?**
A: Pew's US data shows 30% of 50 to 64 year olds and 12% of those 65 and over use TikTok, against 63% of 18 to 29 year olds. Test it only if a meaningful part of your customer base is younger, and put Google first otherwise.

**Q: Can I boost a post I already made?**
A: Yes, that is a Spark Ad. TikTok says engagement from boosting is credited to your organic post, and the caption is locked once the ad runs, so finalise it first.

**Q: What if someone leaves a negative comment on the ad?**
A: Unless it is spam or abusive, reply politely and offer to fix the problem. Other viewers will see how you respond.
