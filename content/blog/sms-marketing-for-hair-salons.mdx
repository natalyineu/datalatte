Texting is the fastest way to fill a cancelled chair, and the easiest way to get a salon into legal or deliverability trouble. This guide covers the rules first, then costs, then a worked example you can adapt.

Important: these figures come from healthcare, not salons. The [Cochrane review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24310741/) found moderate-quality evidence from seven studies (5,841 participants) that text reminders improved attendance (RR 1.14, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.26). A [randomized trial in a pediatric clinic](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5227159/) of 169 patients found no-shows of 23.5% with texts versus 38.1% with standard voice reminders (p = 0.04). We found no rigorous salon-specific study, so use these as evidence that reminders work, not as a promise of your result.

## Rule 1: get written consent before marketing texts

Under FCC rules, autodialed or prerecorded telemarketing calls and texts generally require prior express written consent, meaning a signed agreement (electronic signatures can qualify) that clearly authorizes the seller to send such messages and states the phone number ([47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200)). The stricter "one-to-one" consent rule was vacated by the Eleventh Circuit on January 24, 2025 ([Kelley Drye summary](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule)). Practical version: add an unticked checkbox to your booking form that says clients agree to receive promotional texts, and keep the timestamp.

Appointment reminders and promotions are different. A reminder for a booked visit is a service message; a "20% off Thursday" blast is marketing and needs the consent above. This is general information, not legal advice. Ask a lawyer if you text at volume.

## Rule 2: honor opt-outs fast

Per the same regulation, a client may revoke consent by any reasonable method, replying STOP, QUIT, END, REVOKE, OPT OUT, CANCEL or UNSUBSCRIBE counts, and you must honor it within a reasonable time not to exceed ten business days. You may not designate one exclusive way to opt out for covered marketing texts. Pick a platform that processes STOP automatically.

## Rule 3: register your number

US carriers require businesses sending app-to-person texts over standard 10-digit numbers to register under A2P 10DLC. Twilio's documentation says unregistered senders receive additional carrier fees and registered ones see lower filtering and higher throughput ([Twilio A2P 10DLC](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/compliance/a2p-10dlc)). Most salon texting platforms handle registration for you; ask before you sign up.

## Worked example: filling a cancelled slot (assumptions)

Assumptions: 200 opted-in clients, a $39 plan, a $100 average service, a cancellation with 4 hours' notice.

- You text the 40 clients who booked that service type in the last 12 months. Cost: 40 credits, about $3 at the plan's rate (a $39 plan covers 500 credits, so 40 credits is roughly 8% of it).
- Even if only 1 in 40 takes the slot, you recover $100 that would have been lost.
- Monthly check: if you recover 2 slots a month, that is $200 against a $39 plan.

The decision rule: keep SMS if recovered revenue per month is at least 3x the plan cost after two months. If not, check your list size and consent wording before dropping it.

<Callout type="warning">Do not text a purchased or scraped list. Without written consent you carry the legal risk, and complaints damage your number's reputation.</Callout>

## Message templates that stay compliant

- Reminder (service): "Hi Jenna, reminder: cut with Maya, Thu 2:00 PM at Studio Rowan. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule."
- Fill (marketing, needs consent): "Studio Rowan: a 3:00 PM cut just opened today. Reply YES to book it. Reply STOP to opt out."

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I text clients from my personal cell phone?**

For one-to-one reminders, many solo stylists do. For bulk promotions, use a platform that handles consent records, STOP replies and carrier registration.

**Q: How many promotional texts per month is safe?**

No regulator sets a number. Start with one or two a month and watch your opt-out rate; if it climbs, send less.

**Q: What does an opt-out require?**

Honor STOP-style replies within ten business days at most, per 47 CFR 64.1200, and do not make clients use only one specific word.

**Q: Do texts really reduce no-shows?**

In healthcare studies, yes, by a moderate margin (see the Cochrane and clinic trial above). Track your own no-show rate for 60 days before and after.
