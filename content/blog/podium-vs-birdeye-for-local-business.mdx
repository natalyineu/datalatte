Birdeye sends buyers to a pricing request form that asks for a location band, from 1-3 locations up to 1000+. For a two-site shop, the quote decides the cost. This guide sets out what each vendor says, what it leaves out, and a budget benchmark to test the quote against.

## What Does Podium Charge for Its Plans?

Podium's pricing page lists three plans: Core, Pro and Signature. It does not show a dollar figure for them. On all three, Contacts, One-to-One Text Messages, Conversation History and Team Members are listed as unlimited, so the limits that differ between plans need to be confirmed in writing.

Automated AI Review Responses and AI Instant Answers are listed as add-ons available on the Core plan. That means they may sit outside the base price. Ask whether each add-on is a one-time or recurring charge, and whether it appears on the quote.

Before you agree to anything, ask for the monthly total, the billing period and any carrier or registration fees for business texting. The page does not state those fees.

## How Does Birdeye Price Its Platform?

Birdeye says its pricing is built around outcomes, not seat counts. It describes one platform on one contract, where customers pay only for what they deploy at their locations. Location count and the products you switch on are the main cost drivers.

The request form groups businesses into bands: 1-3, 4-9, 10-39, 40-249, 250-999 and 1000+ locations. A two-location shop falls in the first band, so the quote should reflect that band and the products you list.

The Birdeye pricing page also shows figures such as 98.5K mobile Google Map impressions, 94.7K visitors clicking "call" and 15.3K reviews with a 4.6 star rating. The page gives no time period for these. Treat them as platform claims, not results a shop should expect.

## What Should a Quote Include Before You Sign?

1. The monthly total for your location band, and whether it is billed monthly or annually.
2. Which Podium plan (Core, Pro or Signature) is quoted, and whether Automated AI Review Responses or AI Instant Answers are included.
3. For Birdeye, confirmation that you pay only for the products you deploy at your locations.
4. Contract length, renewal terms and the cost of cancelling early.
5. Any carrier or registration fees for business texting.
6. Length of any free trial. GatherUp's trial is 14 days with no card required; ask whether the others match it.
7. Export rights for reviews and contacts if you leave.

Texting also depends on your Google listing. Google's help page says a WhatsApp or text messaging option on a Business Profile requires a claimed and verified profile, and that these options are available only in select regions. Check both before you buy, using the [Edit your Business Profile help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617).

## FAQ

### Does Podium publish its pricing?

Not as dollar amounts on its pricing page. The page lists plan features and add-ons, so the monthly cost has to come from a quote.

### Does Birdeye publish its pricing?

Not as a dollar figure. It uses a pricing request form that asks for a location band, and it says pricing is based on what you deploy at your locations.

### Does either vendor offer a free trial?

The pricing details above do not show a trial for Podium or Birdeye. Ask each vendor directly. GatherUp lists a 14-day free trial with full access and no card required.

### How much do review management tools cost on average?

CostBench reports an average of $193 per month across 8 tools, and Adwave puts Podium, Birdeye and similar tools at $200 to $400 per month. Both are secondary sources, so confirm current prices with each vendor.

### Which is better for a two-location salon?

Neither page gives enough public detail to pick a winner. Send both vendors the same brief, ask for the same items from the checklist, and compare the totals line by line.
