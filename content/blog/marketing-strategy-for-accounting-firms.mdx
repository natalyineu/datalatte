An accounting firm should market through three channels: a verified Google Business Profile with reviews, a website with clear service pages, and targeted Search ads or email to existing clients. Paid leads are not cheap. WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks list Business Services at $5.87 per click, a 4.85% conversion rate and $93.69 per lead. The report has no separate accounting category, so use that figure as a rough guide.

This guide covers small and mid-sized firms in the US. Firms that practise before the IRS also face advertising rules, covered below. Every figure has a source at the end.

## What Marketing Works for an Accounting Firm?

Accounting is a trust purchase, so proof matters. BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found 97% read reviews for local businesses, 68% require four or more stars, and 89% expect owners to reply.

Start with the profile. Google says local ranking depends on relevance, distance and prominence, and that complete, verified profiles are more likely to appear. List each service in detail, add hours including tax-season hours, and reply to reviews.

Then publish service pages and short guides on the questions clients ask. See our [website guide for accountants](/blog/website-design-for-accountants) and [social media for accountants](/blog/social-media-for-accountants).

## What Do the Advertising Rules Say?

Rules differ by credential and state, so confirm yours. For practitioners before the IRS, Treasury Circular No. 230, section 10.30 (the PDF is marked Rev. 6-2014) says you may not use public communication or private solicitation containing a false, fraudulent, coercive, misleading or deceptive claim. Enrolled agents and registered tax return preparers may not use the word "certified" to describe their designation.

The same section lets you publish fee information: fixed fees for routine services, hourly rates, fee ranges and the fee for an initial consultation. If you publish a rate, you may charge no more than it for at least 30 calendar days. Stop contacting anyone who says they do not want to be solicited.

State boards may add their own rules. This article is not legal advice.

## Should an Accounting Firm Run Google Ads?

Run them only after the profile and website work. A narrow campaign for a service such as small-business bookkeeping or tax-resolution fits better than a broad one. Google's US Local Services Ads list does not name accountants, though it lists tax services and financial planning services, so check eligibility before planning around it.

The cost per lead is high relative to other local trades, so measure clients won, not leads. Our [keyword guide for accountants](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-accountants-in-2026) lists terms to bid on and block.

| Channel | Best for | Cost model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business Profile and reviews | Local searches | No ad cost; ranking cannot be bought |
| Service pages and guides | Specific questions | Your time or a writer |
| Search ads | Urgent needs | Per click |
| Email to clients | Retention and referrals | Your email tool |

## How Do You Keep Clients Coming Back?

Retention is cheaper than acquisition. Send deadline reminders and a plain-language year-end summary to people who agreed to hear from you. Our [email marketing guide for accountants](/blog/email-marketing-for-accountants) shows a simple sequence.

Ask clients for a review after you deliver a filing or plan. The FTC's rule on fake reviews took effect on 21 October 2024 and bars offers of rewards that require a positive review. Keep client details out of replies.

## The Math: What Does $1,000 Buy?

All inputs below are **assumptions** built on the Business Services benchmark.

| Step | Assumption or source | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | Assumption | $1,000 |
| Cost per lead | $93.69 (LocaliQ benchmark) | about 10.7 leads |
| Leads who become clients | 25% (assumption) | about 2.7 clients |
| Annual fee per client | $1,800 (assumption) | about $4,800 a year |
| Cost per client | $1,000 divided by 2.7 | about $375 |

Under these assumptions the first year pays back. If only 10% of leads sign, cost per client rises to about $940 and payback depends on retention. Track lead source in your intake form to learn your real figure.

## FAQ

### How much should an accounting firm spend on marketing?
No source fixes a percentage. A benchmark of $93.69 per lead lets you size a test, such as $1,000 for roughly 10 leads, then compare against your client value.

### Can I advertise my fees?
Practitioners before the IRS may publish fixed fees, hourly rates, fee ranges and the initial consultation fee under Circular 230, with the 30-day rule above.

### Is Google Business Profile useful for accountants?
Yes. It shows hours, services and reviews in local results. Google says no one can pay for a better local ranking.

### Do Local Services Ads cover accountants?
The US list Google publishes does not name accountants. It does list tax services and financial planning services.

### Should I buy reviews?
No. The FTC's rule bars buying reviews and insider reviews without disclosure.
