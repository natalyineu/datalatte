## Why Should an Accounting Firm Focus on Retention First?

AdWave's accountant advertising guide says that once an accountant has a client, they typically retain that client for 5+ years. Its med spa guide makes a broader point about the cost gap. That figure comes from a different industry, so treat it as a directional benchmark. A pet grooming marketing article makes the same comparison and cites a third-party claim of 5 times.

The practical step is to start with clients you already have. Deadline reminders, a plain-language year-end summary and a check-in before each filing season cost far less than a cold search click. Our [blog content strategy guide](/blog/blog-content-strategy-local-business) shows how to plan a year of client-facing articles, each answering a question you already hear at intake.

Raklet's membership marketing outline lists clear goals, a list of existing members, value propositions, methods for getting new members and keeping old ones, and different pricing choices as core parts of a membership strategy. Treat your client list as that existing-member list. Write down the goal, such as keeping every business client through the filing season, and the value each client group receives.

Drafting reminder emails and year-end summaries is a task where AI tools can help. The [prompts guide for local businesses](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) includes starter prompts, and the [AI vs human comparison](/blog/ai-vs-human-marketing-local-business-honest-comparison) explains which tasks still need a person to check the wording.

## How Should a Local Accounting Profile Be Set Up?

Salesforce defines local marketing as a strategy focused on targeting consumers within a specific geographic area, aiming to attract customers who are physically near a business. For an accounting firm, the local profile is the first asset to fix. List each service by name, add opening hours including any extended tax-season hours, and keep the business name, address and phone number identical everywhere they appear.

Build one service page per service, written around a question a prospect would type. Explain what the service includes, who it suits, which documents to bring and how the first meeting works. If you publish fee information, make sure it matches your state rules and current IRS guidance before it goes live.

Ask for reviews after you deliver work. Do not pay for them, and check current FTC and state rules before offering any incentive tied to a review. Reply to every review, and keep client details out of public replies.

## Should You Text Clients, and What Does the Law Require?

Texting works only with permission. An SMS guide for accounting firms states that under the TCPA a firm must collect prior express consent before texting clients, and should keep an auditable record of that consent. Store the date, the channel and the wording the client agreed to, in the same system as the client file.

The same guide recommends skipping SMS marketing software if a firm has fewer than 50 active clients. Smaller practices can send reminders from an email tool they already use and reserve texts for urgent deadlines.

Prices listed in August 2026 show the range. Twilio's US SMS base rate is $0.0083 per segment before carrier pass-through. SimpleTexting's starting published price is $29–$39 for 500 credits, and EZ Texting's Launch plan is $25 per month, or $20 per month on annual billing. Vendor prices change, so check each live pricing page before you sign up.

## Can Google Ads Work for a Local Accounting Firm?

Run search ads only after the profile and service pages are working. CPA Site Solutions suggests using radius targeting to reach clients within a set distance of your office, or targeting specific zip codes where your ideal clients live. Pick the radius you can serve in person, not the widest map you can draw.

The same article suggests keyword phrases such as "certified public accountant near me" or "tax preparation services." Choose phrases that match services you can deliver this month, and add negative terms for work you do not offer. The article also states that smart bidding in an AdWords MCC account can adjust bids automatically based on the likelihood of conversion. That only works when conversions are tracked, so set up tracking for booked consultations first.

The article was published in July 2024, so check current platform help pages before setup. A tax-season push also needs timing, and the [holiday marketing strategy guide](/blog/holiday-marketing-strategy-local-business) covers how to schedule work ahead of a peak period.

Setup checklist:

1. Confirm the profile and service pages are live and accurate.
2. Set conversion tracking for booked consultations, not only form views.
3. Choose a radius or zip codes you can serve in person.
4. Select a short list of service phrases and negative terms.
5. Review cost per booked consultation after the first month and adjust.

## FAQ

### Do I need consent to text accounting clients?
Yes. Under the TCPA, a firm must collect prior express consent before texting clients and should keep an auditable record of it.

### Is texting worth buying software for a small practice?
An SMS guide for accounting firms recommends skipping SMS marketing software with fewer than 50 active clients. Smaller firms can send reminders from an existing email tool.

### Is radius targeting useful for a local accounting firm?
CPA Site Solutions suggests radius targeting around your office or targeting zip codes where your ideal clients live. Choose a radius you can serve in person.

### How long do accounting clients usually stay?
AdWave's accountant guide says accountants typically retain clients for 5+ years once acquired. Check this against your own client records.
