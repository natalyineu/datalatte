## How Much Do Google Ads Cost for Contractors?

LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks, updated June 1, 2026, list Home and Home Improvement at $8.33 per click, a 6.47% click-through rate, an 8.05% conversion rate and $90.92 per lead. The all-industry average is $5.42 per click and $66.69 per lead. So home improvement sits about 54% above average on click cost.

The category is broad, covering many trades, so a roofer, a kitchen fitter and a handyman will pay different amounts. These are averages for a year of campaigns, not quotes. Use Keyword Planner for your trade and area, and judge your own account by cost per booked estimate.

## Should You Use Search Ads or Local Services Ads?

They work differently. Search ads charge per click and let you choose keywords. Local Services Ads charge per lead and show above search results with a screened badge. Google screens or verifies businesses, which may include background, registration, insurance and licence checks.

Under Local Services Ads, a missed call during business hours is billable if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. Leads judged invalid or low quality are not charged, and Google may credit charged leads later, with credits unavailable for some verticals and regions. Repeat contact from the same number within 15 days is not charged again.

Google has said it is moving these campaigns into Google Ads as Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals. Selected US home service advertisers, such as plumbing, HVAC and roofing, began migrating in August 2026, with more categories later. Check which flow your account uses. ## Which Keywords Should a Contractor Start With?

Start from the job, not the trade title. Searchers write the work they need, in their city.

- **Project plus place:** "bathroom remodel [city]", "deck builder near me"
- **Urgent repair:** "roof leak repair [city]"
- **Estimate intent:** "kitchen remodel cost [city]", "free estimate [trade]"

Add negatives for DIY, jobs, courses and materials, such as "how to", "salary", "diy" and "supplies". Google notes negative keywords do not match close variants, so add plurals yourself. Our [contractor keyword list](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-contractors-in-2026) has more examples.

## How Do You Track Phone Calls?

Calls win most contractor jobs, so count them. Google says clicks on a call asset cost the same as headline clicks. For conversion tracking you set a minimum call length, and shorter calls do not count. Calls from ads and website calls need Google forwarding numbers, which are available only in certain countries.

You can also import calls from a CRM so only calls that led to a sale are counted. At the start, set a call length that matches a real enquiry, and listen to a few recordings if you have permission. Without tracking, you cannot tell cost per estimate from cost per click.

## What Does a Landing Page Need?

Send each ad group to a page for that job, not the home page. Show your area, licence number, a phone number that can be tapped, a short form and examples of real work you own the rights to. Keep claims you can prove. Our [hardware store guide](/blog/google-ads-for-hardware-stores) shows how the same call and location tools work for a related retail audience.

## FAQ

### How much should a contractor spend on Google Ads?

There is no standard figure. At $8.33 per click, $1,500 buys about 180 clicks, so start with an amount that gives you enough data to judge and stop if cost per job is above your margin.

### Are Local Services Ads better than Search ads?

They suit different goals. Local Services Ads charge per lead and add a verification badge. Search ads give you keyword control. Test both if available in your area.

### What is a good cost per lead for contractors?

LocaliQ lists $90.92 for Home and Home Improvement. Treat it as a reference, not a target.

### Do phone calls count as conversions?

Yes, if you set up call tracking and a minimum call length. Shorter calls are not counted.

### How do I stop wasted clicks?

Use negative keywords and review the Search terms report weekly.
