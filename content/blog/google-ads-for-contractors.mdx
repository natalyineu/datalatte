Google Ads works for contractors when each campaign targets one job type in one service area, sends clicks to a matching page, and counts phone calls as conversions. Google scores each keyword on a 1-10 Quality Score based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. The score is a diagnostic, not an auction input, so the practical goal is relevant ads that earn calls. This guide explains how to set that up, which keywords to start with, how to track calls, and a $1,500 monthly budget example where every input is labelled as an assumption.

## How Does Quality Score Affect Contractor Ads?

Quality Score is measured on a scale from 1 to 10 and shown at keyword level. It combines three components: expected clickthrough rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Each one is rated above average, average or below average, compared with advertisers whose ads showed for the same search over the last 90 days.

A low rating usually points to a specific fix. Weak relevance means the ad copy should name the exact job. A weak landing page means the page should cover that job, not the home page. Google notes that Quality Score is not a key performance indicator, so judge the account by cost per booked estimate rather than by the score alone.

## Which Keywords Should a Contractor Start With?

Start from the job the customer needs, in the town where the business works. Searchers type the work and the place together, so build ad groups around phrases such as "bathroom remodel [city]", "roof leak repair [city]" and "kitchen remodel cost [city]". Each phrase should have its own ad group and its own landing page.

Keyword research also has to separate buyers from other searchers. A builder-focused analysis by [BrandRocket](https://www.brandrocket.net/resources/construction-bids-google-ads-public-owners) reports that "construction bids" gets about 1,000 searches a month and reads mostly like contractors looking for work. Trade words carry the same risk, so review the search terms before expanding any keyword list. ## Why Do Homeowners Search Online Before Hiring a Contractor?

Homeowners often start with a search, which is why paid search reaches buyers who are already looking. Contractor Marketing Pros, citing Google consumer insights, reports that 97% of consumers search online for local businesses and that 78% of local mobile searches result in an offline purchase within 24 hours. That is a secondary summary of Google's data, so check the original report before using the figures in client material.

For a contractor, the practical point is timing. A search for a leaking roof or a failed furnace is usually followed by a call the same day, so ads need to be live, targeted and easy to act on from a phone.

## How Do You Track Calls as Conversions?

Phone calls often win the job, so they should count as conversions. In Google Ads you set a minimum call length, and shorter calls are not counted. Pick a length that matches a real enquiry, then check a sample of calls to confirm it. If you have a CRM, import outcomes so you can see which calls became estimates and which became jobs.

Track form fills too, but do not treat clicks as success. Without call and form tracking, you can see what each click cost but not what each estimate cost. The second number is the one that decides whether a campaign is worth running.

## What Should a Contractor Landing Page Include?

Send each ad group to a page about that specific job, not the home page. The page should show the service area, the licence number where the law requires one, a tappable phone number, a short booking form and examples of completed work you have the rights to show. Keep every claim something you can prove.

Message match matters here. If the ad promises "roof leak repair in [city]", the first screen of the page should repeat that promise. Pages that drift away from the ad text usually score lower on landing page experience, which feeds back into Quality Score.

Work out margin and close rate first, then raise the budget only if the cost per job stays below what a job is worth to the business.

## Should You Use Local Services Ads Too?

Local Services Ads use a different model from Search. Instead of paying per click on keywords you choose, you pay for leads that Google routes to your business, and the ad carries a verification label. Compare the cost of a lead from each route after a few weeks, using the same tracking. Do not assume one is cheaper. Test both in your area and keep the one that produces booked jobs at a profit.

## FAQ

### How much should a contractor spend on Google Ads?

There is no standard figure. Start with an amount that gives enough clicks and calls to judge, then compare the result with margin per job. Stop or change the campaign if cost per job is above what a job is worth.

### What is a Quality Score?

It is a 1-10 diagnostic score for each keyword, based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Google says it is not a key performance indicator and not an input in the ad auction.

### Do phone calls count as conversions?

Yes, once call tracking and a minimum call length are set. Calls shorter than that length are not counted.

### How do I stop wasted clicks?

Add negative keywords for DIY, jobs, courses and supplier searches, then review the search terms report every week. The [Google Ads for local retail guide](/blog/google-ads-for-local-retail) covers the same review process for shop-based businesses.
