In BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults, 45% said they use AI tools to get local business recommendations, up from 6% the year before, and 23% had used Google AI Mode specifically for that in the last 12 months. That is a sample from one country, so read it as a signal for the US and a likely direction for the UK, Canada and Australia, not a measurement of them.

The honest problem: Google has not published how AI Mode picks local businesses. So this guide sticks to three buckets. What Google itself confirms, what surveys and tests show, and what is our advice. We label which is which, because most articles on this topic blur them.

## What Google Confirms About Showing Up

[Google's Search Central page on AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) is short and unglamorous. It says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond being indexed and eligible to show in Google Search with a snippet. It also says:

- Both features may use "query fan-out", running multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources.
- Meeting the requirements does not guarantee inclusion.
- Standard SEO fundamentals still apply, including letting Google crawl your site.
- You do not need new machine-readable files, AI-specific text files or special schema.org markup.
- AI feature traffic is counted in the "Web" search type in Search Console, not split out.

So there is no AI Mode switch. If someone sells you "AI Mode optimisation" with a special file or markup, Google's own documentation says you do not need one.

For the local side, Google's [Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that prominence includes the number and rating of your reviews. Whether AI Mode uses the same weights is not stated by Google. Treat it as a sensible default, not a fact.

## What Tests Show About AI Mode and Local

A May 2025 test by [Human Level](https://www.humanlevel.com/en/blog/seo/google-ai-mode-and-local-search-results), run from a Chicago IP while AI Mode was in beta, reported:

- The three-business Local Pack did not appear; AI Mode showed lists of up to ten businesses, with a map at the end.
- The businesses normally in the Local Pack's top three also appeared in AI Mode.
- Each business got a context-specific description, and ratings came from the Business Profile.
- Results shifted with the location modifier ("best pizzerias" vs "in Minneapolis" vs "near me").
- Clicking through often opened the business's profile first, so visiting your website required the profile's Website button.

This is one agency's test of one business type on a beta product with an unstated query count. It is useful for direction (profiles matter, ratings show) and weak as proof. Run your own searches.

More from the same reports:

- 68% will only use a business rated 4 stars or higher (55% in 2025).
- 74% look for reviews from the last three months.
- Among AI users, 97% sometimes double-check AI recommendations against real reviews, and 42% always check reviews on native review platforms.
- Of those using AI review summaries, 59% check review profiles for more information, while 23% rely on the summary alone.
- The percentage using Google for reviews fell from 83% to 71%.

Take-away: AI introduces you, and then people check your reviews. Your rating and your recent reviews decide whether the AI's recommendation turns into a booking.

## What to Fix, in Order

This section is our advice, built on the sources above. Nobody can promise placement.

### 1. Make the Business Profile complete and accurate

It is the data source for hours, ratings and attributes, and it is where a click usually lands. Use the most specific primary category, add every true attribute and service, set holiday hours, and keep photos current. Follow the [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist). Google's Gemini integration for profiles is covered in [our Google Business Profile AI features guide](/blog/google-my-business-ai-features).

### 2. Raise your rating above the thresholds people use

If 31% of consumers say they only use businesses with 4.5 stars or more (BrightLocal), a 4.3 rating quietly excludes you from a chunk of the market. See "The Math" below for how many reviews it takes to climb. Ask every customer, never only the happy ones, and never offer incentives, which Google's [contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) prohibits. Our guide to [Google Business Profile reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) covers asking and replying.

### 3. Keep reviews recent

With 74% of consumers wanting reviews from the last three months, a profile with 200 reviews and nothing since spring looks stale. Aim for a steady drip tied to a routine, such as a text link sent after each appointment.

### 4. Say the specific thing in the places you control

AI answers handle requests like "quiet cafe with outdoor seating and oat milk". Write plainly, on both profile and website, what you do, who it is for, where, and what it costs. Create a page per service and per location, with a short FAQ. Do not stuff keywords. Our guide on [generative engine optimization for local business](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-local-business) goes deeper.

### 5. Keep your name, address and phone consistent

Google confirms inaccurate information can keep a profile from showing. Fix mismatches on Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook and key directories. See [NAP consistency](/blog/nap-consistency-local-seo-how-to-fix) and [citation building](/blog/local-citations-building-strategy).

## Tracking Visibility Without Fancy Tools

Because Google folds AI traffic into the standard Web report, combine signals:

1. Each month, search 5 to 10 queries a customer would type ("best [your category] in [town]", "[category] near me open now") in AI Mode and log whether you appear.
2. Watch branded search impressions and clicks in Search Console.
3. Track calls, direction requests and website clicks in your Business Profile performance screen.
4. Ask new customers how they found you, and record "ChatGPT" and "Google AI" as answer options.

## The Math

What does climbing from 4.3 to 4.5 cost? Every number below is an **assumption** chosen to show the arithmetic; use your own rating and count. The 31% figure is BrightLocal's.

| | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Current reviews (assumption) | 40 |
| Current true average (assumption) | 4.3 |
| Total stars now | 40 x 4.3 = 172 |
| Target true average | 4.5 |
| New 5-star reviews needed (n) | (172 + 5n) / (40 + n) = 4.5, so n = 16 |
| Reviews you collect per month (assumption) | 8 |
| Months to reach 4.5, if all new reviews are 5 stars | 2 |
| Realistic case: 6 of 8 new reviews are 5 stars, 2 are 4 stars (assumption) | about 4 months |
| Share of consumers who only use 4.5+ (BrightLocal) | 31% |

How to read it: the 16-review figure comes from algebra, not from a study. It is optimistic, because it assumes every new review is five stars. The realistic row is slower, which is why you start asking now. Whether the extra rating converts into more customers depends on your market, so measure calls and direction requests before and after.

## Do This Today

1. Search three queries a customer would use, in AI Mode, and write down who appears and what they show (rating, review count, description).
2. Open your Business Profile and fix anything wrong: primary category, hours, attributes, services. Compare it with your website and listings.
3. Work out your true average and review count, then calculate how many 5-star reviews would reach 4.5 using the formula above.
4. Set up a review ask: a link or QR code, sent by text within a day of every visit, to every customer.
5. Add or rewrite one page on your site that clearly answers "who is this for, where, how much" for your top service, with three FAQs.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to run Google Ads to show up in AI Mode?**
A: Google's documentation lists no extra requirement to appear in AI Mode and does not say ads buy a place in AI summaries. Treat paid and organic as separate and judge each by cost per customer.

**Q: Does Google say reviews affect AI Mode picks?**
A: Google says review count and rating affect prominence in local results generally. It has not published how AI Mode weights them, so any claim of an exact review threshold for AI Mode is unverified.

**Q: Do I need special schema or an llms.txt file?**
A: Google says you do not need new machine-readable files, AI-specific text files or special schema.org markup to appear in its AI features. Other AI assistants may behave differently, so do not assume.

**Q: How long do changes take to show?**
A: Profile edits can appear within days, but review recency and rating take weeks or months to move, as the math above shows. Google gives no timeline.

**Q: Do negative reviews hurt my chances?**
A: BrightLocal's data says many consumers apply star cutoffs, so average rating matters. A few lower reviews are normal. Reply to them politely and specifically.
