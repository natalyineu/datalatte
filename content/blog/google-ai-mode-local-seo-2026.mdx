To show up in Google AI Mode for local searches, make your Google Business Profile complete and accurate, collect specific and recent reviews, keep your name, address and phone consistent across the web, and publish clear website pages that answer the questions customers ask. Google does not publish how AI Mode picks local businesses, so no one can guarantee placement, but these are the same signals that support strong local rankings in general.

When someone searches "best hair salon for colour correction in Austin" or "coffee shops with good wifi near downtown Denver", they may now get a conversational AI answer with named recommendations instead of only a list of links. Here is what that means for a local business and what to do about it.

## What Google AI Mode Does

AI Mode is a Google Search experience that uses generative AI to answer questions conversationally and to let people ask follow-ups. Instead of scanning ten links, a searcher can get a summary that draws on multiple sources.

For local queries, the answer can draw on information such as:

- Your Google Business Profile (hours, services, photos, attributes)
- Reviews and what customers say in them
- Your website content
- Business listings and citations elsewhere
- Maps data such as location and distance

Features, rollout and wording change often, so check how your own searches look and treat anything you read, including this guide, as a snapshot.

<Callout type="tip">
For local searches, the information on your Business Profile is likely as important as your website. Keep hours, services, photos and attributes current in both places.
</Callout>

## How Local Recommendations Seem to Be Chosen

Google has not published the rules for AI Mode, but traditional local ranking factors (relevance, distance and prominence) are a sensible guide.

### Prominence
Review count and quality, an established profile, mentions across the web and a site that shows expertise all signal that a business is a credible choice.

### Relevance and specificity
AI answers are good at nuanced requests such as "gluten-free friendly coffee shop with outdoor seating". Businesses that state these details clearly on their profile and website, and whose reviews mention them, are easier to match.

### Review text
Reviews are more than a star rating. If many reviews mention a specific service or quality, such as balayage or being gentle with anxious dogs, those themes help describe your business.

### Recent activity
An active profile with current photos, posts and review replies looks more reliable than an abandoned one. Check your own profile to see what has gone stale.

## The Map Pack Still Matters

AI Mode does not replace the local map results. Transactional searches like "coffee shop near me" or "hair salon [city]" commonly still show map listings. AI summaries add a layer on top of this, so you need both traditional local SEO and clear, specific information that an AI can use. ## What to Do: A Local AI Mode Checklist

### Google Business Profile (most important)

- Choose the most accurate primary category and relevant secondary categories.
- List all services or products with short descriptions.
- Keep hours accurate, including holiday hours.
- Add fresh, real photos regularly.
- Fill in attributes (wifi, outdoor seating, dog-friendly, accessibility) that apply.
- Post updates, offers and events on a regular schedule.
- Answer the questions customers commonly ask in the Q&A, where available.

See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and [Google Business Profile service](/services/google-business-profile).

### Reviews

- Ask every happy customer, using a short link or QR code.
- Reply to every review, positive and negative, promptly and professionally.
- Gently invite specifics ("what did you like about your visit?") without scripting or incentivizing reviews, which Google prohibits.

More in our guide to [Google Business Profile reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews).

### Website content

- Create a page for each service and location you serve.
- Answer common questions in plain language, with a short FAQ.
- State attributes clearly rather than leaving them for the AI to infer.
- Mention your city, neighborhood and nearby landmarks naturally.
- Add LocalBusiness structured data and keep it consistent with your profile.

### Citations and directories

Keep your name, address and phone number consistent across your profile, Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook and industry directories. Read about [NAP consistency](/blog/nap-consistency-local-seo-how-to-fix) and [citation building](/blog/local-citations-building-strategy).

<Callout type="warning">
AI answers can be wrong about hours, services or prices. Search for your business in AI Mode now and then. If something is wrong, correct your profile and website and use Google's feedback options.
</Callout>

## Content That Helps AI Answers Cite You

- **"Best for" pages:** Be specific about who you serve, for example "our salon specializes in color correction for curly hair".
- **Concrete descriptions:** Instead of "great service", explain what customers actually experience.
- **Local FAQs:** Questions like "How often should I get a balayage touch-up?" answered by a local expert.
- **"How to choose" guides:** For example, how to choose a groomer for an anxious dog.

Write these for people first. Content that is thin, repetitive or copied rarely helps.

## Tracking Your Visibility

Search Console and GA4 may not separate AI Mode traffic cleanly, so combine several signals:

1. Search your business name and your category plus city in AI Mode and record what appears.
2. Watch branded search impressions and clicks in Search Console.
3. Track direct and branded traffic in GA4.
4. Review Business Profile performance for calls, direction requests and website clicks.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to run Google Ads to show up in AI Mode?**
A: No. Ads do not buy a spot in organic AI summaries, though ads can appear on search results pages. Treat paid and organic as separate.

**Q: Can I pay to be featured in AI Mode answers?**
A: Not for organic recommendations. Be skeptical of anyone who promises guaranteed placement.

**Q: Do multiple locations need separate pages?**
A: It is good practice to give each location its own Business Profile and its own page with a unique address, phone number and local details, so each location is clearly described.

**Q: How long do changes take to show up?**
A: Profile edits can update within days, while website changes depend on how often Google recrawls your site. Expect weeks to see a clear effect, and do not expect guarantees.

**Q: Do negative reviews hurt my chances?**
A: A few negative reviews are normal. What matters is the overall pattern and how you respond. Professional, specific replies show you are responsive.
