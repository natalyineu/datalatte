A wine bar fills its evenings by being easy to find on Google Maps, showing its full menu, running a repeatable event such as a tasting night, and collecting emails from regulars. Paid ads are possible but regulated: Google and Meta both bar alcohol ads aimed at people under the legal purchase age, and Meta sets 21+ for the United States. Google's average cost per click for Restaurants and Food is $2.05 (LocaliQ 2026), but wine bars are not a separate category.

This guide is for independent wine bars in the US. Alcohol advertising is also governed by state and local law, which this article does not cover, so check yours.

## How Do Wine Bars Get Found on Google?

Start with a complete Business Profile. Google's menu editor, available to food and drink businesses, lets you list menu items with descriptions and prices in sections, and edits can take 24 to 48 hours to appear on Maps and Search. Add your by-the-glass list, flights and small plates, not just a PDF.

Keep hours, address and category accurate, and add photos of the room and the pour. Our [Google My Business guide for restaurants](/blog/google-my-business-for-restaurants) lists the other fields. Reviews matter too, since people compare bars on Maps before choosing.

## What Can You Advertise, and to Whom?

Both platforms allow alcohol ads with rules.

| Platform | Key rules from the policy |
| --- | --- |
| Google Ads | Ads must not encourage excessive or irresponsible consumption, must not target or mainly appeal to people under the legal purchase age, and must follow local law. The US is a permitted country |
| Meta | Ads must comply with local laws, may not be targeted to under-18s at a minimum, and the US minimum is 21+ |

Google also says minors must never be shown consuming alcohol, and ads must not imply health benefits. Meta's page covers alcohol venues and events, so a wine bar's event ad falls under the policy. Both pages say to follow local law, and neither describes specific US state rules.

## Which Events Bring People In on Quiet Nights?

No source tells us which event type works best, so pick one repeatable format and measure it. Options:

- A weekly tasting with a producer or distributor rep.
- A themed flight night (a region or a grape).
- Wine and food pairing with a local kitchen.
- A members' club with a pre-paid pour allowance.

Pick the quietest night and keep it fixed for 8 weeks, so people can form a habit. Promote it through your Business Profile posts, Instagram and email. Our [Instagram marketing for bars](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-bars) and [marketing for cocktail bars](/blog/marketing-for-cocktail-bars) guides share more promotion ideas.

## How Do You Build an Email List of Regulars?

Ask at the table or when taking a reservation, and say what you will send. Under the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide, commercial email must carry accurate headers, a non-deceptive subject, a postal address, and a clear opt-out that you honor within 10 business days. Penalties reach $53,088 per violating email.

Send a short weekly or fortnightly note: the week's pours, the event, one reason to come on the quiet night. Our [email marketing for restaurants](/blog/email-marketing-for-restaurants) guide has examples.

## Worked Example: A 20-Seat Tasting Night

All inputs below are **assumptions** to show the arithmetic.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Seats sold | 20 |
| Ticket price | $35 |
| Wine and food cost per guest | $14 |
| Staff cost for the night | $120 |
| Ad spend to promote one event | $60 |

Revenue is 20 x $35 = $700. Costs are 20 x $14 = $280, plus $120 staff and $60 ads, so $460. Profit is $240 for the night. At $2.05 per click, $60 buys about 29 clicks, so selling 20 seats from ads alone is unlikely. Regulars on your email list and walk-ins should fill most seats, and ads are an extra. If only 12 seats sell, revenue is $420 and costs are $348, so profit is $72. Track tickets by source.

## FAQ

### What is the best way to market a wine bar?

A complete Google Business Profile with the full menu, a regular event, and an email list of customers who asked to hear from you. Paid ads are an extra.

### Can wine bars advertise on Facebook and Instagram?

Yes, under Meta's alcohol policy, which requires compliance with local law and sets a 21+ minimum audience age in the US.

### Can wine bars use Google Ads?

Yes. Google allows alcohol ads in the US, but they must not encourage excessive consumption or target minors, and they must follow local law.

### How much do ads cost?

There is no wine-bar benchmark. The nearest published average is $2.05 per click for Restaurants and Food (LocaliQ 2026).

### How often should I email customers?

No source sets a number. Start with one email every two weeks and watch unsubscribes.
