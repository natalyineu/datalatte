Google's Chrome 154, scheduled for October 2026, turns on "Always Use Secure Connections" by default, so visitors get a warning dialog before opening a public site without HTTPS. Free certificates are available from Let's Encrypt and are built into many website builders.

This guide explains what HTTPS does, what it does and does not do for search rankings, how to get it for free and what breaks after the switch. Facts come from Google, Let's Encrypt and Squarespace documentation listed at the end. The example is labelled as an assumption.

## What Does Chrome Do With Sites Without HTTPS?

Google's post of October 28, 2025 says Chrome 147 (April 2026) enabled the public-sites version of "Always Use Secure Connections" for users of Enhanced Safe Browsing, and Chrome 154 (October 2026) makes it the default for everyone. Before opening a public site that lacks HTTPS, Chrome shows a warning that attackers could view or change information sent or received, with "Continue to site" and "Return" buttons.

Chrome does not repeat the warning for sites a visitor opens regularly, and users can turn the setting off. Still, a first-time customer finding your cafe or salon through search could meet a warning page. Google's own testing found the median user saw fewer than one warning a week, which shows how rare insecure sites have become.

| Chrome version | Date | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 147 | April 2026 | Warning on for Enhanced Safe Browsing users |
| 154 | October 2026 | Warning on by default for all users |

## Does HTTPS Help Your Google Rankings?

Not much, and Google says so. Its page experience documentation lists "Are your pages served in a secure fashion?" as a self-assessment question, but states that beyond Core Web Vitals, other page experience aspects do not directly help a site rank higher. Google announced HTTPS as a lightweight signal in 2014, though the current documentation is the better guide.

So treat HTTPS as a trust and safety basic, not a ranking trick. The practical risk is lost visitors and lost form submissions when a browser warns people away. Search Console also has an HTTPS report that shows which pages are served securely. ## How Do You Get HTTPS for Free?

Check your host first, because many include it. Squarespace says all domains correctly pointing to a Squarespace site get free SSL certificates automatically, and the update can take up to 48 hours. Other builders and hosts have their own help pages, and the sources here do not cover them, so check yours.

If you run your own hosting, Let's Encrypt is a nonprofit certificate authority that charges no fee for certificates. Default certificates last 90 days and should be renewed about every 60 days, which is normally automated. Its FAQ notes that hosting providers who integrate Let's Encrypt may charge a small fee for their own management costs, so ask what you are paying for.

1. Ask your host whether SSL is included and whether it is switched on.
2. Turn on the secure option and wait for provisioning to finish.
3. Make HTTP addresses redirect to HTTPS.
4. Check every page for mixed content.
5. Update links in your Business Profile, ads and email signatures.

## What Is Mixed Content and How Do You Fix It?

Mixed content happens when a page loads over HTTPS but some images, scripts or stylesheets still load over HTTP. Web.dev explains that active content such as scripts can let attackers take control of the page, and that most browsers block it. Chrome may upgrade images to HTTPS if a secure version exists, and if not, the asset does not load.

The fix is to change hard-coded HTTP addresses to HTTPS, check the browser developer tools for blocked items and remove any asset that has no secure version. Squarespace notes that with the secure setting, visitors can see browser warnings when custom code or integrations load insecure content, and that fixing this is the site owner's job.

Also be careful with HSTS. Squarespace warns that turning it off can block access for visitors until their policy expires, which can take up to 48 hours.

## FAQ

### Is HTTPS free?

Let's Encrypt certificates are free, and some hosts include SSL at no extra cost, such as Squarespace for correctly connected domains. Some providers charge a management fee.

### Is SSL the same as HTTPS?

HTTPS is the secure web address scheme. SSL is the older name for the certificate technology behind it, now replaced by TLS, though people still say SSL.

### Will HTTPS improve my Google ranking?

Google says other page experience aspects beyond Core Web Vitals do not directly help rankings. Expect trust benefits, not a ranking jump.

### How long does setup take?

Squarespace says up to 48 hours for changes to complete. Other hosts vary.

### Do I need to renew the certificate?

Let's Encrypt certificates last 90 days by default and are normally renewed automatically. Most website builders handle renewal for you.
