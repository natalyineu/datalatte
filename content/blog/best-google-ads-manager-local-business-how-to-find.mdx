Here is the sum most owners skip. Suppose a manager charges a flat $500 a month and each new customer is worth $400 to you in gross profit over their time with you (an assumption; use your own number). The manager has to bring you at least 1.25 extra customers a month, every month, before you are better off. On a $1,000 ad budget that is a tall order. On $3,000 it is easy. The right manager depends less on their badge and more on whether your spend and margins leave room for one.

This guide is for owners of local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada (coffee shops, salons, studios, groomers, trades) who want to hire one person, or a small shop, to run Google Ads. It is about finding and testing the person. For comparing agency fee structures and contracts, read our guide to [choosing a Google Ads agency](/blog/local-business-google-ads-agency-how-to-choose), and for what a month of management should contain, see [what to expect from Google Ads management](/blog/google-ads-management-small-business-what-to-expect). I will not repeat those here.

## First, Decide Whether You Need a Manager at All

A manager earns their fee in three ways: they fix waste (irrelevant clicks), they fix tracking (so you know what works), and they save you time. Before you search, check which of those you actually need.

- **You spend under about $500 a month.** There is probably not enough traffic for a paid manager to change much. Run one tight Search campaign yourself, or pay for a one-off audit.
- **You spend $1,500 or more and the account feels like a black box.** A manager is probably worth interviewing.
- **You have a decent account but no time.** You are buying hours. Price it that way.

If you are unsure whether to hire at all, [should you hire a marketing agency or do it yourself](/blog/should-you-hire-a-marketing-agency-or-do-it-yourself) covers that decision.

## Where to Find Candidates

1. **Referrals from non-competing local owners.** Ask the owner of a business similar to yours, in a different town. They have no reason to hide who they use or what they pay.
2. **Google's partner directory.** Agencies can earn a Partner or Premier Partner badge. Google's [help page on Partner status](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9701987) says the requirements are checked daily and that a company that loses status loses the right to advertise it, after a 60-day grace period. It means the company met Google's criteria on the day you looked. It says nothing about whether they know a one-location coffee shop from a plumbing franchise.
3. **A freelancer with a visible track record.** Look for case-style write-ups with the numbers explained, not star ratings.
4. **Your own web developer or SEO.** Ask, but only hire them if they can answer the tests below.

## What Certifications Prove (and What They Don't)

Google's [certification help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9702955?hl=en-GB) gives exact rules you can use as a filter:

- A certification stays valid for one year, then must be renewed by passing the assessment again.
- The assessment pass mark is 80% and it runs 75 minutes, with the timer unstoppable once started.
- Only the person who passed may call themselves certified. A colleague's certificate or the agency's badge does not count for them.

So ask the individual who will run your account: "Which Google Ads certifications do you hold personally, and when do they expire?" If they cannot answer, they are borrowing someone else's credential. Treat certification as proof of baseline platform knowledge, a floor and not a ceiling. A candidate with none might still be excellent, but then the next section matters more.

## Run a Three-Part Test Before You Sign

Anyone can sound good on a call. Use these tests, in order.

### Test 1: The search terms report

Ask each candidate to look at a search terms report from your account (or a screenshot they request) and tell you, on the call, what they would block. A competent person will point to specific irrelevant phrases, explain negative keywords and mention match types. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) lists the usual culprits for local businesses so you can judge their answers.

No account yet? Ask them to describe the first week for a business like yours: which keywords, which locations, which negatives.

### Test 2: The tracking question

Ask: "What will you count as a conversion, and how will phone calls be tracked?" The strong answer names form submissions, calls from the ad, calls from the website and bookings, set up before ads go live. If the answer is "clicks" or "we track the thank-you page" and stops, pass. Our [conversion tracking setup guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) and [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) show what complete looks like.

### Test 3: The bidding question

Ask: "When would you use automated bidding on my account, and what data would you want first?" Good answers refer to having enough tracked conversions. Google's [Target CPA help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632) recommends measuring performance over periods that include at least 30 conversions, and that is a rule for judging results, not a hard entry requirement. Be wary of anyone who quotes a magic number as gospel, and equally of someone who switches every campaign to automation in week one on an account with five conversions a month.

### Optional: A paid trial

If you are close to choosing, offer a paid, fixed-scope audit or a first-month trial with a written scope and an exit. A good person will take a paid audit gladly, because it shows how they work. One who insists on a 12-month commitment first is telling you something.

## Ownership and Access: Check It Yourself

Whoever you hire, your account should be yours. Google's [access levels page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9977851) lists five levels: Administrative, Standard, Read only, Email only and Billing. It also says that, in a linked client account, a manager account's powers are limited unless that manager is the client's administrative owner. And actions like removing other manager accounts generally need administrative ownership or a direct administrator login to the client account.

In plain English: create the account under your own Google login and payment card, make yourself the administrator, and link the manager to it. Log in during week one to confirm you can see everything. If they want to run your ads inside their own account and bill you, ask what happens to your data when you leave.

## Is a Local Services Ads Specialist Different?

Sometimes. Google's [Local Services Ads help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS) says these ads are for certain service categories, that you pay for leads related to your business when a customer contacts you from the ad, and that the Google Verified badge is not available for auto, beauty or dining verticals. Eligibility depends on category and location. Ask any candidate whether your business qualifies, and whether they have run LSA before. If you are a plumber or a lawyer, that is a separate conversation from a salon on standard Search.

## Red Flags

- **Guaranteed leads, rankings or cost per click.** Auction prices move with competition. No one can promise your number.
- **No questions about your business.** If they do not ask about margin, average sale, service area and how you handle calls, they are running a template.
- **No mention of the landing page.** Clicks that do not turn into calls are the commonest leak.
- **Reports full of impressions and clicks.** Insist on cost per lead, calls and bookings.
- **Hold the keys.** Refusal to give you administrator access.
- **Rushed contract.** A long lock-in with a setup fee before you have seen any work.

## The Math

The table compares the same $1,000 monthly budget with and without a manager who cuts waste. Every figure is an **assumption** for illustration. Replace each with your own after you pull a month of data.

| | Unmanaged account | With a manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $3.00 | $3.00 |
| Clicks | 333 | 333 |
| Clicks from irrelevant searches (assumption) | 30% | 10% |
| Relevant clicks | 233 | 300 |
| Click to lead rate (assumption) | 8% | 8% |
| Leads | 18.7 | 24 |
| Lead to customer rate (assumption) | 25% | 25% |
| New customers | 4.7 | 6.0 |
| Gross profit per customer over time (assumption) | $400 | $400 |
| Gross profit | $1,867 | $2,400 |
| Ad spend | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Manager fee (assumption, flat) | $0 | $500 |
| Net after ads and fee | $867 | $900 |

How to read it: cutting wasted clicks from 30% to 10% adds 1.3 customers a month, which is worth about $533, almost exactly the $500 fee. So at $1,000 of spend, a good manager roughly breaks even in this scenario. The same improvement on $3,000 of spend adds about 4 customers, worth about $1,600 against the same fee. That is the case for hiring at higher spend, and for DIY or a one-off audit at lower spend. The numbers will move a lot with your close rate and customer value, which is why you should compute your own before the first call.

## Do This Today

1. Calculate gross profit per new customer over their time with you, and the most you could pay per customer. Write both down.
2. Pull your last 90 days of Google Ads data (or decide your budget if you have none), and note whether conversions are being tracked at all.
3. Shortlist two or three candidates from referrals or the partner directory, and send each the three tests in this guide by email.
4. Ask each one for the personal certifications they hold with expiry dates, and a sample report showing cost per lead.
5. Before signing, put in writing: you are the account administrator, you own the data, there is a named person on your account, and you can exit after 90 days.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a Google Ads manager cost?**
A: There is no official price. Our agency guide summarises the fee ranges that published agency blogs cite, along with their limits. Start from your own numbers: the fee must be covered by the extra customers the manager brings.

**Q: Is a Google Partner badge enough?**
A: No. Google says the badge is tied to company-level requirements it checks daily, and personal certification lasts a year and belongs to the individual. Neither tells you whether the person has run accounts like yours.

**Q: Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?**
A: For a single location with a modest budget, a specialist freelancer or small shop is often the better fit, because the person you meet is the person doing the work. Agencies suit multi-location or larger budgets. Use the same tests either way.

**Q: Can I just let Google manage it with automation?**
A: You can run automated bidding, but it needs tracked conversions to work from. Without correct tracking and a clean negative list, automation optimises toward whatever you counted, including junk.

**Q: How long until I know if the manager is good?**
A: You should see tracking installed and tested in the first two weeks. Judge cost per lead after 60 to 90 days, which is my rule of thumb and not a Google figure.
