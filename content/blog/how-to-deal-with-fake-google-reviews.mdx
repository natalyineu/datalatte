To deal with a fake Google review, report it from your Business Profile (Read reviews, Report, pick a reason, Send report), check its status in Google's Reviews Management Tool, and appeal once if Google finds no violation. Reply publicly in the meantime. Google removes only reviews that break its content policies, so a harsh but real review stays up.

This guide is for local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Policy details come from Google's own help pages and the FTC, listed at the end. Where Google does not publish a number, the text says so.

## What Counts as a Fake Google Review?

Google's contribution policy lists fake engagement as content that "is not allowed and will be removed". It names paid reviews (paid for "directly or in kind"), reviews posted from multiple accounts at one person's direction, and reviews based on a conflict of interest such as a competitor, a current or former employee, or a relative.

Other policy names relevant to you: Advertising and Solicitation (links and contact details in a review), Repetitive Content, Impersonation, and Off-Topic. A review about a business you never visited, for example a competitor's attack or a rant about a different shop, can fall under Off-Topic or Fake Engagement.

What does not count: a negative review from a real customer, a one-star rating you disagree with, or a review that is rude but about your actual service. Google's help page says to report only reviews that violate policy, and that it does not step into disputes between businesses and customers.

## How Do You Report a Fake Review on Google?

Google documents two routes. Both need a verified Business Profile.

1. Open your Business Profile and select **Read reviews**.
2. Next to the review, select **Report**.
3. Choose a reason, such as **Spam** or **Profanity**.
4. Select **Send report**.

The second route is the Reviews Management Tool, where you choose your business, then **Report a new review for removal**. It also shows the status of earlier reports.

Pick the reason that matches the actual violation. A review from a competitor is not profanity. If you have evidence, such as a reviewer who was never a customer, keep it, because it helps in an appeal.

## How Long Does Removal Take and What If Google Says No?

Google says evaluation "typically takes several days" and does not publish a removal rate or a guaranteed time. Check the status in the Reviews Management Tool. The statuses are:

- **Decision pending:** flagged, not yet evaluated.
- **Report reviewed - no policy violation:** Google found nothing to remove. You get a one-time appeal.
- **Escalated - check your email for updates:** the appeal was escalated and the final decision comes by email.

To appeal, open the Reviews Management Tool, choose **Check the status of a review I reported previously and appeal options**, then **Appeal eligible reviews**. You can select up to 10 reviews per appeal. If the review violates policy it is removed; if not, it stays.

Reviews that Google removes no longer appear on Maps and Search. Some sites claim specific removal success rates by industry. Google publishes none, so treat those figures as unsupported.

## Should You Reply to a Fake Review?

Yes, briefly. Google lets verified owners reply publicly, and the reply shows as "your business replied". The reviewer is notified and can edit their review afterwards. Google says replies usually take up to 10 minutes to be reviewed, and sometimes up to 30 days.

Keep the reply short, factual and written for the next reader, not the reviewer. A neutral template:

> We cannot find a record of this visit or order under this name. Please contact us at [phone or email] with the date and details so we can look into it.

Do not accuse the reviewer of lying, name staff, or post personal details. Delete or edit a reply later from **Read reviews** if needed.

## What Do Google's 2026 Protections Change for Owners?

In April 2026 Google reported that it blocked or removed over 292 million policy-violating reviews and removed over 13 million fake Business Profiles in 2025. It did not break the figure down by country or business type.

It also described a new response to sudden spam spikes: remove the fake content, pause new reviews on the profile, alert the owner, and show consumers a banner explaining the pause. If you see this, do not buy reviews to fill the gap. That would be fake engagement itself.

Google also says its automated spam filter occasionally removes legitimate reviews. If a real customer's review disappears, contact Business Profile support through the help page.

## What Does the FTC Rule Mean for Your Review Strategy?

The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule went into effect on October 21, 2024. It bans fake or AI-generated reviews, buying or selling them, paying for reviews conditioned on sentiment, and undisclosed insider reviews. It also bans review suppression through groundless legal threats, intimidation or false public accusations.

In December 2025 the FTC sent warning letters to 10 companies and stated that civil penalties can reach $53,088 per violation. Letters are not formal findings of violation.

Practical effects: do not write reviews for your own business, do not pay for positive ones, and do not threaten a real customer to remove a review. If family or staff review you, the FTC says they should clearly disclose the relationship, though Google's conflict-of-interest policy may still remove those reviews.

## How Do You Outweigh Fake Reviews That Stay?

Dilution works because a rating is an average. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses and 74% only care about reviews from the last three months, so fresh genuine reviews matter more than old ones.

**Worked example (all inputs are assumptions):** a salon has 40 genuine reviews averaging 4.8 stars, a total of 192 stars. Three fake one-star reviews arrive: 195 stars over 43 reviews gives 4.53, displayed as 4.5. If the salon then collects 20 more genuine five-star reviews, the total is 295 over 63, or 4.68, displayed as 4.7. The fake reviews are still there, but they now move the average far less.

Ask every customer for a review at the point of service with Google's review link or QR code. Never offer a discount or gift for a review; Google's policy lists incentives as a violation. ## What if the Review Is Part of an Extortion Attempt?

Some scammers post fake one-star reviews, then demand payment to remove them. Google's help page points to separate guidance for this case. Do not pay. Screenshot the review, the message and the sender, flag the review, and report the demand to Google Business Profile support.

Reputation firms that promise guaranteed removal are making a claim Google does not support, since only Google (or the reviewer) can remove a review. Check any such promise against Google's policy pages before paying.

## How Do You Monitor Your Profile for New Fakes?

Turn on review notifications in your Business Profile and check **Read reviews** weekly. Look for patterns: several new one-star reviews in a day, reviewers with no other activity, or text about a service you do not offer. Log each report with the date, the reviewer name and the policy reason, so an appeal has a record.

## FAQ

### Can I delete a Google review myself?

No. Google's help says you can flag a review you believe violates policy. Only Google or the reviewer can remove it. You can delete your own reply.

### Will Google remove a one-star review with no text?

Not automatically. A rating without text is not a policy violation by itself. Report it only if you can tie it to a policy, for example a reviewer with a conflict of interest.

### How many times can I appeal?

Google's help describes a one-time appeal for a report that was reviewed with no violation found. You can select up to 10 reviews in one appeal.

### Is it illegal to post fake reviews in the US?

The FTC rule bans fake reviews and sets civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation, and courts apply it to knowing violations. This is a summary, not legal advice.

### Should I ask the reviewer to remove the review?

If the customer is real, a polite reply and a direct contact route can resolve it, and the reviewer can edit the review. Never pay or pressure them. The FTC rule bans suppression through groundless threats.

### Does Google tell me who wrote a fake review?

Google shows only the public reviewer name and profile. It does not give owners private account details.
