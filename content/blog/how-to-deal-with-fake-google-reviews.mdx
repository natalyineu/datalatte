To deal with a fake Google review, report it from your Business Profile. Open Read reviews, select Report next to the review, choose a reason, and select Send report. Then check its status in the Reviews Management Tool and submit a one-time appeal if Google finds no policy violation. Google removes only reviews that break its content policies, so a harsh but genuine review stays up. Reply publicly in the meantime, and collect fresh genuine reviews to dilute what remains.

This guide is for local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. The reporting steps come from Google's Business Profile help pages. The UK fake review rules come from the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA). Where a source does not give a figure, this article says so.

## What Counts as a Fake Google Review?

Google's rule is that only reviews that violate its content policies are removed. A fake review is one that breaks those policies, such as a review written for payment or one posted about a business the reviewer never used. The CMA states that posting or commissioning a fake review is explicitly banned under UK law, and that businesses must take effective steps to prevent and remove fake reviews and hidden advertising. ([CMA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-important-changes-from-google-to-tackle-fake-reviews))

Google's help page is clear about what not to report. You can report any review, but only those that violate policy are eligible for removal. Do not report a review just because you disagree with it or dislike it. Negative reviews from real customers can highlight areas for improvement. ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en-GB))

Fake reviews are not always posted by competitors. A Guardian reporter who was offered paid review work by a Telegram recruiter found that the offers arrived regularly and promised payment in cryptocurrency. The article is a secondary account, but it shows why owners should expect contact from review-for-pay operations. ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/mar/27/paid-write-fake-google-reviews-scammer-cryptocurrency))

## How Do You Report a Fake Google Review?

Google documents two routes. Both need a verified Business Profile. The first route is the fastest:

1. Go to your Business Profile and select **Read reviews**.
2. Next to the review, select **Report**.
3. Select the reason for flagging it, such as **Spam** or **Profanity**.
4. Select **Send report**.

The second route is the Reviews Management Tool. Choose your business, select **Continue**, then **Report a new review for removal**, and then **Report** next to the review. It also lets you check the status of earlier reports. ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en-GB))

Choose the reason that matches the actual problem. Keep any evidence, such as proof that a reviewer was never a customer, because it helps if an appeal is needed.

## What Happens After You Report, and What If Google Says No?

Google says review evaluation "typically takes several days" and does not publish a guaranteed time or removal rate. Each report moves through three statuses in the Reviews Management Tool:

- **Decision pending:** flagged, not yet evaluated.
- **Report reviewed, no policy violation:** Google found nothing to remove, and you can submit a one-time appeal.
- **Escalated, check your email for updates:** the appeal was escalated, and the final decision comes by email.

To appeal, open the Reviews Management Tool, select **Check the status of a review I reported previously and appeal options**, then **Appeal eligible reviews**. You can select up to 10 reviews per appeal. If the review violates policy, it is removed. If it complies, it stays live. ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en-GB))

## Should You Reply to a Fake Review?

Yes, briefly. Google's guidance says that replying to customer reviews shows you value their feedback, and that helpful replies can help a business stand out. ([Google local ranking tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)) Your reply is for the next reader, not for the reviewer. Keep it factual and calm:

> We cannot find a record of this visit or order under this name. Please contact us at [phone or email] with the date and details so we can look into it.

Do not accuse the reviewer of lying, name staff, or share personal details. A reply cannot remove a review, so treat it as a signal to future customers that the business responds.

## Which Response Fits Which Situation?

Each option does a different job. Use this table to pick the right one before acting.

| Option | Use it when | What it needs | What it can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flag the review | The review breaks Google's content policies | Verified Business Profile | Google removes it if a violation is confirmed |
| One-time appeal | Google reviewed the report and found no violation | Status page in the Reviews Management Tool | Removal if Google reverses its decision, otherwise the review stays |
| Public reply | Any review you cannot remove | Verified owner account | Shows future readers you respond; does not remove the review |
| Ask genuine customers | You need to outweigh a fake review | A simple request at the point of service | Adds fresh genuine reviews to the average |
| Offer a reward for reviews | Never | Nothing | Counts as an incentive for a review, which the CMA undertakings cover |

The last row matters. The CMA says the undertakings cover incentives, meaning a payment or reward in exchange for a positive review, whether offered in person or online. ([CMA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-important-changes-from-google-to-tackle-fake-reviews))

## What Do the CMA Rules Mean for Your Business?

The CMA secured changes from Google after investigating whether it was doing enough to detect fake reviews. Under the undertakings, businesses found boosting star ratings with fake reviews can have a prominent warning added to their profile, and their review function can be deactivated. Firms that repeatedly engage in fake review activity can have all their reviews deleted for 6 months or more. ([CMA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-important-changes-from-google-to-tackle-fake-reviews))

The CMA also says it can fine firms that break consumer law up to 10% of their global turnover under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024. For a small business, the practical rule is simple: do not write reviews for yourself, do not pay for them, and do not threaten customers over a review. ([CMA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-important-changes-from-google-to-tackle-fake-reviews))

## How Do You Outweigh Fake Reviews That Stay?

A rating is an average, so fresh genuine reviews dilute a few fakes. The example below uses assumed inputs to show the effect.

**Worked example (all inputs are assumptions):** a salon has 40 genuine reviews averaging 4.8 stars, a total of 192 stars. Three fake one-star reviews arrive, bringing the total to 195 stars over 43 reviews, which is 4.53 and displays as 4.5. If the salon then collects 20 genuine five-star reviews, the total becomes 295 stars over 63 reviews, which is 4.68 and displays as 4.7. The fakes are still live, but they move the average far less.

Ask every customer for a review at the point of service, using the Google review link or a QR code. Do not offer discounts or gifts for a review. ## What if Someone Demands Payment to Remove a Review?

Some bad actors post negative reviews and then demand payment to remove them. Google's help page points to separate guidance for this case, so use the extortion reporting route described there. ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en-GB))

Do not pay. Save screenshots of the review, the demand and the sender details. Flag the review, and report the incident to Google. Be wary of firms that promise guaranteed removal, because only Google can remove a review that breaks its policies.

## How Do You Keep Track of New Fake Reviews?

Check **Read reviews** on a fixed schedule, such as once a week. Look for clusters of new one-star reviews, reviewers with no other activity, and text describing a service the business does not offer. Log each report with the date, the reviewer name and the reason chosen. That record makes an appeal easier to support.

If review volume is high, [AI marketing tools for small business](/blog/ai-marketing-tools-for-small-business) can help monitor reviews and draft replies, but a person should still decide what to flag.

## FAQ

### Can I delete a Google review myself?

No. You can flag a review that you believe violates policy, and Google decides whether to remove it. You can edit or delete your own reply.

### Will Google remove a one-star review with no text?

Google's help says only reviews that violate its policies are eligible for removal, and that you should not report a review just because you dislike it. Report it only if you can tie it to a specific violation.

### How many times can I appeal?

Google's help describes a one-time appeal for a report that was reviewed with no violation found. You can select up to 10 reviews in a single appeal.

### Does Google tell me who wrote a fake review?

Google's help pages do not describe giving owners private reviewer details. Use the public reviewer name and the content of the review when you report it.

### Should I ask a real customer to remove a review?

You can ask a customer if the review is genuine and you want to resolve the issue. Do not pay or pressure them. The CMA undertakings cover incentives, and the CMA says fake reviews are banned.
