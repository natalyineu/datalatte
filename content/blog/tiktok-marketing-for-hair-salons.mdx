Hair salons attract new clients on TikTok by posting short, real videos of transformations, techniques and the salon experience, then making it easy for local viewers to book. You do not need a studio or a big following; you need consistent content that shows your work and a clear link to your booking page.

TikTok suits hairdressing because the product is visual. A cut, colour or styling change is a built-in story. The challenge for a local salon is that TikTok reaches people far beyond your town, so your content should combine broad appeal with local signals.

## Set up your account properly

Before filming, take care of the basics.

1. **Switch to a business account** to get a category, a website link and analytics. Availability of some features differs by country.
2. **Use a clear username** that matches your salon name and other social handles.
3. **Write a bio** that says what you do and where: "Colour specialists in [town]. Book below."
4. **Add your booking link** to the profile. Eligibility for the link depends on account type, so check current TikTok options.
5. **Use a clear profile photo,** such as your logo or a friendly team image.

Also link your TikTok from your website and your Google Business Profile, so people can find you from other places.

## Content ideas that suit a salon

Variety keeps people watching. Mix these formats:

- **Transformations.** The classic before-and-after, filmed in the same lighting and angle.
- **Process clips.** Foils going in, a cutting technique, blending a colour, a blow-dry in 20 seconds.
- **Day in the life.** A morning set-up, a busy Saturday, a staff introduction.
- **Answers to client questions.** "How often should I get a trim?", "What is the difference between balayage and highlights?", "How do I keep my colour from fading?"
- **Myth-busting.** Short corrections of common hair advice.
- **Product use.** How to apply a treatment, how to style at home.
- **Behind the scenes.** Meet the team, a staff in-joke, a team member's first day.
- **Client reactions,** with permission.

Aim for a mix of educational and entertaining, with only an occasional direct promotion.

## Get permission for client videos

Always ask before filming or posting a client. Many salons use a short consent line on booking or at the chair: "We sometimes film results for social media. Are you happy to be included?" Offer the option to blur faces, film only hair from behind or decline entirely.

Keep a note of who agreed. Honour any request to remove a video. For more on handling photos responsibly, see [hair salon before-and-after photos](/blog/hair-salon-before-after-photos-legal-and-converting).

## Filming tips that need no special gear

- **Use your phone,** held vertically.
- **Find good light.** Natural light near a window or a ring light helps hair colour show accurately.
- **Keep it steady.** A small tripod or phone stand makes a difference.
- **Hook in the first two seconds.** Start with the result or an intriguing line, such as "This is what grey blending looks like after one visit."
- **Keep it short.** Many strong salon videos run between 10 and 40 seconds.
- **Add captions.** Many people watch without sound.
- **Show the whole result.** Finish with a clear final shot, front and back where possible.

## Sound, trends and music

Trending sounds can help videos reach more people, but business accounts are typically limited to a library of commercially licensed music, so check which sounds are available to you. Using unlicensed music in business content can lead to muted videos or takedowns.

Use trends when they fit your style and are quick to adapt. Skip those that feel forced. A simple, honest, well-filmed video usually beats a complicated trend.

## Add local signals

Because TikTok shows content widely, make it clear who you serve:

- **Mention your town or neighbourhood** in captions, on-screen text and speech.
- **Add your location** where the platform allows.
- **Use a small set of relevant hashtags,** including a local one such as a town name, plus topic tags like #balayage or #curlyhair.
- **Reference local places or events** where natural.

Do not rely on hashtags alone. Clear captions, spoken words and on-screen text help TikTok understand what a video is about.

## Build a posting routine

Consistency matters more than perfection. A realistic plan for a busy salon:

- **Two to four videos per week.**
- **One filming session,** such as 45 minutes on a quiet morning, to capture several clips.
- **A simple content list** of 10 to 15 ideas you can draw from.
- **Five minutes a day** replying to comments and messages.

Delegate where you can. A team member who enjoys filming can take this on, and a small stipend or time allowance is fair. Our [AI content calendar guide](/blog/ai-content-calendar-for-small-business) can help you plan.

## Turn views into bookings

Views alone do not pay the rent. Plan the path:

1. **Say what to do next** in the caption or on screen: "Book via the link in our bio".
2. **Keep your booking page simple** and mobile friendly.
3. **Offer an easy first step,** such as a consultation, a patch test booking or a new-client slot.
4. **Ask new clients how they found you,** so you can see what TikTok contributes.
5. **Reply to every comment and DM** quickly. Booking questions often begin there.

Pin your best video, such as a standout transformation, so new visitors land on it.

## Track results sensibly

TikTok analytics show views, watch time, followers and traffic. For a salon, the most useful measures are:

- Which videos led to profile visits and booking-link clicks.
- Which content types keep people watching.
- New clients who mention TikTok.

Do not chase follower counts. A smaller, local audience that books is better than a large audience elsewhere. Review monthly and repeat the formats that work.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a big following to get clients from TikTok?**

A: No. Videos can reach people who do not follow you. A smaller audience that sees your work and lives nearby can still book.

**Q: How often should a salon post?**

A: Two to four times a week is realistic for many salons. Consistency matters more than volume.

**Q: Is TikTok worth it for a local salon?**

A: It can be, particularly for colour, curls and transformations. Results vary by market, so test for a few months and track new-client mentions.

**Q: Can I use trending music?**

A: Business accounts are typically restricted to licensed commercial music. Check what is available to your account and avoid unlicensed tracks.
