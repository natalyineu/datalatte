Pew Research Center's 2026 roundup on Americans and TikTok found that 63% of US adults under 30 and 44% of those aged 30 to 49 use the app. Those are the ages that book colour, cuts and extensions. So the question for a salon is not whether your clients are on TikTok, but whether a few hours a month of filming can bring in enough new clients to be worth it. In the maths later in this article, it takes one extra regular client a month to cover the time. Every input there is my assumption, so swap in yours.

This guide is for salon owners and stylists in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers setting up, what to film, consent for client videos, music rules for business accounts, local signals, a weekly routine and how to measure whether it works.

## Why It Can Work for a Local Salon

A haircut is a visual product, and a good transformation tells its own story in 15 seconds. The harder problem is locality. TikTok shows videos to people far from your chair, so most views will not turn into bookings. That is why your plan needs two jobs: videos that get watched, and local signals plus an easy booking path that turn the nearby viewers into appointments.

On engagement, Socialinsider's [TikTok benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/tiktok) (about 2 million videos from roughly 214,500 profiles, January 2024 to December 2025) put the 2025 engagement rate by views at 4.40% for accounts with 1,000 to 5,000 followers and 3.90% for accounts with 10,000 to 50,000. Their formula is likes, comments, shares and saves divided by views. The point for a small salon: smaller accounts are not at a disadvantage on engagement, so you do not need a big following before you start. Engagement is not bookings, though, and Socialinsider does not measure bookings.

## Set Up the Account

1. **Switch to a business account.** Business accounts add a category and analytics. Features such as a website link depend on account type, follower count and region, so check what the Edit Profile screen offers you rather than relying on a tutorial.
2. **Match your handle** to your salon name and your Instagram.
3. **Write a bio that says what and where:** "Colour specialists in [town]. Booking link below."
4. **Put your booking link in the profile** if your app lets you, and point it at a mobile-friendly page with online booking, not your home page.
5. **Link back from your site and your Google Business Profile** so people can find you from elsewhere. Our [TikTok for business account guide](/blog/tiktok-for-business-account-guide) goes through the options.

## Music: Business Accounts Are Restricted

Most salon owners hit this on day one. TikTok's [Commercial Music Library page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library) describes a pre-cleared catalogue of 1 million songs and says businesses cannot use the general music library for commercial usage. It states this covers organic posts as well as ads and branded content. Trending pop tracks are usually not in that catalogue.

What to do: pick sounds from the Commercial Music Library, or use original audio such as your own voice. A stylist talking through a technique often outperforms a trending song anyway, because the viewer learns something.

## Content That Suits a Salon

Mix formats so the feed does not feel like one long advert:

- **Transformations.** Before and after, same light and angle, with the result shown from the front and back.
- **Process clips.** Foils going in, a razor cut, a blow-dry shown in 20 seconds.
- **Answers.** "How often should I trim?", "Balayage or highlights?", "Why does my toner fade?" Film yourself answering one question per video.
- **Myth-busting.** A quick correction of bad hair advice.
- **Behind the scenes.** The Saturday rush, the team, the colour bar.
- **Aftercare.** Wash, heat and product tips for the style you just did.

Aim for mostly useful or entertaining, with an occasional direct offer. Our [before-and-after content strategy](/blog/hair-salon-tiktok-strategy-before-after-content) goes deeper on transformations.

## Get Consent Before You Film or Post Clients

Always ask. Consent has to be specific, and ideally written down. The UK Information Commissioner's Office, in its [guidance on valid consent](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/consent/what-is-valid-consent/), says consent must be unbundled from other terms, specific and informed, easy to withdraw, and recorded so you can demonstrate it. A recognisable client video will generally count as personal data in the UK, so this applies directly there.

A practical salon version for any country:

- A separate tick box on your booking form or consultation card: "I am happy for [Salon] to film my appointment and share it on social media."
- Offer a lesser option: hair only, or face blurred.
- Say what happens to the video and how to ask for removal. Keep a record of who agreed.
- Treat paid ads as a separate use. A client who is fine with an organic post may not be fine with their face in an advert.
- Remove videos on request, promptly.

Laws on using someone's likeness differ by US state and by country, so have a local adviser review your form. Our [before-and-after photo guide](/blog/hair-salon-before-after-photos-legal-and-converting) covers model releases for photos; in the UK, remember the ICO's point that people must be able to withdraw consent easily and at any time.

## Filming Without Special Gear

- Use your phone, held vertically, on a cheap tripod.
- Film near a window, or use a ring light, so colour shows accurately.
- Open with the result or a question in the first two seconds.
- Keep most videos short. Ten to forty seconds is a sensible range to test (my suggestion, not a TikTok rule).
- Add captions, since many people watch without sound.
- Film one 45-minute session on a quiet morning and bank several clips.

## Make It Local

Because views come from everywhere, tell viewers who you serve:

- Say your town or neighbourhood out loud, in on-screen text and in the caption.
- Add a location to the post if your app offers the option.
- Use a few topic tags (#balayage, #curlyhair) plus one local tag.
- Mention the area naturally: "if you are in [neighbourhood], we have a Thursday slot."

Do not lean only on hashtags. Spoken words, captions and on-screen text give TikTok more to work with.

## Turn Viewers Into Bookings

Views do not pay rent. Build the path:

1. Say the next step in the video: "New-client slots are at the link in my profile."
2. Pin your best transformation so new visitors land on it.
3. Offer a simple first booking, such as a consultation or new-client appointment.
4. Reply to comments and direct messages the same day. Booking questions often start there.
5. Ask every new client how they found you and log it. TikTok will not tell you who booked.

Pair this with an Instagram presence; the same clips can be reposted. See our salon Instagram bookings guide.

## The Math

You are investing time, not ad budget, so cost it. Every number is an **assumption** for illustration; replace them with your own.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Videos per month | 12 | 12 | 12 |
| Filming (4 sessions x 45 min) | 3 h | 3 h | 3 h |
| Editing and posting (12 x 20 min) | 4 h | 4 h | 4 h |
| Time cost at $25/h | $175 | $175 | $175 |
| New clients from TikTok per month | 0 | 2 | 6 |
| First-year value per client (4 visits x $120) | $480 | $480 | $480 |
| Revenue in the first year | $0 | $960 | $2,880 |
| Contribution margin on that revenue | 50% | 50% | 50% |
| Profit from new clients | $0 | $480 | $1,440 |
| Net after time cost | -$175 | +$305 | +$1,265 |

Break-even: time cost divided by profit per client = $175 / ($480 x 0.5) = 0.73, so about one new regular client a month covers the effort on these assumptions. The Tough column is not a failure of TikTok so much as a salon that posted, got views but no bookings. If you reach month three with zero clients mentioning TikTok, change the offer, the booking path or the local signals before you change the video style.

Later, if a particular video is working, you can promote it to a local radius. Test that with a small budget and compare cost per booking with your other channels; see [TikTok ads for hair salons](/blog/tiktok-ads-for-hair-salons) and our guide to [organic vs paid TikTok](/blog/tiktok-organic-vs-paid-strategy).

Do not judge TikTok by views. Judge it by the answer to "How did you hear about us?" and by booking-link clicks. Ten thousand views from another continent is a nice story, not a client.

## A Weekly Plan to Copy

- **Monday:** a transformation, with consent on file.
- **Wednesday:** a 20-second answer to a client question.
- **Friday:** a behind-the-scenes clip from your busiest day.
- **Weekend:** an aftercare tip for the style you just did.

Film all four in one session, edit on your phone and schedule or post across the week. After four weeks, repeat the two formats that earned the most profile visits and link clicks.

## Do This Today

1. Add a social-media consent tick box to your booking form or consultation card, with a hair-only option.
2. Switch to a business account, write a bio with your town in it and check which sounds you can use from the Commercial Music Library.
3. Make a list of 12 videos: four transformations, four questions clients ask, two behind-the-scenes, two aftercare tips.
4. Book one 45-minute filming session this week and film four clips.
5. Add "How did you hear about us?" to your booking form and tally the answers monthly against your time cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a big following to get clients from TikTok?**
A: No. Videos can reach people who do not follow you, and Socialinsider's benchmark data shows accounts with 1,000 to 5,000 followers had engagement rates at least as high as larger accounts in 2025. Local bookings depend on nearby viewers seeing your work.

**Q: How often should a salon post?**
A: Two to four videos a week is a realistic pace for a busy salon, my suggestion rather than a TikTok requirement. The consistency matters more than volume.

**Q: Can I use trending songs?**
A: TikTok says businesses cannot use the general music library for commercial usage and should use the Commercial Music Library, which covers organic posts too. Use that or your own audio.

**Q: Do I need written consent from clients?**
A: Ask first, keep a record and make it easy to withdraw. In the UK, the ICO says consent must be specific, unbundled and recorded. Laws elsewhere vary, so have a local adviser check your form.

**Q: Is TikTok better than Instagram for a salon?**
A: Not necessarily. TikTok can reach new people; Instagram is often where existing clients already follow you. Many salons film once and post to both.
