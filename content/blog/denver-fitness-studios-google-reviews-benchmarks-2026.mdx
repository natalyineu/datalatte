Denver fitness studio review benchmarks are easy to quote and hard to verify. This guide does not publish a Denver median, because a citable figure needs a dated count of live Google Maps results for the same search terms. What it offers instead is published guidance on how review volume, timing and profile quality work, a method to count your local competitors yourself, and a worked example that shows how long a review gap takes to close. Treat the figures here as planning inputs, not as a citywide average.

## Why does review volume matter in Denver search?

Google Business Profile is a free tool that displays business hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos directly in search results ([How to Make Your Fitness Business Stand Out on Google, Association of Fitness Studios](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google), secondary). The Google Map Pack lists relevant local businesses for searches such as "personal training studios near me", so a studio that is missing from that group for its core searches is harder to find before a prospect ever reaches a website.

## How do you benchmark Denver competitors yourself?

Use a short, repeatable count instead of a citywide average you cannot date. Allow about 15 minutes.

1. Search the terms your members actually use, such as "yoga studio near me", "Denver pilates studio" and "HIIT gym Denver", in a private browser window.
2. Note the studios that appear in the first results and record each one's review count and rating, with today's date.
3. Repeat for at least three search terms, because the set of studios changes with wording.
4. Calculate the median review count across the list, and count how many studios sit under 50 and over 200.
5. Save the sheet and repeat it every 90 days to see whether the gap is closing.

Your sheet becomes a local benchmark that is dated, specific to your neighbourhood and easy to check. It is more useful than any published figure that does not show when it was collected.

## What does a review gap look like in numbers?

The example below uses assumed inputs, so you can replace them with your own figures.

- Current reviews: 40 (assumption)
- Target reviews: 100 (assumption)
- Members asked for a review each month: 60 (assumption)
- Share who leave a review after a direct ask: 15% (assumption)

Calculation: 60 asks × 15% = 9 new reviews a month. The gap is 100 − 40 = 60 reviews. 60 ÷ 9 ≈ 6.7, so about 7 months.

If only 7.5% respond, the rate drops to 4.5 reviews a month. The same gap then takes about 13 months, which sits at the upper end of the 6 to 12 month range published by Mariana Tek. Response rate matters more than most owners expect, so measure it each month.

## How should you ask for reviews without breaking the rules?

Ask after a positive moment, such as a member's milestone, a completed challenge or a strong class, rather than at sign-up when nobody has an experience to describe yet. Use one direct review link, shown as a QR code at the front desk and in the post-class message.

Do not offer free classes, merchandise or prize draws in exchange for reviews, and do not select only the happiest members to ask. Check Google's current Business Profile review policy in its help centre before you launch, because the rules can change.

Speed of follow-up also matters for trial guests. Mariana Tek advises following up within 24 hours with an automated welcome sequence after a pop-up, since interest fades quickly ([Group Fitness Studio Marketing Strategies, Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). The same timing logic suits a review request sent to trial guests. The [email marketing guide for fitness studios](/blog/email-marketing-fitness-studio) covers sequence setup.

## Does the profile matter as much as the review count?

Reviews are only one part of what a prospect sees. Vertical Impression recommends uploading at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes in action and staff ([Gym and Fitness Studio Marketing Guide, Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026)). Photos show the room a new member will walk into, which supports the reviews that describe it.

Many studios still search for the old name. Google changed Google My Business to Google Business Profile in 2021, along with a few subtle feature changes ([Boost Your Pilates Studio's Visibility with Google Business Profile, Pilates Education Institute](https://www.pilateseducationinstitute.com/optimizing-your-pilates-studios-local-listing-and-google-business-profile/), secondary). The field-by-field checklist in the [fitness studio Google Business Profile guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization) covers category, hours, booking link and photos.

## How much should a studio budget around reviews and ads?

Many boutique studios budget between 5 and 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing ([Group Fitness Studio Marketing Strategies, Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/)). That is a broad planning range, so use it as a starting point for your own numbers.

For paid search, Vertical Impression gives a benchmark of $600–$1,200 a month for Google Ads at a small independent studio with under 200 members. ## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a Denver fitness studio need?

No verified Denver median is published here. Count the studios that appear for your core searches using the method above, and use that dated list as your benchmark. Published guidance suggests building a review base typically takes 6 to 12 months.

### Do more reviews help a studio appear in local results?

Reviews are visible signals that prospects weigh when they choose a studio, and they appear alongside your listing in search results. The Map Pack lists relevant local businesses for searches such as "personal training studios near me", so consistent, recent reviews support your visibility there.

### Can I ask members for reviews?

Yes, asking is normal practice. Offering rewards in exchange for reviews, or only asking people you expect to be positive, goes against Google's policy. Check the current Business Profile policy before you start.

### How much should a small studio spend on marketing?

Mariana Tek reports that many boutique studios budget 5 to 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing. For paid search, Vertical Impression gives a $600–$1,200 monthly benchmark for a small independent studio with under 200 members.
