The typical Denver fitness studio on Google Maps has just **72 reviews**, and **44 of the 113 studios we analysed (about 39%) have fewer than 50**. At the same time the average rating is a very strong **4.87 stars**. That combination is an opportunity: ratings are high everywhere, but review volume is thin, so a studio that earns reviews steadily can stand out faster than a coffee shop or salon in the same city.

## The headline numbers

Snapshot for Denver fitness studios, collected in October 2026:

- **113** fitness studios analysed
- **4.87** average Google rating
- **72** median number of reviews
- **44** studios with fewer than 50 reviews (about 39%)
- **11** studios with 200 or more reviews (about 10%)

As always, "analysed" means the studios our Google Maps searches returned for Denver. It is a sample of what a customer would see, not a complete census of every gym, boutique studio, trainer or class provider in the metro area.

## Why so many studios under 50 is an opportunity

Compare fitness with the other Denver niches in our data. Only 2 of 177 coffee shops and 2 of 84 hair salons analysed had fewer than 50 reviews. For fitness studios it is 44 of 113. Customers choose a studio less often than a coffee, and they are not in the habit of reviewing, so many studios simply have not built a review routine.

That matters for three reasons.

**The bar is lower.** A studio with 100 reviews is above the Denver median of 72. In coffee, 100 reviews would be well below the median of 340. You can reach "typical" for your niche far faster.

**Ratings do not separate you.** When the average is 4.87, almost everyone looks great on stars alone. What can differentiate you is how many people have vouched for you and how recently, along with what they say.

**Most studios are not doing this consistently.** If 39% of analysed studios have fewer than 50 reviews, a repeatable system gives you an edge over a large share of your competitors.

A caution: this is an opening, not a guarantee. Reviews are one input in how people choose and how Google orders results. Class schedule, location, price, trial offers and photos matter too.

## What the numbers mean for a new studio

**Your target is realistic.** Crossing 50 reviews moves you out of the under-50 group, which holds about two in five analysed studios. Reaching around 72 puts you at the Denver median. For a studio with an engaged member base, those are achievable goals within months, not years.

**Ask at the moments of success.** The best time to ask is right after something good: a member's first milestone, finishing a challenge, a great class. Avoid asking at sign-up. Nobody has anything to say yet.

**Collect from trial and drop-in guests too.** Trial classes are a goldmine. A first-time visitor who had a welcoming experience can describe exactly what a prospect wants to hear: "I was nervous, and the coach made me feel at ease."

**Make your profile complete.** Class types, photos of the space, coach introductions, the booking link and a clear category all help. See our [fitness studio Google Business Profile guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization) for a field-by-field checklist.

## What the numbers mean for an established studio

**If you are below 50 reviews with years of members,** you are leaving credibility on the table. Your members already love you. The gap is the ask, not the experience.

**If you are above the median,** you are in the top half. Only 11 of 113 analysed studios (about 10%) have 200 or more reviews, so you do not need to reach that level to be competitive. Focus on recency and responses.

**Mind the rating.** At 4.87, one or two low ratings are visible. Read them for patterns (class size, cancellation rules, billing, cleanliness) and fix what is fixable before replying.

**Reply to everything,** praise included. Personal replies show a studio community is alive. For ideas on tone and automation, see our guide to [AI-assisted review management](/blog/ai-review-management).

## A 30-day review and Google Business Profile plan

About 30 to 45 minutes per week, plus a few seconds after classes.

### Week 1: Set up and audit

1. **Verify your profile** and make sure name, address, phone and website match your signage and booking page.
2. **Set the primary category** that fits you (for example, fitness center or yoga studio) and add accurate secondary ones.
3. **Add your booking or trial link** and describe your classes and services in plain language.
4. **Upload photos**: entrance, studio floor, equipment, coaches, a typical class (with member consent).
5. **Count your current reviews** and write down the number. You are tracking from here.

### Week 2: Create an easy path to a review

1. **Make a direct review link and QR code.**
2. **Place it** at the front desk, by the exit, in class wrap-up slides and in your post-class email or app message.
3. **Ask coaches to mention it** once per class cycle, briefly. Keep it voluntary.
4. **Do not incentivise** with free classes, merchandise or prize draws in exchange for reviews, and do not filter who you ask. Both violate Google's policies.
5. **Reply to existing reviews**, starting with any unanswered ones.

### Week 3: Reach the quiet majority

1. **Send a short thank-you message** to members who hit a milestone, with the review link included once and without pressure.
2. **Message recent trial clients** who attended in the past 30 days.
3. **Ask coaches to share** what members say in person: "That means a lot, would you mind putting it in a review?"
4. **Post an update** on your profile about a new class or schedule.

### Week 4: Measure and plan the next 30 days

1. **Count new reviews** against your Week 1 number.
2. **Compare with the benchmarks:** how far are you from 50, and from the Denver median of 72?
3. **Check Performance** for calls, direction requests and website clicks.
4. **Keep the habit.** Pick the channel that produced the most reviews and double down. Our [Local SEO Grader](/tools/local-seo-grader) can show what else is missing on your listing.

## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a Denver fitness studio need?

In our October 2026 sample the median Denver studio had 72 reviews, and about 39% had fewer than 50. Passing 50 and then approaching the 72 median are sensible first milestones. There is no official number you must hit.

### What is a good rating for a fitness studio in Denver?

The average across 113 analysed studios was 4.87. Anything near that is normal. Because so many studios are rated highly, review count and recency are what separate listings.

### Why do fitness studios have fewer reviews than coffee shops?

Our data shows the difference, but not the reason. A plausible explanation is that studios have fewer, more recurring customers and rarely prompt them. That is a hypothesis rather than something our data proves.

### Do more reviews help me rank higher?

Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence, and review information is part of prominence. The weighting is not public. Reviews also help customers decide, which is why they are worth earning properly.

### Is it okay to ask members for reviews?

Yes, asking is allowed. Paying or rewarding for reviews, or only asking people likely to be positive, is not.

### What should I say in a review request?

Keep it short and specific, for example "If you enjoyed class today, a quick Google review about your experience would really help new people find us." Do not script what members should say.

## Methodology note

- **Source:** our own collection from Google Maps search results for fitness studios in Denver, Colorado.
- **When:** October 2026. We plan to refresh the data and will update the date when we do.
- **Sample:** 113 fitness studios analysed. This is the set of listings our searches returned, not a count of every gym or studio in the city.
- **Metrics:** average rating is the mean of listed Google ratings; median reviews is the middle review count; "under 50" and "200+" count studios with fewer than 50 or at least 200 reviews. The 50 to 199 group and all percentages are calculated from these counts and rounded.
- **Limits:** the category "fitness studio" is broad and results vary by search term and location. Review counts change daily. These numbers describe our sample on the day we collected it.
