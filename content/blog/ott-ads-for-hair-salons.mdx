For most hair salons and barbershops, OTT streaming ads are a second step, not a first one. They are bought by the thousand views, with market prices of roughly $15 to $45 per 1,000 impressions in 2026 according to an AdWave benchmark summary, and a salon needs a tight local radius to avoid paying for viewers who live too far to book. Google search ads, at a $4.62 average click in WordStream's Beauty and Personal Care category, usually reach people who already want a haircut.

OTT can still make sense when you have a strong visual offer such as colour transformations, a booked-out search budget and several chairs to fill. This guide explains how it is priced, what to check before buying, and a worked example.

## What Are OTT Ads, and How Are They Bought?

OTT means video ads inside streaming apps delivered over the internet. Most buys are priced by CPM, the cost per 1,000 impressions. AdWave's July 2026 summary lists free ad-supported services such as Tubi, Pluto and Roku Channel at $15 to $25, and premium services such as Hulu, Peacock and Prime Video at $25 to $45. These are unnamed industry benchmarks, and AdWave sells CTV ads, so treat them as a guide, not a price list.

Our [OTT advertising guide for local business](/blog/ott-advertising-local-business) covers buying routes in more detail, and [CTV vs OTT explained](/blog/ctv-vs-ott-advertising-explained) clears up the terms.

## Can a Salon Target Only Its Own Neighbourhood?

It depends on the platform, and you must check before paying. Amazon's streaming TV ads guide describes content-interest and in-market audiences, says there is no minimum spend, and suggests small and medium businesses start near $150 a day. The same page does not mention location targeting. A salon that cannot limit delivery to nearby zip codes pays for views from people who will never book.

Ask any seller three questions: can I target by zip code or radius, what is the minimum spend, and can I see where impressions were delivered? If the answers are vague, pick another channel.

## OTT or Google: What Does the Salon Gain?

Google search reaches people at the moment they want a service. WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks give Beauty and Personal Care a $4.62 average CPC, a 10.35% conversion rate and $39.25 cost per lead. OTT is a reach tool: a viewer sees a 15 or 30 second ad while watching something else, and it will not produce a lead cost like that on its own.

## What Should the Ad Show?

Use the work itself: a short transformation, a stylist introduction, a clear booking route at the end. Amazon says its streaming ads run 6 to 45 seconds and appear in a non-skippable environment, so the first seconds must make the point. Put your salon name, area and one booking action on the end card.

Do not promise results you cannot support, and get the client's permission before showing their face. If you cannot make video yourself, [Instagram ads](/blog/hair-salon-instagram-ads) can use the same footage at lower cost.

## FAQ

### Are OTT ads worth it for a hair salon?

Sometimes. They suit salons with a visual offer, several chairs to fill and a way to limit delivery to the local area. For most single-chair businesses, search ads and a good Business Profile come first.

### How much do OTT ads cost?

AdWave's 2026 summary gives $15 to $45 per 1,000 impressions across the market, with a blended average of about $26 attributed to eMarketer. Prices depend on platform and targeting.

### Is there a minimum spend?

It varies. Amazon says it has no minimum spend and suggests about $150 a day for small and medium businesses to test. Check each platform.

### Can OTT ads target a single zip code?

Not on every platform. Amazon's guide does not mention location targeting, so confirm before you buy.

### How do I measure OTT results?

Ask new clients how they found you, use a dedicated booking link or phone number for the campaign, and compare new bookings in the weeks before and after.
