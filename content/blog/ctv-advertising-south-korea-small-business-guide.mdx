## How Big Is Korea's Streaming Ad Market?

Interad, quoting Statista, estimates total advertising spend in Korea at $17.31 billion for 2025, with digital at a projected $8.09 billion, up 4.93% from 2024. These are estimates from a secondary page and will differ from Korean industry reports.

For streaming video funded by ads, 3Vision's tracker puts Korean AVOD ad revenue at $1.32 billion in 2026, of which FAST accounts for 43%. These are forecasts, not reported results.

## Which Platforms Run Ads?

Coupang Play launched what the Korea Herald called the country's first free ad-supported streaming service in June 2025. Free access is for Coupang members, with ads in pre-roll and mid-roll formats. It includes original series, films, sports and live news.

3Vision forecasts 1.1 million monthly users for the Coupang Play ad tier in 2026, rising to 3.2 million by 2031. Other services such as Tving and Netflix also exist in Korea, but the sources opened for this article did not confirm their ad options, so check directly before planning on them.

## Can a Small Business Buy These Ads Directly?

Probably not self-serve. The Korea Herald piece describes the ad formats, not how to buy them, and no source opened here shows a small-advertiser booking route or minimum spend. Expect to work through an agency or the platform's sales team, and ask for the minimum in writing.

The practical alternative is Google Ads. Google lists "TV screens" as a device option for Video campaigns, covering smart TVs, game consoles and devices like Chromecast. It is only available for Display and Video campaigns.

## Is the Google TV Network Available in Korea?

Yes. Google's help page lists South Korea among the 28 countries where the Google TV network is available. It extends YouTube video campaigns to in-feed and Masthead inventory on the TV screen.

Set location targeting to your district and exclude audiences you cannot serve. Our [CTV vs OTT explainer](/blog/ctv-vs-ott-advertising-explained) explains the difference between app-based and screen-based buying.

## What Advertising Rules Apply?

Korea's Fair Labeling and Advertising Act, enforced by the Korea Fair Trade Commission (KFTC), bans false or exaggerated, deceptive, unlawful comparative and slanderous labeling and advertising that misleads consumers. The text opened here is a WIPO copy amended through 2010 and is superseded, so check the current version with a Korean adviser.

Keep proof for every claim in a video ad, from "best in the district" to price and results.

## Which Local Businesses Might Test CTV?

Cafes, gyms, clinics and restaurants have local audiences, but no verified Korean data says which category performs best. Pick one offer and one district, and measure visits or bookings, not views alone.

Because the audience on TV screens cannot click, make the next step easy: a short name to search, a map pin and a single offer. ## What Might a Small Test Look Like? A Break-Even Check

All inputs are **assumptions**. No Korean CPM was found in the sources opened.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Budget | ₩1,000,000 |
| CPM (assumption) | ₩10,000 |
| Impressions | 1,000,000 / 10,000 x 1,000 = 100,000 |
| Margin per new customer (assumption) | ₩50,000 |
| New customers to break even | 1,000,000 / 50,000 = 20 |

Ask your platform or agency for a real CPM and replace the assumption. If you need 20 new customers from 100,000 impressions, set a stop rule before you start. For general cost thinking, see [how much CTV advertising costs](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data).
