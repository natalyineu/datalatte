Peacock now has 48 million paying subscribers, and it just reported its first profit. Yet neither of the two numbers a local advertiser most needs, a minimum budget and a price per thousand views, appears in any NBCUniversal source we could open. That is the real story for a café, salon, gym or home service company. You can buy Peacock. You cannot price it from public information, so the first job is to ask the right questions and work out your break-even before you hear a quote.

This guide is for owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Peacock is a US service, so the buying routes below are US ones; if you are elsewhere, treat it as background. It covers the verified facts about the platform, the routes in, what remains unconfirmed, a test-budget calculator and the questions to put to a sales rep. For a wider comparison of streaming options, start with [CTV advertising platforms compared](/blog/ctv-advertising-platforms-compared-2026), and for the other big premium streamer see [Prime Video ads for small business](/blog/amazon-prime-video-ads-small-business).

According to [TV Technology's report on Comcast's second quarter 2026 results](https://www.tvtechnology.com/platform/streaming/peacock-posts-first-profits-adds-2-million-subs-in-q2-2026), Peacock had 48 million paid subscribers, up 2 million in the quarter, and the company's CFO said advertising revenue increased nearly 70%. [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/07/24/peacock-turns-profitable/) reports that Peacock turned a profit for the first time since its 2020 launch, with segment revenue of $1.9 billion for the quarter ended June 30, 2026.

On price, [Peacock's own help page](https://www.peacocktv.com/help/article/how-much-does-a-peacock-subscription-cost) lists Select at $8.99 a month, Premium at $12.99 and Premium Plus at $19.99. The [price increase notice](https://www.peacocktv.com/help/article/price-increase) says new and returning subscribers pay the new prices from August 18, 2026, and existing subscribers from their first billing date on or after September 17, 2026. Premium Plus is described as having "no ads (limited exceptions)", with ads remaining in channels, live sports and events and a few shows. The help pages do not say which of the cheaper plans carry ads, and no source we could open gives a verified ad-supported share of subscribers. Ask your seller for how many of the households you want to reach would actually see an ad.

Two cautions on these figures. Subscribers are national, and your customers live within a few miles of your door. A rising national audience tells you the platform is healthy, not how many people in your town will see your spot.

### Peacock Ad Manager

[NBCUniversal's announcement](https://together.nbcuni.com/insights/news/nbcuniversal-partners-with-tvscientific-to-launch-peacock-ad-manager/) and [tvScientific's release](https://www.tvscientific.com/press/nbcuniversal-partners-with-tvscientific) describe "a brand-new self-service ad buying platform with full-funnel attribution measurement", with "precision audience and geo targeting" and built-in performance measurement from site visits to purchases. They name performance, local and small and medium-sized advertisers as the audience. [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/373440/nbcu-unveils-self-serve-programmatic-ad-manager.html) quoted NBCU's Krishan Bhatia saying it serves advertisers "who don't have existing" DSP relationships, and [Adweek's coverage](https://www.adweek.com/convergent-tv/nbcu-courts-smaller-performance-focused-advertisers-at-one23-ad-tech-event/) says it was opened to all advertisers, designed to resemble the Google and Facebook ad managers performance marketers already know.

None of these pages states a minimum spend. The Ad Manager website appeared in search results but did not load when checked, and the announcements are from 2022 and 2023. Separately, [The Desk reported](https://thedesk.net/2025/12/pinterest-acquiring-tvscientific/) on December 11, 2025 that Pinterest agreed to acquire tvScientific, expected to close in the first half of 2026; the article does not mention Peacock, so what that means for Ad Manager is unknown. Before you plan around it, confirm with NBCUniversal that the product is live in your market and what the current terms are.

### Programmatic and Direct

A [Tatari guide](https://www.tatari.tv/insights/what-marketers-should-know-about-peacock-advertising-before-you-buy) lists self-serve, programmatic and direct buying and describes minimums as "notably accessible versus traditional TV" without giving a dollar figure. Tatari sells Peacock buying services, so it is a vendor view, and the same guide shows inconsistent plan prices and ad-load numbers. Other vendor pages quote minimums from $50 to $150,000 and CPMs of $20 to $44; they disagree, they cite no NBCU source, and some sell the access they describe. Treat all of them as noise until you have a written quote.

## What You Can Safely Say, and What You Cannot

- **Can say:** Peacock is a large, growing, profitable subscription streamer with a premium ad-supported offering and a self-serve tool announced for smaller advertisers.
- **Cannot say, from verified sources:** the cost per thousand views, the minimum, the share of viewers who see ads, the age or income profile of your local viewers, or whether a local business can reach only its own zip codes.
- **Cannot say either way:** that Peacock beats cheaper channels. That depends on your numbers.

If that feels thin, it is. The honest advice is to get the numbers in writing before you spend, which is what the next section helps you do.

## The Math: A Test Budget and Its Break-Even

NBCUniversal publishes no CPM, so every price in this table is an **assumption**, and so are the frequency and response rate. Use it to decide whether a quote you receive is worth taking, not to predict results.

| | Cheap | Mid | Expensive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Test budget (assumption) | $3,000 | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| CPM, cost per 1,000 views (assumption) | $25 | $40 | $60 |
| Impressions | 120,000 | 75,000 | 50,000 |
| Views per household (assumption) | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Households reached | 30,000 | 18,750 | 12,500 |
| Households that become customers (assumption) | 0.05% | 0.05% | 0.05% |
| New customers | 15 | 9.4 | 6.3 |
| Cost per new customer | $200 | $320 | $480 |

How to use it:

- **Break-even is your gross profit per customer, not revenue.** If a new salon client leaves you $120 of profit over a year, the Cheap column loses $80 per customer. A high-value service such as a dental implant, a roof or a full-year gym membership could carry the Expensive column.
- **Check household coverage.** If your trade area has 40,000 households and the Mid column reaches 18,750 of them, you are showing the ad to nearly half. If the buy is spread over a whole metro, most of those households are too far away to ever visit you. Ask what geographic limits exist and whether they change the price.
- **Frequency matters more than reach for a local spot.** Four views of one ad to 18,750 homes is usually a better local test than one view to 75,000 (my judgment, not a measured result).
- **Compare with search.** Cost per new customer from Google Ads in your own account is the benchmark. If search gives you customers at $60, CTV has to earn its place in awareness, referrals and later search volume, not in last-click conversions. Our [real CTV CPM data article](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data) and the [Hulu guide](/blog/hulu-ads-small-business-guide) show what other platforms quote.

## Questions to Put to Any Seller

1. What is the minimum for a one-month test, and is it per campaign or per flight?
2. What is the CPM or flat price for my geography, and does narrowing the area raise it?
3. Which shows, sports and channels will my ad run in, and which can I exclude?
4. What proportion of my targeted households will see ads rather than a no-ads plan?
5. What creative do you need, and who pays for any production?
6. How will you prove delivery, and do I get household or zip-level reports?
7. What changes if the product changes hands or is renamed mid-flight?

Put the answers in writing. If a seller cannot answer the first two, you do not yet have an offer.

## Creative That Works on a Living-Room Screen

A streaming spot plays on a large screen with sound on and no click. Lead with your name and what you do in the first few seconds, show your real place and people, keep one message and one action, and put a short web address or QR code on screen long enough to use. Confirm length and technical specs with your seller first; our [CTV creative specs guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-creative-specs-guide-2026) covers formats and checks.

## How to Measure a Peacock Test

Streaming ads are rarely clicked, so build measurement before you launch:

- A vanity URL and a dedicated phone number for the campaign
- A "how did you hear about us" field at booking
- Branded search volume and Business Profile calls in your area, compared with the four weeks before
- A holdout: an area or period where you did not run the ad

Do not accept a platform's own attribution as the only evidence. For the local-search side, see [CTV vs OTT advertising explained](/blog/ctv-vs-ott-advertising-explained).

## Who Should Test Peacock, and Who Should Wait

**Reasonable fit:** businesses with high customer value (home services, dental, med spa, gyms, auto) and a trade area big enough to justify a metro-scale buy, with search and Business Profile already working.

**Wait:** a single café or salon with a small radius and a low ticket. Roku offers a self-serve tool; compare written quotes using our [Roku ads guide](/blog/roku-ads-local-business).

## Do This Today

1. Calculate gross profit per new customer and your current cost per customer from search.
2. Find your trade area's household count and decide what share of the metro you actually serve.
3. Contact NBCUniversal advertising, or the Ad Manager team, and send the seven questions above; ask for written minimums and CPMs.
4. Set up the vanity URL, tracking number and booking question before any ad runs.
5. Use the Math table with the quote you get, and only proceed if the cost per customer sits below your gross profit per customer.

## FAQ

**Q: Can a small business advertise on Peacock?**
A: Yes in principle. NBCUniversal announced a self-serve Peacock Ad Manager aimed at small and local advertisers in 2022, and other routes exist through DSPs and sales teams. Current minimums and availability are not published in sources we could open, so confirm them directly.

**Q: How much do Peacock ads cost?**
A: NBCUniversal does not publish CPMs in the sources found. Vendor figures range widely and are unsourced. Ask for a written quote and use the break-even table above.

**Q: Does everyone on Peacock see ads?**
A: No. Peacock says Premium Plus has no ads, with limited exceptions such as live sports and events. It does not state which other plans carry ads, so ask what share of your audience sees them.

**Q: Can I target my city or zip codes?**
A: NBCUniversal's announcement describes precision audience and geo targeting. Which geographic level is available to you, and at what price, needs confirmation from the seller.

**Q: Is Peacock better than Hulu or Roku for a local business?**
A: Not in any way we can verify. Compare written quotes on cost per customer, and see the platform comparison linked above.
