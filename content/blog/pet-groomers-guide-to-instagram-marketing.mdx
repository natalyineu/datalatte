Instagram pays off for a pet groomer when the extra bookings it brings cover the hours spent on it. Using assumed inputs of 3 hours a week at $25 an hour, and $45 profit per groom, the break-even point is about 7 extra bookings a month. About half of US adults use Instagram, according to [Pew Research Center's 2026 social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/). That reach only matters if the profile turns viewers into booked slots. This guide covers the setup, the posting plan, photo consent, converting messages into bookings, and the monthly count that shows whether it works.

## Does Instagram reach the people who book a groomer?

Pew's 2026 survey of 5,511 US adults puts Instagram use at 51%, up from 50% in 2025. Facebook (70%) and YouTube (84%) remain larger, and TikTok stands at 37%. The survey covers the US only, so owners in the UK, Australia and Canada may use these apps at different rates.

Reach alone does not book appointments. Owners often find a groomer through a search or a map listing first, then check how the business handles dogs. Instagram works best as a reassurance step, so the content there should match what people have already seen on your listing.

## How do you work out the break-even number?

Break-even bookings equal monthly time cost divided by profit per groom. The example below uses assumed inputs, so replace each one with your own figures before relying on the result.

Assumptions: 3 hours a week over 4.3 weeks gives about 13 hours a month. Valued at $25 an hour, that is about $320. Profit per groom is $45 after supplies and direct costs. Break-even is $320 divided by $45, or 7.1 bookings.

| Scenario (all inputs assumed) | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly time cost | $320 | $320 | $320 |
| Profile taps and DMs from Instagram | 40 | 80 | 150 |
| Share that book | 8% | 12% | 15% |
| New bookings | 3.2 | 9.6 | 22.5 |
| Profit at $45 each | $144 | $432 | $1,013 |
| Net after time cost | -$176 | +$112 | +$693 |

In the Tough case the groomer loses money on the first visit, so the return depends on repeat visits. Count only bookings that would not have happened otherwise, not regulars who would have rebooked anyway. The booking step usually matters more than the number of posts. A link that opens a slow page with no calendar will limit results however good the content is.

## What should the profile do before any posts go up?

Every post sends people to the profile, so the profile has to do the booking work. Switch to a professional account, which is free and provides insights and contact options. Check the Edit profile screen in the app, since available options can vary by country.

Put a search phrase in the name field, such as "Biscuit & Bubbles | Dog Groomer Leeds", and keep the bio to one line that says who is groomed, where, and why to choose the business. Point the single link at the booking page rather than the home page. Pin highlights that answer common questions: prices, vaccinations, first visit, cancellation policy and before-and-afters.

Name, address, hours and booking link should match the Google Business Profile exactly, because owners compare them. ## Which posts should you make each week?

Four content buckets cover skill, trust, people and availability. The table sets out the job each bucket does.

| Bucket | Examples | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transformations | Before and after, matted-coat rescue, puppy first trim | Show skill |
| Care | Calm handling routine, drying station, treats | Build trust |
| People | The groomer, the team, a regular client's dog with permission | Make the business personal |
| Availability | Open slots this week, holiday hours, new service | Prompt a booking |

A schedule that can be kept for a year beats a burst that stops after a month. Film as the work happens: 10 to 15 seconds of the finished coat, the fluff reveal, the dog trotting out. Use natural light, wipe the lens, and add captions, because many viewers watch without sound.

Put the area and service in plain words, such as "Poodle full groom in Headingley", and end each caption with one action, such as "DM the word BOOK". Add a location tag every time.

## How should you handle photo consent?

Some owners do not want their pet's photo or their own face online, so ask before posting. Add a tick box to the intake form confirming that the owner agrees to photos of their pet appearing on the groomer's social media. If the answer is no, do not post that client's dog.

Never show a client's full name or address in a caption, and avoid describing a dog's behaviour or health issue in a way that identifies its owner. If a local pet creator receives a free groom in exchange for a post, state that relationship clearly in the post. Advertising rules differ across the US, UK, Australia and Canada, so check the rules in your own country.

## How do you turn DMs into bookings?

Most groomer messages ask for a price for a breed, whether a slot is free this week, or how to help a dog that dislikes grooming. Replies sent within hours convert best, and the booking should be finished in the chat where possible.

1. Save standard replies: price ranges by size, what a full groom includes, vaccination requirements, and how to book.
2. Quote a range and name the factors that change the price, such as coat condition, size and matting.
3. End every reply with a specific next step, such as "Thursday 10am or Friday 2pm, which suits?"
4. Move the booking into the booking system and collect a phone number so reminders go out automatically. For comparisons of field service software, see the [Jobber vs ServiceTitan guide](/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-small-business-local-business).

## Should you test posts or keep posting?

Testing works best when one thing changes at a time. Meta's help documentation says an A/B test compares two versions by changing a single variable, such as the image, the text or the audience. Each version is shown to a separate segment of the audience, so nobody sees both, and the same budget should go to each version for a fair comparison.

Meta advises against informal testing, such as switching ads on and off manually, because it can create overlapping audiences and unreliable results. For a groomer, that means changing one element per test, such as the opening frame of a Reel, while keeping the posting schedule steady. Scheduling tools can help with consistency, and the [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social guide](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) compares two options.

## How do you count bookings instead of likes?

Likes do not pay the bills, so measure what leads to a booking. Tag the bio link with parameters such as utm_source=instagram and utm_campaign=bio_link, so booking software or analytics can separate Instagram traffic. Add "Instagram" as an option on the booking form and ask new clients directly for a month.

Watch profile visits, link taps, saves and shares in Instagram Insights. Saves and shares often show stronger intent than likes. At the end of each month, list the five posts that led to a DM or a tap, and make more of those. Compare this month with last month rather than an industry average, because no verified pet-grooming benchmark is available for this article.

## What should a groomer do in the next 30 days?

1. Switch to a professional account, set the name field to include the service and town, and make hours, address and link match the Google profile.
2. Put the booking page, with UTM tags, in the bio link.
3. Add a photo-permission tick box to the intake form and ask the next five clients.
4. Film three 15-second finishes this week, each with captions, a location tag and one call to action.
5. Calculate break-even as monthly time cost divided by profit per groom, then check Insights again in 30 days.

## FAQ

### How often should a pet groomer post on Instagram?

There is no official figure. Two Reels or carousels a week plus Stories on grooming days is a workable pace for a solo groomer. Choose a pace that can be sustained for months.

### Do I need a professional camera?

No. A recent phone, natural light and a clean background are enough. Effort is best spent on the finished coat, where the dog looks its best.

### Should a groomer use a personal account?

A separate professional account keeps client photos and messages apart from personal life and provides insights and contact options.

### Is Instagram or Google more important for a groomer?

Google usually comes first, because owners search for a groomer nearby. Instagram supports that search by showing how dogs are handled and what the finished work looks like.

### Can a groomer run ads on Instagram?

Yes, through Meta's ad tools. Start with people who already know the business, and run A/B tests with a fixed budget for each version.
