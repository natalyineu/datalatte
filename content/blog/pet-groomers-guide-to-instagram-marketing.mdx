Here is the number to plan around. If you spend 3 hours a week on Instagram and value your time at $25 an hour, that is about $320 a month. If each extra groom leaves you $45 after supplies and direct costs, you need roughly 7 extra bookings a month before Instagram has paid for your time. Both figures are my assumptions, and the calculation is in the Math section below. Most groomers never do this sum. They post a cute before-and-after, collect likes, and wonder why the diary is still patchy.

This guide is for salon-based and mobile groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It is about turning Instagram into a booking channel rather than a photo album: how to set up the profile, what to post, how to handle photo consent, how to move people from a comment to a booked slot, and how to count it. For the paid side, see our [Facebook ads for pet groomers guide](/blog/facebook-ads-pet-groomers-guide), which covers Meta ads for rebooking.

## Where Instagram Fits in a Pet Owner's Decision

Be realistic about what Instagram does. BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, a representative panel of 1,002 US adults via SurveyMonkey, found that after reading positive reviews, 54% of consumers check the business's website and 24% visit its social media channels. BrightLocal sells review software and this is a US panel, so read it as direction, not a forecast. The pattern for a groomer: an owner finds you on Google Maps, reads reviews, then taps to your Instagram to see whether their nervous spaniel will be handled kindly. Your Instagram is a reassurance check as much as a discovery tool. Our [Google Business Profile guide for groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers) covers the first step in that journey.

Reach is real, though. Pew Research Center's social media fact sheet (survey fielded February to June 2025) reports that 50% of US adults use Instagram:

## Fix the Profile First

Every post sends people to your profile, so make it do the booking work.

- **Switch to a professional account.** It is free and gives you insights and contact options. Instagram's own Help Center did not load for this article, so check the Edit profile screen in your app for the exact options in your country.
- **Name field with a search phrase.** "Biscuit & Bubbles | Dog Groomer Leeds" is easier to find than "Biscuit & Bubbles".
- **Bio in one line.** Who you groom, where, and one reason to choose you: "Gentle grooming for anxious dogs. Hove. Book below."
- **One link, pointed at booking.** Not your home page. If you use a link-in-bio page, keep it to three buttons: Book, Prices, Find us.
- **Highlights that answer DMs.** Prices, Vaccinations, First visit, Cancellation policy, Before and afters.
- **Match your Google profile.** Name, address, hours and booking link should be identical, because owners compare them.

## What to Post: Four Buckets

| Bucket | Examples | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transformations | Before and after, matted-coat rescue, puppy first trim | Show skill |
| Care | Your calm handling routine, a drying station, treats | Build trust |
| People | You, the team, a regular client's dog with permission | Make you human |
| Availability | "Two Thursday slots left", holiday hours, new service | Trigger a booking |

A rhythm you can keep for a year beats a burst you abandon in a month. A realistic solo schedule, which is my suggestion and not an Instagram rule: two Reels or carousels a week, plus Stories on grooming days. Film as you work: 10 to 15 seconds of the finish, the fluff reveal, the dog trotting out. Use window light, wipe the lens, and add captions because many people watch muted.

For caption wording, put the area and the service in plain words ("Poodle full groom in Headingley") and end with one action: "DM us the word BOOK" or "Link in bio". Add a location tag every time. Hashtags are a minor tool: Instagram told Social Media Today in February 2024 that a reported five-hashtag cap was a glitch, so do not build strategy on hashtag rules, and use a few specific local ones rather than #dogsofinstagram.

### Stories link stickers, not "swipe up"

Older guides tell you to use swipe-up links. Instagram replaced swipe-up in Stories with link stickers starting in August 2021, according to coverage of the change. Add a link sticker to a Story pointing at your booking page, and confirm in your app that you have it.

## Photo Consent and Disclosure

Dogs are not people, but their owners are, and a surprising number do not want their pet's photo or their own face online. Ask once, in writing, at booking: a tick box on your intake form saying "I am happy for photos of my pet to appear on the groomer's social media." If a client says no, do not post. Never show a client's address or full name in a post, and do not caption a dog's behaviour or health issue in a way that identifies the owner.

If you give a local pet creator a free groom in exchange for a post, disclosure matters. The US FTC's guide for influencers says to disclose any financial, employment, personal or family relationship with a brand, that free products count, and that the disclosure should be hard to miss and kept with the endorsement. It lists "ad" and "sponsored" as acceptable and warns against vague terms like "collab". The FTC puts responsibility on the influencer, but brief them yourself. UK, Australian and Canadian rules differ, so check your local advertising regulator.

## Turn Comments and DMs Into Bookings

Groomer DMs are mostly three questions: "How much for a Labradoodle?", "Do you have anything Saturday?" and "My dog hates being groomed, can you help?" Reply within hours and finish the booking in the chat if you can.

1. Save your standard replies somewhere on your phone: price ranges by size, what a full groom includes, vaccination requirements, how to book.
2. Quote a range and say what changes the price: coat, size and matting.
3. End every reply with a next step: "I have Thursday 10am or Friday 2pm, which suits?"
4. Move the booking into your booking system and ask for the phone number, so reminders come from there.

## Track Bookings, Not Likes

You cannot manage what you do not count.

- **Tag your bio link.** Google Analytics Help says that when you add parameters to a URL you should always use `utm_source`, `utm_medium` and `utm_campaign`. For example: `?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio_link`.
- **Ask every new client how they found you.** Add it to the booking form, with Instagram as an option, and ask in person for a month.
- **Watch profile visits, link taps, saves and shares** in Instagram Insights. Saves and shares tend to say more about intent than likes.
- **Review monthly.** Which five posts led to a DM or a tap? Make more of those.

I found no verified Instagram engagement benchmark for pet groomers, so do not compare yourself to a "pet industry average". Compare this month to last month.

## The Math

Example only. Everything below is an **assumption** except that your own numbers should replace them.

Assumptions: 3 hours a week x 4.3 weeks = about 13 hours at $25 an hour, so $320 a month. Profit per groom $45 after supplies and direct costs.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly time cost | $320 | $320 | $320 |
| Bio-link taps and DMs from Instagram (assumption) | 40 | 80 | 150 |
| Share that book (assumption) | 8% | 12% | 15% |
| New bookings | 3.2 | 9.6 | 22.5 |
| Profit at $45 each | $144 | $432 | $1,013 |
| Net after your time | -$176 | +$112 | +$693 |
| Break-even bookings ($320 / $45) | 7.1 | 7.1 | 7.1 |

How to read it: in the Tough case you lose money on the first visit alone, so the bet is on repeat visits. If a booked client returns every 8 weeks, a single new client is worth about 6 grooms over a year, or $270 in profit. That is an assumption-based illustration, not a promise. Also count only extra bookings, not regulars who would have booked anyway.

The fastest lever is not more posts, it is the booking step. If your link opens a slow page with no calendar, nothing you film will fix it. See our [pet groomer website pages guide](/blog/pet-groomer-website-pages-and-copy-guide) for the booking page and the copy.

## Do This Today

1. Switch to a professional account, set your name field to "Name | Dog Groomer Town", and make your hours, address and link match Google.
2. Put your booking page, with UTM tags, in the bio link.
3. Add a photo-permission tick box to your intake form and ask your next five clients.
4. Film three 15-second finishes this week, with captions, a location tag and one call to action each.
5. Write down your break-even (monthly time cost divided by profit per groom) and check Insights again in 30 days.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should a pet groomer post on Instagram?**
A: There is no official figure. Two Reels or carousels a week plus Stories on grooming days is a workable pace for a solo groomer. Pick what you can sustain for months.

**Q: Do I need a professional camera?**
A: No. A recent phone, natural light and a clean background are enough. Spend the effort on the finish shot, where the dog looks its best.

**Q: Should I use my personal account?**
A: Use a separate professional account. It keeps client photos and DMs separate from your personal life and unlocks insights and contact options.

**Q: Is Instagram or Google more important for a groomer?**
A: Google first, because it is where owners search for a groomer nearby. Instagram supports it by showing how you handle dogs. BrightLocal's survey suggests many people check social media only after reading reviews.

**Q: Can I run ads on Instagram?**
A: Yes, through Meta. Start with the people who already know you; our [Facebook ads for pet groomers guide](/blog/facebook-ads-pet-groomers-guide) shows how to rebook lapsed clients.
