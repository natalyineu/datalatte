iHeartMedia ad costs depend on the product and your market (the company's own advertise page asks you to call for quotes rather than listing prices, [iHeartMedia](https://www.iheartmedia.com/advertise), checked Oct 2026): it sells local broadcast radio, iHeartRadio streaming audio and podcast ads, each quoted differently. We found no public rate card, so ask a rep or AdBuilder for a written quote and compare it against your cost per new customer. For most small businesses, a small streaming test is the easiest way to start.

Picture the call. A sales rep asks what you want to spend. You say "I don't know, what does it cost?" and you are already behind, because the rep has a number in mind and you do not. Spotify and Pandora (through AudioGO) publish a $250 starting point ([Spotify](https://ads.spotify.com/en-US/news-and-insights/small-business-self-serve-ad-tips/), [AudioGO](https://www.audiogo.com/pandora), checked Oct 2026). iHeart does not. So walk in with your own number: the maximum you can pay for a new customer. The math below shows how to get it in two minutes.

We have not quoted any CPM or minimum, because we could not verify one from an official source. Prices vary widely by city, station, daypart and season, so confirm every figure with iHeartMedia or a media buyer.

## iHeartMedia's three core products for local advertisers

### 1. Local broadcast radio

Traditional AM and FM radio through an iHeartMedia station in your market. It is typically sold through a local account executive, and price usually reflects spot length, time of day and the station's audience.

- **Cost:** Rates vary enormously between a small market and a major city, and between a quiet daypart and morning drive. Expect a package, not a single spot. More popular stations and times cost more.
- **Who it reaches:** Station formats differ (pop, country, hip-hop, news/talk, sports), so your audience match depends on the format you choose. Commute times are commonly the busiest.
- **Test approach:** Ask for a short, concentrated flight on one station with a limited number of dayparts, instead of spreading a small budget thinly. Broadcast is rarely the best first channel if your total marketing budget is small.

### 2. iHeartRadio streaming audio ads

Digital audio ads served to listeners using the iHeartRadio app and streams.

- **Format:** Short audio spots, often 15 or 30 seconds, sometimes with a companion banner
- **Cost model:** Ask whether you are quoted by CPM (cost per thousand impressions) or by package, and request current rates and minimums.
- **Targeting options (often):** location (market, sometimes city), age and gender, genre or station type, and time of day. Available options vary by buy type.
- **Trackability:** Better than broadcast, because you can use unique landing pages, promo codes and call tracking.

### 3. Podcast advertising

iHeartMedia sells podcast advertising. Ads can be host-read or produced, in pre-, mid- or post-roll positions.

- **Cost model:** Ask how host-read and produced ads are priced and what the minimum is
- **Best for:** Brand-building campaigns aimed at a particular show audience, more often a fit for businesses with larger budgets or longer buying cycles

## The math: your break-even before the quote

You cannot compare a quote to anything until you know how many new customers it must produce. Break-even customers = quote / first-job value, rounded up. Every number in the table is an assumption for illustration. They are not iHeart prices and not market statistics.

Example: three possible quotes and three possible job values.

| Assumed quote | Job worth $150 | Job worth $300 | Job worth $1,200 |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | 7 customers | 4 customers | 1 customer |
| $2,000 | 14 customers | 7 customers | 2 customers |
| $4,000 | 27 customers | 14 customers | 4 customers |

(For example: $2,000 / $300 = 6.67, so 7.)

Now the sanity check. Suppose you pay the assumed $2,000 and track 5 new customers who say they heard you on the radio, at $300 each. Cost per customer is $2,000 / 5 = $400. First-job revenue is $1,500. That loses $500 on first jobs, so it only pays if those customers come back or refer others. A home-services job worth $1,200 clears the same quote with 2 customers. This is why radio fits high-ticket services better than low-ticket sales like a latte, and why we say a cafe on a tight budget should fix its Google profile first.

## How to set up iHeartMedia advertising

### Step 1: Choose your channel

For a first audio campaign, many small businesses start with streaming because it is easier to track than broadcast, but compare quotes for both. Consider broadcast when you have a tested message, a clear budget and a customer base that matches a station's audience.

### Step 2: Contact the right team

- **Streaming and digital:** Use iHeartMedia's advertiser contact options or ask a media partner. iHeartMedia promotes iHeart AdBuilder as a self-service way to buy radio advertising for small businesses ([iHeartMedia AdBuilder guide](https://share.iheartmedia.com/iHeartAdBuilder_YourGuideToRadioAdvertising), checked Oct 2026). Features and minimums change, so confirm them before you plan.
- **Local broadcast:** Contact the account team for your local iHeartMedia stations and request a media plan.
- **Programmatic:** iHeartRadio inventory may be available through some demand-side platforms if you already run programmatic campaigns. See our [programmatic audio guide](/blog/programmatic-audio-advertising-local-business-guide).

### Step 3: Plan your creative

A few rules for local audio:
1. **Say where you are early**, for example "If you live in [city]..."
2. **One offer and one action**, such as "Book online at [URL]" or "Call [number]"
3. **Repeat the key detail**, whether name, phone number or web address
4. **Sound conversational**, not like a printed flyer

Ask what file format and loudness specs the platform requires.

### Step 4: Set up tracking

Without tracking, you cannot tell whether the campaign worked. Use:
- **A unique promo code**, such as "mention RADIO"
- **A dedicated landing page**, such as yourbusiness.com/radio, tagged with UTM parameters. See our [UTM guide](/blog/utm-parameters-track-every-marketing-campaign).
- **A call tracking number** used only in the ad
- **A "How did you hear about us?" question** at booking or checkout

Also watch branded search trends in Google Search Console, since audio often increases people searching for your name. For more on measurement see [how to track which marketing is bringing customers](/blog/how-to-track-which-marketing-is-bringing-customers).

## Which businesses should try iHeartMedia?

**Often a good fit:**
- Home services such as HVAC, plumbing and roofing
- Auto services and dealerships
- Healthcare, dental and legal practices
- Real estate and mortgage professionals
- Restaurants with broad appeal in mid-size markets

**Often a weaker fit:**
- Single-location cafes and boutiques on a tight budget, where a strong [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) and [local SEO](/services/local-seo) usually pay back faster
- E-commerce brands with no local presence
- Highly niche B2B services

## Measuring whether it is working

**Weeks 2 to 4:** promo code redemptions, landing page visits, tracked calls, and new customers who mention the ad.

**Weeks 6 to 12:** new customer counts against the same period last year, branded search volume, and review mentions of the ad.

Divide your total ad cost by new customers gained, and compare that with what one customer is worth. A cost per customer that is high for a low-ticket service may still be excellent for a high-value job.

## Do this before you call a rep

1. Write down what one new customer is worth to you. Use the first job, then the first year.
2. Use the table above to find your break-even customer count for a $1,000, $2,000 and $4,000 quote.
3. Ask for a written quote on one station, one daypart, one 4-week flight. Ask for the minimum and any production fees in the same email.
4. Get a second quote for streaming. Ask for the CPM and the targeting options.
5. Create the promo code, the landing page and the call-tracking number before the first spot airs.
6. Put a review date on the calendar for week 4 and week 8.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do iHeartMedia ads cost for a small business?**
A: It depends on the product and market. Broadcast, streaming and podcasts are each quoted differently. Request a written quote and compare it with your cost per new customer.

**Q: Is iHeartMedia advertising worth it for a small local business?**
A: It can be for businesses with higher-value jobs, a broad local audience and repeat exposure, such as home services and healthcare. For small, single-location retail on a tight budget, Google Business Profile and search ads usually come first.

**Q: Can I target iHeartMedia ads by location?**
A: Yes. Broadcast reaches a station's market, and streaming can usually be targeted by market and sometimes city. Exact options depend on the buy type.

**Q: What is the minimum budget for iHeartMedia streaming ads?**
A: We could not verify a published minimum. Ask for the current minimum and avoid tests so small they cannot produce meaningful data.

**Q: Does iHeartMedia have a self-serve platform?**
A: Yes. iHeartMedia promotes iHeart AdBuilder as self-service radio buying for small businesses, and it also sells through sales teams. Confirm current features and minimums with iHeartMedia.

**Q: Is iHeartMedia better than Spotify for local businesses?**
A: It depends on your audience. iHeartMedia suits broad local reach with mainstream formats, while Spotify offers a self-serve tool starting at $250 and postal-code targeting in the US.
