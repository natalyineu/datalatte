## iHeartMedia's Three Core Products for Local Advertisers

### 1. Local Broadcast Radio

Traditional AM/FM radio through an iHeartMedia station in your market. Sold through local account executives, priced by spot length (15, 30, or 60 seconds), daypart, and station audience size.

**What it costs:** Spot rates in mid-size markets (pop. 500k–1.5M) typically run $50–$300 per spot for 30-second ads, with minimum packages starting around $1,000–$2,000/month. Major markets (NY, LA, Chicago) are significantly higher — $500–$2,000+ per spot on popular morning drive.

**Who it reaches:** Adults 25–54 during commute hours (morning drive 6–10am, afternoon drive 3–7pm) and weekend dayparts. iHeartMedia stations skew mainstream pop, Country, Hip-Hop, News/Talk, and Sports, so your audience match depends on station format.

**Minimum viable test:** 50–100 spots over 4 weeks on one station, concentrated in 2–3 dayparts. In a mid-size market, that's roughly $2,500–$5,000. Broadcast radio is not the right starting channel for businesses spending under $1,500/month on all marketing.

### 2. iHeartRadio Streaming Audio Ads

Digital audio ads served to iHeartRadio's streaming listener base — people listening to custom radio stations, playlists, or live broadcast streams via the app.

**Format:** 15- or 30-second audio spots, with optional display banner companion (320×50px shown while the audio plays).

**Cost model:** Primarily CPM-based (cost per 1,000 impressions). Typical CPMs range from $15–$25 for local targeting, rising to $30–$40 for tight demographic overlays.

**Targeting available:**
- Geographic: down to DMA (Designated Market Area), city, or in some cases zip code
- Demographic: age, gender, household income brackets
- Genre/station format: listeners who prefer Country, Pop, Sports Talk, etc.
- Behavioral: inferred interests based on listening history
- Daypart: morning, afternoon, evening, overnight

**Minimum spend:** Practical minimums for a local test start around $500–$1,000 for a 4-week campaign. At $20 CPM and $500 budget, you get 25,000 impressions — enough for a meaningful local awareness test.

**Key advantage over broadcast:** Digital streaming ads are trackable. You can use unique promo codes, UTM-tagged landing pages, or QR codes (for companion display banners) to measure actual response rates.

### 3. Podcast Advertising

iHeartMedia is the largest podcast publisher in the US through iHeartPodcast Network (Crime Junkie, Stuff You Should Know, How Stuff Works, SmartMouth, and 400+ others). Podcast ads run as host-read or produced spots in pre-roll, mid-roll, or post-roll positions.

**Cost model:** CPM-based, typically $15–$40 depending on show size and format (host-read commands a premium). Minimum buys are usually $2,000–$5,000 to reach meaningful scale.

**Best for:** Brand-building campaigns targeting a specific podcast demographic — not a typical entry point for single-location small businesses unless you have strong brand story and longer customer consideration windows.

## How to Set Up iHeartMedia Advertising

### Step 1: Decide Your Channel

For most small businesses running their first audio campaign, **start with iHeartRadio streaming** rather than broadcast radio. Here's why:

- Lower minimum spend ($500 vs. $2,000+)
- Granular targeting at the city level
- Measurable results via promo codes and UTM links
- Easier to pause, adjust, or stop mid-campaign

Broadcast radio becomes a strong consideration when you have a proven message, $2,000+/month in audio budget, and a brand that resonates with the station's listenership.

### Step 2: Contact the Right Team

**For iHeartRadio digital streaming:** iHeartMedia's national digital sales team handles streaming inventory. Visit iHeart's advertiser page or contact them directly for self-serve access or a managed campaign setup.

**For local broadcast:** Call or email your local iHeartMedia station's account team. Look up your city's iHeartMedia stations and find the "Advertise With Us" contact. Account execs are responsive and will offer a media plan within 24–48 hours.

**For programmatic access:** iHeartRadio's streaming inventory is also available through programmatic DSPs including StackAdapt, DV360, and Trade Desk. If you're already running digital campaigns through a programmatic platform, you may be able to access iHeart inventory there without a direct relationship.

### Step 3: Plan Your Creative

**Broadcast and streaming audio specs:**
- 30-second spot: 80–90 words of copy (read at a natural pace)
- 15-second spot: 35–40 words
- Produced audio files: MP3, 44.1kHz, 128kbps minimum
- Companion display banner (streaming only): 320×50px JPG or PNG

**What makes a strong local radio/audio ad:**
1. **State your location clearly** within the first 5 seconds — "If you're in [city], listen up"
2. **One offer, one action** — "Call us at [number]" or "Book online at [URL]" — not both
3. **Repeat the most important detail twice** — name, phone number, or URL
4. **Keep it conversational** — broadcast audio ads that read like flyers don't convert

**Production cost:**
- iHeartMedia's local station teams can often produce simple ads for free or minimal cost as part of a broadcast package
- For streaming-only campaigns, production is usually your responsibility — freelance voiceover artists on Voices.com or Voice123 typically run $100–$300 for a produced 30-second spot
- AI voiceover tools (ElevenLabs, Murf) are a lower-cost option for streaming, though quality varies

### Step 4: Set Up Tracking

Without tracking, you cannot know if your iHeartMedia campaign is generating any business. Set up at minimum:

**Unique promo code:** "Mention IHEART for $10 off your first visit" or "Use code RADIO at checkout." This is the most reliable attribution method for brick-and-mortar businesses.

**Dedicated landing page:** Create a URL like `yourbusiness.com/radio` that only your audio ad mentions. Any traffic to that page is almost certainly from your ad. Set up Google Analytics to track visits and any form submissions or calls from that page.

**Phone tracking:** A call tracking number (CallRail, CallTrackingMetrics) exclusive to your audio ads lets you see exactly which calls came from iHeartMedia. Cost is typically $20–$50/month.

**"How did you hear about us?"** intake question: Low-tech but catches what tracking misses. Many clients hear your ad several times before acting and won't remember to use a promo code.

## iHeartMedia vs. Spotify vs. Pandora: Which Audio Platform?

| Feature | iHeartMedia Streaming | Spotify Ads | Pandora |
|---|---|---|---|
| US monthly listeners | 250M+ (broadcast + streaming) | 100M+ | 40M |
| Streaming min. budget | $500 | $250 | $1,000 |
| Targeting granularity | City + demo + genre | City + demo + playlist interest | City + demo + genre |
| Ad-free paid tier | No (free tier only for streaming ads) | No (free tier only) | No (free tier only) |
| Self-serve platform | Limited | Yes (Spotify Ads Manager) | Yes (AMP) |
| Creative production | iHeart team or self-supply | Self-supply | Self-supply |
| Best for | Mainstream-format audience, reach | Younger 18–34 demo, music listeners | Older 35+ demo, Midwest/rural skew |

**Rule of thumb:**
- If your customers are 35–65, mainstream music and talk radio formats, and in suburban/exurban areas — **iHeartMedia** is strong.
- If you're targeting 18–34 urban millennials interested in lifestyle or fitness — **Spotify** is usually more precise.
- If your market has strong Pandora usage (Midwest, South, older demographics) — **Pandora** reaches an audience that iHeart and Spotify's streaming inventory misses.

Running all three simultaneously with small budgets (even $300–500/channel) and comparing results over 60 days is the data-driven way to choose.

## Which Businesses Should Try iHeartMedia?

**Strong fit:**
- Home services (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, pest control) — local radio is a historically strong channel for this category
- Auto dealerships and auto services — radio built this category for decades
- Healthcare providers (dentists, optometrists, chiropractors) — strong awareness channel for service discovery
- Legal services — high-trust, brand-building format suits legal advertising
- Real estate agents and mortgage brokers — appointment-based, benefit from repeated name recognition
- Restaurants with broad appeal (not ultra-niche) in mid-size markets

**Weaker fit:**
- Single-location coffee shops or boutiques with tight budgets — Google Business Profile and Local SEO deliver stronger ROI at $500/month
- E-commerce businesses without a physical local presence — digital targeting on social is more precise
- Highly niche B2B services — iHeartMedia's broad audience reach is less valuable when you need very specific buyer characteristics

## Measuring Whether iHeartMedia Is Working

### Short-term indicators (weeks 2–4)
- Promo code redemptions
- Traffic to your dedicated landing page
- Calls to your tracking number
- "Heard you on the radio" mentions from new customers

### Medium-term indicators (weeks 6–12)
- New customer count vs. baseline (compare to the same period last year)
- Branded search volume in Google Search Console (iHeartMedia reach increases branded searches even when people don't act immediately)
- Review mentions referencing radio or audio ads

### What success looks like
For a local service business spending $750/month on iHeartRadio streaming, generating 3–5 trackable new customers per month represents a $150–$250 cost per acquisition. Whether that's profitable depends entirely on your average customer value — for a $50/session business, that's break-even; for a $500 HVAC repair, that's excellent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much do iHeartMedia ads cost for a small business?

Local broadcast radio typically starts around $1,000–$2,000 per month in mid-size US markets, with individual spot rates from $50–$300 per 30-second ad. iHeartRadio digital streaming is more accessible — a focused local test campaign can start around $500 for 4 weeks, with CPMs of $15–$25. Podcast advertising requires larger minimums, typically $2,000–$5,000, making it more suitable for businesses with established marketing budgets.

### Is iHeartMedia advertising worth it for a small local business?

It depends on your ticket size, brand-building goals, and competitive context. Home services businesses (HVAC, plumbing, roofing), healthcare providers, and legal services consistently see strong ROI from iHeartMedia's local broadcast reach because the format matches how their customers make decisions — repeated exposure builds trust before the need arises. Single-location retail, coffee shops, or boutique businesses with $300–$800/month budgets typically see better returns from Google Ads and Google Business Profile optimization before adding audio.

### Can I target iHeartMedia ads by location?

Yes. Local broadcast radio is inherently market-specific — you buy a station in your city and reach listeners in that market's broadcast footprint. iHeartRadio digital streaming allows geographic targeting down to the DMA (Designated Market Area) and, in some cases, individual cities. Zip-code-level targeting is possible on some programmatic placements but not standard in direct buys.

### What is the minimum budget for iHeartMedia streaming ads?

The practical minimum for a meaningful test of iHeartRadio digital streaming is $500–$1,000 over 4 weeks. At $20 CPM with a $500 budget, you reach approximately 25,000 local listeners — enough to measure response with a tracking code or unique landing page. Below $300, the impression volume is too low to generate statistically meaningful data about whether the campaign is working.

### How do I measure results from iHeartMedia advertising?

The most reliable attribution methods for local small businesses are: (1) a unique promo code mentioned in the ad ("mention RADIO for 10% off"), (2) a dedicated landing page URL referenced in the ad, (3) a call tracking number exclusive to the campaign, and (4) an intake question asking new customers how they heard about you. Branded search volume in Google Search Console also tends to rise during active audio campaigns, reflecting awareness even when people don't act immediately.

### Does iHeartMedia have a self-serve advertising platform?

iHeartMedia does not have a widely available self-serve platform equivalent to Spotify Ads Manager or Google Ads. Most iHeartMedia local buys go through a direct relationship with a local account executive (for broadcast) or a national digital sales contact (for streaming). Programmatic DSP access is another path — iHeartRadio inventory is available through some demand-side platforms if you already run programmatic campaigns. For businesses that want self-serve control, Spotify or Pandora's self-serve tools may be easier starting points.

### How does iHeartMedia radio advertising compare to Spotify for local businesses?

iHeartMedia offers significantly larger reach (250M+ monthly listeners vs. Spotify's 100M+ in the US), a mainstream-format audience older than Spotify's (skews 35–60 vs. Spotify's 18–34), and the combination of broadcast and streaming inventory in one relationship. Spotify is more precise for younger demographics, has a widely available self-serve platform, and allows playlist and podcast targeting that iHeartMedia streaming does not. For most local businesses, iHeartMedia is stronger for broad-reach brand awareness; Spotify is stronger for targeted millennial audience campaigns.
