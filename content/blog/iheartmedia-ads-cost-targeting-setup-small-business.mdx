iHeartMedia ad costs depend on the product and your market: local broadcast radio is priced by the spot and usually needs a monthly package, iHeartRadio streaming audio is typically priced by CPM (cost per thousand impressions) and can be tested on a smaller budget, and podcast ads are usually the largest commitment. There is no public rate card for most products, so ask a rep for a written quote and compare it against your cost per new customer. For most small businesses, a small streaming test is the easiest way to start.

Prices vary widely by city, station, daypart and season, so all ranges below are approximate and should be confirmed with iHeartMedia or a media buyer.

## iHeartMedia's three core products for local advertisers

### 1. Local broadcast radio

Traditional AM and FM radio through an iHeartMedia station in your market. It is usually sold through a local account executive and priced by spot length (such as 15, 30 or 60 seconds), time of day and the audience of the station.

- **Cost:** Rates vary enormously between a small market and a major city, and between a quiet daypart and morning drive. Expect to be quoted a package rather than a single spot, and expect that more popular stations and times cost more.
- **Who it reaches:** Station formats differ (pop, country, hip-hop, news/talk, sports), so your audience match depends on the format you choose. Commute times are commonly the busiest.
- **Test approach:** Ask for a short, concentrated flight on one station with a limited number of dayparts, rather than spreading a small budget thinly. Broadcast is rarely the best first channel if your total marketing budget is small.

### 2. iHeartRadio streaming audio ads

Digital audio ads served to listeners using the iHeartRadio app and streams.

- **Format:** Short audio spots, often 15 or 30 seconds, sometimes with a companion banner
- **Cost model:** Usually CPM-based. Quoted CPMs depend on targeting and campaign size, so ask for current rates and minimums.
- **Targeting options (often):** location (market, sometimes city), age and gender, genre or station type, and time of day. Available options vary by buy type.
- **Trackability:** Better than broadcast, because you can use unique landing pages, promo codes and call tracking.

### 3. Podcast advertising

iHeartMedia runs a large podcast network. Ads can be host-read or produced, in pre-, mid- or post-roll positions.

- **Cost model:** Typically CPM-based, with premiums for host-read ads and popular shows
- **Minimums:** Commonly higher than streaming, so check
- **Best for:** Brand-building campaigns aimed at a particular show audience, more often a fit for businesses with larger budgets or longer buying cycles

## How to set up iHeartMedia advertising

### Step 1: Choose your channel

For a first audio campaign, many small businesses start with streaming because it usually has a lower entry point, more targeting and better measurement than broadcast. Consider broadcast when you have a tested message, a clear budget and a customer base that matches a station's audience.

### Step 2: Contact the right team

- **Streaming and digital:** Use iHeartMedia's advertiser contact options or ask a media partner. Self-serve availability is limited and changes, so check what is currently offered.
- **Local broadcast:** Contact the account team for your local iHeartMedia stations and request a media plan.
- **Programmatic:** iHeartRadio inventory may be available through some demand-side platforms if you already run programmatic campaigns. See our [programmatic audio guide](/blog/programmatic-audio-advertising-local-business-guide).

### Step 3: Plan your creative

A few rules for local audio:
1. **Say where you are early**, for example "If you live in [city]..."
2. **One offer and one action**, such as "Book online at [URL]" or "Call [number]"
3. **Repeat the key detail**, whether name, phone number or web address
4. **Sound conversational**, not like a printed flyer

A 30-second spot is usually around 75 to 85 spoken words, and a 15-second spot around 35 to 40, though the pace varies. Ask what file format and loudness specs the platform requires.

Production: local stations sometimes help produce ads as part of a package, while streaming-only campaigns often require you to supply the audio. Freelance voice artists and AI voice tools exist, but check quality, licensing and platform rules.

### Step 4: Set up tracking

Without tracking, you cannot tell whether the campaign worked. Use:
- **A unique promo code**, such as "mention RADIO"
- **A dedicated landing page**, such as yourbusiness.com/radio, tagged with UTM parameters. See our [UTM guide](/blog/utm-parameters-track-every-marketing-campaign).
- **A call tracking number** used only in the ad
- **A "How did you hear about us?" question** at booking or checkout

Also watch branded search trends in Google Search Console, since audio often increases people searching for your name.

For more on measurement see [how to track which marketing is bringing customers](/blog/how-to-track-which-marketing-is-bringing-customers).

## Which businesses should try iHeartMedia?

**Often a good fit:**
- Home services such as HVAC, plumbing and roofing
- Auto services and dealerships
- Healthcare, dental and legal practices
- Real estate and mortgage professionals
- Restaurants with broad appeal in mid-size markets

**Often a weaker fit:**
- Single-location cafes and boutiques on a tight budget, where a strong [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) and [local SEO](/services/local-seo) usually pay back faster
- E-commerce brands with no local presence
- Highly niche B2B services

## Measuring whether it is working

**Weeks 2 to 4:** promo code redemptions, landing page visits, tracked calls, and new customers who mention the ad.

**Weeks 6 to 12:** new customer counts against the same period last year, branded search volume, and review mentions of the ad.

Work out your break-even: divide your total ad cost by new customers gained, and compare with what one customer is worth. A cost per customer that is high for a low-ticket service may still be excellent for a high-value job.

## FAQ

**How much do iHeartMedia ads cost for a small business?**
It depends on the product and market. Broadcast is quoted as spot or package pricing, streaming is typically CPM-based, and podcasts have their own minimums. Request a written quote and compare it with your cost per new customer.

**Is iHeartMedia advertising worth it for a small local business?**
It can be for businesses with higher-value jobs, a broad local audience and repeat exposure, such as home services and healthcare. For small, single-location retail on a tight budget, Google Business Profile and search ads usually come first.

**Can I target iHeartMedia ads by location?**
Yes. Broadcast reaches a station's market, and streaming can usually be targeted by market and sometimes city. Exact options depend on the buy type.

**What is the minimum budget for iHeartMedia streaming ads?**
Minimums vary by buy type and partner. Ask for the current minimum and avoid tests so small they cannot produce meaningful data.

**Does iHeartMedia have a self-serve platform?**
Access has historically been through sales teams or programmatic partners, and self-serve options are limited and change over time. Check iHeartMedia's current advertiser page.

**Is iHeartMedia better than Spotify for local businesses?**
It depends on your audience. iHeartMedia suits broad local reach with mainstream formats, while Spotify is often easier to self-serve and target by age or listening context.
