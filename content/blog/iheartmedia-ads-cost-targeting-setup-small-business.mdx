## How much do iHeartMedia ads cost for a small business?

The price depends on the product and the market. Local broadcast is usually sold as a package through a station's account team, and the price reflects spot length, time of day and audience size. Streaming audio may be quoted by CPM (cost per thousand impressions) or by package. Podcast ads depend on whether the read is host-delivered or produced.

None of the sources in this guide list iHeartMedia rates. Treat any figure you hear as unverified until it appears in a written quote that states the total price, the minimum spend and any production charges.

Ask for all three items in the same email. A quote without them cannot be compared fairly with search, social or other audio channels, because the real cost of the campaign is not yet visible.

## What do the digital ad benchmarks tell a local advertiser?

Google's documentation gives the scale of the Display Network. Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190)). That is a primary figure, and it describes how wide the audience can be, not how many people will act.

WordStream's benchmarks, a secondary source, show industry averages. Its all-industry display figures are $0.63 per click and a 0.46% click-through rate, and legal search clicks averaged $6.75 ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks)). These are older averages and not audio figures, so use them only as a reference when comparing channels.

For the trade-offs between formats, read [audio ads vs display ads for local business](/blog/audio-ads-vs-display-ads-local-business).

## How do you calculate break-even before getting a quote?

Break-even customers equals the quote divided by the first-job value, rounded up. Every input in the table below is an assumption for illustration. None of them are iHeartMedia prices or market statistics.

| Assumed quote (assumption) | Job worth $150 (assumption) | Job worth $300 (assumption) | Job worth $1,200 (assumption) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | 7 customers | 4 customers | 1 customer |
| $2,000 | 14 customers | 7 customers | 2 customers |
| $4,000 | 27 customers | 14 customers | 4 customers |

For example, $2,000 divided by $300 is 6.67, which rounds up to 7 customers.

Now test the result. Assume a $2,000 quote (assumption) and 5 tracked new customers who say they heard the ad, each worth $300 on the first job (assumption). The cost per customer is $2,000 divided by 5, which is $400. First-job revenue is $1,500, so the campaign loses $500 on first jobs. It pays only if those customers return or refer others. A $1,200 home-service job clears the same quote with 2 customers, which is why high-ticket services usually fit broadcast-style budgets better than low-ticket sales.

## How should a small business set up a first audio test?

Start with a short, concentrated test rather than a large flight. Work through these steps in order:

1. Write down what one new customer is worth, first on the first job and then over the first year.
2. Use the break-even table to find the customer count your quote must produce.
3. Request a written quote for one station or one channel, one daypart and one four-week flight.
4. Ask for a second quote on a different format, such as streaming, so you can compare cost models side by side.
5. Set up a unique promo code, a dedicated landing page and a call-tracking number before the first ad airs.
6. Book review dates at weeks 4 and 8.

Small tests have a cost of their own. A flight so small that it produces only one or two customers cannot show whether the channel works, so confirm the minimum before you commit. The [Spotify vs Pandora vs iHeart comparison](/blog/spotify-vs-pandora-vs-iheart-best-audio-ad-platform-local-business) sets out the trade-offs between these platforms.

## How do you measure whether the audio test worked?

Track results in two windows. In weeks 2 to 4, count promo code redemptions, landing page visits and tracked calls. In weeks 6 to 12, compare new customer totals with the same period last year and check whether branded searches have increased.

Divide total ad cost by the new customers you can attribute to the campaign. Compare that figure with the customer value you wrote down earlier. Audio often prompts people to search for the business name rather than click the ad, so branded search volume is a useful indirect signal.

If search is part of your mix, [Bing Ads for local business](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business) explains how to compare intent across platforms. Keep the same tracking rules for every channel so the cost-per-customer figures can be compared directly.

## Which businesses should consider iHeartMedia?

Audio ads tend to suit businesses with higher-value jobs, a broad local audience and room for repeated exposure. Home services, healthcare practices and auto services fit that pattern better than low-ticket retail. A single-location shop on a tight budget usually gets more value from fixing its local search presence first.

Check the fit with the break-even numbers. If one customer is worth far less than the smallest quote you are offered, the campaign has to rely on repeat purchases to pay back, and that is a weaker starting point for a first test.

## FAQ

### How much do iHeartMedia ads cost for a small business?

The cost depends on the product and the market. Broadcast, streaming and podcast ads are quoted differently. Request a written quote that includes the minimum spend and production fees, then compare it with the value of one new customer.

### Is iHeartMedia advertising worth it for a small local business?

It can be, for businesses with higher-value jobs and a broad local audience. Single-location retail on a tight budget often gets a better return from local search and [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) first, so test those channels before committing to audio.

### Can you target audio ads by location?

Location targeting depends on the buy type. Confirm the options and their precision in the quote, because none of the sources reviewed here specify what iHeartMedia offers in each market.

### How should you start if the budget is small?

Run one concentrated test with a clear break-even number and tracking in place before the first spot airs. A test that is too small to produce customer counts will not show whether the channel works.
