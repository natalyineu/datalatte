Real estate email works when you send a short, useful message to people who already asked to hear from you, and you keep it up for months. MailerLite's 2025 benchmark for real estate is a 40.37% open rate and a 1.72% click rate. Buying a home takes a long time, so the job of email is to stay in view until a lead is ready to talk.

This guide is for independent agents and small teams. It covers what the numbers look like, which sequences to build, what the law requires and how to judge results. Every figure comes from a source listed at the end, and the example is labelled as an assumption.

## What Are Good Email Numbers for Real Estate?

MailerLite analysed over 3.6 million campaigns from about 181,000 accounts between December 2024 and November 2025 and reports medians. Real estate sits at 40.37% opens, 1.72% clicks, a 6.72% click-to-open rate and a 0.16% unsubscribe rate. The all-industry medians are 43.46% opens and 2.09% clicks.

Treat opens with care. MailerLite notes that Apple Mail Privacy Protection records emails as opened automatically, so real open rates are lower. Clicks and replies are the better signal. Other vendors publish different real estate figures because their data and definitions differ, so compare your own list month to month rather than chasing one benchmark.

## Why Does Email Fit a Long Buying Cycle?

Most buyers still go through an agent. NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers says 88% of buyers used an agent or broker and 91% of sellers did. First-time buyers made up only 21% of buyers, the lowest share NAR has recorded, and their median age was 40.

The point for email: many people take months or years between first interest and a signed agreement, and you do not know who will move next. A regular, useful message keeps your name in front of them without a phone call. It also keeps past clients close, which is where repeat and referral business comes from. NAR does not publish the share of deals that come from email, so do not expect a standard figure.

## Which Sequences Should an Agent Build First?

Start with three, and keep each short. Write them once, then let your email tool send them automatically.

- **New lead sequence (buyer):** an immediate reply with what happens next, then a neighbourhood guide, then a question asking about timeline and budget range.
- **New lead sequence (seller):** a note on how you price a home, a plain list of what you do before listing, and an offer of a valuation visit.
- **Past client and neighbour list:** a monthly note with local market facts, one useful tip and a way to reply.

Use your own sales data and local market figures, and name the source and date of any market number. ## What Does the Law Require?

In the US, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide for businesses lists the rules. Header information must be accurate, subject lines must not mislead, the message must identify itself as an ad, and it must include a valid physical postal address. It must also explain how to opt out, and you must honour opt-outs within 10 business days. The FTC says each violating email can carry a penalty of up to $53,088.

Gmail adds rules for bulk senders, defined as 5,000 or more messages a day to Gmail accounts. They must set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC, support one-click unsubscribe and keep the spam rate below 0.30%. Most small agencies send far fewer, but Google also recommends staying under 0.10%, and authentication helps any sender. ## What Does the Math Look Like?

All inputs are **assumptions** for illustration, applied to the MailerLite real estate medians. They are not a forecast.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| List size | 500 contacts |
| Sends per month | 2 |
| Open rate | 40.37% (benchmark) |
| Click rate | 1.72% (benchmark) |
| Unsubscribe rate per send | 0.16% (benchmark) |

At 500 contacts and 1.72% clicks, one send produces about 9 clicks, so two sends a month give roughly 17. Each send loses about one subscriber to unsubscribes. If 1 in 20 clickers asked for a valuation (assumption), that is under one inquiry a month from the list.

That is small, which is the honest picture. Email grows with list size and with the quality of the offer. A list of 2,000 people who knew you, such as past clients and open-house visitors, will do more than 2,000 bought addresses. ## How Do You Measure Whether It Works?

Track four things monthly: list growth, click rate, replies and booked conversations. Ask each new client how they found you and record the answer. Do not judge by opens alone.

If clicks fall for three sends in a row, shorten the email and give it one link. If unsubscribes jump, check how often you send and whether the list includes people who never asked to be on it.

## FAQ

### How often should a real estate agent send email?

There is no official figure. A monthly note to past clients and two to four messages in a new-lead sequence is a sensible starting point. Watch unsubscribes and click rate, and adjust.

### What is a good open rate for real estate emails?

MailerLite reports a median of 40.37% for real estate in 2025. Open rates are inflated by Apple Mail Privacy Protection, so look at clicks (1.72% median) and replies too.

### Can I email people I met at an open house?

Under CAN-SPAM you can send commercial email without prior consent, but you must follow the rules above, including a clear opt-out. In the UK and EU, rules on consent apply, so get permission first. If in doubt, ask for sign-up on the visitor form.

### Do I need a paid email tool?

Not at the start. MailerLite's pricing page lists a free plan for up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, with up to 3 active automations. Check the page for current limits.

### Is email better than social media for agents?

Neither is proven better in general. Email goes to people who opted in, while social reaches new people. The sources here do not compare them for real estate.
