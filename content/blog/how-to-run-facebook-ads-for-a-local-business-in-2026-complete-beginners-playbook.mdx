To run Facebook ads for a local business, pick one goal (calls, bookings or form leads), target a small radius around your location, start with a modest daily budget and track results outside Facebook as well. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put the average click at $0.60 for traffic campaigns and $1.80 for lead campaigns, with $27.39 per lead. Those are averages across industries, not promises.

This guide is for owners of local shops, salons, studios and cafes in the US and other English-speaking markets. Every figure is sourced at the end, and the budget example is labelled as an assumption.

## Is Facebook Worth It for a Local Business?

Reach is there. Pew's 2026 survey (fieldwork 30 January to 18 June 2026) found 70% of US adults use Facebook, 51% use Instagram and 84% use YouTube. By age, 78% of 30 to 49 year olds and 71% of 50 to 64 year olds use Facebook, which covers many home-owning local customers.

Reach is not demand, though. A Google search shows someone who wants a service now, while a Facebook ad interrupts someone scrolling. Our comparison of [Google Ads and Facebook Ads for local business](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business) covers when each works better.

## How Do You Set Up Your First Campaign?

The steps below are this guide's recommended order, not Meta's required one.

1. **Prepare the Page and the offer.** Hours, address, photos and one clear offer, such as a first-visit price.
2. **Choose one goal.** Leads for forms, traffic for a booking page, or messages if you answer chats fast. One goal per campaign keeps results readable.
3. **Draw a tight area.** Target a radius around the shop you can realistically serve, then widen only if results justify it.
4. **Use one image or short video and one sentence.** Show the real place, staff or result, with the offer and the action.
5. **Set up tracking before spending.** Install the pixel or use your booking tool's source field. Our guide to [Facebook Pixel vs Google tag](/blog/facebook-pixel-vs-google-tag-which-to-install-first) explains which to install first.

## How Much Should You Spend to Start?

Meta's help center says daily budgets are an average: on some days spend can run up to 25% over, and charges average out across the calendar week. Meta also says most advertisers should allocate at least USD 1 a day for an ad set, though this varies by vertical, bid strategy and optimization. 
These Meta points come from search excerpts, because the page text did not load for direct fetching.

Meta says an ad set is flagged as learning limited when it is unlikely to reach about 50 optimization events in the week after its last significant edit. A tiny budget pointed at a rare event, such as a purchase, may never exit that state. Our [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) goes deeper.

## What Mistakes Waste Beginner Budgets?

- Running ads without any way to tell which lead became a customer.
- Targeting a whole city when customers come from three neighborhoods.
- Changing the audience, creative and budget at once, which restarts learning and hides what worked.
- Sending clicks to a home page instead of a booking page or form.
- Ignoring replies. A lead who waits two days has likely booked elsewhere.

## FAQ

### How much do Facebook ads cost for a local business?

LocaliQ's 2026 averages are $0.60 a click for traffic campaigns and $1.80 for lead campaigns, at $27.39 per lead. Industry rows vary, such as $50.91 per lead for Beauty and Personal Care. Your cost depends on your area and offer.

### What is the minimum budget?

Meta says most advertisers should allocate at least USD 1 a day per ad set, but a useful test needs more clicks than that. Plan for a month of data before judging.

### Should I boost posts or use Ads Manager?

Boosting is simpler. Ads Manager gives more control over goals and tracking. This guide recommends Ads Manager once you want to measure leads or bookings.

### How long until I know if it works?

Long enough for roughly 50 optimization events per ad set per week, according to Meta's learning-limited guidance. For a small shop that may mean choosing a more frequent event, like a lead form, instead of a rare one.
