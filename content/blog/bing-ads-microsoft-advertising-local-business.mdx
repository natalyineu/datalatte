Microsoft Advertising (formerly Bing Ads) is worth testing for a local business once Google Ads and your Google Business Profile are working, but it should not replace them. It tends to suit home services, financial services and B2B businesses whose customers search on desktop, and it often has less competition and lower costs per click than Google for the same keywords, though volume is much smaller.

This guide gives an honest look at who it fits, how it compares with Google and Meta, what to budget for a test and how to run that test without wasting money.

## What Is Microsoft Advertising?

Microsoft Advertising is the search advertising platform that serves ads on Bing and across Microsoft's search network, including partner sites and the Edge browser's default search, and ads can also appear in Microsoft products such as Copilot. Its audience is a fraction of Google's, and the share varies by country and by device. Look at your own website analytics (Bing organic traffic) and Google Analytics device reports to see how many of your visitors use Bing.

The audience skews toward desktop users and, in many markets, older and higher-income users than the average Google searcher, but treat that as a hypothesis to test for your business rather than a rule.

## Ad Formats Available

- **Search ads:** keyword-targeted text ads, using the same auction idea as Google Ads.
- **Audience ads:** native-style ads across Microsoft properties, where available in your market.
- **Shopping campaigns:** product listing ads for e-commerce.
- **Local-related features:** location extensions and listings through Bing Places, depending on your market. Check which features are available to you.

A useful detail: you can usually import existing Google Ads campaigns into Microsoft Advertising, then edit them. That makes a test cheaper to set up.

## Who Should Use Microsoft Advertising?

It most often works for:

- **Home services** such as plumbing, HVAC, roofing and cleaning
- **Financial and professional services** such as accountants and insurance agents
- **B2B local businesses** reaching office-based decision makers on desktop

It is less likely to be a priority for businesses driven by mobile "near me" searches, such as coffee shops, nail salons and gyms, where Google Maps and Google Business Profile tend to dominate.

<Callout type="tip">
Microsoft Advertising often has less advertiser competition than Google, which can mean lower cost per click for the same keywords. How much lower varies by industry and market, so compare actual costs in your own account.
</Callout>

## Where Microsoft Advertising Falls Short

Total search volume is far smaller than Google's, so lead volume will be lower. Reporting and local features can differ from Google's, and for mobile-first local searches Google Maps is usually the main competitor to beat. Treat Microsoft Advertising as a supplementary channel, not a Google Ads replacement.

<Callout type="warning">
Do not move budget away from Google Ads or Meta Ads to fund a Microsoft Advertising test unless you have a specific reason to believe your audience is on Bing. Treat it as incremental budget for testing.
</Callout>

## Cost Expectations

Microsoft Advertising uses a cost-per-click model. Click prices depend on your keywords, location and competition, so use the platform's keyword planner and your own data. Because volume is low, a test needs a few weeks to gather enough data. Set a small daily budget you can sustain for 4-6 weeks, and decide your maximum total test spend before you start, so a poor result does not drain your budget.

## How to Test Microsoft Advertising Without Wasting Budget

1. **Start with your best-performing Google keywords.** Import the campaign and review it, removing anything that does not fit.
2. **Run one campaign, one objective, one offer** for the first month. Avoid testing many variables at once.
3. **Set a hard budget cap** for the test period.
4. **Track cost per booking or per lead, not cost per click.** A cheap click that never converts is more expensive than a costly click that does. Set up conversion tracking with the Microsoft UET tag.
5. **Compare against your Google baseline.** A new platform only deserves ongoing budget if it beats or meaningfully diversifies your existing channels.
6. **Claim your Bing Places listing** at the same time so your business details are accurate on Bing and Microsoft Maps.

## Setup Checklist Before You Launch

A few settings decide whether a small test gives you usable data:

- **Install the UET tag and set conversion goals** for calls, form submissions and bookings. Without these you will only see clicks.
- **Check location settings.** As on Google, choose the option that targets people in your service area rather than people who merely show interest in it, and review the locations report.
- **Review imported settings.** Imported campaigns can bring over match types, bids and ad schedules that suit Google but not your Bing test, so check each one.
- **Add location and call extensions** so your address and phone number appear in the ads.
- **Use negative keywords** from your Google search terms report so you do not pay for irrelevant clicks again.

## How to Read the Results

Give the test long enough to collect a meaningful number of clicks and conversions, often four to six weeks for a small account. Then compare:

- Cost per lead or booking against your Google Ads figure
- Lead quality: do Bing leads close at a similar rate?
- Device and time-of-day patterns, such as weekday office hours on desktop
- Whether the leads are additional or just people who would have found you on Google anyway

If Microsoft Advertising produces leads at a similar or better cost, even at low volume, it can be worth keeping on a modest budget. If not, stop the test and put the money back into your stronger channels. A failed test is still useful, because it tells you your audience is on Google.

## Microsoft Advertising vs Google Ads vs Meta Ads

| | Microsoft Advertising | Google Ads | Meta Ads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intent | Search intent | Search intent, largest volume | Interruption and discovery |
| Audience | Smaller, often desktop-heavy | Largest search audience | Large social audience |
| Competition | Often lower | Highest | Varies |
| Best for | Home services, finance, B2B tests | Capturing most local demand | Awareness and retargeting |

## A Quick Pre-Test Checklist

Before you spend anything on Microsoft Advertising, confirm:

- **Google Ads is already profitable.** You should know your cost per lead from Google so you have a baseline.
- **Conversion tracking works.** Install the Microsoft UET tag and test a call and a form submission.
- **Your landing page is ready.** It should load quickly on desktop, state your service area and make it easy to call or book.
- **Your locations are right.** Imported campaigns sometimes carry over broad locations, so check radius, bids and negative keywords.
- **You know your stop rule.** Decide in advance what cost per lead is acceptable and when you will pause.

If your numbers look promising after a month, increase the budget slowly and keep comparing each channel's cost per lead so the budget follows performance.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Microsoft Advertising worth it for small local businesses?**
A: It depends on your audience and business model. It often suits home services, financial services and B2B businesses targeting desktop users, but it is not a good general-purpose channel for every local business.

**Q: How much does Microsoft Advertising cost?**
A: It is pay-per-click, and prices vary by keyword and market. Often it is cheaper than Google for similar keywords, but check in your own account. Set a small test budget and cap.

**Q: Should I use Microsoft Advertising instead of Google Ads?**
A: No. Treat it as a supplementary channel. Google Ads and Google Business Profile should remain your foundation.

**Q: Can I import my Google Ads campaigns?**
A: Usually yes, using the import tool, then review settings, locations and budgets.

**Q: How do I know if it is right for my business?**
A: Check how many of your website visitors come from Bing, what devices they use and what your customers' buying journey looks like. If they match the platform's strengths, run a controlled test.
