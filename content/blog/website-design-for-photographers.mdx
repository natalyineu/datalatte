A photography website gets booked when it shows your strongest work on the first screen, makes package prices easy to find and gives visitors one clear next step, such as checking availability. Photos are heavy, so image handling matters as much as layout. The site also needs visitors. [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/photography-advertising) cites IBISWorld (2024) for roughly 115,000 professional photographers competing in the U.S., so plan the ad budget alongside the build, then judge the site by the enquiries it produces.

## How should the first screen of a photography site look?

Lead with the work you want to be hired for. Visitors decide within the first screen whether your style fits theirs, so the opening image should be the strongest frame in your portfolio, not a logo or a generic banner. Add a short caption with the type of shoot and the location, plus one button such as "Check availability."

Lead time matters as much as style. Adwave says wedding photographers often book 8-18 months in advance, while senior portrait photographers book 3-6 months out. A wedding site should show open dates early and a calendar of upcoming seasons. A senior portrait site can focus on the sessions still open for the coming months.

## Should a photography website show prices?

A visitor who cannot tell whether you fit their budget often leaves without enquiring. Publish a "packages from" line and list what each package includes, such as session length, number of edited images and print options.

Also explain what changes the total, such as travel, a second location or extra hours of editing. Clients who see those factors up front are less likely to feel surprised by the final quote. Set your numbers from your own costs, not from a competitor's page.

## How do you keep image-heavy pages fast?

Compress every photo before upload and use modern formats such as WebP or AVIF where your platform supports them. Give each image a descriptive file name and alt text that describes the picture. Set image dimensions so the page does not jump while it loads.

Do not lazy-load the first image on the page, because it is usually the one visitors need to see immediately. Test the homepage and one gallery page in PageSpeed Insights after each big change, and fix the largest image first if the score drops. ## Should clients book online?

Online booking reduces the back-and-forth that slows enquiries. In a 2025 Eliciting Insights survey of 384 U.S. adults who had visited a healthcare provider, 95% had booked a medical appointment online or said they would if the option existed. That study covers healthcare, not photography, so treat it as a sign of demand for self-service booking rather than a photography benchmark.

The same survey found 92% of people who had already booked online planned to use online scheduling again, per [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/appointment-booking-page-design). Use a calendar or inquiry form that loads on a phone and confirms the request by email.

## How much should a photographer budget for ads?

[Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/photography-advertising) recommends single photographers budget $500-$2,500 a month on Google Ads, depending on specialty and market, and $300-$1,500 a month on Meta. The same page gives an expected Google Ads cost per inquiry of $25-$85, with close rates of 15-35% depending on specialty.

Photography to Profits offers weekly optimization reviews of client campaigns and a 120-day guarantee that refunds management fees if clients do not recover those fees within 120 days. The guarantee does not cover ad spend or other costs, so read the terms before you sign. ## What does a $500 ad month return?

All inputs below are **assumptions**, not benchmarks.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $500 |
| Cost per inquiry | $40 |
| Share of inquiries that book | 25% |
| Average booking value | $600 |

Inquiries: $500 / $40 = 12.5. Bookings: 12.5 x 25% = 3.1 (rounded). Gross booking value: 3.1 x $600 = about $1,875. Subtract ad spend of $500 and the rest of your costs before you judge the result. Gross value is not profit, and a single month can swing widely on booking dates.

Vendor calculators use different defaults. Photography to Profits' calculator uses a $1,000 monthly budget, a $25 cost per lead, a 25% lead-to-booking rate and a $2,500 average booking value. Replace those with your own figures before you rely on any output.

## Should you build the site yourself?

A simple portfolio can be built with a template, but the work that wins bookings is the structure: clear galleries, visible prices and a booking step on every page. If you lack time for that, a developer may be the better use of budget. Our [DIY website vs developer comparison](/blog/diy-website-vs-professional-developer) covers the trade-offs.

Check the commercial rights of any template, font or design-tool asset before launch. Those assets can carry limits on paid promotion, resale, broadcast use or transfer to a client, so record the licence before your site goes live.

## How do you track whether the site is working?

Track each enquiry by source, and record how many turn into consultations, bookings and repeat clients. A weekly scorecard keeps the numbers honest. A salon-focused guide from [Rozhair](https://rozhair.com/blogs/education/hair-salon-advertising-ideas) suggests tracking ad spend, qualified inquiries, consultations booked, appointments completed, revenue from them and second appointments booked. Adapt that list to shoots instead of appointments.

Review the numbers once a week. If inquiries rise but bookings do not, the problem is usually the packages or the prices on the page, not the traffic.

## FAQ

### How many photos should a gallery have?

No fixed number is standard. Show your strongest work for each type of shoot, and keep galleries small enough that pages still load quickly on a phone.

### Do I need a blog?

Not necessarily. A few location or session-type pages with real photos and captions often do more for a local photographer than a long general blog.

### Should I use watermarks?

That depends on how you license and share your work. Decide based on your contracts and on how often your images get shared without credit.

### Which image format should I use?

WebP and AVIF are both supported by modern browsers. Compare file size and visual quality on your own photos before you choose a default.
