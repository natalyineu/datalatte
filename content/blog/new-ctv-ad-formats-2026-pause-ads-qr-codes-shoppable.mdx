The new CTV ad formats in 2026 go beyond the 15 or 30-second pre-roll. The IAB Tech Lab's CTV Ad Portfolio standardizes six of them: pause ads, menu ads, screensaver ads, in-scene ads, squeezebacks and overlays. On top of these sit interactive units, such as Amazon's "Add to Cart" and "Send to Phone" calls to action, and QR codes. For local businesses, pause ads and sequential campaigns are the most practical starting points. QR codes work as an extra layer, not as the main goal.

Picture it. A couple is halfway through a series on Friday night. One of them hits pause to grab the popcorn. The screen freezes on a still image: your salon, a before-and-after photo, "Book this week, 20% off your first visit." Nobody is skipping it. Nobody is on their phone. They are looking at the TV and they are waiting. That is the whole pitch for pause ads, and it is why the format exists.

The IAB Tech Lab published the portfolio and updated programmatic guidance in December 2025 for public comment, distilling the six formats from more than 100 industry submissions ([MarTech](https://martech.org/iab-tech-lab-introduces-new-ctv-ad-formats-updates-programmatic-guidance/), checked Oct 2026). Standardization matters because buyers know exactly what they are bidding on, so these formats are easier to buy through programmatic platforms (DSPs) and not only through direct deals. Availability and minimums still vary by platform. Confirm what each one offers today.

## Format 1: Pause ads

A viewer pauses a stream and a branded still image appears. The viewer is already looking at the screen, so the ad gets unhurried attention.

**Where it is offered:** Hulu, Peacock and Max were reported to be rolling out pause ads in 2023 ([Variety](https://au.variety.com/2023/tv/news/pause-ads-hulu-max-peacock-streaming-11601), checked Oct 2026). Amazon added interactive pause ads on Prime Video, with "Add to Cart" and "Learn More" overlays ([Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/news/amazon-ads-announces-three-new-streaming-tv-ad-formats), checked Oct 2026).

**The creative:** A static or lightly animated image with your logo, one offer and one call to action. It is closer to a billboard than a video, so design it separately from your TV spot. Do not paste in a frame from the video.

**Best for local businesses:** Time-limited offers, seasonal promotions and brand reminders. Ask your platform for current pricing, since rates for pause inventory differ from standard video.

<Callout type="tip">Design pause ads at 16:9 with the key message and offer clearly visible, and leave room for platform UI elements. Test how it looks on a real TV before launch.</Callout>

## Format 2: QR codes on CTV

QR overlays let viewers scan with their phone to visit a landing page, booking form or app download.

**This does not work as a volume driver.** The viewer has to pick up a second device mid-show, find the camera and aim it at a screen across the room. Expect scans to be a small share of impressions. We found no reliable public benchmark, so measure your own with a dedicated landing page. At a small monthly budget you may see only a handful of scans. The next section shows why.

**Where they work better:**

- "Send to phone" actions, which remove the scanning step. Amazon lists "Send to Phone" among its interactive call-to-action options ([Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/interactive-ads-expand-across-streaming-tv-into-prime-video), checked Oct 2026).
- Clear, time-limited offers ("Scan for a free coffee this week") rather than a generic "learn more".

<Callout type="warning">Do not build a CTV campaign around QR scans as the main KPI. Treat them as a supplemental layer and judge the campaign on reach, branded search, visits and bookings.</Callout>

## The math: what a QR code has to deliver

Every number below is an assumption for illustration. None of it is a market statistic or a real campaign result. Swap in your own.

Example: $1,000 budget, assume a $25 CPM (cost per thousand impressions), a $60 average ticket, and that 1 in 4 people who scan goes on to book.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | $1,000 / $25 x 1,000 | 40,000 |
| Scans at 0.05% (assumed) | 40,000 x 0.0005 | 20 |
| Bookings (25% of scans) | 20 x 0.25 | 5 |
| Revenue | 5 x $60 | $300 |
| Cost per booking | $1,000 / 5 | $200 |
| Scans needed to break even | 17 bookings x $60 = $1,020, so 17 / 0.25 | 68 |
| Scan rate needed | 68 / 40,000 | 0.17% |

In this example, the QR code alone loses money: you would need roughly three and a half times the assumed scan rate to break even. That does not make the campaign pointless. The other 39,980 impressions still built awareness, and some of those households will search your name later. It does mean you should not judge a CTV campaign by scans. Track branded search and new-customer counts too, and change the $25, 0.05%, 25% and $60 to your own figures.

## Format 3: Shoppable TV and interactive overlays

Interactive units let viewers act on the TV without a second device, for example using the remote to add a product to a cart. Amazon's interactive formats include shoppable carousels, interactive pause ads and brand trivia ads. When they launched, they were available to select US advertisers that sell on Amazon, so check whether you qualify ([Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/interactive-ads-expand-across-streaming-tv-into-prime-video), checked Oct 2026). Other platforms offer their own versions. These units suit businesses with a clear, high-value action such as selling products online, booking a table or requesting a quote.

## Format 4: Premium takeovers and exclusive placements

Some streaming services sell app-launch takeovers, exclusive ad breaks (solo ads) or first-position ads in a break (prime pods). These are usually negotiated directly with the streamer and are rarely a fit for a single-location business. Know they exist, then buy programmatically.

## Format 5: Sequential storytelling and retargeting

Sequential messaging is one of the most useful CTV approaches for small businesses. It does not need a new format, only smart campaign setup.

1. **Chapter 1 (awareness):** A short spot introduces your business to households in your target ZIP codes.
2. **Chapter 2 (consideration):** Households that saw Chapter 1 get a longer message about your service.
3. **Chapter 3 (offer):** The same households see a clear offer and call to action.

Some programmatic platforms can also extend retargeting to phones and laptops in the same household after someone sees your TV ad. Support and minimums differ by platform, so confirm current requirements. ## The IAB CTV ad portfolio at a glance

| Format | Description | Typical use |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Pause Ad | Appears when the viewer pauses | Offers and brand recall |
| Menu Ad | Shown on the navigation screens of streaming apps | Awareness with low interruption |
| Screensaver Ad | Displayed during periods of inactivity | Longer exposure, lower intent |
| In-Scene Ad | Integrated into the show environment | Recall without an ad break |
| Squeezeback | Video shrinks and the ad displays beside it | Content and ad visible together |
| Overlay Ad | Light graphical element over the content | Direct response layer |

Descriptions follow the [IAB Tech Lab portfolio coverage](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2025/12/12/iab-tech-lab-defines-six-core-ad-formats-in-new-ctv-portfolio) (checked Oct 2026).

## Which format fits your business?

| Business type | Suggested starting point | Why |
|---------------|--------------------------|-----|
| Coffee shop or restaurant | Pause ads with a time-limited offer | Simple creative, foot traffic goal |
| Hair salon or beauty | Sequential campaign with retargeting | Show the work, then retarget with a booking offer |
| Fitness studio | Sequential storytelling | Longer decision cycle suits awareness then a trial offer |
| Pet groomer | Pause ads with a clear booking call to action | Strong visual recognition |
| E-commerce | Interactive or shoppable formats where available | Direct path to purchase |

## Do this before you spend a dollar

1. Write one offer in under 10 words. If it needs a second sentence, it is two offers.
2. Build one landing page with one button, used only by this campaign.
3. Create a promo code (for example "TV") and train staff to ask for it.
4. Ask your platform for three numbers in writing: the CPM, the minimum spend and the targeting options by ZIP code.
5. Note your branded-search clicks in Google Search Console for the 4 weeks before launch, so you can compare.
6. Plan to review results at 4 weeks, not 4 days.

## FAQ

**Q: What are the new CTV ad formats in 2026?**
A: The IAB Tech Lab's CTV Ad Portfolio covers pause ads, menu ads, screensaver ads, in-scene ads, squeezebacks and overlays. Interactive, shoppable and QR-code units sit on top of these.

**Q: How much does CTV advertising cost for a small business?**
A: It varies widely by platform, targeting and format. Many programmatic platforms let you start with a modest monthly budget, but you need enough impressions to learn anything. Check current minimums and CPMs before planning, or see our [CTV cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data).

**Q: Do I need a TV commercial to run CTV ads?**
A: Not for every format. Pause ads and similar units use static images that you can design in a tool like Canva. Standard video formats still need a video spot.

**Q: What is the difference between pause ads and screensaver ads?**
A: Pause ads appear when the viewer deliberately pauses a show. Screensaver ads appear during periods of inactivity, so the viewer may not be looking at the screen.

**Q: How do I measure CTV results for a local business?**
A: Use unique promo codes or landing pages, track branded search and direct traffic, and look at store-visit or household attribution where the platform offers it.
