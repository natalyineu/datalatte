The new CTV ad formats in 2026 go beyond the 15 or 30-second pre-roll: pause ads, menu ads, screensaver ads, in-scene ads, squeezebacks and overlays, plus interactive and shoppable units with QR codes or "send to phone" actions. For local businesses, pause ads and sequential campaigns are usually the most practical starting points, while QR codes work best as an extra layer rather than the main goal.

In October 2025 the IAB Tech Lab released its CTV Ad Portfolio, a standard covering six non-pre-roll formats: Pause Ads, Menu Ads, Screensaver Ads, In-Scene Ads, Squeezebacks and Overlays. Standardization matters because it makes these formats easier to buy through programmatic platforms (DSPs) rather than only through direct deals with each streaming service. Availability and minimums still vary by platform, so check what each one currently offers.

## Format 1: Pause Ads

When a viewer pauses a stream, a branded still image appears after a short delay. The viewer is already looking at the screen, so the ad gets unhurried attention. Platforms and ad vendors often report strong recall and viewability for this format, but results vary by campaign, so treat published figures as vendor benchmarks, not guarantees.

**Where it is offered:** Hulu, Peacock, Amazon Prime Video and several other streaming services, with more inventory becoming available through programmatic buying.

**What the creative looks like:** A static or lightly animated image with your logo, an offer and a clear call to action. It is closer to a billboard than a video, so design it separately from your TV spot.

**Best for local businesses:** Time-limited offers ("Book this week, 20% off your first visit"), seasonal promotions and brand reminders. Pause ads also typically carry a CPM premium over standard video, so check current pricing.

<Callout type="tip">Design pause ads at 16:9 with the key message and offer clearly visible, and leave room for platform UI elements. Test how it looks on a real TV before launch.</Callout>

## Format 2: QR Codes on CTV

QR overlays let viewers scan with their phone to visit a landing page, booking form or app download.

**Be realistic about response.** Industry benchmarks suggest scan rates on CTV are very low, a tiny fraction of a percent of impressions, because viewers have to pick up a second device mid-show. At a small monthly budget you may see only a handful of scans, so QR codes are not a volume driver.

**Where they work better:**

- Native "send to phone" style actions, which remove the scanning step. Check what is currently offered on Amazon and other platforms.
- Shoppable CTV formats that use your product feed, such as YouTube's shopping formats tied to Google Merchant Center.
- Clear, time-limited offers ("Scan for a free coffee this week") rather than generic "learn more".

<Callout type="warning">Do not build a CTV campaign around QR scans as the main KPI. Treat them as a supplemental layer and judge the campaign on reach, branded search, visits and bookings.</Callout>

## Format 3: Shoppable TV and Interactive Overlays

Interactive overlays let viewers act on the TV without a second device, for example using the remote to request information or save a product to a cart. Different platforms support different versions: Amazon offers interactive and "send to phone" ads, Roku has shoppable and interactive formats in its ad manager, and some streamers offer in-TV purchase flows. Interactive units generally cost more than standard video, so they make the most sense for businesses with a clear, high-value action such as booking a table, requesting a quote or starting a trial.

## Format 4: Arrival, Solo and Prime Pod Ads

Some streaming services sell premium placements such as app-launch takeovers, exclusive ad breaks (solo ads) or first-position ads in a break (prime pods). These are typically sold through direct deals with large minimum budgets and are rarely a fit for a single-location business. It is useful to know they exist, but you will usually get better value from programmatic buys.

## Format 5: Sequential Storytelling and Retargeting

Sequential messaging is one of the most useful CTV approaches for small businesses, and it does not need a new format, only smart campaign setup.

1. **Chapter 1 (awareness):** A short spot introduces your business to households in your target ZIP codes.
2. **Chapter 2 (consideration):** Households that saw Chapter 1 get a longer message about your service.
3. **Chapter 3 (offer):** The same households see a clear offer and call to action.

Some platforms can also extend retargeting to phones and laptops in the same household after someone sees your TV ad. Platforms such as The Trade Desk, MNTN, StackAdapt and Roku Ads Manager offer versions of this, with different minimums, so confirm current requirements. ## The IAB CTV Ad Portfolio at a Glance

| Format | Description | Typical use |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Pause Ad | Appears when the viewer pauses | Offers and brand recall |
| Menu Ad | Appears in platform navigation menus | Awareness with low interruption |
| Screensaver Ad | Appears during idle states | Longer exposure, lower intent |
| In-Scene Ad | Virtual product placement inside content | Recall without an ad break |
| Squeezeback | Content shrinks, ad fills the remaining space | Content and ad visible together |
| Overlay Ad | Semi-transparent banner over playing content | Direct response layer |

## Which Format Fits Your Business?

| Business type | Suggested starting point | Why |
|---------------|--------------------------|-----|
| Coffee shop or restaurant | Pause ads with a time-limited offer | Simple creative, foot traffic goal |
| Hair salon or beauty | Sequential campaign with retargeting | Show the work, then retarget with a booking offer |
| Fitness studio | Sequential storytelling | Longer decision cycle suits awareness then a trial offer |
| Pet groomer | Pause ads with a clear booking call to action | Strong visual recognition |
| E-commerce | Shoppable formats tied to your product feed | Direct path to purchase |

## FAQ

**Q: What are the new CTV ad formats in 2026?**
A: The IAB Tech Lab's CTV Ad Portfolio covers pause ads, menu ads, screensaver ads, in-scene ads, squeezebacks and overlays. Interactive, shoppable and QR-code units sit on top of these.

**Q: How much does CTV advertising cost for a small business?**
A: It varies widely by platform, targeting and format. Many programmatic platforms let you start with a modest monthly budget, but you need enough impressions to learn anything. Check current minimums and CPMs before planning.

**Q: Do I need a TV commercial to run CTV ads?**
A: Not for every format. Pause ads and similar units use static images that you can design in a tool like Canva. Standard video formats still need a video spot.

**Q: What is the difference between pause ads and screensaver ads?**
A: Pause ads appear when the viewer deliberately pauses a show. Screensaver ads appear when the device is idle, so the viewer may not be looking at the screen.

**Q: How do I measure CTV results for a local business?**
A: Use unique promo codes or landing pages, track branded search and direct traffic, and look at store-visit or household attribution where the platform offers it.
