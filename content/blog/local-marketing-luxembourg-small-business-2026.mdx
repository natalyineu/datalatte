Local marketing in Luxembourg starts with language and channel choice, not budget. DataReportal's Digital 2026 report puts internet use at 98.8% of the population, and STATEC counts 46.6% of residents as foreign nationals. A small business there usually needs ads and pages in at least French and English, with German or Luxembourgish added where its customers call for it.

This guide is for owners of cafes, salons, studios and similar local businesses in Luxembourg. Every figure comes from a source listed at the end. Where a number is a planning assumption, the text says so. Nothing here is a cost-per-click benchmark, because no public Luxembourg figure was found.

## How big is the Luxembourg market?

STATEC, the national statistics office, counted 690,959 residents on 1 January 2026, up 1.3% on 2025, according to Chronicle.lu's report of the release. DataReportal uses a slightly lower United Nations figure of 682 thousand for October 2025, so the two sources differ by date and method.

The market is small, so the audience you can reach is capped. A salon in Luxembourg City competes for a pool of under 700,000 residents, plus commuters. Because the pool is small, precise targeting by area and language matters more than raw reach.

The foreign-national share is 46.6%, down from 47.0% the year before. The Portuguese are the largest group at 12.8% of the population, then the French at 7.1% and Italians at 3.7%. That mix is why a Portuguese-language line in a Facebook post can make sense for a neighbourhood shop, even though Portuguese is not an administrative language.

## Which languages should a Luxembourg business use?

Luxembourg has no single commercial language, and a private business can choose its own.

The 2021 census gives a practical guide. Luxembourgish was the main language of 48.9% of residents, French of 14.9%, English of 3.6% and German of about 3%. At work, French is the most common language across most municipalities, while English leads in workplaces around the capital: 58% in the capital itself (STATEC, RP2021).

Read this as follows. Customers near offices in the capital are likely to read English. Customers in smaller towns lean toward Luxembourgish and French. If you can run only two languages, French and English cover the most workplaces near the capital, but check your own booking data after the first month.

## What do the social media numbers mean for ads?

DataReportal reports ad reach, not active users, and warns that change figures may be distorted by platform corrections. Within that limit, the Digital 2026 numbers are useful for choosing a channel.

| Platform | Ad reach | Share of population | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook | 313 thousand | 45.9% | down 6.1% |
| Instagram | 290 thousand | 42.5% | up 2.9% |
| TikTok (18+) | 217 thousand | 39.2% of adults | up 15.1% |
| LinkedIn | 470 thousand | 68.9% | up 9.3% |
| Snapchat | 465 thousand | 68.2% | up 5.1% |

LinkedIn's figure counts registered members, so it includes many cross-border workers and inactive profiles. It still suggests the platform reaches a large share of the working population. Whether LinkedIn ads sell haircuts or coffee is a separate question that only a small test can answer. Start with Instagram and Facebook for consumer services, and treat LinkedIn as an experiment.

## How does Google Ads handle several languages?

Google says language targeting reaches people based on the languages they understand, using the search term language, the user's language settings and preferences. Google also announced that the campaign-level language setting for Search campaigns will be removed in September 2026, with Search ads matching on the language of the ads themselves ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722078?hl=en)).

In practice, write separate ad groups with ads, keywords and landing pages in each language you want to serve. Do not rely on a campaign setting to choose for you. A French search should land on a French page, even if your site defaults to English.

Our [Google Ads for European businesses guide](/blog/google-ads-for-european-businesses) covers account setup, and the [Europe local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide-europe) helps size a monthly spend.

## What about cross-border commuters?

Many people commute into Luxembourg from France, Belgium and Germany, but this guide did not verify a current official count of cross-border workers, so no figure is given.

For a lunch-hour business near the financial district, that daytime crowd can matter as much as residents. For a business that depends on repeat visits from people who live nearby, it matters less. Check where your regular customers actually come from before spending on geo-targeting across a border.

Google Ads location targeting lets you include or exclude places. If your booking system records a postcode, compare it with your ad clicks after four weeks and adjust.

## What would a first month look like?

The example below is an **assumption**, not a benchmark. It shows how to split a small test across languages, so you can replace each input with your own numbers.

| Input | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Monthly Google Ads budget | EUR 600 |
| Share to French ad group | 50% |
| Share to English ad group | 35% |
| Share to German ad group | 15% |
| Resulting spend | EUR 300, EUR 210, EUR 90 |
| Test length | 4 weeks, then review |

After four weeks, move budget toward the language with the most calls or bookings per euro spent. If the German group produced nothing from EUR 90, pause it and test Luxembourgish or Portuguese copy with the same money. A group needs enough clicks to judge, so wait before cutting anything on the first week's data.

## How do you get local reviews and listings right?

Local searches draw on Google Business Profile and reviews. Fill in the profile in the languages your customers use, add photos and keep opening hours accurate. Our [Google Business Profile guide for Europe](/blog/google-business-profile-optimisation-europe) lists the fields to complete, and the [online reviews strategy for Europe](/blog/online-reviews-strategy-europe) shows how to ask for reviews in a way that fits local habits.

Ask each happy customer for a review in the language they used with you. Reviews in several languages also show newcomers that the business serves people like them.

## FAQ

### Do I need to market in three languages?

Not always. The census shows French and English cover most workplaces near the capital, and Luxembourgish dominates in smaller towns. Start with two languages, check which brings bookings, and add a third where the data supports it.

### Is Luxembourg too small for paid ads?

The audience is small, so budgets that work elsewhere can saturate quickly. Use tight location targeting and review results monthly. No public Luxembourg click-cost benchmark was found, so measure your own cost per booking.

### Which social platform has the most reach?

By Digital 2026 ad reach, LinkedIn has 470 thousand members and Snapchat 465 thousand, ahead of Facebook at 313 thousand and Instagram at 290 thousand. Reach is not the same as buying intent, so test before committing budget.

### How many people live in Luxembourg?

STATEC counted 690,959 on 1 January 2026. DataReportal shows 682 thousand for October 2025 using UN data.

### Are there legal rules for email or SMS marketing?

Yes, EU data protection rules apply, but this guide did not verify the Luxembourg-specific requirements. Check the national data protection authority, the CNPD, or ask a lawyer before sending marketing messages.
