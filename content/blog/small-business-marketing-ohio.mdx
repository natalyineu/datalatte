## What Does the Ohio Market Look Like?

Large and mostly steady. BLS lists Ohio's September 2026 unemployment rate at 3.4% (preliminary, seasonally adjusted), with 5,688.3 thousand nonfarm jobs and a civilian labor force of 5,853.7 thousand. Leisure and hospitality employs 566.5 thousand, down 0.6% over 12 months.

The Census Bureau estimates Ohio's population at 11,900,510 in 2025, up 39,889 (0.34%). Births (126,825) and deaths (127,554) nearly cancelled, a natural change of minus 729. Growth came from domestic migration of 11,926 and international migration of 28,505.

For a consumer business, a stable population with fewer hospitality jobs means competing on repeat visits and reviews more than on new arrivals.

## What Did the 2026 Ohio State Fair Show?

That a single event can concentrate a lot of spending. WYSU public radio reported the fair's recap with attendance of 1,025,190 at the fair, which ended on 9 August 2026, 5.8% above 2025 and 2% above the 2023 record of 1,006,228. Midway revenue topped $3.3 million, up 8.4%, and concession revenue reached $9.2 million, up 12.6%.

The fair followed a $460 million overhaul of the grounds. The 2027 fair is scheduled for 28 July to 8 August. Businesses near the fairgrounds in Columbus can plan hours and offers for that window. No source gives how much of this spending reached nearby shops, so test with your own sales.

## How Do You Keep Ads Local in Ohio?

Set a radius around the business and leave out places you cannot serve. Google's help page says radius targeting needs a radius of at least 1 km. It recommends broad "Presence or interest" targeting as the best practice on Search, and says "Presence" only suits cases where you want to exclude people who are elsewhere but interested in your offer.

Ohio has several large metro areas and wide rural stretches. In Columbus, Cleveland or Cincinnati a 2 to 4 mile radius is a sensible first test, which is an assumption to check against customer addresses. For a fair-week offer, target the area around the fairgrounds for that window only.

Add negative keywords weekly from the Search terms report: jobs, free, DIY and services you do not offer. Google notes that negative keywords do not match close variants, so add plurals and synonyms yourself.

## How Do You Rank on Google Maps in Ohio?

Google says local results rest mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. It also states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Fill every field: categories, hours, services and website. Upload real photos, reply to every review and keep your name, address and phone identical elsewhere. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) goes through each field.

## Can You Offer a Discount for a Google Review?

No. Google's contributed content policy says merchants may not offer incentives, such as payment, discounts, free goods or services, in exchange for posting a review. You may ask customers for a review. You may not reward them for one. See [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop) for ways to ask.

## Where Can Ohio Owners Get Free Advice?

The Ohio SBDC at Columbus State describes integrated, no-cost services for businesses with fewer than 500 employees, delivered by Certified Business Advisors. Franklin County's page says you can schedule a free phone consultation to start. Other centers serve other regions, and search results list centers at Cleveland State and Ohio University.

## What Mistakes Do Ohio Owners Make?

These are recommendations, not study findings.

- Running offers in the wrong week. Plan around dates you can confirm.
- Counting fair crowds as your customers. Track your own sales that week.
- Stopping ads in slow months instead of lowering spend.
- Spreading across channels. See our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).
