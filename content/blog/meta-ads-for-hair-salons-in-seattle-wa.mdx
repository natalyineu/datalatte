## How Much Should a Seattle Salon Budget for Meta Ads?

[Adwave's channel comparison](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels) lists a standalone Meta (Facebook and Instagram) buy at $300 to $1,000 per month. Its [dental](https://adwave.com/resources/dental-advertising-get-more-patients) and [roofing](https://adwave.com/resources/roofing-company-advertising-getting-more-leads) tables use $500 to $2,000 per month for Meta. Those figures show the range local service vendors plan around, but neither is a salon benchmark.

[Bookeo's guide](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) suggests starting Facebook and Instagram ad spend at $750 to $1,500 a month. That gap matters. A $300 test and a $1,500 test produce very different amounts of data before you can judge results.

Timing matters as much as budget. Adwave lists [1 to 4 weeks to results for Meta](https://adwave.com/resources/restaurant-advertising-complete-marketing-guide), so a one-week test rarely settles the question.

## Worked Example: What a $600 Salon Budget Could Return

The inputs below are assumptions for a single calculation, not reported results. Replace each one with your own numbers.

- Monthly Meta budget: $600 (assumption)
- Cost per 1,000 impressions: $10 (assumption, inside the $5 to $15 range from Adwave)
- Cost per click: $2 (assumption, inside the $1 to $3 range from Adwave's how-to guide)
- Click-to-booking rate: 5% (assumption)
- Average booking value: $80 (assumption)

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | $600 ÷ $10 × 1,000 | 60,000 |
| Clicks | $600 ÷ $2 | 300 |
| Bookings | 300 × 5% | 15 |
| Booked revenue | 15 × $80 | $1,200 |
| Revenue per $1 spent | $1,200 ÷ $600 | 2.0 |

The implied click-through rate is 0.5%. If cost per click rises to $3 with everything else unchanged, clicks fall to 200, bookings to 10, revenue to $800, and revenue per dollar to 1.3. Booked revenue ignores product sales, no-shows and cancellations, so treat 2.0 as an upper planning figure.

## Which Ad Formats Should Salons Use?

Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, as cited by [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners), says short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram. For a salon, that points to short clips of a cut, colour process or treatment rather than a single styled photo.

A pre-shot library helps. [One of One Group](https://oneofonegroup.co.uk/social-media-marketing/hair-salons/) states that a month of salon content can be shot in a single salon visit. That is a vendor claim, so check it against your own schedule.

Restaurant advertising guides describe campaign goals such as traffic or reservations on Facebook, Instagram or both. [The Fork Manager](https://www.theforkmanager.com/en/blog/introduction-paid-ads-restaurants) describes this setup, and a salon can apply the same logic to booking traffic. Keep organic posting as a separate track, and see the [TikTok marketing guide for hair salons](/blog/tiktok-marketing-for-hair-salons) for short-video planning.

## Who Should You Target Around the Salon?

[Bookeo recommends a 5 to 10 mile radius](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) around the salon for Facebook and Instagram ads. A tighter radius may suit a dense neighbourhood, but the source gives no basis for a specific smaller number. Test within the 5 to 10 mile band before narrowing it.

Meta Ads Manager is the central tool for running ads across Instagram and Facebook, as [Adobe Express explains](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising). Because Instagram is owned by Meta, [one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook or both](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business). One budget, one radius and one creative set can serve both platforms.

Before you choose audiences, check when followers are online. Bookeo advises looking at peak times in the Instagram or Facebook Insights tab, which requires a business account. For the [Facebook side of the mix](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons), compare those hours with your booking calendar. Seattle-specific age and income splits are not in these sources, so build them from your own client list.

## How Often Should You Refresh Posts and Ads?

Bookeo says most salon owners find three to four Instagram posts a week and one to two Facebook posts a week sustainable. Use that as the organic floor, and keep ad creative in rotation alongside it. The [Instagram marketing guide for hair salons](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-hair-salons) covers how to build that schedule.

Stories need extra attention. [Associated Hair Professionals](https://www.associatedhairprofessionals.com/updates/blog-posts/power-social-media-your-hairstyling-brand) describes Instagram and Facebook stories as temporary content, so a story-based offer disappears on its own schedule. Swap ad creative on a fixed calendar rather than waiting for performance to drop.

## What Should Meta Ad Copy Say for a Salon?

Write copy that names the service and the booking step, and avoid claims you cannot source. A workable template is: "[Service] at [salon name] in [city]. Book your appointment this week." Swap in your real services and location.

Pair the copy with an offer suited to a first visit. Adwave's dental guide pairs Meta ads with new patient specials, so a first-visit offer is a reasonable test for a salon, although that source does not cover salons directly. Run two creatives at equal budget to see which one drives more bookings.

## What Should You Check Before Launching?

1. Install the Meta Pixel on your booking confirmation page and contact page.
2. Set a Lead or booking event that fires when a client submits the booking form.
3. Add UTM parameters to every ad URL so Google Analytics records Meta traffic as its own source.
4. Connect a business account so Insights shows when followers are online.
5. Review cost per click, cost per booking and bookings each week, not impressions alone.

Without these steps, the worked example above cannot be checked against real bookings. For follow-up after a booking, see the [marketing automation guide for hair salons](/blog/marketing-automation-for-hair-salons).

## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Meta ads each month?

Adwave's standalone range is $300 to $1,000 per month, while Bookeo suggests $750 to $1,500 to start. Use the worked example to test what your own prices return before committing to the higher figure.

### Does using Facebook and Instagram for the salon cost anything?

Bookeo states that Facebook and Instagram can be used to reach audiences and build trust without spending a dollar on the platform itself. Ad spend is a separate cost on top of organic posting.

### Can one campaign run on both Instagram and Facebook?

Yes. Instagram ads run through the same system as Facebook ads, Meta Ads Manager, and one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook or both.

### Where do I see when my followers are online?

In the Insights tab of Instagram or Facebook. That requires a business account, and Bookeo recommends checking it before you schedule posts.
