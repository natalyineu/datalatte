The best-documented way to reduce no-shows is a reminder, ideally a text, sent more than once. A 2016 BMJ Open meta-analysis of 21 studies found that patients who received electronic text notifications missed 15% of appointments versus 21% without them. The evidence comes from healthcare, so treat the size as a guide for salons, studios and service businesses, not a promise. Card holds and clear cancellation rules are the second lever.

This guide covers what published research and platform documentation support. No source cited here publishes no-show rates for salons or gyms, so the worked example uses labelled assumptions.

## Do Reminders Really Cut No-Shows?

Yes. The BMJ Open review pooled 21 studies, with 8,345 patients receiving text notifications and 7,731 receiving none. The risk ratio for non-attendance was 0.75. The same abstract says voice notifications appeared more effective than text, which matters if your clients ignore SMS.

The studies are clinical, so effect sizes at a hair salon may differ. Still, the mechanism, a forgotten or uncertain appointment, is the same in any business that sells a time slot.

## Should You Send More Than One Reminder?

Probably, for clients who are likely to miss. Kaiser Permanente Washington randomized high-risk visits to one text two business days ahead or two texts, two and three days ahead. The second text lowered no-shows by about 7% in primary care (RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.89 to 0.96) and about 11% in mental health (RR 0.89, 95% CI 0.86 to 0.93).

Those are relative reductions among patients already flagged as likely no-shows. A simple version for a small business: one reminder three days out and one the day before, each with a one-tap confirm or reschedule.

## Does the Wording of a Reminder Matter?

It can. In a randomized study of 161,587 appointments at Clalit Health Services in Israel, the standard reminder had a 21.1% no-show rate. An emotional-guilt message got 14.2%, an appointment-cost message 15.3%, and a social-norm message 17.8%. Plain personal-request and professional-figure messages did not differ significantly from the control.

Two limits apply. The authors could not show which psychological effect drove the difference, and the stronger messages also raised advance cancellations, for example 26.3% for the guilt message versus 17.2% in the control. Cancelling early is useful only if you can refill the slot.

## Should You Charge a Deposit or No-Show Fee?

A card hold is the practical option. Square Appointments, for example, lets you require a card to book, held for that one appointment unless the client chooses to save it. You choose a flat fee per appointment, a flat fee per service, or a percentage of each service price. The cancellation cut-off can be set from one hour to two weeks, and a fee can be charged up to 14 days after the appointment. That detail is from Square's Australian help page, so check your own region.

Fees can cost you goodwill. Write the policy in plain words at booking, and waive it for a first offence if that fits your brand. Check local consumer rules before enforcing it.

## Worked Example: Is It Worth It?

All inputs are **assumptions** for a small salon.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Appointments per month | 400 |
| Current no-show rate | 12% (48 missed) |
| Average ticket | $70 |
| Relative reduction from two reminders | 25% |
| Share of freed slots that get filled | 50% |

A 25% cut removes 12 no-shows. Half of those slots refill with a client who would otherwise have not come, so about 6 extra paid visits, roughly $420 a month. Messaging at fractions of a cent to a couple of cents per text costs only a few dollars; your booking software may already include it. Track your own rate for 8 weeks before and after.

## FAQ

### What is a normal no-show rate?

It varies by business, and none of the sources used here publish a salon or gym average. The meta-analysis reports 21% without reminders across the clinical studies it pooled.

### Is text better than email for reminders?

The review studied text notifications. Voice appeared more effective than text in its abstract. Email was not part of the comparison, so test your own audience.

### How many reminders are too many?

Two are supported by the Kaiser trial. Beyond that, watch your opt-out rate and local texting rules.

### Can I charge a cancellation fee?

Platforms such as Square support it with a card hold. Rules on fees differ by place, so state the policy at booking and check local requirements.

### Does the reminder wording matter?

In one large study, yes: the best of eight tested wordings lowered no-shows from 21.1% to 14.2%. It also raised advance cancellations, so measure both.
