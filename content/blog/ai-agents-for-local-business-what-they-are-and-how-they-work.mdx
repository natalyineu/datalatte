An AI agent is software where a language model decides its own steps and chooses which tools to use, such as a calendar, a customer list or a messaging app. Anthropic's engineering guide contrasts this with a workflow, where the model and tools follow predefined code paths. For most local businesses, a simple workflow is the safer first step, and an agent is worth it only when the task is open-ended.

## What Is the Difference Between a Workflow and an Agent?

Anthropic defines workflows as systems where language models and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths. Agents are systems where the model dynamically directs its own process and tool use, keeping control over how it completes the task.

| | Workflow | Agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who decides the steps | The code you write | The model |
| Good for | Fixed tasks: send a reminder 24 hours before an appointment | Open-ended tasks where steps cannot be predicted |
| Main risk | Rigid, breaks on unusual input | Cost, delay and compounding errors |

Anthropic advises finding the simplest solution that works and adding complexity only when needed. A reminder message needs a workflow. A request like "move my Thursday booking to next week and tell my partner" may need an agent. ## What Can an AI Agent Do for a Local Business?

Practical jobs are the ones with clear rules and a human fallback:

- Answer common questions about hours, services and directions
- Offer open appointment slots and add a booking to a calendar
- Draft replies to Google reviews for a person to approve
- Qualify enquiries and pass hot ones to staff

None of these need a new idea. They are what a receptionist already does, written down. See [AI agents for appointment booking](/blog/ai-agent-for-appointment-booking-local-business) and [AI agents for Google reviews](/blog/ai-agent-for-google-reviews-management) for the details. No source checked publishes average time savings or revenue gains for local businesses, so ignore any vendor figure that does not name its study.

## What Are the Risks and Limits?

Anthropic warns that agentic systems often trade latency and cost for better task performance, and that autonomy brings the potential for compounding errors. It recommends extensive testing in sandboxed environments with guardrails, and stopping conditions such as a maximum number of iterations.

For a local business, the practical rules are: let the agent read data freely but write only to approved places, log every action, and keep a person in charge of refunds, complaints and anything involving money. Review transcripts weekly in the first month.

## What Do the Rules Say About Messaging and Calls?

WhatsApp's business policy requires opt-in permission to contact people, and allows automation when responding during the 24-hour customer service window. Automated responders must offer a clear escalation path, such as a human agent, phone, email or in-store visit.

In the US, the FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices count as artificial voices under the TCPA, so the existing consent requirements apply to calls that use them, according to law-firm summaries. Google asks for specific, non-generic review replies. Read [customer service with AI](/blog/ai-customer-service-for-small-business) before you automate replies.

## FAQ

### Is an AI agent the same as a chatbot?

Not necessarily. A chatbot answers in a conversation, while an agent can also take actions with tools. The terms overlap in vendor marketing.

### How much does an AI agent cost?

Most vendors do not publish rates. Meta states about 4 to 5 cents per message for its WhatsApp agent.

### Do I need a developer?

Often not for a ready-made tool. Custom agents that write to your systems need technical help and testing.

### Is it legal to use AI to message customers?

You need consent for marketing messages, and in the US the TCPA applies to AI voices. Check local rules.

### Should an AI answer complaints?

Keep a person in charge of complaints, refunds and anything sensitive.
