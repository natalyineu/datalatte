Canada's anti-spam law says a past purchase can create an "existing business relationship" only if it happened "within the two-year period immediately before the day on which the message was sent." That one phrase is why a reactivation campaign is a permissions problem before it is a marketing problem.

The customers you most want to bring back are the ones who last bought long ago, and they are exactly the ones whose permission may have quietly expired.

This guide is for owners of cafes, salons, studios, groomers and similar businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It does not repeat the basics of subject lines and offers; for the email side, see our [AI-assisted email marketing for coffee shops](/blog/ai-assisted-email-marketing-for-coffee-shops) and [AI automation for hair salons](/blog/ai-automation-for-hair-salons) guides.

Here the angle is different: how to sort a lapsed list by what you are actually allowed to send, then run a short sequence and work out whether it pays. This is general information, not legal advice. Rules change, so check with an adviser before a large send.

## Why Win Back Lapsed Customers at All?

Harvard Business Review's Amy Gallo wrote that "acquiring a new customer is anywhere from five to 25 times more expensive than retaining an existing one," and added that the range depends on "which study you believe" and the industry.

The same article attributes to Bain's Frederick Reichheld the claim that "increasing customer retention rates by 5% increases profits by 25% to 95%." Those are broad figures, not local-business benchmarks, and they are about retention rather than winning back people who left.

Use them as a reason to test, then replace them with your own cost per reactivated customer, calculated below.

## How Do You Define Lapsed From Your Own Data?

Export customers from your POS, booking system or CRM. Take the usual gap between visits for your active customers and multiply it by about 1.5 (an assumption, not a published standard). A client who books every six weeks looks lapsed at about nine weeks; a coffee shop's window is far shorter than a dentist's.

Then add a second clock: **the date of each person's last purchase or enquiry, and how they gave you their contact details.** You need both for the next step.

## Who Are You Allowed to Contact? Sort Into Permission Buckets

The rules differ by country and by channel. The table summarises the official pages listed in Sources; open them for the exact text.

| Where | Email or text rule | Opt-out deadline | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US email (CAN-SPAM) | The FTC says all commercial email must comply, including "a message to former customers announcing a new product line." Accurate header, honest subject line, clear ad disclosure, valid postal address, opt-out explanation | Honor within 10 business days | FTC guide |
| US texts (TCPA) | Separate consent rules apply to marketing texts; get advice before texting a lapsed list | Not verified for this article | Ask an adviser |
| UK (PECR) | Marketing email needs prior consent unless the soft opt-in applies: details gathered in a sale or negotiations, similar products only, simple opt-out offered each time | Offer an opt-out in every message | Regulation 22, PECR 2003 |
| Canada (CASL) | Express consent, or implied consent from an existing business relationship (a purchase within two years, or an enquiry within six months) | No later than 10 business days | CASL ss.10, 11 |
| Australia (Spam Act) | Express or inferred consent (the person gave you their address and it is reasonable to expect marketing from you) | No later than 5 working days | business.gov.au |

A few points from those pages worth spelling out:

- **US email has no exemption for ex-customers.** The FTC guide says the law covers all commercial email, including a message to former customers announcing a new product line, and that each separate email in violation is subject to penalties of up to $53,088 (figure updated January 2024).
- **A "we miss you, here is 10% off" email is marketing.** Treat it as commercial email: accurate header, honest subject line, ad disclosure, postal address and a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days.
- **UK soft opt-in has three conditions.** Regulation 22(3) requires that you got the details "in the course of the sale or negotiations for the sale of a product or service", that the marketing is for your "similar products and services only", and that the person was given "a simple means of refusing" when the details were collected and in each message. Bought-in lists do not qualify. The regulation sets no expiry for soft opt-in, so ask an adviser rather than assume it never lapses.
- **Canada's clock is explicit.** The Act's s.10(10) lists a purchase or lease "within the two-year period" and an inquiry or application "within the six-month period" before the day the message was sent. Section 11(3) requires unsubscribe requests to take effect "without delay, and in any event no later than 10 business days." Read the regulator's current guidance too.
- **Australia asks you to keep records for express consent.** Business.gov.au says to keep a record of who gave permission, when and how.

Three buckets follow from this:

| Bucket | Who | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green | Opted in clearly, recent purchase, nothing on your suppression list | Run the full sequence |
| Amber | Old purchase, unclear source, or contact details collected without an opt-out | Do not send marketing yet. Use a non-marketing channel such as in-person, or take advice on a permission-refresh approach |
| Red | Unsubscribed, bounced, complained, or bought-in list | Never email or text. Keep them on a suppression list so they are not re-imported |

The last point matters more than people expect. If you move to a new email tool, import your suppression list too. Opt-outs belong to the person, not to the software.

## How Should You Segment the Green Bucket?

Inside the green bucket, split by past spend and visit frequency.

- **High value:** your best past customers. A personal note or a call from the owner (a call is subject to different rules than email, so check yours).
- **Medium value:** the three-touch sequence below.
- **Low value:** one automated message.

Add a "likely reason" tag where you know it: moved, price, a bad visit, or simply drifted. Someone who had a bad experience needs an apology, not a coupon.

## What Does a Three-Touch Sequence Look Like?

- **Touch 1, day 1:** a warm note from a named person. Say who you are, why you are writing ("we noticed it has been a while"), what is new, and one link. No offer yet.
- **Touch 2, about day 7:** one specific reason to come back, with a date.
- **Touch 3, about day 14:** a final reminder. After this, stop and move them to your normal low-frequency list.

Every message needs the legal basics. For email in the US, the FTC lists accurate header information, a subject line that does not mislead, a clear disclosure that it is an advertisement, and your valid physical postal address.

Make unsubscribing a single click, and make sure the unsubscribe actually suppresses the person across every list.

## Which Offer Can You Afford?

A deep discount trains customers to wait for sales. Cheaper options: a small add-on that costs you little, bonus loyalty points, early access to something new, or a personal note. Work out the cost per redemption before you send. The worked example below assumes $4.

## Why Did They Leave? Ask Once

Put one question in the first or second message with four or five choices (moved, found another place, price, a bad experience, just busy) and an "other" box. The answers can tell you whether you have a marketing problem or a service problem. Reply to any complaint personally and the same day.

## Does a Reactivation Campaign Pay? A Worked Example

Take a lapsed list of 800 customers. Every figure here is an **assumption** for illustration, except the legal buckets, which come from your own records.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lapsed customers on file (assumption) | 800 | 800 | 800 |
| Share in the green bucket (assumption) | 50% | 65% | 80% |
| Customers messaged | 400 | 520 | 640 |
| Reactivation rate (assumption) | 5% | 8% | 12% |
| Customers who return | 20 | 42 | 77 |
| First-visit spend (assumption) | $45 | $45 | $45 |
| Revenue | $900 | $1,890 | $3,465 |
| Gross margin (assumption) | 60% | 60% | 60% |
| Offer cost at $4 per return | $80 | $168 | $308 |
| Tool and staff time (assumption) | $110 | $110 | $110 |
| Profit from the campaign | $350 | $856 | $1,661 |
| Campaign cost per returning customer | $9.50 | $6.62 | $5.43 |

How to read it: profit is revenue times gross margin, minus offer cost and tool and time. It counts only the first return visit, so it understates the value of customers who stay, and it ignores those who lapse again (track your re-lapse rate).

Compare the cost per returning customer with what you pay to win a new customer from ads. If a new customer costs you $40 (also an assumption), even the Tough column wins on cost per customer. The real risk in this table is the second row.

## What Should You Measure?

Open rates are unreliable, so use outcomes:

- **Reactivation rate:** share of messaged customers who buy again within about 30 days
- **Reactivated revenue:** their total spend
- **Cost per reactivation:** offers plus tools plus staff time, divided by customers won back
- **Re-lapse rate:** how many lapse again within a couple of months
- **Unsubscribe and complaint rate:** a rising number means your list or your message is off

Use your sales or booking records, not just email reports.

## What Should You Do This Week?

1. Export your lapsed customers and add two columns: date of last purchase and how you collected the contact details.
2. Sort them into green, amber and red buckets using the table above, and put every red contact on a suppression list that travels with you between tools.
3. Pick your offer and cost it per redemption.
4. Write the three messages, with sender name, postal address and a one-click unsubscribe in each, and schedule them for days 1, 7 and 14.
5. Four weeks later, calculate reactivation rate, revenue, cost per returning customer and unsubscribes, and decide whether to repeat the campaign next quarter.

## FAQ

### How long should I wait before calling a customer lapsed?

Use your own buying cycle. About one and a half times the usual gap between visits is a workable assumption. Coffee shops use weeks; salons and dentists use months.

### Can I email everyone who ever bought from me?

Not automatically. In the US, CAN-SPAM covers emails to former customers and requires an opt-out. In the UK, you need prior consent unless the soft opt-in applies. In Canada, implied consent from a purchase is limited to two years. In Australia, you need express or inferred consent. Check each against your records.

### Do the rules apply to text messages?

Treat them as a separate channel with their own consent trail. US texts fall under separate TCPA rules, and UK and Canadian rules also cover electronic messages. Get advice before texting a lapsed list.

### Should I offer a discount?

Not first. Try a personal note or a small add-on and check what each redemption costs you.

### What if a customer replies "stop"?

Treat it as an unsubscribe and suppress them everywhere, within the deadlines in the table or sooner. Do not argue with the message.
