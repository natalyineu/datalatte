Display advertising for a small business means banner and image ads shown across websites, apps and Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail. WordStream's benchmarks put the all-industry average display cost per click at $0.63 and the average display click-through rate at 0.46%, compared with $2.69 and 3.17% for search. Display clicks are cheap, but a click is not a customer. Display works best as a reminder for people who already know the business, not as a first channel.

This guide sets out cost benchmarks by industry, what Google's help documentation says about setup, a worked budget example with labelled assumptions, and a checklist for measuring whether display pays.

## How Much Does Display Advertising Cost Per Click?

Display clicks are cheaper than search clicks on average, but the gap varies by industry. WordStream's industry table lists display cost per click (CPC) next to search CPC for each sector. The rows below cover categories most relevant to local service businesses. The benchmark page's text is dated to 2018, so treat these figures as a rough reference and check current numbers in your own account.

| Industry | Search CPC | Display CPC | Search CTR | Display CTR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Auto | $2.46 | $0.58 | 4.00% | 0.60% |
| Consumer Services | $6.40 | $0.81 | 2.41% | 0.51% |
| Health and Medical | $2.62 | $0.63 | 3.27% | 0.59% |
| Home Goods | $2.94 | $0.60 | 2.44% | 0.49% |
| Real Estate | $2.37 | $0.75 | 3.71% | 1.08% |

Real Estate has the highest display click-through rate in this group at 1.08%. Consumer Services has the most expensive search clicks at $6.40, which makes its display clicks look cheap by comparison. Cheap clicks only help when the people clicking are close to buying, so display should be judged by cost per lead rather than cost per click.

## When Does Display Make Sense for a Local Business?

Google describes Display campaigns as a way to reach people at different stages of the buying cycle, from people with demonstrated interests to customers about to buy. The same help page says Display campaigns can drive sales, leads, website traffic or awareness. That range is wide, and a small budget needs a narrower job.

Search ads catch people who type a need into Google today. Display suits reminding people who already visited a website, or building awareness in an area where a business is new. A business without steady search leads will spread a small display budget across people who may never need the service. Building search first and then testing display with a limited budget keeps the spend easier to read.

## What Does Google Say About Display Setup?

Google's Display help page says the Display Network reaches people across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail. Three defaults matter for a small account. Optimized targeting uses signals such as keywords on the landing page. Responsive display ads let the advertiser upload assets, and Google assembles the combinations. Smart Bidding is suggested based on the campaign goal, though manual bidding remains an option.

The same page says Smart Display campaigns have been folded into the standard Display campaign, and that Performance Max or Search are the options to consider for other automated campaign types. An account with an older Smart campaign should check how it now appears. The help page gives no migration date, so none should be assumed.

## What Does $500 a Month Buy?

The example below uses WordStream's all-industry display and search averages. Every other input is labelled as an assumption and should be replaced with real tracking data before any budget decision.

| Input | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly budget | $500 | Assumption |
| Display CPC | $0.63 | WordStream display average |
| Display clicks | about 794 | $500 divided by $0.63 |
| Implied display impressions | about 172,600 | Clicks divided by 0.46% CTR |
| Display click-to-lead rate | 1% | Assumption |
| Display leads | about 7.9 | 794 times 1% |
| Search CPC | $2.69 | WordStream search average |
| Search clicks | about 186 | $500 divided by $2.69 |
| Search click-to-lead rate | 4% | Assumption |
| Search leads | about 7.4 | 186 times 4% |
| Leads that become customers | 50% | Assumption |
| Value of one customer | $200 | Assumption |

On these inputs, display produces about 4 customers and $790 in revenue. Search produces about 3.7 customers and $740. Both exceed the $500 spend, but the gap is small, and the outcome depends on click-to-lead rates that are assumptions here.

Display only covers the $500 spend if it yields 5 leads, which is a click-to-lead rate of about 0.63%. Under the same customer assumptions, display breaks even on revenue at that rate. A site that converts display visitors below it loses money on these inputs, so the real rate should be measured before scaling.

## How Do You Test Whether Display Pays?

Measurement needs to be in place before spending begins. A display test without tracking produces impressions and clicks, which show nothing about revenue. The steps below keep the test small and readable.

1. Confirm that the website records conversions such as form submissions or calls, and that the count matches the booking system.
2. Set a fixed test budget for four to six weeks, and leave it unchanged during the test.
3. Choose one audience, usually past visitors to the site, and one creative with one clear offer.
4. Record cost per lead and the number of leads that become paying customers, not only clicks.
5. Compare the result with search cost per lead over the same period, then continue, adjust or stop.

## FAQ

### How much does display advertising cost per click?

WordStream's benchmark puts the all-industry display average at $0.63 per click, against $2.69 for search. Industry figures vary, from $0.58 for Auto to $0.81 for Consumer Services. The page is dated, so current numbers should be checked in the account.

### Is display better than search for small businesses?

Search usually wins on intent, because people searching for a service are closer to buying. Display is useful for reminders and awareness. Neither wins in every case, so cost per lead for both should be compared over the same period.

### What click-through rate should display ads expect?

WordStream's all-industry display click-through rate is 0.46%. Real Estate is highest in the benchmark table at 1.08%, and most listed industries sit between 0.49% and 0.60%. A rate far below the industry figure usually points to weak creative or the wrong audience.

### How much should a small business spend on display ads?

No source here sets a required amount. A small test budget that can be afforded without strain works as a starting point. Cost per lead should be tracked, and spending should increase only if display beats search cost per lead.
