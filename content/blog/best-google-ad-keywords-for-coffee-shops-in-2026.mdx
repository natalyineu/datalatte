Put them in tight ad groups, use phrase and exact match, and block jobs, recipes, beans and machines with negative keywords.

## Which Keywords Work Best for a Coffee Shop?

Group keywords by what the searcher wants, not by how many words they have. Each group below becomes its own ad group so the ad text can repeat the searcher's phrase.

| Intent group | Example keywords | Landing page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find a shop now | coffee shop near me, espresso near me, open coffee shop near me | Hours, map, directions |
| Drink or food | cold brew near me, oat milk latte near me, breakfast cafe | Menu page |
| Place or need | coffee shop with wifi, quiet cafe to study, outdoor seating | Page that shows that feature |
| Your city | coffee shop [your city], best coffee [neighbourhood] | Home page with address |
| Brand | your shop name, your shop name menu | Home page |

Competitor names are optional. Google can show ads on competitor names, but their ads and reviews are often stronger than yours. Test with a small budget and check the search terms report before spending more.

## Which Match Types Should You Use?

Google's help describes three types. Broad match is the keyword as typed and "may show on searches that are related to your keyword." Phrase match uses quotation marks and may show on searches that include the meaning of the keyword. Exact match uses square brackets and may show on searches with the same meaning or intent.

For a small budget, start with phrase and exact match on the groups above. Broad match is useful later, once conversion tracking works and the negative list is long. A broad single word such as latte can match recipe and equipment searches, so avoid it early.

## Which Negative Keywords Should a Coffee Shop Add?

Google's help says negative keywords "let you exclude search terms from your campaigns." They do not match close variants, so add singular and plural forms yourself.

- Jobs: jobs, hiring, careers, salary
- Equipment: machine, grinder, maker, roaster, supplier, wholesale
- Home use: recipe, how to, beans (unless you sell beans online)
- Free and price hunters: free, coupon

Add these before launch, then add new ones from your search terms report every week. Our guide to [negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) explains the report.

## How Should You Set Location Targeting?

Google's help says radius targeting needs a radius of at least 1 km and that very small targets may show ads only intermittently. It also recommends broad geo targeting ("Presence or interest") as a best practice on Search, while "Presence" fits when you only want people physically in the area.

For a shop that people walk or drive to, test "Presence" and a modest radius around your address, then read the locations report. If clicks come from places nobody would travel from, tighten the area.

## How Should You Structure the Account?

Start with one Search campaign and three to five ad groups, one per intent group in the table above. Write ads that repeat the searcher's words, and send each group to the most specific page you have. Add call and location assets so a phone user can tap to call or navigate.

Brand terms deserve their own campaign only if competitors bid on your name. Otherwise keep them as one ad group. The [complete Google Ads guide for coffee shops](/blog/google-ads-for-coffee-shops-complete-2026-guide) covers seasonal pushes and catering.

## FAQ

### What are the best Google Ads keywords for a coffee shop?

Local intent phrases such as "coffee shop near me", "espresso near me", your city plus "coffee", and need-based phrases such as "coffee shop with wifi". Group them by intent and review the search terms report weekly.

### How much does a coffee shop keyword cost?
