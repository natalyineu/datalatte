Google Search Console is Google's free report on how your site performs in Google Search. To use it, add your site as a property, verify ownership (a DNS record for a Domain property, or an HTML file, tag, Analytics code or Tag Manager container for a URL-prefix property), submit your sitemap, then read the Performance report for clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position.

This guide is for owners of local business sites. It uses Google's own help pages, checked on 1 November 2026. Numbers in the worked example are assumptions, not benchmarks.

## How Do You Verify Your Site in Search Console?

Google offers two property types. A Domain property covers every version of your domain and can only be verified with a DNS record at your domain provider. A URL-prefix property can be verified by DNS, an HTML file upload, an HTML tag, your Google Analytics tracking code or a Google Tag Manager container.

| Method | Domain property | URL-prefix property |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DNS record | Yes, the only option | Yes |
| HTML file | No | Yes |
| HTML tag | No | Yes |
| Google Analytics code | No | Yes |
| Tag Manager container | No | Yes |

Do not remove the file, tag or DNS record afterwards. Google checks periodically, and if it can no longer confirm the token, it notifies you and permissions expire after a grace period. A verified owner has the highest permissions, and several people can verify the same property.

## How Do You Submit a Sitemap?

A sitemap lists the pages you want crawled. Google accepts XML or text sitemaps, sitemap index files, and RSS or Atom feeds. One sitemap can hold up to 50,000 URLs and be 50 MB uncompressed; larger sites use a sitemap index.

Steps from Google's help page: you need owner permissions, post the sitemap on your site and validate its syntax, then in the Sitemaps report paste the URL in "Add a new sitemap" and submit. You cannot upload the file directly.

A sitemap does not guarantee crawling. Google says it might not crawl all URLs and does not guarantee that a listed URL will be indexed. Most website platforms generate a sitemap automatically, so check yours before building one by hand.

## What Does the Performance Report Show?

The Performance report has four metrics. Clicks are the times a user clicked through from Google Search. Impressions are the times your site appeared in results. CTR is clicks divided by impressions. Average position is the average position of your topmost result: for the whole site in the chart, and for the row's URL or query in the table.

The default view is the past three months, and you can change the date filter. The newest data may be preliminary and can change within a few hours; it shows as a dotted line. You can filter by search type: Web, image, video or news.

Position is an average across many searches, so treat it as a trend. Local results also vary with the searcher's location. For map visibility, use Business Profile data instead: see our [Business Profile analytics guide](/blog/google-my-business-analytics).

## How Do You Check Whether a Page Is Indexed?

Paste the full URL into the inspection bar at the top of Search Console. The URL Inspection tool shows Google's indexed version of the page, including whether crawling was allowed, the fetch result and the canonical Google selected. That indexed view is not a live test.

A live test fetches the page in real time and can differ from the indexed version. A pass does not guarantee indexing. You can click "Request indexing" after the live test passes, but Google says a request does not guarantee the page appears. Indexed inspections and index requests have daily limits per property, and Google does not publish the numbers.

For many pages Google recommends a sitemap with lastmod values rather than repeated requests.

## What Should You Check Each Month?

Use a short routine. This is a suggested workflow, not a Google requirement.

1. Open Performance and compare the last 3 months with the previous period.
2. Sort queries by impressions and look for ones with many impressions and few clicks. Those pages may need a clearer title or description.
3. Look for queries with an average position just below the top results. These pages often respond to better content and internal links.
4. Inspect your most important page with URL Inspection after any redesign.
5. Open the Sitemaps report and confirm it still processes.

Write down what you changed and the date. Then you can judge the effect next month. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows where SEO fits against paid channels, and Google Tag Manager for local sites covers the tracking side.

## Worked Example: Reading One Row

All numbers here are **assumptions** for an imaginary dog groomer's site.

| Query | Impressions | Clicks | CTR | Avg position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dog groomer near me | 2,000 | 40 | 2.0% | 9.5 |
| mobile dog grooming | 600 | 48 | 8.0% | 3.2 |

CTR is clicks divided by impressions, so 40 / 2,000 = 2.0%. The first query appears often but ranks low on average, so the page needs work. The second ranks well and converts impressions to clicks at a higher rate. Under these assumptions, the first page gets the next hour of work.

Do not read one row as a verdict. Compare the same query over three months before changing a page.

## How Do You Give Someone Access?

Add a colleague or agency as a user in the property settings instead of sharing your Google login. Google's verification page says a verified owner has the highest permissions, and refers to a separate page for user levels. If all verified owners lose access, all users lose access too, so keep at least two owners if you can.

## FAQ

### Is Google Search Console free?

Yes. Google provides it at no cost. It reports on Google Search only. For what visitors do after the click, use Google Analytics.

### How long does it take for data to appear?

Google's pages do not give a fixed time for first data. The Performance chart can show preliminary data that changes within a few hours.

### What is the difference between a Domain and a URL-prefix property?

A Domain property covers the whole domain and needs a DNS record. A URL-prefix property covers one address prefix and offers more verification methods.

### Does a sitemap guarantee my pages are indexed?

No. Google says it does not guarantee that a listed URL will be crawled or indexed.

### Does Search Console show Google Maps rankings?

Not as a map ranking report. Use your Business Profile performance report for calls, directions and website clicks.
