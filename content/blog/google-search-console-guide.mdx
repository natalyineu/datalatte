Google Search Console is a free tool from Google that shows how your website appears in search: which queries bring people to you, which pages get clicks, whether Google can crawl and index your site, and what technical problems need fixing. For a small business, it is the most direct way to see what customers search for before they find you, and it takes about fifteen minutes to set up.

This guide covers setup, the reports worth checking, a simple monthly routine and the common issues you will see.

## What Search Console does and does not do

It does:

- Show impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position for your pages and queries.
- Tell you which pages Google has indexed, and why others have not.
- Alert you to crawl errors, mobile usability issues, security problems and manual actions.
- Let you submit a sitemap and request indexing for new or updated pages.
- Show which websites link to you.

It does not:

- Track what visitors do on your site after the click. That is Google Analytics 4.
- Show your Google Business Profile performance. That lives in the profile's own insights.
- Guarantee rankings. It reports what is happening and helps you diagnose problems.

## Step 1: Add your site

1. Go to Search Console and sign in with a Google account you will keep long term.
2. Choose a property type. **Domain** covers all versions of your site (http, https, www and subdomains) but needs DNS verification. **URL prefix** is simpler if you only manage one version and can verify through an HTML file or tag.
3. Verify ownership. Common options: add a DNS TXT record at your domain registrar (such as Namecheap, GoDaddy or Cloudflare), upload an HTML file, add a meta tag, or use Google Analytics or Tag Manager if already installed. Most website builders (Wix, Squarespace, WordPress plugins like Yoast or Rank Math) have a guided option.
4. Wait for data. Reports can take a few days to start filling in, and historical data starts only from verification.

## Step 2: Submit your sitemap

A sitemap is a file listing your pages, usually at `yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml`. Many platforms generate it automatically. In Search Console, open **Sitemaps**, enter the path and submit. Check the status says "Success" and that the number of discovered pages looks right.

## Step 3: Learn the key reports

### Performance

This is the report you will use most. It shows:

- **Queries:** what people typed. Look for local phrases like "dog groomer near me" or "coffee shop open late" and see whether you appear.
- **Pages:** which of your pages get impressions and clicks.
- **Countries and devices:** useful if you serve a specific region or have mostly mobile traffic.
- **Filters and date comparisons:** compare the last three months with the previous period.

Key columns: clicks, impressions, CTR and average position. Position is an average across many searches, and local results can vary by the searcher's location, so treat it as a trend, not an exact rank.

### Indexing (Pages)

Shows how many pages are indexed and the reasons others are not. Some exclusions are normal, such as redirected pages or duplicates. Investigate when important pages show "Crawled, currently not indexed", "Discovered, currently not indexed", or "Blocked by robots.txt".

### URL Inspection

Paste any URL to see whether it is indexed, how Google sees it and whether it passes mobile checks. After publishing or updating an important page, use "Request indexing" to speed discovery.

### Experience and Core Web Vitals

Reports on page speed and mobile usability groups. Fix the issues flagged on key pages: slow images, shifting layouts, tiny tap targets.

### Links

Lists your top linked pages and linking sites. Useful to see if local directories, partners or press are linking to you. See our guide on [free backlink methods for local businesses](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks-local-business-free-methods).

### Security and manual actions

Should say "No issues detected". If it does not, treat it as urgent.

## A 20-minute monthly routine

1. **Performance, last 3 months versus previous:** note clicks and impressions changes.
2. **Top queries:** find queries with many impressions but a low click-through rate. Improve those pages' titles and meta descriptions to better match intent.
3. **Queries ranking around positions 8 to 20:** these are your quickest opportunities. Strengthen the page with more detail, internal links and a clearer heading.
4. **Pages report:** check for new indexing problems.
5. **Core Web Vitals and mobile usability:** fix flagged templates.
6. **Sitemaps:** make sure it is still processing properly.

Write down what you changed and the date so you can judge the impact later.

## Turning data into actions

| What you see | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Many impressions, few clicks | Title or description not compelling, or ranking low on page 1 | Rewrite the title with the main keyword and a benefit |
| Query ranks position 5 to 15 | Close to page one top | Add content depth, FAQs, internal links |
| Page not indexed | Thin, duplicate or blocked | Improve content, check robots and canonical tags |
| Sudden traffic drop | Technical issue, algorithm change or seasonality | Check indexing, manual actions, compare year on year |
| Queries from irrelevant places | Wide audience | Add location context to key pages |

## Common mistakes

- Verifying once and never checking again.
- Treating average position as an exact ranking.
- Panicking over small daily fluctuations.
- Ignoring mobile usability.
- Blocking pages by accident with robots.txt or noindex tags after a site redesign.
- Requesting indexing repeatedly for the same URL; one request is enough.

## Your first week with Search Console

1. Verify your site and submit your sitemap on day one.
2. Wait a few days, then open Performance and note your top ten queries and pages.
3. Check the Pages report for anything important that is not indexed.
4. Inspect your homepage and top service page with URL Inspection to confirm they look right on mobile.
5. Write down your baseline clicks and impressions so you can see progress next month.

None of this needs technical skills, only a few minutes and curiosity about what your customers type.

## Sharing access

If an agency, developer or colleague helps with your site, add them as a user under Settings, Users and permissions, rather than sharing your Google login. Choose Restricted access for people who only need to read reports, and Full access for those who must change settings or submit sitemaps. Review the list once or twice a year and remove anyone who no longer works with you.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Google Search Console free?**
A: Yes, completely free, with no paid tier.

**Q: How long until data appears?**
A: Basic reports often start within a few days of verification. Some data, like Core Web Vitals, needs enough visits to appear.

**Q: Do I need Search Console if I have Google Analytics?**
A: Yes, they answer different questions. Analytics describes what visitors do on your site; Search Console shows how you perform in search before the click.

**Q: Can Search Console help local SEO?**
A: It can show which local queries bring visits and whether your location pages are indexed, though it does not report map pack performance. Use your Business Profile insights for that.
