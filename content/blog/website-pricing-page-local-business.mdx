Yes, show prices, or at least "from" prices and typical ranges. Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g) reports that prospective customers rank price as their number one information need on any website, that participants in its studies went to competitors when a site hid prices, and that people see companies that hide costs as evasive. If exact prices vary, a range or sample scenario still helps.

NN/g's work focuses on business-to-business sites, so treat it as strong guidance rather than proof for every salon or plumber. This guide covers when to show prices, how to present them, and how to measure the effect.

## Why does showing prices help?

NN/g says price helps buyers identify the right product category, make trade-offs while comparing, and plan a budget. In its studies, sites that gave early product and pricing information were the ones most likely to make the shortlist. Visitors who cannot find a price often just look at the next site.

For a local business the effect is practical. A pet owner comparing three groomers wants to know whether a large dog costs $40 or $120 before calling. Hiding it adds a phone call that many people will not make.

## What if every job is different?

NN/g's own advice is to show ranges, sample prices, typical-case prices or a manufacturer's suggested price, and to use a few representative scenarios instead of a complex calculator. NN/g found precise configurators were slow and error-prone and used only by highly engaged buyers.

Examples of scenario pricing for service trades:

- "Small bathroom repaint, 1 coat, typical range: your range here."
- "Standard 60-minute massage: fixed price here."
- "Same-day call-out fee: fixed price here, then quoted per job."

Fill in your own numbers. Do not copy a competitor's.

## How should a local pricing page be laid out?

Keep it short and put it one click from the home page. Our [website conversion guide](/blog/website-conversion-rate-optimization) shows why fewer steps help.

1. A line stating what the price includes and excludes.
2. A table of services with "from" prices or ranges.
3. A short note on what changes the price, such as size, time or materials.
4. One clear action: book, call or request a quote.
5. A few FAQs about deposits, cancellations and payment.

Add the price range to your structured data. Google's LocalBusiness guide supports a priceRange field of fewer than 100 characters, such as "$$$". See our [local business schema guide](/blog/local-business-schema-markup-rich-snippets-google).

## Should restaurants and cafes use the menu editor instead?

For food businesses, Google Business Profile lets you add menu items with descriptions and prices, or upload a menu photo or PDF. Keep the web menu and the Profile menu in sync. Our [restaurant Business Profile guide](/blog/google-my-business-for-restaurants) explains the setup.

## When might you hide prices?

If the work is truly custom, such as a full renovation or a wedding package, a single number can mislead. The NN/g answer is still a range or sample scenario. Say clearly that the final figure needs a quote and how fast you reply. Check local consumer-protection rules on how prices must be displayed.

## Worked example: a groomer adds a price table

All inputs are **assumptions**.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly visitors to service pages | 600 |
| Enquiry rate with no prices | 4% |
| Enquiry rate with a price table | 5% |
| Share of enquiries that book | 50% |
| Average booking value | $70 |

Without prices: 600 x 4% = 24 enquiries; 12 bookings; $840. With prices: 600 x 5% = 30 enquiries; 15 bookings; $1,050. The difference is $210 a month. The 1-point lift is invented. Prices might also screen out some enquiries who cannot afford you, which saves you time even if bookings fall. Compare enquiries per week and quality before and after.

## FAQ

### Will showing prices let competitors undercut me?

They can see them, yes. NN/g's advice still is that hiding prices drives prospects to competitors. Compete on service, reviews and speed as well as price.

### What if my prices change often?

Show "from" prices with a last-updated date, and keep one source of truth so the page does not go stale.

### Do I need exact prices?

No. NN/g recommends ranges or sample scenarios when exact prices are not feasible.

### Where should the pricing page link from?

Your main navigation, each service page and your Business Profile website link. See the [CTA guidance](/blog/website-conversion-rate-optimization).

### Does a pricing page help SEO?

It targets searches such as "how much does X cost near me", but no source reviewed here quantifies a ranking effect.
