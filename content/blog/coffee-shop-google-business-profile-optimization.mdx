To optimize a coffee shop's Google Business Profile, verify it, match the name, category and hours to Google's written rules, add your full menu and real photos, collect and answer reviews, and then read the Performance report each month. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. You can only influence two of the three, so the rest of this guide is about the profile fields and habits that do.

This is a setup and management walkthrough for independent cafes in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. ## How Do You Verify a Coffee Shop's Business Profile?

Verification comes first, because Google says it is required to edit business information such as the name or hours and to interact with customers. It also gives you ownership of the profile. Performance data is available only for verified profiles.

Google chooses the method for you. Its help page lists phone or text, email, a live video call, a mailed postcard, and a video recording that Google recommends when your business is eligible. The page states that methods are determined automatically and cannot be changed, and that codes expire after 30 days. For a cafe, have the shop phone and the business email accessible when you start, and be on site with a camera in case a video is needed.

If you already own a listing that someone else created, claim it rather than adding a duplicate. Google allows one profile per physical location.

## What Are the Rules for Name, Address, Category and Hours?

Breaking these rules is the most common reason profiles are edited or suspended, and Google states them plainly in its [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

- **Name:** it must reflect the real-world name. Taglines, hours, phone numbers, URLs and service descriptions are not permitted, and location details such as "near" or "inside" belong in the address field.
- **Address:** use a precise address. P.O. boxes and mailboxes at remote locations are not acceptable, and virtual offices are not eligible. A business that shows its address needs permanent fixed signage with its name at that address. A cafe that serves customers at a counter is a storefront, so a home address is the wrong choice.
- **Category:** use as few categories as possible, choose ones that complete "This business IS a", and do not use categories as keywords.
- **Hours:** give regular customer-facing hours. Seasonal businesses should mark themselves temporarily closed in the off-season.
- **Description:** describe services, products, mission and history. Google says not to focus on promotions, prices or sales, and that no links of any type are allowed.

For holidays, use special hours. Google says they cover changes of up to 6 days in a row, that regular hours must be set to "Open with main hours" for them to appear, and that official holidays should be confirmed even if your hours do not change. Longer closures should be marked temporarily closed. The Google Business Profile categories guide explains how to choose between coffee shop and nearby categories.

## How Do You Add a Menu to a Coffee Shop Profile?

Google offers a menu editor for food and drink businesses. Open your profile, select Edit menu, then add sections and items with descriptions and prices. Google says changes can take 24 to 48 hours to show on Maps and Search. There is also an option to generate a menu from a photo, which Google labels experimental and says you must review before publishing. If you prefer to keep your menu on your own site, add the link under Edit profile, Business information, Contact, Menu link.

Keep the menu current. A wrong price or a drink you no longer sell is the kind of detail that turns into a negative review.

Check the Attributes section of your profile too. Which attributes are offered depends on your category and country, so fill in the ones that appear and are true, such as outdoor seating or dine-in, and leave the rest.

## Which Photos Should a Cafe Upload?

Google's [photo guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862) set the technical limits: JPG or PNG files between 10 KB and 5 MB, recommended 720 by 720 pixels, minimum 250 by 250, in focus and well lit, without heavy filters or significant alterations. Videos can be up to 30 seconds and 75 MB at 720p or higher.

Google says a cover photo is set at the top of the profile to best represent the business, but that does not guarantee it appears first. A logo is shown only when basic information such as a phone number and hours is present, so fix those first.

A practical shot list for a cafe is the exterior from the street, the interior in daylight, the counter, the menu board, your best-selling drinks as served, and a short video of a pour. Replace photos when the room or menu changes.

## How Do You Collect and Answer Reviews?

Google says you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code to leave a review, and that offering incentives in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. That rules out a free cookie for a five-star rating. Ask every customer, not only the ones who look happy, and put the QR code where the transaction happens: at the till, on the pickup shelf and on a cup sleeve. The step-by-step version is in [how to get more Google reviews for a coffee shop](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop).

For replies, Google advises keeping them short, avoiding promotions, answering negative reviews promptly, and never sharing a reviewer's private information or making personal attacks. If a review breaks Google's content policies, you can flag it. BrightLocal's 2026 survey says 89% of consumers expect owners to respond to reviews, and 74% only care about reviews written in the last three months. It is a US panel of 1,002 adults, so treat the percentages as a signal rather than a forecast.

## What Should a Coffee Shop Post on Its Profile?

Google offers three post types: updates, offers and events. Posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set. Updates and events can carry an action button, and offers include a View Offer button. Google does not say that posts raise rankings, so treat them as a way to give a visitor a reason to choose you: a seasonal drink, a weekend event or a dated offer.

A realistic routine is one photo-led post a week. Use the offer or event type with a date range for anything time-limited, and the update type for general news.

## How Do You Measure What the Profile Does?

Google's Performance report shows metrics that apply to your business, including searches, views, directions, calls, website clicks, menus and offers. It is available for verified profiles, and the data includes both organic results and Google Ads. Calls are counted when someone taps the call button, which requires a phone number on the profile. The search-terms data updates at the start of each month and can take up to five days to appear. Views count unique visitors and can be lower than the numbers in notification emails.

To see which visits come from the profile in your website analytics, add UTM parameters to the website link. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers that setup. Review the report on the same day each month and write down directions, calls and website clicks, so changes are visible over time.

## What Is a Profile Worth? A Worked Example

All inputs below are **assumptions** for illustration, not benchmarks. Replace them with your own figures from the Performance report and the till.

| Input | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Direction requests per month | 200 |
| Share of those who actually visit | 40% |
| Average spend per visit | $7 |
| Visits per new customer per month | 4 |

200 direction requests at 40% is 80 visits, and 80 visits at $7 is $560 a month from the profile channel. If better photos, a full menu and fresher reviews lift direction requests by 10% (also an assumption), that is 20 more requests, 8 more visits and about $56 more a month. If those 8 visits are new customers who return four times a month, the figure is closer to $224 a month.

The numbers are not a forecast. They show where to look: the direction count, the share that arrive, and whether new visitors become regulars. Start with a free "how did you find us?" question at the till to calibrate the 40%.

## Do This Today

1. Confirm the profile is verified and the name, address, category and hours follow Google's rules above.
2. Add or review the menu and the attributes that apply to your cafe.
3. Upload at least eight real photos and check the logo and cover photo.
4. Put a review QR code at the till and reply to every unanswered review.
5. Note this month's directions, calls and website clicks as a baseline.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to optimize a coffee shop Google Business Profile?

The initial setup of name, category, hours, menu and photos is a few hours of work once the profile is verified. Reviews and posts are an ongoing routine. Google says menu changes can take 24 to 48 hours to appear.

### Can a coffee shop use a home address on Google Business Profile?

A cafe with a counter is a storefront and needs a precise address with fixed signage. Google says service-area businesses working from home should clear the address from the profile, and that P.O. boxes and virtual offices are not acceptable.

### Can I give discounts for Google reviews?

No. Google says incentives in exchange for reviews are strictly prohibited.

### Do Google Business Profile posts improve ranking?

Google does not state that they do. It says posts older than six months are archived unless a date range is set. Use them to give visitors a reason to choose you.

### How do I see how many people call or ask for directions?

Open the Performance report on a verified profile. It shows calls, direction requests, website clicks, views and searches where they apply to your business.
