Here is a number to keep in mind. At an assumed 40 reviews a month, replying by hand at 5 minutes each costs 3.3 hours. Drafting with AI and editing at about 2 minutes each costs 1.3 hours. That is two hours back every month from one task, and it is only one of five places AI helps with a Business Profile. Those timings are my assumptions, and the math section shows how to test them on your own profile.

AI is a good assistant for the repetitive parts of a Google Business Profile: audits, drafts and summaries. It is a bad source of facts. It does not know your hours, your prices or what your shop looks like, and Google's policies punish listings that mislead. This guide is for owners and managers of local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It gives you a six-step workflow, prompts you can paste, the Google rules to respect, and an honest look at whether paid tools earn their cost.

## What AI Should and Should Not Touch

| Task | Let AI do | You must do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audit | Compare your profile text against a checklist | Verify the pin, phone and hours in person |
| Description | Draft three versions from your facts | Pick, edit, add one detail only you know |
| Services list | Tidy wording and structure | Supply real prices and durations |
| Photos | Shot lists, captions | Take the photos yourself |
| Posts | Brainstorm topics and first drafts | Attach a real photo and check every claim |
| Review replies | Draft short replies | Edit, send, and write every negative reply yourself |
| Review requests | Draft a polite message | Send only to real customers, with no reward |

The dividing line is simple: AI handles wording, you handle facts. For a data-analysis angle, see our companion article on [Business Profile optimization with AI-powered insights](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-with-ai). This one focuses on the hands-on workflow.

## The Google Rules That Matter Most

Check these before you let any tool near your listing.

**Business name.** Google's [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say the name should match your real-world name and that adding taglines, service descriptions or location details to it can get a profile suspended.

**Description.** The same page says a description should explain your services or products, mission and history, in an honest way, and shouldn't focus on promotions or prices. It also says links are not allowed. AI loves to write "Visit us today for 20% off!". Delete it.

**Categories.** Google asks for as few categories as possible and the most specific ones that describe your core business, and says not to use categories as keywords.

**Photos.** Google's [photo requirements](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862) say photos should be in focus, well lit, with no significant alterations or excessive filters, and should "represent reality". Formats are JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB. Videos can be up to 30 seconds and 75 MB. The page does not mention AI-generated images specifically, but the "represent reality" line is reason enough not to upload generated pictures of your premises or products. That is my reading, not a quoted Google rule.

**Reviews.** Google's [prohibited and restricted content policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114) bans offering incentives such as payment, discounts or free goods in exchange for reviews, and bans selectively soliciting positive reviews. It also bans false or misleading descriptions of your goods or services. The page says nothing specific about AI-written text, so the safe rule is: everything published under your name must be true and reviewed by you.

## Step 1: Run an AI-Assisted Audit

Copy the visible text of your profile into a document: name, categories, description, services, hours, attributes and your last 10 reviews. Then prompt:

> You are a local SEO reviewer. Below is the text of my Google Business Profile. List gaps, inconsistencies and missing information. Do not invent facts. Ask me questions if anything is unclear.

Treat the output as a to-do list to verify, not as truth. AI models sometimes describe features that Google has changed. Check each point against Google's help pages.

Then do the manual checks AI cannot do:

- Does the pin sit on your entrance?
- Does the phone number ring a person?
- Does the website link load fast on a phone?
- Are holiday hours set?

## Step 2: Choose Categories From Real Searches

Do not ask AI to pick your primary category from imagination. Write down what customers actually type ("dog groomer", "hair salon", "coffee shop"), then compare it with the categories Google offers in the dropdown. Pick the most specific accurate one as primary. Our [categories guide](/blog/google-business-profile-categories-guide) shows how.

## Step 3: Draft the Description and Services

> Write three versions of a 700-character Google Business Profile description for a [business type] in [neighbourhood, city]. Use only these facts: [list of real facts]. Friendly tone. No exclamation marks, no "best" or "number one", no prices, promotions or links.

Edit what comes back. Cut anything vague, add one specific detail only you know (a house roast, a gentle handling method, a free first class), and put your main service in the first sentence. For services, list each with a short plain description and a "from" price if you have one. Prices belong in the services section, not the description.

## Step 4: Photos Stay Human

Use AI for logistics only: a monthly shot list, captions, a checklist of areas not yet photographed (exterior, interior, team, products, results). Take the photos on a phone in natural light, crop and brighten, and upload 3 to 5 a month. The target is mine, a habit to keep the profile fresh, not a Google requirement.

## Step 5: Posts

Posts are Google's updates, offers and events. Google's [post help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077) notes the prohibited content rules apply to posts, and that posts with unverified contact information may be removed. Use AI for topic ideas:

> Give me 8 Google Business Profile post ideas for a pet groomer in October. Give each a one-sentence hook and a clear call to action. Base ideas only on seasonal pet care and our booking availability.

Choose four, rewrite in your voice and attach a real photo. See [Google Business Profile posts](/blog/google-business-profile-posts) for formats.

## Step 6: Review Replies, With Rules

Google's [review management page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050) says you must verify your business to reply, replies are checked against content policies (usually about 10 minutes, occasionally up to 30 days), approved replies appear as coming from the business, and the reviewer is notified and can update their review. It also says friendly, constructive replies show customers you pay attention.

Customers notice templated answers. BrightLocal's [2026 review survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) reports that 80% of surveyed US consumers are more likely to use a business that responds to all its reviews, and 50% are put off by generic or templated replies. So the workflow is draft, edit, send:

| Review type | AI role | Your role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive, short | Draft a warm reply | Add a personal detail, send |
| Positive, detailed | Suggest points to mention | Write or heavily edit |
| Negative | Outline a calm structure | Write and send yourself |
| Suspected fake or policy-breaking | None | Flag it through Google's review tools |

Useful reply prompt: "Draft a polite reply in under 60 words to this review. Mention [detail]. Do not offer compensation or share private details." For automating parts of this safely, see our [review management agent guide](/blog/ai-agent-for-google-reviews-management).

## Measure What Changed

Google's [performance help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094) lists what you can see for a verified profile: searches (the terms people used), views on Search and Maps (counted once per person per day), directions, calls (taps on the call button), website clicks and bookings (only if handled through a booking provider). Open business.google.com, click Performance and pick a period. Write the numbers down monthly so you can tell which changes helped.

## Do This Today

1. Copy your profile text into a document and run the audit prompt from Step 1. Verify every flagged item by hand.
2. Check your name, categories and description against Google's guidelines: no taglines, no promotions, no links.
3. Generate three description drafts from real facts, edit one, and publish it with your main service in the first sentence.
4. Draft and send replies to every unanswered review this week, writing the negative ones yourself.
5. Open Performance, record this month's searches, views, calls, directions and website clicks, and set a monthly 30-minute slot to repeat the process.

## FAQ

**Q: Will Google penalize me for using AI-written text?**
A: Google's pages I checked do not mention AI-written text at all. They do prohibit false or misleading descriptions and fake engagement. Accurate, edited text is the safe approach; unreviewed AI text is the risk.

**Q: How often should I update my profile?**
A: Update hours and links whenever they change, reply to reviews within a few days, add photos monthly and post when you have real news. Those are habits I recommend, not Google requirements.

**Q: Can AI write my review requests?**
A: It can draft a polite message. Send it only to real customers. Google prohibits incentives for reviews and selectively soliciting positive ones.

**Q: Do I need a paid tool?**
A: Not at first. A free assistant and a spreadsheet cover one location. Consider paid tools when review volume or several locations make manual work slow, using the break-even math above.

**Q: Can I auto-post replies with no human check?**
A: I'd advise against it. Customers dislike templated replies, per BrightLocal, and negative reviews need a person. See our [auto-reply script guide](/blog/ai-agent-google-reviews-auto-reply-script) for a human-in-the-loop setup.
