## Is Google Ads worth it for a Lexington hair salon?

Google Ads suits a hair salon because it reaches people at the point of choosing a stylist. A search for "hair salon near me" shows intent that a general social post rarely matches. The RŌZ page cites Google as saying local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Ads do not replace that local visibility, but they add a paid placement for the searches a salon wants most.

Social exposure can also play a role before a search happens. Bookeo claims that a client who has seen a salon's brand on Facebook 5 to 10 times before searching converts at 2 to 3 times the rate of a cold searcher, but it gives no source for that figure, so treat it as a vendor claim. A practical approach is to run ads for active searchers and use other channels to build familiarity. For a broader view of the category, see the [complete Google Ads guide for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide).

Whether the spend pays off depends on booking value and repeat visits, which only your own numbers can confirm. Test a small budget, measure bookings and expand only when the cost per booking fits your margin.

Avoid broad match on generic terms until you have enough conversion data to judge them. Review the search terms report weekly, and add irrelevant queries as negative keywords.

## How should the landing page and tracking work?

A campaign can get clicks and still fail. SunnyStorm states that if a salon gets 100 visitors and no bookings, there is probably a problem with the landing page. Check the page the ad sends people to: the service named in the ad should appear near the top, the booking button should be visible without scrolling and the phone number should be clickable on mobile.

Track the actions that lead to revenue. A practical set includes phone calls from ads, completed booking forms, direction requests from the Google Business Profile and clicks on "book now" or contact buttons. Booksy says that with Reserve with Google connected, a client can go from searching to booked in under 30 seconds, so a booking path that works directly from search is worth checking. If you use a booking system, confirm it connects to your profile and records the source of each booking.

Keep tracking simple enough to check weekly. A salon that can see which ad group produced each booking can make budget decisions without guessing.

## How wide should the radius and profile setup be?

The CIIM guide recommends a local targeting radius of about 5 to 10 km around the salon. Five kilometres is roughly 3.1 miles, so a radius in that range fits a neighbourhood-level audience. Start at the lower end, check where bookings come from, and widen only if the data supports it. A radius that is too large spends budget on clients who will not travel to your chair.

Your Google Business Profile matters as much as the ad. The RŌZ comparison table lists the typical cost of a Google Business Profile and of Google reviews as free, and both affect how clients judge your salon before they book. Booksy's 30-day roadmap starts with Days 1 to 7 covering claiming the Google Business Profile, syncing it with Booksy and updating the service menu. Complete that work before raising spend, so clicks land on a profile that is accurate and up to date.

## What 30-day checklist should a salon follow?

Use this sequence for a first campaign. Each step is a check you can complete in a working day or two.

1. Claim and update the Google Business Profile, including services and opening hours.
2. Choose three to five exact-match keywords tied to your top services.
3. Set a 5 to 10 km radius around the salon address and exclude areas you cannot serve.
4. Build one ad group per service and send each to a page that names that service.
5. Add an offer that is clear and specific. CIIM gives "First Haircut 20% Off" as an example salon offer, and Zeely gives "$20 off first color service" as a new-client offer.
6. Connect call and booking tracking, then confirm a test booking shows up in the right place.
7. Review the search terms report and add negatives after the first week.
8. Check results at 30 days and decide whether to keep, adjust or pause each ad group.

Repeat clients add value after the first booking. Booksy reports that engaged loyalty members visit salons 2.4 times more often, which supports an email or loyalty step after the first visit. ## FAQ

### How much should a Lexington hair salon spend on Google Ads?

None of the sources here gives a fixed amount for Lexington. Salon Guru quotes Google Ads pricing on request rather than publishing a rate. Test a budget you can afford for a full month, using the worked example above as a template, and scale only the ad groups with a cost per booking that fits your margin.

### How long does it take to see results?

The sources do not give a fixed timeline for paid ads. Salon Guru says an SEO-optimised salon website usually takes a few weeks to reach the top of organic results for several key phrases, which is a separate channel from ads. Judge a paid campaign on at least a month of data, and check the search terms report each week in the meantime.

### Are the 1.5 million searches a Lexington number?

No. The RŌZ page reports Google Ads keyword data estimating about 1.5 million U.S. searches per month for "hair salon near me". It is a national estimate, so use your own search terms report and location reports to see how much of that demand reaches your area.

### Do Google Business Profile and reviews cost anything?

The RŌZ comparison table lists the typical cost of a Google Business Profile and of Google reviews as free. Your profile still needs time to keep accurate, so budget staff hours for updates and review requests.
