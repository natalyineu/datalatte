Small business marketing in New South Wales works best when it starts local: claim your Google Business Profile, collect and answer reviews, then add one paid channel aimed at your own suburbs. Almost all Australians are online, so the question is not whether buyers can find you but whether your listing, reviews and ads win the comparison when they search nearby. This guide covers what to do first, what a small Google Ads budget can realistically return, and how to track which channels produce paying customers.

## What makes New South Wales different for local marketing?

New South Wales is not a separate market in the sources used here, so the strongest evidence covers Australia as a whole. Kepios analysis published by DataReportal put [internet penetration at 97.1 percent](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-australia) of Australians at the close of 2025. Sydney, Newcastle and Wollongong customers are therefore already online, and so are their competitors.

Presence is now ordinary. The Australian Bureau of Statistics found that [51% of businesses used social media](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/technology-and-innovation/characteristics-australian-business/2024-25) for their online presence in 2024-25, up from 47% in 2021-22. Being on a platform is no longer an advantage. What decides the outcome is how a listing, its reviews and its local pages look when a nearby buyer compares it with two or three other names.

For a broader view of the national market, read the [Australia small business marketing guide](/blog/local-marketing-australia-small-business-2026).

## Which marketing channels should a NSW small business start with?

Start with the free, high-impact items before any paid spend. Madsun Media's guide to Australian small business marketing describes the Google Business Profile as the highest-return move for almost every local business, and it costs nothing to claim ([Madsun Media, secondary](https://www.madsunmedia.com/blog/small-business-marketing-australia)). Complete every field, choose accurate categories, add real photos, list services and keep hours current.

The same guide suggests this order when the budget is tight:

1. Optimise your Google Business Profile.
2. Get reviews flowing from recent customers.
3. Make sure your website works on a phone.
4. Pick one paid channel, usually Google Ads or local SEO, to drive enquiries.

The table below compares the main options on the factors that matter most at the start. Cost entries describe the type of spend rather than a price, because sourced figures for local pricing are not available.

| Channel | Cost to start | Best use for a small NSW business | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Free to claim and maintain | Capturing "near me" and map searches | Neglected listings with old hours or photos |
| Reviews process | Staff time only | Building trust before the enquiry | Few recent reviews, so the profile looks dormant |
| Google Ads | Daily cap you set | Fast enquiries for high-intent searches | Paying for clicks from outside your service area |
| Meta retargeting | Daily cap you set | Re-engaging website visitors with one offer | Poor creative or no clear offer |
| Local SEO | Time and content effort | Long-term enquiries from suburb pages | Slow to show results |

Only add channels beyond the first four once those work. Spreading a small budget across five platforms usually means none gets enough data to judge.

## How do you set up Google Ads on a small budget?

Tight targeting is the main lever. Madsun Media recommends focusing on high-intent keywords, excluding irrelevant searches with negative keywords, targeting only the suburbs you actually serve, and sending clicks to a relevant landing page ([Madsun Media, secondary](https://www.madsunmedia.com/blog/small-business-marketing-australia)). A plumber in Penrith gains nothing from clicks in Bondi, so exclude distant areas from the start.

Use the suburb name in headlines where it is accurate. A studio in Merewether can test "Merewether personal trainer" against a broader phrase and keep whichever converts better. Send clicks to a page that names the suburb and describes the service, not to a generic homepage.

Keep the first month small. Set a daily cap, review search terms weekly, add negatives for anything that is not a buyer, and raise the budget only once the cost per enquiry is known.

## What does a small Google Ads budget return?

The example below uses assumed inputs, not sourced figures. Replace each one with numbers from your own first month of data.

Assumptions: monthly budget A$750; average cost per click A$3.40; click-to-enquiry rate 8%; enquiry-to-booking rate 50%; average booking value A$120; gross margin 40%.

- Clicks: A$750 divided by A$3.40 is about 220 clicks.
- Enquiries: 220 multiplied by 8% is about 18 enquiries.
- Bookings: 18 multiplied by 50% is 9 bookings.
- Revenue: 9 multiplied by A$120 is A$1,080, a revenue return of 1.44 times the spend.
- Gross profit: A$1,080 multiplied by 40% is A$432, below the A$750 spend.

On these assumptions the campaign loses money even though revenue exceeds spend. The fix is to test which lever moves most: raise booking value with a package, lift the click-to-enquiry rate with a tighter landing page, or cut wasted clicks with negatives. Revenue return alone never shows whether an ad pays.

## How should NSW businesses handle Google reviews?

Reviews carry real weight in purchase decisions. Research cited by Billo says 81% of consumers use Google reviews to evaluate a business ([Billo, secondary](https://billo.app/benefits-of-google-reviews/)). A separate summary reports that 88% of customers check reviews before visiting a business ([Revenuezen, secondary](https://revenuezen.com/b2b-seo-statistics/)), a figure Mailchimp also cites in its [Australian local marketing guide](https://mailchimp.com/resources/australia/local-marketing-in-australia/) ([Mailchimp, secondary](https://mailchimp.com/resources/australia/local-marketing-in-australia/)).

Ask for reviews as a routine step rather than an occasional favour. Send a text or email with a direct review link after each job or purchase. Respond to every review, positive or negative, in a professional tone. A steady flow of recent, genuine reviews builds trust and supports visibility in local search.

## Should NSW businesses run Meta or Instagram ads?

Paid social suits promotions, events and retargeting more than cold awareness for most small businesses. Madsun Media notes that retargeting people who visited a website but did not convert is especially cost-effective ([Madsun Media, secondary](https://www.madsunmedia.com/blog/small-business-marketing-australia)). Start with one or two offers, test a couple of creatives, and keep what works.

Choose platforms by where customers spend their time. A trades business may do well on Facebook alongside a strong Google presence, while a food or beauty brand may suit Instagram. Consistency on two platforms beats posting weakly everywhere. If scheduling across channels is the bottleneck, compare options in the [Hootsuite and Sprout Social review for local businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business).

## How do you track which channels bring paying customers?

Measurement is where most small businesses fall short. Madsun Media advises, at minimum, asking new customers how they found the business and using Google Analytics and call tracking ([Madsun Media, secondary](https://www.madsunmedia.com/blog/small-business-marketing-australia)). Once it is clear which channels produce paying customers, time and money can move toward them.

Use this short checklist each month:

1. Log the source of every new enquiry, including phone calls.
2. Count bookings and paid jobs per channel, not just leads.
3. Compare cost per paying customer across Google Ads, Meta and the Google Business Profile.
4. Cut or pause any channel with no paying customers after a fair test period.

If bookkeeping needs to tie sales to channels, [FreshBooks and QuickBooks](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business) are worth comparing before choosing a system.

## FAQ

### How much should a small business spend on Google Ads in NSW?

No sourced figure sets a standard budget. Start with an amount that can be sustained for at least a month, cap the daily spend, and raise it only once the cost per enquiry and cost per paying customer are known.

### Is the Google Business Profile still worth it for local businesses?

Yes. Madsun Media describes it as the highest-return move for almost every local business, and it is free to claim and optimise ([Madsun Media, secondary](https://www.madsunmedia.com/blog/small-business-marketing-australia)).

### How many reviews does a business need?

Sourced data does not give a minimum number. Focus on a steady stream of recent, genuine reviews and reply to each one, since visible responses build trust.

### Should a business be on every social platform?

No. Pick the one or two platforms where customers spend time and do them well, as Madsun Media recommends.
