A hair salon in Atlanta, GA gets into Google's Maps local pack mainly through a complete, accurate Google Business Profile, a steady flow of reviews, and a name, address and phone number that match exactly across the web. The local pack is the [top three business listings in local search results](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today), and those three are [pulled directly from their Business Profile listings](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business). ## Which Business Profile fields should an Atlanta salon fill in first?

Google describes its Business Profile as [a free listing on Search and Maps](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/), so setup costs nothing. The profile is the first thing to get right. One Adwave analysis says the primary category is the #1 ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, though it does not name the source of that analysis ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones), secondary).

Adwave recommends looking at the top three to five businesses in local map results for a service search to see which category they use. For a salon, that means checking what competing salons near you have chosen, then selecting the category that matches the service you sell most. Do not stack categories for services you do not offer.

Adwave says a business description allows [750 characters](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent) in the profile guide. Write plain sentences that name your core services and your neighbourhood. Hours, phone number and address should be [100% accurate](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), and your street format should match your website and other listings, down to "St." versus "Street" ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary). If you work from home or travel to clients, you can hide the street address and be designated a Service Area Business.

## How many photos and posts does a salon profile need?

Adwave gives two photo figures. Its profile guide recommends [at least 15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), saying a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls. Its to-do list advises [at least 10](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Start at 15 and keep the set current. Show finished colour work, cuts, the chairs and reception, and the team.

Google Posts appear on your profile in Search and Maps. Google's help page says a post is public only when its status is [Live](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en); Pending and Not approved posts do not show. Check the status after every post, because a post that is not live does nothing for clicks.

Adwave suggests [at least one new post a week](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) to keep the profile fresh. Use posts for offers, new services and seasonal openings. For drafting these faster, see [AI for Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization).

## Do reviews change where a salon appears on Maps?

Google says reviews [show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122). Reviews are the first thing a client reads before calling, so they matter even when they are not a ranking factor. The sources here do not give a review target that guarantees a top-three spot.

A third-party analysis cited by Adwave examined [2 million Google Business Profile pages](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business) and found that businesses with over 200 reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack (secondary). That is an observed association, not a rule. For another city's review counts, see the [Denver review benchmark study](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026).

Ask clients for a review after the appointment, and reply to each review with the service and neighbourhood. Follow Google's own guidance on [getting more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) when you design the request.

## Which citations should a salon check first?

Adwave says your name, address and phone number must be [exactly the same](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses) on your website, your Business Profile, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places (secondary). Start by auditing existing listings. Adding new listings with a wrong suite number makes the mismatch worse.

Apple Business Connect is also worth claiming. It reports Profile Views, defined as [how many people saw your business card in Apple Maps](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring) (secondary). The share figures on the same page are not sourced, so leave them out of your planning.

If you also run ads, Google states that location data for [location assets is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), and corrections made there flow into linked accounts. For the ads side of salon marketing, see the [complete Google Ads guide for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide).

## What does a worked local SEO budget look like?

The calculation below is illustrative. Every input is an assumption, and you should replace them with your own GBP Insights data before using the result.

Assumed inputs:
- 120 calls and direction requests from your profile each month (assumption)
- 25% of those contacts become a booked appointment (assumption)
- $85 average service ticket (assumption)
- 3 hours a month on photos, posts and review replies (assumption)

Calculation: 120 × 25% = 30 booked appointments (assumption-based). 30 × $85 = $2,550 monthly revenue associated with profile contacts (assumption-based). That figure helps you decide whether the 3 hours are worth doing in-house or outsourcing. It is not a forecast.

Six fixes to make first, in order:
1. Compare your primary category with the top local results and correct it.
2. Fill the 750-character description with services and your neighbourhood.
3. Make hours, phone and address 100% accurate.
4. Upload at least 15 photos.
5. Publish one post a week and confirm each one shows Live.
6. Match your name, address and phone exactly on your website, profile and directories.

## How should a salon track whether local SEO is working?

Adwave lists six local search metrics it calls the useful ones: [Google Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent). Review these monthly in your GBP Insights dashboard and in your website analytics, and compare the same months year on year where you have history.

Check your map pack position by running your top keywords in a private browser window each week, and record the result in a simple sheet. Booking also matters. Booksy says [Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), which makes the booking link as important as the call button. ## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a salon?

Yes. Google describes it as a [free Business Profile on Google Search and Maps](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/).

### Do the top local pack listings come from the same profiles?

Adwave says the top three businesses in the map pack are [pulled directly from their Google Business Profile listings](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business) (secondary). Keeping your profile accurate is therefore the starting point.

### Why do guides give different photo counts?

Adwave gives [15 photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) in one guide and [10 photos](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) in another. Start with 15 and keep the gallery current.

### Can a home-based stylist keep their address private?

Yes. Adwave says a business that works from home or serves customers at their location can [hide its physical street address](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack) and be designated a Service Area Business.
