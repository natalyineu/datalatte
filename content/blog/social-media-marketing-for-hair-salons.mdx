A hair salon should post on Instagram first and mirror to Facebook. Pew Research Center's 2026 survey says 51% of US adults use Instagram, rising to 79% of those aged 18 to 29, while 70% use Facebook. Post finished work, a clear booking link and your opening hours, then measure bookings, not likes.

This guide sets out which platforms to use, what to post, how to stay inside advertising rules, and how to judge the result. Only the audience figures are published data. No source used here gives engagement rates for salons, so none are quoted.

## Which platforms should a hair salon use?

Pew's national survey of 5,511 US adults (fieldwork January 30 to June 18, 2026, margin of error 1.8 points) gives the audience picture.

| Platform | US adults | Notes from Pew |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | 84% | Most widely used |
| Facebook | 70% | 76% of rural adults, 67% of urban; 59% of ages 65+ |
| Instagram | 51% | 79% of ages 18-29; 58% of urban adults, 40% of rural |
| TikTok | 31% | 65% of ages 18-29 |

Read this as a guide to where clients are, not as a ranking of results. A salon in a city with younger clients leans to Instagram. A salon in a rural town with older clients should keep Facebook active. Check your own client ages in your booking system before choosing.

## What should a salon post?

Post what a new client wants to see before booking: finished cuts, colour work, the team, prices or a starting price, and how to book. Keep captions short and put the booking link in your profile.

- Finished work photos, with the client's permission
- Short videos of a technique or transformation
- A pinned post with services, prices and the booking link
- Seasonal or last-minute availability

## What are the rules on before-and-after photos and reviews?

Get written permission before posting a client's photo. For reviews and testimonials, the FTC's endorsement guidance says endorsements must reflect honest opinions, and that asking only satisfied customers for reviews is misleading. It also says to disclose it when customers were told in advance they might be featured in an ad.

Do not delete or hide negative reviews, and do not ask customers to change them. For a longer treatment see [before-and-after photos for salons](/blog/hair-salon-before-after-photos-legal-and-converting). This is general information, not legal advice.

## How do you schedule posts without paying for a tool?

Meta Business Suite is free to use, according to Buffer's guide (secondary source). It schedules feed posts, Reels and Stories to Facebook and Instagram, and needs a Facebook business Page or an Instagram business account.

Batch your content: shoot once a week, schedule the rest. If you outgrow it, compare paid tools in [Hootsuite vs Buffer vs Later](/blog/hootsuite-vs-buffer-vs-later-best-social-media-tool). Meta's own help pages could not be opened for this article, so check current features there.

## How do you know it is working?

Count bookings that name social media as the source, link clicks from your profile, and new followers who book. Add "How did you hear about us?" to your booking form. Our [salon analytics guide](/blog/social-media-analytics-for-hair-salons) lists five metrics worth tracking.

## What is a realistic time budget? (worked example)

Every input is an **assumption**: one stylist-owner spends 1 hour a week batching photos and captions, valued at $30 an hour, so about $130 a month. The average service is $85.

- Break-even: 2 extra bookings a month cover the $130 of time ($170 in revenue).
- Check: after 8 weeks, count bookings with social as the source.

Decision rule: if social produces fewer than 2 bookings a month after two months, change the content (more finished work, a clearer booking link) before adding paid ads. For paid options, see [Google Ads for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide).

## FAQ

### Is Instagram or Facebook better for a hair salon?

Instagram suits younger clients, and Facebook reaches more adults overall (70% against 51%). Post to both with Meta Business Suite and keep the one that books more clients.

### How often should a salon post?

No source used here sets a number. Pick a pace you can keep, such as three posts a week, and review after eight weeks.

### Do I need TikTok?

Only if your clients are young and you can make short videos. Pew puts TikTok at 31% of adults and 65% of ages 18 to 29.

### Can I repost client reviews?

Yes with care. Post honest reviews, include negative ones in your overall picture, and follow the FTC guidance above.
