An Amsterdam café should market to two groups at once: visitors who find you on Google Maps, and locals who come back because of the neighbourhood and a reason to return. Fix your Google Business Profile first, answer reviews in the reviewer's language, then use Instagram and a simple loyalty offer to keep regulars.

Here "coffee shop" means a café that sells coffee, not a cannabis coffeeshop. Every figure below has a source at the end, and where no number is published, the guide says so.

## How Many Visitors Does Amsterdam Get?

Amsterdam recorded 23.7 million tourist overnight stays in 2025, about 800,000 more than in 2024, according to municipal figures reported by NL Times. The city's own limit is 20 million, and it has exceeded it repeatedly. Statistics Netherlands (CBS) counted 9.5 million tourists staying in Amsterdam, 2% more than the year before; these figures are provisional.

The city says growth is slowing, partly because of measures such as a higher tourist tax and a ban on new hotels. Do not plan a café around ever-rising visitor numbers. Treat visitors as a bonus that depends on policy and season, and build the base on locals.

## How Should a Café Split Its Marketing Between Tourists and Locals?

Use different channels for each group, because they choose differently. The table is a working guide built from how each channel works, not from measured Amsterdam data.

| | Visitors | Locals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main discovery tool | Google Maps and search | Walking past, word of mouth, Instagram |
| Language of profile | English first, Dutch second | Dutch first, English second |
| What wins them | Photos, rating, opening hours | Consistency, staff, a reason to return |
| Best proof | Recent reviews | Regulars and neighbourhood presence |
| Cost | Free profile work | Time plus a loyalty offer |

The common mistake is to pick one. English-only copy and tourist deals can put off residents, and Dutch-only copy can make you invisible to visitors. A bilingual profile with a Dutch or English lead line is a practical middle path.

## What Should Your Google Business Profile Include?

Google says you must add or claim a business and verify it to show on Search, Maps and other Google services. Once verified, complete the basics: a clear category, opening hours including King's Day and holiday changes, the address, photos of the room, the terrace and your best drinks, and a short description in Dutch and English.

Use neighbourhood names that people actually search, such as De Pijp, Jordaan or Oud-West, in your description and posts. Our guide on [getting walk-ins from Google Maps](/blog/how-to-get-coffee-shop-walk-ins-from-google-maps) and the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) list the profile fields step by step.

## How Should You Handle Reviews in Two Languages?

Verified businesses can reply to Google reviews. Google reviews each reply before it appears, usually within about 10 minutes, though it can take up to 30 days. The reviewer is notified when you reply. Google also says you can share a review request link or QR code to encourage reviews.

Reply in the reviewer's language when you can write it well, and in English otherwise. Keep replies short and specific: thank the person and mention the drink or the staff member. For negative reviews, acknowledge the problem, offer to talk, and avoid arguing in public. A QR code on the counter that opens your review page is the lowest-effort way to collect more.

## How Do You Use Instagram for an Amsterdam Café?

DataReportal counts 8.15 million Instagram ad reach in the Netherlands (44.4% of the population) in late 2025, down 2.4% over the year. The page cautions that falls may partly reflect platform account corrections. YouTube reaches 15.3 million and TikTok 6.3 million adults.

Instagram is still useful for showing the room, the drinks and the neighbourhood. No source shows which Amsterdam content performs best, so test canal views, interiors and drink close-ups and compare how many people mention the post at the counter. A bilingual caption lets one post serve both groups, but watch that it stays short.

## How Do You Build Loyalty With Locals?

A regular comes back for the drink, the staff and the habit. Give them a reason that costs little: a stamp card or a digital loyalty system, a named barista, or a small local event. The aim is a visit pattern, not a discount.

The worked example below uses **assumptions**, because no source publishes Amsterdam loyalty-card results. The arithmetic shows what the offer costs.

| Input or result | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Active card holders | 120 |
| Visits per holder per month | 8 |
| Drinks bought per month | 960 |
| Card rule | 10th drink free |
| Free drinks given | 96 |
| Menu price of a drink | EUR 3.80 |
| Ingredient and cup cost per drink | EUR 0.90 |
| Revenue given up on free drinks | about EUR 365 |
| Direct cost of free drinks | about EUR 86 |

If the card brings one extra visit per holder per month (120 visits), those visits bring about EUR 456 at menu price. Whether that covers the direct cost depends on your labour and rent per drink. Track visits by card for two months before you decide to keep it.

## How Should You Plan Around King's Day and the Seasons?

King's Day falls on 27 April, and the city's own pages call it the busiest day of the year. For 2026, Amsterdam tightened checks on illegal alcohol sales and parties and does not allow amplified or live music in public spaces. Check the municipality's rules for the year before you plan terrace events or street sales.

Other peaks, such as the summer tourist season, Sinterklaas on 5 December and Christmas, are known calendar dates. Plan your menu and staffing for those weeks, but base your yearly budget on regular local traffic. The 2026 guidance changes each year, so confirm it on the municipality's website in April.

## Should a Café List on Delivery Platforms?

Only if you sell items that travel well, such as bakery goods or packaged beans. No source here gives Amsterdam delivery commission rates, so request current terms from each platform before you decide. Hot drinks are usually a poor fit for delivery.

## FAQ

### Should I focus on Google Maps or TripAdvisor?

Start with Google Maps, because it is free, widely used and shows opening hours, photos and reviews in one place. TripAdvisor's help centre could not be loaded for this guide, so no TripAdvisor figures are used. If visitors tell you they found you there, claim the listing and keep it accurate.

### Do I need Dutch-language marketing in Amsterdam?

Locals respond to Dutch, and a Dutch line on your profile and menu shows you belong to the neighbourhood. No source in this guide measures English proficiency, so use both languages and check with a Dutch speaker before publishing.

### How do I get more Google reviews?

Ask in person when a customer has just enjoyed a drink, and make it easy with a QR code or review link, which Google says you can share. Reply to every review.

### How much should I spend on ads?

No published benchmark covers Amsterdam café ad costs. Spend on free profile work first, then test a small local campaign and measure visits or offers redeemed.

### How do I handle seasonal swings?

Build your fixed costs around local trade and treat visitors as extra. Plan staffing for known peaks and review your numbers each quarter.
