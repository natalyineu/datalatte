Local SEO for a hair salon in San Francisco, CA comes down to appearing in the Google Maps local pack, the top three business listings shown for searches such as "hair salon near me." [Adwave's local SEO audit checklist](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today) defines the pack this way. Those three listings are pulled directly from Google Business Profile records, so the profile is where most of the work happens. This guide covers the profile fields, review targets, citation consistency, and tracking steps that matter for salons and barbershops in the city, with sourced benchmarks and a worked example.

## How does the Google Maps pack decide which salons appear?

[Adwave's guide to marketing a local business](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-lawn-care-business) states that the top three businesses in the map pack are pulled directly from their Google Business Profile listings. The profile therefore carries the information Google uses to choose and display a salon. Google's own Business Profile information is consistent with this: the profile is a free tool that manages a business's presence across Google Search and Maps, as [Google's Business Profile page for Canada](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) explains.

Ranking weight is harder to pin down. A [2025 multi-location SEO benchmark, as cited by Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit), reports that profile signals represent 32% of local pack ranking weight. Adwave's beginner guide gives the same 32% figure without naming a source, so treat it as a directional benchmark rather than a fixed rule.

Competition in San Francisco is not measured in these sources. Search your core terms, such as "hair salon near me" and your top service, and note which businesses appear in the three-listing pack and how many reviews each has.

## Which Google Business Profile fields should a San Francisco salon fill in first?

Start with the category, then the description, photos, posts, and hours. Adwave recommends checking the top three to five businesses in local map results for your service search to see which category they use, as described in its [category selection guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones). The same guide says the primary category is the top ranking factor for entering the local pack, though the analysis behind that claim is not named.

Next, write a description of up to 750 characters, which is the limit stated in [Adwave's beginner's guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Lead with your service and your neighbourhood. Write for a customer, not a search engine.

Photos are the next priority. Adwave's [Google Maps marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) recommends at least 15 high-quality photos. Its beginner's guide suggests at least 10. Aim for 15, since that is the higher target.

Posts should go up at least once a week, per the same Adwave guide. A post only appears publicly when its status is Live. [Google's post help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en) states that Pending and Not approved posts do not show on Search or Maps.

Finally, make sure your hours, phone number, and address are 100% accurate, and check that your salon can accept bookings. [Booksy's salon marketing guide](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), a vendor resource, states that Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds. For profile-level work that can be done faster, see [how AI tools can help with Google Business Profile optimisation](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization).

## How many reviews does a salon need to compete for the map pack?

Reviews are visible to customers before they choose a salon. [Google's review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) states that reviews show next to a business's Business Profile in Maps and Search. Adwave's [review guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business) cites a third-party analysis by Blogging Wizard of 2 million Google Business Profile pages, which found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack. The page does not quantify that effect, so use 200 as a target, not a guarantee.

The worked example below uses assumed inputs for one salon. The benchmarks come from the sources above; the salon's current numbers are assumptions.

| Profile item | Sourced benchmark | Example salon (assumption) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google reviews | Over 200 | 180 | 20 |
| Photos | At least 15 | 9 | 6 |
| Description length | Up to 750 characters | 400 characters | 350 characters |
| Live posts per week | At least 1 | 0 | 1 |

At an assumed 12 new reviews per month, the salon needs 20 more reviews, which is about 1.7 months, so roughly two months. This is an estimate based on the assumed rate, not a forecast.

## Why must citation data match exactly across directories?

Citations are listings that repeat your name, address, and phone number (NAP) outside your own profile. [Adwave's voice search guide](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses) states that the NAP must be exactly the same on your website, your Google Business Profile, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places.

Small formatting differences count. Adwave's beginner's guide notes that the website must match the profile exactly, including punctuation and whether "St." or "Street" is used. Check each listing against your profile before adding new ones.

Consistency also matters for paid search. [Google's location assets help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en) states that location data used in Google Ads comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and that successful corrections there flow into linked Google Ads accounts. If your Ads and profile disagree, fix the profile first. For a full look at paid options, read [Google Ads for hair salons: the complete guide](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-complete-guide).

## Does Apple Maps matter for a San Francisco salon?

Apple Maps is worth checking, but treat the share figures with caution. [Adwave's Apple Business Connect article](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring) states that 88% of people use Google Maps compared with 12% for Apple Maps, but the page does not name a source for that split. In a maps-only comparison that excludes web search, the same article says Apple Business Connect has already captured a 7% share and is growing.

The article describes two features worth knowing about. Apple Maps Actions let a business connect third-party services such as OpenTable so customers can book without leaving the app. Apple Business Connect also reports Profile Views, the count of how many people saw the business card in Apple Maps. Claim the listing and check the NAP so it matches your other directories.

## How should a salon measure local SEO progress?

Track a small set of measures each month and compare them with the previous month. [Adwave's local search intent guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent) names six useful metrics: Google Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift over time.

Record the starting values before you change anything. Without a baseline, you cannot tell whether a new photo batch or a review push made a difference. For a salon, clicks to call and direction requests are usually the most direct signals of a booking. Map-pack views show whether you are visible at all.

## FAQ

### How many photos should a salon add to its Business Profile?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos in its [Google Maps marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Its beginner's guide suggests at least 10. Use 15 as the target and update the set regularly.

### Does a new Google Business Profile post show right away?

Not always. [Google's post help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en) states that a post is shown publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Posts that are Pending or Not approved do not show.

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a salon?

Yes. [Google's Business Profile page for Canada](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/) describes it as a free Business Profile on Google Search and Maps. The [Keepers Digital comparison](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/) also describes it as a free tool for managing presence across Search and Maps.

### Which metrics show whether local SEO is working?

Track Google Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from the profile, and branded search lift, as listed in [Adwave's local search intent guide](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent).
