Most hair salons can start testing Google Ads with about $600 to $1,200 a month (roughly $20 to $40 a day), then scale once they know their cost per new client. The right number for your salon depends on your city, your competition and how much profit a new client brings. Work backwards from what a new client is worth, and spend no more than you can recover.

Costs per click vary widely by location and keyword, so use Google Keyword Planner and your own account data for real figures in your area.

## Typical budget ranges

| Monthly budget | What it usually suits |
|---|---|
| Under about $300 | Too thin for most salons to learn from; fine only for a tiny test in a small town |
| About $600 to $1,200 | A realistic starting point for one salon, enough to test several keyword themes |
| About $1,200 to $3,000 | Competitive cities, multiple services or multiple stylists to fill |
| $3,000+ | Large or multi-location salons with proven conversion tracking |

These are planning ranges, not guarantees. If your city is expensive (big metro areas), you may need more clicks' worth of budget to get the same number of enquiries as a small town.

## How to calculate your own budget

### Step 1: Work out what a new client is worth

Estimate the profit (not revenue) from a typical new client over a year.

Example with simple numbers: if a client books 6 visits a year, and you make $25 profit per visit, the client is worth about $150 in profit in the first year. Add colour, treatments and retail if they are regular, and be honest about how many clients actually come back.

### Step 2: Decide what you can pay to win a client

A common approach is to cap your acquisition cost at a share of the first-year profit, say 20% to 30%. In the example, that is about $30 to $45 per new client. Choose a figure you are comfortable with.

### Step 3: Estimate clicks per client

If your average click costs $3 and about 1 in 10 clicks becomes a booking (a rough example only; your rate may be higher or lower), then each booking costs about $30 in clicks.

Cost per booking = cost per click divided by conversion rate. $3 / 0.10 = $30.

### Step 4: Set your monthly budget

Choose how many new clients you want per month, then multiply by cost per booking.

If you want 20 new clients at $30 each, your budget is $600 a month. If clicks cost $6 in your city, the same goal costs $1,200.

### Step 5: Sanity check with your revenue

Some owners set total marketing at a share of revenue as a rough guide, often somewhere in the mid single digits to around 10%. Treat that as a ceiling check, not a rule. Your own cost per client matters more.

## Two worked examples (illustrative)

These use round numbers to show the method. They are not real salon results.

**Smaller town salon**
- Budget: $600 a month
- Assume an average click of about $1.50, so roughly 400 clicks
- At a 5% booking rate, that is about 20 bookings
- Cost per booking: about $30
- If each new client brings $150 in first-year profit, the campaign returns about $3,000 in profit on $600 spend, before your time

**Large city salon**
- Budget: $2,000 a month
- Assume an average click of about $5, so roughly 400 clicks
- At a 5% booking rate, that is about 20 bookings
- Cost per booking: about $100
- If each new client brings $150 in first-year profit, the return is thinner, so tighter targeting, better landing pages and higher-value services matter more

The lesson: the same number of clicks can be very profitable in one market and marginal in another. Know your numbers before you scale.

## Mistakes that waste a salon's ad budget

**1. Starting too small to learn anything.** A very low budget may produce too few clicks to tell what works. Better to run a focused test for a few weeks.

**2. Not tracking conversions.** Track calls, form submissions and bookings. Without that you cannot tell which keywords bring clients. See our [conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup).

**3. Ignoring your Google Business Profile.** Ads work better when your profile has reviews, photos and services. Our [Google Business Profile service](/services/google-business-profile) covers this.

**4. Bidding on broad keywords only.** "Hair salon" is expensive and vague. Specific services such as balayage, extensions or bridal styling often convert better.

**5. No negative keywords.** Block searches like "hair salon jobs", "cosmetology school" and "hair supplies". Learn more in our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).

## When to increase your budget

Raise your spend gradually when:
1. Your cost per new client is comfortably below what you can afford
2. You have empty chairs or capacity for more clients
3. Your campaigns are losing impression share because of budget (visible in Google Ads)
4. You have tracked enough data to trust the numbers

Cut back or fix before spending more when conversions are not being tracked, bookings are not coming in after several weeks of steady data, or your website or booking page is the bottleneck.

## FAQ

**How much should a small hair salon spend on Google Ads per month?**
Many salons start with roughly $600 to $1,200 a month, then adjust once they know their cost per new client. In expensive cities you may need more, and in small towns you may be able to start lower.

**How much does a click cost for hair salon keywords?**
It varies widely by city, season and keyword. Check Google Keyword Planner for your location, and use your own account's average click cost after a few weeks.

**How long before Google Ads show results for a salon?**
You can get clicks within hours and some enquiries within days, but give the campaign several weeks of steady data before making big decisions. The system and your keyword choices need time to settle.

**Is Google Ads better than Instagram or Facebook ads for salons?**
They do different jobs. Google captures people searching for a salon right now, while Meta ads build awareness and visual appeal. Many salons start with Google, then add Meta once search is working. See our [Facebook ads for hair salons guide](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons).

**Do I need a website to run Google Ads?**
You need a landing page. A simple one-page site or a good booking page works, as long as it loads fast, shows services and prices, and makes booking easy.

**What if I am fully booked?**
You can still use ads to promote higher-value services, fill quiet days or reach clients who will replace those you naturally lose each year.
