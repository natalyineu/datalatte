There is no single right Google Ads budget for a hair salon, because it depends on your city, your competition and what a new client is worth. The reliable method is to work backwards: decide what you can afford to pay for a new client, estimate your cost per booking, and multiply by the number of clients you want. Google then lets you cap spend, so you never pay more than you set.

Picture a bride-to-be searching "balayage near me" on a Sunday night. Your ad sits on top. Below it, the map lists the salons she will compare you with. In the 50 Austin hair salons we pulled from Google Maps in October 2026, the median had 359 reviews and 32 had more than 200. In the 50 Chicago salons, none had fewer than 50. Your ad has to win the click against that, so the budget question is really a cost-per-booking question. The math below answers it in five steps.

Costs per click vary widely by location and keyword. Use Google Keyword Planner, which provides estimated clicks and average CPCs for your keywords ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6238816?hl=en), checked Oct 2026), and your own account data for real figures.

## How Google caps your spend

You set an average daily budget per campaign. On a given day Google can spend up to twice that amount, but you will never pay more than the monthly limit, which is your average daily budget multiplied by 30.4 for most campaigns ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6385083?hl=en), checked Oct 2026). For example, a $20 daily budget means a monthly ceiling of $608.

A small budget is not wasteful by itself, but one that produces a handful of clicks a month will not teach you which keywords bring clients. Size the test so it can produce data.

## How to calculate your own budget

### Step 1: Work out what a new client is worth

Estimate the profit (not revenue) from a typical new client over a year.

Example with simple numbers: if a client books 6 visits a year, and you make $25 profit per visit, the client is worth about $150 in profit in the first year. Add colour, treatments and retail if they are regular, and be honest about how many clients actually come back.

### Step 2: Decide what you can pay to win a client

A common approach is to cap your acquisition cost at a share of the first-year profit. If you choose 20% to 30% (a planning assumption, not an industry standard), the example client allows about $30 to $45 per new client. Choose a figure you are comfortable with.

### Step 3: Estimate clicks per client

If your average click costs $3 and about 1 in 10 clicks becomes a booking (a rough example only; your rate may be higher or lower), then each booking costs about $30 in clicks.

Cost per booking = cost per click divided by conversion rate. $3 / 0.10 = $30.

### Step 4: Set your monthly budget

Choose how many new clients you want per month, then multiply by cost per booking.

If you want 20 new clients at $30 each, your budget is $600 a month. If clicks cost $6 and the booking rate stays at 10%, each booking costs $60 and the same goal costs $1,200.

## The Math: Two Markets, Same Clicks

Example only. These are invented round numbers to show the method, not real salon results and not benchmarks. Swap in your own.

Shared assumptions: 5% of clicks become bookings, each new client is worth $150 in first-year profit (6 visits x $25).

| | Smaller-town salon | Large-city salon |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly budget | $600 | $2,000 |
| Click cost (assumed) | $1.50 | $5.00 |
| Clicks | 400 | 400 |
| Bookings (5%) | 20 | 20 |
| Cost per booking | $30 | $100 |
| First-year profit (20 x $150) | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| Profit after ad spend | $2,400 | $1,000 |

Same 400 clicks, same 20 clients, and the large-city salon keeps $1,400 less. Now test the booking rate on the $600 budget at a $3 click (200 clicks):

| Booking rate (assumed) | Bookings | Cost per booking |
|---|---|---|
| 3% | 6 | $100 |
| 5% | 10 | $60 |
| 10% | 20 | $30 |

At 3% you pay $100 to win a $150 client. The fix is rarely a bigger budget. It is a faster booking page, a visible phone number and a landing page for the exact service searched.

## Do This Today

1. Open Keyword Planner and note the average click cost for "hair salon near me" and one service keyword, such as "balayage" plus your city.
2. Work out first-year profit for your average new client, using visits per year x profit per visit.
3. Take 20% to 30% of that figure as your ceiling per new client (a planning assumption, not an industry standard).
4. Set a daily budget that matches: monthly budget / 30.4 = daily budget. For $600, that is about $19.74 a day.
5. Ask your first 10 new clients this week how they found you.

## Mistakes that waste a salon's ad budget

**1. Starting too small to learn anything.** Too few clicks tell you nothing. Run a focused test for several weeks.

**2. Not tracking conversions.** Track calls, form submissions and bookings. Without that you cannot tell which keywords bring clients. See our [conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup).

**3. Ignoring your Google Business Profile.** Ads work better when your profile has reviews, photos and services. Our [Google Business Profile service](/services/google-business-profile) covers this.

**4. Bidding on broad keywords only.** "Hair salon" is expensive and vague. Specific services such as balayage, extensions or bridal styling often convert better.

**5. No negative keywords.** Block searches like "hair salon jobs", "cosmetology school" and "hair supplies"; a negative keyword stops your ad triggering on that word or phrase ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6238816?hl=en), checked Oct 2026). ## When to increase your budget

Raise your spend gradually when:
1. Your cost per new client is comfortably below what you can afford
2. You have empty chairs or capacity for more clients
3. Your campaigns are losing impression share because of budget (visible in Google Ads)
4. You have tracked enough data to trust the numbers

Cut back or fix before spending more when conversions are not being tracked, bookings are not coming in after several weeks of steady data, or your website or booking page is the bottleneck.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a small hair salon spend on Google Ads per month?**
A: There is no fixed number. Work backwards from what a new client is worth and what you can afford to pay for one, set a daily budget that can produce enough clicks to learn from, then adjust once you know your cost per new client. Expensive cities need more per click; small towns may need less.

**Q: How much does a click cost for hair salon keywords?**
A: It varies widely by city, season and keyword. Check Google Keyword Planner, which shows estimated average CPCs, for your location, and use your own account's average click cost after a few weeks.

**Q: How long before Google Ads show results for a salon?**
A: You can get clicks within hours and some enquiries within days, but give the campaign several weeks of steady data before making big decisions. The system and your keyword choices need time to settle.

**Q: Is Google Ads better than Instagram or Facebook ads for salons?**
A: They do different jobs. Google captures people searching for a salon right now, while Meta ads build awareness and visual appeal. Many salons start with Google, then add Meta once search is working. See our [Facebook ads for hair salons guide](/blog/facebook-ads-for-hair-salons).

**Q: Do I need a website to run Google Ads?**
A: You need a landing page. A simple one-page site or a good booking page works, as long as it loads fast, shows services and prices, and makes booking easy.

**Q: What if I am fully booked?**
A: You can still use ads to promote higher-value services, fill quiet days or reach clients who will replace those you naturally lose each year.
