A pet groomer should start with Facebook and Instagram, plus Google Business Profile posts, and post consistently rather than everywhere. Pew Research's 2026 survey found 70% of US adults use Facebook and 51% use Instagram. Among adults 30 to 49, 78% use Facebook and 64% use Instagram. Post real work, show prices and make booking one tap away.

This guide covers where to post, what to post and how to fit it into a few hours a week. Pew data is for all US adults, not pet owners, and no source we checked publishes a groomer engagement benchmark. Examples marked as assumptions are illustrations.

## Which Social Platforms Should a Pet Groomer Use?

Pew's survey (January 30 to June 18, 2026) gives use among US adults: YouTube 84%, Facebook 70%, Instagram 51% and TikTok 31%. By age, Instagram leads among 18 to 29 (79%) and Facebook among 30 to 49 (78%, against 64% for Instagram).

| Platform | US adults 18-29 | US adults 30-49 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facebook | 69% | 78% |
| Instagram | 79% | 64% |

Both reach most age groups, so one of each is a sound start. Use TikTok or YouTube only if you can make video regularly. The American Pet Products Association reports 95 million US households own a pet, so your audience is wide; local reach matters more than size.

## What Should a Groomer Post?

Use content only you can make:

- Finished grooms, with the owner's permission before posting a pet or family photo
- A short explainer, such as how often a breed needs a trim
- Opening hours, holiday closures and available slots
- A clear price guide with a "from" price
- Reviews, quoted from customers who left them publicly

Skip invented "before and after" images and stock photos. They weaken trust, and customers can tell. For more on Instagram specifically, see our guide to [pet groomer Instagram engagement](/blog/maximizing-instagram-engagement-for-pet-groomers).

## Are Google Business Profile Posts Social Media Too?

They work like social posts but live on your Google listing. Google lets you post updates, offers and events, with an optional button linking to your booking page. Offers need a title and dates, and posts older than six months are archived unless you set a date range.

Posts can be scheduled and set to repeat. Google rejects phone numbers it cannot verify as connected to the business, plus low-value or auto-generated text.

This puts your latest news in front of people already searching for a groomer. Our [Google Business Profile guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-for-pet-groomers) covers the rest of the profile.

## How Do You Get Customers to Book From Social?

Put a link in your bio that goes straight to booking or a short enquiry form. Add UTM tags to that link so Google Analytics shows which platform sent the visitor. Google advises always including source, medium and campaign, in lowercase.

Reply to comments and messages within a day if you can, because pet owners often ask about prices and availability. Ask happy clients for a Google review with a link or QR code, and never offer a discount for reviews, which Google prohibits.

## How Much Time Does It Take? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions** for a one-person salon.

| Task | Hours per week |
| --- | --- |
| Photos taken while grooming | 0.5 |
| Writing captions and scheduling 4 posts | 1.5 |
| Replying to comments and messages | 1.5 |
| Checking results | 0.5 |
| Total | 4 |

Four hours a week is 16 hours a month. At an assumed $25 an hour of your time, that is $400 a month. If social media brings 6 new grooms a month at $70, it returns $420 before costs, which is roughly break-even on time alone. Repeat visits change the picture, so track the source of each new client.

A scheduler can cut the writing step. Buffer's free plan covers 3 channels with 10 scheduled posts each, and our [Hootsuite and pricing comparison](/blog/pet-groomer-social-media-management) covers paid options.

## FAQ

### Which social media platform is best for pet groomers?
Facebook and Instagram together. Pew 2026 shows 70% and 51% of US adults use them, with Facebook strongest at ages 30 to 49.

### How often should a groomer post?
No source we checked publishes a groomer-specific number. Pick a pace you can keep, such as three or four posts a week.

### Do I need TikTok?
Pew 2026 shows 31% of US adults use TikTok. Only add it if you can make regular video.

### Can I post customers' pets?
Ask the owner first. Do not post a client's pet or family photo without permission.

### How do I know if social media works?
Ask every new client how they found you and tag your bio link with UTM parameters.
