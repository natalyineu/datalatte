## Who Can You Reach on Facebook for Home Services?

Facebook is the second most used platform among US adults in [Pew's 2026 social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/), behind YouTube at 84%. Its share has stayed between 68% and 71% in every survey since 2016, so it is a stable channel rather than a short-lived trend. Instagram is used by 51% of adults and Nextdoor by 18%, up from 13% in 2021.

These figures describe who uses a platform, not who is planning a job. Pew does not measure homeowners or hiring intent, so treat the numbers as the size of the audience, not a targeting promise. A plumber in a town of 50,000 still needs to reach the right streets, which means the service area and offer matter more than the platform total.

## Is Facebook or Google Better for Home Services?

They catch different moments. Google's [Display Network](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190) reaches people as they browse websites, apps and Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail, and Google states that Display can reach people across 35 million websites and apps. Search ads appear when someone types a request, such as a burst pipe, so the intent is usually stronger.

| Factor | Facebook lead ads | Google Search |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical moment | Browsing or planning | Active need |
| Usual intent | Lower | Higher |
| Best for | Planned jobs such as gutters, painting, seasonal tune-ups | Urgent or same-week jobs |
| Audience control | Service area and form questions | Keywords and location |

These intent patterns are general and are not measured in the sources for this guide. Compare the cost per booked job for each channel in your own records. ## How Much Should a Home Service Business Spend on Facebook Ads?

There is no sourced Facebook benchmark for home services in this guide, so the best starting point is a budget that can be sustained for at least four weeks. WordStream's [Google Ads benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks) list an all-industry average of $2.69 per search click and $0.63 per Display click. That page is a vendor benchmark first published in 2016 and is not specific to home services, so treat it as context only.

The useful question is how many booked jobs a budget buys. Divide the monthly budget by cost per lead, then multiply by the share of leads that are reached, quoted and won. If the win rate falls to 10%, the campaign books 1.2 jobs and the cost per booked job rises to about $833. Follow-up speed and the quote itself often move this number more than extra budget does. Record the average job value and win rate before starting, because without them it is not possible to tell whether any cost per lead is good.

## Can You Target Homeowners on Facebook?

The sources for this guide do not describe Meta's current targeting options for homeowners, so check Ads Manager before assuming a homeowner filter exists. Do not build the campaign around a "homeowner" label until the option has been confirmed.

What can be controlled is the geography, the offer and the form. Draw the service area around the towns or postcodes that can be reached on the same day. Name the area in the ad copy, such as "Serving [town] and nearby suburbs". Then add a qualifying question to the lead form, such as the postcode or suburb, so out-of-area enquiries are easy to spot and drop.

## How Do You Test Facebook Ads Fairly?

Meta's [A/B testing guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669) describes a test as comparing two versions of an ad strategy by changing one variable, such as the image, text, audience or placement. Each version is shown to a separate segment, so nobody sees both. Meta recommends choosing a hypothesis first, for example that a custom audience will outperform an interest-based one.

Use the same budget for both versions so the comparison is fair. Meta advises against informal testing by switching ad sets on and off manually, because that can create overlapping audiences and unreliable results. ## How Should You Set Up a Local Campaign?

1. Choose a lead generation goal, and decide whether leads will come through a form or a phone call.
2. Draw the location around the area that can be reached the same day, and keep it tight.
3. Make one offer for one service, with one clear next step, such as requesting a quote.
4. Keep the form short: service needed, postcode or suburb, and when the job is needed.
5. Call or text each lead quickly. A lead nobody answers is wasted spend, whatever it cost.
6. Track each lead to a booked job in a spreadsheet, so cost per booked job is always visible.

## FAQ

### How much should a home service business spend on Facebook ads?

No sourced benchmark in this guide sets a Facebook budget for home services. Start with an amount that can be sustained for at least four weeks, then scale only if cost per booked job holds up in your own records.

### Are Facebook leads worse than Google leads?

Usually they carry lower intent, because people were often browsing rather than searching for an urgent job. The sources here do not measure lead quality, so compare cost per booked job across both channels in your own account.

### Do I need a landing page?

Not necessarily. If traffic goes to a website, the page should show the service area, phone number and one clear offer.

### Can I exclude people outside my service area?

Set a tight location and add a qualifying question to the form. The sources do not confirm how precisely Meta can exclude out-of-area users, so check the current location options in Ads Manager.
