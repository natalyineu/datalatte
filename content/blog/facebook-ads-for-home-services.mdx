A home service business such as a plumber, cleaner, roofer or landscaper should run Facebook lead ads to a tight service area and judge them by cost per booked job, not cost per lead. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put Home and Home Improvement lead campaigns at $42.95 per lead and $2.18 per click on Facebook. Google search in the same category costs $90.92 per lead, but searchers have usually already decided to hire.

This guide covers what Facebook costs for home services, how to set up targeting, and a worked example. Every figure is sourced at the end, and examples are labelled as assumptions.

## How Much Do Facebook Ads Cost for Home Services?

LocaliQ's Facebook Advertising Benchmarks (updated September 23, 2026) have no separate Home Services row, so Home and Home Improvement is the closest match. Lead campaigns average a 2.14% click-through rate, $2.18 per click, a 5.32% conversion rate and $42.95 per lead. The all-industry averages are $1.80 per click, 8.54% conversion and $27.39 per lead, so home services pay more than average for each lead.

Traffic campaigns are cheaper per click at $0.63, but LocaliQ publishes no cost per lead for that objective. A cheap click is not a cheap customer.

## Facebook or Google: Which Is Better for Home Services?

They catch different moments. Google Search shows your ad when someone types "emergency plumber near me". Facebook interrupts someone scrolling, so it suits jobs people plan: cleaning plans, lawn care, gutter work, painting, seasonal tune-ups.

| Measure (LocaliQ 2026) | Facebook lead ads | Google Search |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost per click | $2.18 | $8.33 |
| Conversion rate | 5.32% | 8.05% |
| Cost per lead | $42.95 | $90.92 |

Leads are not equal. A Google click often comes from active intent; a Facebook lead form can be filled in a few taps with weaker intent. Compare the cost per booked job, which only your own numbers can show. ## Can You Target Homeowners on Facebook?

Only loosely. Detailed targeting such as interests and behaviours works as a suggestion for most campaign goals, not a hard filter. Jon Loomer's 2026 guide (a secondary source, an independent Meta advertising consultant) says detailed targeting inputs are suggestions by default and that for 11 common performance goals they always remain suggestions. He also notes that location targeting covers people who live or have recently been in a place, and advertisers cannot isolate residents only.

So do not build the campaign around a "homeowner" label. Control the things you can: the service area, the offer, the ad wording ("Serving the [town] area") and a qualifying question in the lead form. Meta's own help pages could not be opened for this guide, so check the current options in Ads Manager.

## How Should You Set Up a Local Campaign?

A simple structure that avoids wasted spend:

1. **Goal:** Leads, with an instant form, or Leads sent to calls or messages if you answer them fast.
2. **Location:** Your actual service area, drawn around towns or postcodes you can reach the same day.
3. **Offer:** One service, one price signal or free quote, one next step.
4. **Form questions:** Service needed, postcode or suburb, and when they need it. Extra fields cut conversion but cut junk.
5. **Follow-up:** Call or text back quickly. A lead nobody answers is wasted spend, whatever it cost.

Add the form's qualifying question to filter people outside your area. For budget logic, see [how much to spend on Facebook ads for a local business](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend).

## What Does a Month Look Like? A Worked Example

Every input here is an **assumption**, built on the LocaliQ lead-campaign averages above. Replace them with your own.

| Input | Assumed value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $1,000 |
| Cost per lead | $42.95 (LocaliQ benchmark) |
| Leads | about 23 |
| Share you reach and quote (assumption) | 60% |
| Quotes accepted (assumption) | 25% |
| Booked jobs | about 3.5 |
| Cost per booked job | about $286 |
| Average job value (assumption) | $450 |

On these numbers the first job covers the ad cost per booking with some margin, and repeat customers add to it. If you only reach 30% of leads or close 10%, the cost per job doubles or more. The fix is usually follow-up speed and the offer, not more budget. Another view of the first test: run it for 4 weeks before judging.

## What Creative Works for Home Services?

Meta publishes no home-services creative benchmark we could verify, so test rather than copy a template. Ideas that match how people hire trades: a real before-and-after photo of your own work, a short phone video explaining a common problem and a price range, and a clear local line like "Serving [town]". Avoid stock photos and fake reviews. Retargeting people who watched your video or visited your site is a standard second step, and our [Facebook ads for local business beginner playbook](/blog/how-to-run-facebook-ads-for-a-local-business-in-2026-complete-beginners-playbook) shows how to start.

## FAQ

### How much should a home service business spend on Facebook ads?

There is no standard figure. At LocaliQ's $42.95 per lead, $1,000 buys roughly 23 leads. Start with a budget you can sustain for at least a month, then scale based on cost per booked job.

### Are Facebook leads worse than Google leads?

They are usually lower intent, because people were not searching. LocaliQ shows cheaper leads on Facebook but does not measure lead quality. Compare cost per booked job in your own account.

### Do I need a landing page?

Not for instant forms. A page helps if you send traffic to a website, and it should show your service area, phone number and one clear offer.

### Can I exclude people outside my service area?

Set a tight location and add a form question. Meta treats location as an audience control for people who live or have recently been there, so some out-of-area travellers may still see ads.

### Is 80% of home services relying on word of mouth true?

We could not verify that statistic, so we removed it. Ask your own customers how they found you.
