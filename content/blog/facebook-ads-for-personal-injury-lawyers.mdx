Facebook ads can work for personal injury lawyers as a way to build awareness and capture leads, but they usually support Google search ads rather than replace them. Someone searching "car accident lawyer" shows stronger intent than someone scrolling a feed. LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmarks put the average click for Attorneys and Legal Services at $0.69, but a click is not a case.

To run them well, target a defined local area, use a lead form or a focused landing page, write copy that follows your state bar's advertising rules and Meta's ad policies, and judge results by cost per signed case rather than cost per lead. This guide covers targeting limits, compliance, a worked example and a pre-launch checklist.

## Why Is Cost Per Signed Case the Number That Matters?

A lead form can log a cheap lead at 11 pm that nobody answers until the next afternoon. The dashboard shows a result, but the case may already be gone. For that reason this guide uses cost per signed case, not cost per lead, as the main metric.

The next section shows how the two diverge.

## What Does the Math Look Like? A Worked Example

**Every input below is an assumption, not a market benchmark and not a client result.** Replace them with your own numbers. Assume a $3,000 monthly budget and two form setups. Meta offers two instant form types, "More volume" and "Higher intent" ([Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/252352181957512/)). Assume the first gets cheaper leads and fewer real consultations. In both cases assume 40% of consultations sign.

| | Form A: more volume (assumed) | Form B: higher intent (assumed) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per lead | $40 | $65 |
| Leads ($3,000 / cost) | 75 | 46 |
| Leads that become consultations | 10% = 7.5 | 25% = 11.5 |
| Signed cases (40%) | 3 | 4.6 |
| Cost per consultation | $400 | $260 |
| **Cost per signed case** | **$1,000** | **$650** |

Form A is 38% cheaper per lead and 54% more expensive per signed case. The numbers are invented, but the mechanism holds: lead price tells you nothing until you divide by signed cases. Then compare the result with your average fee after costs. Only you know that figure.

## How Does Facebook Fit With Google Ads?

Google Ads captures people already searching for a lawyer. Facebook and Instagram reach people earlier, or retarget people who visited your website. Many small firms run both: search for high-intent clicks, Meta for local awareness, retargeting and trust. ## What Targeting Is Allowed for Injury Ads?

Meta has narrowed targeting for sensitive topics. From January 19, 2022 it removed Detailed Targeting options tied to categories such as health, race or ethnicity, political affiliation, religion and sexual orientation ([FKKS Advertising Law](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102hb15/facebook-announces-removal-of-targeting-options-related-to-sensitive-topics), secondary).

Meta's Advertising Standards also bar ads that assert or imply a person's personal attributes, including "physical or mental health (including medical conditions)". Its examples show "Do you have diabetes?" as not allowed and "New diabetes treatment available" as allowed, because the second describes a service rather than the viewer ([Meta Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/)). By the same logic, a headline like "Hurt in a crash?" is risky. Write about what you offer, not what the viewer has suffered.

What works instead:

- **Geography:** the city or counties you can serve.
- **Retargeting:** people who visited your site, watched your videos or engaged with your page.
- **Custom audiences:** upload a client list only if you have permission and it fits your confidentiality duties.
- **Exclusions:** existing clients and recent converts.

## What Do the Bar Rules Say About Facebook Ads?

Lawyer advertising is regulated state by state, and most states follow the ABA Model Rules in some form. California's Rule 7.1 reads like the model rule: a lawyer may not make a false or misleading communication, which includes a material misrepresentation or the omission of a fact needed to avoid misleading. Its Comment 2 treats an express guarantee of a result as misleading, and its comments warn that past results or testimonials can mislead without a note that each case depends on its own facts. It also says "no fee without recovery" claims mislead unless they say whether the client owes costs ([State Bar of California, Chapter 7](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/legal-professionals/rules/rules-professional-conduct/current-rules-professional-conduct/chapter-7-information-about-legal-services)).

California Rule 7.2 requires each advertisement to include the name and address of at least one responsible lawyer or firm. Other states differ. The ABA's own Rule 7.1 page returned an error when checked, so read your state's version directly.

## How Should You Write the Creative and Handle Leads?

- **Headlines:** say who you help and what happens next, such as "Free case review for car accidents."
- **Images:** real photos of your team and office usually read as more trustworthy than stock courthouse images. Get permission for photos of real people.
- **Copy:** one clear action, plain language, no promises of results.
- **Forms:** ask only for name, phone and a short description. Add a privacy notice and consent language.
- **Speed:** respond to every enquiry fast. Slow follow-up wastes the spend.

A landing page lets you explain your process and credentials, while an instant form keeps people inside Facebook. Test both and compare cost per signed case. For video formats, see our [Facebook video ads guide](/blog/facebook-video-ads-guide).

## How Much Should You Spend?

No reliable injury-specific cost benchmark was found, and costs depend on market and case type. Plan from the case side instead:

- Start with a test budget you can sustain for several weeks.
- Work backward: average fee per signed case times the share of consultations that sign gives the value of a consultation. That is the ceiling on what you can pay for one.
- Scale gradually once results are stable.

## What Should You Check Before Spending?

1. Write down your average fee per signed case and the share of consultations that sign.
2. Call your own intake line at 9 pm tomorrow and time the pickup.
3. Set a rule: every ad lead gets a call within 5 minutes in business hours.
4. Have your state's rules checked against the first three ads.
5. Add exclusions for current clients before launch.
6. Track every lead in one sheet: date, source, consultation (yes/no), signed (yes/no).
7. Review the sheet on day 30 and day 60 and calculate cost per signed case.

Use conversion tracking on your thank-you page and a unique tracking phone number. Our [analytics service](/services/analytics) can set this up.

## FAQ

### Do Facebook ads work for personal injury lawyers?
They can, especially next to Google Ads and local SEO. Facebook reaches people before they search, but intent is lower, so judge results by cost per signed case.

### How much should a personal injury lawyer spend on Facebook ads?
Start with a test budget for several weeks and set the ceiling from your average fee and sign rate. LocaliQ's legal click price is $0.69 on traffic campaigns, but no lead-campaign benchmark exists for the category.

### Can I target people who were recently in an accident?
No. Meta removed many sensitive Detailed Targeting options in 2022, and its policy prohibits ads that assert or imply a person's health condition. Use local targeting, retargeting and clear creative.

### Are Facebook ads allowed under bar rules?
Lawyers may advertise, but each state sets its own rules on claims, testimonials, disclaimers and required firm information. Check your state bar's rules before launch.

### Should I use Facebook ads or Google Ads first?
Most firms should start with Google Ads and a strong Google Business Profile, then add Facebook for awareness and retargeting. Our content marketing for lawyers guide and website design for lawyers guide cover the pages those ads send people to.
