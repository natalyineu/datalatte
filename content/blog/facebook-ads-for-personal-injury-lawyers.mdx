Facebook ads can work for personal injury lawyers as a way to build awareness and capture leads, but they usually support Google search ads rather than replace them, because someone searching "car accident lawyer" shows stronger intent than someone scrolling a feed. To run them well, target a defined local area, use lead forms or a focused landing page, write copy that follows your state bar's advertising rules and Meta's ad policies, and judge results by cost per signed case rather than cost per lead.

Costs per lead for legal advertising vary widely by city and case type, so test with a controlled budget and track real outcomes before scaling.

## How Facebook ads fit into a personal injury marketing mix

Google Ads captures people who are already searching for a lawyer. Facebook and Instagram (Meta Ads) reach people earlier, or retarget people who have visited your website. Many small firms combine the two: search ads for high-intent clicks, plus Meta Ads for local awareness, retargeting and trust-building. See our guide to [Google Ads for law firms](/blog/google-ads-for-law-firms) for the search side.

## Building a targeting strategy

Meta has narrowed the targeting options available for sensitive topics. Starting January 19, 2022 it removed Detailed Targeting options tied to categories such as health, race or ethnicity, political affiliation, religion and sexual orientation ([FKKS Advertising Law](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102hb15/facebook-announces-removal-of-targeting-options-related-to-sensitive-topics)). Meta's policy also bans ads that assert or imply a person's personal attributes, including health or medical conditions, and ads that suggest you know something about the viewer's situation ([Meta Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/), checked Oct 2026). In practice, write about what you offer, not about what the viewer has suffered.

Sensible approaches:

- **Geography:** Target the city or counties you can actually serve.
- **Clear local area plus strong creative:** Give Meta a defined area and a distinct message rather than stacking many narrow interests.
- **Retargeting:** Show ads to people who visited your site, watched your videos or engaged with your page.
- **Custom audiences:** Upload a customer list only if you have permission and it complies with your professional obligations. Client confidentiality rules apply.
- **Exclusions:** Exclude people who are already clients or have recently converted.

If an option is not in Ads Manager, you cannot use it.

## Creative best practices

- **Headlines:** Be clear about who you help and what happens next, such as "Car accident case? Get a free case review."
- **Images and video:** Real photos of your team and office usually build more trust than stock courthouse images. Get permission for any photos of real people.
- **Copy:** Lead with what you do, explain it in plain language and give one clear call to action.
- **Avoid exaggeration:** Do not promise results or imply outcomes.

<Callout type="warning">Lawyer advertising is regulated by each state. ABA Model Rule 7.1 bars false or misleading communications about a lawyer or the lawyer's services, and most states have adopted a version of it; many add rules on disclaimers, testimonials and past results ([Inns of Court summary of Rule 7.1](https://inns.innsofcourt.org/media/197519/rule_71-exec_summary.pdf)). Have your ads reviewed against your state's rules before they run, and follow Meta's own advertising policies as well.</Callout>

## Lead forms vs. landing pages

Meta's instant forms keep people inside Facebook or Instagram. Meta offers a "more volume" form type and a "higher intent" type that adds a review step so people confirm their details before submitting ([Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/252352181957512/), checked Oct 2026). A focused landing page lets you explain your process, show your credentials and set expectations. Test both and compare cost per signed case, not just cost per lead. Our guide to [Facebook lead ads setup](/blog/facebook-lead-ads-local-business-setup-guide) covers the mechanics.

Whichever you choose:

1. Ask only for what you need, such as name, phone number and a short description of what happened.
2. Add a clear privacy notice and consent language.
3. Respond to every enquiry quickly. Speed to first contact often decides who gets the case.

## Budgeting and bidding

There is no standard budget, and we could not find a reliable benchmark we are willing to quote. Costs depend heavily on your market and the case type. A planning approach:

- **Start with a test budget** you can sustain for several weeks, so you have enough data to judge.
- **Scale gradually** once results are stable.
- **Work backward from case value.** Estimate your average fee per signed case and the share of consultations that become cases. That sets the most you can pay per consultation.
- **Consider a cost cap** once you have a baseline from your own data. Meta describes cost cap as a strategy that keeps your average cost per result around an amount you set ([Meta Business Help](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/272336376749096), checked Oct 2026).

For general budgeting, see [how much to spend on Facebook ads as a local business](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend).

## Measuring success

Track these in order of importance:

1. **Cost per signed case:** the number that matters.
2. **Cost per qualified consultation:** what you pay for each real meeting.
3. **Cost per lead:** useful for optimization but easy to misread.
4. **Lead quality:** ask every new client how they found you and keep call records.

Use conversion tracking on your thank-you page and a unique tracking phone number so you can connect leads to outcomes. Our [analytics and reporting service](/services/analytics) can help set this up.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Targeting too broadly or too narrowly** without testing.
- **Generic stock images,** which people scroll past.
- **Sending traffic to a cluttered homepage** instead of a focused page or form.
- **Ignoring exclusions,** so existing clients see your ads.
- **Set and forget:** changing nothing for weeks, or changing everything every day. Test one variable at a time.
- **Slow follow-up:** paying for leads and then calling back hours later.

## FAQ

**Q: Do Facebook ads work for personal injury lawyers?**
A: They can, especially alongside Google Ads and local SEO. Facebook reaches people before they search, but intent is lower than search, so judge results by cost per signed case.

**Q: How much should I spend on Facebook ads as a personal injury lawyer?**
A: Start with a test budget you can sustain for several weeks, and set the ceiling from your average case value and conversion rates. Costs vary a great deal between small cities and large metro areas.

**Q: Can I target people who were recently in an accident?**
A: No. Meta removed many sensitive Detailed Targeting options in 2022, and its policy prohibits ads that assert or imply a person's health condition or other personal attributes. Use local targeting, retargeting and strong creative instead.

**Q: Are Facebook ads allowed under bar rules?**
A: Lawyers may advertise, but every state has its own rules on disclaimers, testimonials, claims and required firm information, and false or misleading advertising is barred under the ABA model rule most states follow. Check your state bar's rules and have your ads reviewed before launch.

**Q: Should I use Facebook ads or Google Ads first?**
A: Most firms should start with Google Ads and a strong Google Business Profile, since they capture people actively searching, then add Facebook ads for awareness and retargeting. See our [local SEO for law firms](/blog/local-seo-for-law-firms) guide.
