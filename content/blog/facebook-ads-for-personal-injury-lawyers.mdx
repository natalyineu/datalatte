Facebook ads can work for personal injury lawyers as a way to build awareness and capture leads, but they are usually a supporting channel rather than a replacement for Google search ads, because people searching "car accident lawyer" show stronger intent than people scrolling a feed. To run them well, target a defined local area, use lead forms or a focused landing page, write copy that follows your state bar's advertising rules, and judge results by cost per signed case rather than cost per lead.

Costs per lead for legal advertising vary widely by city and case type, so test with a controlled budget and track real outcomes before scaling.

## How Facebook Ads Fit Into a Personal Injury Marketing Mix

Google Ads captures people who are already searching for a lawyer. Facebook and Instagram (Meta Ads) reach people earlier, or retarget people who have already visited your website. Many small firms get the best results by combining the two: search ads for high-intent clicks, plus Meta Ads for local awareness, retargeting and referral-style trust-building. See our guide to [Google Ads for law firms](/blog/google-ads-for-law-firms) for the search side.

## Building a Targeting Strategy

Meta has tightened which targeting options are available, especially around sensitive topics such as health. Check the current options in Ads Manager and Meta's advertising policies, and avoid ads that imply you know about a person's injury or health condition.

Sensible approaches include:

- **Geography:** Target the city or counties you can actually serve, using location settings that match where your clients live and work.
- **Broad audiences with strong creative:** Meta's delivery system often does well when you give it a clear local area and a distinct message, rather than stacking many narrow interests.
- **Retargeting:** Show ads to people who visited your site, watched your videos or engaged with your page.
- **Custom audiences:** Upload a customer list only if you have permission to use the data and it complies with your professional obligations. Do not use client data casually; confidentiality rules apply.
- **Exclusions:** Exclude people who are already clients or have recently converted.

Do not rely on lists of supposed "behaviors" such as searching for lawyers or recent injuries. If a targeting option is not in Ads Manager, you cannot use it.

## Creative Best Practices

- **Headlines:** Be clear about who you help and what happens next, such as "Hurt in a car accident? Get a free case review."
- **Images and video:** Real photos of your team and office usually build more trust than stock courthouse images. Get permission for any photos of real people.
- **Copy:** Lead with the reader's situation, explain what you do in plain language, and give one clear call to action.
- **Avoid exaggeration:** Do not promise results, use the word "guarantee," or imply outcomes.

<Callout type="warning">Attorney advertising is regulated by each state bar. Rules can cover required disclaimers, testimonials, claims about past results and how you describe your practice. Have your ads reviewed against your state's rules before they run, and follow Meta's own advertising policies as well.</Callout>

## Lead Forms vs. Landing Pages

Instant forms keep people inside Facebook and can lift the number of submissions, but lead quality can be lower because the form is easy to complete. A focused landing page lets you explain your process, show your credentials and set expectations. Test both and compare cost per signed case, not just cost per lead. Our guide to [Facebook lead ads setup](/blog/facebook-lead-ads-local-business-setup-guide) covers the mechanics.

Whichever you choose:

1. Ask only for what you need, such as name, phone number and a short description of what happened.
2. Add a clear privacy notice and consent language.
3. Respond to every enquiry quickly. Speed to first contact often decides who gets the case.

## Budgeting and Bidding

There is no standard budget. Personal injury is one of the more expensive categories to advertise in, and costs depend heavily on your market. As a planning approach:

- **Start with a test budget** you can sustain for at least four weeks, so the platform has time to learn.
- **Scale gradually.** Large, sudden budget jumps can disrupt delivery, so raise spend in small steps once results are stable.
- **Work backward from case value.** Estimate your average fee per signed case and the share of consultations that become cases. That tells you the most you can afford to pay per consultation.
- **Consider cost caps** once you have a baseline cost per lead from your own data.

## Measuring Success

Track these in order of importance:

1. **Cost per signed case:** the number that really matters.
2. **Cost per qualified consultation:** how much you pay for each real meeting.
3. **Cost per lead:** useful for optimization but easy to misread.
4. **Lead quality:** ask every new client how they found you and keep call records.

Use conversion tracking on your thank-you page and a unique tracking phone number so you can connect leads to outcomes. Our [analytics and reporting service](/services/analytics) can help set this up.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Targeting too broadly or too narrowly,** without testing.
- **Using generic stock images,** which people scroll past.
- **Sending traffic to a cluttered homepage** instead of a focused page or form.
- **Ignoring exclusions,** so existing clients see your ads.
- **Set and forget:** changing nothing for weeks, or changing everything every day. Test one variable at a time.
- **Slow follow-up:** paying for leads and then calling back hours later.

## FAQ

**Q: Do Facebook ads work for personal injury lawyers?**
A: They can, especially as part of a mix with Google Ads and local SEO. Facebook reaches people before they search, but intent is lower than search, so judge results by cost per signed case.

**Q: How much should I spend on Facebook ads as a personal injury lawyer?**
A: Start with a test budget you can sustain for several weeks, and base the ceiling on your average case value and conversion rates. Costs vary a great deal between small cities and large metro areas.

**Q: Can I target people who were recently in an accident?**
A: No. Meta does not let you target individuals based on a recent accident, and its policies restrict targeting around sensitive health information. Use local, retargeting and creative-led approaches instead.

**Q: Are Facebook ads allowed under bar association rules?**
A: Attorney advertising is permitted in all states, but each state bar has its own rules on disclaimers, testimonials, claims and required firm information. Check your state's rules and have your ads reviewed before launch.

**Q: Should I use Facebook ads or Google Ads first?**
A: Most firms should start with Google Ads and a strong Google Business Profile, since they capture people actively searching, and add Facebook ads for awareness and retargeting. See our [local SEO for law firms](/blog/local-seo-for-law-firms) guide.
