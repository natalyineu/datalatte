Facebook ads can work for personal injury lawyers as a way to build awareness and capture leads, but they usually support Google search ads rather than replace them, because someone searching "car accident lawyer" shows stronger intent than someone scrolling a feed. To run them well, target a defined local area, use lead forms or a focused landing page, write copy that follows your state bar's advertising rules and Meta's ad policies, and judge results by cost per signed case rather than cost per lead.

Here is the trap. A form comes in at 11:20 pm: name, phone, "rear-ended on the highway." Your dashboard logs a cheap lead. Nobody calls until 2 pm the next day. By then the person has talked to two other firms. You paid for the lead and lost the case, and the report still says the campaign "worked". This is why cost per lead is the most misleading number in legal advertising, and why this article keeps coming back to cost per signed case.

Costs per lead vary widely by city and case type, so test with a controlled budget and track real outcomes before scaling.

## The Math: Cheap Leads vs Signed Cases

**Example with made-up inputs. These are not market benchmarks and not a client result. Swap in your own numbers.** Assume a $3,000 monthly budget and two form setups. Meta offers a "more volume" form type and a "higher intent" type that adds a review step before submitting ([Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/252352181957512/), checked Oct 2026). Assume the volume form gets cheaper leads but fewer real consultations. In both cases assume 40% of consultations sign.

| | Form A: more volume | Form B: higher intent |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per lead (assumed) | $40 | $65 |
| Leads ($3,000 / cost) | 75 | 46 |
| Leads that become consultations (assumed) | 10% = 7.5 | 25% = 11.5 |
| Signed cases (40%) | 3 | 4.6 |
| Cost per consultation | $400 | $260 |
| **Cost per signed case** | **$1,000** | **$650** |

Form A looks 38% cheaper per lead ($40 vs $65) and is 54% more expensive per signed case ($1,000 vs $650). Same budget, 1.6 more cases from Form B in this example. The numbers are invented, but the mechanism is real: lead price tells you nothing until you divide by signed cases. Now ask the second question: is the average fee on a signed case more than $650 or $1,000 after your costs of doing the work? Only you know that number.

## How Facebook Fits a Personal Injury Mix

Google Ads captures people already searching for a lawyer. Facebook and Instagram (Meta Ads) reach people earlier, or retarget people who visited your website. Many small firms run both: search ads for high-intent clicks, Meta for local awareness, retargeting and trust. See our guide to [Google Ads for law firms](/blog/google-ads-for-law-firms) for the search side.

## Targeting

Meta has narrowed targeting for sensitive topics. Starting January 19, 2022 it removed Detailed Targeting options tied to categories such as health, race or ethnicity, political affiliation, religion and sexual orientation ([FKKS Advertising Law](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102hb15/facebook-announces-removal-of-targeting-options-related-to-sensitive-topics)). Meta's policy also bans ads that assert or imply a person's personal attributes, including health or medical conditions, and ads that suggest you know something about the viewer's situation ([Meta Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/), checked Oct 2026). A headline like "Hurt in a crash?" is risky under that rule. Write about what you offer, not what the viewer has suffered.

What works instead:

- **Geography:** the city or counties you can actually serve.
- **A clear local area plus strong creative:** give Meta a defined area and a distinct message, not a pile of narrow interests.
- **Retargeting:** people who visited your site, watched your videos or engaged with your page.
- **Custom audiences:** upload a customer list only if you have permission and it complies with your professional obligations. Client confidentiality rules apply.
- **Exclusions:** existing clients and recent converts.

If an option is not in Ads Manager, you cannot use it.

## Creative

- **Headlines:** say who you help and what happens next, such as "Car accident case? Get a free case review."
- **Images and video:** real photos of your team and office usually build more trust than stock courthouse images. Get permission for any photos of real people.
- **Copy:** lead with what you do, explain it in plain language, give one call to action.
- **No exaggeration:** do not promise results or imply outcomes.

<Callout type="warning">Lawyer advertising is regulated by each state. ABA Model Rule 7.1 bars false or misleading communications about a lawyer or the lawyer's services, and most states have adopted a version of it; many add rules on disclaimers, testimonials and past results ([Inns of Court summary of Rule 7.1](https://inns.innsofcourt.org/media/197519/rule_71-exec_summary.pdf)). Have your ads reviewed against your state's rules before they run, and follow Meta's own advertising policies as well.</Callout>

## Lead Forms vs Landing Pages

Meta's instant forms keep people inside Facebook or Instagram. The "higher intent" type adds a review step so people confirm their details before submitting. A landing page lets you explain your process, show credentials and set expectations. Test both and compare cost per signed case. Our guide to [Facebook lead ads setup](/blog/facebook-lead-ads-local-business-setup-guide) covers the mechanics.

Either way:

1. Ask only for what you need: name, phone number and a short description of what happened.
2. Add a clear privacy notice and consent language.
3. Respond to every enquiry fast. Speed to first contact often decides who gets the case.

## Budgeting and Bidding

I could not find a reliable benchmark worth quoting, and costs depend heavily on market and case type. A planning approach:

- **Start with a test budget** you can sustain for several weeks.
- **Scale gradually** once results are stable.
- **Work backward from case value:** average fee per signed case x share of consultations that sign = what a consultation is worth. That is the ceiling on what you can pay for one.
- **Consider a cost cap** after you have a baseline. Meta describes cost cap as a strategy that keeps your average cost per result around an amount you set ([Meta Business Help](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/272336376749096), checked Oct 2026).

## Measuring Success

In order of importance:

1. **Cost per signed case:** the number that matters.
2. **Cost per qualified consultation:** what you pay for each real meeting.
3. **Cost per lead:** useful for optimization, easy to misread.
4. **Lead quality:** ask every new client how they found you and keep call records.

Use conversion tracking on your thank-you page and a unique tracking phone number to connect leads to outcomes. Our [analytics and reporting service](/services/analytics) can set this up.

## Do This Before You Spend

1. Write down your average fee per signed case and the share of consultations that sign. Without both, you cannot set a ceiling.
2. Call your own intake line at 9 pm tomorrow. Time the pickup.
3. Set a rule: every ad lead gets a call within 5 minutes during business hours and a text within 15 minutes after hours.
4. Have your state bar rules checked against the first 3 ads you write.
5. Add exclusions for current clients before launch.
6. Track every lead in one spreadsheet with 4 columns: date, source, consultation (yes/no), signed (yes/no).
7. Review the sheet on day 30 and day 60 and calculate cost per signed case.

## Common Mistakes

- **Targeting too broadly or too narrowly** without testing.
- **Generic stock images,** which people scroll past.
- **Sending traffic to a cluttered homepage** instead of a focused page or form.
- **No exclusions,** so existing clients see your ads.
- **Set and forget,** or the opposite: changing everything daily. Test one variable at a time.
- **Slow follow-up:** paying for leads and calling back hours later.

## FAQ

**Q: Do Facebook ads work for personal injury lawyers?**
A: They can, especially alongside Google Ads and local SEO. Facebook reaches people before they search, but intent is lower than search, so judge results by cost per signed case.

**Q: How much should I spend on Facebook ads as a personal injury lawyer?**
A: Start with a test budget you can sustain for several weeks, and set the ceiling from your average case value and conversion rates. Costs vary a great deal between small cities and large metro areas.

**Q: Can I target people who were recently in an accident?**
A: No. Meta removed many sensitive Detailed Targeting options in 2022, and its policy prohibits ads that assert or imply a person's health condition or other personal attributes. Use local targeting, retargeting and strong creative instead.

**Q: Are Facebook ads allowed under bar rules?**
A: Lawyers may advertise, but every state has its own rules on disclaimers, testimonials, claims and required firm information, and false or misleading advertising is barred under the ABA model rule most states follow. Check your state bar's rules and have your ads reviewed before launch.

**Q: Should I use Facebook ads or Google Ads first?**
A: Most firms should start with Google Ads and a strong Google Business Profile, since they capture people actively searching, then add Facebook ads for awareness and retargeting. See our [local SEO for law firms](/blog/local-seo-for-law-firms) guide.
