Wix sells ease, Squarespace sells design, and WordPress sells control. Paid plans billed yearly start near $17 a month on Wix, $19 on Squarespace and $4 on WordPress.com, on vendor pricing checked in November 2026. For a local business the deciding factor is rarely the starter price; it is who maintains the site in month thirteen.

A café, salon or groomer site has a short job list: services and prices, photos, hours, directions, reviews, and a booking or call button that works on a phone. All three platforms do that list. They differ on selling, content depth and how much technical ownership the business takes on.

## What does each platform really cost?

Wix's free plan carries Wix branding; Light at $17 a month billed yearly removes the worst limits, and Core at $29 is where payments and scheduling start, on Wix pricing checked in November 2026. Squarespace has no free plan, only a 14-day trial; Basic at $19 billed yearly includes selling with a 2% transaction fee that Core removes.

WordPress.com Personal at $4 a month billed yearly is the cheapest custom-domain start, while plugin freedom needs higher tiers; self-hosted WordPress swaps the subscription for hosting plus maintenance, as priced in [Shopify vs WooCommerce for local business](/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce-local-business) for the shop variant.

**Assumption example.** Assume an owner spends 1 hour a month on site updates valued at $40. Wix Core at $29 plus that hour costs $69 monthly in money and time. A self-hosted WordPress site on $25 hosting with 3 maintenance hours costs $145 on the same valuation. The rates are assumptions; the maintenance gap, not the plan gap, drives the result.

## Which platform fits which local business?

Wix fits owners who will edit the site themselves and want bookings, payments and forms in one dashboard. Squarespace fits businesses where the visual impression carries the price (boutique salons, studios, cafés with a strong interior) and the owner prefers constrained, polished templates over free-form dragging.

WordPress fits content-led growth: service guides, location pages and a blog intended to rank for years, where themes, plugins and portability matter. That depth is why the [Local SEO service](/services/local-seo) builds usually land there, and why [AI-powered local SEO tools](/blog/ai-powered-local-seo-tools) assumes exportable content.

Selling changes the answer. A real online shop pushes all three toward their commerce tiers, and at that point the comparison should restart against hosted commerce rather than brochure plans. ## What breaks or ages badly on each?

Wix sites can grow messy as drag-and-drop freedom accumulates inconsistent spacing and duplicate pages; a quarterly tidy keeps it credible. Squarespace's constraint protects design but frustrates unusual layouts or third-party booking widgets that need custom placement.

WordPress ages through neglect: unpatched plugins and themes are the commonest small-site security failure. Managed hosting and a monthly update routine convert that risk into a fixed chore. Any platform fails the same final test, though: hours, prices or phone numbers out of date. That is an ownership problem, not a software problem.

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest website for a local business?

WordPress.com Personal at $4 a month billed yearly is the cheapest custom-domain start on November 2026 vendor pricing, with Wix Light at $17 and Squarespace Basic at $19. Total cost should include the owner's update hours. A $4 site needing 3 monthly maintenance hours can cost more in time than a $29 hosted plan.

### Wix or Squarespace for a salon or café?

Squarespace when the room and portfolio sell the appointment and the owner wants templates that resist ugly edits. Wix when built-in scheduling, payments and forms matter more and the owner edits often. Both handle the core local page list well. Decide by editing both free tiers with real photos before paying either.

### Should a local business choose WordPress?

Choose WordPress when content is the growth plan (guides, location pages, a serious blog) or when portability and plugin choice matter, and someone will run monthly updates. Skip it when nobody will maintain it; an unpatched WordPress site is a liability. Managed hosting narrows, but does not remove, that duty.

### Can these platforms take bookings and payments?

Yes, at different tiers. Wix takes payments and scheduling from Core at $29 a month billed yearly. Squarespace sells on every plan, with Basic charging a 2% transaction fee. WordPress needs WooCommerce or a booking plugin, with plugin access gated by tier on WordPress.com. Price the tier that includes the till, not the brochure tier.
