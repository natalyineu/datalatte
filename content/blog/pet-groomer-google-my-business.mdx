To rank your pet grooming business on Google, claim and verify your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business), choose "Pet groomer" as your primary category, fill in every section with accurate details, add real photos, and keep collecting genuine reviews. Those steps are free, and they are the foundation of showing up in the local map results when someone searches "dog groomer near me".

This guide walks through the full setup for groomers: categories, services, photos, reviews, posts, Q&A and the mistakes that cause suspensions or lost visibility.

## A quick note on the name

Google rebranded Google My Business as Google Business Profile. You now manage it mostly through Google Search and Google Maps while signed in, or through the Business Profile manager. Many groomers still search for "Google My Business", which is why people use both terms. They refer to the same listing.

## Step 1: Claim and verify your profile

1. Search your business name on Google or Maps while signed into the Google account you want to own it.
2. Choose "Claim this business" or "Add your business to Google".
3. Enter your name, category and address. If you work from a mobile van or visit clients' homes, you can set a service area instead of showing your address. Mobile groomers should read Google's guidelines on service-area businesses.
4. Verify as instructed, commonly by video, phone, text or email, depending on what Google offers for your listing.

Use an email you control long term, not an employee's personal address, so you do not lose access.

## Step 2: Choose categories carefully

Your primary category has a large influence on which searches you appear for. Typical choices for groomers:

- **Primary:** Pet groomer (or Dog groomer, if that is your focus and the option is available in your country).
- **Secondary, if they truly apply:** Pet care service, Pet boarding service, Mobile dog groomer, Pet supply store (for retailers).

Do not add categories you do not offer. Check what top competitors use, and see our guide to [Google Business Profile categories](/blog/google-business-profile-categories-guide).

## Step 3: Get the details right

- **Business name:** use your real, legal or signage name. Stuffing keywords such as "Best Dog Grooming Cheap NYC" can lead to suspension.
- **Address and phone:** keep these identical to your website and other listings.
- **Hours:** include holiday hours and update them promptly. Wrong hours are a top complaint.
- **Website and booking link:** add a link to your booking page if you have one. Many groomers use Square Appointments, Booksy, Fresha, MoeGo, Gingr or similar tools that provide a booking link.
- **Description:** about 750 characters is allowed. Describe your services, location, the animals you groom, and what makes you different, written for humans.

## Step 4: List your services and prices

Add each service with a short description, such as "Full groom for small dogs", "Bath and brush", "Nail trim", "Cat grooming", "De-shedding treatment" or "Puppy first groom". Use realistic "from" prices. Pricing varies widely by size, coat and region, so show a starting price rather than a fixed figure if exact costs depend on the pet.

Also fill in attributes that apply, such as "Appointment required", "Wheelchair accessible", "Mobile service", "Online appointments" and pet-specific options if offered.

## Step 5: Add photos and short videos

Photos strongly influence whether someone contacts you. Cover:

- **Exterior and signage**, so people find you.
- **Interior** of the salon, grooming stations and reception.
- **Team photos** with names, which builds trust with anxious owners.
- **Grooming results**: finished dogs and cats in good light. Always get the owner's permission.
- **Logo and cover photo.**

Add new photos regularly, for example a few each month. Avoid stock images and heavily filtered shots, because Google and customers can tell. Use clear, well-lit photos with natural expressions.

## Step 6: Collect and answer reviews

Reviews are a major ranking and conversion factor for local businesses. To get them honestly:

- Ask happy customers at pickup, and send a follow-up text or email with your direct review link (find it in your profile under "Ask for reviews").
- Make it easy: one tap to the review page.
- Do not offer incentives in exchange for reviews or review-gate (asking only happy customers), as this breaks Google's policies.
- Reply to every review within a couple of days. Thank people by name if they used it, mention the service, and keep it short.
- For negative reviews, stay calm and factual: apologise for the experience, offer to resolve it offline, and avoid arguing in public.

Our [Google Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) covers templates and handling difficult feedback, and our [guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-for-pet-groomers) goes deeper on this niche.

## Step 7: Use posts, offers and Q&A

Posts appear in your profile and can show updates, offers, and events. Ideas for groomers:

- Seasonal tips, such as summer coat care.
- Last-minute appointment availability.
- New services, like a spa day or teeth cleaning.
- Holiday booking reminders.

See [Google Business Profile posts](/blog/google-business-profile-posts) for formats and timing. Also monitor the questions section on your profile and answer common ones, such as "Do you groom aggressive dogs?" and "Do you require vaccinations?". Make your policies clear.

## Step 8: Keep information consistent across the web

Search engines compare your name, address and phone number across directories. List your business on Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook and relevant pet directories. Our [citation building guide](/blog/local-business-citation-building-guide-2026) shows how to do this without paying for low-quality packages.

## A weekly routine

| Task | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Reply to new reviews | Within 1 to 2 days |
| Ask recent customers for reviews | After each appointment |
| Add a few new photos | Weekly or monthly |
| Publish a post | Weekly or every two weeks |
| Check hours, services and Q&A | Monthly |
| Review profile insights | Monthly |

<Callout type="coffee">Think of your profile as your shop window. Fresh photos, clear prices and friendly replies tell a nervous pet owner that their dog will be in good hands.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- Using a keyword-stuffed business name.
- Leaving hours outdated, especially around holidays.
- Having duplicate listings.
- Ignoring reviews or replying defensively.
- Hiding the address for a storefront business that should show it.
- Buying fake reviews, which can lead to removal or suspension.

## Your first 30 days

- **Days 1 to 3:** Claim, verify and fill in every section, including categories, hours and services.
- **Week 1:** Upload at least ten real photos, and add your booking link.
- **Week 2:** Start asking every happy customer for a review, and reply to existing ones.
- **Weeks 3 and 4:** Publish your first posts, answer questions on the profile, and check your insights for calls and direction requests.

After that, the weekly routine above keeps your profile fresh without taking much time.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Google My Business free for pet groomers?**
A: Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is free. You pay only if you choose to run Google Ads.

**Q: How many reviews do I need to rank?**
A: There is no magic number. Look at the groomers ranking in your area and aim to keep a steady flow of recent, genuine reviews.

**Q: Can I list my business if I groom at clients' homes?**
A: Yes, as a service-area business. You can hide your home address and specify the areas you serve.

**Q: How long before I see more calls?**
A: Improvements to completeness and photos can show effects within weeks, but ranking changes often take longer, especially in competitive areas.
