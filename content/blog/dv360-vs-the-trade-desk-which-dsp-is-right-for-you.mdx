Choose DV360 (Google Display and Video 360) if your marketing already lives in Google's ecosystem (Google Ads, GA4, YouTube) and you want tight integration. Choose The Trade Desk if you want an independent platform, flexible data options and access to a wide range of publishers and streaming inventory. If you are a small local business spending a few thousand dollars a month or less, neither is usually the right first step: start with Google Ads and Meta Ads, and revisit a DSP once you have conversion data.

Fees, minimums and access rules for both platforms change and often depend on whether you buy directly or through an agency, so confirm current terms before you commit budget.

## What is a DSP and why does it matter?

A demand-side platform (DSP) is software that lets advertisers buy digital ad inventory across many publishers through automated, real-time auctions. Instead of negotiating individual placements, you set budgets, audiences, frequency caps and bids in one dashboard.

The benefits are scale, automation, control over frequency and targeting, and unified reporting. The cost is complexity: DSPs expect you to bring clear goals, good tracking and usually a larger budget. <Callout type="tip">If you are a small local business, master [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and Meta Ads first. They build the audience data and conversion history that a DSP needs to perform.</Callout>

## DV360 vs The Trade Desk at a glance

| | DV360 | The Trade Desk |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Google | Independent, publicly traded |
| Typical user | Enterprise and mid-size advertisers, agencies | Agencies and mid-size to enterprise advertisers |
| Strongest at | YouTube, Google properties, Google-based audiences, measurement with Google tools | Independent, cross-publisher buying, streaming and CTV, open-web data partnerships |
| Formats | Display, video, audio, CTV | Display, video, audio, CTV, digital out-of-home |
| Pricing | Platform fee and media costs, set by contract | Platform fee and media costs, set by contract |
| Google integration | Native | Not native |
| Learning curve | Steep | Steep |
| Access for small advertisers | Often through an agency or partner | Often through an agency or managed service |

Both are powerful. They simply solve different problems.

## DV360: best for the Google ecosystem

DV360 connects naturally to Google Analytics, Campaign Manager and YouTube, so if your data and reporting already sit in Google, it is usually easier to plug in.

**Consider DV360 when:**
- You want to buy YouTube and Google-network inventory in one place
- You already use Google audiences and measurement
- You manage larger budgets and need structured controls and brand safety settings
- Your team or agency already has Google Marketing Platform experience

**Watch for:** access that often requires an agency or partner, and agency fees that vary. Ask any partner for a clear fee schedule before you start.

## The Trade Desk: best for independence and cross-channel buying

The Trade Desk is an independent DSP that buys across a large range of publishers and streaming platforms. Advertisers value its neutrality (it does not own media), its data partnerships and its reach into CTV and audio.

**Consider The Trade Desk when:**
- You want to buy across the open web and streaming services without being tied to one ecosystem
- You plan to use third-party or first-party data partnerships
- Your campaigns span display, video, CTV and audio
- You want flexibility in how creative and inventory are combined

**Watch for:** no native Google Ads integration, so audience and measurement setup may take more work, and a larger budget may be required, particularly through managed service. Check current minimums.

## Which one fits your business?

**Pick DV360 if:**
- You are already deep in Google Ads, GA4 and YouTube
- You want Google-based audiences and Google-based measurement
- You work with an agency that is a Google Marketing Platform partner

**Pick The Trade Desk if:**
- You want independence from Google and broad publisher access
- Streaming and CTV are central to your plan
- You value data partnership options and cross-device measurement

**Pick neither yet if:**
- Your total digital ad budget is small or you have no conversion tracking in place
- You have not yet captured search demand through Google Ads and your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile)

## Common mistakes to avoid on either platform

**1. Treating a DSP as set-and-forget.** Machine learning still needs human review. Check cost per result, frequency and placement performance regularly in the first few weeks and pause what is not working.

**2. Targeting too broadly.** Broad interest categories pull in people who will never visit your business. For local goals, begin with a tight geographic area and layer relevant audience signals.

**3. Ignoring frequency caps.** Without a cap, the same person can see your ad many times. Set a sensible weekly limit per user and lower it for retargeting.

**4. Judging only by last click.** Display and video often assist conversions without getting the final click. Look at view-through and assisted conversions where available, but treat them with caution and compare with a holdout where you can.

**5. Running a single creative.** Test multiple ad variations and formats, and retire the weakest as you learn.

## Metrics that matter for local businesses

- **Cost per store visit, call or booking.** The most useful measure, where location or conversion data is available.
- **Assisted conversions.** Shows whether the ads contributed even without a click.
- **New customer share.** Exclude existing customers from prospecting so you can see genuinely new demand.
- **Frequency.** Watch for overexposure.

Treat impressions, reach and click-through rate as supporting metrics, not goals. Our guide to [tracking which marketing is bringing customers](/blog/how-to-track-which-marketing-is-bringing-customers) shows how to connect ad activity to real bookings.

## A simple 30-day approach

- **Week 1:** Launch with a tight geographic area, a few creatives and no heavy optimisation, so the system can learn.
- **Week 2:** Pause the weakest placements and shift budget toward what produces actions.
- **Week 3:** Test one new creative per audience and review frequency.
- **Week 4:** Review assisted conversions, adjust caps and plan the next month using what you learned.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between DV360 and The Trade Desk?**
DV360 is Google's DSP, tightly integrated with Google tools and YouTube. The Trade Desk is an independent DSP known for open-web reach, streaming inventory and data partnerships. Both automate programmatic buying but differ in ownership, integration and access.

**Which is cheaper, DV360 or The Trade Desk?**
It depends on your contract, agency fees, media mix and data costs. Neither has one fixed price, so request a written fee breakdown and compare total cost per result rather than platform fee alone.

**Can a small local business use DV360 or The Trade Desk?**
Usually only through an agency or partner, and minimum budgets can be a barrier. Most small local businesses get better returns by starting with Google Ads, Meta Ads and local SEO.

**Is The Trade Desk better than DV360?**
Neither is better in every case. DV360 tends to suit Google-centred advertisers. The Trade Desk tends to suit advertisers who want independence and broader streaming and data options.

**Do I need a DSP for CTV ads?**
Not necessarily. Several self-serve and managed options let local businesses run connected TV campaigns without a full DSP seat. See our [connected TV guide](/blog/connected-tv-ads-for-local-business).
