Choose DV360 (Google Display and Video 360) if your marketing already lives in Google's ecosystem (Google Ads, GA4, YouTube) and you want tight integration. Choose The Trade Desk if you want an independent platform, flexible data options and access to a wide range of publishers and streaming inventory. If you are a small local business on a modest budget, neither is usually the right first step: start with Google Ads and Meta Ads, and revisit a DSP once you have conversion data.

Here is the pitch you will hear. A rep calls, says "reach every homeowner in your county on streaming TV, YouTube and thousands of websites," and sends a deck. It sounds like Super Bowl reach for a plumber's budget. Then you ask for a written fee schedule and a minimum, and the answer is "it depends". Neither platform publishes one standard price, and fees, minimums and access rules depend on how you buy. Get terms in writing before you commit budget.

## What a DSP Is

A demand-side platform (DSP) is software that lets advertisers buy digital ad inventory across many publishers through automated, real-time auctions. You set budgets, audiences, frequency caps and bids in one dashboard instead of negotiating placements. The gain is scale, automation and unified reporting. The cost is complexity: a DSP expects clear goals, good tracking and usually a larger budget. New to this? Start with our [DSP vs SSP explainer](/blog/dsp-vs-ssp-explained-local-business) and [real-time bidding explainer](/blog/real-time-bidding-explained-for-non-technical-business-owners).

<Callout type="tip">If you are a small local business, master [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and Meta Ads first. They build the audience data and conversion history that a DSP needs to perform.</Callout>

## The Math: Why Small Budgets Start With Search

**Example with made-up inputs. These are not market benchmarks and not a quote from either platform. Swap in your own numbers.** Assume a local service business with a $2,000 monthly budget, a $60 average job ticket and about 60,000 reachable adults in its service area. Assume display or video buys cost $8 per 1,000 impressions (CPM), 0.1% of impressions get clicked and 3% of those clicks book. Assume search clicks cost $3 and 6% of them book, because the searcher already wants the service.

| Step | Programmatic display (DSP) | Google Search |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | $2,000 | $2,000 |
| Buying unit | $2,000 / $8 x 1,000 = 250,000 impressions | $2,000 / $3 = 667 clicks |
| Clicks | 250,000 x 0.1% = 250 | 667 |
| Bookings | 250 x 3% = 7.5 | 667 x 6% = 40 |
| Revenue at $60 | $450 | $2,400 |
| Cost per booking | about $267 | $50 |

The display side also shows each of the 60,000 people about 4 impressions a month (250,000 / 60,000 = 4.2), which is fine. Display does influence people who never click, and view-through or assisted conversions would add to its column. But you only see those with solid tracking, and a DSP usually adds a platform fee on top of media. In this example the DSP would need to be about 5 times better at converting than assumed to match search. That is the honest reason I would start with search, and revisit a DSP when search is saturated and you have conversion data. Run your own numbers before you take the meeting.

## DV360 vs The Trade Desk at a Glance

| | DV360 | The Trade Desk |

| Owner | Google | Independent, listed on Nasdaq (TTD) |
| Typical user | Enterprise and mid-size advertisers, agencies | Agencies and mid-size to enterprise advertisers |
| Strongest at | YouTube, Google properties, Google-based audiences, measurement with Google tools | Independent, cross-publisher buying, streaming and CTV, open-web data partnerships |
| Formats | Display, video, audio, CTV | Display, video, audio, CTV, digital out-of-home |

Both are powerful. They solve different problems.

## DV360: Best Inside Google

DV360 is part of Google Marketing Platform and lets advertisers plan and buy display, video, audio and connected TV inventory ([Netpeak overview](https://netpeak.net/blog/what-is-dv360-and-what-are-its-benefits), checked Oct 2026). It connects to Google Analytics, Campaign Manager and YouTube, so if your data and reporting already sit in Google, it plugs in more easily.

**Consider DV360 when:**
- You want YouTube and Google-network inventory in one place
- You already use Google audiences and measurement
- You manage larger budgets and need structured controls and brand safety settings
- Your agency already has Google Marketing Platform experience

**Watch for:** access requirements and fees that vary by contract and partner. Ask for a clear fee schedule first.

## The Trade Desk: Best for Independence

The Trade Desk describes itself as the largest independent DSP; Wikipedia notes it is listed on Nasdaq and supports CTV, display, audio and digital out-of-home buying ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trade_Desk), checked Oct 2026). It buys across a large range of publishers and streaming platforms. Advertisers value its neutrality (it does not own media), its data partnerships and its reach into CTV and audio.

**Consider The Trade Desk when:**
- You want the open web and streaming without being tied to one ecosystem
- You plan to use third-party or first-party data partnerships
- Campaigns span display, video, CTV and audio
- You want flexibility in combining creative and inventory

**Watch for:** no native Google Ads integration, so audience and measurement setup may take more work, and minimum budgets that depend on how you buy. Check current minimums.

## Which Fits?

**Pick DV360 if:** you are already deep in Google Ads, GA4 and YouTube, want Google-based audiences and measurement, or work with a Google Marketing Platform partner.

**Pick The Trade Desk if:** you want independence from Google and broad publisher access, streaming and CTV are central, or you value data partnerships and cross-device measurement.

**Pick neither yet if:** your total digital ad budget is small, you have no conversion tracking, or you have not yet captured search demand through Google Ads and your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile).

## Mistakes on Either Platform

**1. Set and forget.** Machine learning still needs human review. In the first few weeks, check cost per result, frequency and placement performance, and pause what is not working.

**2. Targeting too broadly.** Broad interests pull in people who will never visit. For local goals, begin with a tight geographic area and add audience signals.

**3. No frequency caps.** Without a cap one person can see your ad many times. Set a weekly limit per user and lower it for retargeting.

**4. Judging only by last click.** Display and video often assist conversions without the final click. Look at view-through and assisted conversions where available, treat them with caution and compare with a holdout where you can.

**5. One creative.** Test several variations and retire the weakest.

## Metrics That Matter Locally

- **Cost per store visit, call or booking,** where location or conversion data is available.
- **Assisted conversions:** did the ads contribute without a click?
- **New customer share:** exclude existing customers from prospecting.
- **Frequency:** watch for overexposure.

Treat impressions, reach and click-through rate as supporting metrics. Our guide to [tracking which marketing is bringing customers](/blog/how-to-track-which-marketing-is-bringing-customers) shows how to connect ads to real bookings.

## Before You Take the Sales Call

1. Write down your monthly ad budget and your average job value.
2. Ask in writing: platform fee (percent or flat), minimum monthly spend, minimum contract length and what happens to unspent budget.
3. Ask who owns the account and the data if you leave.
4. Confirm conversion tracking works. If you have fewer than a few weeks of clean data, wait.
5. Fill in the math table with your own numbers and get the DSP's quote for the display column.

## A Simple 30-Day Plan

- **Week 1:** Tight geographic area, a few creatives, no heavy optimization, so the system can learn.
- **Week 2:** Pause the weakest placements and shift budget toward actions.
- **Week 3:** Test one new creative per audience and review frequency.
- **Week 4:** Review assisted conversions, adjust caps and plan next month.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the difference between DV360 and The Trade Desk?**
A: DV360 is Google's DSP, tightly integrated with Google tools and YouTube. The Trade Desk is an independent DSP known for open-web reach, streaming inventory and data partnerships. Both automate programmatic buying but differ in ownership, integration and access.

**Q: Which is cheaper, DV360 or The Trade Desk?**
A: It depends on your contract, agency fees, media mix and data costs. Neither has one published price, so request a written fee breakdown and compare total cost per result, not platform fee alone.

**Q: Can a small local business use DV360 or The Trade Desk?**
A: Access and minimum budgets depend on how you buy, so ask each platform or partner for current terms. Most small local businesses get better returns from Google Ads, Meta Ads and local SEO first.

**Q: Is The Trade Desk better than DV360?**
A: Neither wins every time. DV360 tends to suit Google-centered advertisers. The Trade Desk tends to suit advertisers who want independence and broader streaming and data options.

**Q: Do I need a DSP for CTV ads?**
A: Not necessarily. Several self-serve and managed options let local businesses run connected TV campaigns without a DSP seat. See our [connected TV guide](/blog/connected-tv-ads-for-local-business).
