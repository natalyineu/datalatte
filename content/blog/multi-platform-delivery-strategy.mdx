To run Glovo, Wolt and your own ordering on one kitchen, send every order into a single system, keep one master menu, and compare each channel on net profit per order, not order count. Wolt says a POS or middleware integration removes its tablet, syncs menu, pricing and hours, and usually takes 1 to 2 weeks to activate. That is the main fix for tablet clutter.

This guide covers the setup order, the fee lines to compare and a worked example. Older versions of this page quoted market-size and loss-per-order statistics that I could not source, so they are gone. Commission rates are not published by Wolt, so I give none.

## Should You List on More Than One Delivery App?

Often yes, if you can handle the extra work. A second app can reach customers who do not open the first, and the choice depends on which app your local customers use. Our [Glovo vs Wolt comparison](/blog/glovo-vs-wolt-which-is-better) covers how to pick and what to ask for in writing.

The cost is complexity: duplicate tablets, menus that drift apart and missed orders. Check any contract for exclusivity clauses before you sign a second one. If one platform grants a lower rate for exclusive listing, work out whether the reach you give up is worth it.

## How Do You Stop Juggling Tablets?

Use a POS integration or a middleware service. Wolt's merchant help says that once integrated you do not need Wolt's tablet, orders arrive directly in your POS, and menu updates, pricing and store hours sync to Wolt. It lists fewer order errors, saved staff time and all sales reporting in one system as benefits.

The steps are: have an active Wolt contract, a compatible POS or middleware and a menu built in your POS; ask your provider to request the integration; approve your menu, hours and pricing; then wait for activation, usually 1 to 2 weeks. Check Wolt's partner catalog for your country.

Middleware vendors such as Deliverect and Restimo say they connect several delivery apps to one dashboard or POS. I read these only as search summaries, and I have not verified Glovo support or fees for any of them. Confirm before paying.

## What Costs Should You Compare Across Apps?

Wolt's fees page lists a standard commission for Wolt couriers, a slightly higher rate or fixed fee for Wolt+ orders, a lower takeaway rate, a platform fee on top of commission in some countries, and penalties for delays or missing items. It also says devices such as tablets can be repaid gradually from payouts, and that payouts usually come every 5 days, twice a month or monthly.

Build one sheet with the same lines for every channel: commission and what it applies to, platform fee, processing fee, promotion costs, penalties, device charges, payout frequency and contract length. Reading it side by side is the whole point of a multi-platform strategy.

## Worked Example: Net Profit per Order by Channel

All numbers are **assumptions**, not platform rates. Basket EUR 28, food and packaging cost 32% (EUR 8.96).

| Channel | Assumed commission | Commission per order | Left after food cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| App A | 25% | EUR 7.00 | EUR 12.04 |
| App B | 30% | EUR 8.40 | EUR 10.64 |
| Own ordering page | 0% (payment fee ignored) | EUR 0.00 | EUR 19.04 |

Before labor, packaging extras and courier costs, App B leaves EUR 1.40 less per order than App A, and the own page leaves EUR 7.00 more than App A. At 100 orders a month, moving 20 of App A's orders to your own page is worth EUR 140 on this basis. Replace every input with your invoice data. The own page also costs time and payment fees that the example ignores.

## How Do You Keep Menus and Prices Consistent?

Keep one master menu in the POS and push it to every app. If an app has a higher fee, decide in advance whether you change prices there, and check your agreement and local rules first. Build a shorter delivery menu with items that travel well; see [menu optimisation for delivery platforms](/blog/restaurant-menu-optimisation-delivery-platforms).

## How Do You Move Orders to Your Own Channel?

Collect contact details with consent, put a card with a direct-order link or QR code in the bag, and keep your Google Business Profile current. Do not break your app agreements, since some limit direct promotion; read the clauses. Budget guidance is in [how to split a restaurant delivery marketing budget](/blog/restaurant-delivery-marketing-budget-how-to-split), and the wider plan is in delivery app marketing strategy for restaurants.

## What Should You Check Every Week?

Review per channel: orders, net after commission, penalties, refunds, rating and preparation time. Use the delivery platform fees guide to read invoices. If a channel has a net profit per order below your target for two months, renegotiate or drop it.

## FAQ

### Is it worth being on several delivery platforms?
It can be, if each channel earns a profit after commission and you can run them without errors. Compare net profit per order, not revenue.

### How do I manage all delivery orders in one place?
Use a POS integration or a middleware service. Wolt says integration removes its tablet and syncs menu, pricing and hours. Check your POS provider's support for each app.

### What are the usual delivery commissions?
Wolt publishes no percentages; they depend on your market and agreement. Ask each platform for a written offer.

### Can I have a different price on each app?
Whether that is allowed depends on your agreement and local rules. I did not find a rule I can cite, so check your contract.
