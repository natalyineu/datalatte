The cheapest salon marketing ideas are free: complete your Google Business Profile, ask every client for a review and make rebooking part of checkout. Google says local ranking rests on relevance, distance and prominence, and BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found 97% read reviews for local businesses. Paid ads come later. LocaliQ's 2026 search benchmark for Beauty and Personal Care is $4.62 per click, a 10.35% conversion rate and $39.25 per lead.

This guide lists twelve ideas in order of cost, with what each needs and what the sources do and do not support.

## Which Salon Marketing Ideas Cost Nothing?

Start here, because every paid channel sends people to these pages.

1. **Complete your Google Business Profile.** Google says an accurate, complete profile is more relevant to searches, and that you cannot pay for better local ranking. Check categories, hours, services, photos and the booking link. Follow our [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).
2. **Ask every client for a review.** BrightLocal found 41% always read reviews, 31% only use businesses rated 4.5 or more, and 74% want reviews from the last three months. Ask in person and send the link by text. Do not offer a reward for reviews: the FTC says such rewards need disclosure and cannot depend on a positive review.
3. **Reply to reviews.** Google says replying shows customers you value feedback.
4. **Rebook at the chair.** Offer the next appointment before the client leaves.
5. **Post real work.** Before and after photos need the client's permission.

For the local search side, read our [hair salon local SEO guide](/blog/hair-salon-local-seo-guide).

## Which Ideas Need a Little Money or Time?

6. **Referral rewards that are not discounts.** A product sample or add-on costs you less than a price cut. See [salon referral programs without discounts](/blog/salon-referral-program-without-discounts).
7. **A better website and booking page.** Google's good page speed target is an LCP of 2.5 seconds or less. See [website design for hair salons](/blog/website-design-for-hair-salons).
8. **A direct booking link.** Fresha's pricing page lists a one-time 20% commission (minimum $6) on new clients found through its marketplace, and no fee for clients who book through your own link, Facebook or Instagram. Use your own link in your bio and on your site.
9. **Social media.** Photos and short videos of finished work. No source opened gives a booking rate per post, so judge it by bookings that mention it. See [hair salon social media marketing](/blog/hair-salon-social-media-marketing).
10. **Local partnerships.** Wedding venues, boutiques or gyms that swap flyers or referrals. This is practice, not a measured result.

## When Should a Salon Pay for Ads?

11. **Google search ads.** Search catches people who want an appointment now. At LocaliQ's category averages, $600 a month buys about 130 clicks. Our guide to [hair salon ad spend](/blog/how-much-should-a-hair-salon-spend-on-google-ads) shows how to size it.
12. **Instagram or Facebook ads.** Better for showing finished work to people near you. See [hair salon Instagram ads](/blog/hair-salon-instagram-ads). We did not open a current Meta benchmark, so no cost figure is given.

Pay for ads only after the free steps are done, otherwise the click arrives at a thin profile or a weak booking page.

## The Math: What Does $600 in Search Ads Return?

All inputs are **assumptions** built on LocaliQ's Beauty and Personal Care averages.

| Input | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly budget | $600 | Assumption |
| Cost per click | $4.62 | LocaliQ 2026 |
| Clicks | about 130 | Budget divided by CPC |
| Conversion rate | 10.35% | LocaliQ 2026 |
| Conversions (calls, forms) | about 13 | Clicks times rate |
| Share that book | 50% | Assumption |
| New bookings | about 6.5 | Conversions times share |
| Cost per booking | about $92 | Budget divided by bookings |
| First-visit ticket | $85 | Assumption |

At these inputs the first visit does not cover the ad cost, so the plan depends on repeat visits. If an average client returns four times a year (an assumption), six new clients are worth about $2,000 a year. Check your own rebooking rate before you commit.

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest way to market a salon?

Complete your Google Business Profile, ask for reviews, reply to them and rebook at the chair. All are free.

### How much should a salon spend on marketing?

No source opened gives a standard share. Start with the free steps, then add a small ad budget and track cost per booking.

### Do salons need Instagram?

No source proves that it is required. Use it to show finished work and send people to your booking link.

### How many reviews does a salon need?

BrightLocal's 2026 survey reports that 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 or higher. No source opened gives a minimum number.

### Are discounts a good way to get new clients?

They give away revenue on every visit. Rewards that cost you less than a price cut may protect margin better.
