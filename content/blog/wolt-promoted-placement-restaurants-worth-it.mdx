Wolt promoted placement is worth it for a restaurant only when the margin on each promoted order covers what you pay for that order. Wolt reports that over 80% of advertisers run ongoing campaigns, but those are platform figures, not proof that your listing will convert. Per-click and per-order rates vary by market and appear in your Wolt Ads dashboard, so treat any published average with caution. Run the break-even test below before you commit a monthly budget.

## What does Wolt promoted placement do for a restaurant?

Wolt Ads is the paid layer on top of a merchant's regular Wolt listing. Wolt's own Ads page says the product is trusted by over 180,000 brands and merchants worldwide. The page also claims that over 60% of customers click on top positions in the app, which is why visibility matters, though that is still a vendor claim.

A partner testimonial on Wolt's Luxembourg page says over 30% of orders during its campaign came from first-time users, and that it paid only when customers ordered. One testimonial is an anecdote, not a benchmark, so use it as a question to ask in your own test.

## How do Wolt costs compare with other local ad channels?

Adwave, a restaurant advertising vendor, estimates monthly ranges for the main paid channels. These are estimates as of 2026, not quotes, and the table shows why Wolt needs its own number.

| Channel | Monthly range cited | Source type |
|---|---|---|
| Google Ads for restaurants | $300 to $1,500 | Adwave (secondary) |
| Meta ads, single-location restaurant | $500 to $2,000 | Adwave (secondary) |
| Wolt Ads | Set in your dashboard; no public range | Wolt (primary) |

Adwave, citing WordStream, puts the average Google Ads cost-per-click for restaurants at $1.84. Adwave also says established restaurants spend 3% to 6% of revenue on advertising, and new locations 8% to 12%. Those are industry benchmarks, so use them as a ceiling for total ad spend, not a target.

## What does the break-even math look like?

Start with the number that matters: the most you can pay per order before you lose money on it. Every input below is an assumption you should replace with your own figures.

Inputs (all assumption):
- Monthly revenue: $10,000
- Average order value on the promoted item: $25
- Gross margin on that order: 30%, so $7.50 profit per order
- Monthly Wolt budget: $300
- Wolt cost per order: $5

Calculation:
- Orders bought: $300 ÷ $5 = 60 orders
- Gross profit from those orders: 60 × $7.50 = $450
- Net result: $450 − $300 = $150 profit
- Break-even orders: $300 ÷ $7.50 = 40 orders
- Break-even cost per order: $7.50

Sensitivity check: if Wolt's real cost is $8 per order, the $300 buys 37.5 orders, which gross $281.25, a loss of about $18.75. A small change in cost per order flips the result, which is why the dashboard figure matters more than any average.

Adwave also gives a hypothetical in which a customer worth $200 over time makes $20 to $40 of acquisition spend sensible. That is an illustration, but it shows that lifetime value, not a single order, sets how much you can afford.

## Which restaurants should test Wolt first?

Promoted placement suits items with a healthy margin and a short purchase decision, such as coffee, breakfast, or a single high-margin entrée. A large meal with a thin margin leaves little room for the per-order cost in the break-even calculation above.

Repeat business matters too. A page from Adwave states that 65% of restaurant revenue comes from repeat customers, but it names no source, so treat that figure as unverified. If most of your regulars already order through Wolt, a promoted order may just be an existing customer choosing a different placement.

## How do you launch a test without wasting budget?

1. Confirm your city is live on Wolt and open the Wolt Ads dashboard to read your actual rates.
2. Choose one promoted item whose margin per order comfortably exceeds your break-even cost per order.
3. Write two or three offer headlines. [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) can draft variants, but check every price and claim yourself.
4. Set a fixed budget and a stop rule, for example pausing if cost per order stays above break-even for two weeks (suggested, not a platform rule).
5. Track orders from first-time and returning customers separately.
6. Compare results with your break-even number before deciding whether to extend, change the offer, or stop.

Keep the test short and the stop rule written down before the first dollar is spent.

## How do you tell new orders from existing customers?

Wolt's dashboard shows orders, but it cannot tell you whether a buyer was already a regular. Add a simple signal, such as a code printed on the menu link or a note in the order flow, so you can separate new buyers from existing ones.

Then compare your split with the 30% first-time figure from the Luxembourg testimonial. If most promoted orders come from people who already order from you, the ad is paying for demand you already had. That does not mean the spend is wasted, but it changes the return you should expect.

Consider bringing in outside help if the numbers are unclear after one test. [Is a local business marketing consultant worth it?](/blog/local-business-marketing-consultant-worth-it) covers when that cost makes sense.

## Is promoted placement a strategy or a test?

Promoted placement is a test. It amplifies what your listing already shows, so weak photos or a poor offer will cost you money at the same rate as a strong one. Before you increase spend, check your listing, your offer, and your break-even number.

## FAQ

### Is Wolt promoted placement worth it for a new restaurant?

No published figure here shows how new listings perform on Wolt. Start with a small test measured against your break-even cost per order, and extend the budget only if the numbers hold over the test window.

### How much should a restaurant budget for ads?

Adwave cites industry benchmarks of 3% to 6% of revenue for established restaurants and 8% to 12% for new locations. At an assumed $10,000 in monthly revenue, that is $300 to $600 of total ad spend across all channels, with Wolt as one part of it.

### Should I compare Wolt with Google Ads?

Compare cost per order, not headline rates. Adwave's $1.84 average Google Ads cost-per-click for restaurants (citing WordStream) is a different unit from Wolt's per-order pricing, so convert both into cost per completed order using your own dashboards before deciding.

### Is the 30% first-time customer figure reliable?

It is a single partner testimonial on Wolt's Luxembourg page, not an independent benchmark. Use it as a question to test in your own campaign rather than as a forecast for your restaurant.
