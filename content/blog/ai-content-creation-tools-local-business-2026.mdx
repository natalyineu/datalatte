Most small businesses are not using AI yet. In the US Census Bureau's survey, between 17% and 20% of businesses reported using AI between December 2025 and May 2026, and fewer than 20% of firms with four or fewer employees did. That is an opening. If you spend three hours a week writing posts and a free tool gets that to 90 minutes, you get 6.5 hours a month back. This guide shows which kind of tool to start with, what to pay for (and when), and three rules that stop AI content from hurting you.

It is written for owners of local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Prices are in US dollars, and we flag where we could only find them second-hand.

## Start With the Job, Not the Tool

Tools fall into four jobs. Pick one job first, because paying for four overlapping subscriptions is the usual mistake.

| Job | Tool types | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Write captions, posts, emails, replies | General AI assistant | ChatGPT, Writesonic |
| Make graphics and resize for each platform | Design tool with AI features | Canva |
| Turn text into a short video | Text-to-video tool | Lumen5 |
| Plan the month | Assistant plus a spreadsheet | ChatGPT plus your calendar |

## The Tools, Honestly

**Prices below could not be confirmed on the vendors' own pages, which blocked our fetch.** The figures come from third-party price trackers and reviews, which often disagree with each other. Check each vendor's current pricing page before you pay.

**ChatGPT (general assistant).** Trackers report a free tier with limits, a cheaper "Go" tier at about $8, Plus at about $20 a month, and Pro tiers at $100 and $200. For a local business, the free tier or Plus is plenty. It drafts captions, rewrites a Google Business Profile description, suggests a monthly plan and answers "what should I post this week?" It does not schedule or publish. See our guide, [ChatGPT for small business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-for-small-business-marketing).

**Canva (design).** Canva has a permanent free plan, and trackers disagree on the Pro price (figures from about $15 to $18 a month appear). Free plan AI allowances are also reported inconsistently, so check the usage meter in your own account. Many owners already have Canva, which makes it the lowest-friction add-on: templates, resizing and quick image edits.

**Lumen5 (video).** Reviews agree there is a free tier with a Lumen5 watermark, with the entry paid plan reported at about $29 a month in several sources and different numbers in others. It turns a text post or article into a short stock-footage video. Useful for a "tip of the week" reel; it will not show your actual shop, so mix it with real clips.

**Writesonic (writing).** Reported to have a free plan with limits and paid plans from roughly $12 to $20 a month, rising steeply. If you are already using a general assistant, this adds little for a one-location business.

**Niche and bundled tools.** New post generators appear constantly. Judge them by the same test: does the free trial produce something you would actually publish after a two-minute edit?

## Three Rules That Protect You

### 1. Review everything before it goes out

Google's [guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) says generative models "don't retrieve facts, but predict a likely sequence of words", so outputs can contain hallucinations, and publishers should fact-check and review AI-generated content before publishing, including titles and meta descriptions. For you that means opening hours, prices, claims about services and anything involving names. AI invents plausible details.

### 2. Do not mass-produce pages

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) list "using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users" as scaled content abuse. A local business that auto-generates 50 near-identical "plumber in [suburb]" pages is the example to avoid. Write fewer, better pages with real details. Our [AI for local SEO guide](/blog/ai-for-local-seo-how-to-use-it) shows a weekly routine that stays on the right side of this.

### 3. Disclose realistic AI video and audio on Meta, and never fake reviews

Meta's [help page on AI content](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/) says it may require a label on Facebook, Instagram and Threads for photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, and that this requirement does not apply to images (though Meta may still label images if its systems detect AI). It also warns of possible penalties for content shared without a required label. An AI voiceover on a reel is the example it gives.

And on reviews: the FTC's [final rule on fake reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) bans creating or selling fake consumer reviews, including AI-generated reviews attributed to people who do not exist, and lets the FTC seek civil penalties against knowing violators. Never use AI to write your own testimonials. That is US law; if you operate in the UK, Australia or Canada, check your regulator's rules on fake reviews as well.

## A Weekly Routine (60 to 90 Minutes)

- **Monday, 10 minutes:** write down three real things from last week: a customer comment, a new product or service, a behind-the-scenes moment, a local event.
- **Tuesday, 25 minutes:** paste those into your assistant with your tone, town and offer. Ask for three versions for Instagram, Facebook and a Business Profile update.
- **Wednesday, 25 minutes:** edit each one. Add one detail only you know, swap in your own photo, and schedule it. If you need help scheduling, see [how to automate Instagram posting](/blog/how-to-automate-instagram-posting-local-business).
- **Friday, 10 minutes:** look at which post earned replies, saves or calls. Tell the assistant to write more of that kind.

For the wider picture on content that connects locally, see [content creation for local businesses](/blog/local-business-social-media-content-creation) and our guide to [AI social media management](/blog/ai-social-media-management-local-business).

<Callout type="tip" title="The One-Detail Rule">
Never publish a draft that contains nothing only you could have written. One real detail per post (a regular's usual order, the dog that wandered in, tomorrow's weather and what you are baking) is what separates your content from a generic AI feed.
</Callout>

## The Math

Is a paid tool worth it? Every figure below is an **assumption** for illustration, except the tool price, which is the approximate figure trackers report for ChatGPT Plus and is unconfirmed. Replace the hours and your hourly value with your own.

| | Manual | Free AI tool | Paid AI tool |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hours per week on content (assumption) | 3.0 | 1.5 | 1.25 |
| Hours per month (x 4.33) | 13.0 | 6.5 | 5.4 |
| Value of your time per hour (assumption) | $30 | $30 | $30 |
| Time cost per month | $390 | $195 | $162 |
| Tool cost per month (approximate, unconfirmed) | $0 | $0 | $20 |
| Total cost per month | $390 | $195 | $182 |
| Saved versus manual | n/a | $195 | $208 |

How to read it:

- **The free tool does most of the work.** Going from manual to free saves $195 a month in this example. Going from free to paid adds only $13.
- **Break-even for a subscription** is tool price divided by the value of your hour: $20 / $30 = 0.67 hours, about 40 minutes a month. If a tool does not save you 40 minutes, cancel it.
- **The hours are guesses.** Time your current process for two weeks first. If it already takes 60 minutes, AI will not save 90.
- **None of this counts results.** Saved time is only half the case. Check whether replies, calls and bookings hold up, and if they drop, use the saved time to improve the posts.

## Do This Today

1. Time your current content process for one week, and write down hours and the number of posts published.
2. Choose one job from the table (usually writing) and open the free tier of one assistant. Do not buy anything yet.
3. Write down three real things from your week and ask for drafts in your voice. Edit each one and add a detail only you know.
4. Check every price, hour and claim in the drafts against your website and Google Business Profile before posting.
5. After four weeks, compare hours saved with the break-even above and decide whether any subscription is worth keeping.

## FAQ

**Q: Won't AI make my content sound like everyone else's?**
A: It can, if you publish the first draft. Feed it specific local details and edit the result so it sounds like you. The one-detail rule above is the simplest fix.

**Q: Will Google penalise AI-written content?**
A: Google's guidance targets content produced at scale without adding value, regardless of how it is made, and tells publishers to review AI output for accuracy. A handful of reviewed, genuinely useful posts is a different thing from hundreds of generic pages.

**Q: Which tool should I start with on a $0 budget?**
A: The free tier of a general assistant for writing, plus Canva's free plan for graphics. Revisit after a month with your time log.

**Q: Do I have to label AI content on Instagram or Facebook?**
A: Meta says it may require a label for photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio created or altered with AI, and does not apply that requirement to images. Rules change, so check Meta's page.

**Q: Can I use AI to write customer reviews or testimonials?**
A: No. The FTC's rule bans fake reviews, including AI-generated reviews attributed to people who do not exist.
