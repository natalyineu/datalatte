If you run a local service business — a cleaning company, a personal trainer, a pet groomer, a photographer — you've almost certainly looked at Bark and Thumbtack. Both promise to send you clients. Both charge you money before you even speak to a prospect. And both have reviews ranging from "best thing I ever did" to "complete scam."

So which one is actually worth it in 2026?

I've worked with dozens of local service businesses across the US and UK. Here's what the numbers say.

---

## How Each Platform Works

**Thumbtack** operates on a pay-per-lead model. A customer posts a job request, Thumbtack shows them up to 5 matching pros, and you pay when someone views your profile or contacts you. Leads typically run **$5–$150** depending on the job type and your market.

**Bark** sells credits. You buy a pack of credits, browse available leads in your category, and spend credits to contact each lead. One lead costs 1–8 credits. Credit packs run roughly **$1 per credit**, so a typical lead costs $3–$20. Here's the catch: you pay whether the client responds or not.

## The Real Cost: What You Actually Pay Per Booked Client

The headline lead cost matters less than the cost per booked client. Let me break down what local businesses actually experience:

**Thumbtack:**
- 10 leads viewed → 3–4 respond → 1–2 book
- Average lead cost: $25
- Average cost per booked client: **$125–$250**

**Bark:**
- 10 credits spent on leads → 2–4 respond → 0.5–1.5 book
- Average lead cost: $8
- Average cost per booked client: **$50–$160**

On paper, Bark looks cheaper. But Bark leads are lower intent — the same person may have been contacted by 10 other businesses. Thumbtack leads tend to be warmer because the client actively reached out to *you*.

<Callout type="tip">
The best way to evaluate either platform: track your cost per *booked and paid* client, not just cost per lead. One platform might send 3x the leads but convert at 1/4 the rate.
</Callout>

## Category Matters More Than Platform

The platform that works depends almost entirely on what you offer and where you are.

**Thumbtack performs better for:**
- Home services (cleaning, handyman, HVAC)
- Events (photographers, caterers, DJs)
- High-ticket services ($500+ jobs) in competitive US cities

**Bark performs better for:**
- UK-based businesses (Bark originated in the UK and has stronger UK inventory)
- Creative services (tutors, coaches, designers)
- Newer businesses building a review base

**Neither works well for:**
- Restaurants and cafés (use Google Business Profile instead)
- Retail shops (use Google/Meta Ads)
- Any service where clients don't actively search for a pro online

## The Quality Problem Both Platforms Have

The biggest complaint about both Bark and Thumbtack is the same: **tire-kicker leads**.

On Thumbtack, clients post jobs to compare prices with no intention of booking soon. On Bark, the same lead gets contacted by 5–10 businesses and often goes silent.

What separates businesses that succeed on these platforms from those who don't:

1. **Speed** — Respond within 5 minutes of receiving a lead. First-response win rate is dramatically higher.
2. **A strong profile** — 10+ reviews and a professional photo converts at 2–3x the rate of bare profiles.
3. **A script** — Don't wing your first message. A tested opening that addresses the client's specific job converts better.
4. **Selective bidding** — On Bark especially, don't chase every lead. Filter by budget range and job size.

## Platform-Specific Tips

### Thumbtack
- Turn on **Instant Match** — it auto-responds immediately when someone posts a relevant job. Speed is everything.
- Set a weekly budget cap. Without one, costs can spiral fast.
- Collect reviews actively. Pros with 20+ reviews see 4x higher response rates.

### Bark
- Buy credits in bulk for better pricing (larger packs cost less per credit).
- Filter leads by "quick hire" — these clients have a real budget and timeline.
- If a lead hasn't responded in 48 hours, move on. Bark's refund policy for unresponsive leads has improved but varies.

## The Real Alternative Neither Platform Tells You About

Both Bark and Thumbtack are renting you their audience. You pay every time you want a client, with no compounding benefit.

Compare that to **Google Business Profile** + a handful of **5-star reviews**:
- A plumber with a fully optimized GBP in Austin gets 60–80 calls/month with zero ongoing ad spend
- A hair salon in London with 100 reviews ranks above Bark and Thumbtack listings in local search

The platforms make sense as a bridge while you build your own presence. Once you have 25+ Google reviews and a complete profile, many local service businesses find they can reduce or eliminate Bark/Thumbtack spend entirely.

<Callout type="warning">
Don't let Bark or Thumbtack become your only source of leads. They own the client relationship — if your account gets suspended or pricing changes, your pipeline disappears overnight.
</Callout>

## Verdict: Which One Should You Use?

| | Thumbtack | Bark |
|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | Home services, US cities | Creative services, UK market |
| **Lead intent** | Higher | Lower |
| **Cost predictability** | Better | Worse (credits expire) |
| **UK presence** | Weak | Strong |
| **Review building** | Yes | Yes |
| **Long-term strategy** | Bridge, not foundation | Bridge, not foundation |

**If you're in the US** running home services or events: start with Thumbtack. Set a $200/month test budget, track every lead in a spreadsheet, and evaluate after 30 days.

**If you're in the UK** or running a creative service: test Bark with a small credit pack first ($50–80) before committing.

**In either case:** the moment you have 15+ Google reviews and a complete Google Business Profile, run a parallel comparison. Most local service businesses find organic leads convert better and cost less than either platform long-term.
