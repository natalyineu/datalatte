For most local businesses, connected TV (CTV) is the better place to start than traditional TV, because you can target specific zip codes, set a small test budget and see more reporting. Traditional local TV can still make sense for businesses that need broad market reach and can afford production and airtime. Prices vary by market and season, so ask for current quotes from both before deciding.

The main differences come down to targeting, cost structure and measurement. This guide compares them honestly, including where traditional TV still has an edge, and shows how a local business can test CTV without a huge commitment.

## Why Is Connected TV Gaining on Traditional TV?

In July 2026 streaming made up 49.0% of US TV time, ahead of broadcast at 19.5% and cable at 18.7% ([Nielsen](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2026/tv-usage-kicks-usual-summer-slowdown-fueled-by-world-cup-and-streaming-in-nielsens-july-gauge-reports/), checked Oct 2026). Advertisers are following: IAB's buyer survey projects US CTV ad spend to grow 13.8% in 2026 while linear TV spend falls 1.7% ([MediaPost on IAB](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/412351/ctv-ad-spend-estimated-to-grow-138-social-media.html), secondary, checked Oct 2026). A growing share of your neighbors watch through streaming services, and CTV is how advertisers reach them there.

CTV ads play on the same big screen, inside streaming content, often as non-skippable video. Depending on the platform, you can target by location, household characteristics and viewing context. Traditional TV buys are mostly based on the program, the time slot and the broadcast market, which tends to be much broader than a local business's trade area.

## What Can You Measure on CTV and Traditional TV?

Traditional TV reporting is typically based on estimated ratings and reach, and it is hard to tie a specific spot to a specific customer.

CTV platforms generally give you more, such as:

- impressions and completion rates (how many viewers watched to the end)
- delivery by zip code, device and daypart
- reporting on a daily or weekly basis
- the option to connect exposure to website visits or conversions through pixels or partner tools, depending on the platform

Be realistic: CTV is mainly an awareness channel. People rarely click an ad on a TV, so you will usually judge it by indirect signals such as branded searches, direct visits and "how did you hear about us?" answers. See our [analytics service](/services/analytics) if you want help setting that up.

## How Do the Costs Compare?

The pricing models differ:

- **CTV** is typically bought by CPM (cost per thousand impressions). CPMs and minimum budgets vary widely by platform, market and targeting, so ask for a written quote.
- **Traditional local TV** is bought by the spot, and the price of a 30-second slot depends on the market, program and time of day, plus production and often a media buying fee. Ask the station for a rate card.

Because CTV can be sized to a small zip-code cluster, a test is generally easier to fund than a traditional TV flight. For current numbers, read [How Much Does CTV Advertising Cost? Real CPM Data](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data).

## What Does a CTV Test Look Like in Numbers?

Worked example. Every input is an **assumption**, not a market price: a $2,000 budget, a $25 CPM, a target of 4 impressions per household, a $40 average ticket and a $500 price for one traditional 30-second spot.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| CTV impressions | $2,000 / $25 x 1,000 | 80,000 |
| Households reached | 80,000 / 4 | 20,000 |
| Customers needed to cover the spend | $2,000 / $40 | 50 |
| Share of reached households that must buy | 50 / 20,000 | 0.25% |
| Traditional spots for the same money | $2,000 / $500 | 4 spots |

Under these assumptions the CTV test needs one in 400 reached households to buy. Four spots tell you nothing about who saw them, so you cannot run the same check. Replace the CPM and spot price with your own written quotes.

## CTV vs Traditional TV: Quick Comparison

| Feature | Connected TV (CTV) | Traditional TV |
|---|---|---|
| Targeting | Zip code, demographics and audience data on many platforms | Mostly by market, channel and program |
| Buying model | CPM, often with flexible budgets | Per-spot or package, set by station |
| Minimum commitment | Often lower, varies by platform | Often higher once production and airtime are included |
| Measurement | Impressions, completion and optional site-visit tracking | Estimated ratings and reach |
| Flexibility | Creative and targeting can be changed during a flight | Spots are scheduled in advance |
| Best fit | Local trade-area awareness and testing | Broad market reach and community presence |

Traditional TV is not "bad". Broadcast and cable together were still 38.2% of US TV time in July 2026 (Nielsen, above), and live news and big events still reach large audiences. The honest question is whether you need broad reach or precise local reach.

## Why Does CTV Suit Local Businesses?

If you run a salon, coffee shop, fitness studio or pet groomer, your customers live within a few miles. CTV lets you:

- **Target your trade area** with zip codes or a radius instead of paying for an entire metro.
- **Reach households in a lean-back environment**, which suits brand building.
- **Start small and scale**, instead of committing to a long contract.
- **Run alongside Google Ads or Meta Ads**, so the awareness CTV creates can be captured when people search for you.

## How Do You Start a CTV Campaign?

1. **Define the goal.** For most local businesses it is awareness that leads to more branded searches and walk-ins.
2. **Set a test budget.** Ask the platform for a delivery forecast for your zip codes so you know what the budget can reach.
3. **Choose targeting.** Start with location, then add only one or two audience filters. Stacking too many raises CPMs and shrinks reach.
4. **Create a simple 15-second ad.** Lead with something visually local, make sure it works with low or no sound and end with a clear call to action.
5. **Run, check weekly and give it time.** Plan on at least 60-90 days before judging.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid?

**Treating CTV like a cable-TV buy.** Do not set a large budget and walk away. Begin with a modest test, review delivery and creative performance, and scale what works.

**Ignoring frequency.** Without a frequency cap, the same household may see your ad many times in a week, which wastes money and annoys people. Set a sensible weekly cap and adjust from reporting.

**Using bland, generic creative.** Stock footage and a generic voiceover blend in. Show your real business, staff and neighborhood, and put your key message in text on screen because many people watch with the sound low.

**Tracking nothing.** Use a unique URL, a promo code, a dedicated phone number or a platform pixel so you can see signals. Free options such as a promo code or a short URL work well.

**Quitting too early.** CTV is a top-of-funnel channel. Judging it after two weeks is like checking a seed after three days.

## What Does a 90-Day Test Plan Look Like?

**Days 1-7: setup.** Define a tight geographic target, write two or three short creative versions with different angles (offer, local pride, convenience) and set up tracking.

**Days 8-30: review and optimize.** Look at completion rates and delivery by zip code and daypart. Pause the weakest creative and move budget to the best.

**Days 31-90: scale carefully.** If branded searches, direct visits or customer mentions are rising, expand zip codes or budget gradually, and consider retargeting people who visited your site.

There is no universal budget. Match spending to your margins and to what the platform forecast says you can reach, and keep search and Google Business Profile fundamentals in place first.

## What Creative Works on CTV?

- **Lead with a local hook** in the first couple of seconds, such as a barista pouring latte art or a groomer brushing a dog.
- **Design for low sound** with large, high-contrast text.
- **Use real footage** of your business and customers, with permission, instead of generic stock.
- **Repeat the offer or call to action** in the middle and the end.
- **Test two or three versions** and keep the one that performs best. See the [CTV creative specs guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-creative-specs-guide-2026) for technical requirements.

## Which Metrics Matter?

1. **Cost per new customer.** Total spend divided by new customers you can attribute, even roughly through "how did you hear about us?".
2. **Completion rate.** A low rate means the creative is losing people. Compare against the platform's own averages.
3. **Frequency.** Watch for households seeing the ad far more often than intended.
4. **Branded search and direct traffic.** Compare before, during and after the flight in Google Search Console and Google Analytics.
5. **Assisted conversions.** Many customers see the ad and book later through Google, so look beyond last-click.

## FAQ

**Q: Is CTV cheaper than traditional TV?**
A: Often the entry cost is lower because you can size a campaign to a few zip codes, but the real comparison depends on your market, platform and creative. Get quotes for both.

**Q: Does CTV work for small local businesses?**
A: It can, especially for businesses with a clear local trade area and the budget to run for a couple of months. It works best alongside search and a strong Google Business Profile.

**Q: Can I track results from CTV ads?**
A: You get delivery and completion data from the platform, and you can track indirect effects such as branded searches, direct visits, promo codes and customer surveys. Direct clicks are rare.

**Q: Do I need a professional video?**
A: Not necessarily. Clear, well-lit footage with good audio and readable text can work. Quality matters more than expensive production.

**Q: How long should I run a CTV test?**
A: Plan on 60-90 days so you have enough exposure and data to judge.
