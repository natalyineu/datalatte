Of 210 independent cafés, bakeries and roasters in Poznań checked on Google Maps in November 2026, 35% list their own website, 81% a phone number and 44% an Instagram page. Only 58 (28%) have all three.

The best-presented cafés pair that full set with hundreds or thousands of reviews, yet the data holds a surprise: the weakest group is not cafés without a website, it is cafés with no online presence at all.

This is an analysis of public profiles, not of coffee. It scores what a customer can see before visiting: a rating, a count of reviews, a website, a phone, social pages. It also opens 67 café websites to see how they are built.

The tables rank the best-presented cafés, split the market by review volume, postal area and type of place, and end with a self-audit, a Google Business Profile playbook and a 90-day plan.

## What are the main findings?

The analysis is long, so the results come first. Each point is explained with its table further down.

- Ratings do not separate Poznań cafés: 85% of rated cafés are at 4.5 or higher and the average is 4.67. Review volume does, from a median of 219 to over 1,000 for the top tenth.
- The weakest profiles are not those that use a social page as their website. They are the 55 cafés with no website and no social page found, whose median is 65 reviews.
- Only 35% of cafés list an own website, and 28% have the full set of own website, phone and Instagram.
- The best-presented cafés are rarely technical leaders: their websites are similar to the rest, and what sets them apart is reviews and a reachable profile.
- Central postal areas 61-7 and 61-8 show the highest review counts among the larger areas, with medians of 385 and 371.
- Of 55 working café websites, only 25% carry local-business structured data, 51% a tap-to-call link and 42% a Google Maps link.
- Google states that incentives for reviews are prohibited and that there is no way to pay for a better local ranking, so reviews and a complete profile are the legitimate path.
- A café with 60 reviews needs roughly 10 to 20 months to reach the median, at 8 to 15 new reviews a month (assumption).

## How was the dataset built and cleaned?

The starting point was public Google Maps listings returned for searches such as "kawiarnia", "specialty coffee", "palarnia kawy" and "cafe" in Poznań, collected on 2 and 3 November 2026 with a scraping tool. That gave 483 listings. A first pass removed supermarkets, shopping centres, museums, parks and public places.

A second pass removed filling stations, the Wild Bean Cafe chain, restaurants, pizzerias, fast-food outlets, ice-cream parlours, vending machines, canteens, coffee-machine repair and wholesale firms, and a PC repair shop.

What remained is 210 listings of cafés, coffee shops, patisseries, bakeries and roasters, one row per Google listing. A café with two locations counts twice. The table shows how the 483 became 210.

| Step | Listings | What was removed |
|---|---|---|
| Collected | 483 | All Google Maps results for the searches in Poznań |
| First pass | 136 | Supermarkets, shopping centres, museums, parks and other public places |
| Second pass: fuel and chain | 17 | Filling stations and the Wild Bean Cafe chain |
| Second pass: restaurants | 32 | Restaurants, fast-food outlets and pizzerias |
| Second pass: ice cream | 13 | Ice-cream parlours and waffle shops |
| Second pass: services | 19 | Coffee-machine repair, wholesale, shops and a PC repair firm |

The second pass counts are approximate, because some listings match more than one category. Closed places were skipped at collection.

The cleaning was done by name, which is imperfect. A few restaurant-led places with "cafe" in the name were removed, and a few mislabelled places may remain. Every figure below uses the cleaned 210, except the website audit, which uses the 67 distinct websites of the cafés that list their own domain.

## How was the ranking built?

Each café scores up to 100 points from signals a customer can see:

- Own website: 20 points. A Facebook or Instagram page does not count; a third-party menu or ordering page is counted as a website.
- Phone number listed: 10.
- Instagram page linked from the listing or the café's website: 15.
- Facebook page linked: 5.
- E-mail address found on the café's website: 10.
- Google rating of 4.5 or higher: 15 (4.0 or higher: 8).
- Review volume: 1,000 or more reviews 25 points, 500 or more 18, 200 or more 10, 100 or more 5.

Ties are broken by review count. The score measures how complete and proven a public profile looks. It does not measure taste, service or prices, and a café missing from a table is not a bad café.

Instagram and Facebook count only when linked from the Google listing or the café's own website. E-mail counts only when it appears on the website. Both are therefore lower bounds, not a census of what exists.

In the named tables, a place is listed only when its name or website confirms it is a café, patisserie, bakery or roaster. High-scoring entries whose type could not be confirmed, or whose "website" is a third-party ordering page, are not named, which is why a few scores of 93 do not appear in the top ten.

## What does Google say decides local ranking?

Google's help page says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity". It defines prominence as how well-known a business is and says this factor depends on "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have". It adds that "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking".

The same page says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to appear, and that "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google". It lists address, opening hours, category and attributes as the information to complete. It does not name a website or a phone number as ranking factors, so the score above describes profile quality for customers, not a ranking formula.

## What do the best-presented cafés have in common?

Two things show up clearly. Every top-ten café has at least 607 reviews, against a median of 219, and only 53 of the 210 cafés have 500 or more. And their ratings sit between 4.4 and 4.8, right where most of the market already is.

Rating alone therefore does not separate cafés in Poznań. Almost everyone is rated well, and what differs is how many customers have said so. The review count is the number that moves most between a thin profile and a strong one.

## Would a different scoring method change who ranks first?

Three ways of ranking the same 210 cafés give different answers, and comparing them shows what the combined score really rewards.

Ranking by review count alone puts Weranda Caffe, Kociak, U Przyjaciół, Stragan and ABOUT CAFE first, followed by Parlé Pâtisserie and four other well-reviewed places. Ranking by completeness alone, which adds up website, phone, Instagram, Facebook and e-mail for a maximum of 60 points, cannot separate the 44 cafés that reach the full 60. Reviews and rating then have to break the tie, which is what the combined score does.

Only five cafés appear in both the top ten by reviews and the top ten by combined score: Weranda Caffe, Kociak, U Przyjaciół, Stragan and Parlé Pâtisserie. The other five most-reviewed places miss the combined top ten because their profile lacks at least one of the elements the score counts, such as an own website or a published e-mail address.

The overlap is wider across the top 24: nine cafés are in both lists. And 23 of the 24 best-scoring cafés have at least 55 of the 60 completeness points. A strong review count with a thin profile and a complete profile with few reviews both land lower than a café that has both.

That is the intended behaviour of the method, and it is the argument for working on both at once.

## Does having an own website mean more reviews?

Not in this dataset. Grouping cafés by how they present themselves gives a result that is easy to misread.

## Instagram, Facebook or both?

Social links split the cafés four ways. Among the 210 cafés, 54 (26%) link both an Instagram and a Facebook page, 39 (19%) only Instagram, 50 (24%) only Facebook, and 67 (32%) neither.

| Social pages found | Cafés | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram and Facebook | 54 | 26% |
| Instagram only | 39 | 19% |
| Facebook only | 50 | 24% |
| Neither found | 67 | 32% |

So 89 cafés (42%) are present on one of the two platforms only. A customer who looks for them on the other platform finds nothing. The Poland guide on this site, citing DataReportal's Digital 2026 report, puts Facebook ad reach at 18.7 million, down 3.6% on the year, and Instagram at 12.4 million, up 6.9%.

Facebook is still larger, but Instagram is growing, which makes a Facebook-only profile a safe base and an Instagram-only profile a narrower one.

The cheapest improvement is to cross-link: whichever page exists, add the other if it can be maintained, and link both from the Google profile and the website. A page that is rarely updated can hurt more than no page, so only link a page that is current.

## What do café contact details say about the business?

Contact details are the first thing a customer uses, and the dataset shows how cafés choose them.

Of the 171 cafés with a phone number, 150 (88%) list a mobile number, 16 (9%) a Poznań landline starting with 61, and 5 another area code. A mobile number often means the owner or a manager answers directly, which is quick for customers but can mean calls go unanswered during service. The data cannot show how often calls are answered.

E-mail shows a different split. Of the 91 cafés that publish an address on their website, 41 (45%) use a free mailbox such as Gmail, Wirtualna Polska or Onet, and 50 (55%) an address on their own domain. Only 21 (23%) use a generic role address such as kontakt, info or biuro.

An address on the café's own domain looks established and survives a change of owner, while a free mailbox is tied to one person. For a café that wants catering, event or supplier enquiries, a role address on the own domain plus a clear contact page is the simplest fix.

The audit found that 53% of loaded home pages carry an e-mail link and 51% a tap-to-call link, so on about half of sites a phone user cannot call in one tap.

## What do the cafés' own websites look like?

Of the 210 cafés, 74 list an own domain. Those 74 listings point to 67 distinct websites. Each home page was opened once with a plain request, one site at a time, and its HTML was checked. Of the 67 sites, 55 loaded normally. ## Are the top cafés technically ahead of the rest?

Not clearly. Of the ten top-ranked cafés, eight sites loaded in the check. Seven of the eight use HTTPS and have a mobile tag, four have a meta description, three have structured data and three have an analytics tag.

Across the other 47 loaded sites the shares are similar: 62% have a meta description and 55% an analytics tag, higher than the top eight. With a sample of eight, no conclusion follows, but the pattern matches the earlier tables. What separates the leading cafés is review volume and a reachable profile, not website engineering.

## What does a complete café home page contain?

The audit lists what fifty-five working café home pages actually offer. Read as a checklist, it shows what a visitor can expect and where most sites fall short.

- **Menu link (67% of sites).** The most common action a visitor wants. Cafés without it force customers to ask by phone or open Instagram.
- **Opening hours.** Not measured on the page, but Google asks for hours on the profile, including holidays, and a site that repeats them removes doubt.
- **Instagram (76%) and Facebook (80%) links.** Present on most sites, which makes missing links from the Google listing the real gap.
- **Cookie notice (67%).** Common in Poland and the European Union where sites use analytics or advertising tags. The data does not check whether notices are configured correctly.
- **E-mail link (53%) and tap-to-call link (51%).** Roughly half of sites let a visitor make contact in one tap.
- **Google Maps link or embed (42%).** Fewer than half link to directions. A map link on a mobile page saves a customer typing the address.
- **Reservation or booking link (40%).** Mostly restaurants and larger cafés, but it also shows which places take groups.
- **Ordering platform link (22%).** Wolt, Glovo, Pyszne and similar. Each is a way for customers to order without calling.
- **Structured data of a local-business type (25%).** Google's page says this lets a business tell Google about hours and details, and that Google may show a knowledge panel. Only 14 of 55 sites have it.
- **English version (22%).** Poznań has foreign students and visitors. Twelve sites offer an English page or link.
- **Analytics (45%).** A snippet for Google Analytics or Tag Manager lets a café see which pages are used. Without it, a café cannot tell whether the site brings customers.

A complete page does not need to be large. In the dataset, most strong sites combine the menu, hours, address with a map link, phone and e-mail, and the two social links in a few screens. The missing parts are small and cheap to add.

## How not to do it: which gaps are common?

No café is named here, because the data cannot say why a profile looks thin. The aggregate numbers show where customers hit a wall.

- 55 of 210 cafés (26%) list no website at all.
- 81 (39%) use a Facebook or Instagram page as the website link, so there is no own domain for menu, booking or analytics.
- 39 (19%) list no phone number.
- 67 (32%) show no Instagram or Facebook page linked from the listing or the website.
- 47 (22%) have fewer than 50 reviews, including 7 with none listed.
- Of the 67 distinct own websites, 12 (18%) did not load normally when checked.
- Only 7 of the 155 cafés that list a website tag the link with UTM parameters, so most cannot tell traffic from Google Maps apart in their analytics.

Instagram and Facebook are counted only when they are linked from the Google listing or the café's website. A café can have an account that customers cannot find from the listing. That is the gap: a profile customers cannot reach does not help, whether or not it exists.

## What should a café do depending on its review count?

The right next step depends on where the café sits in the distribution. The groups below use the review bands from the tables above.

### Fewer than 50 reviews (47 cafés, 22%)

These are mostly new or quiet cafés, and more than half of this group has no website. The aim is to be found and to look alive. Complete the Google profile, add real photos, link an Instagram or Facebook page, and put a QR code for reviews at the till from the first day.

The median here is far below the market, so even ten new reviews a month change the picture within a year.

### 50 to 499 reviews (110 cafés, 52%)

This is the middle of the market, and the profile gaps matter most here. Check phone, website, hours and links against the self-audit table. Add menu and contact details to the website, and reply to every review in the same week. Moving from 200 to 400 reviews is the steepest climb in the distribution.

### 500 or more reviews (53 cafés, 25%)

These cafés already look established. The risks are staleness and wasted attention: outdated hours, a menu that no longer matches, a Facebook page that is not updated. Keep the profile current, publish an e-mail address on an own domain, add structured data and an English page if tourists matter, and track traffic from the listing with UTM parameters, as only 7 of 155 cafés with a website do.

A café should not copy the top ten blindly. The best-presented places have been open for years, and a smaller, newer café with a clean profile and a good reply habit can be a better neighbour to visit than a crowded one.

## How many months does it take to catch up on reviews?

The median in the dataset is 219 reviews. Suppose a café has 60 reviews (assumption) and wants to reach that median. It needs 159 more. At 8 new reviews a month (assumption) that is about 20 months. At 15 a month (assumption) it is about 11 months. To reach the 603 reviews that mark the top quarter, at 15 a month (assumption), another 543 reviews would take about 36 months.

A café with 100 transactions a day (assumption) and a 2% review rate (assumption) would collect about 60 reviews a month, which shows why the review prompt at the counter matters more than any later campaign.

## What should a café fix first on its Google Business Profile?

Google's Business Profile help gives a short list of what to complete. The items below come from its pages on editing a profile and getting reviews.

1. **Hours.** Google says to set main hours or mark the business closed, to use special hours for holidays and other temporary periods, and to set more hours if different features have different hours. A café with a terrace or a kitchen that closes earlier than the counter fits this.
2. **Categories.** Pick the primary category that best matches what the business does; Google allows up to nine more. Its tip is not to use categories solely as keywords or to describe features.
3. **Attributes.** Google describes attributes as telling customers more, with examples such as Wi-Fi or outdoor seating. For a café these are often the deciding details.
4. **Photos.** Add photos or videos of the storefront, products and services. Google notes that an exterior photo helps customers recognise the place when they arrive.
5. **Verification.** Replying to reviews requires a verified business, and Google reviews edits before they go live, so changes do not appear instantly.

The [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) walks through each field with screenshots of where it lives, and the [coffee shop Google Maps guide](/blog/coffee-shops-dominate-google-maps) covers the review routine.

## How should a café ask for reviews and answer them?

Google's help page on getting more reviews says customers can be reminded to leave reviews by asking them to visit a Google link or scan a QR code. It also says to reply to reviews, that replies should be "conversational, not promotional", and that responses should not be used to offer deals or promotions.

One rule matters more than the rest. Google states that offering incentives, such as free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for reviews is "considered fake and misleading content and is strictly prohibited", and that this covers incentives to change reviews or remove negative ones. A café that gives a free coffee for a five-star review risks having the reviews removed.

Google's review pages also say a business can flag a review it believes violates the content policies, and that a reply is reviewed before it is posted publicly and the reviewer is notified. The sample texts below are templates, not data, and the Polish wording can be adapted to the café's voice.

### What can the counter card say?

A short card with a QR code at the till is the lowest-effort request. A template:

- Polish: "Smakowała Ci kawa? Będzie nam bardzo miło, jeśli zostawisz opinię w Google. Wystarczy zeskanować kod."
- English: "Did you enjoy your coffee? A review on Google would mean a lot to us. Just scan the code."

### How can a café reply to a positive review?

A short, specific reply works better than a stock phrase. A template:

- Polish: "Dziękujemy za miłe słowa i za wizytę! Cieszymy się, że smakował sernik. Zapraszamy ponownie."
- English: "Thank you for the kind words and for visiting. We are glad you enjoyed the cheesecake. See you again."

### How can a café reply to a negative review?

Answer calmly, acknowledge the issue, offer a channel for details, and do not argue in public. A template:

- Polish: "Dziękujemy za opinię i przepraszamy za to doświadczenie. Chcielibyśmy to wyjaśnić, napisz proszę do nas na adres kontakt@twojakawiarnia.pl."
- English: "Thank you for the feedback and we are sorry about the experience. We would like to look into it; please write to us at the address above."

The address is a placeholder. Do not include a discount or an offer in the reply, since Google says replies should avoid promotions.

## What should a café website say in one page?

A single page can carry a café. It needs to answer the questions a visitor has on a phone in front of the door: where, when, what and how to reach. The home pages in the audit most often lack a tap-to-call link, a Google Maps link and structured data, so those are the three cheap additions.

Google's structured-data page for local businesses says the format lets a business tell Google about hours and departments, and that search may show a knowledge panel with business details. It lists only name and address as required properties.

It recommends, among others, the telephone number, the URL of the specific location, geographic coordinates, opening hours, price range and, for food establishments, the URL of the menu. Its examples use JSON-LD, and it advises the most specific business sub-type available.

An example for a café follows. Every value is a placeholder to be replaced, and the result should be checked with Google's Rich Results Test.

```html
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "CafeOrCoffeeShop",
  "name": "Example Café",
  "url": "https://example.pl/",
  "telephone": "+48 000 000 000",
  "menu": "https://example.pl/menu",
  "priceRange": "PLN 10-40",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "ul. Przykładowa 1",
    "addressLocality": "Poznań",
    "postalCode": "60-000",
    "addressCountry": "PL"
  },
  "openingHoursSpecification": [
    {
      "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
      "dayOfWeek": ["Monday", "Tuesday", "Wednesday", "Thursday", "Friday"],
      "opens": "08:00",
      "closes": "18:00"
    }
  ]
}
</script>
```

Beyond the code, a one-page café site needs only six blocks: name and photo, address with a map link, opening hours, menu, phone and e-mail as tap-to-contact links, and links to Instagram and Facebook. The [local SEO audit guide](/blog/local-seo-audit-tool-review) shows how to check that the site and the Maps listing say the same thing.

## What can this data not tell you?

Several limits apply, and they matter for how the tables are used.

- The dataset is a snapshot of 2 and 3 November 2026. Ratings and review counts change daily.
- Café age is unknown, and older cafés have had longer to collect reviews.
- Instagram follower counts, posts and engagement were not collected, and Instagram or Facebook pages count only when linked from the listing or the website.
- E-mail counts include only addresses found on the café's own website.
- Chains and non-cafés were filtered by name, which is imperfect, and the 210 are the listings that matched the searches, not every café in Poznań.
- Third-party ordering or menu pages are counted as websites, which can overstate own-domain share.
- The website audit reads one home page per site, from one machine, at one moment. A site that failed may work for visitors, and a feature on an inner page is not seen.
- Types and postal areas are approximate groupings, and small groups can move with one listing.

## Key terms in this analysis

- **Listing:** one Google Maps entry, which can be a single location of a larger business.
- **Prominence:** Google's term for how well-known a business is, based partly on reviews and links from other sites.
- **Structured data:** code in a page that tells search engines facts such as the business type, address and hours.
- **UTM parameters:** tags added to a link so analytics can tell where a visit came from.
- **JSON-LD:** the format Google's examples use for structured data.
- **Own website:** a site on the café's own domain, as opposed to a Facebook or Instagram page.

## FAQ

### Does having a website improve my Google Maps ranking?

Google's help page does not list a website or phone number as ranking factors. It names relevance, distance and prominence, and says prominence depends partly on how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has. A website still helps customers decide, and links to it from other sites can support prominence.

### Can a café pay for a better position on Google Maps?

No. Google's help page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

### Why are weaker cafés not named?

The data shows what a public profile contains, not why. A thin profile can be a deliberate choice, a new café or a collection gap, so naming it would be unfair. The numbers are published in aggregate.

### How many reviews does a café need?

No threshold is published. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. In this dataset the median café has 219 reviews and the top ten all have at least 607.

### Is a Facebook page enough instead of a website?

In this dataset, cafés using a social page as their website have as many reviews as those with their own domain. A social page is still not a replacement for a domain you control, because you cannot run your own analytics or design the menu and booking flow there.

### Can I offer a free coffee for a review?

No. Google's help page says offering incentives in exchange for reviews is considered fake and misleading content and is strictly prohibited.

### How do I ask for reviews without breaking the rules?

Remind customers to leave a review and give them a Google link or a QR code, as Google's help page suggests. Do not reward the review, and reply to reviews in a conversational way without offering deals.

### Do I need structured data on my café website?

Google's page lists only name and address as required for local business structured data, and recommends fields such as telephone, menu and opening hours. It is not required to appear in Google Maps, but it tells Google facts about the business directly.

### How current is the data and how can a café get corrected?

Listings were collected on 2 and 3 November 2026 and websites were read on the same days. A café that is missing, misclassified or shown incorrectly can write to hi@datalatte.pro and the entry will be corrected.

### Why does the article use 210 cafés and not more?

The first search returned 483 listings, and the cleaning steps removed everything that was not an independent café, bakery or roaster, such as filling stations, chains, restaurants and repair shops.

### Can the same analysis be done for another city or business type?

Yes. The method works for any city and business type that has public Google Maps listings. Write to hi@datalatte.pro with the city and the type of business.

### What is the single most useful thing a café can do first?

Complete the Google profile and make sure the website field and an Instagram link lead somewhere. In this dataset, the cafés with no online presence at all have a median of 65 reviews, against about 250 to 360 for the other groups.
