Of 304 cafés in Poznań checked on Google Maps in November 2026, 46% list their own website, 83% a phone number and 41% an Instagram page. Only 86 (28%) have all three. The best-presented cafés pair that full set with hundreds of reviews, but the data holds a surprise: the weakest group is not cafés without a website, it is cafés with no online presence at all.

This is an analysis of public profiles, not of coffee. It scores what a customer can see before visiting: a rating, a count of reviews, a website, a phone, social pages. The tables below rank the best-presented cafés, break the market down by review volume, postal area and type of place, and end with a self-audit any café can run in fifteen minutes.

## How was the ranking built?

The dataset is public Google Maps listings for cafés, coffee shops, patisseries, bakeries and roasters in Poznań, collected on 2 and 3 November 2026 with a scraping tool and then read page by page from each café's own website for contact details. Supermarkets, shopping centres, museums, parks and chains were removed by name, so a few mislabelled places may remain. After cleaning, 304 listings stayed in the analysis.

Each café scores up to 100 points from signals a customer can see:

- Own website: 20 points. A Facebook or Instagram page does not count; a third-party menu or ordering page is counted as a website.
- Phone number listed: 10.
- Instagram page linked from the listing or the café's website: 15.
- Facebook page linked: 5.
- E-mail address found on the café's website: 10.
- Google rating of 4.5 or higher: 15 (4.0 or higher: 8).
- Review volume: 1,000 or more reviews 25 points, 500 or more 18, 200 or more 10, 100 or more 5.

Ties are broken by review count. The score measures how complete and proven a public profile looks. It does not measure taste, service or prices, and a café missing from a table is not a bad café.

## What does Google say decides local ranking?

Google's help page says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity". It defines prominence as how well-known a business is and says this factor depends on "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have". It adds that "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking".

The same page says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to appear, and that "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google". It lists address, opening hours, category and attributes as the information to complete. It does not name a website or a phone number as ranking factors, so the score above describes profile quality for customers, not a ranking formula.

## What do the best-presented cafés have in common?

Two things show up clearly. Every top-ten café has at least 607 reviews, against a dataset median of 207, and only 72 of the 304 cafés have 500 or more. And their ratings sit between 4.4 and 4.8, right where the market already is: the average rating across 287 rated cafés is 4.58 and 221 of 304 (73%) are at 4.5 or higher.

Rating alone therefore does not separate cafés in Poznań. Almost everyone is rated well, and what differs is how many customers have said so. The review count is the number that moves most between a thin profile and a strong one.

## Does having an own website mean more reviews?

Not in this dataset. Grouping cafés by how they present themselves gives a result that is easy to misread.

| Group | Cafés | Median reviews | Avg rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Own website, phone and Instagram | 86 | 225 | 4.50 |
| Own website, partial profile | 54 | 272 | 4.46 |
| Facebook or Instagram page as the "website" | 90 | 273 | 4.73 |
| No website and no social page found | 74 | 65 | 4.58 |

The cafés that use a social page as their website have as many reviews as those with their own domain, and the highest average rating. The group that stands out is the last one: with no website and no social page linked, the median falls to 65 reviews, about a quarter of the others.

So the practical gap is not between own website and social page. It is between a profile that sends a customer somewhere and a profile that sends them nowhere. An own domain still brings control (menu, booking, analytics), but being reachable online is the first step.

## How not to do it: which gaps are common?

No café is named here, because the data cannot say why a profile looks thin. The aggregate numbers show where customers hit a wall.

- 74 of 304 cafés (24%) list no website at all.
- 90 (30%) use a Facebook or Instagram page as the website link, so there is no own domain for menu, booking or analytics.
- 51 (17%) list no phone number.
- 111 (37%) show no Instagram or Facebook page linked from the listing or the website.
- 69 (23%) have fewer than 50 reviews.
- Of the 140 cafés with an own website, 11 (8%) returned an error or could not be reached when the site was checked: eight connection errors and three pages not found. Another 15 (11%) refused automated checks with HTTP 403, which says nothing about real visitors.
- Only 8 of the 230 cafés that list a website tag the link with UTM parameters, so most cannot tell traffic from Google Maps apart in their analytics.
- Of the 140 own sites, 79 link to a contact page from the home page.

Instagram and Facebook are counted only when they are linked from the Google listing or the café's website. A café can have an account that customers cannot find from the listing. That is the gap: a profile customers cannot reach does not help, whether or not it exists.

## How can a café score itself in 15 minutes?

Open your own Google Maps listing in a private browser window and answer seven questions. Each answer maps to the score used above.

| Check | Points | How to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Website on your own domain in the website field | 20 | Click the website button; it should open your domain |
| Phone number listed and answered | 10 | Call it |
| Instagram page reachable from the listing or site | 15 | Find it in two taps from the listing |
| Facebook page linked | 5 | Same check |
| E-mail address published on your site | 10 | Look for it on the contact page |
| Rating 4.5 or higher | 15 | Read it on the listing |
| 1,000 reviews: 25, 500: 18, 200: 10, 100: 5 | up to 25 | Read the review count |

A score of 80 or more puts a café near the first two tables. Most of the missing points in the dataset are cheap: a phone number, an own website with hours and a menu, a visible Instagram link. Reviews take longer, and the next section shows how long.

## How many months does it take to catch up on reviews?

The median in the dataset is 207 reviews. Suppose a café has 60 reviews (assumption) and wants to reach that median. It needs 147 more. At 8 new reviews a month (assumption) that is about 18 months. At 15 a month (assumption) it is about 10 months. To reach the 500 reviews that only 72 cafés have, at 15 a month (assumption), another 440 reviews would take about 29 months.

Google publishes no minimum review count. Its help page says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking, so a steady monthly rhythm matters more than a one-off push. ## What can this data not tell you?

Several limits apply, and they matter for how the tables are used.

- The dataset is a snapshot of 2 and 3 November 2026. Ratings and review counts change daily.
- Café age is unknown, and older cafés have had longer to collect reviews.
- Instagram follower counts, posts and engagement were not collected, and Instagram or Facebook pages count only when linked from the listing or the website.
- E-mail counts include only addresses found on the café's own website.
- Chains and non-cafés were filtered by name, which is imperfect, and the 304 are the listings that matched the searches, not every café in Poznań.
- Third-party ordering or menu pages are counted as websites, which can overstate own-domain share.

## FAQ

### Does having a website improve my Google Maps ranking?

Google's help page does not list a website or phone number as ranking factors. It names relevance, distance and prominence, and says prominence depends partly on how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has. A website still helps customers decide, and links to it from other sites can support prominence.

### Can a café pay for a better position on Google Maps?

No. Google's help page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

### Why are weaker cafés not named?

The data shows what a public profile contains, not why. A thin profile can be a deliberate choice, a new café or a collection gap, so naming it would be unfair. The numbers are published in aggregate.

### How many reviews does a café need?

No threshold is published. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. In this dataset the median café has about 200 reviews and the top ten all have at least 607.

### Is a Facebook page enough instead of a website?

In this dataset, cafés using a social page as their website have as many reviews as those with their own domain. A social page is still not a replacement for a domain you control, because you cannot run your own analytics or design the menu and booking flow there.

### How current is the data and how can a café get corrected?

Listings were collected on 2 and 3 November 2026. A café that is missing, misclassified or shown incorrectly can write to hi@datalatte.pro and the entry will be corrected.

### Can the same analysis be done for another city?

Yes. The method works for any city and business type that has public Google Maps listings. Write to hi@datalatte.pro with the city and the type of business.
