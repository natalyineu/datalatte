## How was the ranking built?

The dataset is public Google Maps listings for cafés, coffee shops, patisseries and roasters in Poznań, collected on 2 and 3 November 2026 with a scraping tool. Supermarkets, shopping centres, museums, parks, chains and restaurants were removed by name, so some mislabelled places may remain.

Each café scores up to 100 points from signals visible to a customer:

- Own website: 20 points. A Facebook or Instagram page does not count, while a third-party menu or ordering page is counted as a website.
- Phone number listed: 10.
- Instagram page linked from the listing or the café's website: 15.
- Facebook page linked: 5.
- E-mail address found on the café's website: 10.
- Google rating of 4.5 or higher: 15 (4.0 or higher: 8).
- Review volume: 1,000 or more reviews 25 points, 500 or more 18, 200 or more 10, 100 or more 5.

Ties are broken by review count. The score measures how complete and proven a public profile looks. It says nothing about taste, service or prices, and a café missing from the table is not a bad café.

## What does Google say decides local ranking?

Google's own help page says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity". It defines prominence as how well-known a business is and says this factor depends on "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have". It adds that "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking".

The same page says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to appear, and that "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google". It lists address, opening hours, category and attributes as the information to complete. It does not list a website or phone number as ranking factors, so the score above describes profile quality for customers, not a ranking formula.

## What do the top cafés have in common?

Three patterns show in the data. First, review volume. The median Poznań café in the dataset has 207 reviews and only 72 of 304 have 500 or more, yet every café in the top ten has at least 607.

Second, rating alone does not separate them. The average rating across 287 rated cafés is 4.58 and 221 of the 304 (73%) are at 4.5 or higher. Ten top cafés range from 4.4 to 4.8, which is where most of the market sits.

Third, the listing leads somewhere. Each top café has its own domain, a phone number and a social page, so a customer can check a menu, call or look at photos without leaving the café's own channels.

## How not to do it: what do weaker profiles share?

No café is named here, because the data cannot say why a profile looks thin. The aggregate numbers show the gaps.

- 74 of 304 cafés (24%) list no website at all.
- 90 (30%) use a Facebook or Instagram page as the website link, which means no own domain for menu, booking or analytics.
- 51 (17%) list no phone number.
- 111 (37%) show no Instagram or Facebook page linked from the listing or the website.
- 69 (23%) have fewer than 50 reviews.

Instagram and Facebook are counted only when they are linked from the Google listing or the café's website, so some of these cafés may have accounts that customers cannot find from the listing. That is itself the gap: a profile customers cannot reach does not help.

## How many months does it take to catch up on reviews?

The median in the dataset is 207 reviews. Suppose a café has 60 reviews (assumption) and wants to reach that median. It needs 147 more. At 8 new reviews a month (assumption) that is about 18 months; at 15 a month (assumption) it is about 10 months. Google publishes no minimum review count, but its help page says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking, so a steady monthly rhythm matters more than a one-off push. ## What should a Poznań café copy from the top ten?

1. Complete the Google profile first. Google asks for address, opening hours, category and attributes, and the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) walks through each field.

3. Link the Instagram page from the listing and the site, so a customer finds it in one tap.

## FAQ

### Does having a website improve my Google Maps ranking?

Google's help page does not list a website or phone number as ranking factors. It lists relevance, distance and prominence, and says prominence depends partly on how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has. A website still helps customers decide, and links to it from other sites can support prominence.

### Can a café pay for a better position on Google Maps?

No. Google's help page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

### Why are weaker cafés not named?

The data shows what a public profile contains, not why. A thin profile can be a deliberate choice, a new café or a collection gap, so naming it would be unfair. The numbers are published in aggregate.

### How current is the data and how can a café get corrected?

Listings were collected on 2 and 3 November 2026. Ratings and review counts change daily. A café that is missing, misclassified or shown incorrectly can write to hi@datalatte.pro and the entry will be corrected.

### How many reviews does a café need?

No threshold is published. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help, and in this dataset the typical café has about 200.
