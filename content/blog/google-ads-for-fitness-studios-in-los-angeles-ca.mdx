## How much should a fitness studio in Los Angeles spend on Google Ads?

The budget benchmark in the Vertical Impression guide scales with membership size. A small independent studio with under 200 members is pointed to $600–$1,200 a month on Google Ads. A mid-size gym with 200–600 members is pointed to $1,500–$2,500 a month ([Vertical Impression guide](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), secondary).

Google Ads is only one line in a marketing plan. A separate boutique-studio figure from Mariana Tek says many boutique studios budget 5 to 10 percent of monthly revenue for marketing ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/), secondary). For a studio earning $20,000 a month (assumption), that range works out to $1,000–$2,000 across all marketing. Paid search would then need to fit inside that total alongside email, social and reviews.

Los Angeles rent and competition are not reflected in these national ranges, so treat them as a starting band. Set a cap you can hold steady long enough to read the results, then adjust.

## Which keywords should a Los Angeles studio target first?

Start with a small set of exact-match terms that match your class type and location. The intent labels below are a judgement about how close each searcher is to booking, not measured data.

| Keyword | Searcher intent | Suggested match type |
|---------|-----------------|----------------------|
| gym near me | Choosing a nearby option | Exact |
| fitness studio Los Angeles | Local comparison | Exact or phrase |
| yoga classes near me | Class-specific booking | Exact |

Neighbourhood variants, such as a pilates studio in Silver Lake or West Hollywood, can capture people who already know their area. Use the [keyword guide for fitness studios](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-fitness-studios-in-2026) to build the full list before you expand.

## What could a $1,500 monthly budget return for a studio?

Every input in this worked example is an assumption, chosen for illustration rather than measured. The benchmark range supplies the clicks and conversion inputs.

- Monthly budget: $1,500 (assumption, within the mid-size range)
- Clicks: 450 (assumption, near the middle of the 300–600 range)
- Click-to-trial conversion: 4.5% (assumption, the middle of 3–6%)
- Trial sign-ups: 450 × 4.5% ≈ 20
- Cost per trial lead: $1,500 ÷ 20 = $75, against a target under $30
- Share of trials that become members: 25% (assumption), so about 5 members
- Cost per member: $1,500 ÷ 5 = $300, against a target of $150–$200

At this budget, a $30 cost per trial would need about 50 trials a month. That is roughly 11% of 450 clicks, well above the 3–6% range. The gap points to the landing page, the trial offer or the booking flow as much as to the budget itself.

## How do you target Los Angeles neighbourhoods without wasting spend?

Set radius targeting around your studio address rather than the whole metro area. Most members come from nearby, and a tight radius keeps spend on people who can realistically attend. Check the location settings in your account against the areas you serve.

Add neighbourhood names to ad copy and to phrase-match variants. Negative keywords matter just as much. Exclude job-related terms such as "jobs" and "hiring", DIY terms such as "how to" and "course", cities outside your service area, and irrelevant modifiers such as "franchise" or "wholesale". Keep broad match away from generic terms like "gym" until you have conversion data. The [Google Ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-google-ads-guide) covers campaign setup in more detail.

## Why does your Google Business Profile matter for Google Ads?

Google Business Profile is a free tool that displays business hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos directly in search results ([The Association of Fitness Studios](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google), secondary). The same article notes that the Google Map Pack lists relevant local businesses for queries such as "personal training studios near me". A paid click that lands on a thin profile wastes the attention you paid for.

Ads and profile work on different timelines. Mariana Tek says Google review volume can start influencing visibility within a few weeks, while its table lists a 6 to 12 month payoff for local SEO and Google reviews ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/), secondary). Keep paid search running for near-term bookings, and keep building reviews and profile quality for longer-term visibility. The [Google Business Profile optimisation guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization) has a checklist for this.

## What should happen after a prospect clicks, and what should you track?

Mariana Tek advises following up with pop-up contacts within 24 hours using an automated welcome sequence, because interest fades fast ([Mariana Tek](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/group-fitness-studio-marketing-strategies/), secondary). The same logic applies to ad leads. A trial request that waits a week for a reply has usually lost its momentum. Automation and email can handle that first contact, and the [marketing automation guide for local fitness studios](/blog/marketing-automation-for-local-fitness-studios) and the [email marketing guide](/blog/email-marketing-fitness-studio) explain how to set it up.

Track conversions before you judge the campaign. Useful signals include:

1. Phone calls from ads, using a tracked call number
2. Trial or booking form completions on your website
3. Direction requests from your Google Business Profile
4. Clicks on "book now" or contact buttons as micro-conversions

Compare these against cost per trial lead and cost per member, not just clicks.
