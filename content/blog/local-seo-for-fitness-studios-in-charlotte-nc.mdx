## What Is the Google Map Pack, and Why Do Charlotte Studios Need It?

The map pack is the set of local businesses Google lists for many local queries. The Association of Fitness Studios, in an article published May 2024, uses "personal training studios near me" as an example of a query that triggers it ([The Association of Fitness Studios](https://member.afsfitness.com/content/how-make-your-fitness-business-stand-out-google), secondary). The same article describes Google Business Profile as a free tool that shows hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos directly in search results.

Google's own Business Profile page makes the same free-setup point under the headline "Stand out on Google with a free Business Profile" ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)). For a studio, this means the details a prospect checks before booking sit in one place, and the profile is the asset to keep accurate.

Ranking influence is less settled. In a 2021 BrightLocal survey reported by Ahrefs, 36% of SEOs named the Business Profile as the most important factor for the map pack, up from 23% in 2013 ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), secondary). That measures practitioner opinion, not a published Google rule. Treat it as a reason to prioritise the profile, not a guarantee of ranking.

## How Do You Set Up the Business Profile Step by Step?

Work through these steps in order, then repeat the review monthly:

1. Claim and verify the listing under the studio's exact trading name.
2. Describe your class types and services in plain language. Avoid repeating the same keyword in every sentence.
3. Upload at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes in session and coaches. One gym marketing guide recommends this minimum ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), secondary).
4. Publish offers and schedule news as posts, then check each post's status. Google's help page says a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).
5. Update holiday hours and class schedule changes the same day they take effect.

If the studio also runs Google Ads, note that location data is shared. Google states that location asset data comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and successful corrections there flow into linked Ads accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)). The [fitness studio Business Profile optimisation guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization) walks through each profile field in more detail.

## Which Citations Should a Charlotte Studio Fix First?

Citations are mentions of your name, address and phone number on other websites. The rule is consistency: the same spelling, abbreviations and phone format everywhere. "Street" on one directory and "St." on another counts as a mismatch. Audit your existing listings before adding new ones, because more inconsistent listings compound the problem.

One landscaping business reported an 18% rise in local pack appearances after cleaning up its NAP data, according to a vendor article ([AccountabilityNow](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), secondary). Treat that as a single anecdote, not proof. It does show that a cleanup is worth testing before building new listings.

For a Charlotte studio, start with the directories prospects already use: the major map platforms, the class and booking platforms you use, and any local chamber or neighbourhood listings you qualify for. Check each one for identical details. Once the listings match, embedding your map on the contact page is a small, separate step, covered in the [Google Maps embed guide](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

## How Many Reviews Do You Need, and How Should Staff Ask?

Reviews appear next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search, so each one is visible at the moment a prospect decides whether to visit ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Google's own tips page is the best place to check current guidance on requesting reviews.

An unsourced claim in local SEO writing says businesses with over 50 reviews and a 4.7+ average rating dominate top map pack spots ([AccountabilityNow](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), secondary). The page cites no evidence, so use the figure as a target to test against your own rankings.

Ask while the experience is fresh, for example by text or email after a first class. Customers who describe the coaching or the beginner-friendly classes in their reviews give the profile more relevant wording. Reply to every review with a short, specific message that names the class type and the city.

## Which Keywords Should the Website and Profile Target?

Start with the phrases your members actually type. The AFS example, "personal training studios near me," shows the pattern: a service plus a local modifier. For a Charlotte studio, build a short list of class type plus Charlotte, and class type plus a neighbourhood name. Check autocomplete suggestions and a keyword tool for volume in your area. Tools disagree on volume, so this guide does not quote figures.

Use each phrase once in a relevant place: the service page title, a subheading and the profile's services list. For paid search, the [keyword guide for fitness studios](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-fitness-studios-in-2026) covers which phrases tend to carry the most commercial intent.

## What Does a Worked Budget Look Like for a Charlotte Studio?

The inputs below are assumptions for illustration. Replace each one with your own figures before deciding anything.

- New members per month who come from Maps calls and direction requests: 30 (assumption)
- Average monthly membership: $120 (assumption)
- Average member retention: 6 months (assumption)
- Staff time on profile upkeep: 2 hours per week at $25 per hour (assumption)

Lifetime value per member is $120 × 6 = $720. One month's cohort of 30 members is worth 30 × $720 = $21,600. Monthly upkeep is 2 hours × 4.33 weeks × $25 ≈ $217. The break-even point is $217 ÷ $720 ≈ 0.3 members per month, or about one member every three to four months.

Under these assumptions, the profile pays for its upkeep if it brings in one member roughly every three to four months. If your profile sends only a handful of members a month, the case is weaker, and paid search may be the faster lever. The [Google Ads guide for fitness studios](/blog/google-ads-for-fitness-studios-complete-2026-guide) sets out how to compare the two.

## How Do You Track Whether the Profile Is Working?

Check the profile's insights each month for views, searches, calls, direction requests and website visits. Track organic search performance by page in Google Search Console. Record your position for a short list of phrases, noting the search location, because map results change with where the searcher stands. ## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a fitness studio?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as free to set up on Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/)).

### How many photos should a studio upload?

At least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes and staff, according to one gym marketing guide ([Vertical Impression](https://www.verticalimpression.com/guides/how-to-market-health-wellness-business-2026), secondary).

### Why does a new post not appear publicly?

Only posts with the Live status show on Search and Maps. Pending and Not approved posts do not show ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)).

### Can a multi-location studio use its Maps locations in Google Ads?

Google lets advertisers select up to 10 locations from Google Maps to link with an Ads account when Business Profile and chain store options do not work. The locations must belong to the advertiser, or they may be disapproved ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en)).
