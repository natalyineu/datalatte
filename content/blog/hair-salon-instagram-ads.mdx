A hair salon should start Instagram ads with one local offer, such as a first-visit price or a colour consultation, run it as a lead or message campaign for two to four weeks, and judge it by cost per booked client. WordStream by LocaliQ's September 2026 benchmarks put Beauty & Personal Care lead campaigns at $2.97 per click and $50.91 per lead, and traffic campaigns at $0.50 per click. Those are Meta-wide averages, not Instagram-only.

This guide is for salon owners who have never run a paid ad. It covers goals, benchmarks, creative, setup and a worked example. Anything labelled an assumption is an illustration, not a published figure.

## Do Instagram Ads Work for a Hair Salon?

They can, because hair is visual and local. Pew Research Center's 2026 survey found 51% of US adults say they ever use Instagram, against 70% for Facebook and 31% for TikTok. Instagram is one of several places your clients are, not the only one.

The risk is paying to reach people who will not travel to you. Local targeting, a clear offer and a booking path that takes seconds decide the result. Our [Instagram versus Facebook ad comparison](/blog/instagram-ads-vs-facebook-ads) shows when each platform fits a local shop.

## What Do Instagram Ads Cost for Salons?

LocaliQ reports separate numbers by campaign goal. Lead campaigns ask for a form or message, so clicks cost more and the result is closer to a booking. Traffic campaigns send people to a page, so clicks are cheaper but less qualified.

| Metric | Beauty & Personal Care | All industries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traffic CPC | $0.50 | $0.60 |
| Traffic CTR | 2.73% | 1.93% |
| Lead CPC | $2.97 | $1.80 |
| Lead CTR | 1.35% | 2.70% |
| Lead conversion rate | 5.63% | 8.54% |
| Cost per lead | $50.91 | $27.39 |

Beauty lead campaigns are costlier than the all-industry average. The page does not split Instagram from Facebook, and a "lead" may be a form fill, not a booked appointment. Treat the numbers as a ceiling to beat, not a promise.

## What Should the Ad Show?

Use real work from your chair: a finished cut or colour, a short clip of the process, or the stylist. Keep the first seconds clear on what you do and where. The offer should be specific, such as "first colour consultation free this month", and the call to action should match it.

Video and Reels placements are covered in our [Reels ads guide for local business](/blog/instagram-reels-ads-local-business-guide). Get client permission before using their photos, and do not promise results you cannot guarantee.

## How Do You Set Up the First Campaign?

You need an Instagram professional account and an ad account. Instagram's help center describes boosting a post with a goal, audience, budget and duration. We could not read the text of that help page when we checked, so confirm the steps in the app, because the interface changes.

Start simple. Choose a goal that matches the offer (messages or leads for bookings), set a radius around your salon, add an age range that fits your clients, and set a daily budget you can afford for the full test. Keep the setup the same for two weeks so the data is readable. Our [Instagram ad cost guide](/blog/how-much-do-instagram-ads-cost-2026-pricing-breakdown) explains how budgets map to results.

## How Should You Make the Booking Easy?

Every extra step loses people. Link to a page where the offer and a booking button are visible on a phone without scrolling, or use a message goal and reply fast. Make sure your phone number, address and hours match your Google Business Profile, since many people check there before they book.

Track the source. Ask each new client how they found you, or use a dedicated offer name. Without that, you will not know whether the ad or word of mouth brought the booking.

## What Does a $300 Test Return? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions** built on the LocaliQ Beauty & Personal Care lead benchmark.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget (assumption) | Chosen | $300 |
| Cost per lead | LocaliQ benchmark | $50.91 |
| Leads | $300 / $50.91 | about 5.9 |
| Leads who book (assumption) | Chosen | 50% |
| New clients | 5.9 x 50% | about 3 |
| Cost per new client | $300 / 3 | about $100 |
| Client value over a year (assumption) | 5 visits x $75 | $375 |

On these assumptions each new client returns about 3.7 times the ad cost over a year. If only one lead in four books, the cost per client doubles to about $200, still below the assumed value. A single visit at $75 would not cover the cost, so the repeat rate matters. Replace each assumption with your own numbers.

## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Instagram ads?

Enough to get a readable result, which depends on your cost per lead. At the Beauty benchmark of $50.91 per lead, a $300 test buys about six leads, which is few. Plan a budget for at least a month.

### Should I use boosted posts or Ads Manager?

Boosting is simpler. Ads Manager gives more control. Start with boosting a strong post if you are new, then move to Ads Manager.

### Do Instagram ads beat Google Ads for salons?

They do different jobs. Google Search catches people looking for a salon now, and Instagram builds interest. Start with your Business Profile and test one paid channel at a time.

### What offer works best?

No published source ranks offers. Test one clear first-visit offer against your usual price and compare cost per booked client.

### How long until I know if it works?

Run for two to four weeks at a steady budget. We did not find a published minimum.
