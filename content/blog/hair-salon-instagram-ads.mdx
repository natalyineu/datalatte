## Do Instagram Ads Work for a Hair Salon?

They can, because hair is visual and most bookings are local. Age matters. A secondary summary of Pew Research Center data reports that 71% of US adults aged 18 to 29 use Instagram, compared with 48% of those aged 30 to 49 ([Salon Biz Software, citing Pew Research Center](https://salonbizsoftware.com/blog/daily-salon-social-media-marketing-ideas-how-to-engage-your-audience/)). If your regulars are mostly younger, Instagram deserves a test. If they are mostly older, the same budget may do more on Google or Facebook.

The main risk is paying to reach people who will never travel to your chair. Local targeting, a clear offer and a booking path that takes seconds decide the result. A paid campaign also cannot fix a weak profile, so check that your bio, address and booking link work before spending anything.

## How Often Should a Salon Post Alongside Its Ads?

Ads perform better when the profile looks active. The secondary Hootsuite guidance cited by Salon Biz Software recommends posting on Instagram 2 to 7 times per week ([Salon Biz Software](https://salonbizsoftware.com/blog/daily-salon-social-media-marketing-ideas-how-to-engage-your-audience/)). The same source treats these figures as recommendations, not rules, and suggests starting with at least one post a day.

Batching helps. Film several looks in one session, then schedule them. A planning tool keeps the calendar visible across weeks, and the [Later versus Planoly comparison for Instagram scheduling](/blog/later-vs-planoly-instagram-scheduling-local-business) shows how two popular options differ for small teams.

## What Should the Ad Show?

Use real work from your chair: a finished cut or colour, a short process clip, or a stylist speaking to the camera. Salon Biz Software's list of daily ideas includes a Makeover Monday before-and-after and a Transformation Tuesday post, both of which translate directly into ad creative ([Salon Biz Software](https://salonbizsoftware.com/blog/daily-salon-social-media-marketing-ideas-how-to-engage-your-audience/)). Keep the first two seconds clear about what you do and where you are.

The offer should be specific, such as "first colour consultation free this month", and the button should match it. Get written permission from any client shown, and do not promise results a stylist cannot guarantee. If you want help drafting captions, the [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) include caption prompts you can adapt.

## How Do You Set Up the First Campaign?

Follow this checklist. The Meta Business Help Center page that would normally explain boosting returned a 404 error when checked, so confirm each option in Ads Manager, because menus change.

1. Confirm you have an Instagram professional account linked to your business Page.
2. Choose one offer and one goal, such as messages or leads for bookings.
3. Set a radius around the salon that matches your real travel area, not the whole city.
4. Pick an age range that fits your regular clients, then widen it only after the first results.
5. Set a daily budget you can keep up for the full test period.
6. Name the campaign with the offer and the start date so results are easy to compare.
7. Leave the settings unchanged for two weeks so the data is readable.

## How Should the Booking Path Work?

Every extra step loses people. Send clicks to a page where the offer and a booking button are visible without scrolling on a phone, or use a message goal and reply fast. Make sure your phone number, address and hours match your Google Business Profile, because many people check there before booking. The [hair salon local SEO guide](/blog/hair-salon-local-seo-guide) covers that profile in more detail.

Track the source. Ask each new client how they found you, or give the ad offer its own name for bookings. Without that, you cannot tell whether the ad or word of mouth brought the booking. Influencer offers can compete for the same budget, so the [guide to influencer marketing for hair salons](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-hair-salons-how-to-get-free-bookings-from-local-creator) is worth reading before you split spend.

## FAQ

### How much should a hair salon spend on Instagram ads?

Spend enough to get a readable result over two to four weeks. The right amount depends on your own cost per lead, which you only learn by testing. Start with a budget you can keep steady for the whole period.

### Should I boost a post or use Ads Manager?

Boosting is simpler and suits a first test with one strong post. Ads Manager gives more control over audience and placement. Start simple, then move to Ads Manager once you know which offer works.

### Do Instagram ads matter more than Google for salons?

They do different jobs. Search catches people looking for a salon now, while Instagram builds interest among people who may not be searching yet. Test one paid channel at a time so you can read the results.

### How long until I know whether it works?

Give the campaign two to four weeks at a steady budget, and compare cost per booked client rather than clicks or likes. Clicks alone do not show whether a salon filled its chairs.
