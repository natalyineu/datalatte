Marketing automation is software that sends a message for you when a customer does something: signs up, books, buys or goes quiet. Mailchimp describes it as software that runs predetermined campaigns across channels at scheduled intervals. For a small local business it usually means a welcome email, a booking reminder and a "we miss you" note, all written once and sent without you.

It does not find customers or write strategy. It repeats the follow-up you already know you should do. This guide covers what it does, what the tools cost, and one example with the maths shown.

## What does marketing automation actually do?

HubSpot lists four jobs: it automates repetitive tasks such as email drip campaigns, nurtures leads by linking form sign-ups to email sequences, personalises messages using customer data, and measures results so you can see which campaigns earn money.

In plain terms, a trigger starts a sequence. The trigger can be a form submission, a purchase, a booking or a date. The sequence is one or more messages with waits between them. Mailchimp's examples are a welcome email, an abandoned-cart email and a birthday message.

## Which tasks are worth automating first?

Start with messages that are the same every time and that you tend to forget:

- A welcome email when someone joins your list.
- A reminder before an appointment, with a way to reschedule.
- A short thank-you after a visit that asks for feedback.
- A "it has been a while" message to customers who have not returned.

Skip anything that needs judgement, such as handling a complaint. For ideas by trade, see our guides to [coffee shop automation](/blog/coffee-shop-marketing-automation) and [automation for restaurants](/blog/marketing-automation-for-restaurants).

## How much does marketing automation cost?

Entry prices are low. Mailchimp's pricing page lists a Free plan at $0 for up to 250 contacts without automation flows. Paid plans start at $13 a month (Essentials) and $20 a month (Standard) as introductory prices for the first 12 months. The page text we retrieved did not show the price after that period, so check it before you commit.

Constant Contact and Square pages returned errors when we tried to open them, so their prices are not quoted here. Compare tools in our [marketing automation tools guide](/blog/best-marketing-automation-tools-small-business).

| Cost item | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Software | $0 to about $20 a month at entry level (Mailchimp, 2026) |
| Setup | Your time; depends on how many sequences you build |
| Content | Writing the emails, once |
| Compliance | Unsubscribe link and postal address in every marketing email |

## What are the legal basics?

For marketing email in the US, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide requires accurate sender details, a subject line that matches the content, a clear ad disclosure, a valid postal address and an easy opt-out that you honour within 10 business days. Each non-compliant email can carry a penalty of up to $53,088.

Texting has its own consent rules that differ by country. This article does not cover them, so check the rules where you operate before sending any text campaign.

## Worked example: is a welcome email worth it?

Every input here is an **assumption**, not a benchmark. Replace them with your own numbers.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| New email sign-ups per month | 40 |
| Share who make an extra visit because of the welcome email | 10% |
| Average spend per visit | $12 |
| Gross margin on that spend | 60% |
| Software cost | $13 a month (Mailchimp Essentials intro price) |

Extra visits: 40 x 10% = 4. Extra sales: 4 x $12 = $48. Gross profit: $48 x 60% = $28.80. After the $13 software cost, the automation adds about $15.80 a month. That is small, but the email keeps working every month while the list grows. If the extra-visit share is 2%, the same setup loses money, so test before you scale.

Write one welcome email with one offer and one clear next step. Add a link to unsubscribe and your postal address, then switch it on for new sign-ups only.
</Callout>

## FAQ

### Is marketing automation only for big companies?

No. The tools have free or low-cost entry plans, and the first useful automation is a single welcome email. What big companies add is volume and data, which a small shop does not need at the start.

### Will automated emails feel robotic?

They can if the writing is generic. Write them in your own voice, mention a specific product or service, and send them from a named person rather than a no-reply address.

### Can I automate review requests?

Carefully. Google's Business Profile guidelines say you may not selectively solicit positive reviews or offer incentives for reviews. Send the same simple request to every customer. See our [Google review strategy](/blog/google-business-reviews-strategy).

### How long does setup take?

It depends on the tool and the number of sequences. Plan on drafting the messages first, then building the trigger. Keep the first version to one sequence.

### What should I measure?

Opens are a weak signal. Track the action you wanted: bookings, redemptions or repeat visits. Compare them with a period before the automation started.
