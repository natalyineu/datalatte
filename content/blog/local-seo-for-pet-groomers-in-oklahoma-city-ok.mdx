A pet groomer in Oklahoma City, OK earns a place in the Google Maps pack by keeping its Google Business Profile complete and accurate, collecting recent reviews, and making sure its name, address and phone number match everywhere they appear. The map pack is the [top three business listings in local search results](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today). This guide sets out the profile checklist, the citation and review steps, a worked review-gap calculation, and the checks that show whether the work is moving rankings.

## What does Google use to choose the map pack for a pet groomer?

The map pack is drawn from business profiles, so the profile is the starting point. One [Adwave page reports that the Google Business Profile is responsible for 32% of Map Pack ranking potential](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), but it does not name a source for that figure. A [2025 multi-location benchmark cited by Adwave puts Google Business Profile signals at 32% of local pack ranking weight](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit), again as a reported figure rather than a Google disclosure.

Adwave also states that [in-depth analysis of millions of profiles shows the primary category is the #1 ranking factor](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) for getting into the Local Pack. Treat that as a vendor claim. The practical step is the same either way: check which categories the businesses in your local results use.

Google's own help pages are the reliable reference. Google states that reviews [show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), so profile data and reviews appear together for every searcher.

## Which Google Business Profile fields should a pet groomer fill in first?

Start with the primary category. Search a service term such as "pet groomer" in your area and look at the [top three to five businesses in the map results](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones) to see which categories they use. Pick the closest match for your main service, then add secondary categories only where they describe a real service you offer.

Next, write the description. Google allows [750 characters for a business description](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack). Name the service, the Oklahoma City neighbourhood you serve, and one thing that sets you apart. Write it for a pet owner reading it on a phone. Adwave warns that [copying the same content and swapping the city name is an old-school spam tactic that Google will penalize](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), so every page and profile section should be written separately.

Then check the basics. [Hours, phone number and address should be 100% accurate](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Adwave also advises [at least 15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), which is more than the 10 it recommends in its beginner guide. Use the higher number. Finally, Google's Q&A feature, launched in Maps in 2017, is a place to answer common questions, since [responses to AI-driven answers are sourced from business profile data, website content and reviews](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a).

## Should a pet groomer in Oklahoma City set a service area?

It depends on how you work. A groomer who sees clients at a fixed salon should list the physical address. A mobile groomer or one who works from home can [hide the street address and be designated a Service Area Business](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack).

If you set a service area, define it by place, not by distance. Google's service-area guidance says [service areas should be set by city, postal code, or another area type, not by a radius](https://www.cheers.tech/geo-academy/is-google-business-profile-enough-for-ai-visibility). Earlier drafts of this kind of advice suggested a fixed radius around the shop. Use the cities, postal codes or neighbourhoods that your bookings actually come from, and check your booking records to confirm them.

Keep the physical address and service-area list consistent with your website. Adwave's guide says the website address must [match the Google Business Profile listing exactly, including punctuation and whether "St." or "Street" is used](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack).

## How many Google reviews does a pet groomer need?

There is no single official threshold. One [analysis by Blogging Wizard of 2 million Google Business Profile pages found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business). That is a third-party analysis, so use it as a planning target rather than a guarantee.

Here is a worked example. The inputs are assumptions, not measured data.

Assumption: the groomer has 22 reviews today. Assumption: it collects 3 new reviews per week from a text sent 24 hours after each appointment. Target: 200 reviews.

Weeks needed = (200 − 22) ÷ 3 = 178 ÷ 3 ≈ 60 weeks.

If collection drops to 2 reviews per week, the same target takes about 89 weeks. The calculation shows why a steady request process matters more than a one-off review push.

Reviews also carry content. Ask customers to mention the service they had, such as a puppy groom or a nervous dog. Google's review guidance and Adwave's advice both point to reviews as a route to matching searches, but the exact weighting is not published, so keep the focus on real feedback. Respond to every review, and include your service and city in the reply when it reads naturally.

## Which citations matter for a pet groomer in Oklahoma City?

Citations are listings of your name, address and phone number on other websites. Adwave advises that [the NAP must be exactly the same on your website, your Google Business Profile, and major directories like Yelp, Apple Maps, and Bing Places](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses). Those are the directories to check first.

Apple Maps deserves a separate check. Adwave reports that [Apple Business Connect has captured a 7% share of a maps-only comparison and is growing](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), though the page's usage figures are not attributed to a named source. Claim your Apple listing, match the details exactly, and use Apple's own reporting to track it.

Before you build new listings, fix the ones you have. Adwave cites a landscaping firm that [cleaned up its NAP data and saw an 18% jump in local pack appearances](https://accountabilitynow.net/ai-google-business-profile-ranking-for-landscapers/), but that is one anecdote from a different trade. Treat the fix as worth doing and the number as unverified.

## Does the website still matter for map rankings?

Yes, because it is one of the sources Google draws on for profile answers. The website should repeat the same name, address, phone number and services shown on the profile, and it should link to a location page that embeds your map. For the embed steps, see [how to add a Google Maps embed to a local business website](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website).

Service pages should be written for real customers. If you offer mobile grooming, give it its own page and its own profile service item. Keep the wording natural. Adwave's warning about city-name swapping applies here too.

## Which metrics show whether local SEO is working?

Track the numbers that reflect real customer actions. Adwave names [Google Business Profile impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent) as the useful measures. Check them monthly in your profile's insights panel and keep a simple log.

Pick a short list of service searches and check your position for each. Search them in a private browser window from a few points across your area, and note whether you appear in the top three. Log the date and the results so you can compare month to month.

## How do you check your map pack position across Oklahoma City?

A single search from your shop shows only one spot. Adwave's grid-rank guide uses a 7×7 grid scan to [check map-pack visibility across residential suburbs beyond the city-center check](https://adwave.com/resources/geo-grid-rank-tracking). Its example is an illustrative dentist, but the method applies to a groomer. Place a grid of points over your service area, run the same search from each point, and record where you appear.

The result shows you the gaps. If you rank well near the shop but not in the suburbs you serve, the fix is usually a profile with better service-area coverage and reviews from those areas, not a broader keyword list.

## How often should a pet groomer post and upload photos?

Adwave advises [at least one new post a week](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers) to keep the profile active. Posts only appear publicly once they are reviewed and marked Live. Google states that [a post is shown on Search and Maps only when its status is Live](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), so check the status after you publish.

Google Posts are [free mini-billboards on your Business Profile in Search and Maps](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Use them for seasonal grooming, new services and openings. You can manage messages from the [dashboard on a desktop computer](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-messaging-should-you-turn-it-on) as well as the mobile app, which helps when you are managing several locations.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a pet groomer?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as a [free Business Profile on Google Search and Maps](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/). Paid options such as ads are separate.

### How many photos should I upload to my profile?

Adwave recommends [at least 15 high-quality photos](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers). Update the set over time with new work and seasonal shots.

### Do I need a listing on Apple Maps as well as Google?

It is worth checking. Adwave says Apple Business Connect gives access to [Apple Maps, Siri and Wallet users](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), and its usage figures are unattributed. Claim the listing and keep the details identical to your Google profile.

### How long does local SEO take to show results?

No official timeline exists. Check the platform's live help pages for current guidance, and track profile views and map position monthly to see your own trend.
