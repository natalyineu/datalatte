A fitness studio social media strategy has four parts: pick the one or two platforms your members use, post a steady mix of class, coach and member content, make one clear offer (usually a trial class), and measure trial bookings, not likes. Pew's 2026 data shows Instagram reaches 79% of US adults aged 18 to 29 and 64% of those aged 30 to 49. Facebook leads in the 30 to 49 group at 78%.

This guide covers where your audience is, how much paid reach costs, a funnel example labelled as an assumption, and the numbers to track. Sources are listed at the end.

## Where Is Your Audience?

Pew Research's 2026 survey (5,511 US adults, fieldwork January 30 to June 18, 2026) gives usage by age. The Health and Fitness Association reports about 81 million Americans belonged to a fitness facility in 2025, up 5.2%, and members aged 18 to 24 had the highest penetration at 35.5%.

| Platform | Ages 18-29 | Ages 30-49 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | 95% | 91% |
| Instagram | 79% | 64% |
| Facebook | 69% | 78% |
| TikTok | 65% | 45% |

If your members are mostly in their twenties, lead with Instagram and add TikTok. If they are in their thirties and forties, run Instagram and Facebook together. Pick two platforms and do them well. ## What Should a Studio Post?

Use a few repeatable content types so you never start from a blank page. The mix below is a suggestion, not a tested formula:

1. **Class clips:** 15 to 30 seconds of a real session, with the class name and time
2. **Coach moments:** one tip or one movement explained
3. **Member stories:** only with written permission, and disclose any free membership or perk given in return (see the FTC note below)
4. **Offer posts:** the trial class, with one booking link
5. **Schedule posts:** this week's classes and any open spots

More examples and a 4-week plan are in our [fitness studio social media content guide](/blog/fitness-studio-social-media-content) and the [Reels guide](/blog/fitness-studio-marketing-with-instagram-reels).

## Do You Have to Disclose Member Posts?

If you give a member free classes, a discount or another perk for posting, the FTC says that connection generally needs a clear disclosure in each post. Advertisers are responsible for endorsements made on their behalf, and the FTC advises training and monitoring the people who post for you. Hashtags that are ambiguous and disclosures buried in comments are generally inadequate. The guidance is not a safe harbor, so ask a lawyer if you run an ambassador programme.

## What Does Paid Social Cost?

LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmarks (updated September 23, 2026) list Health and Fitness lead campaigns at $1.84 per click, a 7.98% conversion rate and $27.11 per lead. Traffic campaigns cost $0.64 per click. Google Search in the same category, per WordStream by LocaliQ (June 2026), costs $6.17 per click and $67.36 per lead.

A lead is a form fill, not a member. Studio-specific rows do not exist, so these are category averages. For setup details read [Facebook ads for gyms and fitness studios](/blog/facebook-ads-for-gyms-fitness-studios-complete-guide).

## What Does a Trial-Class Funnel Look Like? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions** except the cost per lead, which is the LocaliQ category average. Replace them with your own.

| Step | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $400 |
| Cost per lead | $27.11 (benchmark) |
| Leads | about 15 |
| Leads who book a trial (assumption) | 40% = 6 |
| Trial attendees who show (assumption) | 70% = 4.2 |
| Attendees who join (assumption) | 40% = 1.7 |
| Cost per new member | about $235 |
| Membership price (assumption) | $120 per month |
| Months before cancelling (assumption) | 6 |
| Revenue per new member | $720 |

On these inputs one new member returns about three times the ad cost. Change the join rate to 15% and the cost per member rises above $600, near the revenue. Follow-up speed and trial quality decide the result, not the ad. This uses paid social on top of organic posts; the organic work stays free.

## Which Numbers Should You Track?

Keep it to five numbers each month:

- Trial bookings by source (ask at sign-up)
- Show rate for trial classes
- Join rate after a trial
- Cost per new member if you pay for ads
- Cancellations by month

Followers and likes are not on the list. Add a booking link in your bio and a source question to your form. The [Facebook ads strategy for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-facebook-ads-strategy) shows how to set up a test.

## FAQ

### Which platform is best for a fitness studio?

By age, Instagram for under 30s and Facebook plus Instagram for 30 to 49s. Check your own member list before choosing.

### How often should a studio post?

No source proves an ideal number. Choose a rhythm you can keep for three months and review the trial bookings.

### Is paid social necessary?

Not at the start. Organic posts and a Google Business Profile cost only time. Add paid once you know your trial offer converts.

### What is a good cost per lead?

LocaliQ's 2026 average for Health and Fitness Facebook lead campaigns is $27.11. Your cost per member matters more than cost per lead.

### Can I repost member photos?

With permission, yes. If a member gets a perk for posting, the FTC expects a clear disclosure.
