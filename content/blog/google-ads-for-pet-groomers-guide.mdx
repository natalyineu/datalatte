$600 a month at a $4.06 click buys about 148 clicks. That click price is LocaliQ's 2026 average for the Animals & Pets category. Whether those 148 clicks fill 3 grooming slots or 14 depends on three things: how many clicks turn into calls or forms, how many of those enquiries you actually book, and whether your calendar has room when they arrive. This guide covers the last two, which most Google Ads articles skip.

It is written for a solo or small-team groomer in the US, UK, Australia or Canada, whether you run a salon or a van. It is a 30-day plan: set up tracking first, launch small, sort the leads by quality, then decide. For full keyword lists and city-level budgets, see our [complete 2026 Google Ads guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-ads-for-pet-groomers-complete-2026-guide), [keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-pet-groomers-in-2026) and [budget guide](/blog/how-much-should-a-pet-groomer-spend-on-google-ads). This article is about the first month and about proving that ads produce bookings, not just clicks.

## Why a Groomer's Google Ads Problem Is Different

A gym or a plumber can usually absorb more leads by hiring. A solo groomer cannot. You have a fixed number of table hours a week, and a dog takes anywhere from 45 minutes to three hours. Two things follow:

- **Capacity is your ceiling.** There is no point buying 20 enquiries a month if you have 6 open slots. Decide your slot target before you set a budget.
- **Lead quality matters more than lead count.** A call from someone two towns away, or asking about a breed you do not take, uses up your time and your tracking data.

The upside is repeat business. The American Kennel Club says grooming frequency depends on breed, coat length and type, how dirty the dog gets and the climate, with visits ranging from every six weeks to three times a year. So a new client is worth several visits, not one. But how many visits you really get is something only your own booking history can tell you.

## Before You Spend: Four Things to Fix First

### 1. Know your slot target

Count the open appointments you want to fill next month. If you want 8, and half of your enquiries typically book (your number, not a benchmark), you need about 16 enquiries. Work backward from that.

### 2. Count the right actions

Google's [help page on website call conversions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) says the tracking counts a call as a conversion "when it lasts longer than a minimum length you set." It does not give a default length, so choose one that reflects a real enquiry, such as 45 to 60 seconds (that range is our suggestion). The same page says the standard tag tracks only one number per page and that Google Tag Manager is not recommended if you use dynamic number insertion or third-party call tracking.

For calls from the ad itself, Google's [calls from ads page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095882) says new calls are counted under the default action "Calls from ads", that you must turn on call reporting in account settings, and that calls must come through a call asset or location asset with a verified number. It also says Google can use AI on call recordings to flag high-quality leads, but that recording is limited to calls where both parties are in the US or Canada. If you are in the UK or Australia, plan on duration-based counting plus your own log. Our guides on [conversion tracking setup](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) and [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) walk through the steps.

Set up three conversion actions: ad calls, website calls, and your booking form or confirmation page.

### 3. Build one page for one offer

Send ads to a single page, not your home page: the service and town in the headline, one button to book, a phone number that works with a tap, your real photos, your rating, your prices as ranges, and what breeds or services you do not take. That last item filters out poor-fit leads before they call. See [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices).

### 4. Make a lead log

A plain spreadsheet works. One row per enquiry with: date, source (call or form), service asked about, town, and an outcome: **booked**, **quote only**, **out of area**, **not a fit**, or **no answer**. You will use this in week 4.

## Setting Up the Campaign for a Small Budget

**One campaign, one location.** Pull your last 50 clients' postcodes or zip codes and set a radius that covers most of them. Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722043) says radius targeting needs at least 1 km around a location and recommends broad geo targeting ("presence or interest") as a best practice on Search, but also notes that "presence" makes sense when you want only users physically in specific locations. For a groomer who cannot serve people two towns away, we recommend presence only. That is our judgment, not a Google rule. Mobile groomers should keep the radius to the area they will really drive.

**Phrase and exact match.** Google's [match type help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) says phrase match shows ads on searches that include the meaning of your keyword, and exact match on searches with the same meaning or intent. It also says it is critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match. With a small budget and little conversion data, skip broad match in month 1.

**Three tight ad groups:**

| Ad group | Example keywords | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core grooming | "dog groomer near me", [dog grooming your town] | Highest intent |
| Your specialty | "doodle grooming near me", "puppy first groom", "senior dog groomer" | Fewer clicks, better fit |
| Brand | [your salon name] | Cheap and protects your name |

**Negatives from day one:** `school`, `course`, `jobs`, `hiring`, `diy`, `at home`, `supplies`, `clippers`, `how to`, `free`. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/negative-keywords-google-ads) explains campaign-level lists.

**Bidding in month 1.** Google's [learning period page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13020501) says the learning period depends on the number of conversions, the length of your conversion cycles and the bid strategy, that Manual CPC has no learning period, and that it can take a few conversion cycles (1 to 2 typically) for a strategy to calibrate. Its [Maximize conversions page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7381968) says you must have conversion tracking set up and that the strategy tries to fully spend your average daily budget. At a handful of enquiries a month, there is little for automation to learn from, so a sensible start is manual CPC with a cap, then a switch to Maximize conversions once you have steady results. That sequencing is our suggestion.

**Ad schedule that matches your phone.** Google's [ad schedule help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6372656) says ads are only eligible to show during the times you list, up to 6 schedules per day per campaign, in the account's time zone. If you cannot take calls while a dog is on the table, either show ads when you can answer or push people to a booking form at other times.

## The 30-Day Plan

| Days | Focus | Do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 7 | Launch small | Ads live, tracking tested with a real call and a real form |
| 8 to 14 | Clean the traffic | Read the search terms report, add negatives, pause keywords with spend and no enquiries |
| 15 to 21 | Fix the leaks | Rewrite the page or ad if clicks come but enquiries do not; fill the lead log |
| 22 to 30 | Decide | Cost per booked client against your break-even, then scale, hold or stop |

Do not judge on day 5. With 148 clicks a month, a handful of enquiries can swing results hugely either way.

## The Math

All figures below are **assumptions** for illustration, except the $4.06 click price (LocaliQ 2026 average) and the 16.22% rate used in the Strong column (LocaliQ's category average). The budget is $600 a month.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per click | $4.06 | $4.06 | $4.06 |
| Clicks | 148 | 148 | 148 |
| Click to enquiry rate (assumption) | 6% | 10% | 16.22% |
| Enquiries | 8.9 | 14.8 | 24.0 |
| Cost per enquiry | $67 | $41 | $25 |
| Enquiry to booked rate (assumption) | 35% | 50% | 60% |
| New clients booked | 3.1 | 7.4 | 14.4 |
| Ad cost per new client | $193 | $81 | $42 |
| Average groom price (assumption) | $85 | $85 | $85 |
| Visits per new client in year one (assumption) | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| First-year revenue per new client | $255 | $255 | $255 |
| Ad cost as a share of first-year revenue | 76% | 32% | 16% |

How to read it: revenue is not profit. If your costs of running a table (your time, supplies, rent) take an assumed 60% of revenue, a $255 client leaves about $102. In the Tough column you lose money on the first year; in Base you earn it back and keep a repeat client. And the Strong column books 14 new clients, more than a solo groomer may have room for, so a lower budget or a paused campaign would be right.

**Your break-even click price.** Maximum CPC = the most you will pay per new client x enquiry rate x booking rate. If you will pay $85 for a new client, at 10% and 50% your ceiling is $85 x 0.10 x 0.50 = $4.25 per click. Above that, you lose money on the average click.

**The cheapest lever** is the booking rate, not the click. Going from 35% to 60% of enquiries booked lowers cost per client by more than 40% without touching the account. That means answering fast, returning missed calls the same day and having online booking that works on a phone.

## Week 4: Read the Log, Then Decide

Open your lead log and sort by outcome. Look for patterns:

- **Many "out of area":** shrink the radius or switch to presence only.
- **Many "not a fit":** add the breed or service as a negative keyword and say it on the page.
- **Many "no answer":** fix your hours and ad schedule, or add a form.
- **Many "quote only":** show price ranges earlier on the page.

Optional, for later: Google's [offline conversion import help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7012522) says you need auto-tagging on, a script that captures the GCLID from the landing page URL and stores it with each lead, and that the GCLID is case sensitive. It describes a 90-day window for several data sources and 14 days for some CRM sources. That is more setup than most solo groomers need in month 1, but it lets you feed "booked and arrived" back to Google so bidding learns from real clients, not just calls.

## Do This Today

1. Count your open slots for next month and write down the number of new clients you want. Use it to pick a budget.
2. Pull your last 50 clients' zip or postcodes and draw your ad radius from that.
3. Set up three conversion actions (ad calls, website calls, booking form) with a minimum call length you chose, then test each with a real call and form.
4. Create the lead log spreadsheet with the five outcomes from this article.
5. Launch one campaign with the three ad groups, phrase and exact match, the negative list, and an ad schedule that matches when you answer the phone.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a solo groomer spend on Google Ads in the first month?**
A: There is no official figure. The math above uses $600 as an assumption; at $4.06 a click that buys about 148 clicks. Choose a budget from your slot target and your break-even click price, not from a benchmark.

**Q: Should I use Performance Max or Smart campaigns?**
A: For a first month with a small budget, a plain Search campaign gives you the search terms report and control over which keywords you pay for. Test other types once you have steady tracked bookings.

**Q: Why am I getting calls but no bookings?**
A: Check the lead log. The usual causes are out-of-area callers, price shoppers, breeds you do not take and missed calls. Each has a fix: radius, price ranges on the page, negatives and an ad schedule.

**Q: Can I count phone calls as conversions?**
A: Yes. Google counts a call as a conversion when it lasts longer than a minimum length you set. Set a length that matches a real enquiry, and keep a manual log to see which calls became bookings.

**Q: How long until I know if it works?**
A: Enquiries can arrive in days, but judge after about 30 days and 100 or more clicks. The 30-day mark is our rule of thumb, not a Google figure.
