This guide is for owners of cafes, salons, studios and service firms in towns such as Manchester, Nashua, Concord and Portsmouth. Every figure comes from a source listed at the end. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## How Do You Keep Ads Inside Your Own Town?

Location settings decide how much of your budget is wasted. Google's help page says radius targeting shows ads to people within a set distance of a location, with a minimum radius of 1 km. A very small radius can make ads show only now and then.

Google also recommends the "Presence or interest" setting for Search campaigns, which reaches people who have shown interest in your area even when they are elsewhere. "Presence" reaches only people physically in the area.

For a shop in Nashua or Salem, think about which one you want. A restaurant near the Massachusetts line may want visitors who search for the town before driving up. A salon that only serves regulars may prefer Presence.

## What Is the Cross-Border Opportunity with Massachusetts?

New Hampshire has no general sales tax, and shoppers from nearby Massachusetts towns do cross the line. No public source gives the share of customers at border businesses, so check yours: ask at the till, or look at the postcodes in your booking system.

Be careful with tax claims in ads. Massachusetts can charge use tax on some out-of-state purchases, and the rules differ by item. Do not advertise "save on tax" until you have read the Massachusetts and New Hampshire revenue department guidance for your product.

A safer message is a practical one: easy parking, hours, and the fastest route from the highway.

## How Do You Get Found on Google Maps?

For most local firms the free Maps listing does more work than ads. Google says local ranking rests on relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot change distance, and Google states there is no way to pay for a better local ranking.

What you can control, per Google's help:

- Verify the profile and keep address, hours, category and attributes current.
- Add photos and videos of what you offer.
- Collect reviews and reply to them.
- Show in-store products if you run a retail store.

## How Should You Plan for Tourism and Seasons?

Foliage and ski seasons bring visitors to the White Mountains, the Lakes Region and the Seacoast. No source in this article measures how much they lift a particular business, so use your own data. Compare last year's monthly calls or bookings, then raise budgets in the months that already perform.

Google Ads lets you schedule ads and set bid adjustments for specific days and times. Use that to push spend toward the hours your phone rings, once you have a few weeks of call data.

Publish seasonal offers on your Business Profile as well. They cost nothing and show up where people compare nearby options. For another northeast example, see [marketing in New Jersey](/blog/small-business-marketing-new-jersey).

## Does Meta Advertising Work in New Hampshire?

Meta suits awareness and repeat visits more than urgent searches. Use it to remind past customers and people who visited your site, and keep a single clear offer per ad.

Meta does not publish a state-level CPM, and no verified New Hampshire figure exists, so set a small test budget and read the cost per result in Ads Manager after two to four weeks. Our guide to [Meta ads for local businesses](/blog/local-business-meta-ads) covers audiences and creative.

## How Do You Track Whether It Pays?

Count calls, not just clicks. Google Ads help describes five phone call conversion types, including calls from ads and calls to a forwarding number on your site. You set the minimum call length yourself, and every call at least that long counts as a conversion.

Tag every link with UTM parameters. Google Analytics help says to always use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, and notes that values are case sensitive, so "google" and "Google" count as different sources. Our [UTM guide](/blog/utm-parameters-track-every-marketing-campaign) has a naming template.

## FAQ

### How much should a New Hampshire small business spend on Google Ads?

No public figure exists for the state. With the all-industry average of $5.42 per click, $600 a month buys roughly 110 clicks. Start with an amount you can lose while you learn, then adjust to your cost per customer.

### Is Google Business Profile worth the effort?

Yes, because it is free and sits in the results people use to compare nearby businesses. Google says relevance, distance and prominence decide local ranking, and reviews and complete information feed prominence and relevance.

### Should I target Massachusetts shoppers?

Only if your own data shows they visit. Check postcodes in your bookings, then test a small campaign with "Presence or interest" targeting. Avoid tax-saving claims until you have checked the rules.

### Does Meta work for local businesses?

It can, mainly for reminders and offers. No verified state CPM exists, so measure cost per result in Ads Manager for a few weeks before scaling.

### How do I know which ads bring customers?

Track call conversions, tag links with UTM parameters, and ask new customers how they found you. Compare results monthly.
