For most local businesses, Google Business Profile matters more than Yelp because it appears where people already start searching. A homeowner looking for an emergency plumber or a pet owner looking for a groomer usually meets Google first. Yelp still has a role, mainly when a customer is comparing options close to a decision. The useful question is not which platform is better in general. It is which one reaches your customer when they are ready to call, and what each resulting lead costs.

## Where Do Google Business Profile and Yelp Reach Customers?

Google is built around discovery. A customer types a problem such as "emergency plumber" or "dog groomer near me," and businesses that match the search appear. [iCatch Group](https://www.icatchgroup.com/google-ads-vs-yelp-ads/) describes Google as capturing customers earlier in the buying process, while Yelp often reaches people closer to a purchase decision.

Yelp users are often comparing. They check ratings, photos, and reviews before they contact anyone. That makes Yelp useful for a business that already has strong reviews and wants to win a place on a shortlist. It is less useful when the goal is to be found by someone who has not yet heard of the business.

A Google Business Profile listing sits inside the same search results that bring in paid traffic, so it is the first place to check. The profile controls the basics: hours, service areas, photos, and reviews. If those are incomplete, no ad budget on either platform fixes the gap. The [Google Business Profile optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) lists the fields to complete before any spending.

## How Much Do Yelp Ads Cost?

Yelp advertising does not have one fixed monthly price. According to [iCatch Group](https://www.icatchgroup.com/google-ads-vs-yelp-ads/), Yelp ads generally run on a pay-per-click model, where you set a budget and pay when a prospective customer engages with the ad. Costs change with industry, local competition, season, and search volume.

A plumber in a dense metro area may pay a very different click price than a florist in a small city. A quoted cost per click tells you the price of traffic, not the price of a customer. The more useful measure is what it costs to acquire one paying client, which requires tracking calls, booked appointments, and closed jobs from each platform.

Google Ads also uses pay-per-click, but Local Services Ads use a pay-per-lead model, as [Google's small business marketing guide](https://business.google.com/us/resources/articles/small-business-marketing/) explains. When comparing platforms, check which billing model is in play. A per-click price and a per-lead price are different units.

## Is a Cheaper Click Always the Better Lead?

No. A low cost per click can still produce a higher cost per customer. The worked example below uses assumed inputs, and none of the figures come from a published benchmark.

Inputs (all assumptions for a plumbing business):

- Monthly budget: $600
- Platform A click cost: $6, with 2% of clicks becoming a booked job
- Platform B click cost: $40, with 15% of clicks becoming a booked job

| Scenario | Clicks from $600 | Booked jobs | Cost per booked job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform A ($6 clicks, 2% convert) | 100 | 2.00 | $300.00 |
| Platform B ($40 clicks, 15% convert) | 15 | 2.25 | $266.67 |

Platform B looks expensive on clicks but costs less per booked job in this scenario. Cost per booked job equals the click cost divided by the conversion rate, so Platform B only stays cheaper while its conversion rate is above 13.3%. Below that rate, the $40 click costs more per job than the $6 click does at 2%. Test your own rates before deciding.

Track three numbers each month: cost per click, booked jobs per click, and cost per booked job. Those three figures show whether a cheaper platform is actually cheaper.

## When Should a Small Business Use Google Business Profile First?

Start with Google Business Profile for most local service businesses. It is where customers search for a category and a location, and it connects directly to map results and the business website. A profile with accurate categories, current photos, recent reviews, and regular posts gives a base before any paid media is added.

Profile needs differ by business type. The [pet groomer profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-for-pet-groomers) covers appointment-based bookings, while the [coffee shop profile guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization) focuses on menu photos and opening hours.

Paid Google campaigns are a separate decision. The [Google Ads management guide](/blog/google-ads-management-small-business-what-to-expect) explains what a managed account involves before committing budget. For service businesses that want pay-per-lead, [Local Services Ads](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) is the relevant option to compare against Yelp's per-click model.

## When Is Yelp Still Worth a Budget Line?

Yelp earns a budget when customers in your category actively use it and you can respond to reviews consistently. Restaurants in dense urban areas and businesses serving younger, review-driven audiences are common cases where Yelp activity is high. Check your own category and city before deciding either way.

Do not run Yelp ads just because a competitor does. Run a small test with a fixed budget, track calls and bookings, and compare the cost per booked job against Google results. If the numbers do not separate, keep Yelp as a free profile and put the money into the channel that performs.

Never remove or edit negative reviews to improve a rating. Respond professionally and let the record stand. Deleting reviews can break platform policies and damage trust with customers who read the response.

## How Do You Test Both Without Wasting Time?

Use a short, fixed test rather than an open-ended experiment. The steps below take about one month per platform.

1. Complete the Google Business Profile and confirm categories, hours, and service area.
2. Assign a separate tracking number or call-tracking line to each platform.
3. Record monthly calls, booked jobs, and spend for Google and Yelp separately.
4. Run a fixed Yelp test budget for 30 days, then compare cost per booked job.
5. Keep the platform with the lower cost per booked job and pause the other paid option.
6. Review the numbers every 90 days and adjust budgets only when the data supports it.

## FAQ

### Should I delete my Yelp page if Google drives more traffic?

No. Keep the page, respond to reviews, and update basic details a few times a year. A missing or outdated listing can make a customer assume a business is closed or no longer active. Put most daily effort into the profile that brings in the most calls.

### Can I pay Google to rank my Google Business Profile higher?

Organic profile ranking is not bought with ad spend. Local visibility depends on accurate profile information, categories, reviews, photos, and activity. Paid options such as Google Ads or Local Services Ads run separately and have their own billing models.

### Is a $6 click always cheaper than a $40 click?

No. A cheaper click only matters if it produces booked jobs at a reasonable rate. The worked example shows a $40 click can produce a lower cost per booked job when its conversion rate is high enough. Measure cost per booked job for each platform before choosing.

### How long should I test Yelp ads before deciding?

Run a fixed budget for at least 30 days and compare results against a stable baseline. Short tests can be distorted by seasonal swings or a few unusually strong weeks. Track calls and booked jobs, not just clicks, so the decision rests on outcomes.
