A small shop should send one or two cart recovery emails, starting within hours, to shoppers who gave their email at checkout and then left. Baymard Institute's review of 50 studies puts the average cart abandonment rate at 70.22%, so most carts never become orders. Some of those shoppers were only browsing, and some hit a fixable problem such as extra costs.

This guide is for local shops with an online store, such as a bakery, pet supply shop or salon retail range. It covers why people leave, how to set up recovery emails on common platforms, a sequence outline and a worked example labelled as an assumption. Recovery rates are not published by the platforms below, so none are quoted.

## Why Do Shoppers Abandon Their Carts?

Baymard lists the reasons shoppers gave, after setting aside the 42% who were just browsing. Extra costs such as shipping, tax and fees were the top reason at 40%. Slow delivery followed at 20%, then not trusting the site with card details at 19%, forced account creation at 18%, and a long or complicated checkout at 17%.

Many of these are fixed on the site, not by email. Show the full price early, offer guest checkout and keep the form short. A recovery email helps shoppers who were interrupted, but it cannot repair a checkout that frightens people. ## How Do You Set Up Cart Recovery Emails?

Setup depends on your platform. On Shopify, recovery emails apply to the Online Store and Buy Button channels. You turn on automatic emails in the Shopify Messaging app under Automations, choose which visitors receive them and how long after abandonment they send. Each email includes a link to the cart. Checkouts older than three months are deleted automatically.

Shopify lists cases where no email goes out, such as when the shopper completes the sale first or enters a phone number instead of an email. Shopify's page does not state which plans include the feature, so check your plan.

Constant Contact's help article describes a reminder for Shopify or WooCommerce stores. It goes to shoppers who reach the shipping step or later and leave. It warns that Shopify's own abandoned checkout emails can overlap, and tells you to turn Shopify's version off to avoid duplicates. Constant Contact also says the feature is not on every plan.

## What Should the Emails Say?

Keep it plain. Remind the shopper what is in the cart, show the picture and price, and link back to it. If shipping cost was the usual reason for leaving, say what shipping costs. Avoid pressure language.

- **Email 1, within hours:** the cart contents and a link back. No discount.
- **Email 2, a day or two later (optional):** answer likely worries, such as delivery times and returns policy.
- **Email 3 (optional):** a small incentive only if your margins allow it.

The exact delay is your choice. The platform pages above do not publish an ideal timing, so test two options. Offering a discount to everyone teaches people to abandon carts on purpose, so use incentives sparingly. ## What Rules Apply?

A cart recovery email is a commercial message, so CAN-SPAM applies in the US. The FTC says messages need accurate header information, a non-deceptive subject line, a physical postal address and a working opt-out. Opt-outs must be honoured within 10 business days. Each violating email can carry a penalty of up to $53,088.

Google also requires bulk senders (5,000 or more messages a day to Gmail) to authenticate with SPF, DKIM and DMARC and to offer one-click unsubscribe. A small shop is unlikely to reach that volume, but authenticated sending helps deliverability. In the UK and EU, check consent rules in our [GDPR email guide](/blog/gdpr-compliant-email-marketing).

## What Could It Be Worth? A Worked Example

All numbers are **assumptions**, chosen for easy arithmetic. They are not benchmarks.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly checkouts started | 200 |
| Abandonment rate | 70.22% (Baymard average) |
| Abandoned carts with an email address | 60% |
| Recovery email recovers | 5% of those |
| Average order | $40 |

200 checkouts at 70.22% gives about 140 abandoned carts. With an email on 60% of them, 84 shoppers receive the message. At 5% recovery, that is about 4 orders, or $160 a month. At that size the value is modest. It grows with traffic, and the setup cost on most platforms is little once connected, but the real fix for a 70% abandonment rate is usually the checkout itself.

## FAQ

### How many abandoned cart emails should I send?

Platforms do not publish an ideal number. Constant Contact describes one reminder, and many shops use two or three. Start with one and add a second if unsubscribes stay low.

### Should I include a discount?

Not in the first email. Baymard finds extra costs are the top reason for leaving, so showing shipping clearly may do more than a code. Test a discount on the last email only.

### Do I need the shopper's consent?

Under CAN-SPAM you do not need prior consent but you must give a clear opt-out. In the UK and EU, consent rules are stricter. Check your platform's settings for marketing consent.

### What if my customers buy in the shop, not online?

Cart recovery only applies to online checkouts. For local sales, use a welcome series or booking reminders. See our [welcome email series template](/blog/welcome-email-series-local-business-5-email-template).

### How do I know the emails work?

Your platform reports emails sent and revenue recovered, as Constant Contact notes. Compare orders from recovery emails against the abandoned carts they were sent to.
