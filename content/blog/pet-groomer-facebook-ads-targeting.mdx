Facebook offers around a dozen campaign objectives. A 2016 guide for groomers found that a small budget spread across a huge audience gives each person very few views, so a tighter audience usually works better. ## How Big Is the Local Market for Groomers?

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics counts 413,000 animal care and service workers in 2025. It projects employment in the group to grow 11% from 2025 to 2035, which it describes as much faster than the average for all occupations ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/animal-care-and-service-workers.htm)). The median annual wage was $35,530 in 2025, or $17.08 an hour. Groomers sit within this group alongside trainers and pet sitters.

Growth means more competitors for the same local searches and feeds. For a single shop, that is a reason to target by place. A broad campaign pays for impressions that cannot turn into a booking.

Wage data describes workers, not prices. It should not be used to set service fees or an ad budget.

## Which Targeting Settings Should a Groomer Set First?

Start with location, then pet ownership, then page exclusion. The 2016 guide advises beginning with the geographic area where most business already comes from, and expanding only once the campaign is running ([Groomer to Groomer](https://www.groomertogroomer.com/reaching-more-customers-with-facebook-ads/), secondary). It also treats pet ownership as a vital part of the audience for a groomer.

For brand awareness campaigns, the same guide suggests excluding people who already like your page, so ad money is not spent on existing customers. Save the audience once it is built so it can be reused.

This guide could not confirm current Meta rules on which settings are hard limits and which are only suggestions. The Meta Business Help page cited for this check returned a 404 error, so check Ads Manager before relying on any setting.

## How Wide Should the Location Radius Be?

The right radius depends on how clients reach the business. The 2016 guide gives one example: a shop whose customers live within about 12 miles, where the owner chose a smaller, defined group rather than a large one. It warns that a 20-mile radius with 150,000 pet owners can spread a limited budget so thin that each person sees the ad fewer than 1.5 times ([Groomer to Groomer](https://www.groomertogroomer.com/reaching-more-customers-with-facebook-ads/), secondary).

| Business type | Target area | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salon groomer | Towns or radius clients drive to | Clients come to the shop |
| Mobile groomer | Postcodes or towns on the van route | Only route areas can book |
| Salon with retail sales | Core radius, expand later | Start where most sales happen |

A mobile groomer should avoid the whole metro area. Impressions in streets the van never reaches cost money without any chance of a booking.

## What Should the Ad Creative Say?

Targeting does part of the work, but the image and text decide who clicks. The Love Groomers guide recommends before-and-after transformation photos, challenging cases such as matted coats, and a clear call to action with a booking link ([Love Groomers](https://www.lovegroomers.com/blog/marketing-your-grooming-business-with-social-media/), secondary). It also advises getting permission from owners before sharing pet photos, with a photo release in the intake form.

Name the coat type or the problem in the copy. A line about a double coat or a puppy's first groom tells the right owner the ad is meant for them, without relying on an interest label.

A free Google Business Profile is the other half of the path. Gingr's guide lists accurate hours, quality photos, a full service list and regular customer reviews as the basics ([Gingr](https://www.gingrapp.com/blog/pet-grooming-advertising-ideas-to-grow-your-business), vendor blog). ## How Do You Know the Targeting Is Working?

Use a short checklist each week:

1. Break down clicks and bookings by location to see whether they come from inside the intended area.
2. Ask each new client how they found the business, and record the answer.
3. Keep existing clients out of brand-awareness audiences.
4. Save each working audience so it can be reused.
5. If clicks arrive but nobody books, change the offer or the booking page, not the audience.

The 2016 guide notes that remarketing, which follows website visitors across Facebook, needs some developer knowledge to set up tracking ([Groomer to Groomer](https://www.groomertogroomer.com/reaching-more-customers-with-facebook-ads/), secondary). If that is beyond the team, start with location and creative, and add remarketing later. Choosing between channels also helps. ## FAQ

### What location setting should a pet groomer use for Facebook ads?

Use the towns or radius clients actually travel to. A salon can use a radius, and a mobile groomer should use the postcodes or towns on the route. Avoid the whole metro area.

### What ad frequency suggests a budget is too thin?

The 2016 guide says frequency can fall below 1.5 when a limited budget is spread across a very large audience. Narrowing the audience raises the number of views per person.

### Is a 20-mile radius too big for a salon?

It can be, if the budget is small. The guide's example used about 12 miles for a shop whose customers mostly live nearby. Check where clients come from before choosing a radius.

### Can a groomer use before and after photos in ads?

Yes, the Love Groomers guide recommends them. Get permission from the pet owner first and include a photo release in the intake form.
