## Which Local Search Habits Apply in Sweden?

The source behind these figures is a 2018 post, first published on 22 November 2018 and updated in August 2026. Its survey data comes from the Consumer Barometer 2014/15 and 2017 editions. The numbers are best read as direction, not as current measurement of Swedish behaviour.

The 72% figure is a US statistic reported by WordStream. The author argues that similar conclusions probably apply to Sweden, but the source does not measure that for Sweden. A useful reading is that local searchers expect to find a business online before they visit it, so the listing has to answer their questions first.

The source names the information people look for: opening hours, prices and product availability. It argues that accurate details of this kind can decide whether a new customer chooses one business over a competitor.

## How Much of the Swedish Market Is Online?

The same source puts internet use at 95%, mobile device use at 44% and Google use for local information at 77%. These figures come from survey data, not from a current count of Swedish users.

For a small business, the practical point is that most people in a catchment can be reached online, but a mobile-first experience matters. Pages should load quickly and be easy to navigate on a phone. The source notes that visitors leave a slow or confusing page before they read it, so speed and clarity affect enquiries directly.

## Does Distance Change Where Swedish Customers Buy?

The source argues that distance matters and that consumers prefer to buy close to where they are. It recommends geo-targeted marketing so that spending reaches nearby people who are willing to buy, which also helps the marketing budget go further.

Geo-targeting can run through Google listings, social media ads, display ads or search advertising. The source lists these channels but gives no Swedish performance data for any of them, so the right mix depends on the business's own tests.

## Should You Use Search Ads for Local Reach?

Search matters because customers use it to choose where to buy. The source recommends both search engine optimisation and search engine marketing to appear as high as possible in local results.

Paid search can start small. Compare the cost of a click with the value of an enquiry before scaling. The [Performance Max guide for local businesses](/blog/google-performance-max-for-local-business-complete-guide-2026) and the [Local Services Ads review](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) explain when paid search fits a local budget, and the principles carry over to Sweden.

## How Do You Work Out Cost per Enquiry? A Worked Example

No Swedish click costs or conversion rates appear in the sources checked, so every input below is an **assumption**. Replace them with figures from a real test.

A Stockholm café plans a search campaign with these inputs:

- Monthly budget: 10,000 SEK (assumption)
- Average cost per click: 8 SEK (assumption)
- Share of clicks that become an enquiry or booking: 5% (assumption)

Clicks: 10,000 ÷ 8 = 1,250 clicks per month. Enquiries: 1,250 × 5% = 62.5, or about 62 per month. Cost per enquiry: 10,000 ÷ 62.5 = 160 SEK.

If the real click cost is 12 SEK, the same budget gives about 833 clicks and about 42 enquiries. Cost per enquiry then rises to about 240 SEK. Run the test for at least a month, then compare the result with what one enquiry is worth to the business.

## Which Channel Fits a Swedish Local Business?

The table below sets out the channels named in the source, with the role each plays and the metric to track. It is a planning aid built from the source's list, not a ranking of channel performance, because no Swedish channel results are published in the sources.

| Channel | Named in source | Best use for a local business | Metric to track |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google listing (Business Profile) | Yes, "Google My Business" | Accurate hours, prices and availability for nearby searchers | Calls, direction requests, enquiries from the listing |
| Search ads with a local radius | Yes, as SEM | Capturing people searching for a service close to the shop | Cost per click, cost per enquiry |
| Social media ads | Yes | Reaching nearby people who may not yet be searching | Cost per enquiry by audience |
| Display ads | Yes | Geo-targeted awareness around the premises | Enquiries from geo-targeted campaigns |
| Search engine optimisation | Yes, recommended | Ranking in organic local results over time | Enquiries from organic search |

Start with the first two rows. They match the source's advice on accurate information and search, and they are the easiest to measure from a small budget.

## What Should a Swedish Local Business Check First?

Start with the basics the source treats as the foundation: a fast, easy website with accurate prices, opening hours and availability. Then choose one or two channels to test rather than spreading a small budget across many.

Before sending any bulk email or SMS, confirm the rules that apply. This article does not summarise Swedish consent rules, so check the requirements with a Swedish adviser before building a list. The same applies to any environmental or sustainability claim: if a product is described that way, the claim should be provable.

Set a measurement plan before the first campaign. Record the cost per enquiry, the channel that produced it and the date, so the next budget decision rests on the business's own results. ## FAQ

The Consumer Barometer data cited by webbyrå sigtuna reports that 77% use Google to find information about local businesses. The data comes from the 2014/15 and 2017 surveys, so check current local search behaviour with a small test.

No. It is a US statistic reported by WordStream and cited in a Swedish agency post. The author suggests similar conclusions may apply to Sweden, but the source does not measure that.

### How much does a local search campaign cost in Sweden?

The sources checked do not publish Swedish click costs. The worked example uses assumed figures. A test will show the actual cost per click and cost per enquiry for the business.
