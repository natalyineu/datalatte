What should a small business set up first in GA4? Start by marking the action that earns money, such as a booking, a call tap or a form submission, as a conversion. Google Analytics limits conversion goals to 20 per account, so keep the list short. Allow 24 to 48 hours for data to appear after correct setup, then compare cost per booking by channel rather than traffic alone. Tag every campaign link, check engagement by landing page, and review the figures monthly.

Google Analytics is free, so the real cost is setup time and the habit of checking results ([Slamdot](https://www.slamdot.com/blog/catering-marketing-101-four-google-marketing-tools-you-need-to-master/)).

## What Should You Set Up First in GA4?

Six settings matter most for a local site. Work through them in order, and check each one against your own property, because menu labels change and the platform's live help page is the reference.

1. Confirm the tag is reporting. Data can take 24 to 48 hours to appear after correct setup ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/ca/small-business/marketing/smb-marketing-guide/?bc=OTH)).
2. Choose one primary conversion: the booking, the call tap or the form. Add others only when you will act on them.
3. Stay within the limit of 20 conversion goals per account ([LivePlan](https://www.liveplan.com/blog/starting/track-marketing-return-on-investment?srsltid=AYCX9vJq8fma11GMaPD4YN3YLfAa8k1pwzVHyWBYHsxn5Tqiu_np7zmZ)).
4. Tag paid campaign links. Under the UTM medium parameter, Google Analytics automatically classifies CPM and CPC visitors as paid traffic ([LivePlan](https://www.liveplan.com/blog/starting/track-marketing-return-on-investment?srsltid=AYCX9vJq8fma11GMaPD4YN3YLfAa8k1pwzVHyWBYHsxn5Tqiu_np7zmZ)).
5. Set aside staff and test visits using the internal traffic option described on the live help page before you rely on the numbers.
6. Connect your ad and search accounts so landing-page data from both sits in one report. Check the roles and permissions required on the live help page first.

Treat this list as an order of work, not a finished audit. The first two items need the most care, because everything after depends on a conversion that records correctly.

## Why Do Conversions Need to Exist Before You Spend?

Goals in Google Analytics do not let you look at historical data. They only start recording conversions once you set them up ([LivePlan](https://www.liveplan.com/blog/starting/track-marketing-return-on-investment?srsltid=AYCX9vJq8fma11GMaPD4YN3YLfAa8k1pwzVHyWBYHsxn5Tqiu_np7zmZ)). A campaign launched with no conversion defined leaves a gap you cannot fill later, so the conversion comes first, then the budget.

Setup also needs time before results appear. Salesforce's small business guide says data should take 24 to 48 hours to appear after tracking is set up correctly ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/ca/small-business/marketing/smb-marketing-guide/?bc=OTH)). Check the reports after two days, not two hours, before deciding that tracking is broken.

For a wider view of how tracking fits with other data, read the [local business analytics and tracking guide](/blog/local-business-analytics-and-tracking).

## How Does Google Analytics Credit a Booking?

Google Analytics assigns a sign-up to the last campaign that brought the user to the website, according to LivePlan ([LivePlan](https://www.liveplan.com/blog/starting/track-marketing-return-on-investment?srsltid=AYCX9vJq8fma11GMaPD4YN3YLfAa8k1pwzVHyWBYHsxn5Tqiu_np7zmZ)). That rule favours the final click. A customer who found you through a local listing, then saw an ad, then booked may show the ad alone.

For a small budget, do not switch off a channel because it looks weak on last-click credit alone. Compare channels over the same period, and check whether bookings fall when a channel is paused. For a head-to-head view of two paid channels, see [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for local business](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins).

## Which Figures Should You Compare Each Month?

BoostFloral recommends tracking click-through rate, conversion rate and return on ad spend in both Google Analytics and Google Ads reports ([BoostFloral](https://www.boostfloral.com/blogs/using-google-ads-to-market-your-flower-shop-website)). Those three show whether people click, whether clicks become bookings, and whether spend pays back.

Indelible Think advises checking Google Analytics, Google Search Console and Google Business Insights once a month so you notice patterns ([Indelible Think](https://indeliblethink.co.uk/how-liverpool-businesses-can-improve-seo/)). For a business that relies on listings, Keepers Digital suggests pulling GBP Insights and Yelp analytics for the same 30-day period before comparing them ([Keepers Digital](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/)).

Outside data can help with local targeting. Geotargetly names Google Analytics with location-based goals, Meta Local Insights, Foursquare and GroundTruth as sources of local behavior data ([Geotargetly](https://geotargetly.com/blog/what-is-hyperlocal-marketing)).

## Worked Example: Which Channel Gets the Next Budget?

All inputs below are assumptions for a hypothetical coffee shop. None of them is a platform or survey figure.

| Assumption or result | Instagram ads | Google Ads | Newsletter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly spend (assumption) | $300 | $300 | $20 |
| Sessions (assumption) | 900 | 400 | 120 |
| Bookings (assumption) | 6 | 12 | 5 |
| Cost per booking (calculated) | $50 | $25 | $4 |
| Booking rate (calculated) | 0.7% | 3.0% | 4.2% |

Cost per booking is spend divided by bookings. Booking rate is bookings divided by sessions.

Instagram brings the most visitors but costs $50 per booking in this example. The newsletter is cheapest per booking, but five bookings cannot absorb a larger budget on their own. Google Ads is the most sensible place to test more spend. If cost per booking held at $25 (assumption), an extra $100 would produce about four more bookings. Check whether that holds as spend rises, because costs often change at higher budgets.

The table only works when links are tagged and conversions are recorded. Untagged links can make a channel look smaller than it is.

## FAQ

### Is Google Analytics free?

Yes. Slamdot describes Google Analytics as a free tool for monitoring, tracking and analyzing website traffic ([Slamdot](https://www.slamdot.com/blog/catering-marketing-101-four-google-marketing-tools-you-need-to-master/)).

### Why are my Analytics numbers lower than my bookings?

Goals only record conversions after they are set up, so earlier bookings will not appear in the report ([LivePlan](https://www.liveplan.com/blog/starting/track-marketing-return-on-investment?srsltid=AYCX9vJq8fma11GMaPD4YN3YLfAa8k1pwzVHyWBYHsxn5Tqiu_np7zmZ)). Keep your booking system as the record of sales and use GA4 to see the path that led to them.

### How long before data appears?

Salesforce says data should take 24 to 48 hours to appear after tracking is set up correctly ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/ca/small-business/marketing/smb-marketing-guide/?bc=OTH)). Check the reports after that window.

### How many conversions should I track?

Google Analytics allows 20 conversion goals per account ([LivePlan](https://www.liveplan.com/blog/starting/track-marketing-return-on-investment?srsltid=AYCX9vJq8fma11GMaPD4YN3YLfAa8k1pwzVHyWBYHsxn5Tqiu_np7zmZ)). Track the few you will act on, starting with the booking or the call.
