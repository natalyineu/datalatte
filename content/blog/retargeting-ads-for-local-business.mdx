Retargeting shows ads to people who already visited your website or interacted with you. The setup is the same on both main platforms: install the tracking code, build a list of past visitors, exclude people who booked, and cap frequency.

Google needs at least 100 active users in a list before ads can show, and Meta needs its Pixel installed on your site.

This guide covers the limits each platform publishes, the privacy rules that apply to local businesses, and an example budget. Figures come from Google and Meta documentation and from WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks.

## What is retargeting, and what does Google call it now?

Google now calls remarketing "your data" segments, used to re-engage people who have interacted with your brand.

Its help page describes five ways: ads to past website visitors on the Display Network, dynamic ads showing items people viewed, Search ads to past visitors, ads to YouTube users who interacted with your videos, and Customer Match, where you upload contact details your customers gave you.

On Meta, the equivalent is a custom audience built from your website. It requires the Meta Pixel on your site, and you pick how long people stay in the audience.

## How many visitors do you need before it works?

Google lists a minimum of 100 active visitors or users in the last 30 days for website segments on Display, Search and YouTube. For uploaded customer lists, the 100-user minimum applies to lists uploaded or refreshed after 1 February 2024. Older lists still need 1,000 matched users.

Meta's help pages opened here did not state a minimum. Meta's own audience screen shows whether an audience is ready, so check there. A site with a few hundred visitors a month can reach Google's minimum, but will have a small audience and slow learning.

## How do you set it up on Google and Meta?

Both platforms follow the same steps.

1. Install the tracking code on every page. Google says its tag must run on every page, and Meta needs the Pixel.
2. Create one audience per goal, for example visitors to your booking page who did not finish booking.
3. Set the membership window. Google defaults to 30 days and allows up to 540. Meta's developer documentation gives 1 to 180 days.
4. Build the campaign and attach the audience. Our [step-by-step retargeting setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-retargeting-on-google-facebook-and-more) walks through each screen.
5. Exclude people who already converted, so customers do not see "book now" ads after booking.

## How do you avoid annoying people?

Limit frequency and exclude converters. Google lets you cap how many times Display and Video ads reach the same person per day, week or month. Frequency capping is not supported in Demand Gen campaigns.

Google recommends letting its system optimize the cap, or you can set one manually in the campaign's Frequency management section.

Shorten the window for low-value purchases such as a coffee. A haircut or class membership has a longer decision cycle. Our [retargeting best practices](/blog/retargeting-best-practices-10-rules-to-avoid-annoying-your-audience) lists rules for message and timing.

## What privacy rules apply to local businesses?

Google's personalized advertising policy has two points that matter. First, advertiser-curated audiences, including your data segments and Customer Match, are not allowed when promoting sensitive interest categories such as health, alcohol or gambling. A medical or health business should check the policy before building lists. Second, if you use data segments, your privacy policy must describe how you use visitor data for advertising and how visitors can opt out.

Check Meta's advertising policies for the same questions on Facebook and Instagram, and follow the privacy and consent rules for your country.

## Google or Meta first?

Start where your customers already are. If people find you through search, a Google search audience catches them on the next query. If they discover you on Instagram, Meta shows reminders in the feed. ## FAQ

### How long should I keep visitors in a retargeting list?

Google defaults to 30 days and allows up to 540. Meta's developer documentation allows 1 to 180 days. Use shorter windows for quick purchases and longer ones for considered services.

### How many visitors do I need?

Google needs 100 active users in 30 days for website segments. Meta shows audience status in its audiences tool.

### Can I retarget people who called but never visited the site?

Not from a website tag. Customer Match on Google lets you upload contact details your customers gave you, and Meta supports customer lists too, subject to their policies.

### Is retargeting allowed for health businesses?

On Google, advertiser-curated audiences are not allowed for sensitive interest categories such as health. Read the policy before you build a list.

### Should I exclude existing customers?

Yes. Build an exclusion list of people who booked or bought, so they see a different message or none.
