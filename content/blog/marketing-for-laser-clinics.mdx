## What Marketing Works for a Laser Hair Removal Clinic?

Treatment packages are a considered purchase, so the job is to earn a consultation. Three channels do most of the work.

1. Google Business Profile with every service, photos of the clinic and reviews. Google says complete, verified profiles are more likely to appear in local results.
2. Search ads for people already looking, such as laser hair removal near them.
3. Meta ads for awareness, within Meta's cosmetic rules.

BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read reviews, 68% require four or more stars, and 89% expect owners to respond.

## What Can You Say About Permanent Results?

Be careful. FDA clearances define permanent hair reduction in a specific way.

One FDA 510(k) letter, K182663, dated 24 September 2018 for a long-pulsed alexandrite and Nd:YAG laser, says permanent hair reduction means a long-term, stable reduction in the number of hairs regrowing when measured at 6, 9 and 12 months after a treatment regime. The same document lists the 755 nm wavelength for temporary hair reduction and the 1064 nm wavelength for stable long-term or permanent reduction.

That is one device, not every laser. Check your own device's cleared indications and use wording that matches, such as "permanent hair reduction" instead of "permanent hair removal". Do not promise results to every skin type unless your device's labelling covers it.

## What Do Meta and Google Allow?

Meta's health and wellness standards, whose change log shows an update on 22 July 2026, say ads promoting cosmetic products, procedures or surgeries must be targeted to people at least 18. The same page permits general cosmetic procedures depicting before-and-after transformation, and bars statements of inferiority about physical appearance. Build creative around the service and the result, not around insecurity.

Google's Local Services Ads US list names "Hair Removal Service" as a category. Pre-badge ads are not available for health care verticals, and go-live needs a licence or business registration. Check whether your clinic qualifies before planning around it. Our [Local Services Ads guide](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide) explains the model.

## How Should You Handle Reviews and Before-and-After Photos?

Ask every client for a review after a treatment, and reply to each. The FTC's rule on fake reviews took effect on 21 October 2024. It bars buying reviews and offering rewards that require a positive review. Only publish a client's photo with written permission.

Do not edit photos to exaggerate results. Meta's page allows general cosmetic before-and-after images for adults, but its rules also turn on claims, so keep captions factual. ## The Math: What Does $700 Buy?

All inputs below are **assumptions** except the benchmark. Beauty and Personal Care is the closest category; laser clinics may differ.

| Step | Assumption or source | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | Assumption | $700 |
| Cost per lead | $39.25 (benchmark) | about 18 leads |
| Leads who buy a package | 30% (assumption) | about 5.3 packages |
| Package price | $600 (assumption) | about $3,200 |
| Cost per package sold | $700 divided by 5.3 | about $131 |

A package usually means repeat visits, but the example counts only the first sale. If only 15% buy, cost per package doubles to about $262. Test the consultation offer and follow up within the day.

## FAQ

The closest published benchmark, Beauty and Personal Care, is $4.62 per click and $39.25 per lead. Laser-specific costs are not published in these sources.

### Can I run before-and-after photos on Meta?
Meta's page permits general cosmetic procedure before-and-after images when ads target adults. It also bars statements of inferiority about appearance.

FDA clearances use the term "permanent hair reduction" with a defined meaning. Match your wording to your device's cleared indications.
