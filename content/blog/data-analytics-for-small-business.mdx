A small business needs three free tools for data analytics: Google Analytics 4 for what visitors do on the website, Search Console for how the site appears in Google Search, and Business Profile Performance for calls, directions and bookings from Google Maps and Search. Add the sales figure from your till or booking system, and you can answer the only question that matters: which activity brought paying customers.

Everything below comes from Google's own help pages, linked in Sources. No survey statistics are used, because the ones in circulation rarely say how they were collected.

## What Does Each Free Tool Tell You?

Each tool answers a different question, so use them together instead of picking one.

| Tool | Question it answers | Key limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Analytics 4 | What do visitors do on my site? | Event retention 2 or 14 months for explorations |
| Search Console | Which searches show my pages, and who clicks? | Default view is the past three months |
| Business Profile Performance | Who calls, asks for directions or books from Google? | Verified owners and managers only |
| Your sales system | Who actually paid? | Only you have it |

The first three show interest. Only the last shows revenue, so a number that never reaches your sales records is a hint, not proof.

## What Should You Track in Google Analytics 4?

Start with key events. Google's [key events help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9267568) defines one as "an event that measures an action that's particularly important to the success of your business". For a local business that means form submissions, taps on the phone number, booking-button clicks or order starts. Mark only those, not every page view.

Then change the retention setting. Google's [data retention help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/7667196) says event data can be kept for 2 or 14 months on standard properties, that it affects explorations and funnel reports, and that standard aggregated reports are not affected. Choosing 14 months lets you compare this season with last. Our [GA4 setup guide for small business](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business) walks through the settings.

## What Does Search Console Add?

Search Console shows what Google Analytics cannot: searches before anyone reaches the site. Google's [Performance report help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553) defines clicks as visits from Google Search results, impressions as how often your site appeared, CTR as clicks divided by impressions and average position as the topmost result's average position. The default range is three months.

Look at the query and page tables. A page with many impressions and few clicks needs a better title or snippet, not more content. Our [Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-guide) explains how to read the tables.

## What Does Business Profile Performance Show?

Google's [Business Profile performance help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094) lists calls (taps on the call button), directions (people who asked for directions, adjusted for repeat taps, cancelled requests and spam), website clicks, messages and bookings. Bookings appear only if they are managed through a booking provider. Only verified owners and managers, signed in to the linked Google Account, can see the data.

For a shop, studio or salon, this is often the nearest thing to a foot-traffic report. See the [Business Profile analytics guide](/blog/google-my-business-analytics) for how to read trends over time.

## How Do You Turn the Numbers Into Decisions?

Compare, then change one thing. Compare this month with the same month last year, or with the previous period when the business is new. Pick a single change, such as rewriting a page title, adding a booking button or pausing a weak ad, and write the date down. Judge it a few weeks later.

Avoid reading too much into small numbers. A change from 3 to 5 calls is noise. Look for a direction that holds over several weeks. For a deeper look at attributing revenue to channels, see [how to track marketing ROI for a local business](/blog/how-to-track-marketing-roi-local-business-guide).

## What Is a 30-Minute Weekly Routine?

Do this on the same day each week and keep the numbers in one spreadsheet.

1. **GA4 (8 min):** key events this week against last week, and which page the visitors came to.
2. **Search Console (8 min):** top five queries and pages, and any page with many impressions and few clicks.
3. **Business Profile (5 min):** calls, directions, website clicks and bookings.
4. **Sales system (5 min):** new customers and revenue.
5. **Decision (4 min):** one change to make, with a date.

A simple dashboard can pull the same figures into one view, as described in our [marketing dashboard guide](/blog/how-to-create-marketing-dashboard-small-business).

## The Math: What Does a Booking Cost?

All inputs are **assumptions**, to show how the three tools combine into one number.

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly marketing spend (assumption) | $400 |
| Website booking-button key events (assumption) | 20 |
| Calls from Business Profile (assumption) | 14 |
| Share of those that become bookings (assumption) | 30% |
| Bookings | about 10 |
| Cost per booking | about $40 |

If a booking is worth $55 in margin (assumption), the month earns about $150 after ads. If the booking rate is 15%, the cost per booking doubles to about $190 and the same spend loses money. The booking rate is the figure to measure first, by asking new customers how they found you and recording it.

## FAQ

### Is Google Analytics free?

GA4 has a free version. Google's retention page also mentions 360 properties, which allow longer event retention. A small business rarely needs those.

### Do I need all three tools?

Use GA4 if you have a website, Search Console if you want to appear in Google Search and Business Profile if customers visit a physical location. A service business with no storefront can skip the profile only if it is not listed on Google.

### How often should I check my data?

Weekly for the routine above, and monthly for trends. Checking more often mostly tracks noise.

### What if my numbers do not match?

They will not. Each tool counts differently, for example Business Profile counts a direction request once even after repeat taps. Compare each tool with itself over time.
