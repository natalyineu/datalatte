## Why Automate Marketing for a Fitness Studio?

The [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/fitness-trainers-and-instructors.htm) counted 388,400 fitness trainers and instructors in 2025, and projects employment to grow 7 percent from 2025 to 2035. That growth is faster than the average for all occupations. More trainers and more studios mean more competition for the same local members.

Automation does not win members by itself. It stops good leads going cold because nobody replied in the evening, and it keeps current members informed without manual effort. The BLS page notes that trainers often handle front-desk work, sign up new members and promote their facilities through social media, newsletters and flyers. Automation takes some of that load off the coach.

Start with repeat tasks that follow a fixed rule: if this happens, send that. Leave anything that needs judgment, such as an injury question or a complaint, to a person.

## Which Four Workflows Should You Build First?

The first workflow is a lead welcome by email. When someone books a trial or fills in a form, send a welcome, a short class guide and a clear way to book the next session. A trial-to-membership sequence can follow the same structure later.

The second is class reminders by text. Send one message the day before and a short one a few hours ahead, with a reply option to cancel. A cancellation reply lets you offer the spot to someone on a waitlist.

The third is a win-back message. If a member has not booked in 21 days (assumption), send a friendly note that a spot is held for them. The fourth is a review request: after a visit, send a link to your Google review page.

Build them in order and watch each for two weeks before adding the next. One working workflow is worth more than four half-built ones.

## What Does a Reminder Workflow Cost? A Worked Example

Every input below is an **assumption** you should replace with your own figures. The aim is to show the method, not to predict results.

| Item | Input or result |
| --- | --- |
| Class bookings per month (assumption) | 1,200 |
| Reminders per booking, day before only (assumption) | 1 |
| All-in cost per text, including carrier fees (assumption) | $0.02 |
| SMS cost per month: 1,200 × $0.02 | $24.00 |
| Software subscription (assumption) | $20.00 |
| Total cost per month | $44.00 |
| Baseline no-show rate (assumption) | 12% |
| Baseline no-shows: 1,200 × 12% | 144 per month |
| Reduction in no-shows from reminders (assumption) | 10% |
| No-shows prevented: 144 × 10% | about 14 per month |
| Value of one recovered spot (assumption) | $15 |
| Value recovered: 14 × $15 | about $210 per month |
| Net gain per month | about $166 |
| Break-even: $44 ÷ $15 | about 3 saved spots |

The workflow pays for itself once it saves about three spots a month. The result depends entirely on the assumed no-show rate and reduction, so measure both before you trust the figures. Send reminders to half of your classes for four weeks, then compare no-show rates against the classes that received none.

Replies from members add to the SMS cost. Count them in the budget if your studio gets many cancellations by text.

## Should You Use Email or SMS?

Use both, for different jobs. Email carries longer content such as onboarding, class schedules and newsletters. SMS suits short, time-sensitive messages such as a reminder, a waitlist spot or a last-minute opening.

Texting requires consent. Collect a clear opt-in at sign-up and include an opt-out in each message. Rules for business texting in the US, and the registration steps your SMS provider requires, are separate from message prices. Check those rules before you launch.

## How Do You Choose Tools Without Reading Every Price Page?

Follow a short checklist before you compare products.

1. List the four workflows and mark which need email and which need text.
2. Check whether your booking system already sends reminders. Many do, and a second tool would duplicate them.
3. Count the messages each workflow sends per month, using your own bookings.
4. Ask each vendor for the total monthly cost at that volume, including any per-message and per-task charges.
5. Confirm the tool can pause a workflow when a member replies or books, so people do not get messages they no longer need.

Once the list is short, a [side-by-side comparison of marketing automation tools](/blog/best-marketing-automation-tools-small-business-compared) can help you match features to the four workflows.

Vendors' own campaign templates can speed up setup. The Keap blog describes a "Customer Survey" campaign that can chain into a "Refer a Friend" campaign, and a "Surprise Gift" campaign that can be adjusted to deliver a trial coupon. Those are examples of how a campaign can be structured, not proof of results.

## How Do You Keep Automation Personal?

Write each message as if you were texting a regular. Use first names, name the class and the coach, and keep it short. Automation should handle timing and the rules for sending, while the tone stays human.

If you draft the wording with a writing assistant, edit the output so it sounds like your studio. Guidance on prompts for this kind of copy is in [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing). The same caution applies to AI tools that promise to replace staff. Automation can handle routine messages, but a person should answer questions about injuries, billing disputes and complaints. The argument for that split is covered in [AI tools that could replace a marketing assistant for a local business](/blog/ai-tools-replace-marketing-assistant-local-business).

Check the workflows once a week. Open the last messages sent, confirm each went to the right person, and look for steps that failed. A broken connection usually shows up as messages that stopped, so a regular check catches it early.

## FAQ

### What should a fitness studio automate first?

Start with a lead welcome email and class reminders by text. Both follow fixed rules, take little time to set up, and affect bookings directly.

### Do I need consent to text members?

Yes. Collect a clear opt-in at sign-up, include an opt-out in every message, and check your SMS provider's requirements for US business texting before you launch.

### How do I know if reminders are worth the cost?

Divide the monthly cost by the value of one recovered spot. In the worked example above, about three saved spots cover the assumed $44 monthly cost. Measure your own no-show rate before and after to check.

### Will automation make my studio feel impersonal?

It does not have to. Automate timing and keep the wording human, and handle questions, injuries and complaints yourself.
