A local fitness studio should automate four things first: a welcome sequence for new leads, class reminders by text, a follow-up for people who stop coming, and a review request after a good class. Each can run with a $0 to $20 a month email tool and a few cents of SMS cost. This guide shows the workflows, the real tool prices and a worked example.

## Why Automate Marketing for a Small Studio?

Studios compete for a large pool of members. The Health & Fitness Association's 2026 report says [a record 81 million Americans belonged to a gym, studio or other fitness facility in 2025](https://www.healthandfitness.org/2026-us-health-fitness-consumer-report-headline-trends/), or 26.1% of people aged six and over. The same page says industry churn was at a decade low in 2025.

Automation does not win members by itself. It stops good leads going cold because nobody replied at 9 pm, and it keeps current members informed without you typing the same message every week.

Start with the repeat tasks that follow a fixed rule: "if this happens, send that." Leave anything that needs judgment, such as an injury question or a complaint, to a person.

## Which Four Workflows Should You Build First?

1. **Lead welcome (email).** When someone books a trial or fills a form, send a welcome, a class guide and a reminder of how to book. Our [trial to annual member sequence](/blog/fitness-studio-email-sequence-trial-to-annual-member) has a structure to copy.
2. **Class reminders (SMS).** A text the day before and a short one a few hours ahead, with a reply option to cancel so you can fill the spot.
3. **Win-back (email or SMS).** If a member has not booked in 21 days (assumption), send a friendly "we saved you a spot" message.
4. **Review request.** After a visit, send a link to your Google review page.

Do these in order. One working workflow is worth more than five half-built ones. Build each, watch it for two weeks, then add the next.

## What Do the Tools Cost?

Prices are public, so budget before you choose:

- **Mailchimp:** the [pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/) lists Free at $0 with a limit of 250 contacts and no automation flows, and Standard at $20 a month for the first 12 months with up to 200 flows. Check the renewal price at checkout, because the page left it blank.
- **Zapier:** the [pricing page](https://zapier.com/pricing) lists a free plan with 100 tasks a month and Professional from $19.99 a month for 750 tasks, billed annually. Each automated step counts as a task.
- **Twilio:** its [US SMS pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/us) is $0.0083 per segment outbound plus carrier fees of about $0.0035 to $0.005 on the major carriers, and $1.15 a month for a long-code number.

Many studio booking systems already include reminders, so check yours before you add a tool. Our [Zapier automation guide](/blog/zapier-for-small-business-10-automations-that-save-time) and [Make guide](/blog/make-automation-for-small-business) cover connecting separate tools.

## Should You Use Email or SMS?

Use both, for different jobs. Email carries longer content: onboarding, schedules, newsletters. SMS suits short, time-sensitive nudges: a reminder, a waitlist spot, a last-minute class opening.

Texting needs consent. Get a clear opt-in at sign-up and include an opt-out in each message. Registration rules for US business texting are separate from message prices, so check your provider's requirements and any extra fees before you start. For a side-by-side view, read [SMS vs email marketing for local business](/blog/sms-vs-email-marketing-local-business-which-is-better).

## What Does a Reminder Workflow Cost? A Worked Example

Every number below is an **assumption**. Replace them with your own.

| Item (assumption) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Class bookings per month | 1,200 |
| Cost per reminder: $0.0083 plus $0.005 carrier fee | $0.0133 |
| Monthly SMS cost: 1,200 x $0.0133 | $15.96 |
| Number rental | $1.15 |
| Total per month | about $17 |
| Value of one saved drop-in spot | $20 |
| No-shows to prevent to break even | 1 |

The break-even is a single saved spot a month. If reminders prevent ten no-shows, the workflow returns about $200 on roughly $17 of cost. Test it: send reminders to half your classes for a month and compare no-show rates against the other half before you roll it out everywhere.

Do not forget the replies. Inbound messages are charged at the same $0.0083 per segment on Twilio's page, so a studio that gets many replies should add that to the budget.

## How Do You Keep Automation Personal?

Write each message as if you were texting a regular. Use first names, name the class or coach, and keep it short. Automation should handle timing, not tone.

Check the workflows weekly for a few minutes. Open the last messages sent, confirm each went to the right person, and look for failed steps. A broken integration usually shows up as a message that stopped, so a regular check catches it early.

## FAQ

**Q: What should a fitness studio automate first?**
A: Start with a lead welcome email and class reminders by text. Both follow fixed rules, take little time to set up and affect bookings directly.

**Q: How much does fitness studio marketing automation cost?**
A: Using published prices, email can start free (Mailchimp Free has no automation flows, so a paid plan from $20 a month is needed for them), Zapier has a free plan with 100 tasks, and SMS costs about $0.0083 per message on Twilio plus carrier fees.

**Q: Do I need consent to text members?**
A: Yes. Collect a clear opt-in at sign-up and include an opt-out in every message. Check your provider's rules for US business texting.

**Q: Will automation make my studio feel impersonal?**
A: Not if you automate timing and keep the wording human. Handle questions, injuries and complaints yourself.
