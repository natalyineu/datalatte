To fill a coworking space, focus marketing on one outcome, booked tours, and make the path short: a Google Business Profile that shows photos and hours, a page for each membership type with a price or price range, and fast replies to tour requests. Deskmag's 2025 operator survey says member acquisition remains by far the biggest challenge for the industry.

This guide is for independent coworking operators. Sources are at the end. Examples built on guesses are marked as assumptions.

## How Full Are Coworking Spaces?

Deskmag reports that global occupancy rose to an average of 68% at the start of 2025, and in major cities it was well above 70%. Of operators, 54% were profitable and 18% reported losses. Profit depended on city size: in cities over a million residents nearly two-thirds were profitable, while in towns under 20,000 about one in five was.

Deskmag's survey is global, not US-only, and it does not publish a break-even occupancy in the text we read. Use it as context and calculate your own break-even from rent, staffing and average revenue per member.

## What Do Operators Say Is Hard?

Member acquisition was named as the biggest challenge. About one in three operators cited high real estate or rental prices, and nearly as many cited general price increases. Financial difficulty and insufficient market demand ranked fourth and fifth, more often in smaller towns.

Almost 80% expected a growing number of members in early 2025, and nearly half planned price increases, especially in cities over a million. So many of your competitors are raising prices while also trying to fill desks. A clear tour offer and visible reviews help separate you.

## What Should the Google Profile and Website Show?

Google's guidelines require your profile name to be the real-world name, without taglines or keywords. A space with a staffed front desk and set hours fits the rules, while virtual offices are not eligible unless staffed during business hours. Add photos of desks, offices and meeting rooms, your categories and a booking link.

Create one page per membership type: hot desk, dedicated desk, private office, meeting rooms. Include who it suits, what is included, a price or a starting price, and a tour form. ## Do Reviews Matter for a Coworking Space?

BrightLocal's 2026 survey found that 68% of consumers will only use a business with 4 stars or more, 47% will not use one with fewer than 20 reviews, and 74% only consider reviews from the last three months. It covers local businesses in general, not coworking only.

Ask members at their one-month point using a Google link or QR code. Google bans free or discounted services in exchange for reviews, and the FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews. Reply to each one, since 89% of consumers expect owners to. Our [Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) covers it.

## Should a Coworking Space Run Paid Ads?

Test cautiously. LocaliQ publishes no coworking row. Business Services shows $5.87 per click, 6.10% click-through rate, 4.85% conversion rate and $93.69 per lead, while Real Estate shows $3.22 per click and 3.70% conversion. Neither matches coworking exactly.

Send ads to a tour-booking page, not the home page. Track form fills and phone calls, and match them to signed members in your CRM.

## FAQ

### How do coworking spaces get members?

Deskmag says member acquisition is the biggest challenge. Operators use search, maps, referrals, events and partnerships. Track the source for each tour.

### What occupancy is normal?

Deskmag reports an average of 68% globally at the start of 2025, and above 70% in major cities. Your break-even depends on your costs.

### Is Google Ads worth it for coworking?

It can work if tours convert, but no coworking benchmark is published. Test a small budget and measure cost per signed member.

### Should I show prices?

Visible prices or starting prices help serious visitors self-select. No verified data in this guide shows the effect on conversions.
