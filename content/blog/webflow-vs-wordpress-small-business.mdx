Webflow's cheapest paid site plan costs $15 a month. It has no CMS, which means no blog. If you want to publish articles, you need the $25-a-month Premium plan. That one detail changes the maths for any local business that wants to rank by writing. Add WordPress, where the software is free but hosting, upkeep and your time are not, and the real question becomes: what will each option cost you over three years, and who will look after the site?

This guide uses prices read from vendor pricing pages in October 2026, a worked three-year table with labelled assumptions, and a decision guide. It is written for owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Prices are in US dollars; plans are re-priced often, so check before you buy.

## The Short Answer

- **Choose WordPress** if you plan to publish regularly, want the most plugins and control, and have someone (you, a host or a freelancer) to keep it updated.
- **Choose Webflow** if design matters, you would rather not run updates, and you will pay a little more per month for that. Budget for the Premium plan if you want a blog.
- **Choose neither** if you need five pages, a booking link and nothing else. A simple builder, covered in our [Squarespace review for local businesses](/blog/squarespace-for-local-business-review), may be enough.

## What Webflow Costs Now

From [Webflow's pricing page](https://webflow.com/pricing), as we read it in October 2026:

| Site plan | Price | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | Free | webflow.io domain, 2 static pages, 1 GB bandwidth, 50 form submissions |
| Basic | $15/mo billed yearly | Custom domain, 300 static pages, 10 GB bandwidth, unlimited form submissions, password protection. For "simple sites that don't need a CMS" |
| Premium (new) | $25/mo billed yearly | Everything in Basic plus CMS (20,000 items, 40 collections on the plan comparison), up to 2.5 TB bandwidth, code components, site search, form file upload |

Three things to note. The page offers a yearly price discount of up to 40% versus monthly billing, so paying month by month costs more. Webflow separates Site plans from Workspace plans: a free Starter workspace exists, Core is $19 a month and Growth is $49 a month billed yearly, plus seats priced at $39 a month (Full) and $15 a month (Limited) per additional seat. Most single-site owners can start on the free workspace. And the Premium plan is described by Webflow as new, so older reviews that quote separate "CMS" and "Business" prices are outdated. Third-party sites disagree on the monthly-billing and Basic prices, so rely on the live page, not our table, for exact figures.

## What WordPress Costs Now

WordPress itself is free. The [WordPress.org about page](https://wordpress.org/about/) says it is released under the GPL and describes itself as the platform of choice for "over 43% of all sites across the web". Costs come from the layer around it. Three common routes:

- **Self-hosted on cheap hosting.** [SiteGround's WordPress page](https://www.siteground.com/wordpress-hosting.htm) lists its StartUp plan at $2.99 a month prepaid for 12 months, renewing at $17.99 a month. Introductory prices like this rise at renewal, so budget the renewal figure.
- **Managed WordPress hosting.** [WP Engine's plans page](https://wpengine.com/plans/) shows its managed hosting starting at $28 a month, billed annually ($336 upfront). Managed hosts handle more of the technical upkeep.
- **WordPress.com.** [WordPress.com's pricing](https://wordpress.com/pricing/) shows a Business plan at US$25 a month on one-year billing (US$40 month to month), and says you can install any of over 60,000 plugins from its marketplace or upload your own. This is hosted for you, closer to Webflow in feel.

You still pay for a domain (we assume $15 a year), possibly a premium theme or plugins, and anyone you hire.

## The Math

Three-year running cost, excluding the initial build. Prices come from the pages above. Everything marked "assumption" is ours: replace it with real quotes.

| | Webflow Basic | Webflow Premium | WordPress.com Business | WordPress self-hosted, DIY upkeep | WordPress self-hosted, paid upkeep |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Platform or hosting, 36 months | $540 | $900 | $900 | $468 | $468 |
| Domain, $15/yr (assumption) | $45 | $45 | $45 | $45 | $45 |
| Theme and plugins, $100/yr (assumption) | $0 | $0 | $0 | $300 | $300 |
| Maintenance, $50/mo (assumption) | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $1,800 |
| **3-year total** | **$585** | **$945** | **$945** | **$813** | **$2,613** |
| Has a blog? | No | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

Notes on the table: the self-hosted figure uses SiteGround's $2.99 intro price for year one ($35.88) and the $17.99 renewal for years two and three ($431.76), about $468 in total. The $100 a year for themes and plugins is a placeholder; many sites use free ones. The $50 monthly upkeep is a placeholder for a freelancer who handles updates and backups; the right figure is whatever you are quoted. Sales tax and the build itself are not included.

What this shows:

- **Webflow Premium and WordPress.com Business cost the same** ($900 over three years) and both include hosting and a blog. Comparing "free WordPress" to "paid Webflow" misleads.
- **Self-hosted WordPress is cheapest in cash** but only if your time is free. Put a price on an hour of your own work, then count the hours.
- **Paying someone to maintain WordPress turns it into the most expensive option.** That is the right comparison against Webflow's managed hosting.

## Learning Curve and Day-to-Day Editing

**WordPress** is easy to start and slower to master. The built-in block editor handles pages and posts. Page builders add layout control, but they also add complexity and weight. Expect to learn about plugins, updates and backups.

**Webflow** works like a design tool, with boxes, classes and styles that mirror HTML and CSS. If that is new, the first weeks can be rough. Many owners hire a designer to build the site, then make small edits themselves. Webflow's plan comparison mentions staff accounts that give clients or content editors access to the Editor to update content, so a handover is built into the product.

## SEO and Speed: Platform Matters Less Than You Think

Google's [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) mentions CMSes only in passing: most can turn your titles into a `<title>` element and some generate sitemaps automatically. It does not say that any platform ranks better. What shifts rankings is the usual list: a page per service and area, accurate name, address and phone, reviews, and a strong Business Profile. Our [local SEO service](/services/local-seo) is built around that list.

Speed is where setup shows. Google's [Core Web Vitals documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) defines "good" as Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint under 200 milliseconds and Cumulative Layout Shift below 0.1. Either platform can pass or fail depending on image sizes, scripts and (for WordPress) theme and plugin weight. Test your own pages and see our guide to [core web vitals for local business websites](/blog/core-web-vitals-local-business-website).

## Maintenance and Ownership

With self-hosted WordPress, you or your host is responsible for updating core software, themes and plugins, backups and security. Managed hosts such as WP Engine say on their plans page that security, updates and performance are included. With Webflow, hosting is part of the plan, so there is no separate server to patch, though you still manage your content and integrations. On the other side, WordPress files and database can be moved between hosts, while a Webflow site lives on Webflow. If you ever leave, expect to rebuild the design and set up careful redirects.

## Which Business Should Choose Which

**WordPress** suits a business that wants a content-heavy blog, a specific plugin (membership, custom booking), the lowest cash cost and freedom to switch hosts, with someone to maintain it.

**Webflow** suits a design-led brand site that is mostly marketing pages, where you prefer not to manage updates and are comfortable paying about $25 a month for the CMS-enabled plan.

**By business type:**
- **Coffee shop:** a few image-rich pages with menu and hours. Any option works; Webflow Basic is enough if you never blog.
- **Salon or spa:** services, prices and a booking embed. Make sure booking is smooth on mobile.
- **Pet groomer:** local service pages and reviews. WordPress or Webflow Premium if you will add content.
- **Fitness studio:** timetable and trial offer. Embed your booking platform on whichever site you pick. Our [Wix vs Squarespace vs WordPress comparison](/blog/wix-vs-squarespace-vs-wordpress-local-business-website) covers simpler routes.

## Do This Today

1. Decide whether you will blog or add location pages. If yes, price Webflow Premium or a WordPress route that includes a CMS, not Webflow Basic.
2. Write down who will edit the site, who fixes it when it breaks, and the hourly cost of your own time.
3. Build your own three-year table using the one above and real quotes from a developer, and see our guide to [DIY website vs professional developer](/blog/diy-website-vs-professional-developer).
4. Check each platform's pricing page for the plan you actually need, on yearly and monthly billing.
5. Build one real page on a free Webflow Starter site or a free WordPress.com site before committing. If you hate the editor in an hour, you will hate it in a year.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Webflow better than WordPress for SEO?**
A: Neither is. Google's own SEO guide treats the CMS as a detail and says nothing about one platform ranking better. Content, local signals and page speed matter more.

**Q: Is WordPress cheaper than Webflow?**
A: In cash, often yes: our table has self-hosted WordPress at about $813 versus $945 for Webflow Premium over three years. Add paid maintenance and WordPress becomes about $2,613 under our assumptions. WordPress.com Business matches Webflow Premium at $25 a month on yearly billing.

**Q: Can I have a blog on Webflow's cheapest plan?**
A: Webflow describes Basic as for sites that do not need a CMS, so a blog needs the Premium plan.

**Q: Can I switch later?**
A: Yes, but plan for rebuilding the design and redirecting old URLs to protect your search visibility.

**Q: Which one for a local business?**
A: For most local businesses with modest needs, a simple builder or a well-maintained WordPress site is practical. Webflow suits design-focused businesses willing to pay for managed hosting and a CMS plan.
