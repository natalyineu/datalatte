Webflow's cheapest paid site plan, Basic at $15 a month billed yearly, is described by Webflow as being for "simple sites that don't need a CMS". A blog needs the $25-a-month Premium plan. WordPress software is free, but hosting, upkeep and your time are not, so the real comparison is the three-year cost and who looks after the site.

This guide uses prices read from vendor pricing pages on 3 November 2026, a three-year table with labelled assumptions and a decision guide for owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Prices are in US dollars and change often, so check before you buy.

## Which Should a Small Business Choose?

- **WordPress** if you plan to publish regularly, want the widest range of plugins and control, and have someone (you, a host or a freelancer) to keep it updated.
- **Webflow** if design matters, you would rather not run updates, and you will pay a little more per month for that. Budget for Premium if you want a blog.
- **Neither** if you need five pages, a booking link and nothing else. A simple builder, covered in the [Squarespace review for local businesses](/blog/squarespace-for-local-business-review), may be enough.

## What Does Webflow Cost Now?

From [Webflow's pricing page](https://webflow.com/pricing), read on 3 November 2026:

| Site plan | Price | What the page lists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | Free | webflow.io domain, limited Webflow CMS, 2 static pages, 1 GB bandwidth, 50 form submissions |
| Basic | $15/mo billed yearly | Custom domain, 300 static pages, 10 GB bandwidth, unlimited form submissions, password protection |
| Premium (marked new) | $25/mo billed yearly | Everything in Basic plus Webflow CMS, bandwidth tiers from 50 GB up to 2.5 TB, code components, site search, form file upload |

The page advertises yearly billing savings of up to 40% against monthly billing, so paying monthly costs more. It also lists a Team platform plan at $2,500 a month on an annual contract, which is outside small-business range. Older reviews that quote separate "CMS" and "Business" site plans describe the previous price list.

## What Does WordPress Cost Now?

WordPress itself is free: [WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/about/) says it is licensed under the GPLv2. Costs come from the layer around it. Three common routes:

- **Self-hosted on budget hosting.** [SiteGround's WordPress page](https://www.siteground.com/wordpress-hosting.htm) showed its StartUp plan at $1.99 a month for the first term under an "Early Black Friday" promotion, prepaid for 12 months, renewing at $17.99 a month. Promotional prices end, so budget the renewal figure.
- **Managed WordPress hosting.** [WP Engine's plans page](https://wpengine.com/plans/) lists Essential from $28 a month, billed annually ($336 paid upfront), with daily backups, updates and security handled for you.
- **WordPress.com.** [WordPress.com pricing](https://wordpress.com/pricing/) lists Business at $25 a month on yearly billing (struck-through $40), auto-renewing at $28 a month, with 50 GB storage and the option to install any of over 60,000 plugins from its marketplace.

You still pay for a domain (assumed $15 a year here), possibly a premium theme or plugins, and anyone you hire.

What the table shows:

- **Paying someone to maintain WordPress makes it the most expensive** in this example, and that is the fair comparison against a managed platform.

## How Hard Is Each One to Edit Day to Day?

**WordPress** is easy to start and slower to master. The built-in block editor handles pages and posts. Page builders add layout control but also weight and complexity. Expect to learn about plugins, updates and backups.

**Webflow** works like a design tool, with boxes, classes and styles that mirror HTML and CSS. If that is new, the first weeks can be rough. A common pattern is to have a designer build the site and then make small edits yourself. Test it: build one real page on a free Webflow Starter site or a free WordPress.com site before you commit.

## Does the Platform Change SEO and Speed?

Little. Google's [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) treats the CMS as a detail and does not say any platform ranks better. What shifts local rankings is the usual list: a page per service and area, consistent name, address and phone, reviews and a strong Business Profile. Our [local SEO service](/services/local-seo) is built around that list.

Speed is where setup shows. Google's [Core Web Vitals documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) defines a good result as Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint under 200 milliseconds and Cumulative Layout Shift below 0.1. Either platform can pass or fail depending on image sizes, scripts and, for WordPress, theme and plugin weight. Test your own pages in PageSpeed Insights.

## Who Maintains the Site and Who Owns It?

With self-hosted WordPress, you or your host is responsible for core, theme and plugin updates, backups and security. Managed hosts such as WP Engine list updates, backups and security among the included features.

With Webflow, hosting is part of the plan, so there is no server to patch, though you still manage content and integrations. WordPress files and database can be moved between hosts, while a Webflow site lives on Webflow. If you ever leave, expect to rebuild the design and set up careful redirects.

## Which Platform Fits Which Local Business?

- **Coffee shop:** a few image-rich pages with menu and hours. Any option works; Webflow Basic is enough if you never blog.
- **Salon or spa:** services, prices and a booking embed. Make sure booking is smooth on mobile.
- **Pet groomer:** local service pages and reviews. WordPress or Webflow Premium if you will add content.
- **Fitness studio:** timetable and trial offer. Embed your booking platform. The [Wix vs Squarespace comparison](/blog/wix-vs-squarespace-small-business) covers simpler routes.

## FAQ

### Is Webflow better than WordPress for SEO?

Neither is. Google's SEO starter guide treats the CMS as a detail and says nothing about one platform ranking better. Content, local signals and page speed matter more.

### Is WordPress cheaper than Webflow?

In cash, often yes: the table has self-hosted WordPress at about $801 against $945 for Webflow Premium over three years. Add paid maintenance and WordPress reaches about $2,601 under the stated assumptions.

### Can I have a blog on Webflow's cheapest plan?

Webflow describes Basic as for sites that do not need a CMS, and lists Webflow CMS under Premium. A blog therefore needs Premium.

### Can I switch later?

Yes, but plan to rebuild the design and redirect old URLs to protect search visibility.
