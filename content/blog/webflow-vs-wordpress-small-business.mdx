For most small businesses, WordPress is the safer and cheaper choice if you have someone to maintain it, while Webflow is worth the learning curve if you want a custom-designed site with managed hosting and are happy to pay a higher monthly fee. If you just want something simple you can edit yourself, a builder like Squarespace may suit you better than either.

Here is how the two compare on cost, ease, SEO, maintenance and fit, so you can match the tool to your situation.

## The short version

| | WordPress | Webflow |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Open-source software, self-hosted or managed | Visual design platform with hosting included |
| Learning curve | Moderate; page builders ease it | Steeper if you are new to web design concepts |
| Design freedom | High with themes and builders | Very high, with precise control |
| Hosting | You choose a host | Included in the plan |
| Maintenance | Updates, plugins, backups, security | Handled by Webflow |
| Plugins and integrations | Huge ecosystem | Smaller, relies on integrations and custom code |
| Typical monthly cost | Low to moderate, varies | Moderate; plans scale with features |
| Best for | Flexibility, content-heavy sites, budgets | Design-led, custom marketing sites |

## Cost, realistically

Costs vary, so treat these as ballparks and check current pricing.

**WordPress.** The software is free. You pay for hosting (shared hosting can start at a few dollars a month, managed WordPress hosting such as WP Engine or Kinsta costs more), a domain (typically $10 to $20 a year) and possibly a premium theme or plugins (often $50 to $200 a year each). If you hire someone to set up the site, that adds one-off costs. If you pay for maintenance, add a monthly fee.

**Webflow.** Site plans are billed monthly or yearly and typically begin around the price of a standard hosting plan, rising for e-commerce, higher traffic or CMS (blog) features. Webflow separates "Workspace" plans, where you build, from "Site" plans, where you host, which can make the total more complex than it first looks. A designer or agency to build a Webflow site generally costs more than a WordPress template build.

Think in total cost over two to three years, including your own time.

## Learning curve

**WordPress** is easy to start and hard to master. Installing a theme and editing pages with the built-in block editor is manageable for beginners. Page builders such as Elementor, Bricks or Divi make layouts easier, though they add weight and complexity.

**Webflow** feels like a design tool. You work with boxes, classes and styles, which mirror HTML and CSS. Once you grasp the logic, you can build almost any layout without code, but the first few weeks can be frustrating if you are not familiar with those concepts. Many small-business owners are better off hiring a Webflow designer to build the site and then managing simple edits themselves through the Editor.

## Day-to-day editing

- **WordPress:** straightforward for posting blogs and updating text. Complex layout changes can break things if a plugin or theme conflicts.
- **Webflow:** the Editor lets non-designers update text and images directly on the page, with less risk of breaking layouts. Larger structural changes need the Designer.

If you will have several team members updating content, think about which interface they will actually enjoy using.

## SEO and speed

Both can rank well. Search results depend far more on content quality, local signals, reviews and links than on the platform.

- **WordPress** has excellent SEO plugins (Yoast SEO, Rank Math) that handle titles, descriptions, schema and sitemaps. Speed depends on your host, theme and plugins. A well-built site can be fast; a bloated one is slow.
- **Webflow** outputs clean code and handles titles, meta descriptions, redirects and sitemaps natively, and its hosting runs on a content delivery network. Speed is generally good out of the box, though large images and heavy animations can still slow it down.

For local businesses, the key tasks are the same on either platform: a page for each service and location, consistent name, address and phone, fast mobile pages, and a strong Google Business Profile. ## Maintenance and security

This is the biggest practical difference.

- **WordPress** needs regular updates to core, themes and plugins, plus backups and security monitoring. Neglect is the main cause of hacked sites. Managed hosts handle some of this, and a maintenance plan can handle the rest.
- **Webflow** manages hosting, security patches and backups for you. You still maintain your content and any integrations.

If nobody on your team will look after a website, the lower-maintenance option has real value.

## E-commerce, bookings and integrations

- **Bookings:** both platforms usually integrate with third-party schedulers such as Calendly, Acuity, Fresha or Square Appointments through embeds or plugins.
- **Online store:** WordPress commonly uses WooCommerce, which is powerful and flexible. Webflow has built-in e-commerce, which suits smaller catalogues but has plan limits. If selling is your main goal, compare Shopify as well.
- **Email marketing and CRM:** both connect to Mailchimp, Brevo, Klaviyo and others, via plugins on WordPress and native integrations or Zapier and Make on Webflow.
- **Forms:** both handle forms, and you can route submissions to your CRM or inbox.

## Which business should choose which

**Choose WordPress if:**

- You want the most flexibility for the lowest cost
- You publish a lot of content or run a large blog
- You have a developer, agency or maintenance plan
- You need a specific plugin, such as a membership or custom booking tool
- You want to own and move your site freely between hosts

**Choose Webflow if:**

- Design matters and you want a distinctive site
- You prefer not to worry about updates and security
- You are comfortable with a steeper start, or will hire a designer
- Your site is mostly marketing pages, with a modest blog
- You like having hosting and tools in one place

**Consider neither if:**

- You want the easiest possible start. Read our [Squarespace review for local businesses](/blog/squarespace-for-local-business-review) and the [Wix vs Squarespace vs WordPress comparison](/blog/wix-vs-squarespace-vs-wordpress-local-business-website).
- You have a very small budget and basic needs. A simple builder may do.

## Examples by business type

- **Coffee shop:** a simple, image-rich site with menu and hours. Squarespace, Wix or either of these platforms work. Choose WordPress if you plan a blog and local landing pages, Webflow if brand design is central.
- **Salon or spa:** emphasis on services, pricing and booking. Any of the three works, as long as booking is smooth on mobile.
- **Pet groomer:** local pages, service details and reviews. WordPress is practical if you plan to add content for local search.
- **Fitness studio:** timetable, trial offer and sign-up flow. Often best to link or embed your booking platform, whichever site builder you use.

## A decision checklist

1. Who will edit the site, and how often?
2. Who will maintain it if something breaks?
3. What is the total budget over three years, including help?
4. Do I need unusual features, or are standard pages enough?
5. How important is a custom design?
6. Do I want to own my hosting and files?

Answer honestly, and the choice usually becomes clear. If you are still torn, a one-hour conversation with a developer who works on both is worth the fee. ## FAQ

**Q: Is Webflow better than WordPress for SEO?**
A: Neither is inherently better. Both support the technical basics. Results depend on content, local signals and site quality.

**Q: Is WordPress cheaper than Webflow?**
A: Usually yes for hosting and software, but total cost depends on themes, plugins, maintenance and who builds the site.

**Q: Can I switch later?**
A: Yes, but expect a rebuild of the design and careful redirects to protect your search visibility.

**Q: Which one do you recommend for a local business?**
A: For most local businesses with modest needs and no technical staff, a simple builder or a well-maintained WordPress site is a practical choice. Webflow suits design-focused businesses with budget.
