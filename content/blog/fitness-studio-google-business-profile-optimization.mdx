Optimizing a fitness studio's Google Business Profile means keeping one accurate listing that matches your real-world studio, with a specific category, current hours, clear photos, and replies to reviews. Google's guidelines call for one profile per business, the fewest categories that describe your core business, and no links in the business description. Changes can take a few hours or several months to show, so judge results after a few weeks. The steps below follow those rules.

## What should a fitness studio fix first on its Google Business Profile?

Start by checking whether duplicate listings already exist. Google's guidelines say there should only be one profile per business, because duplicates can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search ([Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). Search your studio name and address on Maps before creating anything new, and flag any duplicates you find.

Next, check the name field. It should reflect your real-world name, as it appears on your signage, website, and stationery. Google does not allow taglines, business hours, phone numbers, or website URLs inside the name ([same guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). A studio called "Harbor Pilates" should not add "Open 6 AM to 8 PM" to that field.

Your address or service area also has to be accurate and precise. For the full setup order, the [Google Business Profile optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) walks through each section in sequence.

## Which categories and description rules apply to a fitness studio?

Google asks businesses to choose the fewest number of categories that describe their overall core business ([Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). For a boutique studio, that usually means one primary category that matches what members actually book, plus only the secondary categories that describe core work. Adding extra categories to chase broader searches can blur what the studio is.

The business description should explain services, mission, and history. It should not contain links of any type, and it should not focus on special promotions, prices, or sale offers. Misspellings and gimmicky characters are also disallowed ([same guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). Put pricing and offers in posts or on your website instead, where they can change without edits to the core listing.

The same listing logic applies to other local service businesses. The [coffee shop listing tips](/blog/google-business-profile-coffee-shop-tips) show how these principles translate to a different category.

## How long do Google Business Profile changes take to appear?

Google's SEO Starter Guide says changes can take effect within a few hours, while others can take several months. It recommends waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change had a beneficial effect, and notes that not every change produces noticeable results ([SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)). Track one change at a time so you can tell what caused a result.

## How should a studio handle review replies?

Google's guidelines allow businesses to post their own contact information, such as a phone number or email, in responses to reviews. They do not allow content that contains personal or confidential information, including contact details linked to a named person ([Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). Replies should point readers to the studio's general contact details and should never reference a member's private information.

Specific replies work well. A reply that names the class type, the studio location, and an invitation to return is more useful to future readers than a generic thank-you. Keep each reply accurate to what happened. When a complaint comes in, direct the conversation to the studio's public contact channel rather than debating details in public.

## What six-step checklist should a fitness studio follow?

1. Confirm there is only one Google Business Profile for the studio, and flag or merge any duplicates.
2. Match the business name to your signage, with no taglines, hours, phone numbers, or URLs.
3. Make the address or service area accurate and precise.
4. Select the fewest categories that describe your core classes.
5. Write a description with no links, prices, or promotions.
6. Check that your website pages are indexed, then wait a few weeks before judging results.

Work through the list once, then review it each quarter as your class schedule changes.
