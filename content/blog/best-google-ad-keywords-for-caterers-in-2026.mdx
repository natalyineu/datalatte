The best Google Ads keywords for a caterer name the event and the place: "wedding caterers" plus your city, "corporate caterers" plus your city, and "lunch catering near me". Public keyword data for catering is thin and mostly global, so the figures below are a starting point. Confirm local search volume in Keyword Planner before setting a budget. Then use a simple cost-per-booking calculation to decide whether a keyword group is worth paying for.

## Which Keywords Should a Caterer Start With?

Group keywords by who is buying. A couple planning a wedding and an office manager ordering lunch ask different questions, so each group needs its own ad copy and landing page. Sending both to one generic page usually lowers click-through and makes the enquiry form harder to complete.

The table uses global monthly search volumes and cost-per-click estimates from AdTargeting, a third-party keyword tool. These are worldwide figures, not your city, so treat them as a guide to relative demand.

| Intent group | Example keyword | Global monthly searches | Global CPC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Near me | caterer near me | 135,000 | $2.60 |
| Weddings | wedding caterers | 22,200 | $2.45 |
| Corporate | corporate caterers | 6,600 | $5.51 |
| Office lunch | lunch catering near me | 6,500 | $12.13 |
| Private parties | birthday party catering | 1,900 | $1.53 |

Source: [AdTargeting catering keyword lists](https://adtargeting.io/industry/catering-keywords) (secondary, global data).

The biggest volume sits in broad "caterer" terms, but those searches are often students, job seekers or people far outside the delivery area. Event-plus-place phrases are narrower and usually cheaper to test. Build each group around a city or suburb the business actually serves, such as "wedding caterers [city]".

## How Should a Caterer Match Keywords to Capacity?

Pick keywords the business can actually fulfil. A two-person catering team cannot take a 200-guest corporate contract, so an ad for that order brings enquiries that have to be turned down. BatLinks, an SEO agency, makes the same point for organic search: target phrases you can realistically deliver on rather than competing with larger caterers for generic terms ([BatLinks, catering keywords](https://batlinks.io/catering-keywords/), secondary).

Match the group to the size of the order. Small teams can run "birthday party catering" and "office lunch catering" with a clear menu and price. Large weddings need a longer sales conversation, so a call or quote form works better than a raw click. If the business also sells desserts or flowers, compare the intent groups with [keywords for bakeries](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-bakeries-in-2026) and [keywords for florists](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-florists-in-2026), since wedding searches overlap.

## What Does $750 a Month Buy a Caterer?

No source gives a standard catering budget, so this example works backwards from one booking. Every input labelled assumption is a placeholder to replace with real figures from the business.

| Monthly budget | $750 | Assumption |
| Average CPC | $2.69 | WordStream all-industry search average |
| Clicks | about 279 | $750 divided by $2.69 |
| Conversion rate to enquiry | 5% | Assumption |
| Enquiries (calls and quote forms) | about 14 | 279 times 5% |
| Enquiries that book | 25% | Assumption |
| Booked events | about 3.5 | 14 times 25% |
| Cost per booked event | about $214 | $750 divided by 3.5 |
| Average event value | $900 | Assumption |
| Revenue from booked events | about $3,150 | 3.5 times $900 |

Now run the same budget on a corporate keyword. At the $5.51 global CPC for "corporate caterers", clicks fall to about 136, enquiries to about 7, and bookings to about 1.7. Cost per booked event rises to about $441. That can still pay if corporate orders are larger, but only if the booking rate holds.

The lesson is to test one group at a time and track cost per booked event, not cost per click. Confirming the booking rate matters more than raising the budget, because a higher budget only multiplies whatever the booking rate turns out to be.

## Which Negative Terms Should Caterers Exclude?

Negative keywords stop ads from showing on searches that will never turn into a sale. Caterers usually waste spend on job searches, do-it-yourself recipes and suppliers.

Start with these groups and add terms from the search terms report each week:

- Jobs and training: jobs, hiring, salary, course, school
- Do it yourself: recipe, how to, diy, at home
- Suppliers: wholesale, catering supply, equipment, for sale
- Free: free, template, sample menu

Coffee and food-service advertisers face similar waste, so the same review habit applies to [keywords for coffee shops](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-coffee-shops-in-2026). Plural and singular forms can need separate entries, so check the report rather than assuming one form covers both.

## How Should a Caterer Measure Results?

Count booked events and the cost of each one. A click that becomes a call is worth more than ten clicks that leave the page, and an enquiry that never books is not a sale.

Set up call tracking and quote-form submissions as conversions. Review results monthly against the sales log, so it is clear which intent group produces bookings at a cost the business can afford. Ask each enquiry where it came from, because a caller who found the business through a search ad may not say so unless asked directly.

## FAQ

### What are the best Google Ads keywords for caterers?

Event-plus-place phrases usually fit best, such as "wedding caterers [city]" and "corporate caterers [city]". Check local search volume in Keyword Planner, because the figures in this guide are global.

### How much does a click cost for a caterer?

No public benchmark lists catering as its own category. The AdTargeting global CPCs range from $1.53 for birthday party catering to $12.13 for lunch catering near me. WordStream's all-industry search average is $2.69.

### Which match type should a caterer start with?

Phrase and exact match give tighter control for a small budget. Broaden only when the search terms report is reviewed weekly and a negative list is kept up to date.

### Which negative keywords matter most?

Job, recipe, free and supplier terms usually drain budget first. Review the search terms report each week and add new ones as they appear.

### How do I know if the budget is working?

Compare cost per booked event with the profit a booking brings in. In the worked example, $750 a month gives about 3.5 bookings at about $214 each, but only if the assumed rates hold.
