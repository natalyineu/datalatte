## Which Keywords Should a Caterer Start With?

Group keywords by who is buying. Each group needs its own ad copy and landing page, because a bride and an office manager ask different questions.

| Intent group | Example keywords | Suggested match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weddings | wedding catering [city], wedding caterer near me | Phrase |
| Corporate | corporate catering [city], office lunch catering | Phrase |
| Private parties | party catering [city], birthday catering | Phrase |
| Cuisine | bbq catering [city], vegan catering [city] | Phrase |
| Ready to buy | catering quote [city], catering for 50 people | Exact |

Replace the brackets with your city or suburb. Volumes are not listed here on purpose. Open Keyword Planner, enter your seed terms and your service area, and read the monthly search ranges for your own market.

## Why Do Event and Location Terms Work Best?

A search such as "wedding catering" plus a city carries two signals at once: what the person wants and where. Broad terms like "caterer" attract students, job seekers and people far outside your delivery range.

No public conversion data proves near-me terms convert at a given multiple, so test it. Put the group with your most profitable orders on its own budget and compare cost per booked event after a month.

## How Do Match Types Work?

Google's help describes three types. Broad match is the default and shows ads on related searches, even ones that do not contain your words. Phrase match shows ads on searches that include the meaning of your keyword. Exact match shows ads on searches with the same meaning or intent, and gives the most control with the least reach.

For a small catering budget, start with phrase and exact, and add broad only when you review the Search terms report weekly and have a negative list ready.

## What Negative Keywords Does a Caterer Need?

Negatives stop ads from showing on searches you do not want. Google's help says negative keywords do not match close variants, so "job" will not block "jobs" unless you add both. Phrase and exact negatives also behave differently from broad negatives, so check the match type of each.

Start with these themes, then grow the list from your Search terms report:

- Work and training: jobs, hiring, salary, course, school
- Do it yourself: recipe, how to, diy, at home
- Trade buyers you do not serve: wholesale, supplier, equipment, for sale
- Free: free, sample menu pdf, template

## How Much Should a Caterer Spend?

No source gives a standard catering budget, so work backwards from one booking. If an average event brings gross profit of a few hundred dollars, you can afford a certain cost per booked event. Divide that by your lead-to-booking rate to find the cost per lead you can pay.

## FAQ

### What are the best Google Ads keywords for caterers?

Event plus place terms, such as wedding catering [city] and corporate catering [city], usually fit best. Confirm volumes for your area in Keyword Planner.

### How much does a click cost for a caterer?

No category named catering appears in the LocaliQ 2026 benchmarks. The nearest, Restaurants and Food, averages $2.05 per click. Your market may be higher.

### Which match type should I use?

Start with phrase and exact. Add broad match only with a long negative list and weekly Search terms reviews.

### Which negative keywords matter most?

Jobs, recipes, free, wholesale and equipment terms. Add singular and plural forms yourself because negatives do not match close variants.

### How do I measure success?

Count booked events and cost per booked event, not clicks. Set up call and form conversions, as in our [setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-ads-for-your-small-business-in-2026-step-by-step-with-screenshots).
