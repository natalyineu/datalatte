A hair salon in St. Louis, MO appears in the Google Maps local pack when its Google Business Profile (GBP) is complete, its name, address and phone match across the web, and recent reviews describe its services. The local pack is the top three business listings in local search results ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-audit-checklist-15-things-to-fix-today), secondary). Google Business Profile is a free tool for managing a business's presence across Google Search and Maps ([Keepers Digital](https://keepersdigital.com/en-ca/resources/local-search/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/), secondary). No source guarantees a ranking, so the steps below focus on the signals the sources describe.

## Which Google Business Profile settings matter most for a St. Louis salon?

Start with the category. Adwave says analysis of millions of profiles shows the primary category is the number one ranking factor for getting into the Local Pack, though it does not name the analysis ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-categories-choosing-the-right-ones), secondary). The same article recommends looking at the top 3-5 businesses in local map results for a service search to see which category they use. Run that check for "hair salon St. Louis" and note what competitors choose.

Accuracy comes next. Adwave advises making hours, phone number and address 100% accurate ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). The business description allows 750 characters ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary). Lead with the service and the St. Louis location, and write sentences a client would read rather than a keyword list.

Photos and posts are the ongoing work. Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos, and says a robust gallery gets far more clicks and calls ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Use real work: colour results, the chair area, the reception desk. Google's help page says a post shows publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not appear ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), primary). Adwave suggests at least one new post a week.

A storefront salon usually shows its street address. Only a business that works from home or serves customers at their location can hide it and become a Service Area Business ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).

## How many reviews does a hair salon need to compete on Google Maps?

Google states that reviews show next to a business's profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), primary). A third-party analysis by Blogging Wizard of 2 million GBP pages found that businesses with over 200 Google reviews are significantly more likely to appear in the local pack ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-for-your-local-business), secondary). That is a correlation, not a rule. Treat 200 as a benchmark to track rather than a target you must hit.

Review text also helps. Ask for feedback after each appointment in the same way every time, and name the service in the request, since customers write about what they experienced. Reply to every review. Replies are public and let the salon describe its services and neighbourhood in plain language. For a closer look at how review counts compare across a single city, see [Denver hair salon review benchmarks](/blog/denver-hair-salons-google-maps-review-benchmarks-2026).

Google's AI answers draw on business profile data, website content and reviews, according to a transition summary cited by Adwave ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-business-profile-q-and-a), secondary). A complete profile and a clear website therefore support each other.

## Do citations still matter for a St. Louis salon?

Yes, because they must agree. Adwave says the name, address and phone number must be exactly the same on the website, the GBP, and major directories such as Yelp, Apple Maps and Bing Places ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/voice-search-optimization-for-local-businesses), secondary). Its beginner guide goes further: the listing should match down to the comma and to whether the address uses "St." or "Street" ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary).

Fix mismatches before adding new listings. A wrong digit copied into ten new directories creates ten new errors. Apple is also worth checking. One Adwave article estimates that Apple Business Connect holds a 7% share in a maps-only comparison and is growing, but the page does not name the data behind that figure ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/apple-business-connect-the-google-business-profile-alternative-youre-ignoring), secondary).

## How should the website support the Google Business Profile?

Your site and profile should use the same name, address and phone. Put the primary service and the St. Louis location in the page title and main heading, and give each major service its own page rather than one long services list. Structured data can name the business type, and schema.org includes a HairSalon type. Embedding a map on the contact page is a small, practical step; the [Google Maps embed guide](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) covers the setup.

Paid search draws on the same data. Google's help page says location asset data in Google Ads comes directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and corrections made there flow into linked Google Ads accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182?hl=en), primary). If you run ads as well, see the [St. Louis Google Ads guide for hair salons](/blog/google-ads-for-hair-salons-in-st-louis-mo).

Booking is the final step. Booksy states that Reserve with Google lets clients book directly from Search and Maps in under 30 seconds ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/salon-marketing), secondary). If your booking software supports it, the booking path sits inside the listing.

## What does a local SEO budget look like for a salon?

The sources give no price for a St. Louis salon's local SEO, so the example below uses assumed inputs. Replace them with your own figures from your profile insights and booking records.

Inputs (all assumption):
- Monthly GBP actions (calls, direction requests, website visits): 80
- Share of those actions that become a booked appointment: 15%
- Average appointment value: $85
- Monthly cost of tools such as rank tracking or a citation audit: $30

Calculation:
1. 80 actions × 15% = 12 bookings per month
2. 12 bookings × $85 = $1,020 monthly revenue
3. $1,020 minus $30 tool cost = $990 monthly net

Posting cadence is a fixed input. At one post a week, a profile publishes 52 posts a year, which matches Adwave's minimum suggestion. The cost of the posting time is the hours you give to it, so record them alongside the revenue figures.

## How do you track whether local SEO is working?

Adwave lists six local search metrics worth tracking: GBP impressions, map-pack views, clicks to call, direction requests, website visits from GBP, and branded search lift over time ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-search-intent), secondary). Record them monthly in one sheet so changes are easy to compare.

Check rankings directly. Search for your core service in a private browser window from the area you serve, and note where the salon appears in the local pack. Repeat the check for each main service, such as balayage or men's cuts, because different services can produce different results.

Expect the first signal to be your own name. Adwave estimates that a newly verified profile may start ranking for its business name in the Map Pack within a few weeks, though this is the vendor's own estimate ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary). Broader service rankings take longer. The [AI optimisation guide for Google Business Profile](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization) and the [best Google Ads keywords for hair salons](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-hair-salons-in-2026) cover the next steps once the basics are stable.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a hair salon?

Yes. Google states that its Business Profile is free to set up and describes it as a free profile on Google Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/ca-en/business-profile/), primary). Free setup does not guarantee a ranking, so the accuracy, photo and review steps still matter.

### How many photos should a salon upload?

Adwave recommends at least 15 high-quality photos ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary). Another Adwave guide suggests at least 10 ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/local-seo-for-small-business-a-beginners-guide-to-the-map-pack), secondary). Start at 15 and update the gallery as new work is done.

### Are reviews the biggest ranking factor?

The sources point to the profile as the largest factor. Adwave's 2025 benchmark says GBP signals represent 32% of local pack ranking weight ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/multi-location-local-seo-audit), secondary). Ahrefs cites a BrightLocal survey in which 36% of SEOs in 2021 rated the GBP as the most important map pack factor ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/seo/local-seo), secondary). Reviews matter as part of the profile, but the sources do not rank them above it.

### How often should a salon post on its profile?

Google's help page says a post appears publicly only when its status is Live, so check that each post has been approved ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), primary). Adwave suggests at least one new post a week ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/google-maps-marketing-getting-found-by-nearby-customers), secondary).
