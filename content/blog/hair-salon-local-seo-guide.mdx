A hair salon ranks in Google's map results by matching the search (relevance), being close to the searcher (distance) and being well known (prominence). Google's own help page lists those three factors and says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. It also says there is no way to pay for a better local ranking.

You cannot move your salon, so the work is on relevance and prominence: a complete Business Profile, a steady flow of recent reviews, consistent listings and a website that says where you are and what you do.

## How does Google decide which salons rank?

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches the search. A profile that lists balayage as a service has a better case for "balayage near me".
- **Distance:** how far you are from the person searching, or from the area named in the search.
- **Prominence:** how well known you are. Google says this depends partly on how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have.

Google does not publish a weighting or a review count that guarantees a top spot. Any claim that "three reviews a week will get you into the top three" is a guess, not a rule.

## Step 1: How do you set up your Google Business Profile?

Start with the free profile, because it feeds the map results directly. Google's guidelines set a few rules worth following exactly ([Google Help: guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)).

- **Name:** use your real-world name as it appears on your storefront. Taglines, hours, phone numbers and service or location details are not allowed in it.
- **Categories:** use as few as describe your core business, and pick the most specific one. For most salons the primary category is Hair salon. Add another only if it describes a real part of the business.
- **Description:** describe your services plainly. Google says not to focus on promotions or prices in this field, and links are not allowed.

List each service you offer, such as cuts, colour, balayage and kids cuts, so the profile can match service searches. Keep hours current, including holidays.

## Step 2: How do you get more Google reviews?

Reviews are the most visible trust signal. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses and 41% always do. It also found 74% care only about reviews from the last three months ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)). Recent reviews matter, so aim for a steady flow, not a one-off push.

Google says you can ask customers to visit a link or scan a QR code to leave a review. Ask after the appointment, when the client has seen the result, and send the link by text or show a QR card at the till.

Do not offer a discount or free service for a review. Google calls offering incentives in exchange for reviews prohibited ([Google Help: reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)).

## How should you reply to reviews?

Reply to all of them, promptly. BrightLocal found 19% of consumers expect a same-day reply, 32% by the next day and 81% within a week.

Google advises keeping replies short and conversational and not sending the same thank-you to everyone. For a negative review, apologise where appropriate, do not share private details and offer to continue by phone or email if the issue is complex. Do not use replies to push deals.

## Step 3: Why do citations and consistent details matter?

A citation is any mention of your salon's name, address and phone number on another site. Matching details everywhere remove doubt about which business is yours. Google has not published how much weight it gives citations, so treat this as housekeeping, not a ranking lever.

Check these first:

- Your Facebook page and any booking platform you use, such as Fresha or Vagaro.
- Apple Maps and Bing Places.
- Yelp, if it is active in your market.
- Your local chamber of commerce listing.

Search your salon name and city, open every result that mentions you, and fix any wrong phone number, address or hours.

## Step 4: What should your salon website include?

Your site needs to say what you are and where you are. Put your name, address and phone number as text in the footer, add a page per main service, and embed your map on the contact page.

Add LocalBusiness structured data. Google's documentation says local business markup lets you tell Google about hours and other details, requires only name and address, and does not guarantee any search feature will appear ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)). Schema.org has a HairSalon type that sits under LocalBusiness ([Schema.org](https://schema.org/HairSalon)).

Check your site on a phone and compress large images. Page speed is a user problem first: a slow booking page loses people before ranking matters.

## Step 5: How do you build local authority?

Google ties prominence partly to links from other websites, so a few local links help. Realistic sources:

- A listing on your chamber of commerce or business association site.
- Sponsoring a local event or team that lists sponsors with a link.
- Partnerships with nearby wedding venues, photographers or gyms who link to you.
- A local news or neighbourhood blog feature, if you have a real story.

Quality and relevance matter more than count. No published figure says how many links a salon needs.

## How do you track progress?

Check three things monthly. Business Profile performance shows searches, calls, website clicks and direction requests. Google Search Console shows which queries bring people to your website. A rank tracker is optional once you are actively optimising. Compare month to month and change one thing at a time.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to rank in the map pack?
Google does not publish a timeline. Rankings depend on competition in your area, your profile and your reviews. Track your own results monthly instead of relying on a promised date.

### I have few reviews and my competitor has many. Can I catch up?
Possibly. BrightLocal found 74% of consumers care only about reviews from the last three months, so recent reviews carry weight. Keep asking every client and reply to each review.

### Can I pay Google to rank higher in the map results?
No. Google's help page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Ads are a separate, paid placement.

### Should each stylist have their own profile?
Google's guidelines are written for the business. List stylists on your website and keep one profile for the salon unless Google's rules for practitioners apply to you. Check the current guidelines.

### Do I need structured data?
It is optional. Google says it does not guarantee any feature will appear, but it gives Google clear details about your hours and address.
