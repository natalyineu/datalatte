You can use an AI assistant such as ChatGPT or Claude to draft the written parts of your Google Business Profile in about 30 minutes, provided you feed it accurate facts and edit what it produces. AI helps with wording, not with truth: it cannot know your real hours, services or prices.

The time-saver is a structured workflow. Gather your facts, ask the AI for specific drafts, check every line, and publish. Here is a 30 minute plan broken into blocks, with prompts you can copy.

## Before you start (5 minutes)

Collect the raw material in one note:

- Business name exactly as on your signage
- Address, phone, website and opening hours
- Your main service or product and 5 to 10 others
- Prices or price ranges you are happy to publish
- What makes you different (specialty, audience, experience, location)
- The neighbourhoods and towns you serve
- Three or four phrases customers repeatedly use in reviews

Do not paste private data such as customer names or contact details into a public AI tool.

## Block 1: Check the foundation (5 minutes)

AI cannot fix a wrong category or missing hours. Open your profile and confirm:

- Primary category is the most specific accurate option (see our [categories guide](/blog/google-business-profile-categories-guide))
- Secondary categories genuinely apply
- Hours, including holiday hours, are correct
- Name, address and phone match your website
- Appointment, menu or booking links work

## Block 2: Business description (5 minutes)

Google gives you up to 750 characters for the description. Use a prompt like this:

> Write a business description of at most 700 characters for a [type of business] in [town]. Facts: [list your facts]. Tone: warm and plain. Put what we do and where in the first sentence. Do not use superlatives like "best" or invent awards, prices or years in business. No promotional language such as discounts or phone numbers.

Review the draft. Remove anything untrue. Make sure the first sentence or two read well on their own, since only the beginning may be visible before people expand it. Keep wording natural and avoid forcing in keywords.

## Block 3: Services and products (5 minutes)

Services give Google and customers a concrete list of what you do. Ask for short, clear entries:

> Write a one-sentence description (under 150 characters) for each of these services: [list]. Use plain language a customer would understand. Do not include prices unless I give them.

Add the service names exactly as customers search for them, such as "full groom for small dogs" or "balayage". Include prices where you can, since transparency tends to reduce time-wasting calls and builds trust.

## Block 4: Posts for the month (5 minutes)

Posts show activity and can promote offers or events. Ask for a batch to schedule or paste across the month:

> Suggest 4 Google Business Profile post ideas for a [type of business] for [month], with a 60 to 100 word draft for each. Include one seasonal idea and one customer-question idea. Leave placeholders where you need real details like dates or prices.

Add a real photo to each post and a button such as "Book" or "Learn more". For more ideas, see our guide to [Google Business Profile posts](/blog/google-business-profile-posts).

## Block 5: Review replies (5 minutes)

Replying to reviews shows you care and gives context to future readers. Use AI to create starting points, not final copy. For example:

> Draft a short, warm reply (40 to 70 words) to this 5-star review: "[paste review]". Mention one specific detail from the review. Do not sound scripted and do not ask for anything.

For negative reviews:

> Draft a calm, professional reply to this 2-star review: "[paste review]". Acknowledge the concern, apologise only for what is genuinely our responsibility, and invite them to contact us at [phone or email]. Do not argue or reveal personal information.

Always edit so each reply sounds different and human. Posting identical responses across dozens of reviews looks automated. For a broader approach, read [how to get more 5-star Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-5-star-google-reviews-local-business).

## What AI should not do

| Do not | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Invent claims, awards or years in business | Misleads customers and may breach Google's policies |
| Write fake reviews | Violates Google policy and risks suspension |
| Stuff keywords or locations into your business name | Name changes should match your real-world name |
| Copy generic descriptions used by competitors | Adds nothing distinctive |
| Reply to reviews with auto-generated text, unreviewed | Can sound tone-deaf or reveal wrong details |

## Quick tools that complement AI

- **Google Business Profile dashboard**: Where you edit and publish.
- **Google Search Console and Analytics**: See how people reach your website.
- **Local rank trackers**: BrightLocal, Local Falcon or Whitespark show how you appear in map results, usually on paid plans from roughly the price of a few coffees up to a modest monthly subscription.
- **Review management tools**: Many tools can send review requests and alert you to new reviews.

## Keep it up after the 30 minutes

A weekly 10 minute routine is more valuable than a single sprint:

- Reply to any new reviews
- Publish one post or upload a few new photos
- Check for edits or suggestions from users and fix inaccuracies
- Review your insights monthly for calls, direction requests and website taps

Finish by comparing your profile with our [optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) to catch anything missed.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use AI to write my Google Business Profile description?**
A: Yes, as a first draft, provided you supply accurate facts and edit the result. Make sure it complies with Google's guidelines, including no links, phone numbers or promotional claims in the description.

**Q: Will Google penalise AI-written content on my profile?**
A: Google's guidelines focus on accuracy and policy compliance, not on how text was written. Misleading or spammy content is the risk, whether human or AI-written.

**Q: Which AI tool is best for this?**
A: ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can all do the job. Choose the one you are comfortable with and be consistent with your prompts and facts.

**Q: How often should I update my profile?**
A: Review it weekly for reviews and posts and monthly for photos, services and hours, and update immediately when anything changes.
