Ahrefs is useful for local SEO in three areas: finding local keywords, checking what competitors rank for, and researching backlinks. It does not track Google Maps rankings, Google Business Profile (GBP) performance or local citations, so most small businesses pair it with free Google tools or a local-specific tool like BrightLocal or Whitespark.

If you have a single location and a tight budget, you may not need a paid Ahrefs plan at all. This guide shows which reports matter, what to skip, and how to decide.

## What Ahrefs Can Do for Local SEO

Ahrefs is a general SEO platform built around organic search and backlinks. For a local business, that covers:

- **Keyword research:** finding what people in your market search for, including "service + city" and "near me" style phrases.
- **Competitor research:** seeing which pages and keywords bring organic traffic to nearby competitors.
- **Backlink research:** finding who links to local competitors so you can pursue similar links (chambers of commerce, local press, suppliers, directories).
- **Site audits:** flagging technical issues on your own website, such as missing titles, broken links and slow pages.

## What Ahrefs Cannot Do for Local SEO

Knowing the limits saves money.

- **No Google Maps / local pack tracking.** Ahrefs reports organic results, not where you appear in the map pack. Local rank-grid tools such as BrightLocal or Whitespark are built for that.
- **No GBP data.** Calls, direction requests, photo views and review activity live in your Google Business Profile, not in Ahrefs.
- **No citation management.** Ahrefs does not audit your name, address and phone consistency across directories. Whitespark and BrightLocal specialize in this.
- **Estimated numbers.** Search volume and traffic figures are estimates. Local volumes for small towns or neighborhoods are often missing or rounded. Treat them as relative indicators.

## Ahrefs Reports That Matter for Local Businesses

### Keywords Explorer

Enter your core service (for example "dog groomer") and review Matching terms and Related terms. Look for city, neighborhood and "near me" variations with low Keyword Difficulty. Low-volume local terms are fine: a search for "coffee shop north portland" may be small, but the searcher is nearby and ready to visit.

Because local volume data is rough, cross-check promising terms in Google Keyword Planner and Google Search Console.

### Site Explorer: Organic Keywords and Top Pages

Enter a local competitor's domain to see the keywords and pages that bring them organic traffic. Look at the Top Pages report for topics your shared audience clearly cares about, then ask whether you can create a more useful, more local version.

### Content Gap

Add your site and up to a few local competitors to see keywords they rank for that you do not. This is a practical content roadmap for blog posts and service pages.

### Backlinks and Link Intersect

Check who links to competitors but not to you. For local businesses the best targets are usually local news sites, business associations, sponsorships, supplier pages and local blogs. Quality and local relevance matter more than volume.

### Site Audit

Run it periodically on your own website. Fix missing titles and meta descriptions, broken links, slow pages and missing alt text first. Make sure your location pages include your name, address and phone number, and consider LocalBusiness schema markup.

### Rank Tracker

Rank Tracker can follow your organic positions for keywords in a chosen location. It is useful for tracking your website's pages, but remember it does not show map pack position. Checking monthly is plenty for most local businesses.

## Ahrefs vs BrightLocal vs Whitespark

| Need | Ahrefs | BrightLocal | Whitespark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keyword and competitor research | Strong | Limited | Limited |
| Backlink research | Strong | Basic | Basic |
| Local pack / Maps rank tracking | No | Yes | Yes |
| Citation audit and building | No | Yes | Yes (a core strength) |
| Review monitoring | No | Yes | Yes |
| Best for | Content and link research | All-in-one local reporting | Citations and local rank tracking |

Pricing changes often and depends on plan and billing period. Ahrefs plans have historically started around the low hundreds of dollars per month, while BrightLocal and Whitespark have generally been priced lower for single-location use. Check each vendor's pricing page for current figures, and look for free trials or limited free tools before committing.

## How a Small Business Should Use Ahrefs

1. **Start with free tools.** Set up Google Search Console and your Google Business Profile first. They show real data about your site and your listing, at no cost. Our [GBP optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) is a good starting point.
2. **Use Ahrefs' free tools or a short subscription.** Many owners get the most value from one focused month: run keyword research, a content gap report and a competitor backlink review, export the results, then pause.
3. **Add a local tool if Maps visibility is the goal.** If most of your customers come from the map pack, BrightLocal or Whitespark will tell you more than Ahrefs.
4. **Act on a short list.** Pick 10 to 20 local keywords, assign each to a service page or blog post, and build a few local links. Data you do not act on has no value.
5. **Measure with real outcomes.** Calls, direction requests, bookings and form fills matter more than rankings.

## Simple Local Keyword Workflow

1. Write 10 ways a customer might describe what you do ("best flat white in [city]", "quiet coffee shop to work").
2. Enter them in Keywords Explorer and collect Matching and Related terms.
3. Keep only terms with local or service intent and manageable difficulty.
4. Group them: ready to buy (service pages), researching (blog posts), looking for you (homepage and GBP).
5. Prioritize by relevance to your services and how realistic ranking is, then build a content calendar.

## FAQ

**Q: Does Ahrefs track Google Maps rankings?**
A: No. Ahrefs reports organic search results, not local pack or Maps positions. For Maps rankings, use a local tool such as BrightLocal or Whitespark, or a local rank grid.

**Q: Is Ahrefs worth it for a small local business?**
A: Sometimes. It is most useful if you publish content, face strong local competition, or want to research backlinks. If you have not yet set up Search Console and a well-optimized Google Business Profile, start there. Check Ahrefs' current pricing before deciding.

**Q: What are the best alternatives to Ahrefs for local SEO?**
A: BrightLocal and Whitespark are popular for local rank tracking, citations and reviews. Google Search Console and Google Keyword Planner are free options for keyword and performance data.

**Q: Can Ahrefs show my Google Business Profile performance?**
A: No. Calls, direction requests, photo views and searches that found your listing are in your Google Business Profile dashboard.

**Q: How often should I check Ahrefs for local SEO?**
A: Monthly is enough for most local businesses. Daily rank tracking adds noise rather than insight for a single-location business.
