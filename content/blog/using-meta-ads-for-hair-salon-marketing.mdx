Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram ads) work for a hair salon when you target a tight radius around your location, promote one specific offer with real photos of your work, and track bookings rather than clicks. Start small, give the campaign a few weeks to gather data, and only scale what is actually filling chairs.

## Why Meta Ads Suit Hair Salons

Hair is a visual business, and Facebook and Instagram are visual platforms. People scroll past a great balayage or a clean fade, save it, and book later. Unlike search ads, Meta Ads reach people who were not looking for a salon yet but are open to the idea, which makes them a good complement to your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) and local search presence.

Meta Ads are not magic, though. Results depend heavily on your offer, your photos, your pricing and how easy it is to book. Treat the first month as a test, not a guarantee.

## Setting Up Your Meta Ads Account

To get started you need a Facebook Page and Instagram business account for the salon, a Meta Business Suite or Business Manager account, and a payment method. Connect your Instagram account to your Page so ads can run on both platforms.

Before spending anything, install the Meta pixel (or use the Conversions API through your booking platform) so Meta can see when someone books. Without this, you are guessing.

## Creating Your First Ad Campaign

Once your account is set up, create a campaign with a leads or sales-style objective rather than simple reach or engagement. Then work through four decisions:

1. **Location.** Target a radius around the salon. Many salons start with roughly 5 to 10 miles in a city or suburb, and wider in rural areas. Adjust based on how far your clients actually travel.
2. **Audience.** Keep it broad within that radius, and let your creative do the filtering. Detailed interest targeting matters less than it used to.
3. **Budget.** Pick a daily budget you can sustain for at least three to four weeks. A small, steady budget beats a big burst followed by silence.
4. **Offer and creative.** One clear message, such as a new-client offer on a specific service, with a booking link or call button.

## Choosing the Right Ad Format

There are several ad formats on Meta, including image ads, video ads (including Reels) and carousel ads. Each has its own benefits, so choose the one that suits your goal.

<Callout type="example">For example, if you specialize in color treatments, a short video or a before-and-after carousel showing your work is often a stronger way to attract potential clients than a stock photo.</Callout>

| Format | Best for | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single image | A simple new-client offer | Use a real photo of your own work |
| Short video or Reel | Color, cuts and transformations | Keep it short and put the hook first |
| Carousel | Before-and-after or service menus | Lead with your strongest result |

## Measuring the Success of Your Ad Campaign

Track the numbers that connect to revenue: cost per booking or lead, number of new clients who mention the ad, and repeat visits from those clients. Reach and clicks are useful diagnostics, but they do not pay the rent. A cheap click that never books is not a win.

Check results weekly, not daily. Meta's delivery needs time to settle, and constant tinkering resets learning. If you want help setting up tracking, see our [analytics service](/services/analytics).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Boosting posts instead of building campaigns in Ads Manager.
- Sending people to a homepage instead of a booking page.
- Running vague discounts rather than a specific offer with a deadline.
- Judging results after three days.
- Using only stock photos when your own work is your best asset.

## Next Steps

Fix the basics first: a clear booking link, a working pixel and three to five strong photos or short videos. Then launch one campaign, one offer and one radius, and review after a few weeks. ## FAQ

**Q: How much should I spend on Meta Ads as a new salon?**
A: A common starting point is roughly $200 to $300 per week, though the right figure depends on your market and your average ticket. That is usually enough to gather meaningful data. Run it for about three weeks, and if bookings are not coming in, fix the creative, targeting or offer before raising the budget.

**Q: Can't I just boost posts on my salon's Facebook page?**
A: Boosting is simpler, but it gives you less control. You get fewer targeting and conversion options and weaker tracking. Running ads through Meta Ads Manager lets you choose a conversion goal, test creative and retarget people who engaged.

**Q: Should I target women only, or include men?**
A: It depends on your services, but do not exclude a group by assumption. If you cut men's hair or offer beard trims and color, leave gender open and let the creative do the filtering. A photo of a men's fade will attract different people than a photo of highlights.

**Q: How do I know if my ads are actually bringing people through the door?**
A: Use three methods together. Install the Meta pixel and track a booking or lead event, use a dedicated phone number or booking link for ad traffic, and ask every new client how they found you. The last one catches people who saw an ad, then searched for you later.

**Q: My salon is in a small town. Will Meta Ads work?**
A: It can, but small audiences are harder for the algorithm to optimize. Keep your radius tight, your daily budget modest, and be patient. If the audience is very small, a strong Google Business Profile and local referrals may deliver more per dollar.

**Q: Do I need a professional photographer for ad images?**
A: Not necessarily. Clear, well-lit photos of real work, ideally taken in natural light, often look more authentic than polished stock-style shots. Invest in a photographer only if your current images are consistently poor.
