Meta ads pay for a salon when a new client is worth more than the cost of winning one. LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmark for Beauty and Personal Care is $50.91 per lead and $2.97 per click on lead campaigns. Assume a visit costs the client $85 at a 50% margin: that is $42.50 of profit, so one appointment alone does not cover a $50.91 lead. The offer, the phone-first creative and the booking flow decide whether repeat visits close the gap.

This guide covers those three levers plus tracking, with a break-even calculation. For audience radius and pixel basics, see our [hair salon Facebook ads strategy](/blog/hair-salon-facebook-ads-strategy). Pricing here is in US dollars; the math works in any currency.

## Should a salon run ads on Instagram, Facebook or both?

Both, then let the data decide. Pew's 2025 survey of 5,022 US adults (fielded February 5 to June 18, 2025) found 50% use Instagram: 55% of women, 44% of men, 80% of ages 18 to 29 and 19% of ages 65 and over. Facebook reaches 71% of US adults. The [Pew report](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/) measures platform use, not who books a haircut after an ad.

Recommendations that follow:

- If your clients are mostly women under 40, Instagram placements (Feed, Stories, Reels) deserve a real test.
- If they skew older, keep Facebook placements on.
- Start with automatic placements, then compare cost per booking by placement in Ads Manager after two to three weeks. Exclude a placement only with booking data in hand.

## What should a new-client offer look like?

Design the offer around the second visit, not the first. A discount on visit one is a trade for visits two, three and four. These are recommended structures, not benchmarks:

| Offer | Margin cost | Best creative | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free add-on (gloss, scalp treatment) with first colour | Product plus 10 to 15 minutes | Close-up of the finish | Stylists must actually offer it |
| Fixed first-visit price on a named service | Roughly the discount | Before and after | Attracts bargain hunters |
| Consultation plus tester service | Stylist time | Stylist intro video | Lower show rate if it feels like a pitch |
| Weekday-morning slot offer | Near zero if the chair is empty | Calendar screenshot | Trains regulars to wait for deals |

An empty chair earns nothing, so a weekday-morning discount costs less than the same discount on a Saturday. Write the offer in one line a stranger reads in two seconds: what it is, the price or inclusion, the end date. "Balayage consultation plus free gloss, new clients, book by Sunday" beats "Look amazing this autumn".

## What creative specs does Meta list for Reels and Feed?

Meta's Ads Guide gives these specs; check Ads Manager's preview before exporting, since they change.

- **[Instagram Reels](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/image/instagram-reels):** 9:16 ratio, 1440 x 2560 pixels. Keep roughly 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% of each side free of text and logos. Primary text: 44 characters.
- **[Instagram Feed video](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-feed):** 9:16, 1080 x 1920 pixels, 125 characters of primary text, 4GB maximum, 1 second to 60 minutes.

In practice, the bottom third is unusable: put the stylist's name, price and offer in the middle of the frame. Say the one thing in the video itself, because Reels primary text is short. Always preview every placement.

Five formats suit a salon: a one-service transformation, a stylist intro, a process close-up, a client reaction (with written consent) and a single strong photo. Show the result in the first two seconds. Use only clips where the client agreed to appear, and show real results. Test one variable at a time: same offer, three creatives, two weeks, then keep the booking winner.

## How should the booking flow work after the tap?

This is where salon ad money is most often lost, and the checks below are recommendations. Walk your own flow on your phone, on mobile data:

1. Does the landing page repeat the offer and image from the ad?
2. How many taps lead to a chosen appointment time? Cut every step you can.
3. Can people see real open slots, or only "request a booking"?
4. Is there a tap-to-call button?
5. Does the confirmation say what happens next (reminder text, deposit, address, parking)?

If your booking tool cannot link straight to a service, build a simple page with the offer on top and the booking button right under it. Reply to every ad enquiry within opening hours the same day, ideally within the hour.

## Which tracking events should a salon set up?

Meta's [Pixel reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/meta-pixel/reference/) defines standard events that fit a salon:

| Customer action | Event | Meta's definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Booking confirmed | Schedule | "When a person books an appointment to visit one of your locations" |
| Taps call or text | Contact | "When a person initiates contact with your business via telephone, SMS, email, chat, etc." |
| Taps directions | FindLocation | "When a person searches for a location of your store ... with an intention to visit" |
| Submits enquiry form | Lead | "When a sign up is completed" |

Check your booking platform's help pages for what it can send to a pixel. Then tag links: Meta's [URL builder page](https://www.facebook.com/business/google-analytics/build-your-url) suggests `utm_source` for the traffic source, `utm_medium` for bid type, `utm_campaign` for a campaign and `utm_content` to tell apart ads with different copy and creatives. Name each creative in `utm_content`, such as `transformation-balayage`. Add "How did you hear about us?" to the booking form, because it catches people who saw an ad and searched later.

## At the benchmark cost per lead, do Meta ads pay for a salon?

Worked example. The $50.91 cost per lead is [LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmark](https://localiq.com/blog/facebook-advertising-benchmarks/) for Beauty and Personal Care. LocaliQ does not state its data period, and the category is broader than hair salons. Every other input is an assumption: a $600 monthly budget, and 50% gross margin.

Break-even lead-to-client rate equals cost per lead divided by gross profit per new client:

| Gross profit per new client (assumption) | Break-even share of leads that become attended clients |
| --- | --- |
| $50 | 102% (cannot pay back) |
| $75 | 68% |
| $100 | 51% |
| $150 | 34% |

At $50.91 per lead, $600 buys about 11.8 leads. If 50% become attended clients, that is about 5.9 clients at $102 each. At $75 gross profit per client the month loses about $158. At $150 gross profit per client the same month earns about $284. Getting from $75 to $150 usually means repeat visits, so rebooking at the chair is part of your ad strategy.

How to decide:

- **Under $75 gross profit per new client:** fix rebooking and pricing before spending on ads.
- **$100 or more, and half of leads attend:** a four-week test is reasonable.
- **Leads that never answer the phone:** speed of reply raises the attended rate, which is the cheapest lever you have.

## Do This Today

1. Book your own appointment on your phone and count the taps. Remove one.
2. Write your first-visit offer in one line and pick the quiet slots it covers.
3. Film three vertical clips with key text out of the bottom 35% of the frame.
4. Set Schedule, Contact and Lead events to match your booking flow and test each with a live booking.
5. Tag each ad link with `utm_content`, add a source question to booking, and set a four-week review with a written continue-or-stop number.

## FAQ

**Should I run Instagram only, or Facebook and Instagram?**
Run both for two to three weeks and compare cost per booking by placement. Instagram reaches 55% of US women (Pew 2025), Facebook 71% of US adults.

**How long should my Reels be?**
Meta's Feed spec allows 1 second to 60 minutes, and no official salon length exists. Show the result in the first two seconds and test two lengths.

**What is the best offer for a new client?**
The one that brings a client who stays. A free add-on or consultation usually protects price better than a deep discount. Judge by cost per attended client and rebook rate. More ideas are in our [Instagram bookings guide](/blog/boosting-hair-salon-bookings-with-instagram).

**How do I know which ad produced a booking?**
Combine the Schedule event, `utm_content` names, a unique offer code and a "how did you hear about us" question. No single method is perfect.
