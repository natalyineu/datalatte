Pew Research found that 55% of US women and 80% of adults aged 18 to 29 use Instagram. Your future clients are there. Whether they book depends on three things the ad platform does not control: what you offer, what the ad looks like on a phone, and how many taps it takes to get from the ad to a confirmed appointment.

This is the second of two salon guides. Our [hair salon Facebook ads strategy](/blog/hair-salon-facebook-ads-strategy) covers benchmarks, audience radius and basic pixel setup. This one is about the part you can actually change this week: the offer, the creative, the placement and the booking flow, and how to measure each one. It is written for salon and barbershop owners in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Percentages are US figures; the maths works in any currency.

## Why Instagram Placement Deserves Its Own Plan

Meta ads run on Facebook and Instagram from one Ads Manager account, but the people are not identical. Pew's 2025 survey of 5,022 US adults (fielded February 5 to June 18, 2025) gives these usage rates:

For context, Pew puts Facebook at 71% of US adults, and Instagram use falls to 19% among people aged 65 and older. Two practical points follow:

- If your clients are mostly women under 40, Instagram placements (Feed, Stories, Reels) are not an optional extra. They are where a large share of your audience is.
- If your clients skew older, Facebook placements matter more, so do not switch Facebook off by reflex.

Pew measures who uses a platform, not who books a haircut after seeing an ad. Treat these numbers as a reason to test Instagram placements, not as a forecast.

**How to test placements.** Start with automatic placements so Meta can find cheap bookings across both apps, then open the placement breakdown in Ads Manager after two to three weeks and compare cost per booking by placement. Only exclude a placement once you have its booking data. Meta changes its placement options from time to time, so check the current list in your own Ads Manager account.

## Step 1: Design the Offer Around the Second Visit

A new-client offer is a trade: you give up some margin on visit one to earn visits two, three and four. Most salons price the discount as if the first visit were the whole sale, then wonder why the ads "do not pay".

Think in three layers:

1. **The hook** gets the booking: a consultation, a gloss with first colour, a first-cut price.
2. **The bridge** gets the rebook: before the client leaves, book the next appointment and tell her why (colour needs a refresh, a cut needs shaping).
3. **The ladder** raises the ticket: a toner, treatment or add-on at visit two, not visit one.

| Offer | Margin cost | Best creative | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free add-on (gloss, scalp treatment) with first colour | Product and 10 to 15 minutes | Close-up of the finish | Needs a stylist who will actually offer it |
| Fixed first-visit price on a named service | Roughly the discount | Before and after of that service | Attracts bargain hunters if the price is the only message |
| Consultation plus tester service | Stylist time | Stylist intro video | Lower show rate if the consult feels like a sales pitch |
| Weekday-morning slot offer | Almost zero if the chair is empty | Calendar screenshot with open slots | Trains regulars to wait for deals |

The weekday-morning offer is underrated: an empty chair earns nothing, so a discount on a slot that would sit unused costs you far less than the same discount on a Saturday.

Write the offer in one line a stranger can read in two seconds: what it is, the price or inclusion, the end date. "Balayage consultation plus free gloss, new clients, book by Sunday" beats "Look amazing this autumn".

## Step 2: Build Creative Meant for a Phone Held Upright

Most of your impressions will be on a vertical phone screen, so build for it first and adapt for other shapes afterwards.

**Reels and Stories specs from Meta's Ads Guide.** For Instagram Reels, Meta lists a 9:16 ratio and 1440 x 2560 pixels as the recommended resolution, and asks you to keep roughly 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% of each side free of text, logos and other key elements, so that the profile icon, caption and call-to-action button do not cover them. It recommends 44 characters of primary text on the Reels placements. The Instagram Feed video spec page lists 1080 x 1920 pixels, a 9:16 ratio, a primary text length of 125 characters and a maximum file size of 4GB. Specs change, so confirm them in Ads Manager's preview before you export.

What that means in practice:

- **The bottom third is unusable.** Put the stylist's name, the price and the offer text in the middle of the frame, not along the bottom edge.
- **Primary text is short on Reels.** Say the one thing in the video itself, because the caption may barely be read.
- **Always preview each placement** in Ads Manager. An ad that looks perfect in Feed can have its offer line hidden in Reels.

**Five creative formats that suit a salon:** a one-service transformation, a stylist intro, a process close-up, a client reaction (with written consent) and a single strong photo with one line of offer text. Keep videos short; the length is your call, not a Meta rule. The principle is to show the result in the first two seconds, because people scroll before they read.

**Get consent and be accurate.** Use only photos and clips where the client has agreed to be in your advertising, and show real results. A filtered image that promises a result your stylists cannot deliver produces unhappy first visits and bad reviews. Our guide to Meta ad formats for local businesses compares image, video and carousel in more depth.

**Test one variable at a time.** Same offer, three creatives (a transformation, a stylist intro, a single photo). Run them in one ad set for two weeks, then keep the booking winner and replace the loser. Do not change offer and creative together or you will not know which one worked.

## Step 3: Fix the Booking Flow Before You Pay for Traffic

This is where most salon ad money is lost. A person taps your ad on a train, lands on a page that loads slowly, finds a 12-field form or a phone number to type in, and leaves. You paid for that click.

Walk your own flow on your own phone, on mobile data, with Wi-Fi off:

1. Tap the ad. Does the landing page repeat the offer and the image from the ad?
2. How many taps from the landing page to a chosen appointment time? Aim for as few as your software allows. Every extra step costs bookings.
3. Can the person see real open slots, or only "request a booking"?
4. Is there a tap-to-call button for people who would rather ring?
5. Does the confirmation say what happens next (text reminder, deposit, address, parking)?

If your booking tool cannot deep-link to a service, create a simple landing page with the offer at the top and the booking button directly under it. Our booking form optimisation guide lists the fields to cut.

<Callout type="tip" title="The Fastest Win">
Reply to every enquiry from an ad within the hour during opening hours. A request left until tomorrow often goes to the next salon that answered.
</Callout>

## Step 4: Measure Bookings, Not Likes

Set up measurement before launch. Two layers work together.

**Layer 1: pixel events that match a salon.** Meta's Pixel reference defines standard events you can use: Schedule ("when a person books an appointment to visit one of your locations"), Contact ("when a person initiates contact with your business via telephone, SMS, email, chat, etc."), Lead ("when a sign up is completed") and FindLocation ("when a person searches for a location of your store via a website or app, with an intention to visit"). For a salon the most useful mapping is:

| Customer action | Event to fire | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Booking confirmed | Schedule | Your real goal |
| Taps "call" or "text" | Contact | Many salon enquiries are calls |
| Taps "directions" | FindLocation | Shows local intent |
| Submits an enquiry form | Lead | For consultation requests |

Many booking platforms (Fresha, Vagaro, Booksy and others) can send events to a pixel, but their settings differ, so check your platform's help pages. Our Facebook Pixel setup guide covers installation.

**Layer 2: links and questions the platform cannot lie about.** Meta's UTM builder page suggests `utm_source` to identify Facebook ads as the traffic source, `utm_medium` for bid type, `utm_campaign` for a specific campaign and `utm_content` to tell apart ads with different copy and creatives. Name every creative in `utm_content` (for example `transformation-balayage`) so your website analytics can show which video produced bookings. Then add "How did you hear about us?" to your booking form, and give each offer its own code. The front desk question catches people who saw an ad, then searched for your salon later.

## The Math

Every figure below is an **assumption** for illustration, not a benchmark. The point is to show how much the booking flow and the second visit change the answer. Replace the inputs with your own.

Assumptions shared by all three columns: $600 monthly ad budget, $1.50 cost per click, 80% of booked people attend, an $85 standard ticket with $20 off the first visit ($65), 50% of new clients rebook for an average two further visits at $85, and a 50% gross margin.

| | Weak flow | Average flow | Strong flow |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clicks ($600 / $1.50) | 400 | 400 | 400 |
| Click to booked-appointment rate (assumption) | 2% | 4% | 8% |
| Bookings | 8 | 16 | 32 |
| Attended new clients (80%) | 6.4 | 12.8 | 25.6 |
| Ad cost per attended client | $94 | $47 | $23 |
| Expected revenue per client over six months | $150 | $150 | $150 |
| Gross profit per client (50%) | $75 | $75 | $75 |
| Monthly gross profit minus ad spend | -$120 | +$360 | +$1,320 |

Where the $150 comes from: $65 first visit plus a 50% chance of $170 more (two visits at $85), so $65 + 0.5 x $170 = $150.

What this table says:

- **The break-even booking rate is 2.5%.** Cost per attended client must stay under the $75 gross profit, and $1.50 / (rate x 0.8) = $75 gives a rate of 2.5%. Below that, you lose money on this offer at this click price.
- **Rebooking is a hidden lever.** In the Average column, if only 25% of clients rebook, revenue per client falls to $107.50 and gross profit to about $54, so the month earns about +$88 instead of +$360. If nobody rebooks, it is about -$184. Rebooking at the chair is part of your ad strategy.
- **Booking-flow improvements beat click-price improvements.** Doubling the booking rate from 4% to 8% adds about $960 a month in this example. Halving the click price from $1.50 to $0.75 gives the same $960 gain, because it doubles the clicks. Cheap clicks help, but a page that converts twice as well is usually easier to build than a click that costs half as much.

Check the inputs against your own books. If your real rebook rate is under 25%, work on rebooking before you spend on more ads.

## Do This Today

1. Book your own appointment on your phone, on mobile data, and count the taps. Remove one step.
2. Write your first-visit offer in one line, then decide which quiet time slots it applies to.
3. Film three vertical clips (a transformation, a stylist intro, a process close-up) with the key text kept out of the bottom 35% of the frame.
4. Install the Meta Pixel and set the Schedule, Contact and Lead events to match your booking flow. Test each with a live booking.
5. Tag each ad link with `utm_content`, add a "how did you hear about us" question and set a four-week review date with a written continue-or-stop number.

## FAQ

**Q: Should I run Instagram only, or Facebook and Instagram together?**
A: Start with both and compare results by placement after two to three weeks. Pew's 2025 survey shows 55% of US women and 80% of 18 to 29 year olds use Instagram, so it is worth testing, but Facebook reaches 71% of all US adults, so do not cut it before you have data.

**Q: How long should my Reels be?**
A: There is no official salon figure. Meta's spec pages allow long videos, but a short clip that shows the result in the first two seconds is a sensible start. Test two lengths against each other.

**Q: What is the best offer for a new client?**
A: The one that brings a client who stays. A free add-on or a consultation usually protects your price better than a deep percentage discount. Test one at a time and judge by cost per attended client and rebook rate.

**Q: How do I know which ad produced a booking?**
A: Combine the pixel (Schedule event), `utm_content` names on each ad link, a unique offer code and the question "how did you hear about us?" at booking. No single method is perfect.

**Q: Do I need a professional photographer?**
A: Not at the start. Clear, well-lit photos and short clips of real work often feel more authentic than polished stock-style images. Hire a photographer only if your current images consistently look poor on a phone.
