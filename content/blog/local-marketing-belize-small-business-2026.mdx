## Which Currency Should a Belize Business Show in Ads?

Belize dollars are the official currency. The same guide says that "99.9% of places accept USD" and that change may be given in BZD, in USD, or in a mix of both ([EF Go Ahead Tours](https://www.goaheadtours.com/travel-blog/articles/belize-travel-guide), secondary). Because visitors arrive with USD, a price that shows only BZD can cause questions at the counter and in messages.

Showing both currencies in ads, menus and booking pages avoids that friction. Use the rate of 2 BZD to 1 USD from the same guide when converting, and label the conversion as approximate.

Check the rate before each campaign. The guide is a secondary source, and official exchange rates can change. Confirm the current figure with a bank or a central bank publication before printing prices or setting an ad budget in either currency.

## When Do Visitors Come to Belize?

The guide describes two seasons. The dry season runs from November through April, with temperatures typically in the 80s. The rainy season, which the guide calls the offseason, runs from May through October, with temperatures of 85-90F ([EF Go Ahead Tours](https://www.goaheadtours.com/travel-blog/articles/belize-travel-guide), secondary).

The guide says the dry season brings more travellers to top attractions, so demand and crowds rise. The rainy season brings lusher landscapes, lower prices and fewer visitors at the top sites, according to the same source. Rain can arrive as afternoon or evening showers, and the guide notes that the rainy season overlaps with the Atlantic hurricane season.

Plan promotions around these windows. A tour operator could run an offer aimed at residents during the rainy season and reserve full-price visitor campaigns for the dry season. Timing spend this way lets a small budget go further than advertising at the same level all year.

## How Should a Small Business Test Ads on a Small Budget?

No source checked publishes Belizean ad prices, so any cost-per-result figure would be a guess. The safer approach is a fixed test with labelled assumptions. The table below sets out the inputs, and the only figure sourced from a published guide is the exchange rate.

| Input | Value | Label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tour price | BZD 200 (USD 100 at 2:1) | BZD conversion from source, price assumed |
| Test budget, 30 days | USD 150 | assumption |
| Enquiries from the campaign | 20 | assumption |
| Share of enquiries that book | 15% | assumption |
| Bookings | 3 | calculated (20 × 15%) |
| Gross margin per booking | USD 40 | assumption |
| Margin from bookings | USD 120 | calculated (3 × 40) |
| Result after ad spend | minus USD 30 | calculated (120 − 150) |

The test loses USD 30 at these inputs. Break-even needs 4 bookings (150 ÷ 40 = 3.75, rounded up), so the campaign must either raise the booking rate or lift margin per booking. Replace every assumption with real figures after the first month, and track each enquiry by channel so the bookings count shows which platform produced the sale.

## What Steps Should a Belize Business Take First?

A practical order keeps the first month cheap and measurable:

1. Set prices in BZD and USD, using the 2:1 rate and a stated conversion date.
2. Split the year into a dry-season plan and a rainy-season plan with separate offers.
3. Choose one or two channels where customers already message you.
4. Fix a test budget in one currency and write it down before launch.
5. Tag every enquiry with its source, then count bookings per source each week.
6. Keep the campaign running only if margin from bookings exceeds spend.

Steps 4 and 5 matter most. Without source tags, a sale cannot be credited to a channel, and the decision to scale is based on guesswork.
