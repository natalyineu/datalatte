Most independent interior designers get work through three channels: referrals, a portfolio that shows finished rooms, and a Google Business Profile that appears when someone searches for a designer nearby. Paid search is optional and expensive: the closest published benchmark, WordStream by LocaliQ's Home and Home Improvement category, is $8.33 per click with an 8.05% conversion rate.

The market is mostly small firms. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics counts 97,100 interior designer jobs in 2025, and 26% of designers are self-employed. That means most of your competitors are individuals with a portfolio, so presentation and proof matter more than ad budget.

## How Do Interior Designers Get Clients?

No source used here splits designer clients by channel, so there is no verified percentage for referrals versus search. What you can control is the order you build channels in: first a clear portfolio page for each project type, then a complete Business Profile, then outreach to trades who already meet your future clients.

Ask every finished client for a review and a project photo release. Reviews feed Google's prominence signal, and photos feed the portfolio.

## What Should a Designer's Website Show?

Each project page needs the room type, the size or scope, the budget range if you can share it, before-and-after photos and a short note on the brief. Add the service area in plain text and a clear contact route.

Do not rely on a gallery of unlabelled photos. A visitor deciding between three designers wants to know whether you do kitchens, whole-home renovations or staging, and whether you work in their town. Link each project to a booking or enquiry form.

## How Do You Set Up Google Business Profile for a Design Studio?

Google says to choose the most specific primary category and to add only a few additional ones for services you really offer. Local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence, and you cannot pay for a better ranking. Reviews contribute to prominence and replying to them helps you stand out.

If you work from home and travel to clients, check Google's service-area rules when you set up. Follow the full [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and see how [other businesses win on Maps](/blog/coffee-shops-dominate-google-maps).

## Does Pinterest or Instagram Work for Designers?

Pinterest describes itself as a place where people discover ideas, plan and shop, but its business page gives no dated user or home-decor statistics, so no percentage is quoted here. For a design studio, Pinterest and Instagram work as portfolios that send people to your site. Post finished rooms with the project page link, and track enquiries that mention either.

Instagram works the same way; see [Instagram story ads for salons](/blog/instagram-story-ads-for-salons-bookings) for a format example. Test one channel for six weeks before adding another.

## Is Google Ads Worth It for Interior Designers?

LocaliQ publishes no interior design category. Its Home and Home Improvement category averages $8.33 per click and 8.05% conversion, and the all-industry average is $5.42 and 8.18%. Treat those as rough ranges only.

A design enquiry is worth a lot more than a haircut, so paid search can pay back if your fees are high enough. The risk is clicks from people who want free ideas rather than a designer. Use negative keywords such as "free" and "DIY"; see [negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).
