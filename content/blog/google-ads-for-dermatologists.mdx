## The ROI Math: What a New Dermatology Patient Is Worth

Before spending on ads, calculate your numbers. Here's the framework:

**Patient Lifetime Value (LTV):** A new patient who comes for a skin cancer screening may return for annual checks, cosmetic consultations, and ongoing treatments. Conservative estimate for LTV over 12 months: $800–$1,500 for a mixed medical/cosmetic practice. Cosmetic-only patients who return for Botox every 3–4 months represent $1,200–$2,400/year.

**Maximum Cost Per Acquisition (CPA):** A healthy marketing investment is 10–20% of patient LTV. At $1,000 LTV, you can afford to spend $100–$200 to acquire that patient profitably.

**Sample calculation:**
- Monthly ad budget: $1,500
- Average CPC: $5.00
- Clicks: 300
- Lead conversion rate (click to form/call): 12%
- Leads: 36
- Lead-to-appointment rate: 50%
- New patients: 18
- Cost per patient: $83
- Revenue at $1,000 LTV × 18 patients: $18,000

A 12:1 return on $1,500 in ad spend is achievable when the campaign is structured correctly and tracking is in place.

## Campaign Structure for Maximum ROI

### Campaign 1: Medical Dermatology

**Target audience:** Patients with immediate medical needs — skin cancer screening, mole removal, rashes, chronic skin conditions.

**Key keywords:** "skin cancer screening near me," "mole removal dermatologist," "acne treatment specialist," "eczema doctor [city]"

**Ad copy focus:** Credentials and reliability. "Board-Certified Dermatologist in [City] — Same-Week Appointments Available." Emphasize expertise, not pricing.

**Landing page:** Credentials prominent at top (board certification, years in practice), patient testimonials, clear appointment booking form. Service list with brief descriptions. No distracting navigation.

### Campaign 2: Cosmetic Dermatology

**Target audience:** Patients considering elective procedures who are comparing practices.

**Key keywords:** "Botox [city]," "laser hair removal near me," "chemical peel cost," "dermal fillers [city]"

**Ad copy focus:** Results and value. "Natural-Looking Botox Results in [City] — Book a Consultation." Include pricing ranges if competitive ("Botox from $X/unit").

**Landing page:** Before/after photos, pricing transparency (ranges), consultation booking form, patient reviews specific to the service. These patients are comparison shopping — give them a reason to choose you.

### Campaign 3: Telehealth (If You Offer It)

**Target audience:** Patients who prefer or need virtual consultations — geographically distant, busy, or hesitant to visit in person.

**Key keywords:** "online dermatologist consultation," "virtual skin care appointment," "telemedicine dermatologist"

**Ad copy focus:** Convenience and access. "See a Dermatologist Today From Home — Video Appointments Available."

### Ad Group Structure Within Each Campaign

Within each campaign, create separate ad groups per service:
- Botox ad group → Botox-specific ads + landing page section
- Laser hair removal ad group → Laser-specific ads + landing page section

This tight relevance improves Quality Score — which directly reduces your cost per click. A campaign that goes from Quality Score 4 to Quality Score 8 on its top keywords can see 40–60% lower CPCs for the same bids.

## Bidding Strategy for Dermatology Practices

**Starting out (first 60 days):** Use **Maximize Clicks** with a maximum CPC cap. This generates traffic while you collect data. Set the CPC cap at 1.5× your target CPC to avoid runaway spend.

**Once you have 30+ conversions/month:** Switch to **Target CPA** (cost per acquisition). Set your target CPA at your maximum allowable cost per new patient. Google's algorithm optimizes toward leads that match your conversion history.

**For cosmetic campaigns:** Consider **Target Impression Share** for your highest-value service keywords (e.g., Botox) — this ensures you appear consistently for searches where patient intent is highest.

## Google Ads Healthcare Compliance: What Dermatologists Must Know

Google has specific policies for healthcare and pharmaceutical advertising. For dermatology practices:

**What's allowed:**
- Ads for cosmetic procedures (Botox, fillers, laser) when performed by licensed medical professionals
- Ads for medical dermatology services (skin cancer screening, acne treatment, eczema management)
- Before/after imagery in ad creative (with patient consent disclosures)

**What's restricted:**
- Claims that guarantee results ("Guaranteed to clear your acne")
- Before/after photos in the primary ad image (allowed in landing pages and some ad formats, restricted in others)
- Misleading claims about safety or efficacy
- Advertising prescription-only treatments as over-the-counter options

**Required for prescription cosmetic treatments (Botox, Dysport, etc.):**
- Must be a licensed medical professional
- Cannot use the brand name "Botox" in certain contexts without Google's healthcare certification
- Ads must not make off-label use claims

**Practical guidance:** Use "botulinum toxin treatment" or "wrinkle reduction injections" when "Botox" triggers policy issues. Google's healthcare ad approval is automatic but can be flagged for manual review if policy issues arise.

## Setting Up Conversion Tracking

Without conversion tracking, you're spending money without knowing what's working. Set up all three:

**1. Form submission tracking**
Install the Google Ads conversion tag on your "appointment confirmed" thank-you page. Every form submission that reaches this page counts as a conversion.

**2. Call tracking**
Enable call extensions in Google Ads. Use Google's free forwarding numbers to track calls from ads. For more detail (call recording, duration, keyword attribution), use CallRail ($45/month) or WhatConverts.

**3. Phone button click tracking (mobile)**
Add click-to-call tracking for your phone number on the website — captures mobile users who call directly without filling a form.

**What to check weekly:**
- Search terms report: add new negatives for irrelevant searches
- Cost per conversion by campaign: reallocate budget from high-CPA to low-CPA campaigns
- Quality Score by keyword: improve ad copy and landing pages for low-scoring keywords

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should I budget for Google Ads as a dermatologist?

A realistic starting budget is $800–$1,500/month for mid-size US markets, rising to $1,500–$2,500/month in major metros. Start on the lower end, collect 6–8 weeks of data, then scale budget toward campaigns with strong conversion rates. Google will spend up to 2× your daily budget on high-traffic days, but won't exceed your monthly cap. In highly competitive markets like New York or Los Angeles, $1,500/month minimum is needed to appear consistently for high-value searches.

### What Google Ads keywords drive the best ROI for dermatology practices?

For medical dermatology: "mole removal near me," "skin cancer screening," "acne treatment specialist," and "eczema doctor [city]" convert at high rates because intent is specific and immediate. For cosmetic dermatology: "Botox [city]," "laser hair removal [city]," and "chemical peel near me" generate the highest volume. Avoid broad terms like "skin care" or "dermatology" alone — they attract casual browsers rather than patients ready to book.

### How do I track appointments from Google Ads?

Install the Google Ads conversion tag on your appointment confirmation page (the page patients see after booking). If you use a scheduling platform like Zocdoc, Phreesia, or a custom patient portal, check whether they support Google Ads conversion tracking via their settings. For phone calls, use Google's free forwarding numbers in call extensions — every call from an ad is tracked automatically. Without tracking, you cannot know which keywords and campaigns are generating patients.

### Can dermatologists advertise Botox and cosmetic procedures on Google?

Yes, with compliance. Google allows cosmetic procedure advertising when the advertiser is a licensed medical professional. You cannot guarantee results, make misleading efficacy claims, or use before/after photos in certain ad formats. Stick to phrases like "Botox for wrinkles consultation," "natural-looking wrinkle treatment," and "dermal filler [city]." Include a consultation-required disclaimer. Ads are approved automatically but may be flagged for manual review if claims trigger policy detection.

### How long until Google Ads generates patients for a dermatology practice?

Most practices see initial leads (form submissions or calls) within 1–2 weeks of a well-structured campaign launch. Meaningful optimization data takes 4–8 weeks. By month 3, you should have enough conversion history for Google's Smart Bidding to work effectively. Medical dermatology searches convert faster (patients have immediate needs); cosmetic procedures take longer (patients research and compare over weeks). Budget for a 90-day test before evaluating whether the channel is working.
