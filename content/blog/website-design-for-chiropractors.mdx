A chiropractic website should answer three questions fast: what conditions you treat, where you are, and how a new patient books. Build six pages (Home, Services, Conditions, About the clinic, Contact and Booking), make booking a visible button on every page, and keep the site quick on a phone. Google's loading target is a largest content paint within 2.5 seconds.

This guide is for solo chiropractors and small clinics. It covers page structure, testimonials, accessibility, health-data caution and builder costs. All figures come from sources at the end. No source publishes a reliable booking-rate lift for chiropractic sites, so none is claimed here.

## What Pages Does a Chiropractic Website Need?

Six pages cover most new-patient questions. Home states who you treat and the town. Services lists adjustments, soft-tissue work and anything else you offer. A Conditions page can have a short section per complaint, such as lower back pain, written in plain language. About shows the practitioners, qualifications and the clinic. Contact has address, phone, hours and a map. Booking links to your scheduling tool.

Put the phone number and booking button in the header. A short list of what the first visit involves reduces anxiety and phone questions. Our guide to [getting more phone calls from your website](/blog/how-to-get-more-phone-calls-from-your-website) covers the call button.

## How Should You Use Testimonials and Claims?

Be careful with health claims. The FTC's endorsement guidance says an endorsement must reflect the honest opinion of the endorser, and that if an endorser's exceptional results are not backed by proof of what people typically get, the ad must make clear the generally expected results. It also says to disclose clearly any material connection, such as payment or free products.

State chiropractic boards add their own advertising rules. This article could not verify them, and they differ by state, so check yours before publishing outcome claims or before and after content. See [trust signals for websites](/blog/trust-signals-website) for ways to build trust without outcome promises.

## Is Your Site Fast and Mobile Friendly?

web.dev sets three Core Web Vitals targets: largest contentful paint within 2.5 seconds, interaction to next paint of 200 milliseconds or less, and layout shift of 0.1 or less, measured at the 75th percentile of page loads across mobile and desktop. Google also says it indexes and ranks the mobile version, so the mobile page must have the same main content as the desktop one.

Check your clinic's pages in PageSpeed Insights. Large before and after photos, embedded video and chat widgets are the usual causes of failure. Read [mobile-first website design for local businesses](/blog/mobile-first-website-design-local-businesses) for fixes.

## What About Accessibility and Patient Privacy?

Accessibility matters for patients with pain or reduced mobility. The W3C's WCAG 2.2 is the current standard, with conformance levels A, AA and AAA. Use readable contrast, labelled form fields and buttons that work with a keyboard. Our [website accessibility guide](/blog/website-accessibility-small-business) lists practical checks.

Privacy rules are the part to take seriously. In the US, forms and tracking tools on a clinic site can raise HIPAA questions. The HHS guidance page returned an access error when checked on 31 Oct 2026, so no claim from it is made here. Ask a compliance adviser before adding tracking pixels to booking pages or collecting symptoms in a web form.

## FAQ

### How many pages does a chiropractor need?

Six is a sound start: Home, Services, Conditions, About, Contact and Booking. Add pages only when you have real content for them.

### Should I show prices?

It is a business decision, and no verified study is cited here. If you publish a starting price, note what it covers, as the FTC expects claims to be clear.

### Can I use patient photos and reviews?

Only with proper consent and within your state board's rules. Testimonials must be honest and not imply typical results unless you can support that.

### Is Google Business Profile enough?

No. Google's guidelines say to link a website that represents your business location, not a social profile.
