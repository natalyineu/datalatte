Thumbtack has no fixed price per lead. Its Help Center says prices vary with the type and size of the job, how many pros are available and your market, and you can set the price you will pay for each service ([Thumbtack Help: lead prices](https://help.thumbtack.com/article/set-lead-prices), checked Oct 2026). Higher-value jobs such as contracting, HVAC and electrical work generally cost more per lead than cleaning or handyman requests. Check the price shown in your own account before you spend, and judge the platform by one number: cost per won job.

Here is the trap. You buy 10 leads on a Monday. Four customers never answer. Three say they only wanted a ballpark. Two go with someone else. One books. You paid for all 10, and you were charged whether or not you won anything. If you only ever look at the price of a single lead, that week looks cheap. If you divide the week's spend by the one job you won, it may not.

## How Thumbtack pricing works

Thumbtack charges professionals per lead instead of a flat subscription. The help center separates two things: your lead price (what you pay for one lead) and your weekly budget (the most you will spend on all leads in a week). Lead price is one of several factors that decide how many leads you receive. Others include your profile, star rating and reviews, how quickly you respond and local demand ([Thumbtack Help: how to set lead prices](https://help.thumbtack.com/article/set-lead-prices), checked Oct 2026).

Charging rules, credits and fees have changed over time. Read the current terms in the pro help center, not an older article, including this one.

## What moves the price

- **Trade and job size.** Thumbtack says price follows the type and size of the job. Bigger jobs usually carry higher lead prices.
- **City and competition.** Your market and the number of available pros affect the price. Large metros generally cost more than small markets.
- **Season.** Landscaping peaks in spring, HVAC in summer and winter. Prices can shift with demand.
- **Your price setting.** You choose what you will pay per service. A lower setting can mean fewer leads.
- **Your profile and speed.** A complete profile, reviews and fast replies can lift the share of leads you win, even when the price per lead stays the same.

## The math: what a lead really costs

The only number that matters is spend divided by jobs won. Here is the arithmetic with invented inputs.

**Example only, not a Thumbtack price or an average:** a plumbing lead priced at $35, an average job worth $400 in revenue, and 40% of that left as profit ($160). Swap in your own numbers.

| Win rate | Leads to win 1 job | Cost per won job | Profit per job | Result per job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 in 4 | 4 | 4 x $35 = $140 | $160 | +$20 |
| 1 in 6 | 6 | 6 x $35 = $210 | $160 | -$50 |
| 1 in 10 | 10 | 10 x $35 = $350 | $160 | -$190 |

Same lead price, three very different outcomes. At 1 in 4 you barely break even on a $400 job. The break-even win rate in this example is $35 / $160, which is about 1 in 4.6. If you cannot reach that win rate, either raise your close rate, quote bigger jobs, or stop buying the leads. The point is not the $35. The point is that win rate decides everything.

Track four numbers for every platform you use:

1. Leads purchased
2. Leads you replied to
3. Jobs won
4. Revenue per job

## Thumbtack versus other lead sources

| Source | How you pay | Typical fit |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Thumbtack | Per lead | Home and personal services with project-based work |
| Google Local Services Ads | Per valid lead, such as a call or message | Many local services; strong buying intent |
| Yelp Ads | Per click | Restaurants, salons and local services |
| Angi / HomeAdvisor | Varies by plan; check current terms | Home improvement |
| Google Ads | Per click | Capturing people searching right now |

Google says Local Services Ads charge for each valid lead, with prices that vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding mode ([Google Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en), checked Oct 2026). Google is also moving these campaigns into Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals, starting with some U.S. advertisers in August 2026 ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585), checked Oct 2026). Compare sources by cost per won job, not cost per lead. ## Get more out of every lead

- **Reply fast.** Turn on app notifications and answer within minutes where you can. A lead you answer in 5 minutes is a different lead from one you answer tomorrow.
- **Quote only jobs you can win.** Skip jobs outside your service area, price range or skill set.
- **Build reviews.** After each finished job, send a direct review link. Our guide on [getting more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-5-star-google-reviews-local-business) has scripts.
- **Set a weekly budget.** Thumbtack lets you cap what you spend on all leads in a week. Review weekly and adjust after about a month.
- **Ask for a refund on invalid leads.** A Thumbtack staff reply in its community forum says a lead that violates the Terms of Use qualifies for a refund, while a customer who simply stops replying is not covered ([Thumbtack Community](https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/5974), checked Oct 2026). Check the current policy in the app first.
- **Automate follow-up.** Templated replies and reminders keep warm leads from going cold. See [AI agents for lead generation](/blog/ai-agent-for-lead-generation-local-business).

## Is Thumbtack worth it?

It can be, for project-based businesses where one job pays for many leads and you can reply quickly. It is a weaker fit for businesses built on repeat visits, such as salons, fitness studios and cafes. For those, your own Google presence and email list pay back more. I would add Thumbtack to your own channels, not rely on it alone, and keep your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) strong.

## Do this today

1. Open a spreadsheet with these columns: date, job type, price paid, replied (yes/no), quote sent, job won, revenue.
2. Set a test budget you can afford to lose over about 4 weeks, and turn on the weekly cap.
3. Finish your profile before the first lead: photos, services, service area, a clear description.
4. Write your break-even win rate: lead price divided by profit per job. Put it at the top of the sheet.
5. After 4 weeks, divide total lead spend by jobs won. If that number is higher than the profit on an average job, change targeting, pricing or response speed, or stop.
6. Compare the result with Google Ads, referrals and Local Services Ads, and move budget to the cheapest profitable source.

## Signs a lead is worth paying for

- The customer gave a clear description, timing and location.
- The job is in your service area and matches your skills.
- The budget is realistic for the work described.
- You can call or message back right away.

Leads missing these details waste money. Skip them where the platform lets you choose.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do Thumbtack leads cost?**
A: It depends on your trade, location and the request. Thumbtack says it varies with job type and size, number of pros and your market, so check the price in your account instead of trusting averages.

**Q: Does Thumbtack charge me even if I don't get the job?**
A: Yes, you pay for the lead, not for winning the job, so a non-responsive customer is generally not refundable. That is why your conversion rate matters.

**Q: Can I get a refund on a bad lead?**
A: Possibly. A Thumbtack staff reply says leads that violate the Terms of Use qualify for a refund, but non-responsive customers are not covered. Check the current policy in the app and submit requests promptly.

**Q: How long does it take to see results on Thumbtack?**
A: Allow several weeks. A complete profile, reviews and fast replies improve your chances over time.

**Q: Should I use Thumbtack and Google Local Services Ads together?**
A: Many service businesses do, because they reach customers at different stages. Compare the cost per won job for each, then put more budget behind the winner.
