Thumbtack leads typically cost anywhere from a few dollars to well over $50 each, depending on your trade, your city and how competitive the request is. Higher-value jobs such as contracting, HVAC and electrical work cost more per lead than cleaning or handyman requests. Thumbtack sets lead prices dynamically and changes its model from time to time, so check the current price shown for each request in your account before you spend.

## How Thumbtack Pricing Works

Thumbtack has generally charged professionals per lead instead of a flat subscription, so you pay when a customer contacts you or when you respond to a request, depending on the current rules. Two typical lead types exist:

- **Direct leads:** A customer finds your profile and contacts you. You may be charged when this happens, before you know how good a fit the job is.
- **Opportunity or request leads:** You see a customer's request and choose whether to respond. You pay according to the price shown for that request, so you can filter before spending.

Charging rules, credits and fees have changed over time, so read the current terms in Thumbtack's pro help center instead of relying on older articles.

## What Drives the Cost of a Thumbtack Lead

**Trade and job size.** Bigger jobs with higher revenue potential usually carry higher lead prices.

**City and competition.** Large metro areas with many pros competing generally cost more than smaller markets.

**Season and demand.** Landscaping demand rises in spring, HVAC in summer and winter. Prices can shift with demand.

**Request details.** Detailed requests with budget and timing information tend to cost more than vague ones.

**Your profile and responsiveness.** A complete profile with reviews and fast replies can improve your results and the share of leads you win, even when the price per lead stays the same.

## What Is a Good Cost Per Lead?

There is no universal answer. The right number depends on your close rate and average job value.

Illustrative math: if a plumbing lead costs $35, you win 1 in 4 leads, and the average job brings in $400, your cost per customer is $140 against $400 revenue. Whether that works depends on your margins. If you win 1 in 10, the same lead would cost $350 per customer and probably not pay.

Track these four numbers for every platform you use:

1. Leads purchased
2. Leads that replied
3. Jobs won
4. Revenue per job

## How to Get More Value From Every Lead

**Respond fast.** The first professional to reply often wins the job. Turn on app notifications and reply within minutes where you can.

**Quote only jobs you can win.** Skip jobs outside your service area, price range or skill set.

**Build reviews.** After each completed job, ask for a review with a direct link. Reviews help conversion on every platform. Our guide on [getting more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-5-star-google-reviews-local-business) has scripts.

**Set a weekly budget.** Use the controls in your account to cap spending, review results every week and adjust after about a month.

**Ask for credits on bad leads.** If a lead is clearly invalid (fake contact details, outside your area, a service you do not offer), check the current credit policy and request one.

**Automate follow-up.** Quick templated replies and reminders help you not lose warm leads. See [AI agents for lead generation](/blog/ai-agent-for-lead-generation-local-business).

## Is Thumbtack Worth It?

It can be, for project-based service businesses where one job pays for many leads, and where you can reply quickly. It is usually a weaker fit for businesses built on repeat visits, such as salons, fitness studios and cafes, where building your own Google presence and email list pays off more. A balanced approach is to add Thumbtack to your own channels, not rely on it alone, and keep your own Google Business Profile strong. ## A Simple Way to Test Thumbtack

1. **Set a test budget** you can afford to lose for about a month, and use the weekly cap in your account.
2. **Complete your profile** with photos, services, service area and a clear description before you start.
3. **Log every lead** in a spreadsheet: date, job type, price paid, replied or not, quote sent, job won and revenue.
4. **Calculate cost per won job** after the test: total lead spend divided by jobs won.
5. **Compare it** with your other sources, such as Google Ads, referrals and Local Services Ads, and put more budget behind the cheapest profitable source.

If your cost per won job is higher than the profit on an average job, change your targeting, pricing or response speed, or stop. Tracking this is more reliable than any average quoted in an article.

## Signs a Lead Is Worth Paying For

- The customer gave a clear description, timing and location.
- The job is in your service area and matches your skills.
- The budget is realistic for the work described.
- You can respond quickly, ideally with a phone call or message right away.

Leads missing these details often waste money, so skip them where the platform lets you choose.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do Thumbtack leads cost?**
A: It depends on your trade, location and the request. Expect a range from a few dollars for simple jobs to much higher for large projects. Check the price shown on each lead in your account.

**Q: Does Thumbtack charge me even if I don't get the job?**
A: Generally yes. You pay for the lead according to Thumbtack's current rules, not for winning the job. That is why your conversion rate matters.

**Q: Can I get a refund on a bad lead?**
A: Thumbtack has had a credit process for invalid leads, such as false information or requests outside your service area. Check the current policy in the app and submit requests promptly.

**Q: How long does it take to see results on Thumbtack?**
A: Allow several weeks. A complete profile, reviews and fast replies improve your chances over time.

**Q: Should I use Thumbtack and Google Local Services Ads together?**
A: Many service businesses do, because they reach customers at different stages. Compare the cost per won job for each, then put more budget behind the winner.
