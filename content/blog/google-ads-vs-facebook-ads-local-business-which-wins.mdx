Google Ads is usually the better starting point for local businesses when customers already search for what they sell. Facebook Ads is cheaper per click, averaging $0.62 against $2.69 for Google Search, but a cheaper click does not mean a cheaper customer. The right choice depends on whether buyers are actively looking or still need to be shown the idea. This guide covers click costs, how intent changes what a click is worth, a worked $600 monthly example with labelled assumptions, and a practical way to choose a starting platform.

The guide is written for salons, cafés, groomers, studios and trades in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. The cost figures below are US averages in dollars. Convert them to local currency and check your own market before relying on them.

## Which Platform Costs Less per Click?

Facebook Ads has the lower average cost per click. According to [Stackmatix's 2026 comparison](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/facebook-ads-vs-google-ads) (a secondary source), Facebook Ads averages $0.62 per click, while Google Search Ads averages $2.69. Google's Display Network sits much closer, at $0.63, so the gap depends on which Google format you buy.

Industry matters as much as platform. Stackmatix lists Google Consumer Services at $6.40 per click, the highest Google industry figure in its tables, and Facebook Legal Services at $1.81. The row for your own sector is more useful than the all-industry average.

Cost per click alone does not reveal cost per customer. A click on a search for "emergency plumber" and a click on a photo of a plumber's van are not equal events, and the next section explains why.

## Why Does Intent Change What a Click Is Worth?

Google Ads captures people who are already looking. Stackmatix describes Google as intent-based advertising, where users actively search for solutions, and notes that Google processes 8.5 billion searches daily. Someone typing "dog groomer near me" is close to booking.

Facebook Ads interrupts. Stackmatix describes it as interruption-based, with users scrolling social content rather than searching. It works better for introducing an offer to someone who did not know they needed it yet, such as a new menu, a class launch or a grand opening.

[Instapage's comparison](https://instapage.com/blog/facebook-ads-vs-google-ads) (secondary) makes a similar point: Facebook suits visual engagement and discovery, while Google suits demand that already exists. Lower Facebook click costs often come with lower purchase intent per click, so the lead-to-customer rate has to be measured directly.

## How Do Targeting and Reach Compare for Local Businesses?

Facebook Ads offers detailed audience controls. Stackmatix lists demographics, interests, behaviours, custom audiences built from customer lists, and lookalike audiences. It suggests a 1% lookalike for precision or 5-10% for reach. It also notes that iOS 14.5 privacy changes have reduced some tracking, which makes first-party data more important.

Google Ads targets by keyword, location, demographics and audiences. Instapage notes that Google serves ads contextually, based on keywords and behaviour rather than a deep personal profile. Stackmatix reports that the Display Network reaches over 90% of internet users, but for a single-town business, reach beyond the service area is wasted spend.

The practical difference is this. On Google, the advertiser chooses the words and the place. On Facebook, the advertiser chooses the audience and the place, then relies on the creative to do the selling. Either way, a tight radius matters, because a wide one is one of the fastest ways to pay for clicks from people who cannot be served.

## What Does a $600 Month Look Like in Numbers?

The example below uses Stackmatix's click costs. Every conversion rate is a labelled assumption. Replace the assumptions with tracked figures after one month.

| Input or result | Google Search | Facebook Ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $600 | $600 |
| Average cost per click (Stackmatix 2026) | $2.69 | $0.62 |
| Clicks from the budget | 223 | 968 |
| Click to lead rate (assumption) | 5% | 1% |
| Leads | 11.2 | 9.7 |
| Lead to customer rate (assumption) | 30% | 12% |
| New customers | 3.4 | 1.2 |
| Ad cost per customer | $179 | $517 |
| First-year value per customer (assumption) | $400 | $400 |
| First-year revenue from the month's customers | $1,340 | $464 |

On these assumptions, Google Search costs about $179 per customer and Facebook about $517. The lower click price does not survive a weak lead-to-customer rate. For Facebook to match Google's cost per customer, its 9.7 leads would need to produce about 3.4 customers, a close rate of roughly 35%, not 12%.

Change any assumption and the answer moves. If Facebook leads are warm, followed up within minutes, and close at 35% or more, Facebook can win. Revenue is also not profit, so subtract delivery costs before setting a ceiling for cost per customer.

## How Does Google Quality Score Fit In?

Quality Score is a diagnostic, not a bill. Google's [Quality Score help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118) says it is measured from 1 to 10 at the keyword level and built from three components: expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Each is rated "Above average," "Average" or "Below average" against advertisers showing for the same search over the last 90 days.

Google states that Quality Score is not a key performance indicator and is not an input in the ad auction. It is useful for finding weak spots. A "Below average" landing page rating points to the page clicks land on, not the bid. Weak ad relevance means the ad text does not match the search. Fixing these before raising budget is a sound habit.

## Which Platform Should a Local Business Start With?

Start with Google Ads when people already search for the service. Emergency trades, urgent repairs and "near me" services fit this pattern. Google is also easier to read in the first month, because every click came from a stated need.

Start with Facebook Ads when nobody searches for the offer yet. New menus, class launches, openings and services that depend on showing the result suit Facebook. It also works well for retargeting past visitors or customers from an existing list.

With a small budget, run one platform first. Splitting $500 across two channels often leaves each with too little data to judge. The [Facebook ads budget guide for local business](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) shows how much to spend before results become readable. If the trade qualifies, [Local Services Ads](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) are worth comparing with standard search. [Google Demand Gen campaigns](/blog/google-demand-gen-campaigns-local-business) suit visual reach beyond search.

## How Do You Tell Which Platform Actually Paid?

Track customers, not platform-reported leads. Use a tracking phone number or an offer code for each channel, ask every new customer how they found the business, and record the answer at checkout or booking. Platform dashboards count clicks and forms. The till and the booking diary show which leads became paying customers.

Set up conversion tracking before spending. For Facebook, the choice of tracking code matters, and the [Facebook pixel versus Google tag guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-vs-google-tag-which-to-install-first) explains which to install first. Without tracking on both platforms, the cost-per-customer calculation above is guesswork.

Review after 30 days. Calculate cost per customer for each platform, compare it with first-year value per customer, and decide whether a second platform is worth funding.

## FAQ

### Which is cheaper, Google Ads or Facebook Ads?

Facebook Ads has the lower average cost per click, at $0.62 against $2.69 for Google Search, according to Stackmatix's 2026 figures. Cheaper clicks do not guarantee cheaper customers, so compare cost per customer rather than cost per click.

### Should I run both with $500 a month?

Usually start with one. Two platforms on a small budget each get too little data to judge. Add the second once the first has a known cost per customer.

### Can I run Google Ads myself?

Many small businesses do. Budget time each week to review search terms, add negative keywords and check that conversions are tracked. Google's Quality Score page works as a checklist for spotting weak ads and landing pages.

### Do Facebook leads convert worse than Google leads?

Often they do, because the person was not searching when they saw the ad. How much worse depends on the business, which is why the worked example treats the lead-to-customer rate as an assumption to replace with real data.

### How do I know if ads bring walk-ins?

Use a tracking phone number or a unique offer code for each channel, and ask every customer how they heard about the business. Compare those answers with platform reports each month.
