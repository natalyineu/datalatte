A lead costs about $66.69 on Google Search and about $27.39 on a Meta lead campaign, according to LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks. So Meta wins, right? Not so fast. A lead is not a customer. If only 1 in 8 of those cheap Meta leads ever books, while 3 in 10 Google leads do, the two platforms end up costing almost exactly the same per paying customer. Which one wins for you depends on that second number, and nobody can give it to you except your own phone log.

This guide is for owners of salons, cafés, groomers, studios and trades in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It shows the benchmarks, the real difference between the two platforms, a worked example for a $600 monthly budget, and how to pick a starting point. Benchmarks are US-based; swap in your own local prices for pounds or Australian and Canadian dollars.

## What Actually Differs

### Intent versus interruption

Google Search shows your ad to someone who typed "dog groomer open Saturday" a minute ago. Meta shows it to someone scrolling who may not have thought about a groomer today. That is why a Meta lead is cheaper and, on average, less likely to book. The size of that quality gap is something no public benchmark measures, so you have to measure it yourself.

### Targeting

Google targets by what people search plus a location. Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) says you can target a radius around a location for most campaign types, and describes the default setting as reaching people in or regularly in your area plus people who have shown interest in it. For a business that only serves one town, switch to the narrower "presence" option. That is my recommendation, not a Google rule.

Meta targets by location, demographics, interests and your own data. Meta's help centre has a page on [website custom audiences](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/610516375684216) (building an audience from your site visitors, which needs Meta's tracking installed on your site). For a local business the most useful audience is usually the simplest: people near you who already visited your site or followed your page.

### Creative workload

Google search ads are text. You can launch with a morning's work. Meta lives or dies on the image or short video, so budget time for photos of your real space, real staff and real results. Our [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) covers what to spend on both ads and creative.

### Time to data

Both platforms produce clicks within days. Search gives you a cleaner read because every click came from a stated need. Meta results are noisier in the first couple of weeks, so resist changing audiences daily.

## The Math

Here is $600 a month, using LocaliQ's all-industry cost per lead for each platform. The lead-to-customer rates are **assumptions** for illustration; replace them with your own after one month of tracking.

| | Google Search | Meta lead ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per lead (LocaliQ 2026 all-industry) | $66.69 | $27.39 |
| Leads | 9.0 | 21.9 |
| Lead to customer rate (assumption) | 30% | 12% |
| New customers | 2.7 | 2.6 |
| Ad cost per customer | $222 | $228 |
| Average first-year value of a customer (assumption) | $400 | $400 |
| Revenue from the month's customers | $1,080 | $1,052 |

At these assumptions the two platforms tie. Move one dial and the winner flips:

- **Break-even rule.** Meta matches Google when its lead-to-customer rate is Google's rate times (Meta cost per lead / Google cost per lead). Here that is 30% x (27.39 / 66.69) = about 12.3%. If your Meta leads close above that, Meta wins; below it, Google wins.
- **Why speed matters on Meta.** Cheap leads go cold fast. If you reply in 5 minutes instead of 5 hours, the close rate is the cheapest improvement you have. I can't cite a study for exact figures, so test your own reply times.
- **Check the industry.** In Beauty & Personal Care the cost-per-lead gap runs the other way, so Google wins on cost per lead before any quality difference.

Revenue is not profit. Subtract your costs of delivery before deciding what you can afford per customer. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows how to set that ceiling.

## When to Start With Which

**Start with Google Ads if:**

- People already search for what you sell ("emergency plumber", "hair salon near me").
- The job is urgent or high value.
- You have under about $1,000 a month and want one platform that you can read cleanly.

**Start with Meta if:**

- Your offer is visual or event-driven: a new menu, a class launch, a grand opening.
- Nobody searches for your thing yet because it is new or unusual in your town.
- You have a past-customer list or website visitors to retarget. See our [retargeting guide for local business](/blog/retargeting-ads-for-local-business).

**Run both only when** the first platform produces leads at a cost you can afford and you can still fund each one properly. Splitting a $500 budget in half often leaves both too thin to learn from. That is my judgement, not a platform rule.

Whichever you pick, the setup mistakes are the same: broad keywords on Google, a radius that is too wide on Meta, and no tracking on either. For Google, start with our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) and conversion tracking setup. For the cost side, see [how much Google Ads costs](/blog/how-much-does-google-ads-cost-for-small-businesses-real-2026-pricing-breakdown).

## FAQ

**Q: Which is cheaper, Google Ads or Facebook Ads?**
A: Per click and per lead, Meta is usually cheaper in LocaliQ's 2026 data (about $27.39 versus $66.69 per lead across all industries), but not in every category: Beauty & Personal Care is cheaper on Google. Per customer, it depends on how many leads actually book.

**Q: Should I run both with $500 a month?**
A: Usually start with one. With a small budget, two platforms each get too little data to judge. Add the second after the first has a known cost per customer.

**Q: Can I run Google Ads myself?**
A: Yes. Plan on roughly 30 minutes a week to read the search terms report, add negatives and check conversions. That time estimate is my rule of thumb.

**Q: Do Facebook leads convert worse than Google leads?**
A: Often, because the person was not searching. How much worse is unknown without your own data, which is why the math above treats it as an assumption you must measure.

**Q: How do I know if ads bring walk-ins?**
A: Use a tracking phone number or offer code, ask at checkout, and watch calls and direction requests on your Google Business Profile.
