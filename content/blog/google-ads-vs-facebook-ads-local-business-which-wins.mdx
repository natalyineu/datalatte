For most local service businesses, Google Ads wins when you want customers who are searching right now ("hair salon near me", "emergency dog groomer"), while Facebook and Instagram ads (Meta) win when you want to create demand, promote an event, or retarget people who already know you. If you only have a small budget, start with Google Ads for high-intent searches and add Meta later. Costs vary a lot by city and industry, so treat any benchmark as a range, not a promise.

## Why Local Business Owners Can't Skip This Comparison

Picture a small coffee shop with $500 a month for ads. You have tried both platforms and results feel like rolling dice. The honest answer to "which one is better" depends on what the customer is doing when they see your ad.

* **Google Ads** reaches people who typed a search. They already want a service, so clicks are more expensive in some categories but often closer to a purchase.
* **Meta Ads** reaches people scrolling feeds. They were not looking for you, so you are interrupting. That works well for visual products, events and offers, but conversion usually takes more touches.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Factor | Google Ads | Meta Ads (Facebook + Instagram) |
|---|---|---|
| Customer intent | High (active search) | Lower (interruption) |
| Best for | Urgent or needs-based services | Visual products, events, awareness, retargeting |
| Cost per click | Varies widely by keyword and city | Often lower per click, but varies |
| Time to first data | Usually days | Usually days, plus a learning phase |
| Targeting | Keywords and location | Demographics, interests, custom audiences |
| Needs creative? | Minimal (text ads) | Yes, images or video matter a lot |
| Local reach | Radius or area targeting | Radius targeting, can drift outside your area |

Actual cost per click and cost per lead depend on your industry, competition and quality of your ads. ## Cost Efficiency: Google Is More Predictable, Meta Needs Testing

Google lets you set a daily budget and cap what you pay per keyword, so spend tracks search demand in your area. The catch is competition: in crowded categories such as dentists or home services, clicks can get expensive.

Meta clicks are often cheaper, but a click is a weaker signal than a search. Many people click, look, and leave. Costs also swing with creative quality and audience size, so you need to test several ads before judging the platform.

<Callout type="tip">If your customers come from within about ten miles and need you soon, start with Google. Use Meta when you have a strong visual offer, an event, or a list of past customers to retarget.</Callout>

## Targeting Power: Meta Wins for Niche Audiences

Meta is strong when you want to reach a specific group, such as dog owners or people interested in yoga. You can also build custom audiences from your own email list or website visitors, where available under current platform rules, and exclude existing customers.

Google's strength is the opposite: you do not need to guess who might want you, because the searcher tells you. Google's automated location settings can work well even when you have little customer data.

One practical limit: Meta's local targeting is only as good as your radius settings and creative. Check where your clicks actually come from and tighten the radius if people from far away are clicking.

<Callout type="warning">If your service is appointment-only, make sure your booking page loads fast on mobile. A slow booking flow wastes clicks on either platform.</Callout>

## Time to Results

Google Ads can show data within days once your campaign is live, because search demand already exists. Meta also starts delivering quickly, but the system needs a learning phase to find who responds, so early results can look noisy. Avoid making big changes in the first week on either platform.

**A simple action plan:**
- **Week 1:** Launch Google Ads on a small set of high-intent keywords such as "[service] near me", with a tight location radius.
- **Week 2 and after:** Add Meta retargeting aimed at people who visited your website but did not book.
- **Every week:** Review search terms (Google) and placements and audiences (Meta) and cut what is not producing leads.

<Callout type="example">A pet groomer might use Google to capture people searching for same-day appointments, then use Meta to remind website visitors who left without booking. The two platforms do different jobs.</Callout>

## How to Decide in Five Minutes

1. Do people search for what you sell? If yes, start with Google.
2. Is your offer visual or seasonal (new menu, class launch, event)? Add Meta.
3. Do you have an email list or past customers? Meta custom audiences can help.
4. Is your budget under a few hundred dollars a month? Pick one platform and focus.
5. Do you have time to review reports weekly? If not, consider help, because both platforms punish neglect.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I run Google Ads without hiring someone?**
A: Yes, but plan to spend about 30 minutes a week checking search terms, adding negative keywords and adjusting bids. Left alone, campaigns can spend money on irrelevant searches. Our guide to [negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) shows what to block first.

**Q: Should I run both platforms if I only have $500 a month?**
A: Usually no. With a small budget, splitting spend means neither platform gets enough data to optimize. Start with the one that fits your customer (Google for search intent, Meta for visual or event-driven offers) and add the second once the first is producing leads at a cost you can afford.

**Q: What is the minimum budget for Google Ads to get useful data?**
A: There is no fixed number, because it depends on your keywords' cost per click. As a rough guide, you want enough budget to collect at least a few dozen clicks per month so you can see a pattern. Check your keyword cost estimates in Google's Keyword Planner and see our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business).

**Q: How do I know if ads are driving foot traffic, not just clicks?**
A: Track calls, direction requests and form fills through Google Ads and your Google Business Profile, use a dedicated phone number or offer code, and ask new customers how they found you. Log the answers at checkout.

**Q: I wasted money on Google Ads before. What should I do differently?**
A: The most common cause is broad keywords that attract browsers rather than buyers. Start with specific, high-intent phrases, use exact or phrase match, and add negative keywords such as "free", "jobs", "DIY" and "how to". Review the search terms report weekly.
