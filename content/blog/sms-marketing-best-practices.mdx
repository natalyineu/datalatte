The core SMS marketing rules for a US small business are: get prior express written consent before promotional texts, honor opt-outs within ten business days at most, register your sending number, and keep messages short enough to cost one credit. Anything beyond that is testing. No source used here publishes a reliable open rate for local businesses, so this guide does not quote one.

Each rule below links to the regulation or vendor page it comes from. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Rule 1: How do you get valid consent?

Prior express written consent is an agreement in writing, with a signature that can be electronic, that clearly authorizes the seller to send advertisements or telemarketing messages. It must include the number and say that the customer does not have to sign as a condition of purchase (47 CFR 64.1200(f)(9)).

In practice: add an unticked checkbox to your booking or checkout form with wording about promotional texts, and store the timestamp. Never text a purchased or scraped list.

## Rule 2: How fast must you honor a STOP?

A customer can revoke consent in any reasonable way, and you must honor it within a reasonable time not to exceed ten business days (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10)). Do not make people use one exclusive word.

Industry guidance goes further. A law-firm summary of the CTIA messaging principles says senders should seek express consent, offer an opt-out such as STOP, and notify recipients periodically how to opt out (secondary source). Send one confirmation and then stop.

A broader "revoke-all" rule, which would treat an opt-out for one type of message as an opt-out for all, has been delayed. A law-firm report says the FCC moved its effective date to 31 January 2027 (secondary source); check the FCC's current order before relying on any date.

## Rule 3: Which hours are safe to send?

The telemarketing rules bar telephone solicitations before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. at the called party's location (47 CFR 64.1200(c)(1)). The regulation's wireless provision applies those solicitation rules to wireless numbers as described in an FCC order, so many senders keep promotional texts inside that window. Ask a lawyer how it applies to you.

Send by the recipient's local time, not yours, if you have customers in more than one time zone.

## Rule 4: Do you need to register your number?

Probably, if you send from a standard 10-digit number. Twilio's documentation says that registering for A2P 10DLC results in lower message filtering and higher throughput, and that unregistered traffic incurs additional carrier fees. Registration asks for your business identity and a description of how people opt in, opt out and get help.

Most small-business platforms handle it for you. SimpleTexting lists a $4 one-time carrier registration fee.

## Rule 5: How long should a message be?

Length sets your cost. On SimpleTexting a text up to 160 characters uses 1 credit, up to 306 characters uses 2, and a picture message uses 3. On Twilio, price is per 160-character segment plus a carrier fee ($0.0083 plus $0.0035 to $0.005).

Write the offer in one line, name your business first, and add opt-out wording. If it needs a paragraph, send an email instead. See [SMS vs email](/blog/sms-vs-email-marketing-local-business-which-is-better).

## Rule 6: What should every marketing text include?

- Your business name, so the customer knows who is writing
- One clear action, such as a reply word or a booking link
- An opt-out instruction, such as "Reply STOP to opt out"
- A response to HELP, which CTIA-based guidance describes as expected (secondary source)

## Rule 7: How do you measure results?

Count booked revenue from a code or reply word, not clicks. Track opt-outs after each send. Our [guide to text campaigns](/blog/sms-marketing-for-local-business-text-campaigns-that-work) lists six campaign types to compare.

## What does message length cost on 300 contacts? (worked example)

Inputs are an **assumption**: 300 opted-in contacts, SimpleTexting's $39 plan (500 credits, extra credits 5.5 cents), and one promotion a month.

| Message | Credits per contact | Credits used | Extra cost beyond plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 150 characters | 1 | 300 | $0 |

Trimming 50 characters saves $5.50 a month in this example. Over a year that is $66, roughly the price of one and a half months of the plan.

## FAQ

### Can I text customers who gave me their number at checkout?

Only for the purpose they agreed to. For promotions you need written consent that covers them. See Rule 1.

### Are appointment reminders marketing?

They are informational, but keep the consent record and include opt-out wording. The regulation's consent definition is about advertisements and telemarketing.

### How often can I send promotions?

No source used here sets a number. Begin with one or two a month and watch opt-outs.

### Is texting cheaper than ads?

It depends on your list size and customer value. Compare cost per booked customer for each channel, as in [how much to spend on marketing](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).
