A law firm website should name your practice areas and city on the first screen, show attorney profiles and bar admissions, make one contact step obvious on mobile, and avoid claims your bar rules treat as misleading. Build it on six core pages and test it on a phone before you launch.

## Which Pages Does a Law Firm Website Need?

Six pages cover a solo or small firm. Add a practice-area page for each service you want to rank for rather than listing everything on one page.

| Page | What it should contain |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Practice areas, city, contact button, short proof of experience |
| Practice areas | One page per area: who it is for, process, what to bring to a consultation |
| Attorneys | Photo, bar admission, education, areas of focus |
| Contact | Phone, form, address, hours, map, parking |
| Results or reviews | Only compliant statements (see below) |
| FAQ or resources | Plain answers to what clients ask first |

## What Do Bar Rules Say About Law Firm Websites?

Rules vary by state, so treat these as examples from one source. California's Rules of Professional Conduct, Chapter 7, say in Rule 7.1 Comment [2] that a communication containing an express guarantee or warranty of the result of a representation is false or misleading. Comment [4] says a testimonial or endorsement may be misleading if it leads a reasonable person to expect the same results for other clients, and that an appropriate disclaimer often avoids unjustified expectations.

California Rule 7.2(c) requires a communication to include the name and address of at least one lawyer or law firm responsible for its content. Comment [3] to Rule 7.1 says "no fee without recovery" is misleading unless it also discloses whether the client is liable for costs. The ABA Model Rules site returned an error when checked, so this article does not quote it. Read your own state bar's version before you publish.

## Can You Show Reviews and Testimonials?

Only genuine ones. The FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews bans creating or selling fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones, undisclosed insider reviews, and review suppression through groundless legal threats. The FTC says it can seek civil penalties against knowing violators, though its announcement lists no dollar figure. Add the disclaimer your bar requires. ## What Should the Site Cost and Run On?

Many small firms use a hosted builder. Squarespace lists Core at $29 a month and Plus at $49 a month on annual billing, with monthly prices of $39 and $65. Wix lists Business at $39.77 a month on a yearly plan. These are builder fees only. Writing, photography, intake software and secure client portals are separate. Clio's pricing page returned an error when checked, so no Clio prices are given here.

## How Fast and Mobile-Friendly Should It Be?

Google's targets at the 75th percentile are LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP of 200 ms or less and CLS of 0.1 or less. Google indexes the phone version and says it should hold the same content as desktop. A firm whose mobile page hides attorney bios or practice details may rank on less content. See our [mobile-first design guide](/blog/mobile-optimization-local-business).

## What About Contact Forms and Email Follow-Up?

A form is the first contact with a prospective client. Keep it short and add a line that sending a message does not create an attorney-client relationship, if your state rules call for it. If you email enquirers, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide requires honest subject lines, an ad disclosure, a valid postal address and an opt-out honoured within 10 business days, with penalties up to $53,088 per email.

## Worked Example: Is a Site Worth It?

All inputs are **assumptions**, not benchmarks.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly builder cost (Squarespace Core, annual) | $29 |
| Site visitors who submit an enquiry | 20 a month |
| Enquiries that become signed clients | 10% = 2 a month |
| Average fee per matter | $1,500 |

Two signed clients bring $3,000 a month against $29 in builder fees. Your own conversion rate and fee mix will differ. If you pay an agency, add its fee and compare against signed matters, not form fills.

## FAQ

### How much should a law firm website cost?

Builder fees start around $19 to $29 a month on Squarespace annual plans. Custom design and copy are separate. No independent survey of agency fees was verified for this article, so get written quotes.

### Can I list case results?

Check your state bar. California's comments warn about guarantees and testimonials that suggest the same result for others. Add a disclaimer where your rules call for one.

### Do I need practice-area pages?

Yes, if you want to rank for specific searches. One page per area lets you answer that query directly.

### Should I run ads too?

Some firms do. See our guide to [Facebook ads for personal injury lawyers](/blog/facebook-ads-for-personal-injury-lawyers) for what is known about the cost.

### How do I make the site accessible?

Follow WCAG basics: contrast, alt text, keyboard navigation. See the [W3C quick reference](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/).
