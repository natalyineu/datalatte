A coffee shop website converts when a visitor on a phone can see your hours, address, menu and an order or directions button without scrolling or signing up. Make the page load fast: Google's Core Web Vitals call a Largest Contentful Paint of 2.5 seconds or less "good." Remove forced account creation, which 18% of online shoppers in Baymard's survey gave as a reason for abandoning a purchase.

No verified conversion-rate benchmark for coffee shop websites turned up in the sources checked, so this guide does not quote an average. Count your own conversions first: taps on call, directions and order.

## What Should a Coffee Shop Homepage Show First?

Visitors usually want one of four things: the opening hours, where you are, what you sell and how to order. Put those in the first screen on mobile. A short headline, a tap-to-call number, a directions link and an order button do more than a slideshow.

Keep your menu as text, not only a PDF image, so people can read it quickly. The [coffee shop website CRO guide](/blog/coffee-shop-website-cro) covers layout in more depth.

## How Fast Should the Site Load?

Google's web.dev defines three Core Web Vitals. Largest Contentful Paint should occur within 2.5 seconds of the page starting to load. Interaction to Next Paint should be 200 milliseconds or less, with values above 500 milliseconds rated poor. Cumulative Layout Shift should be 0.1 or less. A page passes when the 75th percentile of visits, measured separately for mobile and desktop, meets all three.

Run your homepage through PageSpeed Insights and the report names what slows the page, so start with the largest items it flags.

## Should You Add an Ordering Link to Google?

If you take pickup or delivery orders, yes. Google Business Profile Help says you can manage how customers place pickup and delivery orders from your profile: open Food ordering, choose Add a link, and you can set a preferred provider for pickup or delivery. Custom links appear on Search and Maps.

That puts the order button where many people first find you. Our [coffee shop Google Business Profile guide](/blog/coffee-shop-google-business-profile-optimization) covers the rest of the listing.

## Why Is Forced Account Creation a Problem?

Baymard's research on online shopping carts found an average abandonment rate of 70.22% across 50 studies, updated September 2025. Among shoppers excluding people who were just browsing, 18% said the site wanted them to create an account, 17% found checkout too long or complicated and 40% pointed to extra costs such as shipping, tax or fees.

Baymard studies e-commerce, not cafés, so use it as a warning rather than a forecast. If your online ordering asks for a password before showing the total, offer guest checkout and show the full price early.

## How Do You Find What Visitors Struggle With?

Watch where people tap and stop. A free heatmap and recording tool shows rage clicks and dead taps on mobile; see our [Microsoft Clarity guide](/blog/microsoft-clarity-small-business-free-heatmaps). Change one thing at a time and compare the same days of the week, since weekends differ. ## FAQ

### What is a good conversion rate for a coffee shop website?

No verified benchmark was found. Define a conversion as an order tap, call or directions tap, then track the share of visits that do it and improve from your own baseline.

### How fast should my coffee shop site be?

Aim for Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint of 200 milliseconds or less and Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less, measured at the 75th percentile.

### Do I need online ordering?

Not necessarily. If you do not offer it, make the menu, hours and directions the main actions. If you do, link it from your site and your Google profile.

### Is a PDF menu bad?

A PDF is harder to read and tap on a phone than text on the page. Text also loads faster and is easier to update.

### Should I make customers create an account to order?

Baymard's data suggests forced accounts push some shoppers away. Offer guest checkout where you can.
