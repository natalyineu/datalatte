A restaurant website should put the menu as readable text, opening hours, address, phone, and one clear order or reservation button on the first screen. Link to ordering and booking on your own site where you can, because marketplace orders carry commission.

DoorDash's US Marketplace plans list 15%, 25% or 30% on delivery and 6% on pickup, while orders taken through your own website incur no DoorDash commission, only payment processing fees.

This guide covers the pages and technical basics, plus a worked example comparing a marketplace order with an own-site order.

## What should a restaurant home page show?

Follow NN/g's homepage advice: say what you are, show real offerings and put key content above the fold. For a restaurant:

- Cuisine, neighbourhood and a real photo of your food or room.
- Today's hours and phone that taps to call.
- Buttons for order online and reserve, if you offer them.
- A link to the menu, not a PDF only.

BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 54% of consumers visit a business's website after reading positive reviews, so show your Google rating beside the buttons.

## Why should the menu be text, not a PDF?

Text loads faster, reads on a phone and can be indexed. Google's LocalBusiness structured data supports a menu property with the menu's full URL and a servesCuisine property, and both are used for restaurants. Keep prices current, and keep the web menu and your Google Business Profile menu in sync.

Google's Business Profile lets you enter menu items with descriptions and prices, or upload a photo or PDF. Its content guidelines say a Menu URL cannot be a direct link to a third-party ordering or delivery service. See our [restaurant Business Profile guide](/blog/google-my-business-for-restaurants).

## How does ordering link to Google?

Google lets you add custom food-ordering links to your Business Profile that show in Search and Maps, and set one as preferred for pickup, delivery or both. If you turn off "Accept orders on your profile", third-party order links are hidden. Providers must remove third-party links within five business days of a removal request.

Point your preferred link at your own ordering page when you have one, and compare costs in our [DoorDash vs Uber Eats fee guide](/blog/doordash-vs-uber-eats-restaurant-fees-local-business).

## What about reservations and events?

Show the booking method you actually use: a form, a phone number, or a booking provider link. Keep it one tap from the home page. Put private dining and events on their own page with a request form. Link seasonal offers from your email list.

## How fast should the site be?

Google rates a Largest Contentful Paint of 2.5 seconds or less, INP of 200 ms or less and CLS of 0.1 or less as good. Big food photos are the usual problem: compress them, set dimensions, and do not lazy-load the first image. See our [speed guide](/blog/website-speed-optimization-small-business).

## FAQ

### Do I need online ordering on my own site?

It is optional. The advantage is no marketplace commission; DoorDash states that orders through your own website incur no DoorDash commission, only standard payment processing.

### Can I link my Google profile to my ordering page?

Yes. Google lets you add custom order links and mark one as preferred for pickup, delivery or both.

### Should I use a PDF menu?

Use text on the site. A PDF can be an extra download, but it is harder to read on a phone.

### What structured data does a restaurant need?

Use the Restaurant type with name, address, hours, menu URL and cuisine, then test it with Google's Rich Results Test.

### How do I get more diners to book direct?

Show the booking button first and ask guests to review you on Google. See our [Facebook ads guide for restaurants](/blog/facebook-ads-for-restaurants).
