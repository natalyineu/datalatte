"How much should I spend on marketing?" is one of the most common questions small business owners ask — and one of the hardest to answer well, because the right number depends on your industry, growth stage, competition, and what marketing channels you're actually using.

This guide gives you real benchmarks, not vague percentages. Industry standards say "spend 5–10% of revenue on marketing," which tells you almost nothing useful when you're trying to decide whether to spend $500 or $2,000 this month.

---

## The Two Ways to Think About Marketing Budget

**Percentage of revenue (the traditional approach):**

Industry benchmarks for local businesses:
- Retail/restaurants: 3–6% of revenue
- Service businesses (salons, gyms, trainers): 5–10%
- Professional services (consultants, agencies): 8–15%
- New businesses (first year): 15–25% (you're buying your customer base)

A coffee shop doing $30,000/month in revenue → $900–1,800/month marketing budget
A hair salon doing $15,000/month → $750–1,500/month

The problem with this approach: it ties your marketing budget to your current revenue, which limits growth. If you want to grow 50% this year, spending 5% of current revenue won't get you there.

**Goal-based budgeting (the better approach):**

Start with: How many new clients do you need per month to hit your growth target?
Then ask: What does it cost to acquire one new client in your channel?
Calculate: New clients needed × cost per acquisition = marketing budget

Example for a personal trainer:
- Goal: 5 new clients per month
- Google Ads cost per new client: ~$60
- Required Google Ads budget: $300/month + $150 management
- Total: $450/month for 5 new clients

This tells you whether the spend is justified based on expected return.

## The Common Budget Mistakes

**1. Spreading too thin across channels**
$2,000 split across Google Ads, Meta, TikTok, and influencers = mediocre everywhere. $2,000 focused on Google Ads for 90 days = clear data on what works.

**2. Starting too small to learn**
$100/month on Google Ads won't generate enough data to optimise. You'll waste the money and conclude "Google Ads doesn't work for my business." The minimum to get real signal is typically $500–800/month.

**3. Stopping campaigns too early**
Most Google Ads campaigns take 60–90 days to reach their potential. Businesses that stop after 30 days "because it's not working" never give it enough time to learn.

**4. Confusing spend with ROI**
Track cost per new client, not total spend. "I spent $2,000 on ads" tells you nothing. "I spent $2,000 and got 25 new clients worth $400 each" = $10,000 revenue from $2,000 spend = good ROI.

**5. Ignoring retention in the budget**
Acquiring a new customer typically costs 5–7x more than retaining an existing one. A $100/month email platform that reduces churn by 10% is often the highest-ROI line item in the budget.

<Callout type="tip">
Calculate your Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) before setting your acquisition budget. A coffee shop with a $5 average ticket and monthly visit frequency has a very different CLV than a salon with a $120 service booked every 6 weeks.
</Callout>

## Free vs. Paid Marketing: Honest Expectations

**Free channels (time cost, no cash):**
- Google Business Profile (optimised): 50–100 extra profile views/month within 6 months
- Organic social media: 2–5% monthly follower growth if you post consistently
- Word of mouth / referrals: The best channel but can't be forced, only encouraged

**Paid channels (cash cost, faster results):**
- Google Ads: Results in weeks, not months. Most predictable channel for local businesses.
- Meta Ads: Good for visual businesses (salons, restaurants, studios). Takes 4–6 weeks to optimise.
- TikTok Ads: Underpriced in most local markets right now. Worth testing with $300–500/month.

The mistake is treating free and paid as either/or. The businesses that grow fastest use paid ads to acquire clients quickly, while building organic channels (SEO, GBP, social) that reduce paid dependency over time.

## When to Increase Your Marketing Budget

Clear signals it's time to spend more:
- Your current ads are profitable (positive ROAS for 60+ days)
- You're capacity-constrained and need to hire before growing more
- A competitor is outspending you for the same customers

Clear signals to pause before spending more:
- You don't have conversion tracking set up
- Your current leads aren't converting (the problem is sales/service, not marketing volume)
- You don't have a way to handle more leads than you currently get
