## What does Meta advertising cost for a pet groomer?

AdWave's small business channel guide puts a standalone Meta (Facebook and Instagram) buy at often $300 to $1,000 per month, according to [AdWave's channel table](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-choose-advertising-channels). Its [veterinary advertising guide](https://adwave.com/resources/veterinary-advertising) says most veterinary practices can run effective campaigns in the same range. Those are category figures, not Lexington quotes, so treat them as a planning band.

Costs are easier to judge per unit. AdWave's [marketing guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-small-business-guide) lists typical Meta costs of $5 to $15 per thousand impressions and $1 to $3 per click. The [Barklead groomer guide](https://barklead.com/post/using-facebook-ads-for-groomers) suggests starting at $10 to $20 a day. At $10 a day, that works out to about $300 a month (calculated, assuming 30 days).

## Which ad formats work for pet grooming?

Short video is the strongest starting format. Adwave cites Meta's 2025 Creative Best Practices report, which found short-form video generates 2.5 times more engagement than static images on Facebook and Instagram ([AdWave electrician guide](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners)). For a groomer, that means a short clip of a dog before and after a groom, filmed in the salon or van.

Photo ads still have a role, especially for showing a clean finished result. A [Marketing 360 case study](https://blog.marketing360.com/case-studies/pet-groomer-marketing-case-study-driving-success-with-digital-ads/) of a mobile pet groomer describes the business choosing Facebook and Google as its channels. For a wider view of how Instagram fits a groomer's plan, read the [pet groomers' guide to Instagram marketing](/blog/pet-groomers-guide-to-instagram-marketing).

## Who should you target in Lexington?

Barklead's guide suggests targeting pet owners aged 25 to 60 who are likely to have disposable income, within a 10 to 15 mile radius of the grooming location. Use that as the starting audience, then narrow it with real results. A mobile groomer and a fixed salon will usually need different radii.

Lexington-specific Meta benchmarks are not published, so the national ranges here are planning figures. Check the reach estimate Ads Manager shows for your radius before you set a budget. Adwave's restaurant table lists 1 to 4 weeks to results on Meta, so judge a new campaign after that window, not after a few days.

## How should you set up tracking before spending?

Verify these before the first ad goes live:

1. Install the Meta Pixel on the booking confirmation page and the contact form.
2. Configure a Lead event that fires when a booking form is submitted.
3. Add UTM parameters to every ad URL so Google Analytics can report Meta-sourced bookings.

Meta Ads Manager can run ads across Instagram and Facebook from one place ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/social-media-advertising)), and it covers Messenger as well ([Turrboo](https://turrboo.com/blog/organic-vs-paid-social-media)). If you are deciding between Instagram-only and both placements, [Instagram ads vs Facebook ads](/blog/instagram-ads-vs-facebook-ads) compares them. Without tracking, reach numbers look like progress while bookings stay unknown.

## What ad copy and offer should you test first?

Barklead's sample copy offers a discount on a first visit: "Book today and get 10% off your first grooming session." Use an offer like that as your control, then test one change at a time, such as a short video against a photo, or a booking call to action against a learn-more call to action.

Retargeting past clients is worth a separate campaign. AdWave lists a typical Meta retargeting CPM of $5 to $15 ([retargeting guide](https://adwave.com/resources/retargeting-ads-small-business)), the same band as prospecting. The gain comes from the offer and the audience, not from cheaper impressions. For the search side of the budget, see the [Google Ads guide for pet groomers in Lexington](/blog/google-ads-for-pet-groomers-in-lexington-ky).

## FAQ

### How much should a pet groomer spend on Meta ads per month?

Adwave's veterinary guide says most practices can run effective Meta campaigns for $300 to $1,000 a month. Barklead suggests starting at $10 to $20 a day and increasing spend once you find ads that perform.

### Are Lexington Meta ad costs different from national averages?

Published Meta benchmarks are not broken down by Lexington, so national ranges work as planning figures. Your own Ads Manager results are the real test, and they will show your cost per click and cost per booking within a few weeks.

### Can one campaign run on both Instagram and Facebook?

Yes. Instagram ads run through the same system as Facebook ads, Meta Ads Manager, and one campaign can run on Instagram, Facebook, or both ([AdWave Instagram guide](https://adwave.com/resources/instagram-advertising-for-local-business)).

### Are there free courses on Meta advertising?

Meta's Blueprint certification covers Facebook and Instagram advertising at no cost and includes a certificate on completion, according to [a secondary guide](https://sascom247.com/digital-marketing-for-beginners-in-nigeria/). Check Meta's own Blueprint page for current course details.
