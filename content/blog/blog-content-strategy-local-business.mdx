A blog strategy for a local business is a short list of questions your customers ask, one useful page per question, and three numbers checked monthly. Most blog pages never get found: Ahrefs found that 96.55% of about 14 billion pages get no Google traffic. So publish fewer pages, make each one answer a real question better than what exists, and link it to the service it supports.

This guide uses Google's documentation, Ahrefs' study and Search Console help, all read on 2026-10-25. It is for owners of cafés, salons, groomers and studios in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Examples are labelled as assumptions.

## Does a Local Business Need a Blog at All?

Not always. Google's local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity", and its help page names reviews and links from other websites as parts of prominence. A blog helps with relevance for questions your Business Profile cannot answer, and it earns links over time. It does not replace reviews or a complete profile.

Start with your [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) if it is incomplete. If it is done, a blog is the next layer. Our post on [why a local website needs a blog](/blog/blog-content-strategy-local-business) covers the reasoning in more depth.

## What Should a Local Business Write About?

Write about the questions customers ask before they buy, in the words they use. Good sources are your own inbox, the questions staff answer at the counter, your Google Search Console queries and Google's own suggestions in the search box.

Google's guidance tests content with questions such as whether it offers original information beyond the obvious and whether it shows first-hand experience. A local business has an edge here: you know your prices, your area and your customers. A generic national guide does not.

Good post types:

- **Price and cost questions:** what a service costs in your town, and why.
- **Choosing questions:** how to pick between two options, with a table.
- **Preparation and aftercare:** what to do before and after a visit.
- **Local guides:** things only someone in your area would know.

## How Do You Build a Simple Content Calendar?

Keep the calendar small enough to survive a busy month. The numbers below are **assumptions** for a one-person shop.

| Item | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Posts per month | 2 |
| Hours per post | 4 (research, draft, photos, publish) |
| Monthly time | 8 hours |
| Reuse per post | 1 Business Profile post, 1 email, 3 social posts |

Two good posts a month is 24 a year. That is more than most competitors in a town publish, and each one can be updated rather than replaced. Google warns against changing page dates without "substantive updates", so edit when facts change and say what changed.

## How Do You Reuse One Post Across Channels?

Treat the post as the source, then cut it down. A single post can feed a Business Profile update, an email to past customers and short social posts, with a link back to the page.

1. Pull the single most useful tip into a Business Profile post with a photo and a link.
2. Send the full post to your email list with one sentence on why it matters now.
3. Turn the main table or checklist into a social image.
4. Answer related comments or DMs with the post link.

Cutting a post down is fine. Copying the same text to many pages is not. Google's spam policies describe scaled content abuse as producing many pages mainly to manipulate rankings, including generating many pages with AI tools without adding value. Google's wording targets pages made mainly to manipulate rankings, not AI use as such, and it advises disclosing automation where readers would expect it.

## Which Three Numbers Should You Track?

Check these once a month, in this order.

1. **Search Console clicks and impressions for blog pages.** Google defines clicks as the number of times a user clicked your site from Search results, and impressions as how many times it appeared. Rising impressions with few clicks means your title or snippet needs work.
2. **Calls, direction requests and website clicks from your Business Profile.** These are the actions that lead to a visit.
3. **Enquiries or bookings that started on a blog page.** Mark your booking button or form as a key event in Google Analytics, so you can count them by page.

Ignore page views alone. A post with few visits that produces two bookings beats a post with many visits and none. Impressions can take weeks to show, and Google says some changes take effect in hours while others take several months, so judge after at least a few weeks.

## How Do You Make Each Post Easier to Rank?

Follow Google's basics. Give each page a unique, clear title that describes it. Write a short meta description with the main points. Open with a direct answer, since search snippets usually come from page text. Link related posts with descriptive text rather than "click here".

Add the signals readers look for: a named author, the date, sources for any number and a plain note where data is missing. Google lists clear authorship and explaining how content was produced among its quality questions.

Link every post to one service or enquiry page. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows where blog time fits against paid ads.

## FAQ

### How often should a local business blog?

There is no figure from Google. Two useful posts a month is a pace one person can hold. Consistency matters less than usefulness.

### Do blog posts help Google Maps rankings?

Google's local ranking page lists relevance, distance and prominence. It names reviews and links as parts of prominence and does not describe blog posts as a ranking factor. Posts can earn links and answer questions, which supports the website.

### Can I use AI to write blog posts?

Google does not ban it. It warns against generating many pages without adding value, and advises disclosing automation where readers would wonder. Check every fact before you publish.

### How long until a blog brings customers?

Google says changes can take a few hours to several months. Judge results after a few weeks, and keep notes.

### What is the best length for a post?

No source gives a length. Cover the question fully, then stop.
