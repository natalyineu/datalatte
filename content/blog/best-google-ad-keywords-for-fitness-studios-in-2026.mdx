LocaliQ's 2026 search benchmarks list an average click in Health & Fitness at $6.17 and an average cost per lead of $67.36. Now flip it around. If you are willing to pay $250 to win one new member, and 5 in 100 clicks become an enquiry and 1 in 5 enquiries joins, you can afford about $2.50 a click on a generic search like "fitness studio near me". That is less than half the average. The $250, 5% and 20% are my assumptions, not benchmarks, but the gap is the point: the keywords you choose decide whether the numbers can work at all.

This guide covers keyword groups by intent, match types, a negative list and a campaign structure for a studio in the US, UK, Australia or Canada. It does not print search volumes or click prices for individual keywords. We could not open Google Keyword Planner for this article, and those figures change by city and season, so you will pull your own from your account in the steps below. For budgets, tracking and ad copy, read our Google Ads for fitness studios guide, and for larger gyms, our [Google Ads for gyms guide](/blog/google-ads-for-gyms).

The practical reading: expect the "near me" searches to be the expensive ones, and expect cheaper clicks to come with less buying intent. Your job is to find the keyword groups where your numbers still work.

## Keyword Groups by Intent

Group keywords by what the person wants, and give each group its own ad group and landing page. Replace [city] and [neighborhood] with the names people actually use.

| Intent | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready to visit | "fitness studio [city]", "fitness studio near me", "boutique gym [neighborhood]" | Phrase + exact |
| Class type | "hiit classes [city]", "pilates classes [city]", "barre classes [neighborhood]", "spin class near me", "boxing classes [city]" | Phrase + exact |
| Beginner and niche | "beginner fitness classes [city]", "postpartum fitness classes [city]", "women's strength training [city]", "senior fitness classes [city]" | Phrase |
| Small group and personal training | "personal trainer [neighborhood]", "small group training [city]", "personal training studio [city]" | Phrase + exact |
| Offer-led | "free trial fitness class [city]", "first class free [city]", "intro offer fitness studio [city]" | Phrase |
| Membership and price | "gym membership [city]", "no contract gym [city]", "class pass fitness [city]" | Phrase |
| Brand | [your studio name], [your studio name] schedule | Exact |

Why these groups:

- **Class-type terms** usually carry clearer intent than "fitness studio", because the person already knows what they want to do. Keep one ad group per class type so the ad and page can repeat the words they typed.
- **Offer-led terms** catch people close to a decision. They only work if the landing page really offers the trial or first class free.
- **Beginner and niche terms** have fewer searches, but the person has a specific need you can answer. Build two or three around what makes your studio different, such as late hours, small classes or a niche audience.
- **Informational searches** ("how to start strength training", "beginner pilates exercises") belong in blog posts and social, not in a paid campaign.

## Match Types

Google's [match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) defines three:

- **Broad match** shows on searches related to your keyword, including some that do not share its direct meaning. Google says it is critical to use Smart Bidding with it.
- **Phrase match** (quotes) shows on searches that include your keyword's meaning, which can be implied, and on more specific versions of it.
- **Exact match** (square brackets) shows on searches with the same meaning or intent as the keyword. It gives the most control and reaches the fewest searches.

For a single studio, start with phrase and exact. Use phrase to discover how people really phrase it, and exact for the proven terms and your brand. Hold off on broad match until conversions are tracked and your negatives list is mature. Smart Bidding needs conversion data, and a studio with a few leads a month gives it little to learn from.

## Negative Keywords for Fitness Studios

Google's [negative keywords help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972) says a broad-match negative blocks a search that contains all the negative terms in any order, and that negatives do not match close variants, so add singular and plural forms yourself. It also warns that too many negatives can reduce your reach.

Start with this list and review it against what you actually sell:

| Group | Negatives |
| --- | --- |
| Jobs and training | `jobs`, `job`, `careers`, `salary`, `hiring`, `certification`, `certificate`, `course`, `courses`, `instructor training`, `teacher training`, `degree` |
| At-home and online | `at home`, `home workout`, `workout videos`, `youtube`, `online`, `app`, `apps`, `free workout`, `diy` |
| Equipment and shopping | `equipment`, `machine`, `machines`, `for sale`, `second hand`, `used`, `rental`, `buy`, `wholesale`, `clothing`, `leggings`, `supplements` |
| Business | `franchise`, `franchises`, `cost to open`, `business plan`, `insurance`, `software` |
| Wrong service | `physio`, `physical therapy`, `weight loss clinic`, `diet plan`, `meal plan` |
| Unrelated sports | `golf`, `tennis`, `swimming lessons`, `martial arts` (remove any you offer) |

Handle "free" with care. If you offer a free trial, keep "free trial" as a keyword and add exact-match negatives for plain "free gym" instead of blocking the word entirely. Drop `online` if you run virtual classes, `martial arts` if you teach them, and so on.

Then read the [search terms report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708) every week. Google describes it as the actual searches that triggered your ads and got impressions and clicks, with the matching keyword and match type shown. Add irrelevant searches as negatives. ## Campaign Structure

Keep it small enough that every ad group collects data.

1. **Campaign 1: Classes and studio.** Ad groups for ready-to-visit terms and one per class type. This is where most of the budget starts.
2. **Campaign 2: Trials and offers.** Offer-led and membership terms, with a landing page that repeats the offer. Separating it lets you cap the budget and judge it on cost per trial.
3. **Campaign 3: Personal training and small group.** Higher-value work, so it often earns its own budget and a different ceiling (see The Math).
4. **Campaign 4: Brand.** Your name in exact match, so a competitor cannot sit above you when someone searches for you.

Set a tight location, drawn from where your members really live. Pull the zip or postcode of your last 50 sign-ups first. Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) describes presence-or-interest as the default and recommended option and says radius targeting is available for most campaign types. Confirm the default for Search in your own account, because "interest" can bring in people who are not near you.

Match ads to the search: put the class and city in the headline, and keep claims realistic. Google's [unreliable claims policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936857) prohibits promising unrealistic weight loss within a short time or with little effort; "Beginner-friendly classes with a coach" is safe, "Lose 10 pounds in a month" is not.

## The Math

The same click is worth different amounts to different offers. This table shows the most you can pay per click if you hold your target cost per new member fixed. Every input is an **assumption** for illustration. Replace them with your own after 30 days of data.

| | Generic near me | Class type | Trial offer | Personal training | Brand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Max ad cost per new member (assumption) | $250 | $250 | $250 | $400 | $250 |
| Click to enquiry rate (assumption) | 5% | 5% | 8% | 4% | 15% |
| Enquiry to member rate (assumption) | 20% | 20% | 25% | 25% | 35% |
| Ceiling click price | $2.50 | $2.50 | $5.00 | $4.00 | $13.13 |

Formula: ceiling CPC = max cost per member x enquiry rate x member rate. For the trial offer: $250 x 0.08 x 0.25 = $5.00.

How to use it:

- **Compare the ceiling with a real estimate.** Open [Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243) in your own account, set your city, and check the average cost it gives for each group. Google says you must complete account setup, including billing information, to use it, and that very low-volume keywords may return nothing.
- **If the estimate sits above the ceiling, change an input, not the bid.** Raise the enquiry rate with a stronger offer and page, raise the member rate by answering faster, or raise what a member is worth to you. Cutting the bid alone just removes you from the auction.
- **Brand clicks justify a high bid** because those people already know you.
- **Assumptions are not benchmarks.** The 20% member rate in particular depends on how fast you reply and how you run tours. Measure yours.

A $1.50 click for "yoga poses for beginners" costs less than a $5 click for "yoga classes [neighborhood]" and will almost never become a member. Judge keywords by cost per member, not cost per click.
</Callout>

## FAQ

**Q: What are the best Google Ads keywords for a fitness studio?**
A: Local, high-intent phrases that name your class type and place, such as "pilates classes [city]" or "personal trainer [neighborhood]", plus offer-led terms if you run a trial. Which are best for you depends on your market, so test them in Keyword Planner and judge them by cost per member.

**Q: Should I bid on "fitness studio near me"?**
A: Test it in its own ad group. Roundups such as Webtonic's quote high-intent local terms as the most expensive, so check the cost per member before you scale it.

**Q: Should I bid on competitor names?**
A: Only with a clearly better offer and a small test budget, and never use their trademarks in your ad text. See our guide to competitor keywords.

**Q: How often should I update my keywords?**
A: Read the search terms report weekly for the first month, then every two to four weeks. That is my rule of thumb, not a Google rule.

**Q: What if the click price is higher than my ceiling?**
A: Narrow the radius, use longer and more specific phrases, improve the offer and landing page, and show ads only during hours when someone answers the phone. If it still does not fit, put the money into your Business Profile and local SEO first.
