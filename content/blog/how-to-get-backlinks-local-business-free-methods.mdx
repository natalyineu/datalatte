A local business earns free backlinks by being listed or mentioned by real local organisations: suppliers, associations, event organisers, local press and partner businesses. Google's Business Profile help says prominence is "also based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have", so links matter for local rankings. Google's spam policies also name four link practices to avoid, including buying links and automated link creation. No source publishes how many links a local business needs, so aim for relevant ones, not a number.

## Why Do Backlinks Matter for Local Search?

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity. Prominence means how well-known a business is, and it is based on information such as how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking.

So links are one input among several, next to your profile details and reviews. Google's page does not rank them by weight. Start with the profile and reviews, which our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers, then build links on top.

## Which Link Tactics Break Google's Rules?

Google's spam policies define link spam as creating links to or from a site primarily to manipulate rankings. The examples include:

- Buying or selling links for ranking purposes, including sending free products in return for a write-up with a link
- Excessive link exchanges, such as "link to me and I'll link to you"
- Large-scale article marketing and guest posting campaigns, including optimized anchor text in articles
- Using programs or services to generate links automatically

Paid or sponsored links are not a violation if marked with a `rel="nofollow"` or `rel="sponsored"` attribute. A local sponsorship that gives you a link is fine, as long as the link is tagged if you paid for it in exchange for the link.

## What Are 11 Free Ways to Earn Local Backlinks?

These are methods, not guaranteed results. Whether a given site links to you is up to its owner.

| # | Method | What to ask for | Rule check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Your own Business Profile | Website link on the profile | Allowed |
| 2 | Suppliers and brands you buy from | "Where to buy" or stockist mention | Fine if not a paid swap |
| 3 | Local business associations | Member directory listing | Fine |
| 4 | Chamber of commerce | Directory page | Check if free or paid; paid is fine if tagged |
| 5 | Local charities you support | Sponsor or partner mention | Tag if paid |
| 6 | Local events you host | Event listing on community calendars | Fine |
| 7 | Local news and bloggers | Mention in a story you give them | No payment or free product for a link |
| 8 | Partner businesses | Cross-referral page that is useful to customers | Avoid mass link exchanges |
| 9 | Schools and clubs | Discount or class page links | Fine |
| 10 | Local resource pages | Request inclusion if you fit | Fine |
| 11 | Your own useful content | A guide, map or price list others cite | Fine |

Use a spreadsheet: site, contact, date asked, outcome. The practical limit is time, not money.

## How Should the Link Be Written?

Google's link guidance says it generally follows a link only if it is an HTML anchor element with an href attribute. Good anchor text is descriptive, reasonably concise and relevant to both pages. Generic phrases such as "click here" give little context. Avoid keyword stuffing in anchor text.

When you ask for a link, suggest a plain description, such as your business name or what you do, and the exact URL of the most relevant page. That page may be a service page, not the homepage. ## Worked Example: A 4-Week Outreach Plan

The numbers below are an **assumption** for planning. No source says how many replies to expect.

| Week | Task | Count (assumption) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Check profile, own site and social links | 5 profiles |
| 2 | Ask suppliers and associations | 10 requests |
| 3 | Offer a story or event to local press and blogs | 5 pitches |
| 4 | Follow up and log outcomes | all 20 contacts |

At the end, count how many links went live, which pages they point to, and which asks got no reply. Repeat with the types that worked. If none worked, the business may need something worth linking to first, such as a useful guide or event. ## How Do Links Fit With AI Search?

Search results are changing. Our articles on [Google AI Overviews and local SEO](/blog/google-ai-overviews-local-business-seo) and [AI for local SEO](/blog/ai-for-local-seo) cover how mentions and listings may feed those answers. Pew Research found that people clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared and 15% when it did not, so a mention that a person reads can matter even without a click. That study measured US adults in March 2025 and does not measure links directly.

## FAQ

### Do backlinks help local SEO?
Google says prominence is partly based on how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have.

### Is it okay to pay for a backlink?
Google treats buying links to influence rankings as link spam. Paid links are not a violation if marked with nofollow or sponsored.

### How many backlinks does a local business need?
No source publishes a number. Aim for relevant local links and compare your profile with the businesses ranking above you.

### Are link exchanges allowed?
Excessive "link to me and I'll link to you" exchanges are listed as link spam. A genuine partner page useful to customers is different.

### What anchor text should I request?
Google says good anchor text is descriptive, concise and relevant. Avoid stuffing keywords.
