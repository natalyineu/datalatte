The best free way to get backlinks for a local business is to earn links from places that already know you: local business directories, suppliers, partners, community organisations, local press and event pages. A handful of relevant local links usually helps more than dozens of random ones, and none of it requires a budget, only time and a bit of outreach.

Below is a practical list of free methods, how to find the opportunities, what to say when you ask, and what to avoid so you do not trigger spam problems.

## What a backlink is and why it matters

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Search engines treat links as one signal that other sites consider yours worth citing. For local businesses, links matter alongside your Google Business Profile, reviews and on-page content. They are rarely the first thing to fix, but once your profile and website basics are in order, relevant links can help you compete in the local pack and organic results.

Quality beats quantity. A link from your local chamber of commerce or a neighbourhood news site is typically more useful than a link from an unrelated blog network.

## Before you start: get your basics right

- Make sure your website loads fast, works on a phone and has pages worth linking to, such as service pages, a clear About page and useful guides.
- Keep your name, address and phone number consistent everywhere. Our [local citation building guide](/blog/local-business-citation-building-guide-2026) covers this in detail.
- Create at least one page or resource that people would want to cite, for example a local guide, a seasonal checklist or a pricing explainer.

## Free backlink methods that work locally

### 1. Claim your business listings

Complete listings on Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, Facebook and relevant industry directories. Many of these are links with your website address. Some are nofollow, meaning they pass less direct ranking value, but they still build presence and consistency. Do not pay for low-quality directory bundles.

### 2. Join your local chamber of commerce or business association

Membership sometimes costs money, but many local business groups, Business Improvement Districts and neighbourhood associations list members for free, or have free directory submissions.

### 3. Ask suppliers, partners and brands you use

Stockists, equipment makers, franchise parent brands and software providers often have "find a local partner" or "where to buy" pages. Ask to be listed. For a coffee shop this might be your roaster; for a groomer, a product brand you stock.

### 4. Sponsor or take part in local events

Charity runs, school fairs, markets and community festivals often list participants and sponsors on their site. Even a small in-kind donation, such as free class passes or a raffle prize, can earn a link and goodwill. Ask for a link to your website when you agree.

### 5. Create local partnerships

Cross-promote with neighbouring businesses that serve the same customers but do not compete. A salon and a bridal shop, or a gym and a physiotherapist, can each mention the other on a "local favourites" or "recommended" page.

### 6. Pitch local journalists and bloggers

Local newspapers, community blogs and newsletters look for stories: openings, anniversaries, charity efforts, new menu items, seasonal advice. Send a short, relevant pitch with one clear angle and a photo. Free expert-quote request services exist, though their availability changes over time.

### 7. Offer expert comments

If you have specialist knowledge, such as dog coat care or training basics, offer short quotes to local blogs and news sites. A bylined quote often includes a link to your site.

### 8. Reclaim unlinked mentions

Search Google for your business name in quotes, minus your own site. If someone has mentioned you without linking, send a polite note thanking them and asking them to add a link.

### 9. Write guest posts for relevant local sites

Think neighbourhood blogs, hobby clubs, parent groups and industry associations. Share a genuinely useful article, not a sales page. Avoid sites that obviously exist only to sell guest posts.

### 10. Support local organisations

Schools, sports clubs, charities and community groups often thank donors or supporters on their website. A modest, genuine donation can earn a mention.

### 11. Create something worth linking to

Examples that attract natural links in a local context:

- A neighbourhood guide to your category (best dog walks, best study spots).
- A free checklist or seasonal calendar.
- A short, original survey of your own customers with honest results.
- A well-photographed local history or "behind the scenes" page.

## How to find link opportunities

A simple, free process:

1. Search Google for "your city + business directory", "your city + local blog", "your city + community events".
2. Look at which sites link to competitors. Free versions of tools like Ahrefs Webmaster Tools and Google Search Console show your own links; Ahrefs' free backlink checker and similar tools give a limited look at competitors. See our [Ahrefs local SEO guide](/blog/ahrefs-local-seo-guide).
3. Make a spreadsheet: site, contact, relevance, status, date contacted.
4. Work through the list in order of relevance, not size.

If you have not set up Google Search Console, our [Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-guide) shows how to see which sites already link to you.

## A short outreach template

Keep it brief, specific and about them:

> Subject: Local listing suggestion
>
> Hi [Name], I run [Business] on [Street]. I noticed your [page name] lists local [category] businesses. We have been serving [neighbourhood] since [year] and I thought our [page] might be useful to your readers. Would you consider adding us? Happy to share photos or a short description.
>
> Thanks, [Your name]

Follow up once after a week or so. If there is no reply, move on.

## What to avoid

- **Buying links or packages.** Paid links that pass ranking value can violate Google's guidelines and may risk a penalty.
- **Link exchanges at scale.** A few genuine partner links are fine; mass swapping looks manipulative.
- **Comment and forum spam.** It rarely helps and can hurt your reputation.
- **Irrelevant directories** with no local or industry connection.
- **Expecting overnight results.** Link effects often take weeks or months to show.

## Track what you earn

Check your links monthly in Google Search Console (Links report) and note new referring sites. Also watch referral traffic in Google Analytics, because a link that sends real visitors is worth more than a purely cosmetic one.

## A 30-day link-building plan

- **Week 1:** Complete and standardise your main listings and check your Search Console links report.
- **Week 2:** Contact three suppliers or partners about listing pages, and one neighbouring business about cross-promotion.
- **Week 3:** Pitch one local story to a news site or community newsletter, and reclaim any unlinked mentions.
- **Week 4:** Publish one useful local resource and share it with groups likely to find it valuable.

Repeat monthly. Five or six good links over a few months is a solid result for most single-location businesses.

## FAQ

**Q: How many backlinks does a local business need?**
A: There is no fixed number. It depends on how competitive your area is. Look at the sites ranking above you and aim to match or beat their link quality over time.

**Q: Are directory links still worth getting?**
A: Reputable, relevant directories help with consistency and credibility, though they are rarely a big ranking lever alone. Skip low-quality, spammy ones.

**Q: Do nofollow links help?**
A: They pass less direct ranking value but can still send traffic and build brand visibility, and a natural link profile usually has a mix.

**Q: How long does it take to see results?**
A: Often a few weeks to several months. Link building is a steady habit, not a quick fix.
