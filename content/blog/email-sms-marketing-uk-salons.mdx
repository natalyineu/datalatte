A UK salon can send marketing emails and texts only to people who gave specific consent, or to existing clients under the "soft opt-in" rule, and every message must carry a working opt-out. That is the ICO's reading of the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR). Within those limits, the useful messages are a booking reminder, a rebooking nudge and an occasional offer, sent by email or text.

## What Do UK Rules Say About Marketing Texts and Emails?

The ICO says you must not send marketing emails or texts to individuals without specific consent, such as a ticked opt-in box. The rules also cover picture messages, voicemails and social media direct messages.

There is one exception. The soft opt-in lets you email or text a person who bought, or discussed buying, a similar service from you, provided you gave them a clear chance to opt out when you collected their details and you offer the opt-out again in every message. It does not cover prospects or bought-in lists.

Every message needs a working opt-out and a valid contact address, and you must not hide who you are. Sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals. This is general information, not legal advice.

## How Do You Build a List the Right Way?

Collect consent at the moments you already meet clients: at the online booking form, at the till and at the first consultation. Use a separate, unticked box for marketing, and say plainly what you will send and how often.

Keep a record of when and how each person agreed, plus a "do not contact" list, which the ICO recommends. If a client booked only once, treat the soft opt-in carefully: the first message must have offered the opt-out. Do not add people who only followed you on Instagram or who came from a purchased list.

## Which Three Sequences Should You Automate?

Start with messages that serve the client, not the salon.

| Sequence | Trigger | Channel | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Booking reminder | Appointment booked | Text | Time, address, how to reschedule |
| Rebooking nudge | Weeks after a visit (your typical gap, for example 6 for a cut) | Email or text | Link to book, stylist name |
| Win-back | No visit for a set time you choose | Email | Reminder of services, one clear offer if you want one |

The timing figures are examples to adapt to your service cycle; no source publishes a standard interval. Reminders only restate a booking the client made. Once a message adds an offer or promotion, treat it as marketing and apply the consent rules above.

## When Should You Use Email and When Text?

Use text for short, time-sensitive messages: reminders, a last-minute gap, a running-late notice. Use email for anything longer: aftercare advice, a seasonal menu, a gift voucher. Texts cost more per message than emails on the one price list we checked, so keep marketing texts rare.

Both channels share one risk: sending too often drives opt-outs. Pick a limit, such as two marketing messages a month, and measure unsubscribes against it.

## FAQ

### Do UK salons need consent to send marketing texts?
Yes, generally. The ICO requires specific consent, with a soft opt-in for existing clients who were offered an opt-out.

### Are appointment reminders marketing?
The ICO guidance covers marketing messages. A reminder that only restates the booking is not an offer, but once you add a promotion, treat it as marketing and check consent.

### Can I message people who follow my salon on Instagram?
Not by email or text without consent. The rules also apply to direct messages on social media.
