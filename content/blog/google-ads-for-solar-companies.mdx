Solar advertising changed in 2026. The federal residential credit that many ads leaned on is no longer available for new purchases, so the buyer pool is smaller and clicks are less forgiving. This guide covers what has been confirmed, what the cost benchmarks look like, and how to judge a campaign by cost per held appointment instead of cost per click.

## What changed: the 25D credit

The IRS states that the [Residential Clean Energy Credit equals 30% of the costs of qualified property, and is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit). The same page also contains an older sentence about a phase-out in 2033, so confirm current rules with the IRS or a tax professional before quoting anything about eligibility in your ads.

Industry sources add context. [SEIA and Wood Mackenzie report](https://seia.org/research-resources/solar-market-insight-report-2025-year-in-review/) that the residential segment installed 4,647 MWdc in 2025, down 2% from 2024, and their later Q3 2026 report says distributed segments continued to soften year over year after the credit expired. Solar.com and pv magazine describe leases and power purchase agreements as continuing to use a different business credit for several more years; that is trade-press reporting, so verify it against current law before advertising lease offers.

Practical impact for your ads: any copy that promises "30% back" for cash or loan buyers is out of date and risks complaints.

## What clicks and leads cost

Hard solar-only benchmarks from neutral sources are scarce. What we could verify:

- **Category benchmark.** Elevarus, citing WordStream by LocaliQ 2026, lists Home and Home Improvement at [$8.33 CPC, $90.92 per lead and an 8.05% conversion rate](https://elevarus.com/solar-google-ads-lead-generation/), versus $5.42 CPC and $66.69 per lead across all industries. This is second-hand and not solar-specific.
- **Agency estimates for solar.** The same article quotes Sprout Sage at $8-$45 per click, $80-$300 per raw lead and $300-$800 per sat appointment. These are one agency's estimates, which we could not verify independently.
- **Lead quality.** LocalLeads247, quoted by Elevarus, estimates that 50-70% of cheap, broadly targeted solar contacts are renters, have unsuitable roofs or will not buy for years. Again an estimate, but it matches why raw lead cost misleads.

The point is that two quality filters (qualification and appointment show rate) multiply cost per lead by more than three. Better filtering moves the final number far more than a cheaper click does.

## Decision guide

1. **Filter in the ad and the form.** Ask homeowner status and roof or monthly bill range in the first fields. Fewer leads, better leads.
2. **Retire tax-credit keywords and copy** for cash and loan buyers. Isolate them or remove them.
3. **Lead with savings on the electric bill, financing terms and warranty,** whichever you can substantiate.
4. **Optimize for held appointments.** Import offline conversions so Google learns from showed appointments, not form fills.
5. **Add negatives** such as "jobs", "diy", "kit", "course". See our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).
6. **Pick the campaign type deliberately.** Our [Performance Max vs standard campaigns comparison](/blog/performance-max-vs-standard-google-ads-which-should-you-run) explains the tradeoffs when conversion data is thin.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the federal solar tax credit still available in 2026?**

According to the IRS, the 30% residential credit is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Check with a tax professional for your situation.

**How much does a solar lead cost on Google Ads?**

Agency estimates range from $80 to $300 per raw lead, and the home-improvement category average is $90.92. Your cost depends on market and keywords.

**What should I measure instead of cost per lead?**

Cost per held appointment and cost per sale.

**Should I target renters out?**

You cannot target by tenure directly in search, so use qualifying questions in the form and ad copy.
