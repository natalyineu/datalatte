Fire restoration companies win work when they are found and called in the first hours after a loss. The practical stack is a service-area Google Business Profile, a call-first search campaign, a fast process for missed calls, and steady referrals from insurance agents, adjusters and property managers. Lead vendors sell exclusive leads, so compare their terms before you buy. The National Fire Protection Association reports that US fire departments responded to 358,500 home structure fires in 2023.

## How Many Fires Create Demand for Restoration?

The NFPA figures show a large base of residential fires. [PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/emergency-service-marketing/fire-and-smoke-damage-marketing/) reports that these fires caused $9.4 billion in direct property damage in 2023. [HighPoint Digital](https://gohighpointdigital.com/fire-damage-marketing/), citing NFPA data from 2024, says a home structure fire is reported in the US roughly every 96 seconds.

These are national totals for fires, not restoration jobs. Many fires cause little structural damage, and many damaged homes never use a restoration company. For a local firm, the useful point is timing. The owner is usually stressed and searching from a phone, so your profile, reviews and phone answer must work in that moment.

## What Is a Fire Restoration Job Worth?

HighPoint Digital says a typical fire and smoke restoration claim is $20,000 or more, several times a standard water-damage ticket. The same page states that fire and smoke restoration jobs commonly run $8,000 to $50,000 or more per loss, without citing a source for that range.

The Insurance Information Institute puts the average residential fire loss at $26,000, combining structure damage, contents loss and temporary relocation. [PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/emergency-service-marketing/fire-and-smoke-damage-marketing/) also cites the Restoration Industry Association, which says more than 80% of fire restoration jobs over $10,000 involve a homeowners insurance claim. Insurance paperwork is part of most larger jobs, so your marketing should explain the claims process clearly. Do not promise claim approval, because the insurer decides that.

## Should You Buy Exclusive Leads or Run Your Own Ads?

[33 Mile Radius](https://www.33mileradius.com/fire-damage-leads/) says it sends each fire and smoke damage lead to only one partner rather than to several restoration companies. The table compares that model with running your own search ads. The sourced figures apply only to the vendor's stated terms, so check each vendor's live page before you sign.

| Option | Lead delivery | Billing and terms | Control |

| Shared lead sites | Several restoration companies receive the same lead | Check the seller's terms | Limited to what the seller allows |

The vendor also says most of its partners receive their first fire restoration lead within two weeks of activating their account, and that roughly 7 out of 10 calls turn into viable jobs based on its own call statistics. Treat both as vendor claims, not independent results.

## What Does a Worked Lead Budget Look Like?

The inputs below are all assumptions except the average job value, which is the low end of HighPoint's stated range. Replace each one with your own figures.

1. Monthly spend on exclusive leads: $1,200 (assumption)
2. Valid leads received per month: 8 (assumption)
3. Cost per valid lead: $150 (assumption, not a quoted vendor price)
4. Share of leads that become viable jobs: 70% (assumption, borrowed from the vendor's call statistic)
5. Average job value: $8,000 (low end of HighPoint's $8,000 to $50,000 range)
6. Gross margin on a job: 35% (assumption)

Viable jobs are 8 × 70%, about 5.6 per month. Revenue is 5.6 × $8,000, about $44,800. Gross profit is 35% of that, about $15,680. After the $1,200 lead spend, the contribution is about $14,480. The cost per job is about $214.

Now test a weaker month: if only 30% of leads become jobs (assumption), there are about 2.4 jobs, revenue of about $19,200, gross profit near $6,720, and a contribution of about $5,520. The math still works in that case, but the margin shrinks quickly. Track which leads became jobs for at least one month before you decide.

## How Fast Must Your Phone Be Answered?

HighPoint Digital describes a Missed-Call Text-Back Automation that texts the caller back within 60 seconds if a call is missed while the crew is on a board-up. That is a vendor product claim, but the timing principle holds. HighPoint, citing EPA mold guidance, also puts the window before mold can begin colonizing after moisture exposure at 24 to 48 hours, so delays create real damage and real lost work.

Use this checklist for the first week:

1. Put one answered phone number on your Business Profile, and confirm it matches your website.
2. Set an after-hours forward to an on-call phone.
3. Send an automatic text-back within a minute of any missed call. For how these flows are built, see [AI for marketing automation](/blog/ai-for-marketing-automation).
4. Log the source of every call and every job in one sheet.
5. Review the missed-call count each week and fix any gap in coverage.

## Which Referral Partners and Offers Bring Calls?

Insurance agents, adjusters and property managers often hear about a loss first, so a steady relationship with them is worth more than a single ad. The sourced facts do not give a number for how many restoration jobs come from referrals, so track it yourself.

[AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-for-construction-companies) lists a free inspection as a strong lead offer for roofing, storm damage, foundations and restoration, noting it works when there is urgency or visible damage. ## FAQ

### How do fire restoration companies get calls?

Calls come from search, maps, referrals from insurance and property professionals, and lead vendors. The sourced figures do not split calls by channel, so record the source of each job for at least one month.

### Are fire leads shared with other companies?

It depends on the vendor. 33 Mile Radius says it sends each lead to only one partner. Check the terms of any other provider before you buy.

### How long before a new lead source produces work?

33 Mile Radius says most of its partners receive a first fire restoration lead within two weeks of activating. That is the vendor's claim, not an independent result.

### Do I pay for leads that are not valid?

33 Mile Radius states that partners are billed only for valid fire and smoke restoration leads, charged weekly. Confirm the definition of a valid lead in writing.

### What is a typical fire restoration job size?

HighPoint Digital states that jobs commonly run $8,000 to $50,000 or more per loss. Use your own average job value in any budget.
