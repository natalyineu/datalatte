## How Big Is the Demand for Fire Restoration?

The US Fire Administration reports about 344,600 residential building fires in 2023, with 2,890 deaths, 10,400 injuries and $11.27 billion in dollar loss. Those are national totals for fires, not a count of restoration jobs. Many fires cause little structural damage, and many damaged homes never use a restoration company.

For a local firm the useful point is timing. A fire is unplanned, and the owner usually searches in a hurry from a phone. Your marketing has to work at that moment: a profile that shows your phone number and service area, and reviews that read as calm and competent.

## Which Google Ads Options Exist for Restoration?

Google's Local Services Ads help page lists "Water damage services" among home service categories. Fire and smoke restoration, or restoration in general, does not appear on that page, so do not assume you can use that product for fire work. Check Google's current list for your state.

Search ads remain the main paid option. The closest LocaliQ category is Home and Home Improvement at $8.33 per click, 8.05% conversion and $90.92 per lead. No fire-specific benchmark is published. ## How Should a Restoration Company Set Up Its Business Profile?

Use a service-area profile if customers do not visit your office. Google's guidelines say service-area businesses should hide the address, service areas should generally stay within about two hours' drive, and virtual offices are not eligible unless staffed during business hours.

Keep the business name to the real name. Descriptions cannot contain links or focus on prices and promotions. List services separately (smoke odor removal, soot cleaning, contents cleaning) and add photos of completed work with the owner's permission. See the [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## Do Standards and Certifications Belong in Your Marketing?

Yes, if you hold them. The IICRC published ANSI/IICRC S700, the standard for professional fire and smoke damage restoration, on January 3, 2025 (reported by trade publication Cleanfax, a secondary source). It covers assessing fire residue and odor, cleaning and odor management, contractor qualifications and documentation. It excludes wildfires.

IICRC also lists certified technicians in an online directory. State only certifications you actually hold, with the certificate number if the program provides one. Customers cannot judge technique, so proof of standards gives them a reason to choose you.

## How Do Reviews and Referrals Work for Emergency Jobs?

BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 89% of consumers expect owners to respond to reviews, 68% will only use a business rated 4 stars or higher, and 74% only consider reviews from the last three months. Ask satisfied customers when the work is finished, using a link or QR code. Google prohibits incentives in exchange for reviews.

Insurance agents, adjusters, property managers and plumbers meet people right after a loss. No verified data exists on how many restoration jobs come from referrals, so track the source of every job yourself. ## FAQ

### How do fire restoration companies get customers?

Mostly through search, maps, referrals from insurance and property professionals, and reviews. No verified data splits the share by channel, so measure the source of each job.

### Can I use Local Services Ads for fire restoration?

Google's current list shows water damage services but not fire restoration. Check Google's eligible categories for your area before budgeting.

### How much does a click cost?

The closest LocaliQ benchmark is $8.33 per click in Home and Home Improvement. There is no fire-specific published figure.

### Should I mention insurance in my ads?

You can describe that you work with insurers if it is true. Avoid promises about claim approval, because you do not control it.
