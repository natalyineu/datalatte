## Why Do Reviews Matter for Local Search?

Google lists three local ranking factors: relevance, distance and prominence. Prominence depends on how well known a business is, including how many reviews it has. Google's help says "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." It does not say how much reviews weigh compared with other factors, and gives no target score.

Customers read them too. BrightLocal found 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and 41% always do, up from 29% in 2025. Google is still a main source, used by 71%, though that is down from 83%.

## What Rating and Recency Do Customers Expect?

The bar rose in 2026. BrightLocal reports 68% of consumers require four or more stars, up from 55%, and 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 or higher, up from 17%. Recency counts too: 74% only consider reviews from the last three months, and 32% look for the last two weeks.

This means a steady flow of new reviews helps more than a burst followed by silence. Our [review strategy guide](/blog/google-business-reviews-strategy) covers building that flow.

## How Can You Ask for Reviews Within Google's Rules?

Google says you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code. Customers must be signed into a Google Account to post. The policy is firm on two points: reviews must reflect a genuine experience, and offering incentives such as free or discounted goods or services for reviews is strictly prohibited.

Google's contributed content policy also says merchants should not require or pressure users to leave reviews on the premises, and lists requesting that staff solicit a certain number of reviews as a violation. It also says posting from multiple accounts at the request of one person is fake engagement. BrightLocal found 65% of consumers wrote a review after being asked, so asking works. Plan it as a simple message after the visit, as in our [coffee shop review guide](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop).

## How Should You Reply to Reviews?

Reply to all of them. BrightLocal found 89% of consumers expect owners to respond, 80% are likely to use a business that responds to all reviews, and 50% are put off by templated or generic replies. 81% expect a response within a week.

Google says your reply appears publicly under the review, the reviewer is notified, and replies are checked against content policies first. Approval usually takes about 10 minutes, though it can take up to 30 days. For fake reviews, see the [fake review guide](/blog/how-to-deal-with-fake-google-reviews).

## What Does a Review Plan Look Like? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions** for a small studio.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Customers per month | 200 |
| Customers asked for a review | 160 (80%) |
| Share who leave a review | 10% |
| New reviews per month | 16 |
| Time to reply to each | 3 minutes |
| Monthly reply time | 48 minutes |

Sixteen reviews a month means the newest ones are never more than two weeks old, which meets the strictest recency window in the survey. If the ask rate falls to 20%, new reviews drop to about 4 and recency suffers. The 10% response rate is an assumption: BrightLocal reports 65% of consumers wrote a review when asked, but that is a survey answer, not your conversion rate. Track your own.

## FAQ

### Do reviews affect Google local ranking?
Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. It does not say how much.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?
No. Google strictly prohibits incentives such as free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews.

### How do customers leave a review?
They must be signed into a Google Account. You can share a Google link or QR code.

### Should I reply to every review?
BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 89% of consumers expect a response. Reply to positive and negative reviews.

### How many stars do customers want?
BrightLocal found 68% require four or more stars, and 31% require 4.5 or more.
