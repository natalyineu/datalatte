Google Business Profile reviews are a local visibility signal you can build within Google's rules. Google prohibits offering incentives, such as free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for reviews. Reviews appear next to your profile in Maps and Search, and Mailchimp describes Google reviews as especially influential for local search. Asking customers is allowed, but the request must stay free of rewards. Replies are public and must follow the same policies as reviews. This guide covers the rules, how to ask, how to reply, and a worked plan with assumption-labelled inputs.

## Do Google Reviews Affect Local Ranking?

Reviews play a part in local ranking, but Google describes the effect only in general terms. Mailchimp notes that reviews on a Google My Business profile are especially influential and play a role in local search ranking and business visibility ([Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/resources/local-marketing/), secondary). Google's own guidance adds that prominence is based partly on how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

Google does not publish how much weight reviews carry against distance or relevance, so treat reviews as one lever among several. A profile with a steady flow of new reviews and considered replies is easier to improve than one chasing a single ranking formula. ## Where Do Reviews Show Up for Customers?

Google states that reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). That means every review is seen by people deciding whether to call, visit or book, not only by people checking for a problem. A specific, recent review usually does more work than a bare star score.

A third-party study cited by May-Fly reports that 72% of customers say they use Google reviews to find businesses ([May-Fly](https://www.may-fly.co.uk/resources/how-to-generate-leads-with-seo/), secondary). That page is a retelling, so check the original study before quoting the figure in a client pitch. The sample rating panels on the same page, such as 4.5 out of 5 stars from 268 reviews, show the format customers see. They are examples, not benchmarks for your category.

## How Can You Ask for Reviews Within Google's Rules?

Google strictly prohibits offering incentives, such as free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers posting reviews ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Any reward tied to posting falls under that rule. Asking is allowed. Send the same request to every customer, not only to the ones you expect to be happy, and do not attach an offer to it.

Google also says you can flag a review if you believe it violates its content policies ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). Use that route for spam, abuse or content that breaks policy. A negative review that is accurate is not a policy violation, so answer it instead. Keep the ask itself short: thank the customer and share the review link. Check the live help page for the current way to share the link before printing any QR code.

## How Should You Reply to Reviews?

Google says review replies must follow its policies, the same as reviews ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). That creates three firm limits. Do not use a reply to offer deals or promotions to the reviewer. Never share a reviewer's private information. Never respond with personal attacks, even when a review feels unfair.

Replies are where prospective customers see how a business handles problems. Refer to the specific service the customer mentioned, thank them for useful detail, and keep negative replies calm and factual. Templated replies that ignore the review's content are easy to spot and do little for trust, so write each one for the person who left it.

## What Does a Review Plan Look Like? A Worked Example

The inputs below are all **assumptions** for a small studio, not sourced figures. Replace them with your own counts.

| Input | Value | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customers per month | 200 | Assumption |
| Customers asked for a review | 160 (80%) | Assumption |
| Share who leave a review | 10% | Assumption |
| New reviews per month | 16 | Calculated: 160 × 10% |
| Minutes to reply to each review | 3 | Assumption |
| Monthly reply time | 48 minutes | Calculated: 16 × 3 |

At this plan, the studio gains 16 reviews a month and spends under an hour replying. If the ask rate falls to 20%, the same 200 customers produce 40 asks and about 4 reviews. The lower ask rate would cut the flow by three-quarters, which is why the ask step matters more than the reply step for growth.

The 10% response rate is an assumption and should be replaced with your own tracked number after a few months. Use the calculation to set a monthly target, then compare it with what actually arrives.

Use this checklist to start:

1. Write one ask message that offers no reward for posting.
2. Send it after each completed job, to every customer.
3. Reply to each new review promptly, following the policy limits above.
4. Flag only reviews that break Google's content policies.
5. Count new reviews each month and compare the total with your target.

## FAQ

### Do reviews affect Google local ranking?
Mailchimp says reviews are especially influential and play a role in local search ranking. Google's guidance says prominence is also based on how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has. Google does not publish how much weight each factor carries.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?
No. Google strictly prohibits offering incentives, such as free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers posting reviews.

### Should I reply to every review?
Replies must follow Google's policies. Do not use a reply to offer deals or promotions, never share a reviewer's private information, and avoid personal attacks.

### What can I do about a review that breaks policy?
You can flag it if you believe it violates Google's content policies. Flagging is for policy breaches, not for reviews you disagree with.
