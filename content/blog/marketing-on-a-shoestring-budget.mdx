A business with almost no budget should start with free tools: a complete Google Business Profile, a review link, regular social posts and a small email list. Google says creating a Business Profile is free, and Mailchimp's free plan covers 250 contacts and 500 sends a month. Add paid ads only after those basics run.

This guide lists seven tactics with their published limits. Where a number is not available, it says so, and the budget example is an assumption.

## What Is the Cheapest Marketing That Still Works?

The cheapest channels are the ones where customers already look for you. Google says the Business Profile is free and lets customers find you on Search and Maps, read reviews, see posts, book and request quotes. Fill in every field, add photos and answer reviews.

Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) lists what to complete. Then add the tactics below in order.

## The Seven Free or Low-Cost Tactics

1. **Complete your Business Profile.** Free, per Google.
2. **Ask for reviews.** Google lets you share a link or QR code.
3. **Post on one social platform.** Pew's 2026 data shows 70% of US adults use Facebook and 51% Instagram.
4. **Start an email list.** Mailchimp's free plan has limits (below).
5. **Partner with a neighbour.** Swap referrals with a business that shares your customers.
6. **Run a small local ad test.** Use a radius of at least 1 km.
7. **Ask customers where they found you.** Free market research.

## How Do You Get Reviews Without Paying?

BrightLocal's 2026 survey says 97% of consumers read online reviews. Google's help says you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code. Print the QR code at the till and send the link after each visit.

Do not offer discounts or free items for reviews: Google says doing so is prohibited and treated as fake and misleading content. Our [review request automation guide](/blog/how-to-automate-google-review-requests) shows how to make the ask routine.

## What Can You Do With a Free Email Plan?

Mailchimp's pricing page lists a free plan at $0 a month with a limit of 250 contacts, a maximum of 500 sends a month or 250 a day, one seat and one audience. It does not include marketing automation flows or scheduling. Pricing can change, so check the page.

That is enough for a monthly newsletter to regular customers. See the [cheap marketing ideas list](/blog/cheap-marketing-ideas-small-business) for more low-cost options.

## When Should You Start Paying for Ads?

When the free basics work and you know what a new customer is worth. Google's help says radius targeting requires at least 1 km, so a small shop can still target its neighbourhood. Start with a small daily budget and read the results weekly.

How much to set aside overall is covered in the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) and the [digital marketing budget guide](/blog/digital-marketing-budget-guide). The SBA also advises using free federal data such as Census and Bureau of Labor Statistics figures before paying for market research.

## What Does a $100 Month Look Like? A Worked Example

Track new customers by asking "How did you hear about us?" and count the answers monthly. Move money toward the line that produces customers, and cut the rest.

## FAQ

### How much should a small business spend on marketing?
No single figure applies. This guide shows a plan that starts at $0 and an assumed $100 a month. See the budget guides above for percentage-of-revenue methods.

### Is Google Business Profile really free?
Yes. Google's FAQ says creating a Business Profile and listing your business is free.

### Is Mailchimp free?
Its free plan lists $0 a month with 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, without automation flows. Check current limits on its pricing page.

### Which social platform should I use first?
Pew's 2026 data shows Facebook reaches 70% of US adults and Instagram 51%. Use whichever your customers use.

### Can I pay customers for reviews?
No. Google prohibits incentives in exchange for reviews.
