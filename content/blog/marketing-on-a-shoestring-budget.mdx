Marketing on a shoestring budget means spending first on channels that cost little to run: a complete online business profile, a routine for asking for reviews, one social account, a simple email list and referrals from nearby businesses. Paid search comes after those basics work. Benchmarks show why it needs a plan. WordStream's averages put the Google search cost per click at $2.69 across industries, so a small budget buys only a few dozen clicks each month. ## What Is the Cheapest Marketing That Still Works?

The cheapest channel is the one where people already look for you. Customers search for local services on Google, so a complete business listing puts your name in front of them at the moment they decide. Fill in every field, add current photos, list your opening hours and answer the questions customers ask most often.

Keep your business name, address and phone number identical across your website, listings and social profiles. Mismatched details make it harder for customers to trust what they find. Free channels take time to show results, so count new customers each month rather than guessing which tactic is working.

## How Do You Get Reviews Without Paying?

Ask at the moment of service, while the experience is fresh. A printed card or QR code at the counter that links to your review page takes a customer seconds to use. Staff can mention it at checkout, and a short reminder message after the job works well too.

Avoid offering discounts, gifts or other rewards in exchange for reviews. Review platforms set rules on incentivised feedback, and those rules can change, so read the current policy before you start a campaign. Reply to every review within a set window, including the critical ones, because prospective customers read the replies as well.

## Which Social Platform Should You Use First?

Pick one platform where your customers already spend time. Ask your best customers which app they use, then post there consistently instead of spreading thin across five networks. A steady weekly schedule usually beats sporadic bursts of posting followed by silence.

If you manage several accounts or post often, a scheduling tool saves time. Compare options in [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social for small business](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) before paying for one. Start with the free features of whatever you already use, and upgrade only when you can name the specific task the upgrade saves.

## What Can a $100 Month Buy in Paid Search?

The arithmetic is simple. Divide your monthly budget by the cost per click to estimate clicks, then apply your own conversion rate to estimate leads. The table uses WordStream's all-industry average and labels every input you would replace with your own numbers.

| Item | Amount | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly test budget | $100 | Assumption |
| Average search cost per click | $2.69 | WordStream benchmark |
| Estimated clicks ($100 divided by $2.69) | about 37 | Calculation |
| Assumed conversion rate (clicks to leads) | 5% | Assumption |
| Estimated leads (37 multiplied by 5%) | about 1.9 | Assumption |
| Estimated cost per lead ($2.69 divided by 5%) | about $53.80 | Assumption |

In a higher-cost niche the same budget buys less. WordStream lists the legal industry's average cost per click at $6.75, so the same $100 buys about 15 clicks (assumption: same budget, legal benchmark). Replace the 5% conversion rate with a figure from your own month of data. If a lead is worth more than your cost per lead, repeat the test. If not, change the offer before spending more.

## Should You Start Paying for Ads, and Which Type?

Run search ads before display ads. Search reaches people who already want a service, while display reaches people as they browse other sites. Google's help page says Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail.

Display campaigns use optimized targeting by default, responsive ads that combine your assets, and Smart Bidding options. WordStream's all-industry display click-through rate is 0.46%, so expect far fewer clicks than in search. Use display for awareness only after conversion tracking is working. ## How Do You Track Which Tactic Works?

Tracking costs nothing and shows where to spend next. Use this checklist each month:

1. Ask every new customer how they heard about you, and write the answer down.
2. Count the answers by channel at the end of the month.
3. Note which tactic produced the most paying customers.
4. Move the next dollar toward that channel and stop the weakest one.
5. Repeat the count after 90 days to confirm the pattern.

Small numbers are noisy, so look for the same winner across several months before changing course.

## FAQ

### How much should a small business spend on marketing?
No single figure fits every business. The worked example starts from an assumed $100 a month. Set the amount from your margins and the cost of acquiring a new customer, then review it every quarter.

### Can I pay customers for reviews?
Avoid it. Review platforms restrict incentivised reviews, and their rules can change, so check the current policy before running any campaign.

### Which social platform should I use first?
Use the one your customers already use. Ask a sample of customers, then commit to that platform for a few months before judging results.

### Is paid search worth it on a small budget?
It can be, if you track conversions. WordStream's all-industry average search cost per click of $2.69 means a small budget buys a limited number of clicks, so test one offer at a time.
