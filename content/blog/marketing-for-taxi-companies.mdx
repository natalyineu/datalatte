A taxi or private car company should market where people look when they need a ride now or need to book one ahead: Google Maps and search, with a profile that shows the phone number, hours and service area. Ride-hailing apps win on convenience, so compete on what they cannot promise, such as pre-booked airport transfers, fixed quotes and a dispatcher who answers. Paid search is the fastest way to appear for "taxi near me" style queries, and WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks list $2.14 per click for Travel and $7.17 for Personal Services.

## How Big Is the Ride-Hailing Threat?

The most recent Pew figure found is from a fall 2018 survey: 36% of US adults had ever used a ride-hailing service, up from 15% in 2015, but only about 4% used one weekly. Usage was 45% in urban areas and 19% in rural areas, and 51% for ages 18 to 29 against 24% for those 50 and over. These figures are old, so check a current local source before planning.

The practical reading is that ride-hailing is strongest with younger urban riders. Older, rural and pre-booked customers may still prefer calling a local firm. Work out which of those groups you serve.

## Should You Join an App Instead of Competing?

In some cities taxis can appear inside the Uber app. News reports in 2022 described Uber partnering with licensed New York City taxi app providers, so riders could book taxis in the Uber app, with the trip price shown to taxi drivers before they accept. Uber also lists taxis in other cities. These reports are secondary and dated, and terms vary by city.

If you consider an app, ask for the commission on each trip, whether you keep your own customers' details, and whether your own phone and web bookings continue. An app can add trips but also sends the customer relationship to someone else. Keep building direct bookings either way.

## How Do You Show Up on Google?

Start with your Business Profile. Google's guidelines say the name should be your real-world name without extra keywords or taglines, and that you should choose as few categories as possible to describe your core business. Add your phone number, hours, service area and photos of the vehicles.

Ask passengers for reviews right after a good trip, and reply to each. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers the details.

## Do Search Ads Work for a Taxi Firm?

They can, because a ride search is high intent. Use call assets so the ad has a call button. Google says taps on a mobile call asset are billed like other clicks and that call reporting, available on the Search Network, lets you count calls as conversions. You can also set numbers to appear only when you can take calls, which matters for a dispatch desk.

Add negative keywords for job seekers, driver training and unrelated services, and limit the location to where you can pick up. See [negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) and our [Google Ads guide for towing services](/blog/google-ads-for-towing-services) for another on-demand local service.

## Which Offers Beat an App?

Choose promises you can keep and print them in the ad and on the profile: a fixed airport price, a confirmed pre-booked pickup, child seats, large vehicles, or account billing for businesses. Corporate accounts, hotels and restaurants can send you repeat trips. A one-page offer for them, with a contact person and a direct number, costs little.

Do not use fake testimonials or invented savings. If you quote a price comparison with an app, say how and when you checked it.

If an average fare is $25 and the customer never returns, ads lose money on both cases. They pay off for repeat accounts, airport transfers and corporate customers. That is why you should track repeat bookings, not only the first trip.

## FAQ

### How can a taxi company compete with Uber and Lyft?

Compete on pre-booked trips, fixed prices, reliability and a human dispatcher, and make those visible on Google. Join an app only if its terms keep your direct customers.

### Are Google Ads worth it for a taxi company?

They can be for high-intent searches. LocaliQ lists $2.14 per click for Travel and $7.17 for Personal Services in 2026, and no taxi category exists, so test with a capped budget.

### How do I get more calls?

Use a complete Business Profile and call assets in ads. Google bills mobile call taps like other clicks and allows call reporting on the Search Network.

### Is ride-hailing use still growing?

The latest Pew figure used here is from 2018, so it cannot answer that. Look for a current survey or local data.

### Do I need an app of my own?

Not necessarily. Phone, web booking and a simple reminder message cover many small firms. An app costs money to build and maintain, and no source here shows it pays for small fleets.
