## How Crowded Is London for Small Businesses?

Very. The government's 2025 release counts 5,690,265 private sector businesses in the UK, of which 5,643,495, or 99.18%, have 0 to 49 employees. London accounts for 1.042 million. London and the South East together make up 34% of the UK business population. The release gives London's own share only as that rounded count.

Competition means you win by being specific: one neighbourhood, one service, strong proof. A salon in Hackney should not market like one in Croydon.

## How Do You Rank in London Local Search?

Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot change distance, which depends on where the searcher is. You can control relevance and prominence by filling in every field, verifying the profile, keeping hours current, earning reviews and replying to them.

Google also states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. If you visit customers, you can list up to 20 service areas and hide your address. See our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## How Should You Target London Ads?

Google Ads says that, depending on the country, you can target regions, cities or postal codes, and recommends adding the country, as in "Oxford, England". Radius targeting needs at least 1 km. Use postcodes or boroughs that match where customers actually live and work.

"Presence or interest" is Google's recommended default for Search. "Presence" suits cases where you only want people physically in the area. Add exclusions for places you do not serve.

| Approach | Best for | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Borough or postcode targeting | Shops and services with a fixed catchment | Narrow areas get fewer searches |
| Radius around a branch | Walk-in or short-drive businesses | Minimum radius 1 km |
| Whole-city targeting | Few firms | Wastes budget on distant clicks |

## How Do Reviews Work for London Customers?

BrightLocal's 2026 figures come from 1,002 US adults, so treat them as indicative for the UK. They show 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, 74% only consider reviews from the last three months, and 89% expect owners to reply.

Ask every customer for a review. Rewards for positive reviews are a risk; the FTC rule is a US rule, so check UK guidance too. ## The Math: What Does a 600 Pound Budget Buy?

No UK benchmark is published in these sources, so every input below is an **assumption**, not data.

| Step | Assumption | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | £600 | |
| Cost per click | £3.00 | 200 clicks |
| Clicks that become enquiries | 8% | 16 enquiries |
| Enquiries that book | 50% | 8 bookings |
| Cost per booking | £600 divided by 8 | £75 |

Replace each assumption with your own numbers after the first month. If your cost per click is £5, clicks fall to 120 and enquiries to about 10, so cost per booking rises to about £120. See the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) for a way to split spend.

## FAQ

### How many small businesses are there in London?
The UK government counts 1.042 million private sector businesses in London at the start of 2025.

### What is the best first step for a London small business?
Verify and complete your Google Business Profile, then build reviews.

### Should I target all of London with ads?
Usually not. Choose boroughs or postcodes that match your customers and exclude areas you do not serve.

### What does a click cost in London?
No UK figure is published in the sources used here. The example uses an assumption you should replace.

### Do I need a physical shopfront to be on Google?
No. Service-area businesses can hide their address and list up to 20 areas.
