Facebook ads can generate qualified insurance leads when you target a defined local audience, offer something specific (a quote review, a policy comparison), and follow up fast. Budget for testing, expect lead quality to vary, and judge campaigns on appointments and policies written rather than cheap clicks.

Insurance is a trust-based, relationship-driven sale, which makes Meta (Facebook and Instagram) a mixed channel. People rarely browse Facebook looking for a policy, so you are interrupting rather than answering a search. That means your offer, your follow-up speed and your compliance habits matter more than clever targeting. This guide covers how to set it up properly.

## Where Facebook fits for insurance agents

Think of channels by intent:

| Channel | Intent | Typical use for agents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Search ads | High: "car insurance quote near me" | Capture people already shopping |
| Facebook and Instagram ads | Low to medium: interrupt and educate | Build a local audience, generate leads, retarget |
| Organic content, referrals | Trust-based | Convert and retain |

Many agents run both: Google for ready-to-buy searches, and Meta for reach, remarketing and life-event offers. See our guide to [Google Ads for beginners](/blog/google-ads-for-beginners-first-campaign-step-by-step) if you want to pair them.

## Check compliance before you spend

Insurance is regulated, and ad rules vary by country, state and carrier.

- Follow your licensing body's rules on advertising and required disclosures.
- Check your carrier or agency (captive agents especially) for approved wording and creative.
- Avoid guarantees, savings claims you cannot document ("save $500") and misleading comparisons.
- Meta's policies restrict certain claims and personal-attribute targeting. Insurance is not currently one of Meta's "special ad categories" in the way housing, employment and credit are, but policies change, so check Meta's current advertising policies before launching.
- Include your agency name and licence information where required.

When in doubt, ask your compliance contact. A rejected ad is cheaper than a regulatory problem.

## Choose a goal and an offer

The most common setup is a lead generation campaign using Meta Instant Forms, which let people submit their details without leaving Facebook. Alternatives are sending people to a landing page or driving calls and messages.

Offers that tend to work better than "Get a free quote" include:

- A coverage review: "Do you know what your home policy actually covers?"
- A renewal check, for people whose policy is up for renewal
- Life-event content: new home, new baby, new business, retirement
- Business owner topics: general liability, professional liability or commercial auto basics
- A short guide or checklist, such as "5 questions to ask before renewing your car insurance"

Match one offer to one audience and one product line. Broad "we sell all insurance" ads usually perform worse than focused ones.

## Target people sensibly

Start with the basics:

- **Location**: Your licensed area only, using a radius around your office or a list of towns or postcodes.
- **Age and interests**: Use age ranges that fit the product (for example, homeowners and parents for home and life, small business owners for commercial lines). Meta's detailed targeting options have been reduced over time, so lean on creative and offer to reach the right people. See our [Facebook Ads targeting guide](/blog/facebook-ads-targeting-guide).
- **Custom audiences**: Upload your existing customer or lead list (with the right consent) to retarget them or to create lookalike audiences. Retargeting website visitors and people who engaged with your page is often your cheapest source of leads.

## Write ads that sound like a person

Insurance ads usually fail by sounding like every other insurance ad. Try this structure:

1. **Hook** on a real situation: "Renewing your car insurance next month?"
2. **One piece of value**: a tip, a question or a simple comparison idea.
3. **Clear action**: "Request a free review" with what happens next.

Use your own photo and name when possible, since people buy from people. Avoid stock images of smiling families. Short video, even a 30 second phone clip answering one question, can build familiarity. Keep text plain and avoid jargon.

## Build a lead form that filters

An Instant Form with too few fields produces a lot of low-quality leads. Add one or two fields that raise intent:

- Use the "higher intent" form type option, which adds a review step before submitting.
- Ask a qualifying question, such as renewal date or type of coverage needed.
- Add a short custom disclaimer about how you will contact them.
- Include a clear privacy policy link and consent language that fits your region's rules.

You will get fewer leads, but a higher share will pick up the phone.

## Speed of follow-up matters

Leads from Facebook go cold quickly, because the person was not actively shopping. As a rule:

- Call or text new leads within minutes during business hours.
- Use an automatic confirmation message or email right away.
- Try several times across a few days before giving up.
- Log outcomes in a simple CRM or spreadsheet so you can calculate cost per appointment and per policy.

Tools such as HubSpot (which has a free tier), Zoho CRM or your agency management system can handle this, and Zapier or Make can send new leads to your CRM and your phone.

## Budget and expectations

Costs vary widely by region, product and competition. In our experience, many local agents start with something like $10 to $30 per day for a few weeks, which is enough to gather data but not enough to judge long-term results. Lead costs for insurance are typically higher than for restaurants or salons, and a good-looking cost per lead means nothing if few leads become clients.

Track the full funnel:

- Cost per lead
- Percentage of leads reached
- Percentage that become appointments
- Percentage that become policies
- Average lifetime value of a client

Our guide to [Facebook ads budgets for local business](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) covers how to set a starting spend.

## Test one thing at a time

Run two or three ad variations with different hooks, and change one element at a time (headline, image, offer). Give each enough time to gather results, typically a week or more, before judging. Turn off clear losers, scale up what brings appointments, and refresh creative every month or two, as ads wear out.

## Common mistakes

- Targeting too broadly and wasting spend outside your licensed area
- Running ads without a fast follow-up process
- Optimising for cheap leads rather than qualified ones
- Using vague offers
- Ignoring compliance wording
- Judging results after two days

For more on lead campaigns, see [Facebook ads for lead generation](/blog/facebook-ads-for-lead-generation).

## Retargeting and nurturing

Most people will not request a quote the first time they see you. Build a retargeting audience from website visitors, video viewers and people who opened but did not submit your lead form, and show them a different message such as a short explainer of how you work or a helpful checklist. Pair ads with a simple email or text sequence for leads who have not booked, offering useful information at a steady pace instead of repeated pushes. Over a few weeks this familiarity is often what turns a cold lead into a conversation.

## FAQ

**Q: Do Facebook ads work for insurance agents?**
A: They can, particularly when paired with a clear local offer and quick follow-up. Results vary by product, market and how well you handle leads.

**Q: How much should an insurance agent spend on Facebook ads?**
A: Many start with a modest daily budget for several weeks to collect data. The right number depends on your average commission and how many leads you can handle. Work back from the value of a policy.

**Q: Are there special restrictions for insurance ads?**
A: Industry regulations and carrier rules apply, along with Meta's general ad policies. Check your licensing body, your carrier and Meta's current policies before launching.

**Q: Should I use lead forms or a landing page?**
A: Lead forms are quick to start and convenient for mobile users. Landing pages let you explain more and often produce better-qualified leads. Many agents test both.
