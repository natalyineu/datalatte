At a $55 cost per lead, 20% of leads booking a call and 30% of calls becoming policies, $1,500 of Meta ads writes about 1.6 policies. That is $917 per policy. Whether that is a good deal depends entirely on what a policy is worth to you over its life, which is why this guide starts with the rules, then moves to the math. Those three rates are assumptions, not Meta's numbers, and you will replace them with your own.

This is for independent and captive agents in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. One correction first: an earlier version of this article said insurance is not a Meta special ad category. That is wrong for advertisers in the US and Canada, as the next section explains.

## What Meta's Policies Say About Insurance Ads

This section draws on Meta's own policy pages. Where a detail came only from secondary summaries, it is marked.

**Insurance is a restricted category.** Meta's [Financial and Insurance Products and Services policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/financial-services/) covers credit cards, loans and insurance services. It says ads promoting them "must be targeted to people 18 years or older", that advertisers may need to verify their identity and show regulatory authorisation, and that advertisers "may be required to be licensed in the country they are targeting". Insurance is listed among products that may require a licence depending on the targeted country. The policy also lets some things through without authorisation, including brand ads for insurance companies and education content that mentions a product without a way to obtain or connect with it. A lead ad offering quotes is not that.

**You must self-identify for the special ad category.** Meta's [Discriminatory Practices policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/unacceptable-content/discriminatory-practices/) says any US advertiser, or advertiser targeting the US, Canada or certain parts of Europe, running financial products and services, housing or employment ads must self-identify as a Special Ad Category. UK and Australian agents who target only their home markets are not named in that text, but check Ads Manager's own prompts and your local regulator.

**Targeting is narrower in that category.** Meta's Business Help Center has a page on audiences for these campaigns. The following is second-hand, from search summaries of that page: ages 18 to 65 or older with no narrowing, all genders, no postal-code targeting, a minimum 15-mile (25 km) radius around a chosen city in the US and Canada, and limited or unavailable lookalikes, saved audiences and exclusions. Treat this as likely, not confirmed. Open Ads Manager and see which options are greyed out for your account before you plan around any of it.

**Lead forms have a hard limit.** Under Meta's lead ads rules in the [Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/), ads may not request insurance information without Meta's prior permission, and lead form fields asking for a person's insurance company name or details are not allowed. Health information requests are restricted the same way. So "Who is your current insurer?" and "Are you currently insured?" are off the table as form questions. Ask those on the phone.

**Health insurance.** Meta's [Health and Wellness policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/) does not mention insurance in its published text; it covers dietary, weight loss and cosmetic products and sexual and reproductive health. Do not read that as permission to imply a medical condition in an ad. Meta's [Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/) also restrict ads that assert or imply someone's health or financial status, so write to the situation ("renewing in March?") and not to the person ("struggling with medical bills?").

Beyond Meta, your licensing body and carrier set wording, disclosure and licence-number rules. Those vary by state, province and country; ask your compliance contact before you launch.

## Set Up the Campaign

1. **Declare the special ad category** when you create the campaign, before choosing anything else.
2. **Objective:** Leads, with an instant form, a landing page or calls. Our [lead ads setup guide](/blog/facebook-lead-ads-local-business-setup-guide) walks through the form builder.
3. **Location:** Your licensed area only. Expect a larger radius than you want if you target a city in the US or Canada; that is why you should state your service area in the ad copy.
4. **Audience:** Stay broad, since detailed targeting is limited. Let creative do the filtering. Our [targeting guide](/blog/facebook-ads-targeting-guide) covers what is still available.
5. **Tracking:** Install the Meta pixel and track form submissions and calls. See the [Facebook pixel setup guide](/blog/facebook-pixel-setup-guide).

## Offers That Fit a Restricted Category

You cannot target by life stage or finances, so write the situation into the ad. Examples:

- "Home insurance renewing this year? Get a coverage review from a local agent."
- "New business owner? 10-minute liability checklist."
- "Moved house? Here is what to check on your policy."

One offer, one product line, one audience. "We sell all insurance" ads are weak. Put your name, photo and licence number (where required) in the ad. People buy this from a person.

## Build a Lead Form That Qualifies Without Breaking Rules

- Ask for name, phone, email and ZIP or postcode only if the form permits it.
- Add a qualifying question that is not about their current policy, such as "Which coverage are you looking at?" with options (home, auto, business, life).
- Use the higher-intent form type, which adds a review step before submitting.
- Add your own consent line and privacy link.
- Do not ask for insurer name, policy numbers or health details in the form.

## The Math

Every figure here is an **assumption** except the $28 strong-case cost per lead, which echoes the WordStream cross-industry figure above. Replace each with your own after four weeks.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,500 | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Cost per lead (assumption) | $90 | $55 | $28 |
| Leads | 17 | 27 | 54 |
| Lead to booked call (assumption) | 10% | 20% | 25% |
| Booked calls | 1.7 | 5.5 | 13.4 |
| Call to policy (assumption) | 25% | 30% | 35% |
| Policies written | 0.4 | 1.6 | 4.7 |
| Ad cost per policy | $3,600 | $917 | $320 |
| Lifetime commission per policy (assumption) | $1,500 | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Left after ad cost | -$2,100 | $583 | $1,180 |

The $1,500 lifetime figure is itself an assumption: $300 a year in commission over a five-year average client life. Your product mix will differ hugely. Auto is not life is not commercial.

Two takeaways:

- **Your break-even cost per lead** equals lifetime value per policy times your lead-to-policy rate. In the Base column that is $1,500 x 6% = $90. Above that, you lose money on average.
- **The lever you control is follow-up.** Moving lead-to-call from 10% to 20% halves your cost per policy with no change to the ad account.

Note too that renewals and cross-sells improve the real payback, while carrier chargebacks and lapsed policies reduce it. Use your agency data.

## Do This Today

1. Work out lifetime commission per policy for one product line, using your own retention figure.
2. Compute your break-even cost per lead with the formula above.
3. Check your licence, carrier and regulator rules on advertising, and get your ad wording approved.
4. Create one lead campaign with the special ad category declared, one offer and a form that avoids insurer and health questions.

## FAQ

**Q: Is insurance a special ad category on Meta?**
A: For advertisers in the US or targeting the US, Canada or certain parts of Europe, Meta's Discriminatory Practices policy says financial products and services ads must self-identify as a Special Ad Category. Insurance falls under Meta's Financial and Insurance Products and Services policy. Confirm what applies to your country in Ads Manager.

**Q: Can I ask for someone's current insurer in a lead form?**
A: No. Meta's lead ads rules do not allow form fields requesting insurance company name or details, or insurance information in ads, without prior permission. Ask on the call.

**Q: Can I target by age or ZIP code?**
A: Meta requires insurance ads to target people 18 or older. Beyond that, narrower targeting limits were described in secondary summaries of Meta's help page (see above), so check what Ads Manager lets you select.

**Q: How much should I spend?**
A: Work backwards from your break-even cost per lead and how many leads you can call within an hour. Our [Facebook ads budget guide](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) shows the method.

**Q: Lead form or landing page?**
A: Test both. Forms are faster on mobile; landing pages let you explain and qualify. Our [lead generation guide](/blog/facebook-ads-for-lead-generation) compares them.
