Coffee shop email segmentation means dividing your contact list into groups, such as loyal regulars, lapsed customers and new subscribers, so each group receives a message matched to how often it visits. [Square's Future of Restaurants 2025 report](https://squareup.com/ca/en/the-bottom-line/reaching-customers/marketing-plan-for-a-coffee-shop) found that 82% of restaurant leaders say their loyalty program drives repeat visits, and loyalty sign-up data is one of the easiest ways to build those groups. Start with three or four segments, send each one a different offer, and track results per group rather than across the whole list.

## Which segments should a coffee shop start with?

Start with segments you can build from data you already hold. Loyalty sign-ups are the richest source. [Square Loyalty](https://squareup.com/us/en/software/loyalty) integrates directly with Square Marketing, so merchants can send email and text campaigns to loyalty customers. Square's cafe marketing guide also notes that customers only need a phone number to sign up and earn or redeem rewards, regardless of how they pay. That keeps the sign-up step simple, and a segment is only as large as the opt-in list behind it.

Four starter groups cover most coffee shop situations. The rules below are assumptions to adjust against your own POS data.

| Segment | Starter rule (assumption) | Email goal |
|---|---|---|
| New subscribers | Joined in the last 30 days | Turn a first visit into a second |
| Loyal regulars | Visited in each of the last 4 weeks | Reward status and reinforce it |
| Lapsed customers | No visit for 45 or more days | Give a clear reason to return |
| Non-members | Paid at the counter but never joined loyalty | Invite them to join |

UpSwell Marketing's segmentation tools use a similar four-way split of new customers, win-backs, loyal customers and at-risk customers, which is a useful check on the table above ([UpSwell Marketing, fitness-industry agency page](https://upswellmarketing.com/industry/fitness), secondary). Keep the groups broad enough that each one can be reached with a single offer.

## When should you send a win-back email?

Timing is the main trigger for a lapsed segment. [Propello advises](https://propellocloud.com/blog/win-back-campaigns/) starting win-back campaigns 30 to 90 days after last customer activity, typically around day 45 for email. Use day 45 as the starting trigger for your lapsed customers, then test earlier or later windows against your own visit data.

Expectations should stay realistic. Automated email and SMS campaigns typically recover 2–5% of lapsed customers, according to the [Winback Engine guide](https://winbackengine.com/blog/customer-reactivation-guide/) (secondary). Plan for that range rather than a surge. Email and SMS can both reach lapsed customers, and Square Loyalty supports both channels, so segments can use either. Before assigning channels, compare them in [SMS vs email marketing for local business](/blog/sms-vs-email-marketing-local-business-which-is-better).

Customers who have been dormant for a long time need less effort. [Cleverly's tiering model](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/customer-reactivation-campaign-strategies) (secondary) assigns low-lifetime-value customers dormant 12 months or more to light-touch email only or suppression. Removing them from frequent sends protects your overall list health.

## What open rates should you expect?

[Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/customer-reactivation-campaign-strategies) (secondary) gives 12–15% as a healthy open rate for reactivation campaigns, and says an open rate below 8% signals a subject line or list quality problem. The same page cites a third-party benchmark of 21.4% for 2025 reactivation campaigns, up from 17% in 2023. Treat that as a market reference, not a target for a single coffee shop.

If your lapsed segment opens below 8%, fix the subject line and list quality before changing the offer. Cleverly also recommends A/B testing subject lines with at least 200 contacts per variant before scaling a reactivation email. Smaller lists can still test, but results will be less reliable, so run tests across several sends rather than one.

## What do segmentation tools cost?

Check your POS before buying another tool. ## What does a win-back send return?

This example uses assumed inputs, not measured results. Assume a list of 1,200 contacts, of which 300 have not visited in 45 days (assumption). Assume a 3% recovery rate, inside the 2–5% range above, which returns 9 customers (assumption). Assume each returning customer makes 3 visits over the next quarter at an $8.50 average ticket (assumption). Revenue is 9 × 3 × $8.50 = $229.50.

Assume the POS email add-on costs $25 per month, the low end of the range above, so three months cost $75 (assumption). Net revenue before cost of goods is $229.50 − $75 = $154.50. Gross margin will be lower, so rerun the calculation with your real ticket size, visit frequency and margin before deciding on a tool.

## How do you test and track segmented emails?

Give each segment its own unique code. One code for lapsed customers and another for loyal regulars shows which group acted, and redemptions at the till tie back to the send. Track open rate, click-through rate and redemptions for each segment every week, and compare them against the benchmarks above rather than against the whole list.

## FAQ

### How many segments should a coffee shop start with?

Start with three or four segments, such as new subscribers, loyal regulars, lapsed customers and non-members. Add more only after each group has enough contacts to show clear results.

### What is a good open rate for a reactivation email?

Cleverly gives 12–15% as healthy for reactivation campaigns and says below 8% signals a subject line or list quality problem (secondary).

### When should I send a win-back email?

Propello advises starting win-back campaigns 30 to 90 days after last customer activity, typically around day 45 for email. Test the timing against your own visit data.

### Do I need a separate email tool or can my POS handle it?

Check your POS first. The Restaurant HQ reports that most coffee shop POS systems with email tools charge $25 or more per month (secondary). If your POS email tool covers segments and automated sends, a separate platform may not be needed.

Start with one lapsed-customer send this month, using a 45-day trigger and a unique code, and compare it with your loyal-regulars send. If you want a second opinion on how your list is structured, [request a free audit](/free-audit).
