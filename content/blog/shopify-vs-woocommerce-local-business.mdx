Shopify rents a complete hosted shop for $39 a month on Basic, paid monthly, or $29 billed yearly, on Shopify pricing checked in November 2026. WooCommerce is a free WordPress plugin; the shop owner buys hosting, which WooCommerce itself estimates at $25 to $350 a month for most stores, and owns the maintenance.

For a local business the choice is rarely about features, which overlap heavily. It is about who patches, backs up and answers at 9pm when checkout breaks. Shopify sells the removal of that job. WooCommerce sells control and no platform fee.

## What does each platform really cost in year one?

Shopify's bill is predictable: plan plus apps plus transaction terms. Using Shopify Payments keeps card rates at the published tier; outside gateways add 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow and 0.6% on Advanced, on Shopify pricing checked in November 2026. Three paid apps at common prices can add $30 to $100 a month.

WooCommerce's bill is scattered: hosting, a theme, extension licences often $29 to $299 a year each, and maintenance time. The software being free does not make the shop free; it makes the costs optional-looking until an update breaks a payment extension during a sale.

**Assumption example.** Assume a small shop sells $4,000 online a month. Shopify Basic at $39 plus $60 of apps costs $99 before card fees. A WooCommerce shop on $50 managed hosting plus $40 of licences spread monthly costs $90, before valuing 2 maintenance hours at an assumed $40, which takes it to $170. The inputs are assumptions; count real hours honestly.

## Which suits a local shop that also sells in person?

Shopify suits owners who want tills, online stock and gift cards unified without technical help, and who accept template limits. Its support answers at night, which for a one-person business is a feature worth more than most extensions.

WooCommerce suits businesses already on WordPress with content doing real work (guides, local pages, booking content) and someone able to maintain it. It also suits unusual needs: complex delivery zones, trade accounts or custom pricing that hosted tiers resist.

Hybrid reality is common. A café selling beans and merchandise online while the café itself runs on walk-ins needs only the simple end of either platform. The deciding cost is the owner's hours, not the plan table. The [Website service](/services/website) page shows how that build is usually packaged.

## What breaks most often on each platform?

On Shopify: app conflicts at the margins, theme edits that complicate updates, and monthly app creep quietly doubling the platform cost. Audit apps quarterly and delete anything unused for a season.

On WooCommerce: updates. Plugin, theme and WordPress core updates interact, and skipping them creates security exposure while applying them carelessly can break checkout. Managed WooCommerce hosting and a staging copy turn this from a gamble into a routine, at a price that narrows the gap with Shopify.

Traffic and retention sit outside the platform either way: [Best marketing automation tools](/blog/best-marketing-automation-tools-small-business-compared) covers the email layer, [Best AI tools for small business marketing](/blog/ai-tools-for-small-business-marketing-2026) the content layer, and the [Google Display Network guide](/blog/google-display-network-guide) the paid reminder layer. Neither platform fixes weak product pages. Clear photos, delivery facts and a visible local pickup option move conversion more than the platform logo in the footer. Platform choice should follow the maintenance question, not precede it.

## FAQ

### Is WooCommerce really free?

The plugin is free; the shop is not. WooCommerce itself estimates hosting at $25 to $350 a month for most stores, and extensions commonly run $29 to $299 a year each, on figures checked in November 2026. Add maintenance time, valued honestly, before calling it the cheaper option.

### Is Shopify worth $39 a month for a small local shop?

If online sales are real and nobody on staff wants server maintenance, usually yes. The fee buys hosting, security, updates and 24/7 support in one bill. A shop selling a handful of gift cards a month may not recover it; a shop selling weekly recovers it in avoided downtime alone.

### Can a business switch later?

Yes, but migration means moving products, customers, redirects and reviews, and it costs days rather than minutes. Choose for the next 3 years of likely sales, not the next 3 months. Businesses expecting content-led growth often start on WooCommerce; businesses expecting product-led growth often start on Shopify.

### Which is better for local pickup and delivery?

Both handle pickup; Shopify's built-in local pickup and delivery settings need less assembly. WooCommerce reaches the same result with extensions, which adds licence cost and update surface. A business whose whole offer is neighbourhood delivery should test its exact postcode rules on both before migrating anything.
