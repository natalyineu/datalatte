Google Ads can work for a Portland hair salon when it targets high-intent searches such as "hair salon near me," limits clicks to a tight radius around the salon, sends people to a clear booking page and tracks calls and bookings. Google Ads keyword data captured in April 2026 estimated about 1.5 million U.S. searches a month for "hair salon near me," as cited by [RŌZ](https://rozhair.com/blogs/education/hair-salon-advertising-ideas). This guide covers keywords, a budget you can test properly, radius settings and tracking.

## Is Google Ads worth it for a Portland hair salon?

Google Ads suits salons that want clients who are searching now. Someone typing "hair salon near me" has already decided they need a service and is choosing between nearby options. [Trafft](https://trafft.com/salon-ads/) cites searches such as "spa near me" and "hair salon near me" as typical salon queries, which shows how much intent sits in local search terms.

Start with the free groundwork, though. [RŌZ](https://rozhair.com/blogs/education/hair-salon-advertising-ideas) advises that if your budget is close to $0, you should begin with your Google Business Profile, reviews, service descriptions and portfolio. Its comparison table lists the Google Business Profile and Google reviews as free. Paid search works best once the profile is ready to turn clicks into bookings. For profile basics, read the [hair salon local SEO guide](/blog/hair-salon-local-seo-guide).

## What does a realistic monthly budget look like?

No fixed price is published for this work. [Sunnystorm](https://sunnystorm.marketing/blog/google-adwords-salons) suggests a daily budget of $5 to $10 should be enough to get several clicks a day, and advises starting small and adjusting to the clicks you receive.

Use a worked example to test whether a budget pays back. The inputs below are assumptions, so replace them with your own figures:

| Input or result | Value |

| Average cost per click (assumption) | $4.00 |

On these assumptions, first-visit revenue covers the spend about 1.5 times, and repeat visits add to that. Against Booksy's 3% to 7% guidance, a salon with $20,000 monthly gross revenue (assumption) would have a total marketing budget of $600 to $1,400, so $1,000 in ads sits inside that range. ## How tight should your radius and neighborhood targeting be?

[CIIM](https://www.ciim.in/google-ads-for-hair-salons/) recommends a local targeting radius of about 5 to 10 km around the salon. Radius targeting keeps spend on people who can reasonably reach the chair, which is the main reason to start tight and widen only when the data supports it.

Neighborhood names can add relevance where they match your actual location. Test terms such as Pearl District, Mississippi Avenue or Division Street in keywords and ad copy only if clients from those areas really book with you. A generic "Portland" ad usually feels less specific than one that names the area someone lives in.

## Which negative keywords protect the budget?

Negative keywords stop spend on searches that rarely become bookings. Common exclusions for salons include:

- Job-related terms such as "jobs", "hiring", "career" and "salary"
- DIY and training terms such as "how to", "course", "training" and "certification"
- Cities outside your service radius
- Trade terms such as "franchise", "wholesale" and "supplier"

## What offers and ad copy should a Portland salon test?

Offers work best when they are specific and true for your salon. [CIIM](https://www.ciim.in/google-ads-for-hair-salons/) gives "First Haircut 20% Off" as an example salon offer. [Zeely](https://zeely.ai/blog/hair-salon-advertising-ideas/) suggests a new-client offer such as "$20 off first color service," promoted monthly on a Google Business Profile.

Authenticity also matters in ad copy and video. Zeely cites Portland stylist Jules Heron, who built an authentic brand around inclusivity and visibility, as an example of video marketing. Test specialty, values and convenience angles, such as balayage, locally owned, or evening appointments, and keep only the claims you can back up. For creator-led approaches, see [influencer marketing for hair salons](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-hair-salons-how-to-get-free-bookings-from-local-creator).

## How do you know the landing page is converting?

Sunnystorm states that if a salon gets 100 visitors and no bookings, there is probably a problem with the landing page. Check these points before increasing spend:

1. The page loads quickly on a phone
2. It shows the service the ad promised, with a price or starting price where possible
3. A booking button and phone number are visible above the fold
4. Real photos of your work and team appear on the page
5. The address, neighborhood and hours are listed

## What should you track from day one?

Track phone calls from ads, booking form completions, direction requests from your Google Business Profile and clicks to the booking page. Booking tracking should be set up before any budget decisions. Check the platform's live help page for the current steps to set up conversion tracking, because interface details change.

## FAQ

### How much should a Portland hair salon spend on Google Ads?

There is no published Portland benchmark. [Salon Guru](https://www.salonguru.net/google-adwords-for-salons/) gives bespoke quotations, and [Sunnystorm](https://sunnystorm.marketing/blog/google-adwords-salons) suggests a $5 to $10 daily budget as a starting point. Use the worked example above with your own cost per click and booking rate.

### Is Google Ads worth it for a hair salon?

It can be, when the value of a new client is higher than your cost per booking. Track bookings so you can calculate that figure rather than guessing.

### How long should a campaign run before you judge it?

Salon Guru's packages include monthly management and analysis for three months, so plan a multi-month test rather than a few weeks. Avoid changing everything at once.

### Should a salon use Google Ads or social ads?

They do different jobs. Google Ads captures people searching now. [Bookeo](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/) advises that salon social ads target women aged 21 to 55 who live nearby and show interest in hair care and beauty. Many salons start with search for intent and add social later.
