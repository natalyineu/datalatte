## What Are Google's Rules for Review Requests?

Google's help says you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code. Its policy page adds limits that shape any automation:

- Offering free or discounted goods or services for reviews is prohibited.
- Merchants may not discourage negative reviews or selectively solicit positive ones.
- Merchants may not ask for specific review content, including mentioning a staff member.
- Customers must be signed in to a Google Account to leave a review.

So build the flow to send to every eligible customer, with no filter on satisfaction and no reward. A "how was your visit?" screen that routes unhappy people away from the Google link is selective solicitation.

## How Do You Get the Review Link?

Use a computer browser; Google says review QR codes cannot be generated on mobile. Open your Business Profile, choose Read Reviews, then Get more reviews. Copy the link with the copy icon, or right-click the QR code and save the image.

Google suggests sharing the link on receipts, thank-you emails, at the end of chats and on printed signs. Test the link yourself before it goes into an automation.

## What Does the Automated Message Say?

Keep it short, honest and identical for everyone:

- Thank them by first name.
- One sentence asking for an honest review.
- The review link as a button or plain link.

Email is marketing-adjacent, so follow the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide: accurate sender information, a clear opt-out, and honouring opt-outs within 10 business days. The FTC says the law has no exception for business-to-business email. For text messages, rules differ by country; this article could not open an official US page on them, so check yours before sending SMS.

BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found 97% read local business reviews and 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. ## FAQ

### Is it allowed to automate review requests?

Google's help allows asking customers with a link or QR code. The restrictions are on incentives, selective asking and dictating content, not on automation.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?

No. Google calls it prohibited.

### Can I only ask happy customers?

No. Google's policy forbids selectively soliciting positive reviews.

### How long after the visit should I send it?

Google gives no timing. Within a day or two is common practice; test it.

### Do I need a paid tool?

Not always. A free two-step Zap or your booking tool's own follow-up may be enough.
