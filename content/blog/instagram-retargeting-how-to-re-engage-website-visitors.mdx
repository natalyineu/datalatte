Retargeting shows Instagram ads to people who already visited the website, viewed a price list or started a booking and left. Meta requires the pixel or Conversions API on the site, and an audience needs enough visitors to build before delivery is meaningful. For most local sites that means weeks of traffic, not days.

The economics differ from cold ads. The audience is tiny and warm, so click prices matter less than the match between the page visited and the ad shown. A visitor who read the balayage price list should see balayage, not a generic salon logo.

## How does Instagram retargeting get set up?

Install the Meta pixel on every page, or the Conversions API where a developer is available, then verify the booking or call event fires. Meta's Events Manager shows test events live; do not spend until a test booking appears there. Most failed retargeting is actually failed tracking.

Build audiences by intent, not one blended pool. Price-list and booking-page visitors from the last 30 days are buyers comparing options. Blog and homepage visitors from 180 days are browsers. The first group gets a booking offer; the second gets proof, such as reviews or before-and-after work.

Consent and privacy rules apply before any of this. The pixel should respect the site's cookie consent, and customer-list uploads need a lawful basis. [Snapchat ads for local business](/blog/snapchat-ads-local-business) faces the same consent discipline on a younger platform.

## What should retargeting ads offer?

Answer the objection that caused the exit. Price-page visitors often need a starting price, a first-visit add-on or a named next step. Booking abandoners need one tap back into the flow, deep-linked to the service they chose, not the homepage.

Rotate proof, not discounts. Reviews, real results and staff introductions resolve trust at lower cost than permanent sales. [Meta ads for hair salons](/blog/using-meta-ads-for-hair-salon-marketing) shows the creative pattern for one trade; [Facebook ads for pet groomers](/blog/pet-groomer-meta-ads) applies the same sequencing to lapsed lists.

**Assumption example.** Assume a site gets 1,500 visitors a month and 3% join a 30-day booking-intent audience, or 45 people monthly. At an assumed $1 cost per click and 10% booking conversion on return visits, a $60 monthly pool could produce about 4 bookings. Every input is an assumption; small audiences make results lumpy, so judge over a quarter.

## How long should retargeting run?

Run it continuously at low spend. Intent decays: a 30-day window carries most local value because services are booked near the research moment. Longer windows suit considered purchases such as extensions, memberships or events, where comparison stretches over weeks.

Cap frequency before raising budget. Inside a small audience, extra spend buys repetition, not reach, and repetition past a handful of views breeds irritation and hides. Add a second postcode ring or a new audience segment when frequency climbs, exactly as cold campaigns do.

## FAQ

### How many website visitors does retargeting need?

Meta audiences must reach a minimum size before ads deliver, and practically a local business wants hundreds of visitors in the chosen window for steady delivery. A site with 50 monthly visitors should fix traffic first. Retargeting multiplies existing visits; it cannot manufacture them.

### Why is the retargeting audience not delivering?

Usually three causes: the pixel is not firing the event the audience is built on, the window contains too few people, or consent settings block the tag for most visitors. Test a booking in Events Manager first. If the event fires and the audience still starves, widen the window from 30 to 90 days.

### Retargeting or cold ads for a new business?

Cold first, briefly. A business with almost no website traffic has nobody to retarget, so search and cold social build the visitor pool. Switch on retargeting as soon as price or booking pages see steady weekly visits; from then on it usually produces the cheapest bookings in the account.

### Does retargeting need a discount?

No. It needs relevance: the service viewed, a reason to return, and one tap back to booking. Proof-led ads (reviews, real results, named staff) convert warm visitors without training price sensitivity. Save offers for booking abandoners and long windows, where intent has genuinely cooled.
