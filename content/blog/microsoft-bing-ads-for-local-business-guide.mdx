Microsoft Advertising (formerly Bing Ads) can be worth testing for a local business, because it often has less competition and lower cost per click than Google Ads, and it reaches a smaller but distinct audience across Bing, Yahoo, DuckDuckGo results and Microsoft products. Start with a small budget, import your best Google Ads campaigns, and judge it by cost per lead, not by volume.

Bing will not replace Google for most local businesses, since Google has far more search volume. But as a second channel it is easy to launch, and for some businesses it delivers leads at a lower price.

## What Microsoft Advertising Is

Microsoft Advertising runs search ads on Bing and partner networks, including Yahoo and DuckDuckGo (which uses Bing's ad network). Your ads can also appear on Microsoft-owned properties such as MSN, Outlook, and the Edge browser, depending on campaign type.

The platform feels very similar to Google Ads: keywords, match types, ad copy, bids, budgets and location targeting. That is helpful, because you can reuse what you already know.

## Is It Worth It for a Local Business?

It depends on your market and customers. Points to consider:

**Potential advantages**

- **Typically lower competition.** Fewer advertisers often means lower cost per click on many keywords, though this varies by industry and location.
- **A different audience.** Bing users tend to skew older and are more likely to use desktop computers, particularly at work, though this changes over time. Check your own data rather than assuming.
- **Easy setup via import.** You can import campaigns from Google Ads in a few clicks.
- **Extra targeting options,** such as LinkedIn-based audience targeting for some campaign types.

**Possible drawbacks**

- **Lower volume.** Bing has a much smaller share of searches than Google in most countries, so it will not generate the same number of clicks.
- **Fewer features and less documentation** than Google Ads for some local formats.
- **Management time.** A second platform means a second set of reports to watch.

Bottom line: it often makes sense once your Google Ads campaigns are performing and you want to extend reach cheaply.

## Which Local Businesses Tend to Do Well

Bing is not a fit for every business. In our experience, it is more promising for:

- **Home and trade services** (plumbers, electricians, fence companies, movers), where customers search for a specific job.
- **Professional services** (accountants, lawyers, financial advisors) with an older or desktop-leaning audience.
- **Medical, dental and wellness practices** where people search for a named service.
- **Businesses in markets where Bing share is stronger,** such as users on Windows PCs and Microsoft Edge in the US and UK.

Businesses that rely heavily on mobile searches and quick impulse visits, such as a neighborhood coffee shop, usually get more from Google Maps and Google Business Profile first. Our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business) can help you decide where to spend first.

## Step 1: Create Your Account

1. Go to ads.microsoft.com and sign in with a Microsoft account.
2. Enter your business details, time zone and currency.
3. Add payment details. Microsoft sometimes offers promotional credits to new advertisers, though terms change, so check the current offer.
4. Install the **Universal Event Tracking (UET) tag** on your website. This is the tracking tag for conversions, similar to the Google tag.

## Step 2: Import From Google Ads (Optional but Fast)

If you already run Google Ads:

1. In Microsoft Advertising, choose Import campaigns and connect your Google Ads account.
2. Select the campaigns to import.
3. Review everything after import: budgets, keywords, bids, location targeting and ad copy.
4. Set up a recurring import only if you want Google changes mirrored automatically.

Always review the imported campaigns. Some settings, like location options or conversion goals, may not transfer exactly. Also check the Bing keyword bids, since typical costs differ.

## Step 3: Set Up Local Targeting

Local targeting is where you save money.

- **Locations:** choose your service area by radius or city. Use "people in your targeted location" where possible.
- **Exclusions:** exclude areas you do not serve.
- **Ad schedule:** match hours when you can answer calls.
- **Device bids:** adjust for desktop versus mobile if your data shows a difference.
- **Location extensions (location assets):** show your address and distance.

## Step 4: Build Focused Campaigns

Start small. A tidy structure works best.

- One campaign per main service or group of services.
- Tight ad groups with closely related keywords.
- Phrase and exact match to begin, and negative keywords from day one.
- At least two ad variants per ad group, with your location and offer in the copy.
- Assets (extensions): call, location, sitelinks and callouts. Our [Google Ads extensions guide](/blog/google-ads-extensions-guide) applies closely.

## Step 5: Track Conversions

You need to know which clicks produce enquiries.

- Use the UET tag to track form submissions and key page views.
- Enable call tracking if phone enquiries are common. Microsoft offers call tracking options, and third-party services such as CallRail also work.
- Track bookings in your system and compare them against ad data.
- Pair this with GA4 and our [conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) for the principles.

## Step 6: Choose a Budget

Costs vary by industry and location. A sensible approach:

- **Start with roughly 10 to 25 percent of your Google Ads budget,** or a modest daily amount such as $5 to $15 per campaign.
- Run for several weeks to gather data.
- Compare **cost per lead** and **lead quality** with Google.
- Scale up what works, and pause what does not.

Low volume means results can take longer to become reliable, so avoid changing settings every few days.

## Step 7: Optimize Regularly

Weekly:

- Review search terms and add negative keywords.
- Check which ad variants perform best.
- Look at device and location performance.
- Pause keywords that spend without leads.

Monthly:

- Compare Bing and Google cost per lead.
- Test new ad copy and offers.
- Adjust bids and budgets based on lead quality.

## Common Mistakes

- Importing campaigns and never reviewing them.
- Skipping the UET tag, then having no conversion data.
- Expecting Google-level volume.
- Using broad match without negative keywords.
- Judging results after only a few days.

## How It Fits With Your Other Marketing

Treat Microsoft Advertising as an extension of your search strategy, not a separate project. Use your best-performing Google keywords and ad copy, keep your landing pages consistent, and make sure your Bing Places for Business listing is claimed and accurate, since it feeds Bing's local results. ## FAQ

**Q: Is Bing Ads cheaper than Google Ads?**
A: Often, cost per click is lower on Microsoft Advertising because of less competition, but it varies by industry and location. Compare your own cost per lead rather than relying on averages.

**Q: Can I import my Google Ads campaigns into Microsoft Advertising?**
A: Yes. The import tool brings in campaigns, keywords and ads. Review budgets, bids, locations and conversion settings after importing.

**Q: Do I need a separate tracking tag?**
A: Yes. Install the Universal Event Tracking (UET) tag to record conversions on Microsoft Advertising.

**Q: Should a small business run both Google and Bing?**
A: Often yes, once Google Ads is working. Start Bing with a smaller budget as a test, and keep it only if the cost per lead is acceptable.
