Microsoft Advertising is worth a small test for most local businesses that already run Google Ads, provided the result is judged on cost per lead rather than cost per click. Microsoft's [homepage](https://ads.microsoft.com/) claims a 19% lower cost per click and a reach of more than 1 billion people. Those are vendor figures and say nothing about a specific town. A $300 monthly test, run for eight weeks, is enough to show whether Bing sends leads at an acceptable price. This guide covers who should test it, how to set it up, how to target the local area, a worked budget example with assumption-labelled inputs, and a written decision rule.

## Is a $300 Microsoft Advertising Test Worth Your Time?

For most businesses that already make money from Google, yes. The cost of learning is low, and a second search channel can capture leads that Google misses. Microsoft Advertising places search ads on Bing and on Microsoft properties, which the homepage lists as Bing, Edge, LinkedIn, Outlook, Copilot, MSN, Windows and Xbox.

The offer is worth checking before signing up. Microsoft says a $500 ad credit applies once a new account spends $250, but only for new customers in the United States who receive the offer email. The credit expires if unused within 90 days of award and cannot be refunded. Businesses in the UK, Australia and Canada should assume the offer does not apply and plan on standard terms. ## What Do Microsoft's Own Figures Mean for Your Town?

Microsoft's homepage says its audiences are 34% more likely to be top household earners, that 48% made a purchase in the last week, and that they engage with ads 18% more than on competing platforms. These figures come from GWI data that Microsoft cites. They describe Microsoft's audience in general, not people searching for a plumber or a dentist in your area.

The 19% lower CPC claim deserves the same caution. It is attributed to an EMARKETER forecast and compares Microsoft with an unnamed leading platform. It is a reason to test, not evidence that local clicks will be cheaper. Auction prices depend on keywords, competitors and geography.

The only reliable evidence is internal data. Pull Google Ads cost per lead for the last 90 days, then compare it with Bing after eight weeks. Microsoft's figures indicate where to look, not what the test will find.

## Who Should Test Microsoft Advertising First?

A good fit is a business where customers search for a specific job or service on a desktop or laptop. Trades such as plumbing, fencing and roofing fit this pattern, as do professional and medical services where people research at work or from home. Microsoft's audience is weighted toward these settings, so such businesses are more likely to see useful volume.

A weaker fit is a business where most customers find it on a phone while walking past, such as a neighbourhood café. Google Maps and the Business Profile often drive those visits, and a small Bing budget rarely changes the numbers. The [Bing Ads and Microsoft Advertising overview for local business](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business) explains the channel in more depth.

Wait until Google is profitable before adding a second channel. Testing is hard when the main account is losing money, because the results cannot be separated from the underlying problem.

## How Do You Set Up the Account Without Wasting Budget?

Create the account at ads.microsoft.com and check the currency and time zone before saving anything. Changing them later can cause reporting confusion.

Set up conversion tracking before spending money. Decide what counts as a lead: a form submission, a booked appointment or a click-to-call from a tracked number. If leads cannot be counted, clicks cannot be judged.

Import only the best-performing Google campaign rather than the whole account. Set the budget manually during import so it does not copy the Google spend. Hold off on recurring syncs at first, because unplanned changes flowing from Google can waste money quietly.

Review the keyword list before going live. Remove broad terms that only worked on Google because of its automated bidding, and copy negative keywords across so irrelevant searches are not paid for twice. For drafting ad variations, a [ChatGPT prompts guide for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) can speed up the copywriting, though every claim should still be checked against the actual service.

## How Should You Target Your Local Area?

Local targeting is where small budgets are most often wasted. Set the service area from real customer data, such as the postcodes or zip codes of the last 50 customers, rather than a guessed radius around the office. Add exclusions for places that are not served.

Choose the location option that limits ads to people physically in the target area, rather than people who merely search for or read about the town. Option labels can change, so confirm them in the account. Location targeting is approximate on every platform.

Match the ad schedule to the hours when someone can answer the phone or the form. Clicks that arrive when nobody can respond are expensive and rarely become customers.

## What Does a $300 Test Look Like in Numbers?

The table below uses a $1,000 Google baseline and a $300 Bing test. Every input is an assumption for illustration. No verified local benchmark exists for Bing, so replace these with actual figures before deciding anything.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $1,000 | $300 | $300 | $300 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $5.00 | $4.50 | $3.50 | $3.25 |
| Clicks | 200 | 66.7 | 85.7 | 92.3 |
| Click-to-lead rate (assumption) | 8% | 5% | 6% | 8% |
| Leads | 16.0 | 3.3 | 5.1 | 7.4 |
| Cost per lead | $62.50 | $90 | $58 | $41 |

Read the table by cost per lead, not cost per click. In the weak column, Bing clicks are 10% cheaper than Google, yet each lead costs about 44% more. The base column shows a lower click price turning into a lower cost per lead only if the lead rate holds.

The sample is small. At 3 to 7 leads a month, a single week can move cost per lead sharply, so early results should not drive the decision. Track which leads become paying customers as well. If a Bing lead is worth twice as much as a Google lead, the answer changes entirely.

## How Do You Decide at Eight Weeks?

Write the decision rule before the test starts, so the goalposts do not move later. One workable example: keep Bing if its cost per lead is within 20% of Google's after at least 15 leads, grow the budget by 20% if it beats Google, and stop if it is worse after 15 leads. The 20% threshold is a suggested rule for this example, not a platform standard.

Each week, read the search terms report, add negative keywords for irrelevant queries, and pause any keyword that has spent about twice the target cost per lead with no leads. Each month, compare cost per lead and cost per customer side by side. Avoid changing bids every few days, because low volume needs time to settle.

If the test passes, grow the budget slowly and keep the same tracking. If it fails, stop cleanly and reallocate the $300 to whichever channel produced the best Google leads. A failed test that shows clearly what does not work still has value.

## FAQ

### Is Bing Ads cheaper than Google Ads?

Sometimes, but no verified local figure exists. Microsoft's homepage claims 19% lower CPCs, attributed to a forecast, which is a vendor claim. Judge the channel on cost per lead after eight weeks in the local market.

### Do I need separate conversion tracking for Microsoft?

Yes. Set up tracking before spending and decide what counts as a lead for the business. Without it, there is no way to tell whether the budget is working.

### Can I import Google Ads campaigns into Microsoft?

Microsoft offers an import route, but imported settings should be checked before going live. Review keywords, bids, targeting and ads, and avoid recurring syncs until the test has proven itself.

### Does the $500 offer apply outside the United States?

According to the offer terms, no. It is limited to new customers in the United States who receive the offer email, so businesses in the UK, Australia and Canada should plan on standard pricing.
