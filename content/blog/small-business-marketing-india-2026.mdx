## Why does Google Maps decide so many local leads?

Google Maps shapes the decision before a customer ever reaches your website. Local Falcon states that over 1 billion people use Google Maps every month, which makes your Google Business Profile the storefront most searchers see first ([Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/blog/effective-local-marketing-strategies-for-smbs)). For an Indian service business, that means the profile needs a working phone number, a WhatsApp number written into the description, and services listed the way customers search for them.

Check each field against the question a customer would ask. Use the service names people type, not your internal job titles. Replace stock photos with pictures of your premises and finished work. Reply to reviews in the language your customers write in. Google's profile guidelines control categories and contact details, so check the live help pages before changing anything.

## Are Local Services Ads worth testing?

Local Services Ads use a pay-per-lead model. Local Falcon says advertisers pay only for actual leads such as calls, messages, or bookings, and that these ads appear above all search results ([Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/blog/effective-local-marketing-strategies-for-smbs)). That model suits service firms where a lead is easy to define, such as a salon booking or a repair call.

Eligibility depends on category and location, so confirm on Google's Local Services help pages whether your trade qualifies in India. For keyword-based campaigns, the setup steps in [our Germany Google Ads guide](/blog/local-marketing-germany-small-business-google-ads) apply to the account structure, though the market details differ. Track cost per lead from day one so you can compare LSAs with standard search ads on your own numbers.

## How much do no-shows cost a salon or clinic?

Curogram publishes an illustrative calculation, not a reported practice figure. An average visit of $200 with five lost appointments a week comes to $1,000 a week, or roughly $52,000 a year ([Curogram](https://curogram.com/blog/how-to-reduce-no-show-appointments)). Its guide walks through more than 25 tactics to test, so the number is a prompt to measure your own losses, not a benchmark.

Replace those dollar figures with your own records. The worked example below uses assumption inputs for an appointment business in India:

- Assumption: average booking value of ₹800
- Assumption: 5 no-shows per week
- Assumption: reminders and confirmations recover 40% of those slots
- Assumption: reminder tool costs ₹2,000 per month

Weekly lost revenue is 5 × ₹800 = ₹4,000. At 40% recovery, that is ₹1,600 per week. Over 52 weeks, recovery is ₹1,600 × 52 = ₹83,200 per year. Subtracting the tool cost of ₹2,000 × 12 = ₹24,000 leaves a net of about ₹59,200 per year. If your real no-show rate or booking value is different, the same steps still give you a defensible number.

## Should you win back lapsed customers with offers?

Booksy describes an automatic message feature that contacts customers who have not booked a visit in some time ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-clients-vanished-how-to-bring-them-back-v)). The same article advises stopping messages to any client who says they are not interested in welcome-back offers. That rule protects your sender reputation and keeps the list usable.

Booksy also suggests two offer structures. A lower price can be offered only if the client brings a referral, and the referred person gets the same discount. A discounted package can require clients to pay for a few sessions in advance. The article gives no specific session count or discount percentage, so set those numbers from your own margins. For a broader referral framework, impact.com outlines six strategies for building a referral program ([impact.com](https://impact.com/referral/benefits-of-referral-marketing/)).

## Where do WhatsApp and regional languages fit?

WhatsApp works best as the place a customer lands after they have decided to contact you. Put a WhatsApp link wherever your site or profile has a contact button, and keep the message templates short. [Our Brazil WhatsApp guide](/blog/local-marketing-brazil-small-business-whatsapp-meta) covers how WhatsApp-first setups are structured, which is useful when you build your own message flow.

Language is a targeting decision, not a style choice. [Our English-market guide for India](/blog/local-marketing-india-small-business-english-market) explains where English-only copy works and where it does not. Run ad groups in English and in your customers' main regional language, then compare cost per lead in your own account. Do not assume regional copy is cheaper until your data shows it.

## What should you do in the first 30 days?

1. In week one, add a WhatsApp link to your website and Google Business Profile, and complete the services and products sections.
2. In week two, launch a Google Ads campaign with separate English and regional-language ad groups for your city or neighbourhood, and set a cost-per-lead target.
3. In week three, set up Meta ads with WhatsApp as the call to action, starting with Instagram placements that show your work.
4. In week four, upload fresh photos to your profile, reply to every open review, and check every landing page on a phone using the checks in [our Kenya mobile-first guide](/blog/local-marketing-kenya-small-business-mobile-first).
5. At the end of the month, compare cost per lead and booking rate by channel before you add budget.

## FAQ

### Do Local Services Ads charge for clicks or for leads?

Local Falcon says advertisers pay only for actual leads, such as calls, messages, or bookings ([Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/blog/effective-local-marketing-strategies-for-smbs)). Confirm the current billing terms on Google's Local Services help pages before you set a budget.

### Is it worth messaging customers who have stopped booking?

Booksy describes an automatic message to customers who have not booked in some time, and advises stopping contact with anyone who says they are not interested in welcome-back offers ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-clients-vanished-how-to-bring-them-back-v)). Track replies and opt-outs so you can see whether the messages help.

### What is the fastest way to estimate a no-show loss?

Multiply your lost bookings per week by your average booking value, then multiply by 52. Curogram's example uses this method with $200 and five lost appointments a week ([Curogram](https://curogram.com/blog/how-to-reduce-no-show-appointments)). Use your own records instead of its illustrative numbers.

### Does this guide replace a local marketing audit?

No. The checklist covers the main channels, but your category, city and competitors change the priorities. A review of your current profile and ad accounts shows which steps matter first.
