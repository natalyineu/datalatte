A Google Business Profile post gets clicks when it makes one offer or update clear, uses a real photo, and carries a button that leads to the page where the customer finishes the job. Offer posts get a "View Offer" button automatically. Event posts need a start and end time, or they show as lasting 24 hours. Google does not publish click-through benchmarks for posts, so the writing advice below is practice, not a Google rule.

## Which Post Type Should You Use?

Pick the post type by the next step you want the customer to take. Each type has its own rules, and the rules decide what you must fill in before Google will publish.

| Post type | Rules from the sources | Button |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offer | Needs a title, start date and end date. A photo is recommended (secondary). | "View Offer" added automatically |
| Event | Needs a title and dates. Without a start or end time it shows as lasting 24 hours on the event dates. | Choose the button that fits |
| Standard update | No extra rules listed in the help page | Choose the button that matches the next step |

An offer with no dates will not go live, so the dates are part of the copy, not an afterthought. A photo strengthens both offers and events, according to one vendor guide (secondary). Use the photo to show the thing the customer will get, such as the finished haircut or the actual pastry case.

## How Do You Write a Post That Earns a Click?

Google gives no copy rules for clicks, so treat the following as practice. Start with the single thing the customer gets: the offer, the new service or the event date. Put that in the first line, because a reader scanning a Maps listing sees little else.

Send the button to a page that matches the post. A "Book" button that lands on the homepage breaks the link between the post and the action. If the offer is for a midweek slot, the button should open the booking page for that slot.

Leave phone numbers out of the description. Google's guidelines allow a business to post its own contact details, but its help page warns that a phone number in a description may be rejected if it cannot be verified as connected to the business. Keep the number in your profile details instead. ## Where Do Posts Appear and Why Do They Disappear?

Google says a post is public and shows on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Posts marked Pending or Not approved do not show. Check the status after you publish, because a post that never went live gets no clicks.

Posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set. This matters most for seasonal updates, which can drop out of view before the season ends if you set no end date. You cannot edit a published post; one vendor guide says you must delete it and publish again (secondary). Deleting a live post removes it from the listing permanently, so keep a copy of the text and photo first.

Featured posts are available in English for single-location food and drink businesses in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand. If you run a single cafe or restaurant in one of those countries, check whether your profile can use them.

## Can You Schedule or Automate Posts?

One vendor guide says Google has no scheduled posting of its own, so a scheduled post waits outside Google until it is published (secondary). Check your profile's current options on Google's live help page before you build a routine around them.

## How Do You Plan a Month of Posts?

This worked example uses assumptions for a salon. Assumption: 4 posts a month. Assumption: 20 minutes to write and photograph each post. Assumption: one offer runs for 7 days.

Time cost: 4 posts × 20 minutes = 80 minutes a month, or about 16 hours a year (80 × 12 = 960 minutes).

| Week | Post type | Content | Button |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Standard update | New colour service, photo of finished work | Book |
| 2 | Offer | Midweek slot offer with start and end dates | View Offer |
| 3 | Standard update | Stylist introduction | Matches next step |
| 4 | Event | Open evening with start and end times | Matches next step |

The week 1 and week 3 updates expire from view after 6 months unless you set a date range, so review them twice a year and republish the ones that still fit. Keep the offer on its own dates so it ends when the offer ends. Track booking-link clicks for 8 weeks, then keep the post type that brings the most bookings. Zero clicks is possible, and it usually means the offer or the landing page needs work.

## FAQ

### How long does a Google Business Profile post stay up?

Posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set. Set a date range on offers and events so they end on the right day.

### Why is my post not showing?

Only posts with the Live status appear on Search and Maps. Pending and Not approved posts do not show, so check the status after you publish.

### Can I edit a post after publishing?

A vendor guide says no: you delete the post and publish a new one (secondary). Deleting a live post removes it permanently.

### Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Google states that creating a Business Profile and listing your business on Google is free of cost. A business needs a physical location customers can visit, or it must travel to customers where they are.
