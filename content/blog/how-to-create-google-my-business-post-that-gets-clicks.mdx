A Business Profile post that gets clicks has one clear offer or update, a photo, and an action button that links to a page where the customer can finish the job. Google offers three post types (update, offer, event). Offers get an automatic "View Offer" button; updates let you choose a button such as book or order. Google publishes no click-through benchmark for posts.

Everything below about how posts work comes from Google's help page, opened on 2026-11-01. Where this article gives advice that Google does not state, it says so.

## Which Post Type Should You Use?

Pick by what the customer should do next.

| Type | Required | Optional | Button |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Update | none listed | description, photo, video | Your choice, with a link (book, order online, learn more) |
| Offer | title, dates, time | description, photo, video, coupon code, link, terms | "View Offer" added automatically |
| Event | title, start and end dates and times | description, photo, video | Optional |

Our guide to [post types](/blog/google-my-business-post-types-for-local-seo) compares them in more depth. Events without a start or end time show as lasting 24 hours on the event dates, so set times.

## How Do You Write a Post That Earns a Click?

Google gives no copy rules for clicks, so this section is practice, not a Google rule.

- Lead with the one thing the customer gets: the offer, the new service, the event date.
- Name the action in the button, and send it to a page that matches the post. A button to a homepage loses the thread.
- Put dates in offers and events. Google requires them anyway.
- Use a real photo of your product or space.

Google may reject a post that contains a phone number it cannot verify as connected to your business, so leave numbers out of the description and use the profile's call button.

## Where Do Posts Appear?

Google says live posts are public and show on Search and Maps. On mobile they appear in the Updates or Overview tabs, and on desktop in the "From the owner" section. Statuses matter: a pending post is still processing or the profile is unverified; a not-approved post carries a question mark pointing to the policy it broke.

Featured posts are currently available in English for single-location food and drink businesses in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, shown in place of reviews on mobile. If you run a cafe, check whether your profile has them.

## Can You Schedule and Repeat Posts?

Yes. Turn on "Schedule this post" to choose a publish date and time, and use the "Repeats" dropdown to publish weekly, monthly or on a custom schedule. Google also lets you publish to multiple profiles at once.

Because posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set, evergreen content should be refreshed twice a year. Pair this with the [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) and a review routine from [this guide](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop).

## A Worked Example: A Weekly Posting Plan

All inputs are **assumptions** for a salon.

| Week | Post type | Content | Button |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Update | New colour service, photo of finished work | Book |
| 2 | Offer | Midweek slot offer with dates | View Offer |
| 3 | Update | Stylist introduction | Learn more |
| 4 | Event | Open evening with start and end times | Optional |

Set the week 1 and 3 updates on a monthly repeat, and put the offer and event on a date range so they expire on their own. Track calls and booking-link clicks in the profile's performance data for 8 weeks, then keep the type that brings the most bookings. A result of zero clicks is possible and tells you the offer or page needs work.

## FAQ

### How often should I post?

Google gives no frequency. A weekly post is a manageable routine; archiving after 6 months is the only timing rule it states.

### How long does a post stay up?

Posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set.

### Why was my post not approved?

The status shows a question mark that points to the violated content policy. Phone numbers that cannot be verified as yours are one cause.

### Can I use a coupon code?

Yes, on offer posts. You can also add a link and terms and conditions.

### Do posts improve rankings?

Google does not say so on its posts page. Treat posts as a conversion tool, not a ranking lever.
