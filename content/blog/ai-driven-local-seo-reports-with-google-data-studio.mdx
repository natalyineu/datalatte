You can build an AI-assisted local SEO report in Google's reporting tool (now called Looker Studio, formerly Data Studio) by connecting Google Search Console, Google Analytics 4 and your Google Business Profile data, then using an AI assistant to summarise the trends and suggest next steps. The charts come from real data; the AI helps you explain it in plain language.

The important point is that AI should interpret your numbers, not invent them. Done properly, you end up with a one-page monthly report that tells a local business owner what happened, why it probably happened and what to do next.

## A quick note on the name

Google renamed Data Studio to Looker Studio in 2022. The tool is free for most uses, and the steps below apply to it. You may still see "Google Data Studio" in older tutorials and search results.

## What a local SEO report should answer

Owners rarely want a wall of charts. A useful report answers five questions:

1. Are more people finding us on Google?
2. Are they contacting us (calls, directions, bookings, forms)?
3. Which searches and pages bring them in?
4. How are our reviews and Google Business Profile doing?
5. What are we doing next month?

## Step 1: Gather your data sources

| Source | What it tells you | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search Console | Queries, impressions, clicks, average position, pages | Native connector |
| Google Analytics 4 | Sessions, key events (calls, forms, bookings), traffic sources | Native connector |
| Google Business Profile | Profile views, calls, direction requests, website clicks, search terms | Export or a third-party connector, since native connector support has changed over time |
| Rank tracker (optional) | Local map and organic positions by location | CSV, Google Sheets or vendor connector |
| Reviews (optional) | Count, average rating, recency | Manual entry in Sheets or a review tool export |

Check each connector's current availability, as Google and third parties change integrations. A Google Sheet is a reliable bridge: paste or import exports there and connect the sheet to Looker Studio.

You also need conversion tracking in place, or the report will show traffic without outcomes. If you have not set that up, see our [Google Tag Manager guide](/blog/google-tag-manager-for-small-business).

## Step 2: Build the report layout

1. Go to lookerstudio.google.com and create a blank report.
2. Add your data sources one by one and authorise access with the Google account that owns them.
3. Design a single page with a header, a row of headline numbers and a few supporting charts.

A layout that works for most local businesses:

- **Top row:** total clicks from Search Console, GA4 sessions from organic search, key events (calls, bookings, forms), and profile actions.
- **Trend chart:** clicks and impressions over the last 12 months, compared to the same period last year to account for seasonality.
- **Top queries table:** with a filter for queries that include your town or "near me".
- **Top pages table:** which pages are earning clicks and key events.
- **Profile panel:** views, calls, directions and website clicks.
- **Notes box:** space for your commentary.

Use a date range control and a comparison period so the owner can see change at a glance. Keep colours and fonts consistent and limit yourself to a few charts. More charts rarely mean more insight.

## Step 3: Add filters that make it local

Local reports need local views:

- Filter Search Console queries by country, or by regex for your city and neighbourhoods.
- Segment branded queries (your business name) from non-branded queries such as "dog groomer near me". Non-branded growth is a better signal of new demand.
- For multiple locations, add a location dropdown, ideally with one profile or landing page per branch. See our [multi-location SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-for-multi-location-businesses).

## Step 4: Where AI actually helps

Looker Studio itself does not write commentary for you, but you can use a general AI assistant such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini on exported data. Here is a safe workflow:

1. Export the key tables (for example, top queries and top pages for this month and last month) as CSV, anonymised if needed.
2. Paste them into the assistant with a clear instruction.
3. Review the draft against the charts. Correct anything that does not match.

Example prompt: "Here are Search Console numbers for a pet grooming salon for this month and last month. Summarise what changed in plain English in five bullet points, flag anything that looks unusual, and suggest three practical next steps. Do not invent numbers that are not in the data."

Good uses of AI in reporting:

- Turning tables into a short plain-English summary.
- Spotting queries that are rising or falling.
- Grouping search terms by theme (services, neighbourhoods, brand).
- Drafting a next-steps list for you to edit.
- Flagging possible anomalies, such as a sudden drop in clicks, for you to investigate.

Poor uses:

- Asking it to "predict" rankings.
- Asking it to explain causes it cannot see (it does not know you changed your website or that a competitor opened).
- Publishing its commentary without checking it against the data.

## Step 5: Explain the numbers honestly

Local SEO data is noisy. A few principles keep reports honest:

- Compare like with like: month against the same month last year where possible.
- Treat average position as a rough guide. Local results vary by searcher location and device.
- Remember Search Console and GA4 will not match each other exactly, because they measure different things.
- Show actions (calls, bookings), not just traffic.
- Say "we think" when you are inferring a cause.

## Step 6: Schedule and share

Looker Studio can email the report on a schedule (for example, on the first Monday of each month) as a PDF or a link. Add a short note at the top with the three key takeaways and the next actions. Consistency matters more than perfection, so pick a day and keep it.

If you manage reports for clients, or just want a set-and-forget approach, consider a monthly review session, even a 20-minute call, so the numbers lead to decisions. ## A simple monthly checklist

- Are branded and non-branded clicks up or down versus last year?
- Which three pages earned the most key events?
- Did calls and direction requests move with profile views?
- Did review count and rating change?
- Are there pages with many impressions but few clicks (title or snippet issues)?
- What are the top two actions for next month?

## FAQ

**Q: Is Google Data Studio still available?**
A: It was renamed Looker Studio in 2022. The core tool is free, and older Data Studio tutorials mostly still apply.

**Q: Can Looker Studio pull Google Business Profile data automatically?**
A: Availability has changed over time. Check the current connector list, and if there is no direct option, use a Google Sheets bridge or a third-party connector.

**Q: Can AI write my whole SEO report?**
A: It can draft commentary and summaries from data you provide, but you must verify it. AI cannot see your actual analytics unless you give it the data, and it can state incorrect conclusions confidently.

**Q: How often should a local business review SEO reports?**
A: Monthly is typical, with a quick check after any major website or profile change. Local rankings fluctuate, so trends over several months are more meaningful than weekly changes.
