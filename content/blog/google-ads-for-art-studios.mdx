If a six-week painting course sells for $240 and you are willing to spend 30% of that, $72, on ads to fill one seat, then at 6 enquiries per 100 clicks and 1 in 4 enquiries booking, the most you can pay for a click is $1.08. Go above it and you lose money on average. Art studios sit in an awkward spot: classes are cheap per seat compared with, say, a wedding photographer's package, so the click price you can afford is low, and the keyword and landing page work has to be tighter.

Those three inputs ($72, 6%, 25%) are **my assumptions for illustration**, not benchmarks. This guide covers how to set up a Search campaign for classes, workshops, kids' camps and private parties, which keywords to start with, what to block, and how to work out your own ceiling. It does not quote industry click prices or conversion rates for art studios, because we could not find a public, sourced benchmark for the category and will not print numbers we cannot back up. It is written for studios in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: read "zip code" as "postcode" and adjust currencies as needed.

## Why Searchers Are Not All the Same Buyer

"Art classes" covers a parent booking a kids' camp, an adult wanting a relaxing Friday night, a hen party organiser and a hobbyist who wants a studio membership. Each searches differently and is worth a different amount. Give each its own ad group and landing page.

| Intent | Example keywords (replace [city]) | Match type | Landing page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adult classes | "painting classes [city]", "pottery classes [city]", "art classes for adults [city]", "beginner drawing class" | Phrase + exact | Class page with schedule, price, level and a booking button |
| One-off workshops and "date night" | "paint and sip [city]", "pottery workshop [city]", "date night art class" | Phrase | Workshop page with the next three dates |
| Kids and teens | "kids art classes [city]", "art camp [city]", "after school art classes" | Phrase + exact | Kids page with ages, safeguarding info and term dates |
| Private parties and groups | "art party [city]", "birthday party painting", "team building art class", "hen party art class" | Phrase | Parties page with group sizes and a quote form |
| Studio hire and memberships | "pottery studio membership [city]", "open studio [city]", "kiln hire" | Phrase + exact | Membership page |
| Brand | [your studio name] | Exact | Home page |

Only include rows you actually sell. If you run a medium-specific studio (ceramics, glass, printmaking), put the medium in the keyword. "Pottery classes [city]" brings a different buyer from "art classes [city]", and usually a more qualified one.

## Match Types: What Google Actually Says

Google's [keyword match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) defines three. Broad match can show on related searches, including ones that do not share the keyword's literal meaning, and is the default. Phrase match (in quotation marks) can show on searches that carry your keyword's meaning, including implied meanings and more specific versions. Exact match (in square brackets) can show on searches with the same meaning or intent, with the most control and the smallest reach.

Google recommends Smart Bidding with all match types and says it works best with broad match. Smart Bidding needs conversion data, and a small studio may get only a handful of enquiries a month. So begin with phrase and exact, set up tracking, and consider broad match later.

## Negative Keywords for Art Studios

Art terms attract shoppers for supplies, students looking for free tutorials and job seekers. Google's [negative keywords help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972) explains that a broad-match negative blocks searches containing all its terms in any order, that negatives do not match close variants (so add singular and plural), and that too many negatives can reduce your reach.

| Group | Negatives |
| --- | --- |
| Free and DIY | `free`, `diy`, `tutorial`, `tutorials`, `youtube`, `how to`, `printable`, `download`, `coloring pages`, `lesson plan` |
| Supplies | `supplies`, `paint set`, `canvas`, `canvases`, `brushes`, `easel`, `kit`, `store`, `shop`, `wholesale` |
| Jobs and study | `jobs`, `job`, `career`, `salary`, `degree`, `university`, `college`, `masters`, `scholarship`, `school` |
| Online-only | `online`, `zoom`, `virtual`, `app` (remove if you teach online) |
| Wrong art | `tattoo`, `makeup`, `nail art`, `wall art`, `museum`, `gallery hours`, `exhibition` (remove those you offer) |

Edit before pasting: if you sell supplies, drop `supplies`; if you run kids' classes, make sure nothing blocks "kids". Then read the [search terms report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708). Google describes it as showing the actual searches that led to your ads, with the matching keyword and match type, and recommends using it to spot irrelevant searches and add them as negatives. Check it weekly. Our negative keywords guide walks through the steps.

## Location: Draw It Around Where Students Live

Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) says that for most campaign types you can target countries, cities, territories or a radius around a location, and exclude locations to keep ads out of specific regions. It also says accuracy is not guaranteed in every situation. For a studio, start by listing the home postcodes or zip codes of your last 30 students and drawing a radius that covers most of them. Weekly classes draw from a small radius; one-off workshops and parties can draw from further. Consider splitting those into separate campaigns with different radii.

## Ads That Match the Search

Google's [responsive search ad requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791) say you need at least 3 headlines and 2 descriptions and can add up to 15 headlines (30 characters each) and 4 descriptions (90 characters each). Use the room. Write specific, true lines:

- `Pottery Classes in [Town]`, `Beginners Welcome, All Materials Included`, `6-Week Course From $240`, `Small Groups of 8`, `Next Course Starts [Date]`
- Description: `Wheel-throwing for complete beginners. Take home what you make. See dates and book online.`

Put the town and the class type in the first headlines so they mirror the search. Only claim what you can show: "Rated 4.9 by 120 students" is fine if your Business Profile says so, and invented awards are not.

Add a call asset, a location asset tied to your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist), and sitelinks such as "Class Schedule", "Kids Camps", "Private Parties" and "Gift Vouchers". People compare photos and reviews before they book, so a complete profile helps every ad.

## Landing Pages and Tracking

Send each ad group to its own page. A class page needs the date, price, level, what is included, the studio's address and a booking button above the fold, plus a few real photos and reviews. See our [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices). Before turning ads on, set up tracking for bookings and calls using our conversion tracking guide.

Then log every enquiry and whether it booked. A class that fills from word of mouth looks identical to one filled by ads if you do not ask "how did you hear about us?" at booking.

## Remarketing: Possible, but Check the Size First

Many visitors browse the schedule and leave. Google's [help on remarketing lists for Search ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2701222) describes using your website visitor segments to raise bids or show different ads when past visitors search on Google. Its example is a 25% bid increase for people who visited in the last 30 days. It also says a segment needs at least 1,000 cookies before it can tailor Search ads. A studio with a few hundred visitors a month may need time to build that list, so treat it as a later step. For the basics, see our remarketing guide.

## The Math

Every input below is an **assumption** for illustration. Replace them with figures from your own enrolment and enquiry records.

| | Drop-in workshop | 6-week course | Kids camp week | Private party | Original artwork sale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue per booking (assumption) | $45 | $240 | $300 | $450 | $600 |
| Max ad spend per booking, 30% (assumption) | $13.50 | $72 | $90 | $135 | $180 |
| Click to enquiry rate (assumption) | 6% | 6% | 6% | 5% | 3% |
| Enquiry to booking rate (assumption) | 25% | 25% | 25% | 30% | 10% |
| Ceiling click price | $0.20 | $1.08 | $1.35 | $2.03 | $0.54 |

Formula: ceiling CPC = max ad spend per booking x enquiry rate x booking rate. For the course: $72 x 0.06 x 0.25 = $1.08.

What it tells you:

- **Single-seat workshops barely work as stand-alone ad targets.** At $0.20 you will rarely win the click. Use ads for courses, camps and parties, and fill workshops from your email list, Business Profile and social channels.
- **Courses and parties carry more.** Keep their keywords and pages sharp and give parties their own campaign so a cheap workshop click never eats their budget.
- **Selling original work through search ads is a long shot** under these assumptions. If you try it, send traffic to a page showing the specific piece, and compare the cost per sale with the gallery or marketplace fee you would pay otherwise.
- **Repeat students change the answer.** If many course students return for a second term, count that revenue. That is how a $1.08 ceiling may become a $2 ceiling.

For the real click estimates in your area, use Google's [Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243). Google says it estimates monthly searches and average cost per keyword and requires account setup, including billing information. It also says keywords with very low search volumes are not discoverable or forecastable, so a very specific local term may return nothing. Compare each estimate with your ceiling before committing budget.

## Budget and Bidding

There is no official art-studio budget. Work backwards: decide how many seats you want to fill from ads, multiply by your ceiling cost per booking, and see if the total fits. Google's [daily budget help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6385083) says that for most campaigns daily spend can reach up to twice your average daily budget, with a monthly cap of 30.4 times the daily budget. Set the daily figure as your month divided by 30.4.

On bidding, Google's [bid strategy comparison](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268637) describes Maximize conversions as spending your budget to get as many conversions as possible with no target cost, and Target CPA as aiming for conversions at a specific cost per action. Let a campaign collect real conversions under tracking before adding a target, and check the strategy's own requirements in your account.

## Do This Today

1. Write your revenue per booking for workshops, courses, camps and parties, plus the most you would pay in ads for each. Calculate your ceiling click price with the formula.
2. Open Keyword Planner, set your town and enter "painting classes [city]" (or your medium) and your next two best terms. Compare the estimated average cost with your ceilings and drop any group that cannot work.
3. Build one Search campaign for your best-fit group (usually courses), phrase and exact match, one landing page per ad group, with the negatives above edited to your offer.
4. Set up booking-form and call conversion tracking before ads go live, and add "how did you hear about us?" to your booking form.
5. After two weeks, read the search terms report, add negatives and log which enquiries booked. Pause any ad group that has spent twice your target cost per enquiry with none.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should an art studio spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no official figure. Work backwards from the seats you want and your ceiling cost per booking. At the assumed $72 per course seat, 10 course seats means about $720 of ad spend if you hit your target.

**Q: Which keywords are best for an art studio?**
A: Start with your medium plus your town, such as "pottery classes [city]" or "painting classes [city]", in phrase and exact match, and add kids, party and workshop groups if you sell them. Judge each by bookings, not clicks.

**Q: Is "paint and sip" worth bidding on?**
A: It can bring group and date-night customers, but it is a different buyer from a six-week course student. Put it in its own ad group and judge it by cost per booking.

**Q: Should I use broad match or Performance Max?**
A: Not first. Smart Bidding and automated campaigns need conversion data, which a small studio may not have yet. Start with Search, tracking and a strong negative list.

**Q: Can I sell my artwork with Google Ads?**
A: You can send traffic to a page showing a piece, but the economics are tight. Work out your ceiling click price for a sale first, then test one specific piece before committing budget.
