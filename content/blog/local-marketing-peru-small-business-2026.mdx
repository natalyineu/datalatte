## What do the available sources say about Peru?

The sources gathered for this update are mostly agency websites. They publish their own totals for projects, clients and countries. These totals describe the size of each agency's business, not the size of the Peruvian market. Onza Marketing, based in Lima, lists more than 1,000 projects and 15 countries. Manya reports more than 800 happy clients, and Marketing Branding Peru reports more than 400 companies across Peru, Chile and Colombia.

Two US government pages were also checked. The SBA marketing page returned "Page not found", and the BLS page contained navigation only. Neither supports a Peru-specific claim, so neither is used for figures here. Government data on Peruvian consumers would need a separate source, and none was available for this update.

For a small shop, the practical point is simple. No agency total tells you how many nearby customers will see your listing. Your own order log will.

## Which agencies work in Peru and what do they offer?

Onza Marketing lists branding, web design, SEO, online sales, social media, paid advertising and graphic design as separate services. Each service has its own page, so a business can buy one part rather than a full package. That suits a shop that only needs a better listing or a few ads.

Manya lists branding, web development, website support, SEO for AI search, performance marketing and process automation. Its pages describe outcomes such as visibility and lead generation, but they do not give measured results for a named client. The testimonials on its homepage are quotes from clients and do not include dates, metrics or scope.

When comparing agencies, ask for the scope in writing, the start and end dates of any case study, and the metric used to judge success. A total of clients served is a volume claim, not evidence of results.

## Which channels should a small shop test first?

Start with the channels a customer already uses to find a shop nearby, then add one new channel at a time. A free business listing, a social page with a clear phone or messaging link, and a short video show what people actually do. Paid ads come after you know which offer converts.

Track each order by source. Ask every new customer how they found you, and write the answer on a sheet. Over four weeks, that tally tells you more about your market than any general report.

Do not split your time across every platform. Pick two or three channels, post on a fixed schedule, and drop the one that brings the fewest paid orders per hour. If you schedule posts across Facebook and Instagram, a tool comparison such as [Hootsuite vs Sprout Social for small businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) can help you choose one that fits your team size and budget.

## How do payments and messaging change the offer?

A customer who sees an offer on a phone should be able to pay or reserve in the same session. Show the payment methods you accept on your listing, your social profiles and any ads, and make the path from message to payment as short as possible. The sources here do not name payment wallets or give usage figures, so confirm which methods customers use locally before you choose.

Messaging is often the main sales channel for small Latin American businesses. A regional comparison such as [local marketing in Brazil with WhatsApp](/blog/local-marketing-brazil-small-business-whatsapp-meta) shows how a messaging-led model works, though the rules there differ from Peru's. Keep a record of each order and payment so sales can be reconciled. If invoices are spread across chat and card payments, a simple tool like [FreshBooks vs QuickBooks for local businesses](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business) can help keep them in one place.

## What rules apply to messaging customers?

The sources available for this update do not cover Peru's data-protection or advertising rules, so this guide does not state them. Before sending bulk messages, emails or calls to people who have not asked to hear from the business, check the current official text and ask a local lawyer.

Keep records of how each contact joined your list. If you cannot show that a customer agreed to receive messages, do not add them to a campaign list. Treat this as a working rule until the legal position for your sector and city has been confirmed.

## How could a Peruvian shop test channels? (worked example)

All figures below are **assumptions** for illustration, not benchmarks. A bakery in Arequipa has 10 hours a week for marketing and a small fixed test budget set by the owner (assumption).
