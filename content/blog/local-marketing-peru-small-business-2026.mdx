Local marketing in Peru should be built around Facebook, TikTok and YouTube, plus messaging and mobile payments. DataReportal counts 28.4 million internet users (82.0%) at the end of 2025, and its ad-tool figures show 28.3 million TikTok adults, 24.7 million on Facebook and 21.7 million on YouTube. Peru is also a fast-moving payments market, which changes how a small shop turns attention into sales.

## How many Peruvians are online?

DataReportal's Digital 2026 report counts 34.7 million residents, 37.5 million mobile connections (108% of the population) and 28.4 million internet users. About 6.25 million people (18.0%) are offline. Social media user identities reached 28.3 million, up 4.4 million (+18.5%) in a year.

Mobile connections can include voice-only lines, and social identities are not unique people. The World Bank lists a 2025 population of 34.58 million and GDP per capita of about US$9,684, which is a reminder that purchasing power differs a lot from the US or EU.

## Which platforms reach Peruvian customers?

The figures below are advertising reach from platform tools. DataReportal says they are not monthly active users and can differ from real user bases.

- TikTok: 28.3 million (adults 18+)
- Facebook: 24.7 million
- YouTube: 21.7 million
- LinkedIn: 12.0 million registered members
- Instagram: 11.3 million
- Messenger: 10.9 million

Facebook and TikTok are the widest reach for a local business. Instagram reaches less than half of Facebook's audience, so it suits a visual business more than a general one.

## How big is digital payment use in Peru?

Payments matter because a customer who sees an offer can pay at once with a phone. According to press reports citing the central bank (BCRP), digital payments per adult rose from 90 in 2021 to 625 in 2025, about 1.7 a day, mainly through Yape, Plin and cards. Reported Yape users range from 16 to 17 million depending on the source and the date, and the same coverage says the two wallets became interoperable in 2022-2023.

These are secondary sources because the BCRP site blocked automated access, so verify the figures on bcrp.gob.pe before quoting them. For a shop, the practical point is to show a Yape or Plin payment option on its profile and in ads.

## What do you know about Google Ads in Peru?

Google Ads Help lists the countries that do not support prepay for new accounts. Peru did not appear in that list when checked, so prepay may be available, although you should confirm in your own account.

No Peruvian cost-per-click or cost-per-thousand-impressions benchmark from a primary source was found, so none is quoted. If a vendor gives you a price per click for Peru, ask for the source and the date. Start with a small test and read the numbers from your own account.

## What does Peruvian data protection law require?

Peru's personal data law (Ley 29733) requires prior and express consent from the person whose data you collect, according to a university explainer. Press coverage says a new regulation took effect on 31 March 2025, and that a first sales call may only ask for general consent for commercial prospecting before any specific offer.

A separate El Peruano headline refers to Ley 32323 on advertising without express consent, but only the headline was seen. Fines and article numbers were not verified. Treat all of this as unverified, and read the law on gob.pe or ask a Peruvian lawyer before running calls or bulk messages.

### Is Facebook still useful for Peruvian businesses?
Its ad reach is 24.7 million, second only to TikTok adults at 28.3 million. These are ad-tool estimates.

Press reports citing the BCRP show strong growth in digital payments per adult. Verify the numbers on the BCRP site.
