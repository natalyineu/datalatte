Pet groomers should run Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) as a small local lead or booking campaign, with a tracked booking page and a budget you can afford to test for a month. LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmarks put the all-industry cost per lead at $27.39, Personal Services at $38.09 and Beauty & Personal Care at $50.91. LocaliQ lists no lead figure for Animals & Pets.

This guide is for salon and mobile groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Prices are US dollars from US benchmark data, so treat them as a range. Illustrations are labelled as assumptions.

## How Much Do Meta Ads Cost for a Pet Groomer?

LocaliQ's page, last updated 23 September 2026, covers 20 industries and does not describe its sample. For leads campaigns the all-industry average is a 2.70% click-through rate, $1.80 per click and an 8.54% conversion rate. Personal Services shows $1.62 per click, a 6.61% conversion rate and $38.09 per lead. Beauty & Personal Care shows $2.97 per click, 5.63% and $50.91.

Animals & Pets appears only in the traffic tables: a 1.85% click-through rate and $0.51 per click. Traffic clicks are not leads, so do not read $0.51 as the price of a booking. Use Personal Services and Beauty & Personal Care as a rough range until your own account has data.

## What Campaign Goal Should a Groomer Choose?

Pick the goal that matches the action you can measure. If clients book on your website, use a sales or conversion-style goal tied to a booking event. If most people message or call, use a leads goal with a lead form or messages. Traffic is cheaper per click, but a click does not tell you who booked.

Do not send the ad to your Facebook page as the only landing page. A booking page with prices, area served, hours and a clear button gives the ad something to measure.

## How Do You Set Up Tracking?

Meta describes its Conversions API as a way to send marketing data from your server, website platform, app or CRM to Meta, and says server events are processed like Meta Pixel events. Its documentation also covers event deduplication, which matters when both send the same action.

For a small groomer, the practical step is to install the Pixel on the booking confirmation page, or ask your booking software whether it connects. Then test one booking yourself. Without a booking event, Meta can optimise only for clicks or messages.

## Who Should You Target?

Start with a radius around your shop or your mobile route, set to the area you can drive to or serve. Add the offer to the creative instead of relying on narrow interests: "first groom for puppies", "de-shedding for double coats" or "nervous dog friendly" tell people who the ad is for.

Existing clients are a second audience. Upload your customer list only if your privacy notice allows it and follow Meta's rules. Our guide to [Facebook ads that win back lapsed groomer clients](/blog/pet-groomer-meta-ads) covers that job in detail.

## What Should the Ad Show?

Show the dog, the groomer and the result. Use real before-and-after photos from clients who agreed to be shown, because people can judge your actual work, and test them against other images. Put the price or starting price, the area and the booking action in the first line.

Keep a simple ad set: three images or short videos, two headlines, one offer. Change one thing at a time so you know what moved results.

## How Do You Know the Ads Work?

Track cost per booking, not likes. Ask every new client how they found you, and compare the answers to Meta's report. Check that your Google Business Profile is complete, because people often search your name before booking; our [Business Profile guide for groomers](/blog/pet-groomer-google-business-profile-optimization) covers it.

Review weekly for the first month: spend, leads, bookings, cost per booking. If no one books after the first full month with working tracking, change the offer or the page before raising the budget. For setting up tracking, see [pet groomer analytics and tracking](/blog/pet-groomer-analytics-tracking).

## FAQ

### How much should a pet groomer spend on Meta ads?
There is no source-backed minimum here. A $600 month, as in the example, is an assumption you can scale down. Work back from cost per booking and your average ticket.

### Do I need a website for Meta ads?
You need a booking page that can record the action. A simple page with prices, area, hours and a booking button is enough. Sending ads only to a Facebook page makes results harder to measure.

### Is Facebook or Instagram better for groomers?
No source we opened ranks them for groomers. Run both placements with the same creative and compare cost per booking in your own account.

### What cost per lead should a groomer expect?
LocaliQ's 2026 figures for related categories are $38.09 (Personal Services) and $50.91 (Beauty & Personal Care). It publishes no lead figure for pets.
