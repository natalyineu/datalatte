## How Much Does Vistar Media Cost for a Local Business?

AdQuick reports that Vistar's campaign minimums start around $1,500 and suit small businesses that want to reach specific audience segments (secondary). That is a figure reported by a third party, not a published price list from Vistar, so treat it as a starting point rather than a quote.

Vistar's homepage says it facilitates connections between advertisers and screens "hundreds of thousands of times a day." A description of the platform does not set a price, so request a quote for your own market and your own schedule.

Decide the job the money must do before you ask. A $1,500 campaign concentrated in one small area produces a very different test from the same spend spread across several neighbourhoods. The worked example later in this guide shows how to check whether a result pays for itself.

## What Does Programmatic DOOH Cost per Thousand Impressions?

StackAdapt lists three common DOOH pricing models: CPM, flat-rate and auction-based (secondary). It puts programmatic DOOH CPMs at typically $4 to $50. AdQuick lists digital billboard CPMs at $5 to $10 (secondary). The spread is wide, which is why the quoted rate for your location matters more than any national range.

StackAdapt names location, traffic volume, demographics, billboard size, campaign duration, timing and seasonality as factors that influence DOOH cost (secondary). A screen in a busy area during peak season will usually price differently from a quieter screen in a slow month.

For fixed screens, AdQuick reports monthly digital billboard costs from $1,200 in smaller markets to $15,000 or more in major cities (secondary). StackAdapt reports that non-programmatic spot buys are usually made weekly or monthly and can range from around $1,000 per week to $100,000 or more per month (secondary). For a broader view of platform choices, see [DOOH advertising platforms compared for small business](/blog/dooh-advertising-platforms-compared-small-business).

## How Does Programmatic DOOH Differ From a Billboard Booking?

A fixed booking gives you a spot in a rotation, and the share of the loop you hold is the key number. StackAdapt notes that one spot in a loop of 10 equates to 10% share of voice in non-programmatic DOOH loop buys (secondary). Programmatic DOOH works differently: you buy impressions at a CPM across qualifying screens, not a fixed slot.

| | Fixed billboard or loop spot | Programmatic DOOH |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you buy | A spot in a loop, such as 1 of 10 (10% share of voice) | Impressions at a CPM across qualifying screens |
| Price basis | Weekly or monthly spot | CPM, flat-rate or auction-based |
| Reported price range | About $1,000 per week to $100,000+ per month | CPM typically $4 to $50 |
| Speed to launch | Set by the booking | Can be launched and optimised in days rather than weeks (StackAdapt) |

The comparison matters for [billboard versus TV advertising](/blog/billboard-vs-tv-advertising-local-business), because a fixed spot and a programmatic buy answer different questions. The first buys presence at a known location. The second buys reach across screens that match your criteria, with less control over where each impression appears.

## Which Fees Should You Ask About?

Blip, a digital billboard seller with pay-as-you-go pricing, states it does not charge setup fees, platform fees, agency fees or monthly retainers (primary). That is one vendor's stated policy, not a benchmark for Vistar, but it gives you a useful test: ask for a written list of every charge.

Request an itemised quote from Vistar that shows the rate, any platform or setup charge, and any agency or service fee. Ask whether the CPM quoted includes all of them. If a fee is missing from the quote, do not assume it is included.

## How Do You Set Up a Small Test?

StackAdapt states that programmatic campaigns can be launched and optimised in days rather than weeks (secondary), so a short test is practical. Keep it narrow enough that one result can be read clearly.

1. Pick one question: store visits, phone calls, or redemptions of one specific offer.
2. Choose one area and one creative, so the result can be traced to a single variable.
3. Get a written CPM quote and fee list for that area before you commit any budget.
4. Attach a unique code, short URL or QR code to the creative, so each action can be tied back to the screens.
5. Divide the campaign cost by the tracked results, then decide whether to repeat, change the area, or stop.

If the campaign uses a short programmatic flight, [the programmatic DOOH strategy guide](/blog/programmatic-dooh-strategy-local-business) covers how to set the schedule and read the early numbers.

## Worked Example: Cost per Redemption

All inputs below are **assumptions**. The $8 CPM sits inside the $5 to $10 digital billboard range AdQuick reports, but it is an invented figure for this arithmetic, not a Vistar quote.

| Input or result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Campaign budget (assumption) | $1,500 |
| Dynamic CPM, all fees included (assumption) | $8 |
| Impressions | $1,500 / $8 x 1,000 = 187,500 |
| Redemptions of a screen-only offer (assumption) | 60 |
| Cost per redemption | $1,500 / 60 = $25 |
| Average sale (assumption) | $18 |
| Gap on the first sale | $25 - $18 = $7 loss |

At $25 per redemption against an $18 average sale, the campaign loses $7 on the first visit. It can still pay if repeat visits or larger baskets close the gap. Replace every assumption with your real quote, redemption count and sale value before you decide.

The sum that matters is the value of one new customer over a year, not the impression count. Work that figure out first, then judge the campaign against it.
