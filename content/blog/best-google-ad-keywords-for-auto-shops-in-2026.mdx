Keep body shop and tire terms in their own ad groups if you do not offer them. A broad single word such as "auto shop" has no stated intent, so leave it out at the start.

## How Do Match Types Work for Auto Keywords?

Google defines three. Broad match may show your ad on searches related to the keyword. Phrase match may show on searches that include the meaning of the keyword. Exact match may show on searches with the same meaning or intent, and gives the most control with the fewest searches reached.

Phrase match is a sensible start for a local shop because it reaches more than exact but less than broad. Move your best two or three keywords to exact once your account shows which ones produce calls and bookings. Google says broad match depends heavily on Smart Bidding, so only use it with conversion tracking in place.

## What Negative Keywords Does an Auto Shop Need?

Negative keywords stop your ads showing on searches that contain certain terms. Google notes that they do not match close variants, so add plural and misspelled versions yourself. Search terms worth blocking for a repair shop:

- Do-it-yourself intent: "diy", "how to", "tutorial", "free"
- Job seekers: "jobs", "hiring", "salary", "apprenticeship"
- Parts buyers, if you only fit parts you supply: "used parts", "junkyard", "wholesale"
- Services you do not offer, such as a body shop or tires

Check the Search terms report weekly at the start and add what you find. Too many negatives can shrink reach, so block terms you have seen rather than guessing.

## How Should You Set Location Targeting?

For a shop whose customers live nearby, Google recommends radius targeting around the business. Google also lists "Presence or Interest" as a best practice on Search, which includes people who have shown interest in your area. Choose "Presence" only if you want to reach people physically in the area. The minimum radius is 1 km.

## The Math: What Does $600 a Month Buy?

All inputs are assumptions except the LocaliQ averages.

- **Assumption:** monthly budget of $600.
- **LocaliQ average CPC:** $4.35, so $600 buys about 138 clicks.
- **LocaliQ average conversion rate:** 15.51%, so about 21 leads.
- **Assumption:** 40% of leads become booked jobs, so about 8 to 9 jobs.

That works out to about $28 per lead and about $70 per booked job. Your own numbers will differ, and a high-ticket job such as a transmission repair may cost far more per click than an oil change. The useful number is cost per booked job against the profit on that job. ## FAQ

### What is the average CPC for auto repair on Google Ads?

LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark for automotive repair, service and parts is $4.35 per click across Google Ads and Microsoft Ads search. Your own CPC depends on city, competition and quality.

### How much should an auto shop spend on Google Ads?

There is no published right figure. Work backwards from what a booked job is worth to you, then use the cost per lead in the example above to see how many jobs a budget could produce.

### Should I use broad match?

Not at the start. Google says broad match relies on Smart Bidding, so wait until conversion tracking is working and your negative list is built.

### Is Keyword Planner free?

You need to finish account setup and add billing information, but Google does not say a live campaign is required.

### How many negative keywords do I need?

Google gives no number. Start with the groups above and add terms from your Search terms report.
