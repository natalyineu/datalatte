## Which keywords should an auto shop bid on first?

Lead with terms that describe a job or a location in the same breath, because those searchers are closest to booking. Repair-shop searches run from very cheap to very expensive per click, and the gap is large enough to change which terms are worth funding.

| Keyword | Monthly searches (US) | CPC (US) | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| auto body repairs near me | 450,000 | $5.64 | Own ad group, only if you do body work |
| auto repair shop near me | 165,000 | $4.29 | Core term |
| auto repair shop | 135,000 | $8.35 | Test in phrase match |
| auto repair shops near me | 90,500 | $9.01 | Expensive, bid conservatively |
| mobile auto repair shop | 14,800 | $3.15 | Strong fit if you travel to customers |
| nearest auto parts store | 27,100 | $1.61 | Usually block, parts buyers rarely book labour |

Sources: [Serpwars](https://serpwars.com/auto-repair-shop-keywords/) (secondary). Volumes and CPCs vary by city and will not match your market.

Cheap terms can still mislead. A $1.61 click that comes from someone looking for a part does not help a repair shop. Judge each keyword by the booked job it is likely to produce, not by its price alone.

## How should match types be set for repair keywords?

Start with phrase match for near-me and service-plus-location terms, because it reaches close variations without the open reach of broad match. Keep "auto repair shops near me" on a tighter setting while you learn what it costs you, since it has the highest CPC in the table above.

Move the two or three terms that produce calls and bookings into exact match once you have data. Broad match is harder to control for a single-location shop, so avoid it until conversion tracking shows which searches are paying off. The aim is fewer irrelevant clicks, not more volume.

## Which negative keywords stop wasted clicks?

Negative keywords block your ads from showing on searches that signal no booking intent. For a repair shop, block terms that point to DIY, job hunting and parts-only buying. Review the search terms report every week during the first month, because the real negatives show up there rather than in a planning list.

- DIY and tutorial intent: "how to", "diy", "tutorial", "free"
- Job seekers: "jobs", "hiring", "salary", "apprenticeship"
- Parts buyers, if you do not sell parts: "used parts", "junkyard", "wholesale", "auto parts store"
- Services you do not offer, such as body work or tyres, if those are not in your business

Be careful with blanket blocks. Each negative you add can remove a search that might have converted, so block what you have seen rather than what you imagine.

## Does the search surface change which keywords are worth it?

Yes. A keyword that returns an AI-generated answer may send a searcher nowhere, while a keyword that shows a local map pack rewards businesses with a complete local listing. Data from VulcanAX shows "mechanic" resolves to an AI Overview, while "auto repair" and "car repair" resolve to the local pack.

The practical split: for local pack terms, paid ads and your business profile work together. For AI Overview terms, paid clicks may be fewer and the page that answers the question matters more. Check the live results for your own city before you commit budget to a term, because the surface often differs by location.

## What does a $600 month buy at repair-shop prices?

The example below uses the US CPC for "auto repair shop near me" and the costs of the two most expensive terms. Every input except the CPC is an assumption, so swap in your own figures.

- **Assumption:** monthly budget of $600.
- **Assumption:** conversion rate from click to call or booking request of 10%.
- **Assumption:** 40% of those leads become paid jobs.
- **Sourced CPC, "auto repair shop near me":** $4.29.

Clicks: $600 ÷ $4.29 ≈ 140 clicks. Leads: 140 × 10% ≈ 14. Booked jobs: 14 × 40% ≈ 6. Cost per lead ≈ $42.90, and cost per booked job ≈ $107.

Now the same budget on "auto repair shops near me" at $9.01: about 67 clicks, 7 leads and 3 jobs, roughly $225 per booked job under the same assumptions. The formula is CPC ÷ (conversion rate × booking rate). Compare that cost with what one job earns you before you choose the expensive term over the cheaper one.

## How do you check the numbers before you commit?

Work through these steps before any budget is set. Each takes minutes and prevents a month of paying for the wrong clicks.

1. Pull monthly volume and CPC for each keyword in Keyword Planner, filtered to your town and radius.
2. Run each keyword in a private search window and record what appears: an AI Overview, a map pack or only organic links.
3. Assign every keyword to one service page or ad group, and keep body work and tyres apart from core repair.
4. Calculate cost per booked job using the formula above, with your real conversion and booking figures.
5. Review the search terms report weekly and add negatives from real searches.

## FAQ

### What is a typical CPC for auto repair keywords?

Third-party data shows "auto repair" at $3.24 and "auto repair shop near me" at $4.29 for US searches, while "auto repair shops near me" reaches $9.01. The broader Auto industry averages $2.46 per search click in a WordStream benchmark, but that includes dealers and parts sellers, so it is not a repair figure.

### Should an auto shop bid on "auto parts" keywords?

Usually not, unless you sell parts. "Nearest auto parts store" carries 27,100 US monthly searches at $1.61, but most of those searchers want a product rather than a repair. Add parts terms as negatives first, then test only if your shop actually supplies parts.

### How much should an auto shop spend on Google Ads?

There is no universal figure. Start with what one booked job is worth to you, then use the cost-per-booked-job formula in the worked example to see whether a given monthly budget produces jobs at a profit.

### Are the keyword figures in this article exact?

No. The volumes and CPCs come from third-party tools that estimate US demand, and the data will differ for your city and your bids. Treat them as starting points and confirm each keyword in Keyword Planner.
