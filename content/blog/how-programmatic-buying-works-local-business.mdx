Programmatic buying is the automated purchase of ad space. Each ad slot is offered in a real-time auction, and software decides which website, app or video service shows the ad and what the advertiser pays. For a local business, the buyer is usually a platform account rather than a media rep, so the work shifts to choosing the audience, setting the budget and judging the results. Some vendors say campaigns can start at small amounts, but no platform guarantees results at any size.

This guide covers how a single impression is bought, which platforms let a small team buy, how cost is measured for video and TV, and a worked test budget you can adapt.

## How Does One Ad Get Bought in Real Time?

When someone opens a page or app with an ad slot, the publisher offers that slot to an exchange along with basic signals such as location and device. Buyers' systems, called demand-side platforms, decide whether to bid for that person and how much. The highest bid wins and its ad loads. The whole sequence happens while the page loads, which is why buyers talk about impressions rather than placements.

For a small business, the practical steps look like this:

1. Pick a platform that buys programmatically on your behalf.
2. Define who you want to reach, such as people within a set distance of your store.
3. Set a daily or total budget and a single goal, such as enquiries or visits.
4. Upload creative sized for the formats the platform supports.
5. Let the system bid, then review placements and results on a fixed schedule.

Most of the real decisions happen in steps 2 and 3. Software handles the auction; your choices determine whether the auction buys anyone worth reaching.

## Which Platforms Let a Small Business Buy Programmatically?

The main options are self-serve demand-side platforms, ad products inside large platforms, and managed services run by agencies. Amazon's guidance says its DVA+ product combines what used to run through Sponsored TV, Sponsored Display and programmatic buying into one campaign covering streaming TV, online video and display, with audio coming soon, as [Canopy Management describes it](https://canopymanagement.com/amazon-streaming-tv-mid-market/). Check the live product pages before committing, because availability and minimums change.

Take10 Media states that its self-serve Ad Portal can be used for instant digital buying 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, which is aimed at local out-of-home campaigns. Adwave says programmatic infrastructure serves campaigns starting at $50, a figure from a vendor page rather than an independent benchmark.

Demand is also moving toward programmatic. Netflix told investors on its first-quarter 2026 earnings call that programmatic buying was on course to exceed 50 percent of its non-live advertising business, [as PPC Land reported](https://ppc.land/netflix-opens-250m-user-inventory-to-trade-desk-buyers-drops-spend-minimums/). For a local advertiser, the lesson is that more inventory is becoming available through automated buying, but access still depends on each platform's rules.

## How Is Cost Measured for Video and TV Ads?

Display and video are usually priced per thousand impressions, known as CPM. Connected TV is often bought on different terms. Amazon describes cost per completed view (CPCV), which measures the price of each completed ad, and says it is used alongside video completion rate (VCR) in programmatic pricing. [Amazon's connected TV guide](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/connected-tv-advertising) is the primary reference for those definitions.

Pricing models differ by seller. The TV Agency says Sky Advance, Sky's programmatic platform in the UK, works on a CPM basis, [as described on its UK connected TV page](https://thetvagency.com/insights/connected-tv-ads-in-the-uk/). Ask any vendor which metric you are paying for, and whether a completed view means a full-length play or a percentage of the ad. Comparing a CPM quote with a CPCV quote directly tells you very little.

Local TV is a different buying model. The [local TV advertising cost guide](/blog/local-tv-advertising-cost-small-business) covers that side, and [geofencing](/blog/geofencing-advertising-local-business-guide) is one way to focus programmatic spend on a service area.

## What Does a Test Budget Look Like?

No platform in this guide publishes a reliable typical CPM for small advertisers, so cost has to be learned through testing. The table below shows the arithmetic with made-up inputs. Every input is an assumption and should be replaced with your own numbers after the first week.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget | $500 over 30 days |
| CPM | $6 |
| Impressions | about 83,333 |
| Click-through rate | 0.5% |
| Clicks | about 417 |
| Landing page conversion rate | 3% |
| Enquiries | about 12.5 |
| Cost per enquiry | about $40 |

If the CPM were $10 instead, the same budget would buy about 50,000 impressions, around 250 clicks and about 7.5 enquiries, or roughly $67 per enquiry (assumption). A small change in the auction price moves the outcome a lot, which is why the first week should check CPM and click-through rate before you scale.

A simple test plan:

1. Set one goal, such as calls or booked appointments, and confirm it is tracked before launch.
2. Cap spend at an amount you can lose without strain.
3. Target one area and one audience at a time, so results are readable.
4. Review at the end of week one and again at day 30.
5. Change one variable at a time, either creative or audience, never both.

## Does Programmatic Beat Other Options for Local Reach?

Vendor case studies suggest it can, under the right conditions. AIDigital reports that HP's Trade Desk campaign had a cost per unique household 23 percent below HP's previous campaign, which had used programmatic guaranteed and private marketplace buying. That is a single vendor-reported result from 2024, not a benchmark for a local shop, so treat it as a reason to test rather than a forecast.

Programmatic also reaches people on sites and apps rather than in search results. The [in-app advertising guide](/blog/in-app-advertising-local-business-guide) explains how mobile inventory differs from web display. Agency guidance such as [GreenBananaSEO's CTV page](https://greenbananaseo.com/ctv-advertising/) lists targeted reach, real-time optimization, cost-effectiveness and scalability as reasons programmatic is useful, but those are claims to verify in your own account, not guarantees.

## When Should a Local Business Hold Off?

Programmatic is a reach tool. If your offer, tracking or reputation is weak, a wider audience will not fix that. A 2018 survey cited by ReferralRock found that 97 percent of consumers look for reviews and recommendations before buying online, [per PowerReviews via ReferralRock](https://referralrock.com/affiliate/affiliate-marketing-for-small-business/). That figure is dated, but the principle holds: people who arrive from an ad will check your profile.

Hold off if you cannot yet measure enquiries, if your Google Business Profile is incomplete or if your reviews are thin. Start with a clear offer and working tracking, then test programmatic in one area. ## FAQ

### What is programmatic buying in simple terms?

It is ad buying done by software. Each ad slot is offered in an automated auction, and the winning ad loads on the page or app in the moment.

### Can a small business start with a low budget?

Some vendors say campaigns can start at small amounts; Adwave states $50 as a starting point. Start with a capped test and judge cost per enquiry rather than impressions alone.

### How is programmatic different from Facebook ads?

Meta's ads run inside Meta's own apps. Programmatic ads run across many websites, apps and video services, with placements chosen by an auction.

### Do I need a customer list to use programmatic?

No. Many platforms also target by location and context. Check each platform's live help pages for the audience options available in your region and plan.
