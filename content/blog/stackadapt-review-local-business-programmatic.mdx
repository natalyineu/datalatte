StackAdapt can work for local businesses, but it is rarely the first platform to try. It is a programmatic platform that buys native, display, video, audio and connected TV placements across the web ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/platform), checked Oct 2026). That makes it good for awareness and retargeting, but typically less efficient than Google Ads for reaching people who are already searching. It suits businesses with a higher average customer value, a decent monthly budget and conversion tracking already in place. StackAdapt's fees and any minimums are quote-based, so ask for current terms.

## What StackAdapt Is

Programmatic advertising automates buying ad space in real time. StackAdapt is a demand-side platform. Its site lists native, display, video, audio, connected TV, digital out-of-home and in-game inventory, with targeting by audience, context and location, and activation of first-party audiences ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/platform)). It is aimed at agencies and marketers who want more reach than search ads alone can deliver.

## Pros and Cons for Local Businesses

**Pros**

- Wide reach across native, display, video and CTV in one platform.
- Contextual and audience targeting, plus retargeting of site visitors.
- Ability to upload first-party lists such as email subscribers, where you have consent.
- Useful for awareness before a high-consideration purchase.

**Cons**

- Typically more complex than Google Ads or Meta Ads, with a steeper learning curve.
- Local targeting is mostly country, state, city or DMA level. In the US and Canada you can upload a list of ZIP or postal codes, and radius targeting around addresses or coordinates exists but is described for mobile in-app environments ([StackAdapt geotargeting guide](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/mapping-out-your-geotargeting-strategy), checked Oct 2026). Expect less precision than Google Ads location targeting.
- Needs enough budget and time for the algorithm to learn. Minimums vary, so confirm with the vendor.
- Awareness-style clicks are often less likely to convert than search clicks.

<Callout type="warning">Programmatic usually costs more per conversion than search for businesses with local, high-intent demand. Capture people who are already searching first, then use programmatic to reach new audiences.</Callout>

## What Does StackAdapt Cost?

Pricing is mostly CPM-based (cost per thousand impressions), and CPMs vary a lot by format, targeting and market. Native and display CPMs typically sit lower than video and CTV. Platform fees, minimum budgets and data costs can also apply, depending on how you buy (self-serve or through an agency). Request current numbers from StackAdapt or your agency instead of relying on averages from articles.

To estimate a test, take your target number of impressions, divide by 1,000, multiply by the quoted CPM, and then estimate click-through and conversion rates from your own data. If the math does not give you a customer at a cost you can afford, skip the test.

## How to Structure a First Campaign

1. **Set a single goal.** Calls, bookings or a lead form, not "awareness" in general.
2. **Layer audiences carefully.** For a coffee shop, you might combine interest in specialty coffee with a tight local geography. For a salon, combine beauty interests with ZIP-code targeting.
3. **Schedule by time of day if your setup allows it.** Run ads when your audience is likely to act, such as mornings for a cafe or evenings for a fitness studio.
4. **Retarget your site visitors.** Retargeting is usually the most efficient programmatic use for small businesses. Make sure your tracking is installed first; see [Facebook Pixel vs Google Tag](/blog/facebook-pixel-vs-google-tag-which-to-install-first).
5. **Send traffic to a focused landing page** with a single call to action.
6. **Test for at least a few weeks** before deciding. Programmatic campaigns need time to learn.

## Who Should and Should Not Use It

| Business | Fit |
|----------|-----|
| High-ticket local services (hair colour, bridal, fitness memberships) | Possible, especially for retargeting |
| Pet groomers with repeat customers | Possible for local awareness if tracking is in place |
| Coffee shops with low ticket sizes | Usually hard to justify on cost alone |
| Appointment businesses with weak tracking | Fix tracking first |

Think about customer lifetime value: if a new customer is worth a few hundred dollars over time, programmatic awareness may pay back. If a sale is a few dollars and rarely repeats, it probably will not.

## Pre-Launch Checklist

Before you spend on programmatic, make sure these are in place:

1. **Tracking works.** Conversions are recorded for calls, bookings or form submissions.
2. **A landing page matches the ad.** The offer on the page should match what the ad promised.
3. **A frequency cap is set, if the campaign type supports one,** so the same person is not shown your ad too many times in a short period.
4. **Brand-safety and fraud settings are reviewed.** Ask what viewability and invalid-traffic tools are available and whether they add cost.
5. **You know your target cost per lead,** based on what a customer is worth over time.
6. **You have a review date.** Pick a point at which you will pause, adjust or scale.

## Our Take

For most small local businesses, the order of investment is: a complete Google Business Profile, search ads for people ready to buy, Meta ads for local awareness and retargeting, and then programmatic platforms such as StackAdapt if you have a larger budget and a higher customer value. Programmatic is a precision tool, not a shortcut. If you cannot yet say what a new customer is worth, fix that before testing new channels.

## FAQ

**Q: Is StackAdapt good for local businesses?**
A: It can be, particularly for retargeting and awareness in a defined area, but most local businesses should get search and Meta campaigns working first.

**Q: Can StackAdapt target a small radius around my business?**
A: StackAdapt supports city, DMA and (in the US and Canada) ZIP or postal-code lists, plus address-based radius targeting that it describes for mobile in-app environments. Confirm what is available for your inventory, and expect less precision than Google Ads location targeting.

**Q: How much do I need to spend to get useful data?**
A: Enough to produce a meaningful number of clicks and conversions over several weeks. Ask StackAdapt for current minimums and set a test budget you can afford to lose.

**Q: Can I upload my email list for targeting?**
A: StackAdapt says you can activate first-party audiences and connect CRM data ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/platform)), but you need appropriate consent and compliance with advertising and privacy rules.

**Q: How long should I run a test?**
A: A few weeks at minimum, so the platform has time to learn and you can compare cost per lead against your other channels.
