## Why does the Google Map pack matter for a fitness studio?

The map pack is the block of local results that appears above standard web listings for many "near me" searches. Google's own help material says that a Business Profile displays business hours, phone numbers, addresses, reviews and photos directly in search results, so a prospect can decide to call or visit without clicking through to a website. That makes the profile itself a booking surface, not just a directory entry.

Industry data points in the same direction. In a BrightLocal survey cited by Ahrefs, the share of SEOs who rated Google Business Profile signals as the most important factor for the local pack rose from 23% in 2013 to 36% in 2021 (secondary). That is a survey of practitioners, not a measure of how Google weights signals, so treat it as a sign of where professional attention sits rather than a ranking formula.

Albuquerque has its own competitors in each category, and the realistic goal is to be stronger than the nearby studios that have not finished their profiles. Start by searching your core terms from a phone in a few neighbourhoods and noting which businesses appear.

## What should your Google Business Profile checklist include?

Work through the profile in this order. Each step is a plain action, and none depends on a number you have not verified.

1. Confirm the business name, address and phone number exactly as they appear on your website and signage.
2. Choose a primary category that matches your main service, then add secondary categories for specific class types, such as yoga, Pilates or personal training.
3. Write a description that names your service, your Albuquerque neighbourhood and one clear differentiator. Our [fitness studio GBP optimisation guide](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization) walks through each field.
4. Upload at least 15 authentic photos, covering the training floor, class sessions, coaches, changing areas and the entrance. Vertical Impression recommends this minimum in its gym marketing guide (secondary).
5. Publish posts on a regular schedule. Google's help page says a post appears publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live, so check that each post has passed review and is showing.
6. Seed the Q&A section with the questions prospects ask most, such as trial pricing, class times and beginner friendliness.

Some studios also use AI tools to draft descriptions and post ideas. The [AI for GBP optimisation](/blog/ai-for-google-business-profile-optimization) article covers where that helps and where a human should check the output.

## Which keywords and categories should you target?

Keyword targeting for a studio is mostly about matching the wording prospects use in a search box. The example from the Association of Fitness Studios is "personal training studios near me" (primary to that article), so build your page titles and service descriptions around pairs such as "[class type] in Albuquerque" and "[class type] near me."

Use one page per major class type where you can. A separate page for "Hot Yoga in Albuquerque" or "Beginner Pilates in Albuquerque" gives each service its own target phrase, and each page should describe the class, the schedule, the trial offer and the location. Avoid stuffing the city name into every sentence. Read the page aloud and cut any phrase that sounds unnatural.

## How do citations and NAP consistency fit in?

Citations are listings of your name, address and phone number on other websites. Consistency matters more than volume: a studio that shows "Ave" on one listing and "Avenue" on another creates conflicting data. Google's Ads help confirms a link between profile data and paid placements. Location asset data used in Google Ads is sourced directly from Google Maps and Google Business Profile, and corrections made there flow through to linked accounts (primary). Fix the profile first, then audit the directories your customers already use.

Google Ads also allows up to 10 locations to be selected from Google Maps for linking, when GBP and chain-store options do not work. Those locations must belong to you or they may be disapproved (primary). This matters if you operate more than one studio in the Albuquerque area.

A free citation audit tool can list inconsistencies in one pass. Correct the existing listings before adding new ones, because duplicate or conflicting listings make the underlying data harder to trust.

## How many reviews do you need, and what does the evidence show?

Reviews are shown next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search, according to Google's guidance on reviews (primary). That means each review is a visible trust signal for a prospect deciding between two studios.

You will often see specific review thresholds quoted online. One landscaping site claims that businesses with over 50 reviews and a 4.7+ average dominate top map pack spots, but it cites no source and is not fitness-specific, so treat that figure as unverified. The same site describes a landscaping firm that gained 18% more local pack appearances after cleaning up its NAP data. That is a single anecdote, not a controlled result.

A practical approach is to ask for reviews consistently after classes or trials, mention your class types in your replies, and respond to each review. Ask customers to describe what they valued, such as a beginner-friendly coach or a flexible schedule, because that wording helps prospects who search for the same thing.

## Worked example: what is a map pack gain worth?

The figures below are assumptions for a single studio, not benchmarks. Replace them with your own numbers from enquiries, trials and membership sales.

Inputs (all assumption):
- Extra enquiries per month from a stronger profile: 12
- Share of enquiries that book a trial: 35%, giving about 4 trials
- Share of trials that join: 50%, giving 2 new members
- Monthly membership price: $99
- Average months a member stays in the first six months: 6
- Staff time for photos, posts and review replies: 4 hours per month at $35 per hour

Calculation:
- First six months of revenue from one month's new members: 2 × $99 × 6 = $1,188
- Monthly labour cost: 4 × $35 = $140
- Net first-cohort value: $1,188 − $140 = $1,048

If the gain holds for a month and the members stay, the result compounds. If the enquiries do not appear, the labour cost still applies, which is why tracking enquiries and trials each month matters before you commit to a larger budget.

## How should you track whether the work is paying off?

Set your baseline before you change anything. Record how many profile views, calls, direction requests and website visits you get each month, and log the enquiries and trials that come from them. Use Google Search Console to see which pages earn clicks for your class terms.

Check your map pack position for your top few terms every week, in a private browser window and from a location near your studio, and note the results. Rankings shift with location and time, so a single check is not a reliable measure.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for fitness studios?

Yes. Google describes its Business Profile as a free tool that lets businesses manage their presence across Google Search and Maps (primary). Paid options such as Google Ads are separate.

### How many photos should my studio profile have?

Vertical Impression recommends at least 15 authentic photos showing the facility, classes and staff (secondary). Update them as your space and class types change.

### Do posts on my profile show to everyone straight away?

Not necessarily. Google's help page says a post is shown publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Posts that are pending or not approved do not appear, so check the status after publishing.

### Can I link more than one studio location to Google Ads?

Google Ads allows you to select up to 10 locations from Google Maps for linking when standard GBP or chain-store options do not work. The locations must be yours, or they may be disapproved (primary).

### Should I rely on map pack rankings alone?

No. Map pack visibility is one channel. Track enquiries and trials as well, and consider paid search for terms where you need faster results, as covered in the [fitness studio Google Ads guide](/blog/google-ads-for-fitness-studios-complete-2026-guide).
