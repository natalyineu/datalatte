A local business can use Telegram as a free broadcast channel for offers, schedule changes and updates, plus a business account that answers messages when you are closed. It only pays off if your customers already use Telegram. Telegram does not publish how many people use it in your town, and Pew's US survey does not list it, so ask your customers before you build anything.

This guide covers what Telegram offers businesses, how to set up a channel, and how to measure it. Every platform fact comes from Telegram's own pages, listed at the end. The example numbers are assumptions.

## What Can a Telegram Channel Do for a Local Business?

Telegram says channels are a tool for broadcasting to unlimited audiences. Only admins post, and each post reaches subscribers as a notification. Public channels get a web link that works without the app and can be found by search engines. Private channels use invite links.

Useful features for a shop or studio: scheduled posts, polls, pinned messages, hashtags to find older posts, reactions, and analytics with growth and post-view graphs. Telegram says subscriber lists are visible only to the owner and admins. View counters are approximate.

Comments are optional. You can attach a discussion group, and subscribers then see a comment button under each post.

## What Does Telegram Business Add?

Telegram Business turns an ordinary account into a business account. According to Telegram's announcement it includes business hours and a map location, an intro shown in empty chats, quick replies, greeting messages, away messages, chat links, chatbots and chat labels. Telegram states these are free for Premium subscribers for now.

Chat links are the most practical for tracking. You create a link that opens a chat with you and pre-fills a message, and Telegram shows how many times each link was tapped. Put a different link on each flyer, Instagram bio or window sticker and you can see which one brings people in.

## How Do You Set Up a Channel in One Afternoon?

1. Create a public channel with your business name as the link, so it is easy to read out loud.
2. Add a short description: what you post, how often, and your opening hours.
3. Invite your first subscribers by hand. Telegram lets the owner invite the first 200 subscribers directly.
4. Pin one post with your booking link and location.
5. Add a QR code to your receipts, counter and window that opens the channel.
6. Post on a fixed rhythm, such as twice a week, and schedule posts ahead.

Do not post every day. A channel sends a notification with every post, so a slow rhythm keeps it welcome.

## What Should You Post?

Post things a customer would otherwise have to ask about. Telegram supports polls and quiz polls, so a café can ask which seasonal drink to bring back, and a groomer can run a quiz on coat care.

- Last-minute openings, with a booking link
- Holiday and weather closures
- New stock or the day's menu
- A poll that decides something real, such as next month's class time

Skip anything you would not put on a shop window. Photos, short videos and albums all work. Our [social media guide for hair salons](/blog/social-media-marketing-for-hair-salons) shows what local customers respond to.

## How Do You Measure It? (Worked Example)

All numbers below are **assumptions**, not benchmarks. A hair salon runs a channel for one month.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Subscribers | 200 |
| Posts in the month | 8 |
| Taps on the booking chat link per post | 5 |
| Taps in the month | 40 |
| Share of taps that book | 25% |
| Bookings | 10 |
| Average ticket | 70 dollars |
| Revenue | 700 dollars |
| Staff time | 3 hours |

Replace every row with your own counts after the first month. Telegram's chat-link tap counter gives you the taps. Ask each new client how they booked to get the rest. If taps are near zero after two months, your customers are probably elsewhere.

## Telegram, WhatsApp or Something Else?

Pew's 2026 US survey of adults found WhatsApp use at 26% and does not list Telegram. DataReportal's European reach tables also omit both. So for local customers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada, the safe starting point is a free [WhatsApp Business account](/blog/whatsapp-business-marketing-local-business) or [WhatsApp for customer service](/blog/how-to-use-whatsapp-business-for-customer-service), and Telegram is a test.

Telegram may fit better if you serve a community that already uses it, such as expats, students or a language group. Paid options exist too: our [Telegram sponsored messages guide](/blog/telegram-sponsored-messages-cost-setup-guide-2026) explains pricing and setup.

## FAQ

### Is Telegram marketing free?

Channels are free to create and have no subscriber cap, according to Telegram. Telegram Business features are currently free for Premium subscribers. Paid advertising on Telegram is a separate product.

### Can customers see who subscribed to my channel?

No. Telegram says the subscriber list is visible only to the owner and admins.

### How many subscribers do I need for statistics?

Telegram says advanced statistics are available once a channel has more than 500 subscribers. Below that you still get view counters, which Telegram describes as approximate.

### Do Telegram sponsored messages show in my channel?

Telegram says sponsored messages appear in some countries in one-to-many channels with more than 1,000 subscribers. Premium subscribers do not see them.

### Is Telegram better than Instagram for a small shop?

Not for most. Instagram helps people find you. A Telegram channel helps people who already know you stay in touch.
