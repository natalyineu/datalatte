For most small local businesses, DV360 (Display and Video 360) is not worth it. Google positions it as end-to-end campaign management for enterprises, publishes no price list, and charges a fee calculated as a percentage of media cost under a contract. A coffee shop, salon or studio spending a few thousand dollars a month can reach much of the same display and YouTube inventory through Google Ads with less setup.

This guide explains what DV360 is, what Google says about its fees, when it could fit a multi-location business, and what to use instead. Where Google does not publish a number, the article says so. Worked examples label their inputs as assumptions.

## What Is DV360?

DV360 is Google's demand-side platform (DSP) for buying programmatic inventory across many publishers, including YouTube and third-party exchanges. Google's product page describes it as "end-to-end campaign management for enterprises in one tool" for organizations with separate creative, analytics, TV and digital teams ([Google Marketing Platform](https://marketingplatform.google.com/about/display-video-360/)). Getting started goes through "Talk to Sales".

If you spend a modest amount on [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and wonder whether DV360 is the next step, the usual answer is no. Google's help page says Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, plus YouTube and Gmail ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404190?hl=en)). For the basics of how DSPs work, read our [DSP vs SSP explainer](/blog/dsp-vs-ssp-explained-local-business).

## What Does DV360 Cost?

Google does not publish a price. Its help page says the platform fee is calculated as a percentage of the media cost and that the rates are defined in the contract. You find the applied rate in partner settings once you have an account ([Google DV360 Help](https://support.google.com/displayvideo/answer/16272705?hl=en)). Pricing is therefore not public, and a quote is the only reliable figure.

Other costs sit on top:

- **Agency or reseller fees.** Google's help describes partners as agencies, trading desks or large individual advertisers, and a partner must show at least five local full-time experts to be certified ([Google DV360 Help](https://support.google.com/displayvideo/answer/7622449)). Small advertisers usually go through such a partner, who sets their own fees and minimums.
- **Setup and management time.** Linking accounts, building insertion orders, trafficking creative and adding conversion tags takes work.
- **Data and other fees.** These can be added to media cost and should be itemized in a written quote.

<Callout type="warning">
DV360 is not a cheaper way to buy Google inventory. It adds a platform layer and, for small advertisers, usually an agency layer. Ask for a written breakdown of every fee before you commit.
</Callout>

## How Much of Your Budget Reaches the Ad?

The percentages below are **assumptions to show the method, not Google's rates**. Suppose you hand an agency $2,000 a month and the platform fee takes 15% and the agency fee 15% of spend.

| Item | Amount (assumption) |
|---|---|
| Monthly budget | $2,000 |
| Platform fee at 15% | $300 |
| Agency fee at 15% | $300 |
| Left for media | $1,400 |

On those inputs only 70% of the budget buys impressions, against a Google Ads Display campaign where the whole budget is media (plus whatever you pay a manager). Ask any DV360 reseller for the real figures, then run this table with them.

## When Can DV360 Make Sense?

It is worth a conversation in a few cases:

- **Several locations with one shared budget.** One platform can manage geo-targeted campaigns by location.
- **A large first-party data set.** If you hold many customer records collected with consent, you can build suppression lists and audiences.
- **High-ticket services with long decisions.** Sustained video and display retargeting can suit practices with long consideration cycles.
- **Cross-channel measurement needs.** A DSP can consolidate reporting, if someone can read the data.

A single cafe, a two-chair salon or a small studio rarely meets these conditions.

## FAQ

### Can a small business get a DV360 account?
Google directs interested advertisers to its sales team, and partners are agencies, trading desks or large advertisers. Smaller businesses normally go through an agency or reseller, who may set their own minimums.

### Is DV360 the same as Google Ads?
No. Google Ads is self-serve and strongest for search and simple display and video. DV360 is an enterprise DSP for programmatic buying across many publishers.

### What is the cheapest way to run programmatic-style ads locally?
Usually Google Ads Display or YouTube with remarketing. Compare total fees, not just the headline rate.

### How much should I spend before considering DV360?
Google publishes no threshold. If you do not already run several channels with healthy budgets and clear tracking, you are unlikely to benefit yet.

### What should I do first if I want more customers now?
Tighten your Google Business Profile, run Search campaigns for high-intent queries and add remarketing. For a wider view, read our [programmatic advertising guide](/guides/programmatic-advertising-guide).
