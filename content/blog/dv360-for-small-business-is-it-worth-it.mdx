For most small local businesses, DV360 (Display & Video 360) is not worth it. It is Google's enterprise programmatic platform, built for agencies and large advertisers running big, multi-channel budgets, and access, fees and setup effort rarely make sense for a coffee shop, salon or studio spending a few thousand dollars a month. The exceptions are multi-location brands with serious budgets and first-party data.

## What Is DV360, and Should Your Small Business Care?

DV360 is Google's demand-side platform (DSP). It lets advertisers buy display, video, audio and connected TV inventory across many publishers from one place, with audience targeting, frequency control and cross-channel reporting. If you want the basics first, read our [guide to demand-side platforms](/blog/demand-side-platform-guide-small-business).

If you are spending a modest amount on [Google Ads](/services/google-ads) and wondering whether DV360 is the next step, the usual answer is no. You can reach much of the same display and YouTube inventory through Google Ads itself, with far less setup.

## What DV360 Costs

Exact pricing depends on how you access it, so treat any figure you read (including ours) as a rough guide and ask for a written quote.

- **Platform fee:** Google has historically charged a percentage of media spend for DV360. Check the current rate with Google or your reseller, because it has changed over time.
- **Access:** Many small advertisers cannot get direct access. Most go through an agency or reseller, who add their own management fee or minimum spend.
- **Setup and management:** Linking accounts, building insertion orders and line items, trafficking creative, and adding conversion tags (Floodlight) takes real time. Expect agency setup fees and ongoing optimisation hours.
- **Media cost:** The ad inventory itself, which is the part that should be most of your budget.

| Option | Typical minimums | Setup effort | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| DV360 | Often high; set by agency or reseller | Heavy | Large or multi-location advertisers |
| Google Ads (Search, Display, YouTube, Performance Max) | Flexible; you set your own budget | Light | Most local businesses |
| Local programmatic platforms (for example Simpli.fi, AdRoll) | Varies; often modest | Moderate | Geo-targeted display and retargeting |
| Local Services Ads | Pay per lead, availability depends on category | Light | Eligible service businesses |

Our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business) explains how to size a realistic starting budget.

<Callout type="warning">
DV360 is not a cheaper way to buy Google inventory. It adds a platform layer and, usually, agency costs. It earns its keep only when you need its control, scale and measurement.
</Callout>

## When DV360 Can Make Sense for a Local Business

There are a few situations where it is worth a conversation:

- **Several locations with a shared budget.** One platform can manage geo-targeted campaigns per location and unify retargeting.
- **A strong first-party data set.** If you have thousands of customer records (with proper consent), you can build suppression lists and lookalike-style audiences.
- **Long consideration cycles and high-ticket services.** Dental, cosmetic and similar practices sometimes want sustained video and display retargeting.
- **Cross-channel measurement needs.** If you run several channels and need consolidated reporting, a DSP can help, as long as you have someone who can read the data.

A single café, a two-chair salon or a small studio will almost never meet these conditions.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Treating DV360 like Google Search ads.** DV360 is for display, video, audio and connected TV, not keyword search. Intent-based searches ("barber near me") are better handled in Google Ads.

**Skipping conversion tracking.** Without Floodlight or Google Ads/GA4 conversions imported, the bidding system has nothing to optimise toward. Set up [analytics and tracking](/services/analytics) first.

**Over-narrow targeting.** Stacking geofences, demographics and weather triggers can shrink your audience until the campaign cannot deliver or learn. Start broad enough to gather data, then narrow.

**One creative for every format.** Display, video and connected TV need different assets. Plan for several sizes and at least one short video.

**Underestimating management time.** Programmatic needs regular monitoring of placements, frequency and invalid traffic. If no one has the time, results suffer.

## Smarter Options for Smaller Budgets

1. **Google Ads Display and YouTube campaigns.** Same family of inventory, simple setup, you control the budget. Use remarketing lists and in-market audiences.
2. **Programmatic platforms aimed at local advertisers.** Tools such as Simpli.fi or AdRoll offer geo-targeting and retargeting with lower setup barriers. Compare fees and minimums before committing.
3. **Local Services Ads or Search campaigns.** If customers search for what you do, capturing that demand first is usually more efficient than interrupting people.
4. **Connected TV through a local-friendly partner.** If you want TV-style reach, see our [CTV advertising service](/services/ctv-advertising).

## FAQ

**Q: Can a small business even get a DV360 account?**
A: Direct access is generally aimed at agencies and larger advertisers. Smaller businesses normally access it through an agency or reseller, who may set their own minimums.

**Q: Is DV360 the same as Google Ads?**
A: No. Google Ads is self-serve and strongest for search and simple display/video. DV360 is an enterprise DSP with more control over programmatic buying across many publishers.

**Q: What is the cheapest way to run programmatic-style ads locally?**
A: Usually Google Ads Display or YouTube with remarketing, or a local-focused programmatic platform with low minimums. Compare total fees, not just the headline rate.

**Q: How much should I spend before considering DV360?**
A: There is no official threshold, but if you are not already running several channels with healthy budgets and clear tracking, you are unlikely to benefit yet.

**Q: What should I do first if I want more customers now?**
A: Tighten your Google Business Profile, run Search campaigns for high-intent queries, and add remarketing.
