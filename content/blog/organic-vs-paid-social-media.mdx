For most local businesses the answer is both, in different roles: organic social builds trust and keeps existing customers engaged, while paid social puts your offer in front of new local people on a predictable schedule. If you have to choose one first, start organic to build a base, then add small paid campaigns for specific goals.

Organic reach on Facebook and Instagram has been limited for business pages for years, so relying on free posts alone rarely fills a calendar. Ads alone have the opposite weakness: if your profile looks abandoned, people who click through leave. This guide explains what each does, how to split your effort and what to measure.

## What each approach does

| | Organic social | Paid social |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Mostly your time, plus tools | Ad spend plus creative time |
| Speed | Slow, compounding | Fast, switches off when you stop paying |
| Reach | Mainly existing followers and some discovery | New people you select by location and interests |
| Best for | Trust, community, retention, showing personality | Promotions, launches, filling slots, retargeting |
| Control | Limited | Detailed targeting, budget, schedule |
| Measurement | Engagement, saves, shares, DMs | Cost per lead or booking, conversions |

## When organic social is the right focus

Organic content earns its keep when you want to:

- Show what it is like to visit or buy from you (staff, space, process)
- Build credibility with reviews, before and after photos and customer stories (with permission)
- Keep regulars engaged with events, news and updates
- Answer common questions in short posts and Stories
- Support your ads: people who see an ad often check your profile before acting

It works best with a simple routine. Pick two or three content themes, such as behind the scenes, customer highlights and offers, and a posting rhythm you can sustain. ## When paid social is the right focus

Paid social is useful when you need results on a schedule:

- Opening a new location or launching a service
- Promoting a limited-time offer, seasonal event or class intake
- Filling quiet days or empty appointment slots
- Reaching people who have never heard of you in a defined radius
- Retargeting people who visited your website or engaged with your content

You can target by location, age and, to a more limited degree today, by interests and behaviours. On Meta (Facebook and Instagram), campaigns are managed in Meta Ads Manager. See our [Instagram ads vs. Facebook ads comparison](/blog/instagram-ads-vs-facebook-ads) if you are deciding where to advertise.

## A simple decision guide

- **No audience, no budget**: Start organic. Post consistently for a few weeks, set up your profile properly and collect reviews.
- **Some audience, small budget**: Keep organic going and test one small paid campaign with a clear goal, such as booking a first appointment.
- **Established business with spare capacity**: Make paid social a monthly line item, with organic content supplying the creative and proof.
- **Time-poor, some budget**: Reduce organic to the essentials (weekly posts, Stories, replying to messages) and let ads do the heavy lifting.

## Suggested split of time and budget

There is no universal ratio, but as a starting point many small local businesses can think in terms of rough shares:

| Stage | Time on organic | Money on paid | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starting out | Most of your effort | Little or none | Profile, content habits, reviews |
| Growing | Steady routine | Small test budget, often $100 to $300 a month | One offer, one audience |
| Established | Maintained | A larger, tracked budget | Retargeting and scale |

These are guidelines, not rules. The right spend depends on your margins, capacity and how quickly customers convert. Our guide to [Facebook ads budgets for local business](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) explains how to work it out.

## How organic and paid work together

The two channels are strongest when connected:

1. **Test with organic**: Post a few variations and see which get the most saves, shares and comments.
2. **Promote the winners**: Boost or build ads from posts that clearly resonated.
3. **Retarget**: Show follow-up ads to people who watched videos, visited your site or engaged with your page.
4. **Capture**: Send them to a booking page, lead form or message with a clear offer.
5. **Retain**: Use organic posts, email and SMS to keep them coming back. See [email marketing for small business](/blog/email-marketing-for-small-business).

## What to measure

For organic, look at reach, saves, shares, profile visits and direct messages rather than only follower counts.

For paid, focus on business outcomes:

- Cost per lead, booking or sale
- Conversion rate on your landing page or form
- Calls and messages generated
- Return on ad spend, where you can track revenue

Use a simple tracking method such as a UTM-tagged link, a dedicated booking link, a promo code or a "How did you hear about us?" question. Our guide to [conversion tracking for Google Ads](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) explains the principles, which also apply to Meta ads.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Boosting every post without a goal
- Spending on ads while the profile has no recent posts, photos or reviews
- Targeting an area that is much larger than your customers will travel
- Changing campaigns every few days, before there is enough data
- Counting likes as success
- Ignoring messages and comments, which are often the real leads
- Posting only promotions

## A 30 day starter plan

**Week 1**: Update your profile, bio, link and highlights. Collect five to ten photos and short clips of real work.

**Week 2**: Post three to four times, use Stories daily, and ask recent customers for reviews.

**Week 3**: Pick one offer (a first class, a new customer discount or a seasonal special). Create a simple ad with a strong photo, a short message and a booking link or lead form. Target a radius around your location.

**Week 4**: Review what happened. Which ad, post or audience produced bookings? Keep the winner, drop the rest and decide next month's budget.

## Choosing a platform

You do not need to be everywhere. Facebook still reaches many older and family audiences and is strong for local groups and events. Instagram suits visual businesses such as food, beauty, fitness and pets. TikTok can reach younger local audiences through short video, though local conversion is harder to measure. Pick one primary platform where your customers already spend time, do it well, and add a second only when you can maintain both.

## FAQ

**Q: Is organic social media dead for businesses?**
A: No. Organic reach from business pages is limited, but profiles still matter because customers check them before buying, and organic content provides the proof and creative your ads need.

**Q: How much should a local business spend on social ads?**
A: It varies. Many start with a modest monthly test budget and scale once they know what a booking costs. Work backwards from what a new customer is worth.

**Q: Should I boost posts or use Ads Manager?**
A: Boosting is simple but limited. Ads Manager gives more control over objectives, placements, audiences and tracking, so it is usually better once you are past your first test.

**Q: How long before paid social shows results?**
A: Early signals can appear within days, but plan for a few weeks of testing before judging, since platforms need time to learn and you need enough data.
