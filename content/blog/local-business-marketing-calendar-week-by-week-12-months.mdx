A local marketing calendar has two layers: a weekly routine that never changes, and a short list of seasonal peaks you prepare for four to six weeks ahead. The routine is one review request batch, one profile or social update, one email or message, and a ten-minute numbers check. The peaks are the dates when US shoppers spend most, such as holidays, Valentine's Day and Mother's Day.

NRF forecasts 2026 holiday sales of $1.013 trillion, up 4.2%. These are national retail figures, so they show timing, not your sales. The calendar example is labelled as an assumption.

## What Goes Into a Weekly Routine?

Keep the routine to four tasks so it survives a busy month. Each week: ask recent customers for reviews with a Google link or QR code; post one update to your Business Profile or social page; send or schedule one message to your list; and check calls, directions and bookings.

Google says changes to a profile are reviewed before they go live, so update hours and details before a holiday, not on the day. Do not offer rewards for reviews, which Google's policy prohibits. ## Which Dates Deserve Extra Planning?

| Period | Date rule | What the data says | Prepare |

| Mother's Day | Second Sunday in May | NRF 2026: record $38B, $284.25 average; special outings $6.4B | Gift cards, booking slots |
| Back to school | Late summer | NRF 2026: K-12 spending $43.3B, up from $39.4B | Family offers, school-year memberships |
| Small Business Saturday | Saturday after Thanksgiving | Nov 28 in 2026 | Local offer, email, window signs |
| Holiday season | Nov 1 to Dec 31 | NRF: $1.013T, up 4.2% | Gift cards, extended hours, booking |

NRF's holiday number excludes car dealers, gas stations and restaurants, and its release says it focuses on core retail. A service business should read it as a sign that people are spending, not as a forecast for you.

## What Should Each Quarter Focus On?

Spread themes across the year so no month is empty:

- **Winter (Jan to Mar):** New-year routines, Valentine's offers, and a profile clean-up with fresh photos.
- **Spring (Apr to Jun):** Mother's Day gift cards, outdoor events, and a review drive.
- **Summer (Jul to Sep):** Slower months for many shops; use them for email list building and loyalty offers, then back-to-school promotions in late summer.
- **Autumn (Oct to Dec):** Holiday hours, Small Business Saturday and gift card sales.

These themes are a framework, not a research finding. Check your own sales history for your real busy and slow months, then place your effort accordingly. If budget is tight, our [zero-budget guide](/blog/local-business-marketing-when-you-have-zero-budget) lists free options.

## How Far Ahead Should You Prepare?

No source we read gives a standard lead time, so use a working rule: start planning four weeks before each peak, and finish creative two weeks before. Set special hours as soon as you know them, since Google reviews profile changes before they appear.

For paid ads, build campaigns early enough to test one week before the peak. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) shows how to reserve money for peaks. Keep a short checklist per peak: offer, dates, hours, creative, landing page, and the number you will compare afterward.

## What Does a Peak Plan Look Like? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions** for a nail salon preparing for Mother's Day, which falls on a Sunday in May.

| Weeks before | Task |
| --- | --- |
| 6 | Choose the offer (gift card with a small bonus) and set a cap |
| 5 | Update Business Profile: hours, gift card link, photos |
| 4 | Email the list; post the offer |
| 3 | Ask recent clients for reviews |
| 2 | Reminder email; post slots left |
| 1 | Final reminder; check bookings against capacity |
| After | Count gift cards sold and bookings; write down what to change |

The success measure is a number set before the peak, such as gift cards sold or booked appointments. Without it you cannot tell if the plan worked.

## FAQ

### Do I need a full-year marketing calendar?

A calendar helps you avoid last-minute work, but the weekly routine matters more than a detailed plan. Start with the routine and add the peaks that fit your trade.

### What are the biggest retail dates?

NRF's data points to the holiday season, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day and back to school. Check your own history for local events too.

### When is Small Business Saturday in 2026?

November 28, the Saturday after Thanksgiving, per press listings.

### How do I know the calendar works?

Compare calls, directions, bookings and sales in each peak with a normal period. Our KPI guide lists the numbers.

### Should I post every day?

No source we read sets a daily posting standard. Choose a rate you can keep up for a year.
