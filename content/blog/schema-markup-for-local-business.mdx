Google's LocalBusiness documentation lists two required properties: `name` and `address`. Everything else, from opening hours to a phone number, is recommended. So the real job is not "learn schema". It is to write one block of JSON-LD that matches your Google Business Profile, put it on one page, and check it with three free tools. This walkthrough does exactly that for a single-location business, start to finish.

If you want the bigger picture, including why rich snippets are never guaranteed and which schema features Google has retired, read our [guide to LocalBusiness schema and rich snippets](/blog/local-business-schema-markup-rich-snippets-google). This article is the hands-on companion: the file you paste, the edge cases in opening hours, and the testing routine. It is written for owners of cafés, salons, studios and shops in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Where something is my assumption rather than Google's documented behaviour, I say so.

## Before You Write Any Code: Gather Four Things

Schema describes facts that already exist on your page and your listing. Google's [structured data guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) say not to "mark up content that is not visible to readers of the page" and that structured data "must be a true representation of the page content." So collect the facts first:

1. **Exact business name, address and phone number**, copied from your Google Business Profile. Our [NAP consistency guide](/blog/nap-consistency-local-seo) explains why these should match everywhere.
2. **Opening hours**, including any split shifts and any closed days.
3. **Social and listing URLs** that genuinely belong to you (Instagram, Facebook, Yelp, your Business Profile).
4. **Coordinates**, if you want to add `geo`. Google says precision "must be at least 5 decimal places".

Put them in a note. That note is your single source of truth, and the JSON-LD is just the same facts in a machine-readable shape.

## The Template: A Coffee Shop, Line by Line

Here is a complete block for a made-up café. Every value is a placeholder. I chose `CafeOrCoffeeShop` because Google says to use "the most specific subtype", and [schema.org](https://schema.org/FoodEstablishment) lists CafeOrCoffeeShop under FoodEstablishment, alongside Bakery, BarOrPub, Restaurant and others.

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "CafeOrCoffeeShop",
  "@id": "https://www.example-cafe.com/#business",
  "name": "Example Corner Coffee",
  "url": "https://www.example-cafe.com/",
  "telephone": "+1-555-010-0199",
  "priceRange": "$$",
  "servesCuisine": "Coffee, pastries",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "45 Oak Avenue",
    "addressLocality": "Springfield",
    "addressRegion": "IL",
    "postalCode": "62701",
    "addressCountry": "US"
  },
  "geo": {
    "@type": "GeoCoordinates",
    "latitude": 39.80123,
    "longitude": -89.65012
  },
  "openingHoursSpecification": [
    {
      "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
      "dayOfWeek": ["Monday", "Tuesday", "Wednesday", "Thursday", "Friday"],
      "opens": "07:00",
      "closes": "17:00"
    },
    {
      "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
      "dayOfWeek": ["Saturday", "Sunday"],
      "opens": "08:00",
      "closes": "15:00"
    }
  ],
  "sameAs": [
    "https://www.instagram.com/example-corner-coffee",
    "https://www.facebook.com/example-corner-coffee"
  ]
}
```

What each part does:

- **`@context` and `@type`** tell parsers this is schema.org vocabulary and which kind of thing it is.
- **`@id`** is an optional label for this entity. Google's guidelines say you can "use `@id` to link related separate items", which becomes useful if you later add other blocks, such as an Article that points back to the business. It does not need to be a live page.
- **`name`, `address`** are the two required properties. Google's page says to "include as many properties as possible" inside the address.
- **`telephone`, `url`, `priceRange`, `geo`, `openingHoursSpecification`** are on Google's recommended list.
- **`servesCuisine`** is a FoodEstablishment property. Delete it if you are not a food business.
- **`sameAs`** is defined on schema.org as a "URL of a reference Web page that unambiguously indicates the item's identity". Use it for official profiles only. Google's Organization documentation describes it as a page "on another website with additional information about your organization", such as a social media or review profile.

One more food-business note. Google's page lists `menu` as the recommended property, while schema.org says `hasMenu` "replaces the older `menu` property". I have not tested how the validators treat each, so if you add a menu link, pick one and see what the tools report.

## Opening Hours: The Part Most People Get Wrong

Google's LocalBusiness page spells out the rules. Using them, here are the cases a small business actually meets.

**Weekdays and weekends differ.** Use one `OpeningHoursSpecification` per pattern, as in the template. Google says a single object or an array are both supported, and that `dayOfWeek` accepts short names like "Monday".

**Open past midnight.** A bar or late-night shop that closes at 3 a.m. on Saturday uses a single specification for that day. Google's example is Saturday 18:00 to 03:00. Schema.org agrees that if `closes` is earlier than `opens`, the hours are assumed to run into the next day.

**Open 24 hours.** Google says `opens` "00:00" and `closes` "23:59".

**Closed all day.** Google says to set both `opens` and `closes` to "00:00".

**Seasonal or holiday closures.** Google says to use `validFrom` and `validThrough` in YYYY-MM-DD format, and to leave them out for year-round hours. Here is how I would mark a one-day closure on Friday, 25 December 2026:

```json
{
  "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
  "dayOfWeek": "Friday",
  "opens": "00:00",
  "closes": "00:00",
  "validFrom": "2026-12-25",
  "validThrough": "2026-12-25"
}
```

That is my reading of the rules, not an example Google publishes. Google does not say how it resolves a one-day closure that overlaps your normal Friday hours, so treat this as an assumption, and keep the hours on your Business Profile as the source customers rely on. Remove expired closures after the date passes.

**Lunch break or split shift.** Add two specifications for the same days, one for each block. Google does not show a split-shift example, so this is again standard use of the array rather than a documented pattern.

## Where to Put It

Place the block on the page that describes the business, normally the home page or a contact page, inside a script tag in the `<head>` or `<body>`:

```html
<script type="application/ld+json">
  { ...your JSON-LD... }
</script>
```

Google recommends JSON-LD over Microdata and RDFa, and says all three formats are "equally fine for Google, as long as the markup is valid and properly implemented". For which platform lets you do that, see our companion piece on [schema by page type and CMS](/blog/schema-markup-local-business-website). If you have more than one location, Google says to define each one as its own LocalBusiness, covered in our [multi-location SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-for-multi-location-businesses).

## Validate in Three Steps

Google's [introduction to structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data) tells you to check markup with the Rich Results Test during development and to monitor pages with Search Console reports afterward. A routine that covers both:

1. **Rich Results Test.** Paste your code or live URL. Google's LocalBusiness page says to validate with it and "fix critical errors". Warnings about optional fields can wait.
2. **Schema Markup Validator** at validator.schema.org. It is a separate tool for general schema.org syntax. It is useful as a second opinion because Google's tools focus on the features Google supports; the URL Inspection documentation notes that unsupported rich result types "may be valid but won't appear in the tool".
3. **Search Console URL Inspection.** Inspect the live page, then use Test live URL. Google's help page says this fetches the page "in real time", and the Enhancements section lists detected rich results with valid items, warnings and errors. If the page is not indexed yet, you can request indexing, though Google states a request "does not guarantee that the page will appear in the Google Index". The page also gives two timeframes, "a day or so" in one place and "up to a week or two" in another, so budget a couple of weeks before judging.

Also confirm the page is not blocked by robots.txt, `noindex` or a login, since Google's LocalBusiness page lists those as reasons it cannot read your markup.

In Search Console's rich result status reports, an item is "valid" if it has no critical issues and "can appear on Google as a rich result". That wording is deliberate: eligible is not the same as shown.

## What to Leave Out

- **Self-written star ratings.** Google's review snippet documentation says pages about an entity that controls its own reviews, using LocalBusiness or other Organization markup, are ineligible for the star review feature. The LocalBusiness page limits `aggregateRating` and `review` to sites that capture reviews about other local businesses.
- **FAQPage markup for search results.** Google's changelog says the FAQ rich result would stop appearing from May 7, 2026, and that the documentation was removed on June 15, 2026.
- **Anything not on the page.** If hours or services are not visible to visitors, do not put them in the markup.

## Do This Today

1. Copy your exact name, address, phone, hours and social links from your Google Business Profile into a note.
2. Copy the template above, swap in your values, and choose the most specific schema.org subtype from the [LocalBusiness list](https://schema.org/LocalBusiness). Delete any line you cannot fill truthfully.
3. Paste the block into your home or contact page, or ask your web person to.
4. Run the Rich Results Test and the Schema Markup Validator on the live page, fix critical errors, then use URL Inspection in Search Console.
5. Add a quarterly reminder to compare schema, website and Business Profile, and remove expired holiday closures.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the minimum I need for LocalBusiness schema?**
A: Google's documentation lists `name` and `address` as required. Telephone, URL, hours, geo and price range are recommended.

**Q: Do I need `sameAs`?**
A: It is not required. Schema.org defines it as a URL of a reference page that identifies the item, and Google's Organization page names social and review profiles as examples. Only list profiles you actually own.

**Q: How do I show different hours on weekends?**
A: Add a second `OpeningHoursSpecification` object for those days, as in the template. Google says a single object or an array are both supported.

**Q: Will valid markup give me rich results?**
A: Not necessarily. Google says it "does not guarantee that features that consume structured data will show up in search results".

**Q: How often should I check it?**
A: After any change to hours, address, phone or website theme, and at least quarterly. Search Console's reports help you catch breakage after site changes.
