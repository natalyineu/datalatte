## Why Do Before-and-After Videos Suit a Salon?

The result is visual, and the change happens in seconds. A viewer sees the starting point, the work and the outcome without reading anything. That makes the format easy to follow with the sound off, and it gives the viewer a reason to watch to the end.

It also gives a salon proof without making claims. Show the real hair, the service name and the approximate time the service took. Avoid editing that changes the colour or length, because the client will compare the video with the result in the chair. Consent and converting questions are covered in more detail in [before-and-after photos, legal and converting](/blog/hair-salon-before-after-photos-legal-and-converting).

Demand for the work is also steady. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects employment of barbers, hairstylists and cosmetologists to grow 7% from 2025 to 2035, much faster than the average for all occupations. Competition for local clients will keep rising with that growth, so a clear, repeatable video habit helps a salon stand out.

## How Do You Set Up the Account?

TikTok advises anyone promoting a business to use a Business Account, according to [TikTok's account guidance](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/growing-your-audience/switching-to-a-creator-or-business-account) (secondary, as summarised in the source article). Business Accounts can place a website link on the profile, so link to the booking page rather than the homepage. Business Accounts also only have access to Commercial Sounds, the library cleared for commercial use, instead of the wider music library.

Fill the profile with the salon name, city, opening hours and the services offered, such as colour, cuts and extensions. Pin the three strongest transformations so a new visitor sees proof straight away. Then post on a steady schedule for at least a few weeks before judging the account.

Keep the Google profile aligned as well. A listing that names the same city and services as the captions makes it easier for local searchers to match a video to the business. The steps are set out in the [hair salon local SEO guide](/blog/hair-salon-local-seo-guide).

## What Consent and Disclosure Rules Apply?

Get written permission before posting a client's face or hair. Keep a simple form that records the client's name, the date, what may be posted and where, and how the client can withdraw consent. This protects the salon if a client later changes their mind.

If a salon gives a free or discounted service in return for a video, the Federal Trade Commission treats that as a material connection that must be disclosed. Its guidance says terms like "ad" or "sponsored" are generally enough, while vague shorthand such as "collab" is not. The disclosure should appear in the video itself, not only in the description ([FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers), secondary). The same rule applies when a local creator films a service in exchange for a booking, which is covered in [influencer marketing for hair salons](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-hair-salons-how-to-get-free-bookings-from-local-creator).

These rules are US guidance. Salons in the UK, Australia or Canada should check their own advertising regulators before posting, because the wording and enforcement can differ.

## How Do You Structure a Transformation Video?

A repeatable structure saves filming time and makes each video easier to compare:

1. First second: the "before" shot with a one-line text overlay, such as "Level 5 to blonde".
2. Middle: two or three quick process shots.
3. End: the "after" from the same angle and in the same light as the "before".
4. Caption: the service, the city and how to book.

Film in the same spot with the same lighting each time. Mention the real time the service took, and if the result needed two sessions, say so in the caption. Honest captions reduce the chance that a client feels misrepresented in the chair.

Drafting captions and text overlays can be quicker with a template. The [blog content strategy for local business](/blog/blog-content-strategy-local-business) explains how to plan a month of posts so the videos follow one theme.

## Should You Pay to Boost a Video?

Boost only after a video has performed well organically. TikTok says Spark Ads let an advertiser promote its own posts and credit the views, likes and follows to the original post ([TikTok Spark Ads help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/spark-ads), secondary). According to the source article's reading of TikTok's budget rules, Ads Manager requires a daily budget above $20 for an ad group and above $50 for a campaign. The lifetime ad group budget must equal the number of days times that minimum, so a 31-day run needs at least $620 ([TikTok budget help](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/budget?lang=en), secondary).

The table below sets the options side by side for a salon that is deciding where to spend.

| Option | Best use for a salon | Budget rule (as summarised) | Where results are credited |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Organic post | Testing hooks and first-second openings | No spend | Your own account |
| Spark Ads boost | Promoting a video that already performs | Above $20 per day per ad group | The original post |
| Campaign budget | Several boosted videos grouped together | Above $50 per day per campaign | Per campaign setup |

Here is a worked example. Every input is an assumption, not a result:

- Assumption: the salon boosts its best video for 7 days at the $20 ad group minimum, so total spend is $140.
- Assumption: the video carries a booking link and a promo code, and the salon counts bookings that use the code.
- Assumption: average booking value is $95.
- Assumption: 3 bookings use the code during the boost.

Revenue from the code is $285, about 2.0 times the $140 spend. Cost per booking is about $47 before product costs and before the client's later visits. The three bookings are invented for the arithmetic and are not a forecast. A salon that sees fewer bookings should test a different video or caption before raising the budget.

If the salon also runs paid Meta ads, Meta advises using the same budget for both versions of a test and avoiding informal on-and-off testing, which can overlap audiences and make results unreliable ([Meta A/B testing help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669)). The same discipline helps when comparing two TikTok hooks.

## Which Numbers Should You Track?

TikTok defines a 6-second view as a play lasting at least 6 seconds, or the full video if it is shorter. A 100% view counts a video watched to the end, excluding replays ([TikTok video play metrics](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/video-play?lang=en), secondary). Compare these with total views. A video with many plays but few 6-second views has a weak opening, so change the first second before changing the service.

Also track profile link clicks and bookings made with the code. Record each video in one sheet with the date, service, city and promo code, so it is clear which transformations bring bookings and which only bring views.

Judge the account over 8 to 12 weeks. No source gives an exact posting frequency, so choose a rate that can be kept for that period and compare results across the same window.

## FAQ

### Do I need a Business Account for my salon?

TikTok recommends a Business Account for anyone promoting a business. A website link can be added to the profile, but the account only gets access to Commercial Sounds.

### Can I post a client's before-and-after without asking?

No. Get written consent first. If a discount or free service was given in return for the video, disclose it clearly in the video itself.

### How much does it cost to boost a video?

Based on TikTok's budget rules as summarised in the source article, the minimum is above $20 a day for an ad group and above $50 for a campaign. Typical click or view prices are not published as averages in the sources used here, so test with a small budget first.

### How many videos should a salon post?

No source gives an exact number. Pick a rate that can be kept for 8 to 12 weeks, then compare 6-second views, link clicks and code-based bookings across that period.
