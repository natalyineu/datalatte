Accountants can use email for planning content and light deadline reminders, sent only to clients and contacts who opted in. Vendor-published figures put the average email open rate at 21.3% in 2020, while vendor figures also say 90% of texts are read within three minutes. For time-sensitive deadlines, the sources in this guide favour text, with email handling longer updates. Consent rules, not open rates, should set your list. This guide covers the sources' rules and a worked budget with assumed inputs.

## Why Does Email Alone Miss Deadline Weeks?

Email competes with a full inbox, and the sources describe weak results for bulk email. [Textellent](https://textellent.com/blog/best-practices-for-tax-preparation-marketing/) states that an average email campaign generates less than a 5% click rate and that consumers perceive most emails as spam. [SimpleTexting](https://simpletexting.com/guide/sms-marketing-for-accountants-and-tax-firms/) reports that the average email open rate dropped to 21.3% in 2020. These are vendor figures, so treat them as directional rather than fixed benchmarks.

The cost of a missed document is practical. [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/best-sms-marketing-software-for-accounting-firms-playbook-2026) cites the Journal of Accountancy for a month-end close that runs five to six business days, and says chasing missing data inflates that time. A reminder that gets read sooner shortens the wait. The same firm's peak-season figure, 85-95% tax-prep utilization per [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/) as quoted by US Tech Automations, shows why the weeks before a deadline are the ones to plan for.

## Which Rules Apply Before You Send?

Text marketing has the clearest rules in these sources. Textellent notes that the Telephone Consumer Protection Act governs text messaging for marketing but not customer servicing messages. US Tech Automations calls consent and TCPA compliance non-negotiable and advises choosing software with documented opt-in capture, keyword opt-in, logged STOP requests and audit logging.

Deadline reminders are service notices, not sales. US Tech Automations advises keeping marketing campaigns on a separate campaign from service messages, so a promotion never travels with a filing reminder.

The sources in this guide do not cover email-specific rules, such as the CAN-SPAM Act or IRS limits on using tax return information for marketing. Confirm those separately with a tax adviser before you email clients about anything beyond their own engagement.

## Who Should Receive Your Emails?

Start with people who have asked to hear from you: current clients and contacts who joined through a clear sign-up form. Keep a record of how and when each person opted in. Do not add anyone from a purchased list.

Segment by service line, such as individual returns, business returns, advisory or payroll, so each message stays relevant. Textellent gives a clear example: a discount offer for lapsed clients must not go to the whole list, even though that is easy to do. Keep offers to a targeted segment and let everyone else receive only the service notices they need.

## What Should a Year of Client Email Look Like?

Timing matters more than volume. Textellent notes that reminders sent too early reach clients who have not gathered their papers, and reminders sent too late miss the window entirely. Plan your calendar from the real dates that apply to each segment, using published deadlines rather than memory.

A workable calendar has four parts:

1. A short reminder before each filing or payment date for the segment it applies to.
2. A "what to bring" note ahead of the busy season.
3. A plain-language update when a rule changes for your clients.
4. A quiet check-in with one next step, such as booking a planning call.

Each email should ask for one action. For a comparison of channels, read [SMS vs email marketing for local business](/blog/sms-vs-email-marketing-local-business-which-is-better).

## How Do Texts Fit Alongside Email?

SimpleTexting reports that 90% of texts are read within three minutes, that a simple SMS generates a response rate five times that of email, and that almost two-thirds of customers would switch to a company that offered text messaging. These are vendor-published figures, so use them to decide where to test, not as guarantees.

Textellent recommends texts that start with the recipient's name, come from a business line that accepts replies, and stay near the 160-character guideline. Two-way replies matter for accountants, because a client may need to answer with a question or a document. ## FAQ

### Can I email clients about services beyond their engagement?

The sources here do not settle this. Check with a tax adviser first, because tax return information carries its own limits. Keep messages to clients who opted in, and separate them from service notices.

### Is email or text better for deadlines?

The sources favour text for time-sensitive reminders, because vendor figures show higher read rates. Use email for longer planning content, and keep text for short, consented reminders.

### How often should an accountant email clients?

None of the sources sets a standard frequency. Tie sends to real deadlines and to segments that need them, and avoid filler messages between dates.

### Do I need a dedicated tool?

Not always, but the sources recommend choosing a tool that records consent, handles opt-outs and keeps audit logs. Compare credit costs and whether the tool can split marketing from service messages.
