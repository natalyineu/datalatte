Yes, Google Ads can work for photographers, because they put your studio in front of couples who are already searching for a wedding or event photographer. The catch is that it only pays off when you bid on specific, high-intent keywords, send clicks to a focused landing page, and answer inquiries fast. Below is a practical setup you can run on a modest budget.

## Why Google Ads Works for Photographers

Instagram is a discovery channel: people see your work and may remember you later. Google Search is an intent channel: someone types "wedding photographer near me" and is often ready to contact a few studios that week. That is the moment search ads are built for.

A few reasons the format suits photographers:

- You pay when someone clicks, not for passive impressions.
- You can limit ads to the cities you actually travel to.
- Ad scheduling and budgets let you concentrate spend around your booking season.
- Call and form conversions can be tracked, so you know which keywords produce real inquiries.

Results vary a lot by city, competition and the strength of your portfolio, so treat any benchmark you read (including ours) as a starting hypothesis rather than a promise. If you would rather have someone run it, see our [Google Ads management](/services/google-ads) service.

## Choosing the Right Keywords

Start with terms that name both the service and the place, then expand once you see data.

- "wedding photographer [city]"
- "engagement photographer [city]"
- "elopement photographer [city]"
- "wedding photographer near me"
- Longer phrases such as "affordable wedding photographer [city]" if that matches your pricing

Use Google's Keyword Planner to see estimated volume and cost per click in your area. Wedding-related clicks are often priced in the low single-digit dollars or higher in competitive metros, but check your own market rather than trusting a national average.

Avoid broad single words like "photographer" or "photography", which attract people looking for stock images, jobs, classes or equipment. Add negative keywords such as "free", "course", "jobs", "student", "equipment" and "stock" so you stop paying for those clicks. Our guide to [negative keywords](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) explains how to build the list.

<Callout type="warning">Do not bid on "photography" alone. Most of those searchers are not looking to hire a wedding photographer.</Callout>

## Setting Up Your First Campaign

1. Create a Search campaign and target only the cities and radius you serve.
2. Build one ad group per service: weddings, engagements, elopements, portraits.
3. Write at least two or three headline variations per group so Google can test them.
4. Add call, location and sitelink extensions (called assets in newer accounts).
5. Send each ad group to a landing page that matches it, not just your home page.
6. Set up conversion tracking before you spend anything.

A simple way to size the budget: decide the monthly amount you are comfortable testing, divide by 30 for the daily budget, and plan to run it long enough to collect meaningful data. For example, a $300 month is roughly $10 a day. At typical wedding-click prices that is only a handful of clicks daily, so keep the campaign tightly targeted. Our [Google Ads budget guide](/blog/google-ads-budget-for-small-business) covers how to size spend for small businesses.

| Campaign element | Sensible starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Network | Search only (turn off Display expansion at first) |
| Bidding | Manual CPC or Maximize Clicks with a cap, then switch to conversions once you have data |
| Locations | The cities you will actually travel to |
| Schedule | Business hours for call extensions; all day for forms |
| Remarketing | Add after the Search campaign is stable |

Add a remarketing list later to re-engage visitors who viewed your pricing page but did not inquire. Do not expect Display, Video or Shopping to help much for a small studio at the start.

## Optimizing Ad Copy for Weddings

Good ad copy answers three questions quickly: what you shoot, where, and why you.

- State the service and city in the headline: "Wedding Photographer in Denver".
- Add a differentiator you can back up, such as your editing turnaround or what is included in packages.
- Use a clear call to action: "Check Your Date" or "Request Pricing".
- Only quote review counts or ratings that are accurate and up to date.
- Mention booking windows honestly rather than inventing urgency.

If your click-through rate is low, test a headline that adds the city or a specific package. If clicks are fine but inquiries are weak, the problem is usually the landing page: it should show your best work, pricing guidance, availability and a short inquiry form.

<Callout type="coffee">One easy test each month: rotate "wedding photographer" and "engagement photographer" angles in your headlines and compare conversion rates, not just clicks.</Callout>

## Tracking Results and Adjusting Budgets

Track phone calls and form submissions as conversions, and review the search terms report weekly to add negatives and spot new keyword ideas. Judge performance by cost per inquiry and, more importantly, cost per booked client.

A simple example: if an average wedding package is worth $3,000 and you book one in every ten inquiries, you can afford a meaningful cost per inquiry. Plug in your own numbers to decide where to cap bids.

- Pause keywords that spend steadily without producing inquiries.
- Shift budget to the ad groups that generate qualified leads.
- Watch Quality Score as a diagnostic; higher scores generally lower your costs.
- Review the account on a fixed rhythm, such as 15 minutes each week.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I rely on Instagram and word of mouth instead of Google Ads?**
A: You can, and many photographers do. Social and referrals build awareness, while Google Ads captures people who are searching right now. Using both usually covers more of the buying journey than either alone.

**Q: Will this work for event, corporate or elopement photography?**
A: Yes. Swap the keywords to match, for example "corporate event photographer [city]" or "elopement photographer [city]", and run each service in its own ad group with its own landing page.

**Q: How much should a photographer spend on Google Ads?**
A: There is no universal number. Many studios test with a few hundred dollars a month, then scale only if cost per booked client works. Competitive cities may need more to get enough clicks to learn from.

**Q: How long until I see results?**
A: Clicks can start within hours and inquiries within days, but couples often compare studios for weeks before booking. Judge the first month on lead quality and cost per inquiry, then on bookings.

**Q: What if few people in my town search for wedding photographers?**
A: Then search volume may be too low for ads to pay off. Focus on your Google Business Profile, [local SEO](/services/local-seo) and social channels, and consider targeting nearby larger cities if you travel.
