Run the numbers on a $600 month. With a $3.50 click, 5 in 100 clicks becoming an inquiry and 15 in 100 inquiries booking, you get about 171 clicks, 8.6 inquiries and 1.3 booked clients. That is roughly $470 in ads per booking. If your package is $3,000, that is about 16% of the sale. Change any of those three dials and the answer swings between roughly $125 and $1,670 per booking. Those inputs are my assumptions, not benchmarks.

Photography is a different game from a gym or a salon. You sell fewer, bigger jobs, the buyer compares several studios for weeks, and the sale happens in an inbox, not on the website. So the questions are not "how many clicks?" but "how many inquiries, and how do I know which ones booked?" This guide covers that for photographers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Dollar figures are US-based; for other currencies, run the same arithmetic with your own click prices.

## What Do the Benchmarks Say?

There is no trustworthy photographer-specific benchmark, and it is worth saying so plainly. The closest public source is LocaliQ's [search advertising benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/) (updated June 2026), which gives all-industry averages and a row for Arts & Entertainment. The page has no row for photography or wedding services.

Arts & Entertainment is a broad bucket (venues, shows, galleries) and is a poor proxy for a wedding photographer bidding on "wedding photographer [city]". An agency guide from [Mega Digital](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/google-ads-for-photographers/) gives an approximate CPC range of $2.30 to $4.00 for photography keywords, but it labels that as its own estimate with no named source. Treat it as an agency's rule of thumb. The honest approach: set your location in Google's Keyword Planner, read the estimate for "wedding photographer [city]", and then check it against your first two weeks of real data.

## Why the Order Value Changes the Rules

A second-hand reference point: a [Paperlust guide](https://paperlust.co/blog/how-much-does-a-wedding-photographer-cost/) reports that The Knot's Real Weddings Study (nearly 10,500 couples who married in 2025) puts the average wedding photographer at about $3,000, with the lower quartile under $1,500 and the top quartile $4,700 or more. The Knot's own study page was not available to check, so treat this as reported, not confirmed. It is US data; prices elsewhere differ.

What matters is not the national number, it is yours. A $3,000 package can support a $450 ad cost per booking (15%). A $500 mini-session package cannot support the same bids. Everything below, including your break-even click price, flows from your own average booking value.

## Set Up the Campaign

### 1. Campaign type and location

Run a Search campaign, one location group per area you will actually travel to. Google's [location targeting help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6317) calls "presence or interest" the default and recommended setting. For a photographer who travels, that default can be useful, because couples planning a wedding in your area may be searching from elsewhere. If you only shoot locally, check the setting in your account and consider presence only. Either way, add travel destinations as separate locations rather than leaving the radius huge.

### 2. Ad groups by service

One ad group per job type, each with its own landing page:

| Ad group | Example keywords | Match type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weddings | "wedding photographer [city]", [wedding photographer your city] | Phrase + exact |
| Elopements | "elopement photographer [location]" | Phrase + exact |
| Engagements | "engagement photographer [city]", "engagement photos [city]" | Phrase + exact |
| Events/corporate | "corporate event photographer [city]", "headshot photographer [city]" | Phrase + exact |
| Brand | [your studio name] | Exact |

Google's [match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836) says exact match gives the most control and reaches the fewest searches, and that broad match needs Smart Bidding. At a studio budget, start with phrase and exact. The full term list is in our [photographer keyword guide](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-photographers-in-2026).

### 3. Negative keywords

Photography searches attract students, job seekers and DIYers. Add negatives from day one: `jobs`, `course`, `class`, `school`, `tutorial`, `free`, `stock`, `camera`, `lens`, `equipment`, `rental`, `software`, `presets`, `how to`, `salary`. Review the search terms report weekly. Our [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget) shows how to build the list.

### 4. Ads and assets

Write headlines that mirror the search and the buyer's worry: `Wedding Photographer in [City]`, `Check Your Date in 60 Seconds`, `Unedited Gallery Preview Available` (only if true), `Packages From $X`. Showing a price floor can filter out clicks that would never book; whether to do it is your call.

Add sitelinks (Portfolio, Pricing, Real Weddings, Check Availability), a location asset if you have a studio, and a call asset. Per Google's [assets help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7331111), call assets can be scheduled to show only when you can answer, and assets display only when predicted to help the ad.

## Capture the Inquiry, Then Qualify It

The landing page is where most of the budget is won or lost. It needs your best 10 to 15 images for that service, a short pricing guide, a plain statement of what is included, two or three real testimonials, and a short form: names, date, location, budget range. A date field is useful twice over: it filters out "just looking" and lets you reply with real availability. See [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices).

You can also add a lead form directly in the ad. Google's [lead form help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9423234) says lead forms let people submit contact details inside the ad, can be added to Search and Performance Max campaigns, and that leads can be downloaded as a CSV for 30 days (Google stores them for 60), or sent by email, API, webhook or Zapier. It also says lead form ads need a conversion-focused bidding strategy and a privacy policy link. Because Google only holds the leads for a limited time, export or connect them to your inbox from the start.

## Tracking: Inquiries Are Not Bookings

Set up conversions for the form, ad calls and website calls (see our [conversion tracking guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup)). Then deal with the part most photographers skip: which inquiries booked.

Google's [offline conversion guidelines](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15081888) say standard offline conversions uploaded more than 90 days after the associated last click will not be imported, and enhanced conversions for leads have a shorter 63 day limit. Google's [overview of enhanced conversions for leads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11909980) says it lets you measure offline leads using first-party data from your CRM, hashed before it is sent. If couples take longer than that window to sign, the booking cannot be tied back to the click, so also keep a simple spreadsheet: inquiry date, source, quote sent, booked yes or no, package value. Even with no upload at all, that sheet tells you cost per booking.

## The Math

Take a $600 monthly budget and a $3,000 package. All rows marked assumption are mine. Replace them with your own after a month of data.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly budget (assumption) | $600 | $600 | $600 |
| Cost per click (assumption) | $5.00 | $3.50 | $2.50 |
| Clicks | 120 | 171 | 240 |
| Click to inquiry rate (assumption) | 3% | 5% | 8% |
| Inquiries | 3.6 | 8.6 | 19.2 |
| Cost per inquiry | $167 | $70 | $31 |
| Inquiry to booking rate (assumption) | 10% | 15% | 25% |
| Booked clients | 0.4 | 1.3 | 4.8 |
| Ad cost per booking | $1,667 | $467 | $125 |
| Package value (assumption) | $3,000 | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| Ad cost as share of package | 56% | 16% | 4% |

The Strong column is better than any figure above, so do not plan around it. A realistic reading: at this budget you might book one client a month in a good market, or none in a bad one, which is why a single month proves little.

Two things follow:

- **Your break-even click price.** Maximum CPC = the most you will pay per booking x inquiry rate x close rate. If you will pay $450 per booking, at 5% and 15%, your ceiling is $450 x 0.05 x 0.15 = about $3.38 a click. Above that you lose money on average.
- **The lever that moves most is the close rate.** Going from 10% to 25% cuts cost per booking by more than half without touching the ad account. That means reply speed, a clear quote and follow-up.

Budget mechanics: Google's [daily budget help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6385083) says to divide the monthly figure by 30.4, and that daily spend can reach up to twice the average, with a monthly cap of 30.4 times the daily budget. So $600 is about $19.70 a day.

## Optimise Weekly

1. Read the search terms report and add negatives.
2. Pause keywords that spent about twice your target cost per inquiry with no inquiries (my rule of thumb).
3. Compare ad groups by booked clients, not by inquiries.
4. Check that your form and phone work on mobile, since many couples search from a phone.
5. Raise budget by about 20% only where cost per booking is on target. Pair this with [local SEO for photographers](/blog/local-seo-for-photographers), since your Google Business Profile, reviews and photos get viewed before the click. For social, see [Facebook ads for photographers](/blog/facebook-ads-for-photographers).

## Do This Today

1. Work out your average booking value from your last 10 clients, and decide the most you will pay for one new booking.
2. Use the break-even formula above to set your maximum click price, then check the Keyword Planner estimate for "wedding photographer [city]" to see whether it fits.
3. Build one Search campaign with ad groups per service, phrase and exact match, and the negative list above.
4. Build or fix one landing page per service with a date field, then set up form and call conversions before turning ads on.
5. Start the inquiry spreadsheet: date, source, quote sent, booked, package value. Update it every Friday.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a photographer spend on Google Ads per month?**
A: There is no official figure. Mega Digital's guide uses a $300 month as an example scenario, which is an agency illustration, not data. Use the break-even formula: if your market's click price is above your ceiling, the budget does not matter, because each click loses money.

**Q: Is "wedding photographer near me" worth bidding on?**
A: It can be, since the intent is high, but it often costs more. Run it with city-specific terms and judge it by cost per booked client, not cost per click.

**Q: Google Ads or Instagram for photographers?**
A: Instagram builds awareness; Google catches people searching right now. If your city has too few searches, spend on your Business Profile and social first. See [Google Ads vs Facebook Ads for local business](/blog/google-ads-vs-facebook-ads-local-business-which-wins).

**Q: How long before ads bring bookings?**
A: Inquiries can start within days. Couples compare studios for weeks, so judge the first month on inquiry quality and cost per inquiry, then on bookings after two to three months.

**Q: Should I use Performance Max?**
A: Not first. Prove Search with tracked inquiries before handing Google more control.
