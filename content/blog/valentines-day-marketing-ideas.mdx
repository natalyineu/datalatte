The best Valentine's Day marketing for a local business is a clear, time-limited offer for couples, friends and pets, posted on your Google Business Profile and sent to your email list before the weekend. The National Retail Federation's 2026 survey forecast record US spending of $29.1 billion, and 55% of consumers planned to celebrate. Evenings out, flowers and pets all had their own budgets.

Valentine's Day 2027 falls on Sunday 14 February, so plan the final push for the preceding week. This guide uses NRF data from January 2026; examples are labelled as assumptions.

## What Does the Data Say About Valentine's Day Shoppers?

NRF's survey of 7,791 adult consumers, taken 2 to 8 January 2026, found an average gift spend of $199.78 per person, up from $188.81 the year before. Spending by category:

| Category | 2026 forecast spending | Share planning to buy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jewelry | $7.0 billion | Not given |
| Evening out | $6.3 billion | Not given |
| Flowers | $3.1 billion | 41% |
| Friends | $2.4 billion | 33% |
| Pets | $2.1 billion (up from $1.7 billion) | 35% |
| Candy | Not given | 56% |

Shoppers planned to buy online (38%), at department stores (35%), at discount stores (30%) and at specialty stores (21%). The release does not say when people start shopping, so no timing figure is quoted here. Local businesses compete on personal service, not price.

## Which Ideas Work for Local Businesses?

Pick two or three that fit your capacity. Each links to a category in the table above.

1. **Couples packages** for restaurants, cafes, spas and studios: a fixed price and limited time slots. Evenings out are a $6.3 billion category.
2. **Pet offers** for groomers, vets and pet shops: pets are a $2.1 billion category, up from $1.7 billion.
3. **Friends and Galentine's offers:** NRF puts friends at $2.4 billion, with 33% planning to buy.
4. **Gift cards and certificates**, sold in advance so customers can give them. See our [pet grooming gift certificate guide](/blog/pet-grooming-gift-certificates-holiday-marketing) for the mechanics.
5. **Small add-ons** such as a chocolate with a coffee, a rose with a service, or a card.
6. **Single-person offers:** a treat for yourself keeps singles in the audience.
7. **Partner bundles** with a neighbouring florist or restaurant, each promoting the other.
8. **Reservations only:** limit capacity and take bookings to avoid queues.
9. **Event nights:** a class, tasting or workshop with a fixed ticket price.
10. **Thank-you messages** to regulars with a small, specific offer.

## How Do You Promote It on Google?

Use Business Profile posts. Google's help page says posts share announcements, offers, updates and event details on Search and Maps. An Offer post requires a title, dates and time and adds a "View Offer" button. An Event post shows for 24 hours on the event dates if you leave out times. Posts older than six months are archived unless you set a date range.

Change your hours for the day with special hours, which Google describes as the setting for holidays and other temporary periods. Check the rest of your listing with our [Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist).

## Should You Use Email and SMS?

Yes, if people have opted in. In the US, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide requires accurate sender details, honest subject lines, clear ad disclosure, a valid postal address and an opt-out honoured within 10 business days. Penalties run up to $53,088 per violating email. Read our [holiday email campaign guide](/blog/holiday-email-marketing-campaigns) for structure, and keep text messages only for people who agreed to receive them.

## What Should the Timeline Look Like?

This is a suggested plan, not a benchmark. Work back from 14 February.

| When | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Early January | Decide the offer, capacity and price |
| Mid-January | Publish the offer on your website and Business Profile |
| Late January | Email your list; start gift card sales |
| First week of February | Post reminders; confirm staff and stock |
| The week of 14 February | Last reminder with remaining availability |

## Worked Example: Is a Couples Package Worth It?

All inputs are **assumptions**.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Package price for two | $90 |
| Cost to deliver | $45 |
| Spots available | 20 |
| Spots sold | 14 |
| Promotion cost (a few hours and $50 of printing) | $50 plus $150 of time = $200 |

Margin per package is $45. Fourteen sold gives $630 of margin, less $200 of cost, so $430 of profit. If only 4 sell, margin is $180 and you lose $20. Set the break-even (here 5 packages) before you launch and decide when to stop promoting.

## FAQ

### When should I start promoting Valentine's Day?

NRF's release gives no shopping-start data. Many businesses begin in January to allow for gift cards and bookings; test it against your own booking history.

### Do I need to discount?

No source shows that discounts are needed. Packages and limited spots can sell without a lower price.

### How do I include people who are single?

Offer a treat for yourself or a friends option; NRF counts friends as a $2.4 billion category.

### Can I use Google posts for it?

Yes. Offer and Event posts are described on Google's help page.

### How do I measure it?

Use a code, a booking link or a special menu name and count uses.
