A plumber's website should let a stranger with a leak find a phone number in seconds and trust the business enough to call. In practice that means a tap-to-call button on every page, a services page, a service-area page, proof of who you are, and a page that loads fast on a phone. Google's guideline for a good load is a largest content paint within 2.5 seconds.

This guide is for owner-operators and small plumbing firms. It covers page structure, speed, structured data, platform costs and the mistakes that waste ranking effort. No conversion-rate promises are made, because no source publishes a reliable one for plumbing sites. Examples marked as assumptions are for arithmetic only.

## What Pages Does a Plumber's Website Need?

Start with five pages: Home, Services, Service Areas, About and Contact. Each has one job. The home page states what you do, where, and how to reach you. The services page lists work such as drain clearing, water heaters and repiping, with one short section per service. The About page names the people and shows licence and insurance details where your state requires them.

Put the phone number in the header, as a tap-to-call link on mobile. If you offer emergency work, say the hours plainly. Add a short contact form as a second option, not the only one. Our [guide to getting more phone calls from your website](/blog/how-to-get-more-phone-calls-from-your-website) covers the call button in more detail.

## How Fast Should the Site Load?

Google's Core Web Vitals give three targets. Largest Contentful Paint should occur within 2.5 seconds of the page starting to load. Interaction to Next Paint should be 200 milliseconds or less. Cumulative Layout Shift should be 0.1 or less. web.dev says to measure the 75th percentile of page loads, split by mobile and desktop.

Plumbing sites often fail on large hero photos and chat widgets. Compress images, avoid auto-playing video, and test with PageSpeed Insights, which reports these metrics. Google says it ranks and indexes using the mobile version, so check the phone view first.

## Should You Make a Page for Every Town?

Be careful. Google's spam policies list "multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse, and warn against many near-identical pages. A town page is acceptable only when it holds facts that are true for that town: jobs you did there, local rules you work with, response times you can really meet.

If you cannot write something specific, list the towns on one Service Areas page instead. Read our guide on [mobile-first website design](/blog/mobile-first-website-design-local-businesses) before building anything new.

## What Structured Data Should You Add?

Google supports LocalBusiness structured data. Name and address are required for rich result eligibility, and Google recommends fields such as telephone, URL, opening hours and geo coordinates. It advises using the most specific sub-type possible, and its own example shows a business typed as both Electrician and Plumber. Google also states that markup does not guarantee any special display in results.

Make the details match your Google Business Profile exactly. For the profile, Google's guidelines say to link a website that represents your individual location, and not a social media page. See [schema markup for local business websites](/blog/schema-markup-local-business-website).

## FAQ

### How many pages should a plumber's website have?

Five is enough to start: Home, Services, Service Areas, About and Contact. Add pages only when you have real content for them, such as a page per major service.

### Do plumbers need a blog?

Not at the start. Fix the core pages, your Google Business Profile and speed first. Add articles later if you can answer real customer questions.

### Is a free website enough?

Wix lists a free plan, but its page lists a free domain only on paid plans. A branded domain and email look more credible to a customer choosing between three plumbers.

### What matters most on mobile?

A visible phone number that can be tapped, fast loading and a layout that does not jump. Google indexes the mobile version of your content.
