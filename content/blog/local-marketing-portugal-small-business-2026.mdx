## What Does the Portuguese Marketing Market Look Like?

The Portuguese Association of Marketing Professionals (APPM) published a Marketing Barometer listing the main trends for 2024, as reported by [Portugal Business News](https://www.portugalbusinessesnews.com/post/top-4-marketing-trends-in-portugal-in-2024). Artificial intelligence ranked first, mentioned by 94% of participants. Sustainability, measured through ESG metrics, ranked second. Digital marketing ranked third.

The same barometer lists the digital strategies Portuguese companies use most: social networks, content marketing, traditional advertising, collaborations with agencies and freelancers, SEO and email marketing. For a small shop, that list works as a checklist of what competitors already do. It does not show how well each channel performs in your category or city.

A secondary summary on [Rauva's advertising-business guide](https://blog.rauva.com/blog/how-to-open-an-advertising-business-in-portugal) cites a PwC forecast of a 5.1% compound annual growth rate for Portuguese advertising from 2023 to 2027. The same summary says digital takes over 60% of ad spend. Both figures are secondary, so check the original PwC report before using them in a plan.

## Which Portuguese City Gives a Local Business the Most Visitors?

Porto is the obvious test city for visitor-facing businesses. [One Day Agency's Porto page](https://oneday.agency/advertise-in-porto) describes a population of just over 230,000, plus business and leisure travellers. It reports almost 7 million visitors a year, a 7.1% rise in arrivals in the latest year, and growth of 28% compared with pre-pandemic figures. The page does not name an underlying source for these numbers.

The same page forecasts that outdoor advertising across Portugal will reach over £82.5 million this year, growing 2.46% a year. That is a national outdoor figure, not a measure of what a café earns from a billboard.

For a shop, the useful detail is where people move. The agency names the Aliados and Bolhão area in the centre and the Boavista district as commercial hubs. Use these as places to test, not as proof that footfall will follow. Ask customers where they found you, and count visits from the neighbourhoods you target.

## Is Transit Advertising Worth It for a Small Business?

The One Day page says Porto's public transport operator, STCP, carries an average of 216,000 people every day. That is a ridership figure, not a count of people who notice an ad. Transit and billboard placements usually suit brands with wide reach. A café or salon is more likely to get value from a complete business profile and paid search in the area it serves.

If you test an outdoor or transit placement, give it a unique phone number or landing page. Without that, you cannot separate its bookings from the rest of your results.

## How Do You Set Up a Business Before Advertising in Portugal?

Rauva, a company-formation provider, lists five steps for opening an advertising business in Portugal. The steps are promotional and secondary, so confirm each one with the tax authority or an accountant:

1. Obtain a personal tax number (NIF).
2. Secure a Portuguese address, which may be virtual.
3. Register the company and obtain its corporate tax number (NIPC).
4. Open a business bank account and set up accounting within 15 days of registration.
5. Check the rules for your specific services.

Owners who already trade in Portugal probably have these in place. Owners new to the country should confirm the requirements before launching any campaign.

## What Should a Worked Budget Look Like for a Small Test?

The table below is an illustration. Every input is an **assumption**, not a benchmark, and none comes from the sources. It shows how to decide whether a test is working.

| Step | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly test budget | €400 |
| Split | €250 Instagram, €150 Google Search |
| Average ticket | €12 |
| Gross margin | 65% |
| Margin per sale | 12 × 0.65 = €7.80 |
| Sales needed to cover the test | 400 ÷ 7.80 = about 51 |
| Decision rule | Fewer than 20 tracked sales in week four, change the offer before spending more |

Track sales with a unique code for each channel, such as a tagged booking link or a dedicated phone line. Record each channel's sales separately in your accounting software so you can compare them with spend. [FreshBooks or QuickBooks](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business) can handle this, depending on how many channels you run.

## What Should a Small Business Test First?

Start with free basics before any paid spending. A complete Google business profile puts your shop on the map and in local results. Then test one paid channel for four weeks, rather than running several at once, so you know which one produced the result.

For social channels, compare scheduling and reporting tools before committing budget to posting. The [Hootsuite and Sprout Social comparison](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) covers which fits a small team. If you sell a service rather than a product, read [whether Google local service ads are worth it](/blog/google-local-service-ads-worth-it-local-business) before choosing paid search.

Write messages in Portuguese, and add English if tourists are a meaningful share of your customers. Check how many bookings come from each language group before you split the budget.

## What Is Still Unverified?

No source opened for this guide gives Portuguese cost-per-click, cost-per-thousand-impressions or local ad price data. Set a daily cap in your own account and use the keyword planner to estimate costs in your category.

Rules for email and direct marketing were not verified against a primary source for this guide. Check the national data protection regulator's current guidance, or speak to a Portuguese lawyer, before sending any campaign. Do not buy email lists. Collect consent through a clear sign-up form that people opt into themselves.
