To optimise a Google Business Profile for a pet grooming business, choose "Pet groomer" as your primary category, list every service with a description and price range, add real photos of your salon and finished pets, collect reviews steadily, and keep your hours and booking link accurate. These steps are what decide whether you appear in the Map Pack when someone searches "dog groomer near me".

Most pet owners search from their phone, often with a specific need: a first groom for a puppy, a matted coat, a last-minute appointment. Your profile is frequently the first thing they see, before your website. Here is how to build one that gets chosen.

## Why your profile matters more than your website

For local searches, Google typically shows a map with three businesses above the regular results. Appearing there depends largely on three things Google has described publicly: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how close you are to the searcher) and prominence (how well known you are, including reviews and links). You cannot move your salon, but you can improve relevance and prominence, and both start with your profile.

## Step 1: Claim and verify

1. Search your business name on Google or go to business.google.com.
2. Claim the existing listing if there is one, or create a new one.
3. Verify by the method offered (video, phone, email or postcard).
4. If you are mobile-only and travel to clients, set a service area and hide your home address. Google's guidelines distinguish between storefront and service-area businesses, so follow the one that is true for you.

Duplicate listings can split your reviews and confuse customers, so search for old listings (a previous name or location) and request removal or merging.

## Step 2: Choose categories carefully

Your **primary category** has a strong influence on which searches you appear for. For most groomers, "Pet groomer" is the primary. Depending on what you really offer, secondary categories might include "Dog trainer", "Pet supply store", "Pet boarding service" or "Mobile pet groomer" if available in your country. Only add categories that describe real services, because misleading categories can lead to suspensions and unhappy visitors.

Google adds and renames categories from time to time, so browse the current list when you edit.

## Step 3: Fill out the basics accurately

- **Business name:** exactly as it appears on your signage. Adding keywords ("Best Dog Groomer in Austin") breaks Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.
- **Address and service area:** consistent with your website and other directories.
- **Phone:** a local number, ideally the same one used across the web.
- **Hours:** including holiday hours. Update them before bank holidays and busy seasons.
- **Website and booking link:** link directly to your booking page if you have one.
- **Attributes:** options like wheelchair accessible, appointment required, or "pet owners welcome" where applicable.

## Step 4: Write a useful description

You have 750 characters, and the first 250 or so are most visible. Mention what you groom, where you are, and what makes you different, in plain language.

An example structure (replace with your facts): "[Business name] is a [independent / family-run] dog and cat grooming salon in [neighbourhood]. We offer full grooms, bath and brush, nail trims and de-shedding for all breeds, and we specialise in [anything true, e.g. anxious dogs or doodles]. Book online or call [number]."

Skip superlatives you cannot prove and avoid stuffing keywords.

## Step 5: List every service

The Services section lets you add each service with a short description and price (or price range). Typical lines for a groomer:

| Service | Useful detail to include |
|---|---|
| Full groom | Bath, haircut, nails, ears; what size ranges you price by |
| Bath and brush | Good for short coats; typical duration |
| Puppy intro groom | Age guidance and vaccination requirements |
| De-shedding treatment | Which coats it suits |
| Nail trim | Walk-in or booked |
| Cat grooming | Only if you truly offer it |
| Matted coat work | Whether extra charges apply |

Listing prices (even "from" prices) helps pre-qualify enquiries and reduces calls from people shopping on price alone. Be honest that prices vary by coat and size.

## Step 6: Photos that earn bookings

Real photos usually beat stock images because people want to see the actual place their pet will visit. Aim to add:

- Exterior and entrance, so people can find you.
- Reception and grooming area, clean and well lit.
- Your team at work (with permission).
- Before-and-after groom photos, with the owner's permission.
- Happy-pet photos on a clean backdrop.

Upload new photos regularly, perhaps a few per month, rather than all at once. Check that images are sharp and do not include people's faces without permission.

## Step 7: Collect and answer reviews

Reviews are among the most visible signals on your profile. Ask every happy customer, at the moment they are happiest: when they pick up a freshly groomed pet. Make it easy.

1. Get your direct review link from your profile.
2. Make a QR code for the counter and put the link in the follow-up text or email.
3. Ask staff to mention it at pickup.
4. Reply to every review, positive or negative, within a day or two.

Never offer rewards in exchange for reviews or review-gate (only asking happy customers) because both can violate Google's policies. For tactics and wording, see our guide to [Google Business Profile reviews](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) and [automated review responses](/blog/automated-review-response-ai-local-business).

In replies, mention the service naturally ("Thanks for trusting us with Bella's first groom") without keyword-stuffing, and never share private details about the pet or owner.

## Step 8: Use posts, Q&A and messaging

- **Posts:** share offers, seasonal tips (shedding season, summer haircuts) and availability. They do not guarantee ranking but help visitors see that you are active.
- **Q&A:** seed common questions yourself and answer them: vaccination requirements, how long a groom takes, handling nervous pets.
- **Booking and messaging:** if you use a booking tool that integrates with Google, enable it. Only turn on chat if you will respond promptly.

Our [Google Business Profile posts guide](/blog/google-business-profile-posts) covers formats and ideas.

## Step 9: Keep your information consistent elsewhere

Google cross-checks your details with other sources. Make sure your name, address and phone number match on your website, Facebook, Apple Maps, Yelp, Bing Places and local directories. Inconsistent details can undermine trust. ## Step 10: Measure what happens

In your profile's performance section you can see how many people viewed it, called, requested directions or clicked your website. Review it monthly, note changes after you update photos or reviews, and use a tracked booking link to see how many enquiries become appointments. Do not obsess over week-to-week swings, which are normal.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best primary category for a dog groomer?**
A: For most salons, "Pet groomer" is the best primary category. Add secondary categories only for services you genuinely offer, such as boarding or training.

**Q: Can I appear on Google Maps if I am a mobile groomer?**
A: Yes. Set up a service-area business, hide your home address and list the areas you cover. Be realistic about the radius you can serve.

**Q: How many reviews do I need to rank?**
A: There is no fixed number. Rankings depend on distance, relevance and prominence, and the competition in your area. A steady flow of recent, genuine reviews is more useful than a one-off burst.

**Q: How often should I update my profile?**
A: Check it monthly, add photos and a post regularly, and update hours before holidays. Respond to reviews as they arrive.
