BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 found 68% of US consumers will only use a local business rated four stars or higher, and 74% look for reviews from the last three months. For a groomer, that means your profile can lose a booking before anyone reads your website. The good news is that most of what decides the outcome is under your control and costs nothing.

This guide is for salon-based and mobile pet groomers in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It follows Google's own published rules wherever they exist, and flags advice that is only common practice. The survey is a US panel of 1,002 adults from a vendor that sells review tools, so read the percentages as a signal, not a forecast.

## How Google Decides Who Shows Up

Google's help page says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity". It defines relevance as how well your profile matches the search, distance as how far you are from the searcher, and prominence as how well known you are, based partly on links to your business and your reviews. It adds that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking, gives no review count to aim for, keeps the exact details confidential, and says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

You cannot move your shop closer to the searcher. You can make your profile more complete and relevant, and you can earn recent reviews. That is the whole job.

## Step 1: Claim, Verify, and Pick the Right Business Type

Search your business name on Google or go to the Business Profile dashboard, claim any existing listing and verify it with the method Google offers. Search for old duplicates (a previous name or address) and request removal or merging, so your reviews are not split.

Then decide which type you are, because Google treats them differently:

- **Salon with a storefront:** show your address and hours, and be staffed during them.
- **Mobile groomer with no storefront:** Google's guidelines say a business that travels to customers can have one service-area profile, and a service-area business should hide its address. A plumber working from home, in Google's example, removes the home address. Google says service areas generally should not extend beyond about two hours of driving time from your base.
- **Hybrid (salon plus van):** Google says hybrid businesses can show a storefront address and set a service area, as long as the location is staffed and able to receive customers during posted hours.

Choose the one that is true. A fake storefront risks suspension.

## Step 2: Name It Exactly

Google's rules say to use the real-world name you show customers on your storefront, website and stationery, and that including unnecessary information in the name is not permitted and can lead to suspension. That covers taglines, services and location details. So "Paws & Claws Grooming" is fine; "Paws & Claws | Best Dog Groomer Austin TX" is not. Do not write your name in capitals or add trademark symbols.

## Step 3: Choose Categories

Google's guidance: use the fewest categories needed to describe your core business, as specific as possible, and choose ones that complete "This business IS a", rather than categories listing every service you offer. Google also adds broader related categories automatically.

For a groomer, start typing "groom" in the category field and pick the closest primary match. Guides commonly recommend "Pet groomer" as the primary category, but Google's list changes and we found no official list to confirm exact names, so trust what your dashboard offers. Add a secondary category only if you run a separate business line, such as boarding. Do not use categories as keywords.

## Step 4: Write the Description

Google's help text, as quoted by BrightLocal (second-hand), caps the description at 750 characters, allows no links or HTML, and discourages promotions and prices. BrightLocal also says only the first 250 characters show before the "read more" cut, so lead with the facts that matter.

A template, with your true details swapped in:

> [Business name] is a family-run dog and cat grooming salon in [suburb]. We offer full grooms, baths, nail trims and de-shedding for all breeds, with one groomer per dog. We specialise in [anxious dogs / doodles / seniors]. Book online or call [number].

Skip superlatives you cannot prove.

## Step 5: List Services, Hours and Booking

Add each service with a short description. Say what is included and what changes the price: size, coat, matting. Keep hours accurate, including holiday changes, which you should update before each holiday. Link directly to your booking page, not your home page.

On the dashboard, Google's performance page lists a Bookings metric that counts completed bookings, but only if your bookings are managed through a booking provider. Check whether your groomer software connects. If it does not, use a tracked link so you can count clicks yourself.

## Step 6: Photos and Video

Google's photo help gives the specs: JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB, 720 x 720 pixels recommended, 250 x 250 minimum, sharp and well lit without heavy filters. Video can be up to 30 seconds and 75 MB at 720p or higher. Media appears after verification and can take 24 to 48 hours to go live. Add a logo, a cover photo and business photos, with exterior shots helping people find you.

For a groomer, upload:

- The storefront and entrance (or the van and your branded uniform).
- The grooming area, clean and well lit.
- You and the team at work, with permission.
- Before-and-after photos, with the owner's permission.
- Finished pets on a plain backdrop.

Add a few each month instead of fifty on day one. Whether new photos help rankings is not something Google publishes, so judge them by whether they help a nervous owner decide.

## Step 7: Earn Reviews the Allowed Way

Ask at pickup, when the owner has just seen the groomed dog. Create your review link, put it in a QR code at the counter and in your follow-up text, and reply to every review.

Google's contributed content policy is strict. It bans reviews paid for "directly or in kind", bans merchants offering "incentives – such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services", and lists "selectively solicit positive reviews from customers" and "discourage or prohibit negative reviews" as prohibited. It allows asking customers for genuine reviews without incentives or trying to influence the rating or content. So: no "free nail trim for a 5-star review", no screening unhappy customers out of your request, and no friends or staff reviewing. Our [Google Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) has wording, and our guide to [automated review replies](/blog/automated-review-response-ai-local-business) covers tooling.

When you reply, thank the person and mention the service ("thanks for trusting us with Bella's first groom") but never share health or behaviour details about the pet or owner.

## Step 8: Posts and Consistency

Posts are a way to show you are active: seasonal shedding tips, holiday hours, new availability. We have no Google data that posts raise rankings, so treat them as a trust signal for visitors. See our [Google Business Profile posts guide](/blog/google-business-profile-posts).

Keep your name, address and phone the same on your website, Facebook, Apple Maps, Yelp and Bing. Google does not publish a ranking weight for citations, but mismatches confuse customers. Our [local SEO tips for pet groomers](/blog/local-seo-tips-for-pet-groomers) and [audit checklist](/blog/local-seo-audit-checklist-for-pet-groomers) go further.

## Step 9: Measure Monthly

Google's performance page shows searches (the terms people used), views on Search and Maps, directions, calls (taps on the call button), website clicks, messages and bookings, and only the metrics that apply to you appear. Search terms update at the start of each month, and calls count taps, so a person who reads your number and dials by hand will not show. Log the numbers monthly and compare them after you change photos or earn a batch of reviews. Do not read much into one week.

## Do This Today

1. Search your business name, claim or verify the listing, and flag any duplicates for removal.
2. Check your business name against your signage and delete any keywords or taglines Google does not allow.
3. Open the category field, pick the closest groomer primary category and remove categories that describe features rather than your business.
4. Upload five real photos (exterior, grooming area, team, a before-and-after, a finished pet) meeting Google's JPG or PNG specs.
5. Create your review link, make a QR code for the counter and send it to your last ten happy clients, with no incentive and no screening.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best primary category for a dog groomer?**
A: Guides usually recommend a "Pet groomer" style category. Google says to pick the most specific category that completes "This business IS a", so choose the closest match your dashboard offers and keep secondary categories to real business lines.

**Q: Can a mobile groomer appear on Google Maps?**
A: Yes. Google's guidelines allow a service-area business with no storefront to have a profile, with the address hidden. Service areas generally should not extend beyond about two hours of driving from your base.

**Q: How many reviews do I need to rank?**
A: Google gives no number. It says more reviews and positive ratings can help, alongside relevance and distance. Recent, genuine reviews matter to shoppers too: 74% in BrightLocal's US survey look for reviews from the last three months.

**Q: Can I offer a discount for a review?**
A: No. Google's policy prohibits incentives such as payment, discounts, free goods or services in exchange for reviews.

**Q: How often should I update my profile?**
A: Check performance monthly, add a few photos, update hours before holidays and reply to reviews as they arrive.
