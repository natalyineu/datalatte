An independent pharmacy should start with a complete Google Business Profile, a steady flow of honest reviews, and a short list of services that chains handle badly, then add paid ads only inside Google's healthcare rules. Two rules shape everything else. Google Ads restricts prescription drug advertising, and HIPAA requires patient authorization before protected health information is used for marketing, with narrow exceptions.

This guide is for owners of independent community pharmacies, mainly in the US, with notes for the UK, Canada and Australia where Google's rules apply. It is general information, not legal advice. Every figure comes from a source listed at the end, and examples are labelled as assumptions.

## How Big Is the Independent Pharmacy Market?

Drug Topics, reporting on the 2026 NCPA Digest (published October 2026), says independents operate nearly 19,000 locations, more than a third of US retail pharmacies, in a $103 billion marketplace. So a pharmacy owner is competing for local patients with a large number of similar stores plus the chains.

Marketing differs from other local trades because the product is regulated. The next sections cover what Google and HIPAA allow, then what to do first.

## What Can a Pharmacy Advertise on Google Ads?

Google's healthcare and medicines policy restricts promoting online prescribing, dispensing or sale of prescription drugs. Advertisers "must apply to serve ads for prescription drug services", and offering prescription drugs without a prescription is prohibited. Prescription drug terms are restricted in ads, landing pages and keywords. Violations can lead to account suspension without warning.

Online pharmacies are allowed with limits in many countries, including the US, UK and Canada, and usually need local registration or accreditation. Poland is listed as not allowed. For a walk-in pharmacy, the safer ad topics are services and products that are not prescription drugs: vaccinations where you offer them, delivery, compounding, medication reviews, and over-the-counter ranges. Check the policy page for your country before launching.

## What Does HIPAA Say About Marketing to Patients?

Under 45 CFR 164.508, a covered entity "must obtain an authorization for any use or disclosure of protected health information for marketing." The regulation names two exceptions: a face-to-face communication to an individual, and a promotional gift of nominal value. If a third party pays you for the marketing, the authorization must say so.

In practice, do not upload your patient list to an ad platform or an email tool without checking with a compliance adviser. The HHS guidance page returned a 403 error when checked, so this article relies on the regulation text only. Other communications, such as refill reminders, have their own rules not covered here.

## How Should a Pharmacy Set Up Google Business Profile?

Treat the profile as your main free channel. Google's guidelines say the business name must be your real-world name, with no extra services or hours, and the address must be a real physical location. Use a local phone number rather than a call-centre line. If your pharmacy sits inside a supermarket, Google says it should have its own profile.

Google also lists pharmaceuticals among regulated products it does not allow in some content, which matters if you upload products. Pick the fewest categories that describe the core business. ## How Do You Get Reviews Without Breaking Google's Rules?

Ask every customer, but never pay for a review. Google's help says offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for posting, changing or removing a review is "strictly prohibited". You may remind customers with a Google link or QR code at the counter. Reviews also feed the prominence part of local ranking, according to Google.

Reply to reviews in a short, specific way and avoid putting any patient details in a reply. Naming a medication or condition in public can disclose health information. See our [Google Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) for reply templates and timing.

## Which Local Services Are Worth Promoting?

Pick services a patient can use within days and that a chain counter does not make easy. Examples to consider, depending on your licence and local law: free local delivery, medication synchronisation so all refills fall on one day, vaccinations, and a pharmacist you can talk to without waiting. None of these figures are published as benchmarks, so measure your own uptake.

Put each service on its own page of your website and in your Business Profile services list. Then track calls and direction requests, as described in our [guide to Business Profile analytics](/blog/google-my-business-analytics).

## FAQ

### Can a pharmacy run Google Ads for prescriptions?

Not freely. Google restricts prescription drug advertising and requires advertisers to apply for prescription drug services. Offering drugs without a prescription is prohibited. Check the policy for your country and apply before running any such ads.

### Can I email my patient list about new services?

Not without checking HIPAA first. The regulation requires authorization for using protected health information for marketing, apart from face-to-face communication and nominal gifts. Ask a compliance adviser before using patient data in any tool.

### Is it allowed to offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Google's help says giving free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited.

### How many independent pharmacies are there in the US?

The 2026 NCPA Digest, as reported by Drug Topics, counts nearly 19,000 locations, which is over a third of US retail pharmacies.

### What should a new pharmacy do first?

Complete the Business Profile, ask for reviews at the counter, and publish one page per service. Add paid ads only after those are working.
