An electrician website needs a tap-to-call number and your license details on the first screen, one page for each core service, and a short request or booking form that works on a phone. Adwave, a marketing vendor, says most electricians need $1,000 to $3,000 a month in ad spend for steady lead flow, so the site has to convert every visitor that paid ads send. Speed and a single clear next step matter as much as layout.

## What should appear above the fold on an electrician's homepage?

Visitors arrive needing one of three things: urgent help, a quote for planned work, or proof that you are licensed. The first screen should make it obvious which one applies and offer one clear next step. Keep the list short so the eye lands on the right button.

- A phone number that dials when tapped on a mobile device.
- The cities or neighbourhoods you serve.
- Your license number, shown the way your state licensing board requires.
- One primary button: call, request a quote or book.

Landing pages with a single primary call to action performed best in a ZoomInfo analysis of more than 18,000 pages, at an average 13.5% conversion rate, against 11.9% for pages with two to four links and 10.5% for five or more ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/landing-page-best-practices-that-increase-conversions), secondary). The same principle applies to the homepage. See [above-the-fold design for local business](/blog/above-the-fold-design-local-business) for layout ideas.

## Which service pages should an electrician build first?

Give each high-value job its own page, such as panel upgrades, EV charger installation, rewiring, lighting and emergency repairs. Start with the three jobs that earn the most per visit, not the longest list of services.

Each page should describe the work, what the customer needs to prepare, how you quote, the areas you cover and the next step. StackAdapt notes that sales-focused landing pages run longer than lead capture pages and include social proof, FAQs and sometimes case studies ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/landing-page-conversion-rate), secondary). Use the questions customers actually ask as FAQ headings.

Do not invent fixed prices. If you cannot quote a price, name what changes it, such as access, panel age or permit requirements. For testing page layouts over time, see [website conversion rate optimization](/blog/website-conversion-rate-optimization).

## How much should an electrician budget for online leads?

Adwave's electrician guide says most electricians need $1,000 to $3,000 per month in ad spend for consistent lead flow. Its comparison table places Google Ads and Local Services Ads in the same range, with immediate results, and puts social media (Meta, Nextdoor) at $500 to $2,000 a month with results in 2 to 4 weeks ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/electrician-advertising-reaching-homeowners)).

| Channel (Adwave figures) | Monthly cost | Time to results | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Ads / LSAs | $1,000-$3,000 | Immediate | Emergency calls, high-intent leads |
| Social media (Meta, Nextdoor) | $500-$2,000 | 2-4 weeks | Awareness, seasonal promos, retargeting |

Adwave's starting tier is $500 to $1,500 a month, split 50% to Google LSAs, 30% to Google Business Profile and reviews, and 20% to truck wraps and yard signs. Adwave also says shared lead platforms send a homeowner's contact details to three to five other electricians, which is one reason to build a site and phone line you control.

## Does online booking actually get used?

A 2025 Eliciting Insights survey of 384 U.S. adults who had visited a healthcare provider found that 95% had booked a medical appointment online or would do so if the option existed. Among those who had already booked online, 92% said they planned to use online scheduling again ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/appointment-booking-page-design), secondary). The sample is healthcare, not electrical work, so read it as evidence that people expect online options, not as a booking rate for your trade.

For electricians, the practical split is simple. Emergency calls usually need a phone number, while planned work such as EV chargers or panel upgrades can go through a request form or scheduling link. Check whether your current field-service software already takes online requests before adding another tool, and keep the form short enough to finish on a phone.

## How fast does an electrician website need to load?

Google's mobile speed guidance cites a SOASTA study finding that mobile pages loading one second faster achieved up to a 27% increase in conversion rates ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/appointment-booking-page-design), secondary). Treat that as a relative change, not a promised gain. Adwave makes the same caution about observational ecommerce data.

Delay also shows up in bounce rates. Site Builder Report, a third-party source cited by Adwave, puts the average bounce rate at 9% for pages that load in 1 to 2 seconds and 38% for pages that take 5 seconds ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/website-speed-why-it-matters-and-how-to-fix-it-for-free), secondary). Compress job photos, check the first screen on a phone using mobile data, and fix the slowest element first.

## How should you track calls and bookings from the site?

Every tap-to-call and booking link should log an event in analytics. Adwave sets this as a standard for mobile pages ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/mobile-first-website-design)). Add the same tracking to the request form and its confirmation page, so you can see which page produced each request.

StackAdapt advises placing a tracking pixel on all pages of a website to build an audience pool for retargeting and conversion tracking ([StackAdapt](https://www.stackadapt.com/resources/blog/audience-targeting), secondary, as of 2022). Ryze AI, a reporting tool, says its Local Services Ads reporting tracks 18 metrics, including call duration, message response rate and booking completion percentage ([Ryze AI](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/google-local-services-ads-reporting-tool), secondary). Adwave also recommends tracking movement from blog posts to service pages, booking pages, forms and click-to-call buttons ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/blogging-for-small-business-is-it-still-worth-it-in-2026)).

Define conversion rate before you measure it: the share of visitors who take the action you want, such as a call or a booking ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/how-to-market-my-online-business)). Published averages vary. Outbrain cites an all-industry average between 2% and 3% ([Outbrain](https://www.outbrain.com/blog/advanced-retargeting-tips/), secondary). Compare your own site against your own history first. The [CTR and conversion benchmarks for local business](/blog/ctr-conversion-rate-benchmarks-local-business) guide covers how to do that.

## Worked example: what does a month of online leads cost?

All inputs below are assumptions, except the budget split, which follows Adwave's starting tier.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly budget | $1,000 (assumption, inside the $500 to $1,500 tier) |
| Google LSAs (50%) | $500 |
| Google Business Profile and reviews (30%) | $300 |
| Truck wraps and yard signs (20%) | $200 |
| Booked jobs from online channels | 6 (assumption) |
| Average job value | $400 (assumption) |

Cost per booked job: $1,000 / 6 = $166.67.

Revenue from booked jobs: 6 x $400 = $2,400.

Revenue per budget dollar: $2,400 / $1,000 = 2.4.

Rerun the sum with your own numbers. If only 3 of those 6 bookings turn into paid jobs, the cost per job roughly doubles to $333. Also note that the $300 and $200 lines may not map cleanly to a single booking, so count them only if you can track them.

## What should you check before launch?

1. Tap the phone number on a mobile device and confirm it dials.
2. Confirm the first screen shows your license number, service area and one primary button.
3. Open each service page and check that it names the job, the preparation needed, how you quote and the next step.
4. Submit the request form and the booking link, then confirm each event appears in analytics.
5. Test page speed on a phone using mobile data, and fix the slowest element first.
6. Change one headline at a time. HubSpot's 2024 marketing report, cited by Universal Mailworks, found that companies that regularly A/B test their materials see 20% to 30% higher conversion rates than those that do not. That figure covers marketing materials generally, not websites ([Universal Mailworks](https://www.universalmailworks.com/blog/door-hanger-marketing-design-costs-distribution), secondary).

## FAQ

### How much does an electrician website cost?

Build costs depend on the designer and the number of service pages, and no figure for them is given here. Plan the ad budget and tracking first, using the ranges above, then price the build against those numbers.

### Do I need online booking?

Not always. Emergency work usually needs a tap-to-call button, while planned jobs such as EV chargers or panel upgrades can use a request form or scheduling link.

### Should I use Google Local Services Ads?

Adwave places Local Services Ads at $1,000 to $3,000 a month, in the same band as Google Ads, with immediate results. Check Google's current eligibility and verification rules on its help pages before committing budget.

### What conversion rate should an electrician site aim for?

No electrician-specific benchmark is established here. Leadpages says top-performing landing pages reach a conversion rate of 10% or higher when the page stays focused on one goal ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/landing-page-best-practices-that-increase-conversions), secondary). Measure your own calls and requests before setting a target.

### Does page speed affect bounce rate?

Site Builder Report figures cited by Adwave show a 9% average bounce rate for pages loading in 1 to 2 seconds, rising to 38% at 5 seconds ([Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/website-speed-why-it-matters-and-how-to-fix-it-for-free), secondary).
