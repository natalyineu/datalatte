Start with the commission, because it is already your largest delivery marketing cost. On DoorDash's US plans the delivery commission is 15%, 25% or 30%, and on Uber Eats the marketplace fee is 20%, 25% or 30%. Only after you have chosen a plan should you decide how much extra to spend on in-app ads and offers. No source cited here publishes a standard "right" share of budget, so the split below is a method with labelled assumptions, not a benchmark.

This guide is for independent US restaurants using DoorDash or Uber Eats. Prices were read from each platform's merchant pricing page on 31 October 2026 and can change.

## What Do the Platforms Charge Before You Advertise?

DoorDash lists three plans. Basic has a 15% delivery commission, Plus 25% and Premier 30%, with pickup at 6% on all three. Online Ordering through your own DoorDash storefront is described as commission-free, though a payment processing fee applies and its amount is not listed. Uber Eats lists Lite at 20%, Plus at 25% and Premium at 30%, with pickup at 7% when in-store pricing is validated and 10% without.

Higher tiers buy more reach. DoorDash Plus and Premier add a wider delivery radius, and Premier includes a Sponsored Listing at no extra cost. Uber Eats Plus and Premium list boosted in-app visibility, and Premium includes up to $100 a month in matched ad credit.

| | Cheapest tier | Mid tier | Top tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DoorDash delivery | Basic 15% | Plus 25% | Premier 30% |
| Uber Eats marketplace | Lite 20% | Plus 25% | Premium 30% |
| Pickup (DoorDash / Uber Eats) | 6% / 7% or 10% | 6% / 7% or 10% | 6% / 7% or 10% |

## How Do Platform Ads Charge You?

DoorDash Sponsored Listings are pay-per-order, not pay-per-click. You set a bid, described as the maximum you will pay per order, and are not charged for clicks or impressions. Eligible stores can get up to $100 of credit per store, and stores that used Sponsored Listings before are not eligible. A minimum budget is not stated on the page.

The pricing for Uber Eats ads was not readable on the pages checked, so this guide gives no Uber Eats ad rates. Check your dashboard before you commit. Whichever platform you use, cap spend weekly and compare it with orders you could not have got otherwise.

## Which Orders Should You Pay to Acquire?

Pay for orders from people who are not already your customers. Paying a 25% commission and an ad bid to serve a regular who would have ordered direct is the most common leak. Separate three groups: new customers on the platform, existing customers who found you there, and customers who order from your own channels.

Uber Eats and DoorDash do not give you every customer's contact details, so push a reason to order direct, such as a card in the bag with a pickup discount, and look at what your local pickup rate costs (6% to 10% in the plans above, versus 15% to 30% for delivery). ## Worked Example: A $1,500 Monthly Budget

All inputs are **assumptions** for a restaurant doing $12,000 a month through one platform.

| Bucket | Share | Amount | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Platform plan upgrade (extra commission points) | decide first | see below | Visibility |
| In-app ads and offers | 40% | $600 | New customers only |
| Own channels: Google Business Profile, Instagram, email | 40% | $600 | Direct orders |
| Photos and menu work | 20% | $300 | Conversion |

The commission is not in the $1,500. Moving from a 15% to a 25% plan on $12,000 of delivery sales adds $1,200 a month, so it must beat other uses of that money. Keep it simple: run one plan for 4 weeks, track orders by source, then move 10% of the budget toward whichever bucket produced the cheapest new customer. Recheck after another 4 weeks.

## How Do You Know If It Works?

Track three numbers: platform orders from new customers, total cost per new customer (commission plus ad spend), and the share of orders that come direct. Many platform dashboards show new versus returning customers, but which reports you get differs by plan, so check your own.

Link your menu photos and offers to the on-platform listing, and make your Google profile show direct ordering. ## FAQ

### How much should a restaurant spend on delivery marketing?

No source cited here gives a standard share. Start with the commission as a fixed cost, add a small test budget for ads, and judge it by cost per new customer.

### Is DoorDash or Uber Eats cheaper?

On the lowest tiers, DoorDash Basic lists 15% and Uber Eats Lite 20% for delivery. The plans include different reach and features, so compare net profit per order, not only the rate.

### Are Sponsored Listings worth it?

DoorDash charges per order, with a bid you set and a trial credit of up to $100 per eligible store. Run a time-boxed test and compare new customers to cost.

### Can I avoid commissions entirely?

DoorDash describes Online Ordering as commission-free, with a processing fee. Direct orders through your own site or phone also avoid marketplace commission.

### How often do platform fees change?

They change without a fixed schedule, so re-read the merchant page and your contract each quarter.
