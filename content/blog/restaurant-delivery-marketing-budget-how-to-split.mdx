## What Does the Commission Cost Before You Advertise?

Marketplace commission is a cost on every delivery order, whether or not you spend a dollar on ads. Platforms usually sell several plans, and the higher plans typically give more visibility or a wider delivery area in exchange for a higher rate. That trade-off is the first decision in your budget, because it changes what each order is worth to you.

Pickup orders often carry a lower rate than delivery orders on the same platform. That difference matters because it shows you what a direct or pickup order costs compared with a delivered one. Read the pickup and delivery rates for each plan on the merchant page, and write them down before you look at any ad option.

Do not compare platforms only by their headline rate. Compare net profit per order after commission, packaging, and driver costs, which is the figure that shows whether a plan pays for itself. A lower commission on a plan with little visibility may earn less than a higher commission on a plan that brings in more new customers.

## What Does a $1,500 Monthly Budget Look Like?

The example below uses assumed inputs for a restaurant that does $12,000 a month in delivery sales through one platform. Every input is an **assumption** you should replace with your own figures.

| Bucket | Share | Amount (assumption) | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Platform plan upgrade (extra commission) | Decide first | See below | Visibility |
| In-app ads and offers | 40% | $600 | New customers only |
| Own channels: Google Business Profile, Instagram, email | 40% | $600 | Direct orders |
| Photos and menu work | 20% | $300 | Conversion |

The commission is not part of the $1,500. Assume your current plan charges 15% on delivery sales. Assume a higher plan charges 25%. On $12,000 of sales, the extra 10 points cost $1,200 a month, so the higher plan must bring in more profit than that from new customers before it is worth the switch.

Run one plan for 4 weeks, track orders by source, then move 10% of the budget toward whichever bucket produced the cheapest new customer. Recheck after another 4 weeks. A small, steady shift like this is easier to judge than a large change made on one month of data.

## Which Orders Are Worth Paying to Acquire?

Pay for orders from people who are not already your customers. Paying a commission and an ad bid to serve a regular who would have ordered direct is the most common leak in delivery budgets. Separate three groups: new customers found on the platform, existing customers who found you there, and customers who order through your own channels.

Marketplace apps usually do not share every customer's contact details, so you have to create a reason to order direct. A printed card in the bag offering a pickup or direct-order discount is one option, as is a QR code on the receipt. Once you know how many orders come direct, you can estimate how much of your platform spend goes to customers you could have kept without it.

The menu itself also affects conversion. The article on [restaurant menu optimisation for delivery platforms](/blog/restaurant-menu-optimisation-delivery-platforms) covers how item names, photos, and descriptions change whether a visitor orders, which matters before you pay for more visibility.

## How Do Platform Ads Compare with Google Ads Benchmarks?

Platform ads are not Google Ads, but Google's published benchmarks give a useful outside reference for paid clicks. Across all industries, WordStream's benchmark reports an average search click-through rate of 3.17% and an average search cost per click of $2.69, with display figures at 0.46% and $0.63. These are secondary figures, and the restaurant industry is not listed separately, so treat them as context rather than targets.

Google's own help documentation describes its Display Network as reaching 35 million websites and apps, which is the scale behind any display campaign you might run alongside platform ads. Whatever you choose, set a weekly cap and a clear test period. A campaign with no cap can spend a monthly budget in days, and a test with no end date is hard to judge.

If you run search ads for your own website, the [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) explains how to set a cap based on what each new customer is worth to you.

## How Do You Know If the Split Is Working?

Track three numbers: new customers from the platform, the total cost per new customer (commission plus ad spend), and the share of orders that come direct. Many platform dashboards show new versus returning customers, but the reports you get depend on your plan, so confirm which ones you can access before you start the test.

Compare the cost per new customer across buckets at the end of each 4-week test. If one bucket costs noticeably more per customer than the others, reduce its share and move the money toward the cheapest one. If you cannot tell where an order came from, that is a tracking problem to fix before you spend more.

Automation can help you keep this tracking up to date without a full-time marketing hire. The [guide to marketing automation tools for small business](/blog/best-marketing-automation-tools-small-business) compares options for sending order reminders and tracking results. If you want an outside check on where your delivery budget leaks, a [free audit](/free-audit) or a review of your [Google Ads setup](/services/google-ads) can show which channel is returning the most per dollar.

## FAQ

### How much should a restaurant spend on delivery marketing?

No cited source gives a standard share. Treat the platform commission as a fixed cost, set a small test budget for ads, and judge the spend by cost per new customer rather than a fixed percentage of sales.

### Should I pay for a higher-tier plan?

Only if the extra visibility brings in enough new profit to cover the higher commission. Use your own sales figures and the commission rates on the merchant page to calculate the break-even point before you switch.

### Are platform ads worth testing?

They can be, if you cap spend and track which orders are truly new. Run a time-boxed test and compare the new customers it brings in with what you paid for them.

### Do Google Ads benchmarks apply to restaurant delivery?

Not directly. The benchmarks cover industries broadly and are a secondary source, so use them to set expectations for paid clicks, not as a restaurant-specific target.

### How often should I review the budget split?

Review it every 4 weeks during testing, then once a quarter. Platform rates and fees can change, so check the merchant page each time you review.
