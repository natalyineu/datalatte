UTM parameters are tags added to the end of a link so Google Analytics can show which email, post, flyer QR code or ad sent each visitor. Google says to always use three of them: utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign. Three more are optional or recommended: utm_id, utm_term and utm_content. Any missing required tag shows up as "(not set)" in reports.

This guide covers what each tag means, the naming rules that keep reports clean, how GA4 sorts tagged traffic into channels, and a worked example for a local business.

## What do the UTM parameters mean?

Google's Analytics Help lists the following. The examples are Google's own.

| Parameter | Status | What it identifies | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| utm_source | Always use | The referrer | google, newsletter4, billboard |
| utm_medium | Always use | The marketing medium | cpc, banner, email |
| utm_campaign | Always use | Product, slogan or promo code | spring_sale |
| utm_id | Strongly recommended | A specific campaign or promotion; match the IDs in your data uploads | not given |
| utm_term | Optional | The paid keyword | not given |
| utm_content | Optional | Which creative or link was clicked, such as two links in one email | toplink |

A tagged link is the normal page address, then a question mark, then pairs joined with equals signs and separated by ampersands. For example: yourshop.com/offer?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=spring_sale.

## Why do naming rules matter so much?

Google states that parameter values are case sensitive. utm_source=google and utm_source=Google are different values, and SpringSale and Spring_Sale are reported separately. Google's own advice is to pick lowercase as a standard.

In practice, write down a short naming sheet before the first link goes out. Decide the exact words for each source (instagram, facebook, newsletter, flyer) and each medium (email, social, cpc, qr), then reuse them. One shared spreadsheet with a row per link is enough for a small team.

Untagged or half-tagged links cause the "(not set)" rows, so check each link before you publish it.

## How does GA4 sort tagged traffic into channels?

GA4 assigns every session to a default channel group using source and medium. Your medium value decides where the traffic lands, so it is not a free-text field.

- **Email:** source or medium is email, e-mail, e_mail or "e mail".
- **Organic Social:** the source is a known social site, or the medium is social, social-network, social-media, sm, "social network" or "social media".
- **Paid Social:** the source is a known social site and the medium contains "cp", equals ppc, or starts with retargeting or paid.
- **Referral:** the medium is referral, app or link.
- **Unassigned:** used when no rule matches the data.

Channel matching is not case sensitive, but the medium words must match exactly, except where a pattern is listed. A medium of "newsletter" would not count as Email, so a mailing would land in Unassigned. Use "email".

## Can you use UTM tags on Google Ads links?

Not usually. Google Ads auto-tagging adds a GCLID (Google Click Identifier) to the landing page address. Google's help states that in GA4 auto-tagging is prioritized over manual tagging, and there is no setting to override it. So leave auto-tagging on for Google Ads and use UTM tags for everything else: email, social posts, Business Profile links, partner sites and printed QR codes.

For paid Meta or TikTok ads, UTM tags are the standard way to see the click in GA4. Google also suggests using the dynamic tags that an ad platform offers where possible, which saves typing. The dynamic fields differ per platform, so check each platform's own help page.

## Worked example: a salon tags four links

All inputs below are **assumptions** for illustration, not benchmarks.

A hair salon sends a monthly email, posts on Instagram, prints a QR code on window flyers and puts a booking link in its Google Business Profile. It tags each one:

| Where the link lives | utm_source | utm_medium | utm_campaign |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly email | newsletter | email | november_colour_offer |
| Instagram bio | instagram | social | always_on_bio |
| Window flyer QR | flyer | qr | november_colour_offer |
| Business Profile booking link | google_business_profile | referral | always_on_profile |

Result in GA4: the email lands in the Email channel, the Instagram link in Organic Social and the Business Profile link in Referral, because those mediums match the rules above. The medium qr is not on the lists above, so the salon checks where flyer traffic lands and reads the source / medium report to compare the flyer with the email.

One campaign name across email and flyer lets the owner compare both against the same offer.

## What mistakes should you avoid?

Three problems account for most broken reports. First, mixed case and spelling variants, which split one source into several rows. Second, a missing required tag. Third, medium values that do not match the channel rules above.

Use tags only on links that bring people in from outside. ## FAQ

### Which UTM parameters are required?

Google says to always use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign. utm_id is strongly recommended. utm_term and utm_content are optional.

### Are UTM values case sensitive?

Yes. Google states that parameter values are case sensitive, so Google and google are separate values. Use lowercase throughout.

### Should I add UTM tags to Google Ads links?

Normally no. In GA4, auto-tagging is prioritized over manual tagging, so manual tags on Google Ads clicks are overridden.

### What does "(not set)" mean in my reports?

Google states that missing UTM parameters result in "(not set)" values in reporting. Check that all three required tags are on the link.

### Why is my email traffic in Unassigned?

Probably the medium does not match a channel rule. Email is matched when the source or medium is email, e-mail, e_mail or "e mail". Other words, such as "newsletter", do not count.
