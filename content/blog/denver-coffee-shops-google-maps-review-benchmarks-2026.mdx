In other words, almost every shop you compete with already has serious social proof, and a rating a little above 4.4 is what "normal" looks like.

- **177** coffee shops analysed

One word about "analysed": these are the coffee shops our Google Maps searches returned for Denver, not a census of every place that sells espresso in the metro area. Denver was searched more deeply than most cities in our dataset, which is why the sample is larger than the roughly 50 businesses we collected elsewhere. Treat the figures as a realistic picture of who shows up when a customer searches, not as an official count.

## What the numbers mean for a new coffee shop

If you opened in the last year or two, the table can feel discouraging. A median of 340 means a typical competitor has been collecting reviews for years. Here is a calmer way to read it.

**You will not catch up on volume quickly, and you do not need to in order to get customers.** Only 2 of 177 shops have fewer than 50 reviews, so under 50 reviews would put you in a very small group. That is a signal to take reviews seriously from week one, not a reason to panic. A new shop with a fresh, well-run profile can still be found for specific searches, such as "coffee near me" from someone standing two blocks away, because proximity and relevance matter alongside review counts.

**Compete on what a big number cannot show.** A shop with several hundred reviews may have most of them from a few years ago. Recent reviews, specific mentions of your drinks and your space, photos from this month and fast replies tell a visitor that you are open, active and loved right now.

**Aim for steady, not heroic.** Instead of chasing 340, set a pace you can keep: for example, a handful of new reviews per week from real customers. Consistency compounds, and it also looks natural.

**Use the rating gap.** The average rating of 4.45 is not especially high. If you keep your own rating strong by serving customers well and responding quickly to problems, a rating at or above that average is within reach even with fewer reviews. Just remember that a rating based on a small number of reviews can move quickly with a single bad day.

## What the numbers mean for an established coffee shop

If you are one of the shops sitting at or above the median, your problem is different. You already have the volume, so your risks are staleness and complacency.

**Check your recency.** Open your profile and look at the newest reviews. If the latest ones are months old, a newer shop with fewer total reviews but a steady stream of recent ones may look more attractive to a customer scrolling the list.

**Protect the rating.** With a Denver average of 4.45, even a few percentage points of difference in rating is visible. Read your lower-star reviews for patterns, such as wait times at the morning rush, mobile order mix-ups or seating, and fix the pattern rather than only replying to the individual review.

**Reply to everything.** With hundreds of reviews, replies are the one thing a competitor cannot copy overnight. A short, human reply to every review, positive or negative, signals an owner who is paying attention. ## A 30-day review and Google Business Profile plan

This plan works whether you have 20 reviews or 500. It assumes you can spend about 30 to 45 minutes a week.

### Week 1: Fix the foundation

1. **Claim and verify** your Google Business Profile if you have not already, and make sure your name, address, phone number and website match your signage and website exactly.
2. **Choose the right primary category** (for example, coffee shop) and add secondary categories that truly describe what you do.
3. **Check your hours**, including holiday hours, and your attributes such as outdoor seating or Wi-Fi if they apply.
4. **Upload fresh photos**: storefront, interior, your best drinks, your team. Aim for a dozen good images rather than a hundred mediocre ones.
5. **Read your last 20 reviews** and write down the three things people praise most and the two they complain about.

### Week 2: Make asking easy

1. **Get your direct review link** from your profile and turn it into a QR code.
2. **Place the QR code** where happy customers already pause: next to the register, on the pickup counter, on takeaway cup sleeves or table cards.
3. **Train your team** to ask in a single friendly sentence after a positive moment, for example when a regular says they love the new seasonal drink. Never offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, and never ask only for 5-star ratings. Google's policies prohibit both.
4. **Reply to every unanswered review** from the last 60 days. Keep replies short, specific and warm.

### Week 3: Add signal beyond the star count

1. **Post an update** on your profile, such as a seasonal menu or event. Posts show activity.
2. **Add menu items and products** so people can see what you serve from the listing itself.
3. **Answer questions** that customers have asked on your profile, and seed the common ones such as parking, oat milk and dog-friendly seating.
4. **Add photos weekly**, ideally taken by your team during the day rather than stock images.

### Week 4: Measure and adjust

1. **Count your new reviews** this month and note how many mention specific drinks or the space.
2. **Look at the Performance data** in your profile for searches, calls, direction requests and website clicks.
3. **Decide what to repeat.** If the QR code at the register produced most of your reviews, add a second one at the pickup counter.

## FAQ

### How many Google reviews does a Denver coffee shop need?

There is no magic number. In our October 2026 data the median Denver coffee shop has 340 reviews, so that is the middle of the market you are being compared to. Use it as context, not a target you must hit before you can compete. A steady stream of recent, genuine reviews matters more than a single number on one day.

### What is a good Google rating for a coffee shop in Denver?

Across the 177 shops we analysed, the average rating was 4.45. A rating at or above that level puts you in line with the typical shop. Ratings are an average of what customers say, so the better question is what the written reviews say about your coffee, your speed and your space.

### Is it too late to compete if I have fewer than 50 reviews?

No. Only 2 of 177 shops analysed were under 50 reviews, so you would be in a small group, but reviews are only one part of how people choose. Complete profile details, current photos, close proximity to the searcher and quick replies all help. Build the review habit now and the count will rise.

### Do more reviews mean I will rank higher on Google Maps?

Not by themselves. Google has said that local results are based on relevance, distance and prominence, and prominence includes information like review count and score. Reviews are one input among several, and we cannot say precisely how much weight they carry. Quality, recency and responses are worth caring about because customers read them, whatever the algorithm does.

### Should I reply to negative reviews?

Yes, calmly and briefly. Thank the person, acknowledge the specific issue, and offer to fix it offline. Future customers read your replies to judge how you treat people.

### Can I ask customers for reviews?

Yes. Google allows you to ask. What it does not allow is paying for reviews, offering incentives, or selectively gating who you ask based on how happy they seem. Keep it simple and honest.
