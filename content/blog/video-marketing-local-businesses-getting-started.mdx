## Why Does This Article Leave Out Platform Statistics?

The source set for this article contained no survey data on video reach, and no platform guidance on clip length or resolution. Earlier figures on platform use and upload rules could not be matched to any source, so they were removed rather than repeated from memory. A video plan built on unverified platform rules can waste a week of filming.

The [Small Business Administration marketing page](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/grow-your-business/marketing-your-business) returned "Page not found" when checked, so it contributed no guidance. Before filming, open the help page for each platform you plan to use and note its current length and format rules.

## Which Channel Should a Local Business Start With?

Start with the place where customers already find or contact you. For many local businesses that is a Google Business Profile, a social page or a messaging app. Choose one channel for the first eight weeks so that results are easy to read.

Scheduling tools become useful once posting spans several channels. The comparison of [Hootsuite and Sprout Social for small businesses](/blog/hootsuite-vs-sprout-social-small-business-local-business) explains the cost and feature differences, which matter once a weekly routine is in place.

## How Can a Venue Turn Its Own Details Into Video Ideas?

The [Local Beer website](https://localbeer.co/) lists several details that suit short clips: a patio, more than 40 local beers on tap daily, a dish list, and a happy-hour window from 3:00 PM to 6:00 PM. Each detail points to a filming idea that needs no script. A clip of the tap wall during happy hour shows the offer and the place together.

The same site lists catering and private-party pages. A short tour of a room being set up before a party is a second idea drawn from what the business already sells. A salon, plumber or bakery can use the same method: list five things customers can see, then film each one in about ten seconds.

## What Does a Weekly Video Habit Cost?

The table below uses assumptions, not sourced figures. It models a coffee shop with one staff member who films three clips in a single batch each week.

| Step | Minutes (assumption) |
| --- | --- |
| Film three clips in one batch | 25 |
| Trim and add captions | 40 |
| Upload to one profile and one social channel | 25 |
| Total | 90 |

Worked example, with every input an assumption:

- Labour: 1.5 hours a week at an assumed $18 an hour, so $27 a week.
- Average order from a tracked mention: an assumed $6.
- Break-even: $27 divided by $6 is 4.5, so about 5 tracked orders a week cover the labour.
- If only 4 orders a week mention the clips, the video costs more than it returns. That is a reason to change the format, not a reason to keep filming unchanged.

## How Do You Track Whether Video Brings Customers?

Give each channel its own code or link, such as a discount word in the caption or a booking link used only on that post. Ask every new customer where they heard about you, and record the answer alongside each sale. Simple bookkeeping software can hold these records, and the [FreshBooks and QuickBooks comparison](/blog/freshbooks-vs-quickbooks-small-business-local-business) covers how small firms keep sales records.

Use this routine each week:

1. Count the tracked orders or bookings for each clip type.
2. Compare the total with the labour cost in the worked example.
3. Keep the format with the most tracked mentions for the next four weeks.
4. Drop any format that produced no tracked mentions after eight weeks.

Views and likes show attention. Only tracked orders show whether a video brought a sale.

## What Should You Film First?

Film what a customer would see on arrival: the entrance, the main product being made or served, and one short process clip. Keep each clip to a single idea. A venue like the one above could film the tap wall, a plated dish and the patio in turn, one clip each.

Hands, products and the workspace can carry a clip without anyone on camera. That is a practical choice rather than a platform requirement, and it suits owners who prefer not to be filmed.

## Does Video Need Special Software?

Not for a first test. A phone camera and a basic trimming app cover the weekly plan above. Specialist tools exist for other jobs. The [Local website](https://localwp.com/) states that its download is free and always will be, but it describes itself as a WordPress development tool, not a video editor. Choose the tool that fits the job, and avoid paying for features you will not use in the first eight weeks.

## FAQ

### How long should a local business video be?

No source checked for this article confirmed a platform limit, so check the current help page for each channel before filming. Keeping each clip to one idea and under a minute is a reasonable starting assumption.

### Do I need to be on camera?

No. Hands, products and the workspace can carry a clip, so being on camera is optional.

### How do I know whether video works?

Give each clip its own code or link, then count tracked orders or bookings each week. Views alone do not show whether customers came in.

### Which platform should I start with?

Start with the place where your customers already contact you. Use one channel for eight weeks before adding another.
