LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put the average Education & Instruction click at $4.81. At that price, $500 of search ads before term starts buys about 104 clicks. If 8 in 100 clicks become a trial booking, three-quarters of those turn up, and half of them enrol, you have paid roughly $160 for each new child. Every one of those rates is my assumption, not a benchmark. The point: a dance school, swim school, martial arts club or music studio can work out its own number before spending anything, and then decide where to put the effort.

This guide is for owners of children's classes in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Parents buy differently from adults joining a gym. They are checking safety, convenience and how their child will feel, usually on a phone in the evening. So the plan below leads with search, proof of safety, a low-risk first class and retention across terms. Dollar figures are US benchmarks; run the same math with your local click prices.

## How Parents Decide

Most parents move through the same sequence:

1. A trigger: a child asks for a hobby, a term is about to start, a friend recommends something.
2. A search ("kids swimming lessons near me") or a question in a local parents' group.
3. A shortlist of two or three, compared on reviews, photos, timetable, location and price.
4. A trial or enquiry, often outside working hours.
5. A decision after the first class, based on how the child felt and how staff handled it.

You can influence steps 2 to 4 with marketing and step 5 with the quality of the first class. Spend your effort in that order.

## Get Found: Google Business Profile First

Searches like "gymnastics club near me" often return a map pack before any website, so the profile does a lot of work. Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) give rules that matter here:

- **Name:** exclude taglines, hours, phone numbers, URLs and "service or location details". Your profile name should be the name on your sign, not "Little Stars Dance Classes Best Kids Ballet Austin".
- **Categories:** use as few as possible to describe your core business, and pick ones that say what you are, not everything you offer.
- **Hours:** the guidelines say certain businesses shouldn't provide hours, including those with varied schedules, "like schedules for different types of activities, including showtimes, worship services, or classes", and they list schools among the examples. Whether a class studio falls under that is a judgment call. My reading is that if your hours genuinely vary by class, check what the guidelines say for your situation before listing front-desk hours that do not match what a parent will find, and put the timetable on your website.

Add photos with consent (see below), and link the profile to a page for each age group. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers the rest.

## Pages Per Class, Not One Page for Everything

Create one page for each offer a parent searches for: "Toddler Swim Lessons", "Teen Street Dance", "Beginner Piano for Age 7+". Each needs the age range, level, timetable, price, location, what to bring and one booking button. Put the neighbourhood and nearby areas in the text naturally.

## Paid Search: A Small, Timed Campaign

Run Search ads in the few weeks before each term, not all year at a flat level. Setup in brief:

1. **Location:** a radius drawn from where your current families live. Pull your last 50 enrolments and look at the zip or postcodes.
2. **Keywords:** group by what the parent wants, such as "kids swimming lessons [town]", "ballet classes for 4 year olds", "beginner karate for children", all in phrase or exact match. Add negatives: `jobs`, `teacher training`, `course online`, `free`, `adult`, `worksheet`, `app`. Our negative keywords guide explains how to apply them.
3. **Ads:** put the age and the offer in the headline ("Kids Swim Lessons Ages 3-12. Free Trial Class"). Say only what is true.
4. **Landing page:** the class page above, not your home page. See [landing page best practices](/blog/landing-page-best-practices).
5. **Tracking:** count form submissions and calls, not clicks. Our conversion tracking guide has the steps.

Targeting children is not the goal here; parents buy. Google's [personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) says users under 18 are not eligible for personalized advertising of any kind, so aim at the adult searcher through what they type. If you are new to the platform, start with the Google Ads beginner guide.

## Show Safety Plainly

Safety is often the tie-breaker, so put the evidence where a parent will see it. Include only what is true and current:

- Staff checks. In England and Wales, the government's [guidance on regulated activity with children](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dbs-guidance-leaflets/regulated-activity-with-children) lists "teaching, training, or instruction, care for, or supervision of children" as a specified activity, counted when it happens on more than 3 days in a 30-day period, and says employers and organisations request the Enhanced DBS check with a Children's Barred List check. Australia's Working With Children Checks and US state requirements differ by state or territory and are not covered here, so check your own state's screening body.
- First aid training, class sizes and child-to-staff ratios.
- A written safeguarding policy, insurance and your sickness and pick-up rules.
- A "Meet the team" page with names, short bios and photos.

## Photos and Video of Children

The NSPCC's [photography and sharing images guidance](https://learning.nspcc.org.uk/safeguarding-child-protection/photography-and-sharing-images) is UK guidance, but the practice is sensible anywhere. It says children should be consulted about use of their photo or video, that for under-16s you should also get parental consent before taking it, and that organisations should have a written photography and filming policy. Do the same: get written consent, avoid names in captions, and remove images when asked. If you are in a place with different laws, check them. When in doubt, show hands, feet, backs and staff.

## Offer a Low-Risk First Step

A trial or taster is the bridge between interest and enrolment: a free or paid trial class, a short taster course, an open day or a bring-a-friend week. Make booking possible at 9pm, confirm it automatically and say what to bring. The gap between "booked" and "turned up" is where money leaks, so send a reminder the day before.

## Reviews and Referrals

Parents trust other parents. Ask after the first few weeks, send a direct review link and reply to every review. One rule matters for referral schemes: Google's [review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) says merchants may not offer rewards in exchange for posting a review, and may not selectively solicit positive ones. A free class for introducing a friend is a referral, not a review reward, so keep the two completely separate and never tie a perk to a Google review. More tactics in how to get more 5-star Google reviews.

## Where Else Parents Look

- Local parent groups, following each group's rules on promotion
- School and nursery newsletters (ask first)
- Libraries, cafes and community centre boards
- Partnerships: a dance school with a shoe shop, a swim school with a children's physio

Be helpful first and mention your classes where they fit. Paid social can fill a new intake; see [Facebook ads for family businesses](/blog/facebook-ads-for-family-businesses).

## Keep Families Across Terms

Retention is where this business model makes its money. Send a welcome message, a reminder before each class, a short progress note each term, an early re-enrolment prompt before term ends, and a message when a waiting-list place opens. Use an email tool or your booking system, with consent and an unsubscribe link. Our email marketing guide covers the basics. Plan the calendar: back-to-school, new year, holiday camps, birthday parties and recitals. See back-to-school marketing for the autumn push.

## The Math

Take a $500 budget for the weeks before a term. Every row is an **assumption** except the click prices, which come from the LocaliQ lines above. Replace the rest after one term of data.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget (assumption) | $500 | $500 | $500 |
| Cost per click | $6.50 (assumption) | $4.81 (LocaliQ Education) | $2.77 (LocaliQ Sports & Rec) |
| Clicks | 77 | 104 | 180 |
| Click to trial booking (assumption) | 4% | 8% | 10% |
| Trial bookings | 3.1 | 8.3 | 18.0 |
| Booking to attended (assumption) | 65% | 75% | 85% |
| Attended to enrolled (assumption) | 35% | 50% | 60% |
| New enrolments | 0.7 | 3.1 | 9.2 |
| Ad cost per enrolment | $714 | $160 | $54 |

The Strong column pairs the cheapest benchmark click with generous rates, so do not plan around it. Two uses:

- **Your ceiling click price.** Max CPC = most you will pay per enrolled child x booking rate x attendance rate x enrolment rate. In the Base column the three rates multiply to 0.03, so if you will pay $150 per child, the ceiling is $150 x 0.03 = $4.50 a click.
- **What a child is worth.** Multiply the term fee by the number of terms a child typically stays. A child who pays $180 a term and stays three terms is $540 in revenue (assumed numbers). Use your own enrolment records, not a guess.

The levers you control most are the ones outside Google: a reminder text lifts attendance, and a good first class lifts enrolment.

## Do This Today

1. Pull your last 50 enrolments and note each family's town or zip code. Draw your ad radius from them.
2. Work out the most you will pay for one enrolled child: term fee x average terms stayed x the share you can spend on acquisition.
3. Check your Google Business Profile name, categories and hours against the Google guidelines above, and add a "Meet the team" and safeguarding section to your site.
4. Build a trial-class page for your biggest age group with one booking button, then run a small Search campaign starting four weeks before term.
5. Set up a day-before reminder for every trial and a written photo-consent form before posting any images.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best way to market a kids activity business?**
A: Start with the basics parents check: a correct Google Business Profile, a page per class, clear safety information, reviews and a low-risk trial class. Add paid search before each term, and email for retention.

**Q: Can I post photos of children on my website and social media?**
A: Only with consent and under a written policy. The NSPCC's guidance says children should be consulted and, for under-16s, parents should also consent. Local law may add requirements, so check yours.

**Q: How much should I spend on advertising?**
A: There is no official number. At LocaliQ's $4.81 Education & Instruction click, $500 buys about 104 clicks. Work backward from what an enrolled child is worth, using the ceiling formula above. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) helps set the overall figure.

**Q: Can I give a discount for a Google review?**
A: No. Google's policy says merchants may not offer rewards in exchange for reviews. Keep review requests and referral rewards separate.

**Q: How do I reduce drop-off between terms?**
A: Send an early re-enrolment prompt, share a short progress note each term and make rebooking one click. Track re-enrolment by term so you can see the drop-off in numbers.
