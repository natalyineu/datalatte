To attract more families to a kids activities business, make it easy for parents to find you on Google, trust you through reviews and clear safety information, and try you with a low-risk trial class. Then keep them with reminders, term-by-term communication and referrals.

Parents choose kids activities differently from adults choosing a gym. They are checking safety, convenience, teacher quality and how their child will feel, often on a phone late in the evening. Marketing that answers those worries tends to beat marketing that shouts about discounts. Here is a practical plan for dance schools, gymnastics clubs, swim schools, martial arts, art and music classes, tutoring and play centres.

## Understand how parents decide

Most parents move through a rough sequence:

1. They notice a need (a child wants a hobby, a school term is starting, a friend recommends something).
2. They search or ask: "kids swimming lessons near me", or post in a local parents' group.
3. They shortlist two or three options and check reviews, photos, schedule, location and price.
4. They book a trial or enquire, often outside working hours.
5. They decide after the first class, based on how their child felt and how staff handled the experience.

Your marketing needs to support each step, especially steps two to four.

## Get found on Google

Many parents search for activities close to home. The main assets are:

- **Google Business Profile**: Choose the most accurate category (for example "Dance school" or "Gymnastics center"), add classes as services, list ages and levels in the description, and upload real photos with permission and care around children's images. Use our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) as a guide.
- **Website pages per class or age group**: One page for "Toddler Swim Lessons", another for "Teen Street Dance", each with timetable, price, location, what to bring and a booking button.
- **Local wording**: Mention the neighbourhood and nearby areas you serve.
- **Google Ads**: Useful before term starts or for new classes, with targeting limited to a sensible radius. See our [Google Ads beginner guide](/blog/google-ads-for-beginners-first-campaign-step-by-step).

## Show safety and quality plainly

Safety is often the deciding factor, so put the evidence on your site and profile:

- Staff qualifications, background checks (DBS in the UK, working with children checks in Australia, state requirements in the US and similar elsewhere) and first aid training
- Child-to-staff ratios and class sizes
- Your safeguarding or child protection policy
- Insurance, facility cleanliness and equipment checks
- Arrival, pick-up and sickness policies

Only state what is true and current, and make documents easy to find. A "Meet the team" page with names, short bios and photos reassures parents.

## Be careful with photos and video of children

Children's images need extra care. Get written consent from parents before publishing, explain how you will use images, avoid naming children in captions, and respect any requests to remove content. Check your local laws and your own safeguarding policy. When in doubt, show activities from behind, show hands and feet, or feature staff and equipment.

## Offer a low-risk first step

A trial lesson or open day lets parents and children test the fit. Options include:

- A paid or free trial class
- A short taster course
- An open day or "bring a friend" week
- A free consultation or assessment, for tutoring or therapy-adjacent activities

Make booking easy: online scheduling with tools such as Mindbody, Acuity Scheduling, Jackrabbit Class, Teamup or similar systems, or a simple form if you are small. Confirm bookings automatically with a clear message about what to bring and where to go.

## Use reviews and word of mouth

For kids activities, parents trust other parents. Build a steady flow of reviews:

- Ask at pick-up or via a short text or email after the first few weeks
- Send a direct Google review link
- Reply to every review, thanking parents and staying professional
- Share parent comments (with permission) on your site and social channels

Referral schemes also fit well. A small thank-you for introducing a friend, such as a free class or a discount on the next term, is common and usually inexpensive. Check that your scheme is fair and clear. For more tactics, see [how to get more 5-star Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-5-star-google-reviews-local-business).

## Reach parents where they gather

Parents share recommendations in communities. Consider:

- **Local Facebook groups** for parents or neighbourhoods, following each group's rules on promotion
- **Schools and nurseries**: Ask permission to leave flyers or send information through newsletters
- **Libraries, community centres and cafes** with notice boards
- **Local family event listings** and parenting websites
- **Partnerships** with complementary businesses, such as a dance school and a shoe shop, or a swim school and a children's physio

Be helpful rather than promotional: answer questions, offer genuine tips and mention your classes where it fits.

## Social media for family businesses

Instagram and Facebook are common for reaching parents. Share:

- Class highlights, with consent
- Staff introductions and short tips
- Term dates, waiting list updates and events
- Showcase performances or progress milestones
- Answers to common parent questions

Paid ads can fill new term intakes. Target parents within your travel radius and send them to a page with timetable and booking. ## Email and text keep families enrolled

Kids activities depend on retention across terms. A simple communication plan might include:

- A welcome message with what to expect and what to bring
- Weekly or term reminders for classes, closures and events
- A progress update or news email each term
- Early re-enrolment reminders before term ends
- Waiting list notifications when a place opens up

Use a tool like Mailchimp, Brevo or the email features in your booking system, and make sure you have proper consent and an easy unsubscribe. Our guide to [email marketing for small business](/blog/email-marketing-for-small-business) explains how to start.

## Plan around the calendar

Demand is seasonal. Plan campaigns for:

- Back-to-school and new-year sign-ups
- Holiday camps and school break programmes
- Birthday party bookings, which can be a strong secondary revenue stream
- Summer or autumn enrolment drives
- Recital, tournament or showcase events

Start promotion several weeks ahead, since parents plan their schedules early.

## Track what works

Keep a simple record of where each enquiry or booking came from:

- Ask "How did you hear about us?" on forms and at first contact
- Use dedicated links or codes for flyers, partners and ads
- Review monthly: enquiries, trial attendance, conversion to enrolment and retention across terms

Focus on the channels that bring families who stay, not just families who enquire.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best way to market a kids activity business?**
A: A strong Google Business Profile, a clear website, parent reviews and a low-risk trial class are the core. Add local groups, referrals, email and social media as you grow.

**Q: Can I post photos of children on my website and social media?**
A: Only with clear, written parental consent and in line with your safeguarding policy and local laws. Avoid naming children and honour removal requests promptly.

**Q: How much should I spend on advertising?**
A: It varies. Start with a small budget focused on your busiest enrolment periods, and measure cost per trial and per enrolment. Our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide) can help set a figure.

**Q: How do I reduce drop-off between terms?**
A: Communicate regularly, show progress, make re-enrolment simple and early, and ask parents for feedback. Small touches, like recognising a child's achievement, tend to matter.
