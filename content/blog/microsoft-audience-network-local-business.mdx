The Microsoft Audience Network lets a local business show native, display and video ads outside Bing search results. Microsoft's current documentation calls these ads "Audience ads" and lists MSN, Outlook, Microsoft 365, Microsoft Casual Games and other partners as placements. The old claim that it is a "cheap" alternative to display cannot be checked: Microsoft does not publish a typical cost per click or per thousand impressions.

This guide explains what the product is, the two ways to start, what targeting is documented and how to test it with a small budget. Anything Microsoft does not publish is marked as such.

## What Are Microsoft Audience Ads?

Per Microsoft Learn, Audience ads target people "based on powerful intent signals" and reach them in display, native and video placements. The placements work across devices and include MSN, Microsoft Outlook, Microsoft 365 and Microsoft Casual Games. Clicks go to the landing page you set.

Microsoft says the targeting draws on its audience intelligence, which includes search and web activity, LinkedIn profile data, demographic and customer data. The page was last updated in June 2026. Older articles that mention Edge placements or Taboola partnerships may be outdated, so check your own account for what is available in your country.

## How Do You Start With Audience Ads?

Microsoft's documentation describes three routes:

- **Search ads extended to native.** Your search ads can appear in native placements using your headlines, text, URL and image extensions. A search ad in Outlook matches the inbox look, and one on MSN matches the slideshow around it.
- **An Audience campaign with responsive ads.** You add images and build a responsive ad. At least one wide image is needed.
- **A feed-based Audience campaign.** This needs a Microsoft Merchant Center store and product catalog, so it suits shops with an online catalog.

For most local service businesses, the first route is the lowest effort because it reuses a search account you already have.

## What Targeting Can a Local Business Use?

For responsive Audience ads, Microsoft lists age, company name, gender, industry, job function, location and radius criteria. Feed-based campaigns add audience criteria. Location and radius are the two that matter for a shop, salon or clinic.

Two practical rules come from the documentation. First, supply four images, one for each supported aspect ratio, if you want to qualify for all Audience placements. Second, if targeting is too narrow the ad group will not serve, so widen the location or add dimensions. Microsoft also notes that licensed stock images may be added automatically if you have no image extensions; you can opt out through support.

## How Does It Compare With Google Display?

| | Microsoft Audience ads | Google Display campaigns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where ads run | MSN, Outlook, Microsoft 365, Casual Games, partners | Google Display Network, including YouTube and Gmail |
| Stated reach | Not published as a single number | 35 million websites and apps |
| Default targeting | Intent signals, plus age, gender, location, radius | Optimized targeting, based on landing page keywords |
| Learning period | Not stated | Typically 5 to 7 days |
| Published CPC or CPM | None | None in Google Ads Help |

The table shows why "cheap" is a guess. Neither company publishes a price; both run auctions. The only reliable comparison is a test on your own account. ## Is It Worth Testing for a Local Business?

It can be, in one situation: you already run Bing search and want extra reach for the same message. Read our [Microsoft Advertising for local business guide](/blog/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising-local-business) first and prove Bing search works before adding native ads.

If you have no search history, start with Google or Meta instead. Audience ads reward accounts with clear conversion tracking, and they reach people who were not searching for you. That is awareness, so judge it by calls, bookings and store visits, not by clicks alone.

## How Much Should You Test With?

Microsoft does not publish a minimum for Audience campaigns in the pages checked, so use your own budget rule. The example below is an **assumption** to show the arithmetic, not a forecast.

| Input (all assumptions) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Test budget | $300 for 30 days |
| Share going to Audience ads | 30% ($90) |
| Cost per click you must beat | $1.50 |
| Clicks at that price | 60 |
| Landing page conversion rate | 3% |
| Enquiries | about 2 |
| Cost per enquiry | about $45 |

If a new customer is worth more than that to you after your close rate, keep testing. If not, move the money back to search. Because the real CPC is unknown, replace $1.50 with your own first-week number from the reports. Microsoft's reporting lets you filter ad distribution by Audience, so you can see these ads apart from search. ## FAQ

### Is the Microsoft Audience Network still called that?

Microsoft's current developer documentation uses the name Audience ads and says they run on the Microsoft Audience Network. Older posts and tools still use MSAN. Both names refer to the same non-search placements.

### Can I run it without a search campaign?

Yes. Microsoft describes dedicated Audience campaigns with responsive ads, and feed-based Audience campaigns for product catalogs. Extending an existing search campaign is the simpler route.

### What does it cost?

Microsoft does not publish a typical price in the pages checked. Cost depends on the auction, your targeting and your creative. Treat any "cheaper than Google Display" claim as unverified until you see your own numbers.

### How do I see how Audience ads perform?

Microsoft says you can filter performance reports by ad distribution to isolate Audience ads. Two reports, age and gender audience and professional demographics audience, are specific to these campaigns.
