## What Are Nextdoor Recommendations and Faves?

A recommendation is a neighbor's public endorsement of a local business. It appears on your Business Page and, according to Nextdoor, in the main Nextdoor newsfeed. A Fave is the lighter action: Nextdoor's business blog calls it the primary action a neighbor can take on your page, and every Fave is shown there.

In August 2025 Nextdoor introduced a feature also named Faves, described as AI-powered and built from neighbors' past recommendations, with summaries and pre-answered questions. Nextdoor's announcement does not say what business owners can do with it, so treat it as a reason to collect recommendations, not as a tool you control.

The numbers in the stat row come from Nextdoor's own marketing and are not independent research. They show how Nextdoor describes its audience, not what your page will earn.

## How Do You Set Up Your Page to Collect Them?

Claim your free Business Page first. Nextdoor's guide says to find your unique page link in your page settings, then share it anywhere: social media, email, your website or storefront signage. Nextdoor's own posts name that location differently, as the "Page" tab in one and "Your Reputation" with a "Copy link" button in another, so look for whichever your account shows.

Add your address, contact details, services and a clear photo before you ask anyone. Our [Nextdoor business page setup guide](/blog/nextdoor-business-page-setup-guide) walks through the claim step by step.

## Where Should You Ask for a Recommendation?

Nextdoor suggests asking in person after a sale or job, in social posts, in Business Posts, by email and with a website banner. Pick the places your customers already see you.

- At the counter or end of the appointment, with a small sign that shows the page link.
- In the receipt or confirmation email.
- On your website footer, next to your Google review link.
- In a reply when a neighbor tags your business, thanking them and pointing them to your page.

Recommendations do not have to come from paying customers. Nextdoor says likes and recommendations "don't need to only come from people that have completed a transaction", so a regular, a neighbor or a nearby business owner can also vouch for you.

## How Many Posts Do You Get, and What Should They Say?

Nextdoor's pages disagree here. One business post says verified businesses get two free Business Posts per month. Another says "unlimited business posts". Check the allowance in your own account before you plan a calendar.

Use posts to tell your story, spotlight an existing recommendation and ask past customers to Fave or recommend you. Showing an actual recommendation works better than a bare request because it proves the page is active. Keep one post to one idea.

## Should You Offer a Discount for a Recommendation?

Nextdoor's holiday guide lists incentives, such as a discount, a free item, a donation to a local charity per recommendation, or a prize draw. Nextdoor's pages do not explain how that fits its community guidelines, and its guidelines page returned a server error when opened for this article, so no rule is quoted here.

Before you try an incentive, read the current Nextdoor community guidelines. Nextdoor's own pages do not say whether an incentive is allowed. A safer start is to ask without a reward and thank people afterwards. Yelp is much stricter and discourages asking at all; see our [review platform comparison](/blog/review-platform-comparison-yelp-google-facebook-tripadvisor).

## How Should You Reply to Recommendations?

Reply to every recommendation, positive or negative. Nextdoor advises prioritizing responses from your page, thanking neighbors, and treating good and bad feedback as a chance to build trust.

Keep replies short and specific: name the service they mentioned, thank them, and invite them back. For a negative one, apologize for the issue, offer one concrete fix and move on. Nextdoor does not publish a required response time, so set your own, such as one working day.

## A 10-Minute Weekly Plan (Worked Example)

All numbers here are **assumptions** for a shop with about 40 customer visits a month.

| Step | Time | Assumed result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hand or text the page link to customers who say thanks | 5 min a week | 40 asked a month |
| Share of those who leave a Fave or recommendation (assumption) | none | 25%, so 10 a month |
| Reply to each new one | 3 min a week | 10 replies |
| Post a recommendation on your Business Page (one of your allowed posts) | 2 min | 1 to 2 a month |

If 10 a month is wrong for you, that is the point of tracking. Add "How did you hear about us?" to your booking form and count how many new customers name Nextdoor. For the broader budget picture, see our [local marketing budget guide](/blog/local-marketing-budget-guide).

## FAQ

### Do I need to be a customer to recommend a business on Nextdoor?

No. Nextdoor says likes and recommendations do not need to come only from people who have completed a transaction with the business.

### Is a Nextdoor Business Page free?

Nextdoor's business guide describes claiming a Business Page as free. Advertising is a separate paid product.

### Where do recommendations appear?

On your Business Page and, according to Nextdoor, in the main Nextdoor newsfeed.

### Do Faves affect ranking?

Nextdoor's business blog says your Fave count helps you rank higher in search results. It does not explain how.

### How many free posts can I make?

Nextdoor's own pages say two Business Posts per month in one and unlimited in another. Check your account.

### Can I ask for recommendations on Yelp the same way?

No. BrightLocal's summary of Yelp's guidelines says Yelp may filter reviews that seem requested. Nextdoor's pages encourage asking.
