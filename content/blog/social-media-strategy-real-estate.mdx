A real estate agent's social media strategy should keep past clients and neighbours thinking of you, and show local expertise to people who are about to move. NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, as reported by Virginia REALTORS, says 43% of buyers used an agent they found through a referral and 18% used an agent they had worked with before. Social media supports that referral loop; it rarely replaces it.

This page covers which platforms US adults use, what to post, how Meta's housing ad rules limit targeting, and how to judge results. The NAR figures come from a secondary summary because NAR's own report page did not show the numbers.

## Which Platforms Should an Agent Use?

Pew Research's 2026 survey of US adults found these shares who ever use each platform: YouTube 84%, Facebook 70%, Instagram 51%, LinkedIn 33%, TikTok 31%, and X 25%. TikTok use is 65% among adults 18 to 29, 45% for ages 30 to 49 and 31% for ages 50 to 64.

| Platform | US adults who use it (Pew 2026) | Good fit for an agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facebook | 70% | Local groups, listings, past-client updates |
| Instagram | 51% | Photos and short video of homes and neighbourhoods |
| YouTube | 84% | Longer neighbourhood and buyer-process videos |
| TikTok | 31% | First-time buyers under 30 |
| LinkedIn | 33% | Relocation, investors, referral partners |

Pick two to start. Pew measures how many people use a platform, not whether they are about to buy a home.

## What Should You Post to Get Referrals?

NAR's figures say people choose agents they know or are referred to, so the aim of your content is to be the first name someone thinks of. Four kinds of post carry that:

- Just-listed and just-sold posts, with the street or neighbourhood (and the client's permission)
- Local guides: what a street, school area or commute is like
- Answers to common buyer and seller questions, in a short video
- Client moments: a key handover, with written permission

Skip generic motivational quotes and national market commentary that any agent can copy. Post consistently instead of in bursts. Our [YouTube Shorts vs TikTok vs Reels guide](/blog/youtube-shorts-vs-tiktok-vs-reels-local-business) helps you choose a short-video home.

## What Are Meta's Rules for Housing Ads?

If you pay to promote listings on Facebook or Instagram, you must declare the housing special ad category. Jon Loomer's guide to the rules, last modified 1 March 2025 (secondary; Meta's own help page did not load), lists these limits: age is fixed at 18 to 65 plus, gender cannot be edited, location must cover at least a 15-mile radius with no ZIP code targeting, lookalike audiences are unavailable, and detailed targeting exclusions are blocked.

HUD's overview states the Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status and disability. Write ad copy and choose images with that in mind. Check Meta's current help page before launching, because these rules change. ## FAQ

### Which social media platform is best for real estate agents?

For most US agents, Facebook and Instagram, because Pew finds 70% and 51% of adults use them. Add YouTube for longer videos and TikTok if you target buyers under 30.

### Do buyers find agents on social media?

NAR's 2025 figures say referrals (43%) and past agents (18%) were the main ways buyers found their agent. The summary of NAR data used here gives no social media share, so treat social media as a support channel.

### Can I target ads by ZIP code?

Not for housing ads on Meta. According to Jon Loomer's guide, housing ads cannot target by ZIP code and must cover a radius of at least 15 miles.

### How often should an agent post?

No public source gives a correct number. Choose a rhythm you can keep for a year, such as three posts a week, and track results.

### Do I need a paid budget?

No. Start with organic posts and a monthly email. Add paid promotion once you have a listing or event worth promoting.
