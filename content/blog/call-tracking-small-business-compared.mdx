## What is call tracking and how does it work for a small business?

A tracking number is a phone number that rings through to the business's real line but is tied to one source. A salon might show one number on its Google Ads call extension, another on its website and a third on printed flyers. Each call is then logged against the number that was dialed.

Website visits work a little differently. The cookie list on [WhatConverts' pricing page](https://www.whatconverts.com/pricing/) shows how one vendor attributes activity. Its wc_client cookie lasts 6 months and records the landing page and source of a visit. The wc_visitor cookie lasts 1 year 1 month 4 days and identifies a unique visitor so lead activity can be tied to the correct visit. A session cookie, wc_client_current, keeps temporary attribution data during a visit.

When a website swaps the displayed number depending on the visitor, the technique is called Dynamic Number Insertion (DNI). Google notes that its automated verification system cannot verify a phone number by scanning source code when a site uses DNI or third-party call tracking ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883)).

## How does Google Ads report phone calls?

Google Ads can count a phone call as a conversion. According to [Google](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883), a call is tracked as a conversion when it lasts longer than a minimum length the advertiser sets. A business can therefore filter out hang-ups and wrong numbers by choosing a sensible minimum.

Google Ads also offers AI analysis of call recordings to judge lead quality, but only where recording is available, and only in the U.S. and Canada, as [Adwave's reading of Google's support page](https://adwave.com/resources/call-tracking-for-small-business) puts it (secondary). Whether recording is available for a given account is not detailed in the sources, so check the account settings or ask support.

Calls from ads are one piece of the picture. Calls from the Google Business Profile listing are covered in the [Google Business Profile optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist), and campaign-level setup is covered in the [Performance Max step-by-step guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-performance-max-campaigns-step-by-step). Web sessions and form fills are better measured in [Google Analytics 4](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business).

## What setup limits does Google document?

Google's help page lists several constraints that matter before a tracking vendor is chosen.

- Only one number can be tracked on a single page with the default JavaScript tag.
- In Google Tag Manager, the phone number entered in the website call conversion tag must match the exact digits shown on the website.
- The standard Tag Manager call conversion tag is not compatible with international number formats and can display unintended plus signs on forwarding numbers.
- Google does not recommend installing the call conversion tag through Tag Manager when the site uses DNI or third-party call tracking.
- The Google tag cannot verify a domain when it is installed through a tag management system.
- After Google Search Console is linked to Google Ads, the link takes 24 hours to take effect, and call assets must be resubmitted for review.

Manual tag installation is possible, but Google calls it more advanced and recommends it only for advertisers comfortable using JavaScript. A small business that relies on a web builder may need the developer or agency to handle it. Google's forwarding numbers are currently available only in the countries listed on its support page, so confirm that the business's country appears there.

## When does a small business not need call tracking?

Call tracking pays for itself only when calls are a meaningful share of how customers arrive and when the business will act on the result. A few situations make it unnecessary or premature.

- Bookings happen mostly through an online scheduler, so calls are a small fraction of inquiries. An [online booking system](/blog/online-booking-system-for-salons) already records where those bookings start.
- Only one paid channel runs. If every call follows a single ad, the source is already known.
- Call volume is very low. Per-minute and per-number charges are small, but a monthly plan still has to be justified by decisions it improves.
- Nobody reviews the reports. Without a monthly review of calls by source, the data does not change spending.

In these cases, Google's own call reporting for ads, plus a habit of asking new callers how they found the business, may cover the need. The sources do not give a call-volume threshold, so the decision rests on the business's own numbers.

## What does a two-week setup look like?

The checklist below assumes one location, Google Ads running or about to run, and a website the owner can edit. Where a step depends on a vendor or Google account, the documented limits above apply.

### Week 1: decide and choose

- Days 1 and 2: pull the last 30 days of calls from the phone carrier or the front-desk log, and note how many were new customers.
- Day 3: list every place the phone number appears (website, Google Business Profile, ads, flyers, directories).
- Day 4: request written quotes from two or three vendors covering monthly fee, included minutes, per-minute rate, cost per extra number and contract length.
- Day 5: ask each vendor whether it supports DNI and how it works with Google Ads call conversions.
- Days 6 and 7: pick one vendor and choose the number of tracking numbers needed, one per source.

### Week 2: install and test

- Day 8: link Google Search Console to Google Ads if needed, and note that the link takes 24 hours to take effect and call assets need resubmitting for review.
- Day 9: install the vendor's website snippet, or the Google call conversion tag, avoiding Tag Manager where Google advises against it.
- Day 10: set the minimum call length for conversions in Google Ads.
- Day 11: place the new numbers on the Google Ads call asset and other listings, keeping the exact digits consistent.
- Day 12: place test calls from a mobile phone to every number and confirm each call lands in the right source.
- Day 13: add a cookie and call-recording notice to the website and phone greeting, after checking local requirements with a lawyer.
- Day 14: schedule a monthly review of calls by source, and write down what result would change the ad budget.

## What does call tracking cost in a salon example?

This worked example uses assumptions for a single-chair-to-small salon. Every input is an assumption unless a source is noted.

Assumptions:

- Three tracking numbers: Google Ads, website, Google Business Profile.
- Plan fee: $30 a month (assumption, set equal to the lowest full-featured price listed in the [Adwave roundup report](https://adwave.com/resources/call-tracking-for-small-business), secondary).
- Number fee: $0.50 a month each, from one provider's published rate (secondary), treated as separate from the plan.
- Usage: $30 to $75 a month, that provider's stated range for 3 to 5 numbers (secondary).
- Google Ads spend: $600 a month (assumption).
- Calls from ads over the minimum length: 60 a month (assumption).
- Share of those calls that become bookings: 40% (assumption).
- Average ticket: $90 (assumption).

Calculation:

- Number fees: 3 × $0.50 = $1.50.
- Monthly tracking cost: $30 + $1.50 + $30 = $61.50 at the low end, and $30 + $1.50 + $75 = $106.50 at the high end.
- Bookings from ads: 60 × 40% = 24.
- Revenue from those bookings: 24 × $90 = $2,160.
- Ad cost per booking: $600 ÷ 24 = $25.
- Tracking cost per booking: $61.50 ÷ 24 ≈ $2.56 to $106.50 ÷ 24 ≈ $4.44.

Under these assumptions, tracking adds between 10.25% and 17.75% to the ad budget. That is a meaningful share of a small spend. If the reports show that calls from the website and the listing book at a similar rate, the tracking spend might be better placed in follow-up work such as [review requests](/blog/review-management-software-small-business-compared) or [reactivation campaigns](/blog/customer-reactivation-campaigns). Real vendor plans may bundle minutes differently, so these results are a template, not a forecast.

## FAQ

### Does call tracking work with Google Ads call conversions?

Yes, with caveats. Google counts a call as a conversion when it exceeds a minimum length the advertiser sets. Google also says its automated verification cannot verify a number by scanning source code when a site uses DNI or third-party call tracking, and it does not recommend Tag Manager installation in that case.

### How much does call tracking cost per month?

Published figures in the sources include tracking numbers from $0.50 a month each plus per-minute charges, a lower-tier plan at $22 a month and a full-featured tool from $30 a month. These come from separate vendors and are secondary reports. WhatConverts' pricing details are not stated in the page text reviewed here, so ask for a current quote.

### Can a business track more than one phone number on a page?

Not with Google's default JavaScript tag. Google states that only one number can be tracked on a single page with it. A business with several numbers on one page should ask its call tracking vendor how it handles the situation.

### Is Google's AI call analysis available to every small business?

No. According to Adwave's reading of Google's support page, AI analysis of call recordings is offered only where recording is available, and only in the U.S. and Canada (secondary). Check the Google Ads account for availability.
