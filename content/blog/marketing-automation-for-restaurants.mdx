## The 4 automations worth setting up

Square states that recurring campaigns such as welcome emails, birthday offers and win-back messages to lapsed customers are included in its marketing subscription, along with Google Review campaigns at no extra cost. That maps neatly to four jobs:

1. **Welcome.** Sent once, when a customer first joins the list at checkout or on your site.
2. **Win-back.** Sent to customers who have not returned in a period you choose, for example 45 days for a cafe or 90 days for a table-service restaurant.
3. **Birthday or anniversary offer.** One message a year per guest.
4. **Review request.** Send the Google review link after a visit. Google's policy prohibits offering free or discounted goods or services for reviews ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)), so ask, do not bribe.

What not to automate: replies to reviews and comments. They are read by the public, so write them by hand.

## Worked example (assumption): a 60-seat restaurant

Invented numbers for illustration, to show the maths. Replace them with yours.

- Customer list: 1,200 guests with a valid contact.
- A win-back message goes to guests lapsed 90 days, assume 300 people.
- Assume 5% come back within 2 weeks: 15 visits.
- Assume an average check of $38 per visit: $570.
- Message cost: 300 texts. On Square Plus these fall inside the 500 included texts, so the marginal cost is 0 beyond the subscription. On a pay-per-text plan at 3 cents, 300 texts cost $9.

At those assumptions, one win-back send can pay for itself many times over. If the real return rate is 1% instead of 5%, you still recover 3 visits, about $114. The test is cheap: send to half of the lapsed list and hold back the other half, then compare returns after 2 weeks.

## How often to message

Email benchmarks from Mailchimp put the all-industry average open rate at 35.63%, click rate at 2.62% and unsubscribe rate at 0.22% (last updated December 2023; Mailchimp's table did not include a restaurant row). Use these as a rough yardstick for your own list, not a target. A practical rule: one email or text a week at most for promotions, and automated welcome, birthday and review messages on top.

<Callout type="warning">Check local consent rules before texting. In many places you need clear opt-in for marketing texts, so collect consent at sign-up and keep the record.</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How much does restaurant marketing automation cost?**

Published entry points: Mailchimp Standard from $20 per month, and Square marketing bundled into Square Plus or Premium with 500 or 2,500 texts included. Confirm current monthly prices on the vendor pages.

**Q: Can I do this on a free plan?**

Mailchimp's Free plan has no automations and a limit of 250 contacts, so it covers newsletters only. Automations need a paid plan.

**Q: Should I automate review requests?**

Yes, the request, but not incentives. Google prohibits discounts or freebies in exchange for reviews.

**Q: How do I know it is working?**

Track returning guests from each campaign using a unique code or a held-back control group, and compare revenue to the monthly software cost.
