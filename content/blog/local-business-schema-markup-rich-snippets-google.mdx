Google's LocalBusiness structured data documentation lists exactly two required properties: `name` and `address`. It then lists about ten recommended ones, from opening hours to a phone number. That is the whole job for most small businesses, and it takes an hour, not a month. What it does not do is promise anything.

Google's own page says it does not guarantee that features consuming structured data will show up in search results, even when the markup is valid.

This guide is for owners of local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada who want to add LocalBusiness schema correctly, without chasing rich snippets that Google may never show. It is based on Google Search Central's documentation and corrects a claim still found on older posts: FAQ rich results no longer exist.

## What Is Schema Markup and What Should You Expect?

Structured data is code on your page, usually JSON-LD, that labels facts such as your name, address and hours so a machine does not have to guess.

Google's [introduction to structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data) calls it a standardized format for providing information about a page and classifying the page content, and says it can enable search results that are more engaging to users. The page does not describe structured data as a ranking factor, so do not sell it to yourself as one.

Realistic expectations:

- **It labels your facts clearly.** The [LocalBusiness documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) defines which business details Google can read from a page.
- **It does not guarantee any visual result.** Even valid markup that passes the Rich Results Test may not produce an enhanced result.
- **Your Google Business Profile still carries the local pack.** Treat schema as the tidy-up that makes your website agree with your listing, after the profile, reviews and page content are in good shape.

## What Does Google Require for LocalBusiness Schema?

From the LocalBusiness documentation:

| Status | Properties |
| --- | --- |
| Required | `name`, `address` (a PostalAddress; Google says to include as many properties as possible) |
| Recommended | `geo` (latitude and longitude), `openingHoursSpecification`, `priceRange`, `telephone`, `url`, `menu` and `servesCuisine` for food establishments, `department` for departments |
| Only for sites that capture reviews about other local businesses | `aggregateRating`, `review` |

Notable rules from the same page:

- **Use the most specific LocalBusiness subtype possible,** such as Restaurant or DaySpa, specified as an array if more than one applies. `additionalType` is not supported.
- **Multiple locations:** define each location as its own LocalBusiness.
- **Hours past midnight** use a single specification, and seasonal closures use `validFrom` and `validThrough`.
- **Coordinates** need at least 5 decimal places.
- **Price range** must be under 100 characters or Google will not show it.
- **URL** must be a working link to the specific business location.

Subtypes exist for many local trades. At [schema.org](https://schema.org/CafeOrCoffeeShop), CafeOrCoffeeShop sits under FoodEstablishment, [HairSalon](https://schema.org/HairSalon) under HealthAndBeautyBusiness and [PetStore](https://schema.org/PetStore) under Store. [HealthClub](https://schema.org/HealthClub) also exists for gyms. Not every trade has its own type, so photographers and similar businesses should browse the [LocalBusiness subtype list](https://schema.org/LocalBusiness) and pick the closest, or fall back to plain LocalBusiness.

## What Does a Copy-Paste JSON-LD Look Like?

This is a template for a hypothetical salon. Replace every value with your real details, delete lines you cannot fill truthfully, and keep it matching what visitors can read on the page.

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "HairSalon",
  "name": "Example Hair Studio",
  "url": "https://www.example.com",
  "telephone": "+1-555-000-0000",
  "priceRange": "$$",
  "image": "https://www.example.com/images/salon.jpg",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "123 Main Street",
    "addressLocality": "Springfield",
    "addressRegion": "IL",
    "postalCode": "62701",
    "addressCountry": "US"
  },
  "geo": {
    "@type": "GeoCoordinates",
    "latitude": 39.79999,
    "longitude": -89.64999
  },
  "openingHoursSpecification": [
    {
      "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
      "dayOfWeek": ["Tuesday", "Wednesday", "Thursday", "Friday"],
      "opens": "09:00",
      "closes": "18:00"
    },
    {
      "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
      "dayOfWeek": "Saturday",
      "opens": "09:00",
      "closes": "15:00"
    }
  ],
  "sameAs": [
    "https://www.instagram.com/example",
    "https://www.facebook.com/example"
  ]
}
```

Put it in a `<script type="application/ld+json">` tag on the page that describes the business, usually the home or contact page. Google recommends JSON-LD "if your site's setup allows it".

For UK, Australian or Canadian businesses, change `addressCountry` ("GB", "AU", "CA"), use the local region and postal code style, and write the phone number in international format.

## Where Do You Put It on Your Platform?

- **WordPress:** SEO plugins such as Yoast, Rank Math or a dedicated schema plugin can generate LocalBusiness data from settings. Open the output and compare it to the template above rather than trusting defaults.
- **Squarespace, Wix, Shopify:** these may add some structured data on their own. For custom JSON-LD, use the code injection or custom code area your plan allows.
- **Custom or Next.js sites:** render the script from one source of truth for your name, address and hours so they cannot drift.
- **Google Tag Manager:** possible, but placing JSON-LD directly in the page markup is simpler to audit.

The fuller guides schema markup for local business and schema markup for local business websites cover platform-by-platform setup.

## How Do You Test It?

Google's introduction page recommends the Rich Results Test while building and the Search Console rich result status reports after launch, to catch templating or serving problems. A working routine:

1. **Rich Results Test** (search.google.com/test/rich-results): paste your URL or code and check for errors.
2. **Schema Markup Validator** (validator.schema.org): checks general Schema.org syntax.
3. **Search Console:** watch the enhancement or rich result reports for new errors after any site change.
4. **Compare with your listing.** Open your Google Business Profile and confirm the name, phone and hours match the schema character for character. The [Google Business Profile optimisation checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) lists what to keep in sync.

Fix errors first. A missing optional field is not urgent.

## Which Two Schema Traps Should You Avoid?

**Review stars.** Google's [review snippet documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet) says that when a reviewed entity controls the reviews about itself, its pages that use LocalBusiness or other Organization structured data are ineligible for the star review feature. That includes reviews placed on your own site through an embedded third-party widget. It also says ratings must be sourced directly from users, and that editors should not create or compile ratings. The LocalBusiness page itself says `aggregateRating` and `review` are for sites that capture reviews about other local businesses. So adding your own five-star average to your own home page schema is not going to earn stars. Google says violations may lead to a manual action, and structured data on a page under manual action is ignored, though the page can still appear in results.

**FAQ markup.** Many older articles say FAQ rich results are limited to a few sites. The current position is stricter. Google's [Search Central changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) says the FAQ rich result feature would no longer appear in Google Search starting 7 May 2026, and a later entry says it is no longer shown in results. So do not add FAQPage schema hoping for expandable answers in results. You can still publish a visible FAQ section for customers, which is useful on its own.

The same changelog shows a pattern: Google has retired several other structured data features since 2025, including practice problems and course info. Write schema to describe your business accurately, not to unlock a particular visual, because that visual can disappear.

## What Are the Most Common Schema Mistakes?

- **Mismatch:** phone, address or hours in schema differ from the page or your listing.
- **Markup for content visitors cannot see,** which makes the data disagree with the page.
- **Two sources of schema:** both a theme and a plugin print LocalBusiness, creating duplicates.
- **A generic type** where a specific one exists.
- **Stale hours** after a holiday or seasonal change.
- **Treating schema as a substitute** for reviews, a complete profile and good pages.

## What Should You Do First?

1. Open your Google Business Profile and copy the exact name, address, phone and hours into a note. These become your single source of truth.
2. Pick the most specific schema.org subtype for your business from the [LocalBusiness list](https://schema.org/LocalBusiness).
3. Fill in the template above with the required `name` and `address`, plus the recommended `telephone`, `url`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `geo` and `priceRange` (if it fits).
4. Add it to the home or contact page, then run the Rich Results Test and the Schema Markup Validator and fix errors.
5. Remove any FAQ or self-review schema you added for rich results, and set a quarterly reminder to compare schema to your listing.

## FAQ

### Does schema markup improve my Google rankings?

Google's introduction to structured data describes benefits in terms of search appearance and does not call it a ranking factor. Treat it as a way to label your facts clearly, not a ranking lever.

### Will I get rich snippets if my markup is valid?

Not necessarily. Google says it does not guarantee that features consuming structured data will show up in search results, even when a test passes.

### Can I add star ratings to my own business schema?

Google says pages about an entity that controls its own reviews, using LocalBusiness or Organization markup, are ineligible for the star review feature. Skip it.

### Do I still need FAQ schema?

Not for search results. Google announced that the FAQ rich result stopped appearing from 7 May 2026. A visible FAQ section for customers is still worthwhile.

### Which format should I use?

Google recommends JSON-LD if your site's setup allows it.
