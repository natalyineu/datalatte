Schema markup is a block of structured data (usually JSON-LD) that you add to your website so Google can clearly read details like your business name, address, hours, services and reviews. It does not guarantee rich snippets, and it is not a direct ranking boost, but it helps search engines understand your pages and makes you eligible for some enhanced results.

For most local businesses, the practical job is simple: add LocalBusiness schema to your homepage or contact page, add FAQ or other relevant markup where it genuinely applies, and test it before publishing.

## What Schema Markup Actually Does

Search engines read your page text and try to guess what it means. Schema gives them an explicit label: "this is the business name", "this is the phone number", "these are the opening hours". The vocabulary comes from Schema.org, and Google recommends JSON-LD as the format.

What to expect, honestly:

- **It helps Google understand your business,** which can support local search and knowledge panels.
- **It can make pages eligible for enhanced displays,** such as star ratings or FAQ sections, but Google decides what to show, and the rules change over time.
- **It is not a magic ranking factor.** Your Google Business Profile, reviews, content and links still matter most for local results.

Google has also reduced which rich results it shows for some markup types (FAQ results, for example, are now limited for most sites), so use schema to describe your site accurately rather than chasing a specific visual result.

## Which Schema Types Matter for Local Businesses

| Schema type | Use it for | Where to place it |
|---|---|---|
| LocalBusiness (or a more specific subtype) | Name, address, phone, hours, geo location, social profiles | Homepage and/or contact page |
| Subtypes such as CafeOrCoffeeShop, HairSalon, BeautySalon, HealthClub, PetStore, AnimalShelter, Dentist | A more precise description of the business | Same as above |
| Service | A specific service you offer, with area served | Service pages |
| FAQPage | Genuine on-page FAQs | The page that shows those FAQs |
| Review / AggregateRating | Ratings, only when allowed and truthful | Be careful, see below |
| Organization / Person | Brand or founder details | About page |
| Article | Blog posts | Blog templates |
| BreadcrumbList | Navigation trail | Site-wide templates |
| Menu (via hasMenu) | Cafes and restaurants | Menu page |

Use the most specific subtype that fits. For example, a coffee shop can use CafeOrCoffeeShop, a salon HairSalon, and a gym HealthClub or SportsActivityLocation.

## Step 1: Gather Your Business Details

Collect the facts exactly as they appear on your Google Business Profile:

- Business name
- Address (street, city, region, postal code, country)
- Phone number in international format
- Opening hours, including special days
- Website URL, logo and a main photo
- Price range (for example "$$")
- Social profile URLs
- Latitude and longitude, if available
- Service area, for mobile or home-based businesses

Consistency matters. The details in schema should match your visible page content and your Google listing. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) helps you verify the source details.

## Step 2: Write the JSON-LD

Here is a simplified example for a hypothetical salon. Replace the placeholders with your own real details.

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "HairSalon",
  "name": "Example Hair Studio",
  "url": "https://www.example.com",
  "telephone": "+1-555-000-0000",
  "priceRange": "$$",
  "image": "https://www.example.com/images/salon.jpg",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "123 Main Street",
    "addressLocality": "Springfield",
    "addressRegion": "IL",
    "postalCode": "62701",
    "addressCountry": "US"
  },
  "openingHoursSpecification": [
    {
      "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
      "dayOfWeek": ["Tuesday", "Wednesday", "Thursday", "Friday"],
      "opens": "09:00",
      "closes": "18:00"
    }
  ],
  "sameAs": [
    "https://www.instagram.com/example",
    "https://www.facebook.com/example"
  ]
}
```

Place it inside a script tag of type `application/ld+json` in the page head or body. You do not need all fields, but the ones you include must be accurate.

## Step 3: Add It to Your Site

How you add it depends on your platform.

- **WordPress:** plugins such as Yoast SEO, Rank Math or Schema Pro can generate LocalBusiness markup from settings. Check the output rather than trusting it blindly.
- **Squarespace, Wix, Shopify:** these add some schema automatically. For custom JSON-LD, use the code injection or custom code area, depending on the platform and plan.
- **Custom or Next.js sites:** add the script in the page template, ideally generated from one source of truth for your business details.
- **Google Tag Manager:** possible, but placing JSON-LD directly in the page is generally more reliable.

## Step 4: Test and Validate

Always test before and after publishing.

1. **Rich Results Test** (search.google.com/test/rich-results): shows which rich result types Google can detect.
2. **Schema Markup Validator** (validator.schema.org): checks general Schema.org syntax.
3. **Google Search Console:** the Enhancements reports flag errors and warnings for detected structured data over time.

Fix errors first. Warnings for optional fields can usually wait.

## Step 5: Use Service and FAQ Markup Carefully

**Service markup** can help describe individual offerings. Include the service name, a description, the provider (your business) and the area served. Only mark up services that are actually visible on the page.

**FAQ markup** is appropriate only when the questions and answers are visible on the page and written for users. Google now shows FAQ rich results for only limited types of sites, so do not expect extra search real estate, but the markup is still a valid way to describe the content.

## Reviews and Ratings: Be Careful

This is where many local businesses get into trouble.

- Google's guidelines say review markup must reflect genuine reviews that visitors can see on the page, and self-serving reviews for your own LocalBusiness or Organization often are not eligible for star display.
- Do not mark up ratings you copied from other sites without clear permission and context.
- Never invent ratings or review counts. It violates guidelines and can lead to a manual action.

If in doubt, leave review markup out and show your Google reviews through a normal widget or link instead. Our guide to [trust signals for your website](/blog/trust-signals-website) covers how to present reviews well.

## Common Mistakes

- **Mismatched details:** the phone number or hours in schema differ from the page or Google listing.
- **Marking up content that is not on the page,** such as hidden FAQs.
- **Duplicate or conflicting schema** from both a plugin and a theme.
- **Using a generic type** when a specific subtype exists.
- **Forgetting to update hours** after seasonal changes.
- **Treating schema as a replacement** for solid content, reviews and a complete Google Business Profile.

## FAQ

**Q: Does schema markup improve my Google rankings?**
A: Not directly. It helps Google understand your content and can make pages eligible for enhanced displays, but your profile, reviews, content and links are still the main drivers of local rankings.

**Q: Do I need a developer to add LocalBusiness schema?**
A: Not always. Many website platforms and SEO plugins can generate it, and you can paste JSON-LD into a custom code area. A developer helps for custom sites or complex setups.

**Q: How do I know whether my schema works?**
A: Use Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema Markup Validator, then monitor the enhancement reports in Google Search Console.

**Q: Will schema guarantee star ratings in search results?**
A: No. Google decides which rich results to show, and review stars for a business's own site are often not eligible. Focus on accurate markup rather than guaranteed visuals.
