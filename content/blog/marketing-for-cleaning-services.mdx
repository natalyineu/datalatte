The best marketing for a cleaning company wins recurring clients at a cost you can recover within a few visits. Start with a complete Google Business Profile and reviews, add paid search for customers ready to book, then use rebooking and reminders to turn one-off cleans into repeat work. For a solo or new cleaning business under 25 clients, [Adwave's cleaning ad guide](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-cleaning-janitorial-services) recommends a total of $1,000-$2,300 a month, aiming for 15-35 quote requests.

## Which marketing channel should a cleaning company start with?

Rank channels by how close the customer is to booking. Someone searching for a cleaner nearby is close to a decision. Someone scrolling social media is not, so social ads work better for offers and follow-up than for first contact.

| Channel | Customer intent | Cost signal from sources | Start here if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile and reviews | High | Your time to respond and request reviews | Your profile is incomplete or has few reviews |
| Google search ads | High | $400-$1,000 a month for a solo cleaner (Adwave) | Your profile is complete and you can answer calls fast |
| Local Services Ads | High | $8-$25 per lead for cleaning services (Ryze, secondary) | You qualify and want pay-per-lead placement |
| Referrals and local partners | High | Your referral reward | You already have happy recurring clients |

The sources do not give a cleaning-specific Meta figure, so set a fixed test budget before scaling social ads. Referrals can come from local events and partner tie-ins, which is covered in [event marketing for local business](/blog/event-marketing-for-local-business). Treat every cost figure as a starting point, since results vary by market and service mix.

## How much should a cleaning business spend on ads each month?

Adwave sets monthly budgets by client count. For a solo or new cleaning business under 25 active clients, the total is $1,000-$2,300 a month, with Google Ads at $400-$1,000. The goal in that tier is 15-35 quote requests per month.

A growing residential business with 25-100 active clients gets a total of $3,300-$7,100 a month, aiming for 40-90 quote requests. Adwave suggests CTV spend of $1,500-$2,500 a month as a starting point in that tier. An established business with 100+ active clients gets a total of $7,500-$17,000 a month.

Adwave's [TV ad spending guide](https://adwave.com/resources/small-business-tv-ad-spending) puts home services (plumbing, HVAC, cleaning) at $30K-$60K average annual ad spend as of 2025. Dividing by 12 gives about $2,500-$5,000 a month (derived).

## Why do recurring clients matter more than cheap leads?

Adwave uses a hypothetical to make the point. A channel that produces 30 quotes and converts them to 5 one-time jobs is worse than a channel that produces 12 quotes and converts them to 6 recurring weekly clients. Fewer quotes can still win on revenue.

Measure your channels by the revenue each client brings over a year, not by how many leads they generate. A cheap lead that books once is often worth less than a pricier lead that stays on a schedule.

Recurring work is also the norm in adjacent services. A 2025 NPMA and PCO Bookkeepers cost study, as summarized by [Built Right Digital](https://builtrightdigital.com/pest-control-local-services-ads-problems/) (secondary), reports recurring revenue is 74% of pest control income.

## How do I win visibility and reviews on Google Maps?

Most customers search "cleaning service near me" and pick from the map results, so your profile does much of the selling. Keep the business name, hours, service area and photos current, and choose services that match what you actually deliver.

Ask for a review right after a clean, while the result is fresh. Reply to every review, positive or negative, so prospects can see how you handle problems. Add before-and-after photos where clients agree to them.

## Is SEO worth it for a cleaning company?

SEO takes longer than ads, but it can produce steady inbound bookings. [Thrive Agency](https://thriveagency.com/nyc-digital-marketing-agency/) reports that its NYC Cleaning Service case study client earned $100K in online revenue and 246 organic conversions after technical SEO, content and local optimization work. This is an agency's own case study, so treat it as a direction, not a forecast for your business.

The gap is also an opportunity. A vendor estimate from [B2B Lead Finder](https://b2bleadfinder.io/blog/list-of-businesses-without-websites) puts 40-50% of cleaning services businesses as having no website. A simple site with services, service area, pricing guidance and a booking form stands out in that field. ## Worked example: what does a recurring client cost to win?

Every input below is an assumption. Replace them with your own prices, conversion rates and margins.

1. Monthly ad budget: $1,000 (assumption; low end of Adwave's solo tier).
2. Quote requests per month: 20 (assumption; inside Adwave's 15-35 goal).
3. Quote-to-booking rate: 1 in 4, so 5 bookings (assumption).
4. Bookings that choose a recurring plan: 2 of 5 (assumption).
5. Recurring plan: bi-weekly clean at $140, two visits a month, so $280 revenue (assumption).
6. Gross margin: 40%, so $112 gross profit per client per month (assumption).

Cost per recurring client: $1,000 divided by 2 is $500 (derived from assumptions). Payback: $500 divided by $112 is about 4.5 months of that client's gross profit (derived). After payback, each month that client stays adds profit. If your conversion or margin is lower, the payback period grows, so track the real numbers each month.

## Should a cleaning company use Local Services Ads?

Local Services Ads charge per lead rather than per click, which makes costs easier to compare. Ryze's 2026 guide puts the typical cost per lead for cleaning services at $8-$25 (secondary). Another industry list (secondary) names carpet cleaning among currently eligible industries.

A third source (secondary, [Wizerunek w Sieci](https://wizerunekwsieci.com/local-advertising-on-google-for-small-businesses-ready-to-grow/)) states that Phase 1 of the LSA migration to Google Ads began in August 2026 for select US home service categories, including house cleaning. Confirm current eligibility and migration timing on Google's live help pages before you plan around it.

## How do I turn one-off cleans into recurring clients?

- Offer weekly, bi-weekly or monthly plans at checkout, with a discount for committing to a schedule.
- Ask for the next visit date before the crew leaves, while the client is still happy with the result.
- Send a reminder before each visit to reduce surprise cancellations.
- Message clients who have not rebooked with a simple reason to schedule again.

For the wording of reminders and win-back messages, [ChatGPT prompts for local business marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-local-business-marketing) gives drafts you can adapt. Keep a human check on every message so it still sounds like your team.

## What should I measure each month?

Track four numbers: total spend, leads, first-time bookings and clients still active after 90 days. Divide spend by first-time bookings to get cost per new client, then compare that figure with the gross profit in the worked example.

Decide which parts of this reporting to automate and which need your judgment. [AI vs human marketing for local business](/blog/ai-vs-human-marketing-local-business-honest-comparison) compares the trade-offs for small teams.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should a cleaning company spend on marketing?

Adwave recommends $1,000-$2,300 a month in total for a solo or new cleaning business under 25 active clients. Larger businesses have higher ranges. Start in the tier that matches your current client count, then scale once your cost per new client is known.

### Is Google Ads or Facebook better for cleaning services?

Google search reaches people already looking for a cleaner, so it usually suits first-time bookings. Facebook and Instagram work better for offers and retargeting. The sources here give no cleaning-specific Meta benchmark, so test one channel at a time with a fixed budget.

### Do I need a website?

Yes, in most cases. A vendor estimate puts 40-50% of cleaning businesses as having no website. A simple page with services, service area, pricing guidance and a booking form gives your ads and profile somewhere to send people.

### Is SEO worth it for a cleaning company?

It can be, but it takes longer than paid search. An agency case study reports $100K in online revenue for one NYC cleaning client, though that result is not a guarantee. Use paid search for quick leads and SEO to build steady inbound work over time.
