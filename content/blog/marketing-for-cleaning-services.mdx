The best marketing for a cleaning service is the one that wins recurring clients at a cost you can recover within a few visits. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks for the closest category, Home and Home Improvement, show $90.92 per Google search lead and $42.95 per Facebook lead. Start with your Google Business Profile and reviews, add paid search for ready-to-book customers, then use follow-up to turn one-off jobs into repeat bookings.

## Which marketing channel should a cleaning company start with?

Rank channels by how close the customer is to booking.

| Channel | Customer intent | Benchmark (Home & Home Improvement) | Start here if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile and reviews | High | Free | You have fewer than a handful of reviews |
| Google search ads | High | $8.33 per click, $90.92 per lead | Profile is complete and you can answer calls fast |
| Meta (Facebook and Instagram) ads | Medium | $42.95 per lead | You want to retarget visitors or promote an offer |
| Referrals and local partners | High | Cost of your referral reward | You already have happy recurring clients |

These benchmarks come from [LocaliQ's search advertising report](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/) (updated June 2026) and [Facebook advertising report](https://localiq.com/blog/facebook-advertising-benchmarks/) (updated September 2026). LocaliQ does not publish a cleaning-only category, so Home and Home Improvement is a proxy. Expect your own numbers to differ.

## How do I get found on Google Maps as a cleaning company?

Customers search "house cleaning near me" and choose from the map results, so the profile does much of the selling. Google's guidelines for [service-area businesses](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say that if you travel to customers you should hide your home address and list your service area, and the area should not extend much beyond about two hours of driving time.

Then work on proof. [BrightLocal's 2026 survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) found 68% of consumers require at least four stars, and 80% are likely to use a business that responds to all its reviews. Ask for a review right after a clean, reply to every review, and add before-and-after photos.

## Worked example: how long does a recurring client take to pay back?

This is an assumption-based example, not a result. Use your own prices and costs.

1. Cost per lead: $90.92 (Google search benchmark).
2. Assume 1 in 3 leads books a first clean: cost per new client is about $273.
3. Assume a bi-weekly clean at $140 with 40% gross margin: about $56 gross profit per visit.
4. Payback: $273 / $56 is about 5 visits, or roughly 10 weeks.

If the client cancels after one visit, you lose about $217. This is why recurring plans and rebooking at the end of the first visit matter more than a cheaper click.

## How do I turn one-off cleans into recurring clients?

- **Offer plans at checkout:** weekly, bi-weekly or monthly, with a small discount on the plan price.
- **Rebook before you leave:** ask for the next date at the end of the first visit.
- **Send reminders:** a text a day before each visit cuts surprise cancellations. See our guide to AI appointment reminders.
- **Win back lapsed clients:** message customers who have not booked in 60 days with a simple offer.

Track retention by cohort (clients booked in month X still active after 3 months). It tells you more than total bookings.

## Which keywords and pages convert best?

Bid on service plus place ("move-out cleaning [city]", "deep cleaning [neighborhood]"), and send each to a page that matches. Our guide to [Google Ad keywords for cleaning services](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-cleaning-services-in-2026) lists keyword groups and negatives, and [website design for cleaning services](/blog/website-design-for-cleaning-services) covers the booking page. Add negative keywords such as "jobs", "hiring", "salary" and "training" so you do not pay for job seekers.

## What should I measure each month?

Keep four numbers: spend, leads, first-time bookings and clients still active after 90 days. Divide spend by bookings to get cost per new client, and compare it with gross profit per client from the worked example. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should a cleaning company spend on marketing?

Work backward from the number of new clients you can serve. Multiply that by your cost per new client from the worked example, and add a test budget before scaling.

### Is Google Ads or Facebook better for cleaning services?

Google search reaches people already looking for a cleaner. Facebook and Instagram work better for offers and retargeting. Test one at a time.

### How many reviews do I need?

There is no fixed number. BrightLocal's survey shows most consumers want at least a four-star rating, so keep collecting reviews steadily and reply to each.

### Do I need a website?

Yes. Even a simple page with services, service area, pricing guidance and a booking form gives ads and your profile somewhere to send people.

### How do I get commercial cleaning contracts?

Commercial buyers search differently and decide slower. Create a separate page and ad group for offices and property managers, and track those leads separately.
