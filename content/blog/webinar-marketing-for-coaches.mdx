## Why Should the Registration Page Collect Less?

A landing page is a page built for lead capture. It is simpler than a website and asks the visitor to take one action, such as entering an email address or registering for a webinar, as [Victoria Gibson's Facebook ads guide for coaches](https://victoriagibson.com/3-2/) describes it (secondary, a coach's own account). Keep the webinar page focused on that one step.

The same guide advises against sending ads straight to a coaching payment page when the people clicking have not heard of you. Registration comes first, and the sale happens after the session.

Adwave's guidance on contact forms makes a related point. Collect less information upfront, qualify people through follow-up, and tighten the form only when low-quality sign-ups become a real operational problem (secondary). A long form cuts registrations before you know whether the audience is right. For more on building this part of the funnel, see [email marketing for coaches](/blog/email-marketing-for-coaches).

## What Does the Reminder Schedule Look Like?

A webinar plan from Adwave's customer-appreciation guide sets a three-step timeline (secondary). The timings are a useful template for a coaching session.

- Seven days before: pick the topic and write the three takeaways you will teach.
- Five days before: send one email invite and post one social reminder.
- One day before: send a reminder with the time and the join link.

Keep the same order for every session so you can compare results. Changing the timing and the content at once makes it impossible to see which change helped. For social reminders, the [Instagram guide for coaches](/blog/instagram-marketing-for-coaches) covers formats that suit a short announcement.

## What Does Paid Traffic Cost per Registrant?

Adwave cites WordStream and LocaliQ benchmarks showing an average cost per click of around $1.92 for Facebook leads campaigns (secondary). LocaliQ defines conversion rate as leads divided by clicks (primary), so track the same ratio on your own registration page. The worked example below applies that to a hypothetical budget. Every input except the $1.92 is an assumption.

| Input or step | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad budget | $300 | Assumption |
| Cost per click | $1.92 | Benchmark, Facebook leads campaigns |
| Clicks | 156 | $300 divided by $1.92 |
| Registration rate | 20% | Assumption |
| Registrants | 31 | 156 times 20% |
| Cost per registrant | $9.68 | $300 divided by 31 |
| Live attendance | 30% | Assumption |
| Attendees | 9 | 31 times 30% |
| Close rate from attendees | 10% | Assumption |
| Expected clients | 0.9 | 9 times 10% |
| Program price | $1,500 | Assumption |
| Expected revenue | $1,350 | 0.9 times $1,500 |

At these inputs, one sale covers the $300 spend five times over, and the expected value is close to break-even. The close rate and attendance rate are the largest unknowns, so measure both in your first session. A cost per click from your own campaigns will replace the benchmark once you have data.

## How Should Follow-Up Reach Non-Attendees?

Most registrants will miss the live session, so the replay and follow-up carry much of the result. Win-back benchmarks summarized by SendPulse place recovered inactive subscribers at about 5 to 15%, with stronger programs reaching 10 to 20% or more (secondary, via Adwave). These figures describe inactive subscribers, not webinar registrants, so treat them as a rough guide.

Send the replay to everyone who registered, not just people who were absent. Adwave's guidance on sales enablement reports that teams with a structured 90-day plan outperform ad-hoc trained teams by 45% in win rate (secondary, citing Vouris). That is a sales-team result, but the principle applies: a planned sequence beats improvised follow-up. ## Which Leads Are Worth the Most?

A HubSpot survey cited by May-Fly reports that 60% of marketers say inbound sources, such as SEO and blog content, are their highest-quality source of leads (secondary). Attendees who found you through content may be a better fit than cold ad clicks, so tag where each registrant came from.

Referrals also convert well. The Amplifinity State of Business Customer Referral Programs report, cited by Adwave, states a 13% overall conversion of referral leads into customers (secondary). Ask attendees who would benefit to share the replay link. ## FAQ

### What attendance rate should a coaching webinar plan for?

None of the sources used here gives a coaching webinar attendance rate. Track your own by dividing live attendees by registrants after each session, then plan with the figure you measure.

### How much does a click cost?

Adwave cites WordStream and LocaliQ benchmarks of around $1.92 per click for Facebook leads campaigns (secondary). Costs vary by niche and location, so check your own campaign results.

### When should reminders go out?

A published webinar plan uses three steps: pick topics and takeaways 7 days before, send an email and social reminder 5 days before, and send a time and link reminder 1 day before (secondary).

### Should ads link to the sales page?

Not if the people clicking have not heard of you. Send ad traffic to a registration page first, then offer the program after the session (secondary, Victoria Gibson).

### Can one free group produce clients?

One coach reports that a client, Hayley, won 15 six-month coaching clients from her free Facebook group using Facebook ads (secondary). This is a single case, not a benchmark, so use it as an example of what a test might show.
