The cheapest full local SEO audit tool on BrightLocal's price page is $41 a month for one location on monthly billing, or $31 a month if you pay annually. Two new customers a year can cover that. But a tool only finds problems; it does not fix them, and two of the best audit tools for a small business cost nothing. This guide shows what each tool checks, what it costs today, and how to run a useful audit in an afternoon before you pay for anything.

It is written for owners of single-location local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Prices below are US dollars taken from each vendor's pricing page when this article was updated, and they change often, so check before you buy.

## What a Local Audit Has to Cover

Google says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity" (it uses the word prominence on the same page), and that it keeps the details confidential. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Distance is how far you are from the searcher. Prominence is how well known you are, which Google says is based partly on links to your business and your reviews. Google also says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking, and gives no number.

You cannot change distance. So a useful audit checks the things you can change:

1. **Google Business Profile (GBP):** name, categories, services, photos, hours, accuracy.
2. **Reviews:** count, rating, recency, whether you reply.
3. **Website basics:** a unique title tag per page, your city and service named in plain language, your address and phone, a mobile layout that loads quickly.
4. **Listings elsewhere:** Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp, Facebook and directories showing the same name, address and phone number as your profile. This is common industry practice and sensible hygiene; Google does not publish a ranking weight for it.
5. **Links:** do any local sites link to you?
6. **Results:** calls, direction requests and website clicks from your profile, and search queries from Search Console.

No single tool covers all six. That is the reason to mix a few.

## The Free Tools (Start Here)

### Google Search Console

Search Console's Performance report shows queries, clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position. Per Google's help page, average position is the position of the topmost result from your site, and the default view covers the past three months. Use it to find queries where you appear but get few clicks, then improve that page's title and content.

**Misses:** nothing about your Maps ranking, listings or competitors.

### Google Business Profile Performance

Google's help page lists the profile metrics: searches (the terms people used to find you), views on Search and Maps, directions, calls, website clicks, messages and bookings. Only the metrics that apply to your business appear. Calls count taps on the call button, not people who read your number and dialled it by hand. Searches update at the start of each month.

**Misses:** it reports what happened, not what to fix.

### Your own eyes

Open your profile on a phone in a private window and search your main service plus your town. Note who appears above you, how many reviews they have and what their photos look like. That is a competitor audit that costs nothing.

Track includes local SEO audits, rank tracking (up to 100 keywords and four competitors), geo-grid rankings for up to five keywords, citation accuracy monitoring and Google Business Profile audits. Citation building is separate, pay as you go, from $2 per citation in bulk. There is a 14-day free trial with no card needed. Best fit for most single-location owners who want audits and tracking in one place.

**Whitespark.** Its pricing page shows Local Citation Finder plans from $33 to $149 a month, Local Rank Tracker from $14 to $200 a month and Reputation Builder at $79 a month per location. Best fit if you already use Semrush for your website.

**Ahrefs.** Ahrefs' pricing page lists Starter at $29 a month and Lite at $129, and it was unclear from the page whether those are annual or monthly rates. It is a general SEO and backlink tool, not a local one. Useful if you write a lot of content; skip it if you only need to audit a profile.

## A DIY Audit You Can Finish in an Afternoon

Use a spreadsheet with one row per check and a column for "fixed on [date]".

**Profile (30 minutes)**

- Business name matches your real-world name. Google's guidelines say extra information such as taglines, services or location details in the name is not permitted and can lead to suspension.
- Categories are as specific as possible and as few as needed. Google's wording: choose categories that complete the sentence "This business IS a".
- Hours, phone, website and service list are accurate. Photos are sharp and real; Google asks for JPG or PNG between 10 KB and 5 MB, with 720 x 720 pixels recommended.
- You reply to reviews and never offer discounts or gifts in exchange for them, which Google's review policy prohibits.

**Website (20 minutes)**

- Every page has its own title. Google's title guidance says titles should be descriptive and concise, unique per page, and not stuffed with repeated keywords.
- Your address and phone are on the page as text, not only in an image.
- Mobile speed: Google's Core Web Vitals "good" targets are Largest Contentful Paint at 2.5 seconds or less, Interaction to Next Paint at 200 milliseconds or less and Cumulative Layout Shift at 0.1 or less, measured at the 75th percentile. See our Core Web Vitals guide for local sites.
- LocalBusiness structured data: Google's documentation requires name and address and recommends fields such as telephone, opening hours and geo coordinates. Google also states it does not guarantee that features using structured data will show up, and that page says nothing about rankings. Treat it as helpful labelling, not a ranking trick.

**Listings (20 minutes)**

- Search your business name and check what appears in the first ten results.
- Compare name, address and phone on Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook and one or two industry directories. Fix mismatches at the source.

## What Reviews Mean for Your Audit

BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (1,002 US adults, published February 2026) found 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, 68% will only use a business rated four stars or higher, and 74% look for reviews from the last three months. It is a US survey from a vendor that sells review tools, so use it as a directional signal. The audit takeaway: review recency and rating are visible to every shopper, so a pile of old reviews with no replies is a finding worth fixing first.

## Do This Today

1. Open Search Console and your Business Profile performance page, and screenshot last month's searches, calls and direction requests as your baseline.
2. Run the 30-minute profile checklist above and fix the name, category, hours and photos.
3. Search your main service plus your town on your phone and note the three businesses ahead of you, with their review counts and ratings.
4. Check your name, address and phone on Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp and Facebook, and correct any mismatch.
5. Put a calendar reminder for 30 days out to re-read the same numbers. Only then decide whether a paid tool is worth it.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best free local SEO audit tool?**
A: Google Search Console plus your Business Profile performance page. Together they show how people find you on Search and Maps. They will not audit listings or compare you with competitors.

**Q: Is BrightLocal worth it for one location?**
A: It can be if you will act on the audits and rank tracking. Its Track plan is $41 a month on monthly billing, and the Math section above shows how many extra customers cover that. Use the 14-day free trial before you commit.

**Q: Do citations still matter?**
A: Accurate listings help customers and keep your details consistent, but Google does not publish a ranking weight for them. Fix mismatches, then spend your time on reviews and your profile.

**Q: How often should I run an audit?**
A: A light check monthly (numbers, reviews, hours) and a full pass twice a year.

**Q: Can an audit tool guarantee better rankings?**
A: No. Tools report problems. Google says rankings are based on relevance, distance and prominence and cannot be bought.
