A good Google review strategy is a repeatable habit: ask every happy customer, make the link one tap away, reply to everything, and never offer a reward. BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found that 97% read reviews for local businesses, and 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher. Google itself says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

This guide is for owners of coffee shops, salons, groomers and studios in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. Every number comes from a source listed at the end. Anything I made up for illustration is labelled as an assumption.

## Why Do Reviews Matter So Much for Local Businesses?

Reviews do two jobs at once. They persuade people who are already comparing you with a rival, and they feed Google's ranking. Google's help page on local results says ranking is mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. It adds that prominence reflects things like how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help.

The bar is rising. The share of consumers who demand 4.5 stars or more went from 17% in 2025 to 31% in 2026. Recency counts too: 74% look for reviews written in the last three months, and 32% want some from the last two weeks. A big pile of old reviews no longer carries a profile. Steady, fresh ones do.

## What Does Google Allow When You Ask for Reviews?

Google's rules are stricter than most owners assume. Its contributor policy says incentivized reviews, including payment, discounts and free goods or services, are not allowed and will be removed. The same policy bars merchants from discouraging negative reviews or selectively soliciting positive ones, which is what "review gating" means.

Asking is fine. Google's own help says to remind customers to leave reviews and that you can share a link or a QR code. It also says merchants should not pressure people to review while on the premises, and should not ask staff to hit a review quota. So: ask everyone politely, offer nothing in return, and keep it low pressure.

<Callout type="warning" title="No Free Coffee for a Review">
A free drink, a discount or a raffle entry in exchange for a review breaks Google's policy. Reward loyalty through a separate program, never through reviews.
</Callout>

## How Do You Turn Asking Into a Habit?

Most review gaps are not caused by unhappy customers. They come from nobody asking. BrightLocal found that 78% of consumers were asked to write a review in the last 12 months, and 28% say they will always write one if asked, up from 16% the year before. People are open to it.

Build the habit in four steps:

1. **Get the link.** In your Business Profile, copy the review link and turn it into a QR code. See our [Google Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) for where it lives.
2. **Pick one moment.** Ask right after the service ends, by text, receipt QR or follow-up email, not in front of the queue.
3. **Ask everyone.** Do not filter for happy customers first. That is gating.
4. **Automate the nudge.** A simple script that sends the link after each visit saves hours. Our [guide to automating review requests](/blog/how-to-automate-google-review-requests) shows the options.

## How Should You Reply to Reviews?

Replies are part of the strategy, not decoration. BrightLocal reports that 89% of consumers expect owners to respond, 80% are likely to use a business that replies to all its reviews, and 42% are unlikely to use one that never replies. Generic or templated replies put off 50% of consumers, so write two real sentences, not a copy-paste.

Speed matters as well: 19% expect a same-day response, 32% by the next day, and 81% within a week. Google says replies are public and should be personal, and it advises against promotional offers in them. Google does not say replies change your ranking, so treat them as a trust tool, not a ranking trick. ## What If a Review Is Fake or Unfair?

You cannot delete a customer's review yourself, but you can flag one that breaks Google's content policies. Google's help says that if you believe a review violates them, you can flag it. Reply calmly while it sits there, and say you have no record of the visit if that is true. Never argue.

For the full process, evidence to gather and what to expect, read our [guide to fake Google reviews](/blog/how-to-deal-with-fake-google-reviews). Note that 77% of consumers are less likely to use a business after reading negative reviews, so one handled-well complaint still beats silence.

## The Math: How Many Reviews Does It Take?

All inputs below are **assumptions** for illustration. Use your own counts.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Current reviews / average rating | 20 reviews, 4.4 stars (88 stars in total) |
| Customers per month | 200 |
| Customers who get the link | 200 (everyone) |
| Share who leave a review | 10% |
| New reviews per month | 20 |

To lift a 4.4 average to 4.5 with only 5-star reviews: (88 + 5n) / (20 + n) = 4.5, so n = 4. About four new 5-star reviews get you to roughly 4.5, the level 31% of consumers now filter for. At 20 new reviews a month you pass that in a week. If you start with under 20 reviews, you are also below the line that 47% of consumers won't cross, so reaching 20 comes first.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I offer a discount or free item for a review?**
A: No. Google's policy says incentivized reviews, including discounts and free goods or services, are not allowed and will be removed from Maps.

**Q: How many reviews do I need to rank well?**
A: Google gives no number. It says more reviews and positive ratings can help, alongside relevance and distance. BrightLocal's data suggests 20 is a useful first milestone, because 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer.

**Q: Is it okay to only ask my happiest customers?**
A: No. Google's policy forbids selectively soliciting positive reviews. Ask everyone, with the same link and the same wording.

**Q: Do replies help my ranking?**
A: Google says replies show you value feedback and help you stand out, but it does not state a ranking effect. BrightLocal shows they affect customer choices: 80% are likely to use a business that replies to all reviews.

**Q: How long do my replies take to appear?**
A: Google says replies usually take up to 10 minutes to review, though sometimes up to 30 days.
