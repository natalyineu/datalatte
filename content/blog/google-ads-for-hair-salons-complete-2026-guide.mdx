Google Ads can bring new clients to a hair salon when the campaign targets people searching for a specific service near the salon, sends them to a page that books, and tracks which clicks become appointments. A salon can start with a small daily budget, test two or three service keywords, and judge results on cost per booking rather than clicks. The rest of this guide covers setup, keyword choice, a worked budget example, and how to measure whether the ads pay off.

Hair salons have a clear advantage here. The work is local and appointment-based, so a search such as "balayage near me" signals intent that a general brand ad does not.

## Does Google Ads work for a hair salon?

It can, but the economics depend on ticket size and repeat visits. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reports 670,800 barbers, hairstylists, and cosmetologists employed in 2025, and projects about 80,300 openings per year over the decade, many from workers leaving the field ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/barbers-hairstylists-and-cosmetologists.htm)). That means competition for local clients is real and steady, so a salon cannot rely on being the only option in its area.

Those figures describe the market, not ad performance. Median pay of $17.25 an hour also suggests that many salons work on modest tickets, which makes cost per booking the number to watch. The worked example below shows how to test that before committing a larger budget.

## How do you set up a Google Ads campaign for a salon?

Work through these steps in order. Each one reduces wasted spend later.

1. Create a [Google Ads](https://ads.google.com/) account and add a billing method.
2. Set up a [Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/business/) with accurate hours, address, photos of your work, and recent reviews. Ads and map visibility depend on it.
3. Build one landing page for each main service, with prices, a booking button, and your address. A homepage alone usually converts worse.
4. Start with a Search campaign limited to your service area. The radius is a business decision; a 5-mile radius is a reasonable assumption for a single-location salon in a dense city.
5. Choose three to five high-value services, such as colour, extensions, or bridal styling, and build ad groups around each.
6. Write two or three ad versions per group, each with a clear booking call to action.
7. Install conversion tracking for booking confirmations and calls before the first click is paid for.

Keep the first campaign small. Changing many settings at once makes it impossible to know which change worked. ## Which keywords and negative keywords should a salon use?

Target phrases that show someone is ready to book. Examples include "hair salon near me," "balayage [your city]," "book hair appointment online," and "highlights near me." Broad terms such as "haircuts" or "hair salon" attract browsers and cost more per click, so use them sparingly or not at all.

Negative keywords stop your ads from showing for searches you do not want. For a salon, common negatives include "training," "wholesale," "supplies," "course," and "jobs." Review the search terms report weekly during the first month and add new negatives as they appear.

Quality Score matters too. Google describes it as a 1-10 diagnostic score at the keyword level, based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing page experience, each rated above average, average, or below average. It is not an auction input, but a low landing page rating is a clear signal to fix your page ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118)). If your colour keyword has a weak landing page rating, the fix is usually a colour-specific page, not a higher bid.

## What does a realistic salon ad budget look like?

The table below uses assumption-labelled inputs for a single-location salon testing one colour service. Replace them with your own numbers after the first two weeks of real data.

| Line | Input or result | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Daily budget | $15 | Assumption |
| Days in test | 30 | Assumption |
| Monthly spend | $450 | 15 x 30 |
| Average cost per click | $2.00 | Assumption |
| Clicks | 225 | 450 / 2.00 |
| Click-to-booking rate | 5% | Assumption |
| Bookings | about 11 | 225 x 0.05 = 11.25 |
| Average ticket | $60 | Assumption |
| Booked revenue | about $675 | 11.25 x 60 |
| Cost per booking | $40 | 450 / 11.25 |
| Revenue per $1 spent | about $1.50 | 675 / 450 |

The result shows a campaign that pays back only slightly on first visits. A $40 cost per booking against a $60 ticket leaves little room for product, staff time, and rent. The decision rule is simple: if cost per booking is above your gross profit per appointment, fix the landing page, tighten keywords, or pause that service. If the client returns for repeat appointments, the value of each booking rises, which is why repeat-visit rate belongs in your calculation.

Start with one service, track results for 30 days, and only add budget when the numbers hold.

## How do you tell whether the bookings are real?

Count only bookings your ads produced. Use tracked booking confirmations, call tracking, and a unique phone number on the landing page where possible. Compare these with what your booking software reports. Mismatches usually mean tracking is set up wrong, not that the ads are working.

Reviews help conversion too. Ask recent clients to name the service they had, and reply to every review. Booksy's own guidance treats reviews as a ranking and trust signal ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-marketing-ideas)), though its claim that over 90% of consumers check reviews is a vendor figure and is not repeated here as fact.

## FAQ

### How much should a single-chair salon spend on Google Ads?

Start small enough that a bad month is survivable. A $15 daily budget over 30 days, as in the worked example, gives roughly 225 clicks at the assumed cost per click. Increase spend only after you see cost per booking that fits your margin.

### Do I need a separate website for Google Ads?

A dedicated landing page usually converts better than a general homepage, because it matches the ad's service and gives a clear booking path. A booking platform link can work, but test whether it converts before relying on it.

### Which services should I advertise first?

Advertise the services with the highest ticket value and the best margin, such as colour or extensions. Lower-margin haircuts can wait until you have open slots that would otherwise stay empty.

### How long before a salon sees results?

Clicks appear within days. Bookings should appear within a few weeks if tracking and the landing page work. If there are no bookings after two weeks, check tracking, keywords, and the landing page before changing budget.

### Is Google Ads better than a Local Services Ad for a salon?

It depends on the market and the format available to you. Compare cost per booking for each option over the same period, and read the [Local Services Ads guide](/blog/google-local-services-ads-complete-guide) before deciding.
