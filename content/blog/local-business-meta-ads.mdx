## When Should a Local Business Use Meta Ads Instead of Google Ads?

Use Meta to create demand and Google to capture it. Google shows your ad to someone typing "dog groomer near me". Meta interrupts someone scrolling, so it suits:

- **Openings and new services** in your neighbourhood.
- **Offers with a hook**, such as a first-visit price or a free trial class.
- **Retargeting** website visitors and people who engaged with your Instagram.
- **Slow periods**, like Tuesday mornings.

It is weaker for urgent needs ("emergency plumber"), where Google wins. If you only have budget for one channel, pick the one nearer the purchase decision, and see our [Google Ads keyword guide for pet groomers](/blog/best-google-ad-keywords-for-pet-groomers-in-2026) for the search side.

## How Do You Set Up Meta Ads for a Local Business?

Do the foundations before spending a dollar.

1. **Page and Instagram account** connected in Meta Business Suite, with address, hours and phone.
2. **A business portfolio** so the ad account belongs to the business, not a personal profile.
3. **Tracking:** the Meta Pixel or Conversions API on your booking page, so Meta can see who converts.
4. **A landing page that works on a phone:** one offer, one button.

Without step 3 you cannot tell which ads produce bookings, and a slow homepage wastes paid clicks. See our guide to [how much to spend on Facebook ads](/blog/facebook-ads-budget-local-business-how-much-to-spend) for the money side.

## How Should You Target a Local Audience?

Set the location first, then leave the rest broad.

- **Location:** drop a pin on your shop and choose people living there. A 3 to 8 mile radius is a sensible start in a city (a planning range, not a Meta rule); go wider only if your best customers travel.
- **Age and gender:** narrow only if your service truly skews.
- **Interests:** use sparingly and let creative do the filtering.
- **Custom audiences:** upload your customer list or build a retargeting audience; these are usually your cheapest leads.

For niche-specific examples, see [Meta Ads targeting for coffee shops](/blog/meta-ads-for-coffee-shops-2026) and [Meta Ads for hair salons](/blog/using-meta-ads-for-hair-salon-marketing).

## What Offer and Creative Should You Run?

The offer matters more than any targeting setting. A weak ad says "best salon in town". A strong one names the audience, the deal and the next step.

| Business | Example offer | Single action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coffee shop | Free pastry with a first coffee this week | Show the ad at the counter |
| Hair salon | First-visit colour consultation with a price range | Book online |
| Pet groomer | First-groom welcome price for new dogs | Message to book |
| Fitness studio | Free intro class, limited spots | Reserve a spot |

Use real photos or short vertical video from your own phone, put the offer in the first line, and run two or three variations, not ten. A small budget spread over many ads teaches you nothing.

## Why Does Budget Matter for Meta's Learning Process?

Meta flags an ad set as "learning limited" when it is [unlikely to receive around 50 optimisation events in the week after its last significant edit](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981). Meta's fixes include raising the budget, widening the audience, or optimising for a more frequent event.

For a local business this creates a real trade-off, shown here with an **assumption**-based calculation:

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Cost per lead (benchmark) | $27.39 |
| Events needed per week (Meta guideline) | about 50 |
| Weekly spend to reach 50 lead events | about $1,370 |
| A small local budget (assumption) | $10 a day, $70 a week |
| Leads at that budget | about 2.6 a week |

At $70 a week you will not reach 50 leads, so the ad set may stay learning limited and results will be noisy. Options: optimise for a cheaper event such as link clicks or landing page views, widen the audience, or accept noise and judge by total bookings over 30 days. Do not change settings daily; every significant edit restarts the clock.

## What Does a 30-Day Test Look Like?

Assumptions: $300 for the month ($10 a day), one offer, one audience, two creatives, a $27.39 lead cost, 11 leads, 40% of leads book, and each new customer is worth $90 in first-year profit.

- Week 1: launch, check tracking, reply to every message within hours.
- Weeks 2 to 3: pause the weaker creative, change nothing else.
- Week 4: count bookings, not leads.

Result: 11 leads x 40% = 4.4 bookings x $90 = about $396 against $300 spend, a small profit. If only 20% book, you get 2.2 bookings and about $198, a loss of roughly $100. That loss tells you to fix follow-up speed or the offer before scaling.

Scale in steps of about 20% (a rule of thumb, not a Meta rule) only while cost per booking stays below your profit per customer.

## What Are the Most Common Meta Ads Mistakes?

- Targeting a 20-mile radius and paying to reach people who will never visit.
- Sending clicks to a homepage, not an offer page.
- Using "boost post" as the main tool, which gives less control over objective and tracking.
- Ignoring comments and messages; a slow reply often loses the booking.
- Judging after three days.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a local business spend on Meta Ads?**
A: Start with an amount you can lose for 30 days, such as the $300 in the example, and base the ceiling on what a new customer is worth.

**Q: Do I need a website?**
A: Not strictly; lead forms and message ads work. A booking page with the Pixel gives better tracking.

**Q: How long until I see results?**
A: Enquiries can arrive within days, but judge cost per booking after about 30 days.

**Q: What is a good cost per lead?**
A: LocaliQ's all-industry average for Facebook lead campaigns is $27.39 in 2026, with beauty at $50.91 and fitness at $27.11. Compare your own booking value against that.
