Rebooking is a timing system, not a discount system. Three touches do most of the work: an invitation before the client leaves the chair, a reminder 48 hours after a missed rebook window opens, and a win-back when the usual service interval passes. Booking software the salon already uses can automate all three.

No percentage belongs in that system without the salon's own records. The right first step is measurement: export the last 6 months of appointments and count how many clients returned inside their service interval. That baseline, whatever it is, is the number every change gets judged against.

## When should a salon ask for the rebooking?

At the chair, before payment. The stylist knows how the service will grow out and can name a week rather than a vague "next time". "Colour will need attention around the second week of June" converts better than "Shall I book you in?" because it gives a reason.

If the client declines, the reason matters. Price, timing and uncertainty each need a different follow-up. Booking software notes should capture it in one tap; a stylist remembering at the front desk an hour later will not. [Email marketing for yoga studios](/blog/email-marketing-for-yoga-studios) uses the same decline-reason logic for class bookings.

The 48-hour touch catches the polite decline. One short message with two concrete slots outperforms an open-ended "let me know". Concrete slots reduce the decision to yes or no, which is why appointment reminders in [Meta ads for hair salons](/blog/using-meta-ads-for-hair-salon-marketing) use the same pattern.

## What intervals should reminders follow?

Match the service, not the calendar. A fringe or fade client runs on 3 to 5 weeks. Colour commonly runs 6 to 8 weeks. Extensions and treatments vary by system and aftercare, so the stylist's chair-side estimate should set the individual date.

Software that cannot store a per-client interval will send generic monthly reminders, which clients learn to ignore. If the current booking system lacks interval reminders, that gap alone justifies comparing alternatives during the next quiet month.

**Assumption example.** Assume a salon sees 300 colour clients a quarter and 40% rebook inside 8 weeks today, or 120 clients. Lifting that to 50% adds 30 visits. At an assumed $95 average colour ticket, the lift is $2,850 a quarter. The percentages are assumptions for the arithmetic; measure the real baseline first.

## What should the win-back offer say?

Acknowledge the gap without blame, name one specific slot pattern, and add a small reason to act now. A complimentary treatment add-on protects price better than a percentage discount, and it fills the same chair.

Send once, then stop. A second and third chase message converts a lapsed client into an unsubscribed one, closing the cheaper channel permanently. Lapsed clients who return on their own should exit win-back lists immediately; booking data must feed the suppression list weekly.

Seasonal moments give the message a natural hook: pre-holiday colour, summer blonding, wedding season. [AI-powered email tools](/blog/ai-powered-email-marketing-tools) can draft variants, but send times should follow the interval data, not the tool's default schedule.

## FAQ

### What is a good salon rebooking rate?

There is no sourced universal benchmark worth trusting; published figures rarely define the interval or service mix. Measure the salon's own baseline over 6 months: clients returning inside their service interval divided by clients due. Improvement against that baseline, by service type, is the only honest target.

### Should salons discount to win back lapsed clients?

Start with specificity, not price: a named stylist, two concrete slots and a small add-on. Discounts work fastest and teach clients to wait for them. Reserve percentage offers for long-lapsed clients, 6 months or more, where the relationship itself needs restarting rather than reminding.

### Which software handles interval rebooking?

Most modern salon booking systems support automated reminders; the deciding feature is per-client or per-service intervals plus suppression when a client rebooks. Check those two during any trial with real past data. A system that only sends monthly blasts will not run this strategy, whatever its marketing claims.

### How soon should a salon follow up after a visit?

Within 48 hours for clients who left without rebooking, while the service and the stylist's interval advice are fresh. Later than a week, the message becomes a cold reminder and performs like one. The follow-up should offer two concrete slots, not an open question.
