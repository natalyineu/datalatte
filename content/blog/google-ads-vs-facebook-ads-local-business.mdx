For most local service businesses, Google Ads is the better first channel because it reaches people who are already searching for what you sell, such as "hair salon near me" or "dog groomer near me". Facebook and Instagram ads (Meta Ads) are better for building awareness, promoting offers and retargeting, and they usually work best as a second channel once your search ads are running.

Both platforms can waste money if used badly. They reach people in different mindsets and suit different businesses, so the right split depends on what you sell. Costs vary by city, industry and season, so use the figures below as context and check your own account data.

## The fundamental difference: intent vs. interruption

**Google Ads is intent-based.** When someone types "hair salon near me" into Google, they already want a hair salon. Your ad appears at the moment they are looking, so you are showing up where the searcher already is.

**Facebook and Instagram Ads are interruption-based.** Someone scrolling a feed is not searching for anything specific. Your ad interrupts friends' photos and videos, so it has to earn attention. If it is relevant and compelling they may engage, but you are competing with entertainment, not answering a search.

Neither approach is inherently better. They serve different purposes.

## When Google Ads works better

- **High-intent local services.** If someone needs a plumber, a dentist, a locksmith or a dog groomer now, they search Google.
- **Services people know they need.** Someone who has decided on a personal trainer, a yoga studio or a haircut searches for one. Search ads reach that decision in progress.
- **Speed matters.** A customer who needs same-day or this-week service searches, rather than scrolling and hoping.
- **B2B and professional services.** Business owners looking for marketing help, accounting or supplies tend to search.
- **A clear keyword maps to your service.** If a well-defined search term describes what you do, you can compete for it in your local area.

## When Facebook and Instagram Ads work better

- **Visual products and services.** Hair colour transformations, interior design, food and drink, fashion and fitness results all communicate through images or video.
- **A new business nobody knows yet.** There is little search volume to capture because people do not yet search for you by name, so awareness ads can introduce you to the neighbourhood.
- **Specific demographics.** Meta lets you combine location, age, interests and other attributes. Google targeting is built mainly around what people search for.
- **Aspirational offers.** A luxury spa, a specialty coffee experience or a boutique fitness studio can create desire before someone has an active need.
- **Retargeting.** Both platforms let you show ads to people who visited your website, but Meta is often the cheaper way to stay visible.

## Cost comparison

Neither platform publishes a single price for "local businesses", because you bid in an auction and costs depend on your market and competition.

**Google Search Ads.** WordStream's 2026 benchmark report, based on US search campaigns from April 2025 to March 2026, puts the all-industry average cost per click at $5.42, with a wide spread by industry ([WordStream via PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-ads-cpl-drops-for-first-time-since-2020-as-cpc-hits-5-42/), checked Oct 2026). Your local category may sit well below or above that. For an estimate in your area, use [Google Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243), which shows search volumes and the cost to target keywords.

**Meta Ads.** Meta charges by auction too, and published CPM benchmarks differ widely between tracking tools, so no single number is reliable for your town. Meta shows delivery estimates when you set up a campaign, and your own results after a few weeks are the best guide.

In general, search clicks cost more than social clicks, but the intent behind them is higher. As a made-up illustration: a $3.00 click from someone searching "book hair salon near me" can be worth more than a $0.80 click from someone scrolling past. Compare cost per booking, not cost per click.

## The return on investment question

Which platform pays back better depends on your funnel.

Google Ads tends to give:
- Higher intent, and so often higher conversion rates
- Higher cost per click
- Faster feedback, since clicks and bookings can start within days
- Limits on growth, because you can only reach as many people as search for your service

Meta Ads tends to give:
- Lower intent at the moment of the click
- Lower cost per click
- A learning period before delivery stabilises
- Broader reach beyond people already searching

For most local service businesses, search is the quicker route to measurable bookings, while a consistent Meta presence builds awareness over time. Measure both on cost per booking.

## The real answer: use both, with a sensible split

A common starting point for a local service business with a $1,000 monthly ad budget:

- **$650 on Google Ads** to capture high-intent local searches
- **$350 on Meta Ads** to build awareness, promote offers and retarget website visitors

This is a planning example, not a rule. If you only have $300 to $400 a month, lean toward Google Ads first, especially if you are a newer business that needs bookings soon. Once you are established with a strong Google presence, shift more budget to Meta to reach people who are not yet searching.

## By business type

- **Hair salons:** Google Ads for high-intent searches (balayage, haircut, colour). Meta Ads for transformation content and local brand building.
- **Coffee shops:** Meta Ads often fit better because the content is visual and visits are impulse-driven. Use Google Ads for people searching your brand name.
- **Pet groomers:** Google Ads works well for "groomer near me" searches. Meta Ads suit community building and new-client offers.
- **Fitness studios:** Google Ads for "gym near me" and specific class searches, with extra budget in peak months such as January. Meta Ads for transformation content, challenges and community.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Google Ads or Facebook Ads better for a local business?**
A: For most local service businesses, Google Ads is the better first channel because it captures people searching right now. Facebook and Instagram ads suit awareness, visual businesses and retargeting.

**Q: Which is cheaper, Google Ads or Facebook Ads?**
A: Social clicks are usually cheaper than search clicks, but search clicks tend to come from people closer to buying. Compare cost per booking, not cost per click.

**Q: How should I split a small advertising budget between Google and Meta?**
A: With $300 to $400 a month, start with Google Ads. With around $1,000, a split of roughly two thirds Google and one third Meta is a common starting point. Adjust based on cost per booking.

**Q: Can I run both Google Ads and Facebook Ads at the same time?**
A: Yes. Search captures existing demand, and Meta builds awareness and retargets website visitors. Make sure conversion tracking works for both.

**Q: How long does it take to see results from local ads?**
A: Search ads can produce clicks and inquiries within days, but give them several weeks of data before making big changes. Meta campaigns also need time to learn who responds to your ads.
