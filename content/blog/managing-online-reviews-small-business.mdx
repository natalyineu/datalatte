Managing online reviews means asking every customer for an honest review, replying to all of them, and flagging only what breaks the platform's rules. BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US consumers found 97% read reviews for local businesses, 89% expect owners to respond, and 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. Google bans paid and selectively requested reviews, so the routine has to be honest to be safe.

This guide is for owners of small local businesses in the US. Survey numbers are from BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (published February 11, 2026). Policy points are from Google's own pages.

## Why Do Online Reviews Matter for a Small Business?

Reviews are one of the first things a new customer checks. In BrightLocal's data, 41% of consumers always read them, up from 29% in 2025. Star rating counts: 68% require four or more stars, 31% only use businesses rated 4.5 or higher, and 10% only use five-star businesses.

Where people look is shifting. 71% use Google for reviews, down from 83% the year before. BrightLocal also reports 40% trust AI platforms for business recommendations. So keep your profiles accurate on Google and the other sites your customers use. Our [review platform comparison](/blog/review-platform-comparison-yelp-google-facebook-tripadvisor) covers where to focus.

## How Do You Ask for Reviews the Right Way?

Google says you can share a review request link or QR code from your Business Profile, and that merchants may encourage genuine reviews as long as they offer no incentive and do not try to influence the rating or content.

Google's contribution policy forbids:

- Offering incentives to post, revise or remove a review.
- Selectively asking only for positive reviews.
- Pressuring customers to review on the premises.
- Asking for specific content, such as a staff member's name.
- Setting staff a target number of reviews to collect.

Ask everyone the same way, right after the service, with a short message and the link. See [how to get more Google reviews for a coffee shop](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-coffee-shop) for a niche example.

## How Fast Should You Reply?

BrightLocal says 89% of consumers expect businesses to respond to reviews, 19% expect a same-day reply and 81% expect one within a week. So a weekly check is the minimum, and a two-day target is safer.

To reply, open your Business Profile, choose Read reviews, then Reply. Google checks replies against its content policies before posting and may ask you to edit them. Keep replies short, use the reviewer's name if shown, and avoid sharing private details about the visit.

## What Should You Do About a Negative or Fake Review?

For a real unhappy customer, reply politely, say what you will check, and invite them to contact you offline. Do not argue in public.

For a review that breaks policy, flag it. Google's help says you can flag a review that violates its content policies, and its page does not say owners can remove customer reviews. Policy-breaking content includes fake engagement and reviews written with a conflict of interest. Read our guide on [how to deal with fake Google reviews](/blog/how-to-deal-with-fake-google-reviews) before you report one.

## Can AI Write Your Review Replies?

It can draft them, and you should read each before posting. The FTC's final rule on fake reviews bans fake or false reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones, and allows civil penalties against knowing violators. Writing replies is different from writing reviews, but never invent customer feedback. See our [AI agent for Google reviews](/blog/ai-agent-for-google-reviews-management) guide for approval rules.

## Worked Example: A Weekly 30-Minute Review Routine

All inputs below are **assumptions**, not benchmarks.

| Step | Time (assumption) |
| --- | --- |
| Read new reviews on Google and one other site | 8 min |
| Reply to each, about 5 a week | 15 min |
| Send review requests to the week's customers | 5 min |
| Note themes in a spreadsheet | 2 min |

That is 30 minutes a week. If you have 12 reviews and want to pass the 20-review mark that 47% of consumers use as a cutoff, you need 8 more. At 5 new reviews a month you reach it in 2 months. The rate is an assumption. Your own response rate to requests decides it. Use the notes to spot repeat complaints, such as slow service, and fix them at source.

## FAQ

### How many reviews does a small business need?

BrightLocal found 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, so 20 is a sensible first target. Quality and recency count too, since 74% only care about reviews from the last three months.

### Can I pay customers or give discounts for reviews?

No. Google's policy bans incentives for posting, revising or removing a review.

### Can I delete a bad review?

Not as the owner, per Google's help. You can flag reviews that violate policy and reply to the rest.

### How quickly should I reply?

81% of consumers expect a reply within a week and 19% the same day, per BrightLocal.

### Should I ask only happy customers?

No. Selectively soliciting positive reviews is against Google's rules. Ask every customer. Our [Google reviews strategy](/blog/google-business-reviews-strategy) explains how to do it ethically.
