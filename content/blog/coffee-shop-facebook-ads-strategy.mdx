A coffee shop can run effective Facebook and Instagram ads by targeting a small radius around the shop, promoting one clear offer with real photos or short video, spending a modest daily budget and tracking visits with a redeemable code. The goal is simple: turn people who live or work nearby into first-time customers, then into regulars.

Facebook and Instagram ads are both managed in Meta's Ads Manager. They work well for cafes because you can show your drinks and space to people within a short walk, for a few dollars a day. Here is a practical process.

## Step 1: Decide the goal

Pick one goal per campaign. Common ones for a coffee shop:

- **Get new customers through the door:** a first-visit offer.
- **Promote a new product or season:** a new menu item or limited drink.
- **Build your email or loyalty list:** sign-ups for rewards or offers.
- **Fill quiet times:** a mid-afternoon deal.
- **Announce an opening or event.**

A clear goal tells you which offer, creative and measurement to use. Avoid campaigns that try to do everything at once.

## Step 2: Set up the foundations

Before spending anything:

1. **A Facebook Page and Instagram professional account** linked together.
2. **A Meta Business account** and Ads Manager access.
3. **An ad account with a payment method.**
4. **A landing point:** your Google Business Profile, a menu page or a sign-up page. Make sure it loads fast on mobile.
5. **A tracking method:** a unique code, a QR coupon or a staff prompt, so you can see which customers came from ads.
6. **Meta Pixel** installed if you drive traffic to a website, so you can measure and retarget.

Make sure your profile pictures, opening hours and menu are current. Ads send people to your pages, so they need to look good.

## Step 3: Choose the campaign type

Meta offers several objectives, and names change over time. For a local cafe, these tend to fit:

- **Awareness or reach,** for a new opening or local brand introduction.
- **Traffic,** to send people to a menu, a page or a map.
- **Engagement,** for events, posts or message conversations.
- **Leads,** to collect email addresses or offer sign-ups using an instant form.
- **Sales,** if you sell beans, gift cards or merchandise online.

If you are new, start with one campaign focused on a single offer and a single objective such as Leads or Traffic. Our [Meta Ads service](/services/meta-ads) can help with account structure.

## Step 4: Target locally

Local targeting is your biggest advantage.

- **Location:** set a radius around your shop. For a walk-in cafe, one to three miles in a city, or a larger radius in a suburb, is typical. Adjust for how far your customers travel.
- **Age and interests:** broad often works well. Overly narrow targeting can limit delivery and raise costs.
- **Exclusions:** exclude existing customers if your offer is for first-timers, for example by uploading an email list.
- **Placements:** automatic placements are a fine starting point. Check results by placement later.

Think about who passes your shop: commuters, students, office workers, parents, tourists. Tailor creative to the group you most want, such as a morning commute offer for nearby offices.

## Step 5: Create ads people stop for

Coffee is visual, so lean on strong images and short video.

**Photos and video that work:**

- Close-ups of drinks, latte art and steam.
- A short clip of a barista pouring or pulling a shot.
- Your storefront and seating, so people recognise it when they arrive.
- Real staff and real customers (with permission).

**Copy that works:**

- **Lead with the benefit and place:** "Fresh pastries and great coffee on [street name]".
- **State the offer plainly:** "Free cookie with any large drink this week".
- **Add a deadline or limit,** only if real.
- **One clear call to action:** "Show this ad at the counter".

Keep the text short. Use vertical formats for Stories and Reels, and square or 4:5 for the feed. Test two or three versions of an image or headline against each other.

## Step 6: Pick a budget

There is no standard budget, but most coffee shops can start small.

| Stage | Daily budget (typical) | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test | Roughly $5 to $10 | Run one offer for two weeks; learn what works |
| Grow | Roughly $10 to $20 | Increase spend on ads that bring customers |
| Retarget | Roughly $3 to $5 | Show offers to people who engaged or visited your site |

Adjust for your market and margin. Do the maths first: if an average first-time customer is worth a certain amount over their first few visits, how much can you spend to win one? Let the ads run for several days before judging, since Meta needs time to learn. Make changes gradually.

## Step 7: Choose an offer worth showing

The offer is often more important than the ad design. Ideas that suit cafes:

- A free add-on, such as a pastry with a first drink.
- A "bring a friend" offer.
- A morning-commute deal.
- A loyalty card sign-up bonus. See our ideas for a [loyalty program without an app](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app).
- A limited seasonal drink.

Protect your margin. A discount that loses money on every cup is not a strategy, so price your offer to cover costs.

## Step 8: Track visits

Ads are easy to waste if you cannot see the results. Simple ways to track:

- **Unique code or phrase:** "Say 'Latte20' at the till."
- **QR coupon** to scan or show at the counter.
- **Tally sheet** for staff to log redemptions.
- **Ask new customers,** "How did you hear about us?"
- **Ads Manager data:** reach, clicks, cost per result and sign-ups.

Compare redemptions to spend. If an ad costs $100 and brings in 20 new customers who each spend a few visits' worth, you have a real picture of whether it is working.

## Step 9: Retarget and nurture

Warm audiences are generally cheaper to reach. Once you have some data:

- **Retarget people who engaged** with your page, video or website.
- **Show offers to your email list** through a custom audience, where consent allows.
- **Promote loyalty sign-ups** to recent customers.

Connect ads to your email list to continue the conversation. Our [coffee shop email list building guide](/blog/coffee-shop-email-list-building) explains how.

## Common mistakes

- **Boosting posts without a goal.** Use Ads Manager for more control.
- **Targeting too wide a radius.** People will not travel across town for a coffee.
- **Weak images.** Dim, cluttered photos drag results down.
- **No tracking.** You cannot improve what you cannot see.
- **Changing everything daily.** Give ads time to settle.
- **Running ads for hours when you are closed.** Use scheduling where possible, or set the campaign to match your opening hours.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a coffee shop spend on Facebook ads?**

A: Many start with roughly $5 to $10 a day and adjust. The right figure depends on your margins, your market and how much a new customer is worth over time.

**Q: Should I use Facebook ads or Google Ads?**

A: They do different jobs. Facebook and Instagram introduce your cafe to people who are not searching yet; Google reaches people looking for coffee now. Many shops start with a good Google profile and add Meta ads for awareness.

**Q: How long until I see results?**

A: Allow at least two weeks for a first test, since delivery needs time to stabilise. Track redemptions throughout.

**Q: Do boosted posts work?**

A: They are easy but offer fewer controls. Ads Manager lets you set goals, radius and placements more precisely.
