Social media works for a food business when it shows the food, the people behind it and the daily specials, posted on a schedule the owner can keep. Many diners now find new places online before they walk past them. Start with free profiles on one or two platforms, add a small paid boost once organic posts perform, and reply to every review. ## Why does social media matter for food businesses?

Food is one of the most visual categories on social platforms. According to a 2024 Sprout Social report cited by [Adwave](https://adwave.com/resources/bakery-advertising), food and beverage content generates 0.63% engagement on Instagram, the highest rate of any industry on that platform. Engagement rate is a relative measure, so treat it as a signal that food content travels well, not as a promise of sales.

The bigger business question is discovery. The same Adwave bakery guide cites the National Restaurant Association's 2024 State of the Industry report, which says 60% of consumers discover new food businesses through online searches and social media rather than by driving past them. For a shop on a quiet side street, that shift matters more than any single post.

Intuit's 2025 Small Business Advertising Trends Report found that 75% of surveyed small businesses considered social media an effective advertising channel, according to [Adwave's summary](https://adwave.com/resources/when-to-quit-social-media-platform). Survey responses describe perception, so check the results against your own sales records.

## Which platforms should a food business pick?

Pick the one or two platforms where your customers already spend time, and do them well. Madsun Media's small business guide makes the same point: choose the platforms where customers actually spend time, then commit to them, as described in [its Australian marketing strategies article](https://www.madsunmedia.com/blog/small-business-marketing-australia). Spreading a small team across five networks usually produces five thin feeds.

Google Business Profile is often the most valuable profile a food business controls. Google's help page says featured posts are available in English for single-location food and drink businesses in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, per [Google's post guide](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en). Check the live help page to confirm whether your listing qualifies.

Link your accounts properly. Google lets you add one social media link per platform to your Business Profile, covering Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Pinterest, TikTok, X and YouTube, as explained in [Google's profile editing help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617). Instagram now allows up to five links in a profile bio, according to [Bookeo's beauty salon guide](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/), which helps if you run a menu page, an online order page and a catering form.

## What content makes people want to visit?

Show three things: the process, the people and the real service moments. A kitchen clip of dough being shaped, a chef introducing the day's special and a busy lunch rush give followers a reason to care beyond a product photo. A menu board alone rarely does this, and a static price list gives people no reason to visit.

Test before you polish. Adwave's 2026 trends article argues that small businesses lose weeks polishing one video when they should test several versions, and suggests testing about five versions of a short-form concept, as described in [its trends guide](https://adwave.com/resources/social-media-trends-for-small-business-in-2026). Shoot the same dish three ways, then keep the version that gets saves and replies.

Staff can help without posting from personal accounts. Adwave's employee advocacy guide recommends offering separate opt-in channels, such as a professional account, an internal content-sharing group or a company-managed profile, so team members can contribute without using their own accounts, as outlined in [its employee advocacy article](https://adwave.com/resources/employee-advocacy-social-media). Ask a cook to film a prep step on a phone, then post it through the business account.

Offline material can feed the feed. Adwave's free graphics guide notes that restaurants and similar businesses often have plenty of printed menus and signage but inconsistent social execution, and that tools such as Desygner can turn that collateral into posts, per [its graphics tools article](https://adwave.com/resources/creating-social-media-graphics-without-a-designer-free-tools). Reuse one good photo across Instagram, Facebook and your Business Profile.

## How much should a food business spend on social ads?

Start small. Adwave's food truck guide says even $5 to $10 a day targeted to a metro can meaningfully grow a local following, and that some food trucks add $5 to $15 a day on paid social boosts or Google Ads after using free organic channels, as described in [its food truck advertising article](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-food-trucks-mobile-vendors). Adwave's separate local channels guide puts monthly social advertising budgets for local businesses at $200 to $1,500 in [its channel comparison](https://adwave.com/resources/best-local-advertising-channels).

Adwave also cites a U.S. Small Business Administration rule of thumb that many small businesses spend roughly 7% to 8% of gross revenue on marketing when trying to grow, in [its ad spend guide](https://adwave.com/resources/how-much-should-a-small-business-spend-on-social-media-ads). That is a benchmark, not a target. Use it to sanity-check whether your budget is in a reasonable range.

Here is a worked example with every input labelled. The figures are assumptions, not sourced data:

| Line item | Monthly cost | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Paid Instagram boost, $10 a day over 30 days | $300 | Assumption: $10/day, within the $5-15 range above |
| Scheduling tool on a free plan | $0 | Assumption: a free plan covers your volume |
| Phone tripod and light, $300 spread over 12 months | $25 | Assumption: one-off equipment cost |
| Total | $325 | Sum of rows |

Assume monthly revenue of $25,000 and an average ticket of $15. The 7% to 8% benchmark would allow $1,750 to $2,000, so $325 is well under it. To cover the $325 from new visits alone, you need about 22 extra tickets a month ($325 divided by $15, rounded up). If boosted posts bring in fewer than that, cut the boost before you cut the content.

## How should reviews fit into social media?

Reviews are part of your social media, whether you post them or not. BrightLocal's 2026 figure, cited by [OnEveryMap](https://oneverymap.com/insights/what-is-multi-location-social-media-management), says 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses. A cook's photo beside a detailed five-star review often works better than a generic brand graphic.

Choose reviews carefully. Yotpo advises businesses to focus on the top 10 to 20% of reviews for social publishing, as summarised in [Adwave's social proof guide](https://adwave.com/resources/using-reviews-and-testimonials-as-social-proof-on-social-media). Ask for permission before reposting. Yotpo's guidance says to capture consent with a checkbox at submission that grants permission to reuse the review in marketing.

Google's own guidelines allow businesses to post their own contact details on their profile or in responses, but soliciting personal or confidential information is not allowed, per [its representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177). Keep replies to reviews focused on the food and the visit, and move private issues offline.

## How do you measure whether social media is working?

Track sales, not just likes. Add a "How did you hear about us?" prompt at the till, or ask it on your online order form, and log the answers weekly. Create one promo code per platform so you can see which channel produces redemptions. Check your Business Profile insights monthly for calls, direction requests and website clicks.

Use this checklist each month:

1. Record new customers mentioning each platform, using the till prompt.
2. Compare boosted spend against the tickets you can credibly attribute to it.
3. Note which post types earned the most saves and replies, and make more of them.
4. Count how many reviews you replied to within the week.
5. Drop any paid boost that cannot be linked to visits after two months.

For deeper tracking, see [how AI tools can summarise social analytics](/blog/ai-powered-social-media-analytics-for-local-businesses). Scheduling tools can also reduce the weekly effort. Adwave's scheduling tools review notes that Metricool offers a real free plan and a pricing structure that works for businesses managing several brands, while Zoho Social's per-brand pricing can become less attractive for multi-location businesses, according to [its comparison](https://adwave.com/resources/best-social-media-scheduling-tools-for-small-business). If you want automation beyond scheduling, read [the guide to AI agents for social posting](/blog/ai-agent-for-social-media-posting-local-business) before committing.

## FAQ

### Which platform should a small café start with?

Start with the platform your regulars already use, and add Google Business Profile posts if your business qualifies. Madsun Media's advice is to pick one or two platforms and do them well, so a café usually starts with one social account plus its Google listing.

### How often should a food business post?

Choose a schedule you can keep for months. The guidance here does not set a universal number. Batch content weekly, use a scheduler, and review what gets saves and replies. For a fuller plan, see [the local business content creation guide](/blog/local-business-social-media-content-creation).

### Do I need TikTok or short video to succeed?

No. Short video can help, but Adwave's trends guide recommends testing concepts rather than assuming one format wins. If video is hard to produce, a steady stream of clear photos and short staff updates can work on Instagram or Facebook.

### Should I use AI to write captions?

AI can speed up drafts, but check every caption against what you actually serve. For a broader view of automation, read [how AI-driven social content works for local businesses](/blog/ai-driven-social-media-content-for-local-businesses).
