A local business needs a small, repeatable content routine, not a big strategy: a few useful posts a week showing what you sell, who you are and when to come in. Pew Research's 2026 survey of 5,511 US adults found 84% use YouTube, 70% use Facebook and 51% use Instagram, so most local customers can be reached on at least one of them. No source checked publishes how often a local business should post, so the plan below is a starting point to test.

This guide is for shops, cafes, salons, studios and trades. The weekly example is labelled as an assumption. Tool prices come from the vendor's page.

## What Should a Local Business Post?

Post things that help a nearby customer decide to visit. Four types cover most needs: what you sell (products, services, prices), proof (reviews, before-and-after with permission), people (staff, process) and practical news (hours, offers, events). Each post should have one purpose and one next step, such as booking, calling or visiting.

Avoid generic quotes and stock images. A phone photo of today's real product beats a polished graphic that could belong to any business. Our [content calendar guide](/blog/how-to-create-social-media-content-calendar-small-business) and the [coffee shop content ideas](/blog/coffee-shop-social-media-content-ideas) give examples you can adapt.

## Which Platform Should You Use?

Choose by where your customers already are, then do one platform well. Pew's 2026 figures put Facebook ahead of Instagram among all US adults, 70% versus 51%, but those are averages across ages. Check your own followers' ages and where bookings come from. The Pew page's other platforms could not be matched reliably from the data available, so they are not quoted here.

Google Business Profile deserves a place on the same list. Posts there can be Updates, Offers or Events, and only live posts appear on Search and Maps. Offer posts need a title, dates and time and include a "View Offer" button. Posts older than six months are archived unless you set a date range.

## What Tools Do You Need?

Few. Buffer's pricing page lists a Free plan with up to 3 channels and 10 scheduled posts per channel, which refill as posts publish, and an Essentials plan at $5 per channel per month. The page's yearly and monthly figures do not fully reconcile, so confirm the price at checkout. Canva's pricing page returned an access error when checked, so no Canva prices are given.

You may not need paid tools at all at first. A notes app, your phone camera and the native scheduling in each platform are enough for a month. Add a tool only when posting on time becomes the bottleneck.

## Worked Example: A Weekly Routine

All inputs are **assumptions** for a small cafe or salon with one person handling posts.

| Day | Post | Time to make |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Product or service photo with price | 10 minutes |
| Wednesday | Short video of a process or staff member | 20 minutes |
| Friday | Offer or event for the weekend, also on Google profile | 15 minutes |
| Weekend | Share a customer review with permission | 5 minutes |
| Total | Four posts | about 50 minutes a week |

At an assumed $25 an hour of owner time, that is roughly $21 a week, or about $90 a month, before any ad spend. If a post brings two extra bookings a month at $40 each, that is $80, so the routine roughly pays for itself on those assumptions. Measure instead of guessing: ask each new customer how they heard of you and track it for eight weeks.

## What About Influencers and Customer Content?

Customer photos and local creators can fill your calendar, but ask permission before reposting and disclose paid or gifted relationships. The FTC says to disclose any financial or personal relationship with a brand, including free products, in clear words such as "ad" or "sponsored". ## FAQ

### How often should a local business post?

No source we opened sets a standard. Start with three to four posts a week you can keep up for two months, then compare enquiries.

### Which platform is best for a local business?

It depends on your customers. Pew's 2026 survey shows Facebook at 70% and Instagram at 51% of US adults. Use the platform where your bookings already come from.

### Do I need a paid scheduling tool?

No. Buffer lists a free plan with 3 channels and 10 scheduled posts per channel, and native platform schedulers are also free to use.

### Should I post on Google Business Profile too?

Yes. Offer and Event posts can appear on Search and Maps. Keep them current, since older posts are archived after six months.

### What if I run out of ideas?

Reuse your questions from customers: each frequent question is a post.
