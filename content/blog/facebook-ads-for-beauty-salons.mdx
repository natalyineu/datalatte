## Why do Facebook ads suit beauty salons?

Clients spend time on social platforms before they choose a salon. Bookeo reports that potential clients spend [over 17 hours per week on social media](https://www.bookeo.com/news/2026/09/social-media-marketing-beauty-salons-ultimate-guide-filling-appointment-book/), which gives a salon a steady place to show its work. The same guide cites about 4.7k monthly global searches for "natural hair salon near me", showing how many people look for a salon by specialty.

Scale figures should be read carefully. The 4.9 billion social media users figure is cited in a [secondary university page](https://douglasj.edu/why-social-media-is-important-in-the-beauty-industry/), and the 4.7k search figure is global, not local. A salon in one town reaches only the people within its area, so the useful question is how many nearby clients see the ad each week. For a broader look at whether paid social fits a local business, read [do Facebook ads actually work for local businesses](/blog/do-facebook-ads-actually-work-for-local-businesses-data-backed-answer).

## How should a salon set its objective and targeting?

Choose a conversion objective when the goal is bookings. Facebook's conversion objective optimises for actions you define, such as a completed booking, so the ad platform needs a working tracking setup before you launch. Awareness or engagement objectives suit brand building but do not measure appointments directly.

Targeting should follow the salon's real catchment. Set a radius around the salon that matches where clients actually travel, often a few miles for a neighbourhood salon. Avoid country-wide or worldwide targeting, which spends money on people who cannot book a chair. Narrow interests such as bridal, colour or lash services can help when the salon specialises.

## Which creative shows transformations best?

Before-and-after video is the strongest format for a salon because the result is visible in seconds. Keep the first two seconds on the finished look, then show the process briefly. Captions should name the service, the duration and the booking step, for example "Balayage in about 3 hours, book this week".

Creator content can supply fresh footage and social proof. A guide to [working with local creators for salon bookings](/blog/influencer-marketing-for-hair-salons-how-to-get-free-bookings-from-local-creator) explains how to brief creators and collect usable clips. Test two or three creative versions against each other rather than relying on one video. Write headlines that name a specific service or offer, and avoid claims about results you cannot document.

## How do you track which bookings come from ads?

Tracking tells you which ad produced which appointment. Without it, the salon only sees total bookings and guesses at the cause.

1. Install the Meta pixel on the website and on the booking page.
2. Set up a conversion event that fires when a booking is confirmed, not when someone just views the page.
3. Add a campaign tag to booking links so the booking software records the source.
4. Ask new clients at check-in how they heard about the salon and record the answer.
5. Compare cost per booking by ad set once each ad has spent enough to show a pattern.

Check the setup with a test booking before spending the full budget. A broken event leads to wrong decisions, so confirm that each booking appears in the ads dashboard.

## Should you retarget people who visited the booking page?

Retargeting shows ads to people who already visited the salon's website or booking page. Create a custom audience from website visitors over the last 30 days, then exclude anyone who has already booked. Show this group a reminder with a specific service or offer, such as a short-notice slot or a free add-on that the salon already offers.

Keep retargeting frequency low so the ad does not feel pushy. A simple sequence works well: one reminder with the booking link, then a single offer ad a week later. Retargeting depends on the pixel, so the tracking checks above must come first. ## FAQ

### How much should a small beauty salon spend on Facebook ads?

Start with a daily budget the salon can afford to lose during a test, often around $10 per day as in the worked example above. Judge the spend on cost per booking against the profit from a visit, not on clicks alone. Increase budget only after a cost per booking that meets the target holds for several weeks.

### Which objective should a salon choose for Facebook ads?

Choose a conversion objective when the goal is bookings, because the platform then optimises for completed appointments. This requires the pixel and a confirmed booking event. Awareness objectives suit brand building but do not show which ads fill the appointment book.

### How long before Facebook ads show results for a salon?

Allow enough time for the platform to gather data before judging the ads. Two to three weeks with consistent spend and working tracking usually gives a clearer picture than the first few days. Pause ads only when the cost per booking stays far above the target.

### Do Facebook ads work for salons outside big cities?

They can, provided the radius covers the salon's real catchment. A smaller local audience means the budget reaches fewer people, so the test budget should stay modest and the targeting should match where clients live. Check whether the cost per booking meets the target before expanding the radius.
