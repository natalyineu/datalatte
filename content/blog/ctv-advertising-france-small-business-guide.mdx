Connected TV (CTV) ads run on internet-connected TVs and streaming services, and vendors now sell them through self-serve platforms and managed services. A small French business can plan a test by checking four things: which geographic area it can reach, what the spot format is, how cost is measured and what the campaign must earn back. This guide sets out those checks. The sources used here are vendor and platform material, mostly US or global, so they contain no verified French prices, broadcaster figures or audience counts.

## Can a Small Business Buy CTV Ads Without an Agency?

Some platforms allow it. Paramount's CTV dictionary describes self-serve campaign creation through online advertising platforms, with household-level targeting and real-time reporting. Amazon's guide to connected TV takes a different route: brands with limited experience can request services managed by Amazon Ads, and budget minimums apply.

The sources do not say which platforms sell self-serve CTV in France, or what their minimums are. Check each provider's current terms before planning a budget. Other European markets are covered in the [Germany CTV guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-germany-small-business-guide) and the [Ireland CTV guide](/blog/ctv-advertising-ireland-small-business-guide), which describe the same mechanics.

## How Does Targeting Work for a Local Business?

Geographic targeting is the most useful feature for a shop, salon or restaurant. Paramount's dictionary lists targeting by country, state, DMA (Designated Market Area), ZIP code, or a radius around a specific address, and calls this particularly valuable for local TV advertising.

CTV also offers household-level targeting by age, gender and income, contextual targeting by the content being watched, and custom audiences built from first-party data such as CRM email lists or website visitors. Ask the provider which of these work at the size of your catchment area. A radius around a premises may be too small for a platform's minimum audience.

## What Does a CTV Spot Look Like?

Paramount describes CTV ads as non-skippable video, usually 15 or 30 seconds, shown full screen. Its advice is a strong opening hook in the first three to five seconds, clear and simple messaging, on-screen branding and a verbal or visual call to action.

Amazon notes that the video completion rate of CTV ads tends to be very high because many streaming services do not allow ads to be skipped. That makes the opening seconds and the closing call to action the parts to write carefully. A clear offer, a street name and one phone number or web address fit a 15 second spot better than a long list of services.

## How Do You Measure a CTV Campaign?

The sources give standard formulas. Paramount defines CPM as (Total Spend ÷ Impressions) × 1,000, cost per lead (CPL) as the cost to generate one qualified lead, ROAS as Revenue ÷ Ad Spend, and video completion rate (VCR) as Completed Views ÷ Total Impressions.

Amazon adds brand reach, brand lift and, for brands that also sell on Amazon, new-to-brand customers. CPM is useful for comparing buying options, but a campaign is better judged on CPL or ROAS. A low CPM with no visits is still a poor result for a local business.

## What Should You Check Before Signing?

Work through these checks in order. Each one comes from the platform material above.

1. Confirm the smallest geographic unit the provider can target, such as a ZIP code, DMA or radius.
2. Ask for the minimum audience size and the minimum budget for that unit.
3. Choose a spot length of 15 or 30 seconds and write the offer for the first five seconds.
4. Agree on the metric that decides success, such as CPL or ROAS, before the first impression is bought.
5. Set up a tracking code or landing page unique to the campaign, so visits can be linked to the spend.

## What Must a Campaign Earn Back? A Worked Example

The example below uses assumptions throughout. None of the inputs is a sourced price. Replace them with your own numbers and with the CPM the provider quotes.

| Input | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Campaign budget | $2,000 | Assumption |
| CPM quoted by provider | $25 | Assumption |
| Impressions bought | 80,000 | Budget ÷ CPM × 1,000 |
| Average sale per new customer | $60 | Assumption |
| Gross margin on that sale | 50% | Assumption |
| Gross profit per new customer | $30 | Sale × margin |
| New customers to cover budget | 67 | $2,000 ÷ $30, rounded up |
| ROAS needed to break even | 2.0 | 1 ÷ 50% margin |

Sixty-seven new customers is about 0.08% of the 80,000 impressions. That is the bar a campaign must clear. If the provider's CPM is $40 instead, impressions drop to 50,000 while the customers needed stay at 67, so the bar rises in relative terms. Run the numbers with the real margin before signing anything.

## Is Google's Display Network an Easier Test?

Google's help page says Display campaigns can reach people across 35 million websites and apps, and on Google-owned properties such as YouTube and Gmail. Campaigns using Target CPA bidding can be set to pay for conversions rather than clicks, which ties spend more closely to results.

That makes Display a cheaper way to test whether streaming audiences respond to an offer before committing to TV-scale budgets. ## FAQ

### Is there a verified CTV price list for France?

Not in the sources used for this guide. Ask each provider for a quote and compare it against the CPM formula above.

### What is the difference between CTV and OTT?

Strategus describes CTV as the internet-connected TV device, such as a smart TV, set-top box or gaming console. OTT describes the delivery method, which can reach phones, tablets and laptops as well as TVs.

### How long are CTV ads?

Paramount says CTV ads typically run for 15 or 30 seconds and appear full screen and non-skippable.

### Can I target only customers near my shop?

Paramount lists geo-targeting by ZIP code, DMA or a radius around an address. Confirm the minimum audience size with the provider.
