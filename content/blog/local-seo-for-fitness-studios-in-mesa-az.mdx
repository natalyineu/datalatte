The Map Pack lists relevant local businesses for searches such as "personal training studios near me," and the profile supplies the hours, phone number, address, reviews and photos shown in those results. Ranking in that block depends on how complete the listing is, how consistent your business details are across the web, and how specific your reviews are. ## How does the Map Pack decide which Mesa studios appear?

The Map Pack is the block of local listings shown for searches with local intent. The Association of Fitness Studios describes it as listing relevant businesses for queries such as "personal training studios near me" (secondary). A prospect who sees your studio in that block can read your hours, reviews and photos without opening your website, so the listing does much of the first introduction.

Google describes its Business Profile as free, and its Canadian setup page uses the headline "Stand out on Google with a free Business Profile" (primary). Setup carries no listing fee, so the real cost is staff time. Paid ads are a separate decision, and you can make it after the listing is complete. A step-by-step profile walkthrough is in the [Google Business Profile guide for fitness studios](/blog/fitness-studio-google-business-profile-optimization).

## How much does Google Business Profile affect map pack rankings?

A BrightLocal survey, reported by Ahrefs, found that 36% of SEOs in 2021 believed the Google Business Profile was the most important ranking factor for the map pack (secondary). The same series rose from 23% in 2013, so the share of practitioners who put the profile first has grown over time.

That figure measures expert opinion, not a measured ranking weight. Treat it as a reason to prioritize the profile, not as a formula. Keep in mind that Google shows the profile itself in Maps and Search, so any empty field is information a prospect cannot use.

## Which Business Profile details should a Mesa studio complete first?

Work through this sequence on the live listing, not a draft.

1. Claim the listing and confirm the name, address and phone number match your real studio exactly.
2. Upload at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes in session and coaches, as one guide recommends (secondary).
3. Publish posts about classes, trial offers or events, then check each one shows a Live status. Google shows a post on Search and Maps only when it is Live, and Pending or Not approved posts do not show (primary).
4. Write answers to the questions prospects ask most, such as whether there is a free trial, and check them on the live profile.
5. Choose a primary category that matches your core service, then describe secondary class types in the description text.

Photos and posts are the fields that go stale fastest. Set a monthly calendar so the profile does not sit idle between promotions.

## Which citation sources matter for a Mesa fitness studio?

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address and phone number. Consistency matters more than volume. If one listing says "St." and another says "Street," or one uses a different phone format, pick a single format and use it everywhere.

Start with the directories you already use, such as Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Facebook, and any booking platform that lists your classes. Add local sources such as a chamber of commerce or neighborhood association if they list businesses in Mesa. Before creating new listings, run a citation audit with a tool such as BrightLocal or Moz Local and correct existing errors. New citations with wrong details add noise rather than strength.

On your own site, a [Google Maps embed on the location page](/blog/google-maps-embed-local-business-website) reinforces the address your profile shows.

## How do reviews shape a studio's visibility in Maps?

Google says reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search (primary). That makes reviews visible at the moment a prospect compares studios, which is why they matter beyond any ranking effect.

Some guides give specific review-count targets, but treat any number without a cited study as a hypothesis to test. Track your review count and average rating monthly and watch how they move after you change your request process.

Ask customers after a class or trial, and invite them to mention what they valued, such as the coaching or beginner-friendly classes. Reply to each review with your service and city named naturally, so the response reads as a genuine answer rather than keyword filler.

## How should a studio track whether the profile is working?

Measure monthly, using the same queries each time. Check the Map Pack for your main search terms in a private browser window, and log whether your studio appears and where. Google's own tools help too: Business Profile insights show searches, views and actions such as calls and direction requests, and Search Console shows organic clicks by page.

## FAQ

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a fitness studio?

Google describes its Business Profile as free and uses the headline "Stand out on Google with a free Business Profile" on its setup page (primary). Setup carries no listing fee. Ads are a separate cost.

### Do posts appear right after I publish them?

Not always. Google reviews each post, and it appears publicly on Search and Maps only when its status is Live. Pending and Not approved posts do not show (primary).

### How many photos should the profile have?

One guide recommends at least 15 authentic photos of the facility, classes in action and staff (secondary). Use real images of your studio rather than stock photos.

### Do reviews show in Maps?

Yes. Google says reviews show next to your Business Profile in Maps and Search (primary).

### Can I link several studio locations to Google Ads?

If the standard link does not work, Google Ads lets you select up to 10 Google Maps locations, and they must be your own (primary).
