To rank on Google Maps, a pet groomer needs a complete, accurate Google Business Profile, reviews, and a website that repeats the same details. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. You can influence relevance and prominence. You cannot change distance.

This guide gives a six-step plan for a salon or mobile groomer. All facts come from the sources at the end, and examples marked as assumptions are illustrations.

## How Does Google Rank Local Results?

Google names three factors. Relevance is how well your profile matches a search. Distance is how far you are from the searcher, or from the location Google estimates for them. Prominence is how well known you are, and Google says it is based on information such as how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have.

Google does not disclose how the factors are weighted. It adds that businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to appear in local results.

The market is large. The American Pet Products Association reports 95 million US households own a pet (2025 survey) and $14.3 billion of 2024 spending in an "Other services" group. That group covers grooming, boarding, training, insurance, sitting and walking together, so APPA does not publish a grooming-only figure.

## 1. How Do You Set Up the Google Business Profile?

Fill in every field: categories, hours, phone, website, services and photos. Use your real-world business name, the one on your storefront and stationery. Google says not to add hours, locations, service descriptions or taglines to the name, and that doing so can lead to suspension.

Our [Google Business Profile guide for pet groomers](/blog/google-business-profile-for-pet-groomers) goes through each field, and the [optimization checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) gives a general list.

## 2. What If You Are a Mobile Groomer?

Google's guidelines say a business that serves customers at their location should have one profile for its central location with a defined service area, and should hide its address from customers. A groomer who works from a van parked at home falls under that rule.

A virtual office, a rented mailing address you do not operate from, is not eligible for a profile.

Choose the service area you can actually reach. Google does not publish a maximum radius in the guidelines we read, so check the current limits in the Business Profile editor.

## 3. How Do You Keep Name, Address and Phone Consistent?

List the exact name, address and phone number once, then copy them to your website, social profiles and directories. BrightLocal says wrong or missing digits are the real danger, while "St" versus "Street" is treated as the same by Google. Our [NAP consistency guide](/blog/nap-consistency-local-seo) has a five-step audit.

## 4. How Do You Get and Handle Reviews?

Reviews feed prominence, and Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. Ask clients with a Google link or QR code at pickup, since customers need a Google Account to review.

Do not offer discounts or gifts for reviews. Google lists incentives as fake and misleading content and prohibits them. Reply to each review briefly and honestly, apologise where appropriate, and never share a reviewer's private details. Our [Google Business Profile reviews guide](/blog/google-business-profile-reviews) has more.

## 5. What Should Your Website Say?

Repeat your services and town in plain text, and add LocalBusiness structured data. Google requires the name and address and recommends telephone, opening hours, URL and geo coordinates, using the most specific business type.

Google indexes the mobile version, so check your phone layout holds the same content as desktop. Our [mobile guide](/blog/mobile-optimization-local-business) covers the checks.

## 6. How Do You Measure Local SEO?

Business Profile reports calls (clicks on the call button), directions, website clicks and bookings, and the searches people used to find you. The searches metric updates at the start of each month, and data can take up to 5 days to appear. Bookings show only if a booking provider manages them.

Record the numbers monthly in a sheet and compare month to month. Seasonality for grooming is not published as a benchmark, so judge against your own history.

## The Math: What Is One Extra Map Position Worth? A Worked Example

All inputs are **assumptions**, not benchmarks.

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly profile calls | 20 |
| Calls that become a booked groom | 40% |
| Average groom price | $70 |
| Repeat visits per client in a year | 4 |

Twenty calls at 40% is 8 new clients a month. At $70 and four visits each, one month of new clients is worth 8 x $70 x 4 = $2,240 over a year. If better visibility lifts calls from 20 to 26, that adds 6 x 40% = 2.4 clients, or about $672 a year per month of gain.

Real retention and prices vary, so use your own numbers.

## FAQ

### Can I pay Google to rank higher in the Map pack?
Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Paid ads are separate, and our [pet groomer Google Ads cost guide](/blog/how-much-should-a-pet-groomer-spend-on-google-ads) covers them.

### Do I need a physical shop?
No. A service-area business can have a profile with its address hidden. A virtual office is not eligible.

### How many reviews do I need?
Google does not publish a number. It says more reviews and positive ratings can help.

### Does NAP consistency matter?
Google says complete and accurate information helps. BrightLocal cites an industry survey placing citations in the top five factors.

### How long does it take to rank?
Neither Google nor APPA publishes a timeline. Track your own profile metrics monthly.
