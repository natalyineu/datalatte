In a 2017 interview, Anytime Fitness's national media director said the chain adds about 7% to 8% of its annual memberships in an average month, and 13% in January. Life Time's CEO described a bump closer to 50%. Those figures are old and come from gyms, so treat them as a sign of the shape, not a forecast for your cafe or salon. The shape is the point: one month carries far more new customers than the others, and what you do in the next 6 weeks decides whether they become regulars or a one-time spike.

This guide is for local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. It covers a calendar built on real 2027 dates, an offer you can afford, the consent rules for email and text, and the math of what a January customer is worth. It does not repeat the gym-specific retention advice in January surge, February drop, and the general holiday planning lives in our [holiday marketing strategy](/blog/holiday-marketing-strategy-local-business).

You are not fighting bad customers; you are fighting a weak first month. The rest of this guide is about making that first month good.

## Your Calendar Is Not Everyone's Calendar

"New Year" lands differently by country, and the dates that matter for bookings are not January 1.

- **England:** Maintained schools in Nottinghamshire, for example, reopen Monday 4 January 2027, with February half term from 15 to 19 February. Academies set their own dates, so check the schools your customers use ([Nottinghamshire County Council](https://www.nottinghamshire.gov.uk/education/school-holidays-and-closures/school-holiday-and-term-dates)).
- **Ontario, Canada:** The Upper Canada District School Board's winter break runs from 21 December 2026 to 1 January 2027, so the first school day back is probably Monday 4 January (the board page does not state it, so confirm with yours; [UCDSB calendar](https://www.ucdsb.on.ca/for_families/school_year_calendar/approved_2026-2027_school_year_calendar)).
- **New South Wales, Australia:** January is mid-summer holidays. Eastern Division students return on Wednesday 3 February 2027 and Western Division students on 10 February ([NSW Education](https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/calendars/2027)). An Australian business running a "back to routine" message on 4 January is talking to families still on the beach. Time the push for late January.
- **US:** School calendars are set by district, so check the ones near you before fixing dates. We did not verify a single national date.

Build the plan backwards from your own customers' return-to-routine date, not from the 1st. Our [back-to-school plan](/blog/back-to-school-marketing-ideas) uses the same approach for September.

## The Six-Week Plan

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Early December | Set your offer, capacity and a spending cap. Set special hours on your Google Business Profile. |
| Mid December | Tell your list what is coming and offer regulars first access. |
| Last week of December | Open January bookings. Sell gift cards if you take them. |
| Return-to-routine week | Main push by email, text, profile post and ads. |
| Two weeks later | Reminder to people who opened but did not book. |
| Weeks 4 to 6 | Welcome and rebooking sequence for every new customer. |

Google's help page says to use special hours to change your hours for holidays or temporary periods; the steps are in a linked article. Do this in early December so customers who search "open now" on 2 January see the right answer ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617)). Post the offer with a [Business Profile post](/blog/google-business-profile-posts).

## Design an Offer You Can Afford

Pick one offer with one action and one end date.

- **Cafes:** a card reset or "new year, new order" stamp bonus. See [loyalty without an app](/blog/coffee-shop-loyalty-program-ideas-without-an-app).
- **Salons and groomers:** a booking-window offer (for example, the first 20 slots in the second half of January) rather than a percentage off everything.
- **Studios and gyms:** a short trial or intro week. Check [Google Ads for gyms](/blog/google-ads-for-gyms) for the cost-per-member math.

Rule of thumb: spend the discount on getting someone through the door once, not on the whole relationship. A price cut that cannot be repeated teaches people to wait for the next sale.

## Email and Text: What You May Send

Your list is the cheapest audience, but it comes with rules.

- **UK:** The ICO's PECR guidance says you generally need consent to send marketing emails or texts. The "soft opt-in" covers existing customers who bought a similar product from you, were given an opt-out when you collected their details and get one in every message. It does not cover prospects or bought-in lists. The ICO notes this guidance is under review because of the Data (Use and Access) Act ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/)).
- **US texts:** Under 47 CFR 64.1200, telemarketing texts to wireless numbers need the recipient's prior express written consent, which must clearly authorise messages, list the number, and say signing is not a condition of purchase. A reply such as STOP must be honoured within ten business days ([Cornell LII: 47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200)). Not legal advice; check with a professional before a first text campaign.
- **Australia and Canada:** each country has its own anti-spam law; we did not verify their details here, so read your regulator's guidance before sending.

For templates and timing, see [SMS marketing best practices](/blog/sms-marketing-best-practices) and email marketing for small business.

## The Math

A worked example for a local service business that sells a $60 first visit. Every number below is an **assumption** for illustration, not a benchmark. Swap in yours.

- Ad spend for the campaign: $1,000, producing 40 new customers.
- New-customer discount: $20 off the first visit.
- Profit per visit before any discount: $30 (assumes a 50% margin on a $60 ticket).
- Returning customers come back for extra visits in the next six months.

| | Tough | Base | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| New customers (assumption) | 40 | 40 | 40 |
| Ad spend (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Profit from first visits at $10 each | $400 | $400 | $400 |
| First-visit result after ad spend | -$600 | -$600 | -$600 |
| Share who return (assumption) | 30% | 50% | 65% |
| Extra visits each (assumption) | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Profit from repeat visits at $30 | $720 | $1,800 | $3,120 |
| Six-month result | +$120 | +$1,200 | +$2,520 |

How to read it: every scenario loses $600 on the first visit, so the campaign is a bet on the second visit. At 3 extra visits per returner, you break even when about 17% of new customers come back ($600 divided by 40 customers x 3 visits x $30). Doubling the return rate does more than any improvement to the ad.

The Planet Fitness cohort numbers are a billing-membership measure and are not comparable to appointment-based rebooking, so we did not use them as inputs. Measure your own: count how many of your January customers book again within 60 days.

## Do This Today

1. Choose your return-to-routine date from your customers' school and work calendars, then write it at the top of a one-page plan.
2. Set special hours on your Google Business Profile for the holiday period.
3. Work out your profit per visit and the break-even return rate using the table above.
4. Write one offer with a single action and end date, and one rebooking script for the till or front desk.
5. Check consent: confirm every address or number you will message has permission under the rules for your country.

## FAQ

**Q: When should I start New Year marketing?**
A: Plan in early December and open bookings before Christmas week, with the main push when your customers are back in routine. For Australian businesses that is usually late January or early February.

**Q: Should I discount for New Year?**
A: Only enough to get someone through the door once. Use the break-even return rate above to see how much discount your repeat business can pay back.

**Q: Do I need a separate New Year landing page?**
A: Yes if you run ads, because one offer and one button convert better than a home page. Our [landing page guide](/blog/landing-page-best-practices) lists what to include.

**Q: How do I keep January customers?**
A: Rebook at the first visit, send a short welcome sequence (email automation sequences), and check in personally after two to three weeks.
