A small business in Prince Edward Island markets to two audiences at once: residents who buy all year and visitors who arrive in waves. The island had 5,246 small employer businesses at December 2024 (federal ISED data) and welcomed 1.87 million non-resident visitors in 2025 (Tourism PEI). The practical plan is a complete Google Business Profile, reviews, a small local ad budget and a seasonal calendar.

This guide uses published provincial and federal figures. No public source gives PEI-specific ad costs, so cost numbers below come from US benchmarks and are labelled. Worked examples are assumptions.

## How Big Is the PEI Small Business Market?

ISED's Key Small Business Statistics 2025 counts 5,339 employer businesses on PEI at December 2024: 5,246 small (1 to 99 employees), 79 medium and 14 large. Small firms are 98.3% of the total. PEI has 36.2 businesses per 1,000 people aged 18 and over, and 48.9 thousand private-sector jobs.

The count covers employer businesses only. Solo operators without staff are not in it, so the real number of local competitors is larger. Check your own search results to see how many rivals you face in your town.

## How Much Do Visitors Matter?

Tourism PEI reported 1.87 million non-resident visitors in 2025, up 10% on 2024, with visitor spending up 12.4% per Destination Canada's initial estimates against 4.4% nationally. The release is dated March 9, 2026.

The practical point for cafés, shops and personal-service businesses: visitor demand is large relative to the resident base, and it is not evenly spread through the year. Tourism PEI's own home page promoted fall festivals in October 2026, such as the Charlottetown Scarecrow Festival and the Summerside Pumpkin Festival, so events create demand outside summer.

## Which Channels Should a PEI Business Use First?

Start with free and cheap channels, then add paid ads once the basics work.

- Google Business Profile: verified, correct hours, photos, services and a booking link
- Reviews: ask every customer and answer all of them
- Google Search ads: a small radius campaign around your location
- Meta ads: for events, offers and seasonal pushes
- Email or SMS list: for repeat customers and locals

Google's help says reviews must reflect a genuine experience and that offering rewards for reviews is strictly prohibited. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers the setup, and the [Canada local marketing guide](/blog/local-marketing-canada-small-business-2026) shows the national picture.

## Do Reviews Matter for Visitors?

Yes, if US survey data is a guide. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read local business reviews, 74% only consider reviews from the last three months, and 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. These are US consumer figures, not PEI, but visitors use the same Google search.

A visitor who has never been to the island has only your profile and reviews to judge you. Ask for reviews year-round, not only in peak weeks, so the newest ones are always recent.

## How Do You Plan the Year?

Build a calendar around three questions: when do locals buy, when do visitors arrive, and which events can you tie into. Festival dates are published on the Tourism PEI site, so check them each season.

Plan offers for each window, update hours before each, and check them on Google. See also our guides for neighbouring provinces: [Nova Scotia](/blog/small-business-marketing-nova-scotia-canada) and [Newfoundland](/blog/small-business-marketing-newfoundland-canada).

## FAQ

### How many small businesses are in Prince Edward Island?
ISED counts 5,246 small employer businesses (1 to 99 employees) at December 2024, which is 98.3% of PEI's employer businesses.

### How many tourists visit PEI?
Tourism PEI reported 1.87 million non-resident visitors in 2025, a 10% increase over 2024.

### How much do Google Ads cost on PEI?
No PEI figure is published. US benchmarks average $5.42 per click across industries, and your local cost may differ.

### Should I advertise to tourists or locals?
Depends on your business. Check your own sales mix, then use Business Profile and reviews for both.

### Is paid advertising necessary?
Not at first. A complete profile and recent reviews cost nothing and support every ad you run later.
