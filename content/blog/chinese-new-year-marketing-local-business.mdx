Chinese New Year 2027 falls on Saturday, February 6, with the Lantern Festival closing the holiday on February 20. To market to it, start planning 8 weeks ahead (around December 12), publish your holiday hours early, offer something that fits the occasion (a gift voucher, a pre-holiday booking slot, a reunion-dinner menu) and show customers you understand the holiday rather than borrowing its symbols.

This guide gives a week-by-week plan, the audience numbers that are worth knowing and a worked cost example.

## When Is Chinese New Year 2027 and How Long Does It Last?

The festival follows the lunisolar calendar, so the date moves each year. In 2027 it starts on February 6 and, per [Farmers' Almanac](https://www.farmersalmanac.com/when-chinese-new-year), runs 15 days to the Lantern Festival on February 20. New Year's Eve, the day of the family reunion dinner, is February 5.

Other communities mark the same new year under other names, including Korea's Seollal and Vietnam's Tết. Many English speakers say Lunar New Year. According to Wikipedia, some Chinese speakers prefer the Chinese name, so for local marketing it is safest to use the name your own customers use.

## Who Are You Marketing To?

Pew Research Center reports about 5.5 million people in the US identified as Chinese in 2023 (Census Bureau estimate, alone or in combination), around 22% of the Asian American population. Pew's own analysis counts 5.04 million, of whom 58% are immigrants. About 64% of Chinese people aged 5 and over are English proficient.

The largest numbers are in California (1.8 million), New York (760,000) and Texas (260,000), and the biggest metro areas are New York, Los Angeles and San Francisco. If you are outside those areas, check your own customer base before investing in a campaign.

## What Is Your Marketing Timeline?

Work back from February 6, 2027.

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| About December 12 (8 weeks out) | Decide the offer, the budget and who will check the translations |
| Early January (5 weeks out) | Update your Google Business Profile and website, open pre-bookings |
| January 9 (4 weeks out) | Launch emails, social posts and any paid ads |
| January 23 (2 weeks out) | Send a reminder, push the last available slots |
| February 5 to 6 | Check hours, stock and staffing |
| February 7 to 20 | Follow up with a thank-you and a rebook offer |

## What Promotions Fit the Holiday?

Several traditions give you natural hooks, as described on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_New_Year). Families clean their homes before the new year, and many have haircuts before it begins. The reunion dinner is compared to Thanksgiving or Christmas dinner. Red envelopes with money (hongbao) are given to younger relatives, and amounts containing 8 are favored.

Ideas that follow from these:

1. **Salons and barbers:** pre-holiday appointment slots, booked early.
2. **Restaurants and cafes:** a reunion-dinner set menu or a group booking offer.
3. **Gift shops and florists:** red-themed gift bundles.
4. **Any business:** a gift voucher customers can give, sold in amounts that avoid the number 4.
5. **Fitness and wellness:** a new-year starter pass.

## How Do You Avoid Cultural Mistakes?

The same source notes that the number 4 is considered especially unlucky and that red is central to the festival. Do not price packages at 4 or use 4 items in a set.

Ask a Chinese-speaking customer, staff member or community organization to review any Chinese text before it goes live. Machine translation can miss tone, and Chinese comes in Simplified and Traditional forms. Keep your claims specific and avoid implying the holiday is only for one group.

## How Should You Update Your Google Presence?

Set special hours on your Business Profile before the holiday. Google's [Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en) says to set special hours to change your hours for holidays or other temporary periods. Add a post about your offer and update your photos.

If you advertise in Chinese, Google lists Chinese (Simplified) and Chinese (Traditional) as available [ad language targets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6333734?hl=en). Ads in an unsupported language are disapproved. Our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers the full setup.

## What Does It Cost in Practice? A Worked Example

Everything below is an **assumption**: a salon spends $500 on search ads for the pre-holiday window, an average haircut is $80, and half of leads book.

LocaliQ's [2026 benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/) put the all-industry average cost per lead at $66.69.

| Step | Figure | How it was calculated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad budget | $500 | Assumed |
| Leads | about 7.5 | $500 divided by $66.69 |
| Bookings | about 3.75 | 50% of leads, assumed |
| First-visit revenue | $300 | 3.75 times $80 |
| Break-even value per new customer | about $133 | $66.69 divided by 0.5 |

On the first visit alone, the ads lose money in this example. They only pay back if each new customer is worth at least $133, for example by rebooking twice. This is why a rebooking offer matters more than the ad itself. Use your own cost per lead and booking rate, since a niche local audience can differ from the all-industry average.

## FAQ

**Q: When is Chinese New Year in 2027?**
A: Saturday, February 6, 2027, with the Lantern Festival on February 20. New Year's Eve is February 5.

**Q: How early should I start marketing for it?**
A: Plan about 8 weeks ahead, launch promotions about 4 weeks ahead and send reminders 2 weeks ahead.

**Q: How many Chinese Americans are there?**
A: Pew reports about 5.5 million people identified as Chinese in the US in 2023 per Census estimates, and 5.04 million in its own tabulation.

**Q: Should I advertise in Chinese?**
A: If your customers read it, yes. Google supports both Simplified and Traditional Chinese for ad targeting. Have a fluent speaker review all text.

**Q: Are there numbers I should avoid?**
A: The number 4 is considered especially unlucky, so avoid pricing or packaging in fours.
