CPC means you pay when someone clicks. CPM means you pay for every 1,000 times your ad is shown, whether anyone clicks or not. For a local business with a small budget, the useful question is not which model is "better" but which one gets you a customer for less money. The answer comes down to one number: the click-through rate (CTR) your ad earns.

Those figures come from WordStream by LocaliQ's 2026 search benchmarks, as summarized by [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/what-are-good-google-ads-benchmarks/546995/) (data from April 2025 to March 2026, 20 industries). They describe search ads, not display or video, so use them as a yardstick for CPC campaigns only.

## How each model actually charges you

Google's own help pages describe the split clearly. With [CPC bidding you set a maximum bid for each click and pay only for the clicks you receive](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2630842). With viewable CPM (vCPM) you set a maximum bid per 1,000 viewable impressions, and Google states you do not pay for impressions that were not viewable. vCPM is available on the Google Display Network, not on Search Network campaigns.

Google defines a viewable display impression as one where [50% of the ad is visible for at least 1 second](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7029393) (30% for very large formats of 242,500 pixels or more). So a CPM buy does not guarantee anyone read your ad; it guarantees a chance to see it.

| | CPC | CPM / vCPM |
|---|---|---|
| You pay for | Each click | Each 1,000 impressions |
| Typical placement | Search ads, some display | Display, video, streaming TV |
| Main risk | Clicks that never convert | Impressions nobody acts on |
| Best signal it is working | Cost per lead | Reach and later branded searches |

## The one formula that settles it

Convert any CPM into an effective CPC:

**Effective CPC = CPM divided by (1,000 x CTR)**

A $10 CPM with a 0.2% CTR works out to $5 per click. The same $10 CPM with a 1% CTR is $1 per click. CPM buying is not expensive or cheap on its own; it depends on how many people respond.

## Worked example (assumption, not a benchmark)

Take a salon with $1,000 a month and a landing page that turns 3% of visitors into booking requests. These inputs are assumptions for illustration only.

| | Option A: CPC | Option B: CPM |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $2.00 per click | $10 per 1,000 impressions |
| Impressions | not billed | 100,000 |
| CTR | n/a | 0.2% |
| Clicks | 500 | 200 |
| Bookings at 3% | 15 | 6 |
| Cost per booking | about $67 | about $167 |

Under these assumptions CPC wins by 2.5 times. Flip one input and the result changes: if the CPM ad earned a 0.8% CTR, Option B would deliver 800 clicks and 24 bookings, or about $42 each. Your job is to find out which world you are in, and a short test budget does that cheaply.

## Decision guide

- **Someone is already searching for you** (haircut near me, emergency groomer): use CPC on search. Intent is the product you are paying for.
- **Nobody knows you exist yet** (new location, new class, new menu): CPM formats such as display, video or [streaming TV](/blog/how-much-does-ctv-advertising-cost-real-cpm-data) can build reach, but expect fewer direct conversions and judge them on a longer window.
- **You have a small email or website audience:** retargeting on CPC or CPM both work; compare effective CPC using the formula above.
- **Budget under roughly $500 a month:** start with CPC search. A CPM test at that size buys too few impressions to learn from. Our [budget breakdown for Google Ads](/blog/how-much-does-google-ads-cost-for-small-businesses-real-2026-pricing-breakdown) shows what small budgets realistically buy.

## Mistakes that distort the comparison

1. **Comparing a CPM campaign's clicks to a CPC campaign's leads.** Measure both on cost per booking or cost per call.
2. **Ignoring viewability.** On display, you can request vCPM so unviewable impressions are not billed, per Google's help page above.
3. **Judging brand campaigns in a week.** Reach campaigns need a longer window and a way to see lift, such as branded search volume or "how did you hear about us" answers.
4. **Skipping quality work on CPC.** Higher relevance lowers what you pay per click; see the [Quality Score guide](/blog/google-ads-quality-score-guide).
5. **Running both without tracking.** Without conversion tracking neither model can be judged. For channel context, see the [programmatic cost benchmarks](/blog/how-much-does-programmatic-advertising-cost-real-cpm-benchmarks-2026).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is CPM or CPC cheaper?**

Neither is cheaper by default. Convert CPM to effective CPC with the formula above, using your own CTR.

**Is CPC better for small businesses?**

For search ads aimed at people ready to buy, usually yes, because you pay only when someone shows interest. Reach-focused goals can justify CPM.

**What CPC should a local business expect?**

The 2026 cross-industry search average is $5.42 per click, but it varies widely by industry, so check your own account data after a few weeks.

**Can I use both at the same time?**

Yes, but give each its own budget and conversion goal so results do not blur together.
