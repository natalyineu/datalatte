## How much does website hosting cost for a small business in 2026?

Prices below come from each vendor's own pricing page, checked on 2026-10-11. Intro prices require a long prepaid term and rise at renewal.

| Option | Intro price per month | Renewal price per month | Term for intro price | Notable limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Hostinger Premium](https://www.hostinger.com/web-hosting) | $2.99 | $10.99 | 48 months | 3 sites, 20 GB, weekly backups |
| [Hostinger Unlimited](https://www.hostinger.com/web-hosting) | $3.99 | $16.99 | 48 months | Unlimited sites, 50 GB, daily backups |
| [Bluehost Starter](https://www.bluehost.com/hosting/shared) | $3.99 | $9.99 | 36 months | 10 sites, 10 GB |
| [Bluehost Business](https://www.bluehost.com/hosting/shared) | $6.99 | $13.99 | 36 months | 50 sites, 50 GB |
| [Squarespace Basic](https://www.squarespace.com/pricing) | $19 (annual billing) | $25 if billed monthly | 12 months | Hosting and SSL included |

Both Hostinger and Bluehost list a 30-day money-back period. Hostinger's page did not state an uptime guarantee, while Bluehost lists a 99.99% SLA.

## What does a 3-year hosting bill really look like?

Assumption for this example: a salon with a 5-page site that needs one site, a free first-year domain and no e-commerce. Totals use the plan prices above and ignore taxes and domain renewal.

- **Bluehost Starter, 36 months prepaid:** $3.99 x 36 = $143.64. Year four at renewal: $9.99 x 12 = $119.88.
- **Hostinger Premium, 48 months prepaid:** $143.52 for four years. Year five at renewal: $10.99 x 12 = $131.88.
- **Squarespace Basic, annual billing:** $19 x 36 = $684 over three years.

On price alone, shared hosting is about a fifth of a builder's cost. The gap pays for your time: with shared hosting you install and update a site (for example WordPress) yourself or pay someone to. If you would rather not, read [DIY website vs hiring a developer](/blog/diy-website-vs-professional-developer) before choosing.

## Do I need 99.9% or 99.99% uptime?

The arithmetic is simple. A year has 8,760 hours.

- 99.9% uptime allows about 8.76 hours of downtime a year.
- 99.99% uptime allows about 52.6 minutes a year.

Worked example (assumption): your site brings in $4,000 a month in bookings, or $48,000 a year. Spread evenly, that is about $5.48 per hour. Eight hours of downtime then costs roughly $48. Downtime hurts more if it lands on a peak day or during a paid ad campaign, but for a typical local site the SLA tier matters far less than backups and support.

A guarantee is also not the same as measured uptime. Vendor SLAs usually pay out as hosting credit, not lost sales, so treat them as a tiebreaker only.

## Does hosting speed affect bookings?

Speed affects conversions, but hosting is only one input next to image size and plugins. A [Deloitte and Google study](https://www.deloitte.com/ie/en/services/consulting/research/milliseconds-make-millions.html) of mobile sites over four weeks in Europe and the US linked a 0.1 second speed improvement to 8.4% more conversions for retail brands and 10.1% for travel brands. Those are correlations from large brands, not a promise for a three-chair salon.

Google's [Core Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) sets the targets worth checking: Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint of 200 milliseconds or less, and Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less, judged at the 75th percentile. Test your own site free in PageSpeed Insights before blaming the host. Our guide to [website speed optimization](/blog/website-speed-optimization-small-business) covers the usual fixes.

## Which option fits which business?

- **Choose a builder (Squarespace)** if you want booking pages and a gallery, no updates to manage, and can accept $228 a year or more.
- **Choose shared hosting** if you already run WordPress or have a freelancer, and one site is all you need. Pick a plan with daily backups, since Hostinger's cheapest tier lists weekly backups only.
- **Choose a higher tier** only when you run several sites or an online shop. Bluehost's e-commerce plan lists $14.99 a month intro and $21.99 at renewal.
- **Hire help** if the site drives paid traffic. Our [website service](/services/website) is built for local businesses.

Whatever you pick, set up [Google Analytics 4](/blog/google-analytics-4-for-small-business) so you can see whether speed or downtime is actually costing visits.

## FAQ

### Is cheap shared hosting good enough for a local business?

Usually yes for a small brochure or booking site. The risks are renewal jumps (Hostinger Unlimited goes from $3.99 to $16.99 a month) and limited backups on entry plans. Check the renewal price before you pay for 36 or 48 months.

### Should I pay for several years upfront?

The intro prices above require 36 to 48 month terms. Both vendors list a 30-day money-back window, so test speed and support in that window. After it, you are committed, and renewal prices are what you will pay next.

### Do I need a free domain deal?

Both hosts include a free domain for the first year, but renewal is billed at the normal rate. Register the domain in your own account so you can move hosts later.

### Is a website builder better than hosting?

A builder trades flexibility for convenience. Squarespace includes hosting, SSL and a free first-year domain on annual plans, with prices from $19 to $99 a month billed annually.

### How do I know if my host is the problem?

Run PageSpeed Insights and compare against the 2.5 second LCP target. If the server response is slow even with small images, hosting is a likely cause. If images are large, fix those first.
