The best Google Ads keywords for a pet groomer are local, service-specific phrases that show someone is ready to book, such as "dog grooming near me", "[city] dog groomer" and "mobile dog grooming [city]".

Start with those, set the match types carefully, and block the searches that never book, such as "dog grooming jobs" or "how to groom a dog at home". This guide gives you the keyword groups, the exclusions, and a budget example.

Search volumes change by city, so check your own numbers in Google's Keyword Planner rather than trusting a generic list.

## Which Keywords Should a Pet Groomer Bid On?

Group keywords by how close the searcher is to booking. Put each group in its own ad group so the ad copy can match the search.

| Group | Example keywords | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready to book | dog groomer near me, [city] dog grooming, cat grooming [city] | Location plus service means a nearby buyer |
| Specific service | mobile dog grooming, de-shedding treatment, puppy first groom, nail trim near me | Searcher knows what they want |
| Problem or timing | dog groomer open saturday, same day dog grooming, groomer for matted dog | Urgency, higher intent |

Keep a fourth group, broad research terms like "dog grooming tips", out of your paid plan. They bring clicks, not appointments.

The category benchmark above is the average for Animals & Pets, not groomers specifically. The LocaliQ page has no pet grooming row, so use it as a rough guide. Your real cost per click will depend on your city, and Keyword Planner shows the range for your area.

## What Do Pet Owners Spend On Services?

Demand is large, which is why competitors bid. The American Pet Products Association reports [$158 billion in total US pet spending in 2024](https://www.americanpetproducts.org/industry-trends-and-stats), of which $14.3 billion went to "Other Services" (boarding, grooming, insurance, training, sitting and walking combined). The same page reports that 95 million US households own a pet. Grooming is not broken out separately, so treat these as market-size context only.

The takeaway: you are bidding against other local groomers, mobile groomers and chains, so specific wording matters more than volume.

## Which Match Types Should You Use?

Google describes three main match types in its [keyword match type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2497836):

- **Exact match** (square brackets) shows ads on searches with the same meaning or intent. Most control, fewest searches.
- **Phrase match** (quotation marks) shows ads on searches that include the meaning of your keyword. Reaches more searches than exact, fewer than broad.
- **Broad match** is the default and the most comprehensive. Google states that it is critical to use Smart Bidding with broad match.

For a small groomer with a small budget, a practical order is: start with exact and phrase on your core terms, watch the search terms report for a few weeks, and try broad only with conversion tracking and Smart Bidding in place. That ordering is a cautious approach, not a Google rule.

## What Negative Keywords Should Pet Groomers Add?

Negative keywords stop ads showing on irrelevant searches. Google notes that they [let you exclude search terms](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972) and that they do not match close variants, so add plurals and synonyms yourself.

A starter list for groomers:

- jobs, career, hiring, salary
- school, course, training, certification
- diy, at home, how to, tutorial
- supplies, clippers, shampoo, brush (unless you sell them)
- free, cheap (optional, depends on your pricing)
- vet, veterinarian, emergency (unless you offer those)

Then open the [search terms report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708) every week for the first month. Google advises that if a search term isn't relevant to what you offer, you should add it as a negative keyword. Each irrelevant term you block frees budget for buyers.

## How Do You Target Only Nearby Pet Owners?

Use a radius around your shop or your service-area postcodes, and mention the area in ad text. Google's [location options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038) include "Presence or interest" (the default and recommended) and "Presence". The default also reaches people who show interest in your area, such as someone searching for a groomer in a town they are moving to. If you want only people physically in your area, choose Presence.

Mobile groomers should draw their service area honestly. A van that cannot reach a postcode should not pay for clicks from it. Pair ads with a complete Google listing; our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) covers it.

## What Does a Starter Budget Look Like?

The numbers below are **assumptions** for illustration. Replace them with your own.

| Input (assumption) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly ad budget | $450 |
| Cost per click (near the $4.06 benchmark) | $4.00 |
| Clicks | about 112 |
| Click-to-booking rate (benchmark conversion is 16.22%, so use a cautious half) | 8% |
| New bookings | about 9 |
| Revenue per first-year client (4 visits at $75) | $300 |
| Margin after groomer pay and supplies | 50% |

Nine clients at $150 profit each is $1,350 against $450 of spend, a 3x return on paper. Break-even is $450 / $150 = 3 clients, which is 3 of 112 clicks, or about 2.7%. If your real booking rate sits below that, pause and fix the landing page, phone answering or offer before adding budget.

Also remember that only the first-year value counts here, so repeat grooming can raise the return later.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the best keyword for a pet groomer?**
A: A local, service-specific phrase like "dog grooming near me" or "[city] dog groomer". Search volume differs by city, so check Keyword Planner for your area.

**Q: Should I use broad match?**
A: Only with conversion tracking and Smart Bidding. Google says Smart Bidding is critical with broad match. Many small accounts start on phrase and exact.

**Q: How much does a click cost?**
A: LocaliQ's 2026 benchmark for Animals & Pets is $4.06 per click and $31.50 per lead. Pet grooming is not listed separately.

**Q: Do I need negative keywords?**
A: Yes. They block searches like jobs, courses and DIY, which almost never book.

**Q: Where do I start if I am new to ads?**
A: Read about [Google Ads management for local businesses](/services/google-ads) or the [pet groomer marketing overview](/for/pet-groomers), then launch one campaign with 10 to 15 keywords.
