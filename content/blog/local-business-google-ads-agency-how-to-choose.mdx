Most local business owners who've hired a Google Ads agency have a story. Either the agency ran ads for 6 months with "great impressions but no leads," or they disappeared after the contract was signed, or they charged management fees larger than the actual ad spend.

The problem isn't that Google Ads agencies are bad — it's that local businesses and agencies often have misaligned expectations, and the local business owner doesn't know enough about Google Ads to catch the problems early.

This guide gives you the questions to ask, the red flags to watch for, and what good Google Ads management looks like for a local business.

---

## What "Managing" Google Ads Actually Means

Many business owners assume that once an agency sets up their campaigns, they just run. In reality, Google Ads requires ongoing work to perform:

- **Weekly:** Review search terms, add new negatives, check budget pacing
- **Bi-weekly:** Adjust bids based on what's converting
- **Monthly:** Test new ad copy, review quality scores, analyse conversion data
- **Quarterly:** Restructure campaigns based on seasonal trends, competitive changes

An agency that "set it and forget it" is billing you for nothing. Good Google Ads management is an active, ongoing process.

## Red Flags: Walk Away If You See These

**1. They can't explain what they're doing in plain language**
If an agency can't tell you in simple terms what campaigns they're running, what keywords they're targeting, and why — that's a problem. You don't need to understand every detail, but you should understand the strategy.

**2. They don't ask about your business before proposing**
A good agency asks about your services, your average customer value, your target geography, and how you track leads. An agency that proposes a "package" before understanding your business is selling a product, not a service.

**3. No conversion tracking**
If they're not tracking phone calls, form submissions, or bookings from your ads, they have no idea what's working. All they can report is clicks — which means nothing without knowing how many became customers.

**4. They own your ad account**
Your Google Ads account should be in your name, with your payment method. Some agencies create accounts under their own management and don't give you access. If you leave, you lose all your data and conversion history.

**5. They promise specific results**
"We guarantee 50 leads in the first month" is a red flag. Google Ads results depend on your industry, market, competition, landing page, and product. Ethical agencies give realistic ranges, not guarantees.

**6. Management fee exceeds ad spend**
If you're spending $500/month on ads and paying $600/month in management fees, the math rarely works. For a small local business, management fees should be 15–30% of ad spend, or a flat fee that makes sense at your scale.

<Callout type="warning">
Any agency that doesn't give you direct admin access to your own Google Ads account should be considered carefully. Your data, your conversion history, and your ad account are valuable assets — they should be yours.
</Callout>

## The Right Questions to Ask Before Hiring

**About their experience:**
- Do you work with other businesses in my industry/location?
- Can you share examples of results from similar local businesses?
- Who will actually manage my account day-to-day?

**About strategy:**
- What keywords would you target for my business in the first month?
- How will you track leads and bookings from the ads?
- How do you decide when to increase or decrease bids?

**About reporting:**
- What will my reports look like, and how often will I receive them?
- What metrics will you focus on, and why?
- Will I have direct access to my Google Ads account?

**About contracts and fees:**
- What's your management fee structure?
- What's the minimum contract term?
- What happens to my ad account if we stop working together?

## What Good Local Google Ads Management Looks Like

A well-managed Google Ads account for a local business delivers:

**Month 1:** Campaign setup with proper structure, conversion tracking installed, initial keyword lists with negatives, first A/B tests running

**Month 2–3:** Optimisation based on real data — pausing what doesn't work, scaling what does, improving landing page if needed

**Month 4+:** Stable, improving performance with clear data on cost per lead and ROAS

Reports should show:
- Clicks, impressions, CTR (surface metrics)
- Conversion volume and cost per conversion (what matters)
- Top performing keywords and ad copy
- Search terms report summary (what people actually searched)

## DIY vs. Agency: When Does It Make Sense to Hire?

**DIY makes sense if:**
- Your budget is under $500/month (management fees eat too much of the margin)
- You have time to learn and manage it (expect 3–5 hours/month once set up)
- You're in a low-competition niche where basic targeting is enough

**Hire an agency if:**
- Your budget is $1,000+/month (professional management pays for itself)
- Google Ads is a primary growth channel, not a side experiment
- You've tried DIY and are seeing poor results you can't diagnose
- Your industry is highly competitive and requires sophisticated bid management

**Hire a specialist vs. a generalist:**
A generalist marketing agency that "also does Google Ads" is not the same as a specialist who lives in Google Ads daily. For local businesses, the difference in results is often significant. Look for someone who primarily works with businesses like yours.

## Pricing: What Local Google Ads Management Actually Costs

Management fee structures vary:

**Flat monthly fee:** $300–800/month for single-location local businesses
**Percentage of spend:** 15–25% of monthly ad spend, typically with a minimum
**Performance fee:** Some specialists charge a flat fee plus a per-lead bonus (aligns incentives)

Avoid: agencies that charge setup fees AND high monthly fees AND a percentage. You're paying three times for one service.

For a coffee shop, salon, or fitness studio spending $500–1,500/month on ads, expect to pay $200–500/month in management fees from a qualified specialist.
