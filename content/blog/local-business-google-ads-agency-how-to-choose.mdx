Here is a number to hold onto. If you spend $1,000 a month on Google Ads and the agency charges a $600 flat fee, your ads need to produce 2 or 3 extra customers a month, beyond what you would get on your own, just to cover that fee (assuming each customer is worth $250 in gross profit; the math is below). Many owners never do that sum before signing. Then they find out six months later that the agency delivered "impressions" and the account is locked in someone else's login.

This guide is for owners of local businesses in the US, UK, Australia and Canada: coffee shops, salons, studios, groomers and the like. It covers what agencies and freelancers are reported to charge, the one account-ownership check that protects you, ten questions that separate real operators from salespeople, the red flags, and when you should not hire anyone at all. Prices are in US dollars, because that is how the published guides quote them.

## What Agencies Reportedly Charge (and Why the Numbers Disagree)

There is no independent survey of Google Ads management fees, so be wary of anyone who quotes you "the market rate". The published guides are agency blogs. Two that were opened:

- [Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/the-price-is-right-navigating-google-ads-agency-fees/) lists freelancers and solo consultants at $500 to $3,000 a month, percent-of-spend fees of 10% to 20% (10% to 25% in another section), and a hybrid of a base fee plus 5% to 10% of spend. For spend under $2,500 a month it estimates a total fee of $300 to $1,000. It says most reputable agencies will not take local accounts under about $2,000 to $3,000 a month in spend. It names no pricing survey, and its own tables overlap in places.
- [groas](https://groas.com/post/google-ads-agency-pricing-2026-costs-hidden-fees-what-to-pay.md) gives similar percentages, notes some agencies charge up to 25% on smaller accounts, and lists setup fees of $1,000 to $5,000, contracts of 3, 6 or 12 months, and early-termination charges from one month's fee up to the whole remaining contract. groas sells its own automated Google Ads management product, so weigh its framing accordingly, and it also admits its figures are estimates rather than sourced market data.

The common thread across both: fees are negotiable, minimums are real, and setup fees plus long contracts are where small accounts get hurt. A rule of thumb that appears in older versions of articles like this one, that "healthy" fees are 15% to 30% of spend, is not supported by either source above, so do not anchor on it.

If your ad budget is small, the percentage model gives you a tiny fee that an agency may refuse to work for. That is why many small accounts end up with a freelancer, a flat-fee specialist or doing it themselves. ## The Ownership Check That Protects You

Before you sign anything, ask: "Whose Google Ads account will my campaigns live in, and will I be an administrator on it?"

Google's help page on [manager account access levels](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9977851) explains the mechanics. Agencies normally manage your campaigns through a manager account (an MCC) that links to your client account. Google lists five access levels (Administrative, Standard, Read only, Email only, Billing), and says linking a manager account does not by itself make it an administrative owner of your client account. Its [overview of the same topic](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7476552) says some tasks may require logging in directly to the client account with administrator credentials.

What that means for you:

- The cleanest setup: the account is created under your own Google login and payment method, and the agency is linked as a manager. If you part ways, you unlink them and keep the history.
- The risky setup: the agency creates the account inside their own system, pays Google on your behalf and bills you. You may never see the data, and leaving can mean starting from zero.
- Ask for administrator access to your own account on day one, and log in yourself in the first week to check it works.

<Callout type="warning" title="Make it a contract line">
Put it in writing: you own the Google Ads account, the conversion tracking and the data, and the agency must remove its own access within a set number of days of ending the contract. Walk away from any agency that will not agree to this.
</Callout>

## Ten Questions to Ask Before You Hire

1. **Whose account is it, and will I have administrator access?** (See above.)
2. **Who actually works on my account each week?** A named person, not "the team".
3. **What will you track?** The right answer includes phone calls, form submissions and bookings, set up before the ads go live. Our [conversion tracking setup guide](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) lists what to expect, and [call tracking for local business](/blog/call-tracking-for-local-business) covers calls.
4. **What will my first 30 days look like?** Expect tracking, structure, negatives and ad copy, not "optimisation" with no data.
5. **What do you do on a typical week?** Look for search terms review and negative keywords. See [negative keywords guide](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-stop-wasting-budget).
6. **Show me a report.** A real one, with cost per lead and calls, not a page of impressions.
7. **What does it cost in total?** Management fee, setup fee, landing pages, call tracking, minimum term and cancellation terms, all in one number for year one.
8. **Do you work with businesses like mine?** Ask for references you can phone, not logos.
9. **What happens if results are poor after 90 days?** There should be a way out without paying the rest of the contract.
10. **What do you need from me?** A good answer mentions your pricing, your best customers and answering the phone. Ads cannot fix a business that misses calls.

## Red Flags

- **Guaranteed results.** "50 leads in month one" ignores your market, landing page and phone handling. Google does not promise results either.
- **A package before a conversation.** If nobody asks about your average sale, service area or how you currently get customers, the proposal is a product, not a plan.
- **No conversion tracking.** If all they can report is clicks, they cannot tell you what is working.
- **Vague scope.** groas points out that retainers can cover only a few hours of work, with extra charges beyond that. Ask how many hours a month are included.
- **They hold the keys.** Refusal to give you administrator access.
- **Long lock-in plus a setup fee.** You are paying twice for the same thing.
- **A badge as the whole pitch.** A Google Partner badge shows an agency has met Google's criteria, which third-party posts describe differently and which have changed over time. It does not tell you whether they understand a one-location coffee shop. Treat it as a minor signal.

## What Good Looks Like

In the first month you should see tracking installed and tested, a campaign structure with tight ad groups, negatives, ads that mirror search terms, and a landing page plan. After that, you should get a regular report showing spend, conversions, cost per conversion, the search terms that triggered ads and what changed this month. If you still do not know your cost per customer after 90 days, something is wrong with tracking, the offer or the follow-up, and a good agency will tell you which.

## The Math

Take a $1,000 monthly ad budget and compare doing it yourself with two fee models. Every figure is an **assumption** for illustration. Your numbers will differ.

| | DIY | Agency, 20% of spend | Agency, $600 flat fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly ad spend (assumption) | $1,000 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Management fee | $0 | $200 | $600 |
| Total monthly cost | $1,000 | $1,200 | $1,600 |
| Customers if agency adds nothing (assumption) | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| Gross profit per customer (assumption) | $250 | $250 | $250 |
| Net if no improvement | +$500 | +$300 | -$100 |
| Customers if agency lifts results by a third (assumption) | n/a | 8 | 8 |
| Net with the lift | n/a | +$800 | +$400 |
| Extra customers needed to cover the fee | n/a | 0.8 | 2.4 |

How to read it: the fee is only worth paying if the agency adds customers worth more than the fee. At $250 of gross profit per customer, the $600 fee needs 2.4 extra customers every month. If you think a specialist cannot get you that, do it yourself or buy a one-off audit. The 33% lift is a hypothetical, not a promise.

One more step: set a stop-loss. Agree a target (for example, "cost per lead under $X by month three") and a clause that lets you leave if it is missed.

## Do This Today

1. Calculate your gross profit per new customer and the most you can pay for one. Write it down before any sales call.
2. Check whether you already have a Google Ads account and who is the administrator. If you do not, create it under your own login first.
3. Shortlist two or three candidates and send each the ten questions above by email, so you can compare written answers.
4. Ask each for a sample report and one phone reference from a similar local business.
5. Add the ownership clause, a 90-day exit and a named account manager to the contract before signing.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should a small local business pay for Google Ads management?**
A: There is no verified market rate. Published agency guides cite 10% to 20% of spend (sometimes up to 25%) or flat fees from a few hundred to several thousand dollars a month, and several reputable agencies set minimum spends. Work out what an extra customer is worth to you and negotiate from that.

**Q: Should the agency own the Google Ads account?**
A: No. Your account should be yours, with the agency linked as a manager. Google says linking a manager account does not automatically make it the administrative owner, so check the ownership and your access level on day one.

**Q: Is a Google Partner badge enough?**
A: No. Third-party descriptions of the criteria differ and have changed over time. Ask about local-business experience, tracking and reporting instead.

**Q: Is it better to hire a freelancer or an agency?**
A: For under about $2,000 a month in spend, many agencies will not take you on at all, according to Linear Design. A specialist freelancer who works with your type of business is often the better fit. Use the same ten questions either way.

**Q: How long before I should see results?**
A: Clicks start the same day. Judge on cost per lead and customers, not clicks, after roughly 60 to 90 days, which is my rule of thumb rather than a Google figure.
