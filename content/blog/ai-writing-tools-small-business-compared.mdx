AI writing tools for small business range from free tiers with a fixed allowance to plans listed at $1,000 per month. For a coffee shop, salon, groomer or studio writing captions, promo emails and service descriptions, the listed plans that fit start around $7.50 to $29 per month. The limits (characters, one-time words, credits, brand voice slots) differ as much as the prices. ## What do small businesses use AI writing tools for?

According to the SBE Council's small business AI report, as retold by [AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/ai-writing-tools-for-small-business-practical-uses-and-limitations) (secondary), 82% of small business employers had invested in AI tools by March 2026. Content creation was the most common use case among them.

The jobs that fit a local business are short and repeatable: social captions, promotional email drafts, service or menu descriptions, and Google Business Profile posts. Long, research-heavy pieces are a different category. A [ChatGPT workflow for local marketing](/blog/how-to-use-chatgpt-for-local-business-marketing) covers prompts for these short formats, and the [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) shows where posts and descriptions are used.

None of the pages cited in this article publishes an accuracy or error rate for AI-written copy. That gap shapes the rest of the advice: output is a draft, and the checks and review policy below exist because no vendor figure can be relied on to replace them.

## Why do prices differ between sources?

Several tools show conflicting numbers across pages. Rytr appears at $7.50 and $24.16 per month on annual billing in one article, at $9 to $49 per month in a CostBench roundup, and at $0 entry, $14 median and $29 top in CostBench's benchmark table. Jasper's own page shows both $69 and $59 for Pro, and the page text does not explain the difference.

Copy.ai is the clearest case. The benchmark lists a $650 top tier, while Copy.ai's own page lists Growth at $1,000 per month, Expansion at $2,000 and Scale at $3,000. ContentKing is described as custom-priced with no free tier in one CostBench page, and listed at $39, $179 and $1,279 in another.

CostBench says its prices come from vendor pages and are re-checked. It also says "custom" tiers have no public list price, and negotiated enterprise deals are not shown. It notes such deals typically land 15% to 40% below list, which matters little for a single-location shop.

Its benchmark across 12 tools puts the entry-tier median at $0 and the top-tier median at $59. It also says most tools start at $10 to $20 per user per month for solo use, with team tiers 2 to 3 times higher. Treat the vendor's page on the day of sign-up as the final word.

A roundup's order also reflects its own weights. CostBench's ranking of 6 tools out of 12, last researched 2026-04-06, weights price and ease of use at 5 of 5, features at 4, and scalability and support at 3 each. A shop's priorities may differ.

## Which limits matter more than the monthly price?

Usage caps vary in kind, not just size. Rytr's free plan gives 10,000 characters per month, which the source translates to roughly 1,500 to 2,000 words. Simplified's 5,000 free words are a one-time quota. eesel's 100 free credits do not refill, and the agent pauses when they run out until a paid plan is chosen.

Copy.ai's free Chat tier has no stated word limit at sign-up, but its pricing page, as of May 2026, does not publish specific limits. Its workflow credits work differently: a credit represents computational power, and workflows with more steps and more content cost more credits.

Brand voice is the second big difference.

- Jasper Pro includes 2 Brand Voices, and the Business plan lists unlimited voices.
- Rytr's custom tone training requires the Unlimited plan.
- eesel states a claimed 94% voice match. That is a vendor claim, and no independent test appears in the sources.

Seats and languages matter for teams. Copy.ai Chat includes 5 seats and Jasper Pro includes 1. Rytr's free plan covers one language, while Jasper states content can be created in over 30 languages. A single-owner shop will rarely need more than one seat.

## What should be checked before AI copy is published?

Every draft needs the same short checklist, because the tool does not know the shop's facts.

- Hours, prices, addresses, phone numbers and booking links match the current versions.
- Offers have correct start and end dates, and any holiday cutoff is accurate. This matters most for [holiday email campaigns](/blog/holiday-email-marketing-campaigns).
- Staff names, product names and service names are spelled as customers know them.
- Superlatives and results claims ("best in town", "guaranteed results") are supportable by the business, or removed.
- No invented reviews, testimonials or customer stories appear in the text.
- Local details (neighborhood, parking, seasonal items) are real, not generic filler.
- The tone matches the last three posts or emails that performed well.
- Repeated phrases and stock openers are cut, so posts do not read like every other AI draft.

Email drafts deserve a second pass on subject line, preview text and the link. The [email marketing ideas](/blog/email-marketing-ideas-for-small-businesses-20-campaigns-that-drive-revenue-2026) list offers campaign formats worth drafting, while the facts inside each one still need a human check.

## What human review policy works for a small team?

A policy can be one page. These five rules fit most owner-operated shops.

1. Every piece has a named approver, usually the owner or manager, and nothing goes out on the tool's say-so.
2. A fact sheet with hours, prices, current offers and service names is pasted into each prompt, so the tool is not guessing.
3. Anything with a price, discount, date or health or results claim gets a second set of eyes before it is scheduled.
4. Emails are sent to a test address first and checked on a phone.
5. Customer personal details stay out of prompts until the vendor's data terms have been read. The sources cited here do not state those terms.

Add a monthly habit: pull five published pieces, check them against the fact sheet, and note the errors found. If the same error type repeats, the prompt or the tool needs to change. Automation tools that schedule posts or emails add speed but not accuracy. ## What does a two-week trial look like?

Two weeks is enough to test two or three tools on the same tasks. The aim is a fair comparison, not a long trial.

**Week 1: set up and draft**

- Day 1: write the fact sheet and a short brand voice note with three real captions or emails the business likes.
- Day 1: pick two or three tools from the table, preferring free tiers, and note each tool's limit (characters, words or credits).
- Days 2 to 3: time how long five current tasks take by hand, such as 3 captions, 1 email and 1 description.
- Days 4 to 5: run the same five tasks through each tool with the same prompt.
- Day 5: note edits needed per draft and whether the voice note was followed.

**Week 2: publish and measure**

- Days 8 to 10: edit the best drafts, run the checklist above, and publish or schedule them.
- Days 8 to 12: log minutes spent per piece, including review.
- Day 11: record when any free allowance ran out or a feature sat behind a paid plan.
- Day 12: check the vendor pricing page again and note per-seat, annual and monthly prices.
- Day 14: compare time saved against the plan price using the formula in the next section, then keep one tool, keep two, or stop.

## Is a subscription worth it for a coffee shop? A worked example

All inputs below are assumptions for illustration, not benchmarks. A coffee shop owner posts 8 captions per week, sends 1 email per week and updates 4 menu or promo descriptions a month.

- Pieces per month: 32 captions, 4 emails and 4 descriptions, so 40 pieces (assumption).
- Time per piece: 20 minutes by hand, 10 minutes with a draft plus review (assumption), so 10 minutes saved.
- Time saved: 40 pieces × 10 minutes = 400 minutes, about 6.7 hours.
- Value of the owner's time: $25 per hour (assumption), or about $0.42 per minute. That makes 400 minutes worth $166.67.
- Plan prices come from the table: Rytr Unlimited $7.50, Copy.ai Chat $29, Jasper Pro $69.

| Plan | Monthly cost | Break-even minutes saved | Net at 400 min saved | Net at 120 min saved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rytr Unlimited (annual) | $7.50 | 18 | $159.17 | $42.50 |
| Copy.ai Chat | $29.00 | 70 | $137.67 | $21.00 |
| Jasper Pro | $69.00 | 166 | $97.67 | -$19.00 |

The 120-minute column assumes only 3 minutes saved per piece (assumption), to show a pessimistic case. At that level the $69 plan loses money on time alone.

A free-tier check also applies. If captions average 300 characters, emails 1,000 and descriptions 500 (assumptions), monthly output is 15,600 characters. That exceeds Rytr's free 10,000 characters before any redrafts.

The example ignores revenue effects and extra review time beyond the 10 minutes assumed. Local campaigns that turn those posts into visits are a separate question, and the [marketing funnel guide](/blog/marketing-funnel-for-local-business) is the next step.

## FAQ

### Is a free AI writing tool enough for a small business?

It can be for light use. Rytr's free plan gives 10,000 characters per month, Simplified gives 5,000 words once, eesel gives 100 credits that do not refill, and Copy.ai's free Chat has no stated word limit at sign-up. The coffee shop example above, with assumed volumes, exceeds the Rytr free allowance.

### Do the cheaper tools offer brand voice controls?

Some do. Rytr's free plan includes 20+ preset tones, and custom tone training requires its Unlimited plan at $7.50 per month billed annually. Jasper Pro includes 2 Brand Voices. Other tools in the table do not have brand voice limits stated in the cited pages.

### Why is Copy.ai listed at both $29 and $1,000 per month?

They are different plans. Chat is listed at $29 per month ($24 annual) with 5 seats, while Growth is $1,000 per month, billed $12,000 per year, with 75 seats and 20K workflow credits per month. A single shop would look at Chat.

### How often should tool prices be rechecked?

Before each purchase and at renewal. Sources disagree on several prices, and this article does not document when any vendor changes its rates. Ask for current rates and confirm what annual billing locks in.
