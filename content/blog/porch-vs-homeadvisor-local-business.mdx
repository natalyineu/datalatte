Porch sells homeowner project requests to contractors, and you pay per lead. Porch publishes no lead prices on its pro pages, so the price is only visible inside an account.

HomeAdvisor works the same way: the price varies by trade and area, and you pay for each lead whether or not you win the job. The real difference is the credit rules for bad leads, which are spelled out in writing on Porch's side.

## How do Porch contractor leads work?

According to Porch's own pro page, a professional gets a profile with credentials, reviews and projects, then sets where they will travel and which project types they want. Leads can arrive by email and text, and you can also opt in to direct phone call leads ([Porch Pro](https://pro.porch.com/pro), read Nov 1, 2026).

Porch's page on pro reviews describes an "extended network" of professionals who receive leads from homeowners visiting Porch.com and partner sites, choosing their skills and service area ([Porch, reviewed by pros](https://pro.porch.com/reviewed-by-pros)). That page is Porch's own marketing, so treat its praise as the company speaking about itself.

## How much does Porch charge per lead?

Porch does not publish a price list on the pages we could open. The pro page says only that you can buy leads individually, set a monthly budget and receive leads automatically, or combine both.

Any dollar range you see for Porch leads comes from review sites and contractor forums, not from Porch. No current primary source gives a number, so this article does not give one. The reliable way to learn the price is to open an account and read the price shown on each lead for your trade and zip code, before you load money into it.

## What do Porch lead credits cover?

Porch's lead credit policy is the most concrete contract-style document on either platform. A credit adds the lead cost back to your Porch balance. Terms of Use say Porch does not refund to your credit card ([Porch lead credit policy](https://pro.porch.com/pro/lead-credit-policy)).

Credit-eligible, with proof of outreach: disconnected or wrong phone number, solicitor, duplicate within 30 days, wrong service type, outside your service area, customer says they never asked, stale lead, customer cannot authorize the work. Automatic credits: customer cancels within 24 hours, or the homeowner answers no pro within seven days.

## What does Porch not credit?

The policy lists the cases that are yours to carry: you did not win the job, the lead was too far away for the job size, the customer found someone else, changed their mind, or was only shopping for prices. After 30 days, no credit is granted.

For unresponsive homeowners the lead must stay open seven days, and you must message and call at least two times each during the first three days. Quick Reply messages do not count. For phone leads, bad contact information, unresponsive customers and cancellations do not qualify for credit.

## What does Vetted Pro add?

Per the Porch pro page, a Vetted Pro gets a profile badge, 5% back on leads bought on demand, and a Connection Guarantee: automatic lead credits if you meet the outreach requirements and still do not connect with the homeowner. Porch counts a connection when the homeowner messages or calls you, or when you call and the call lasts over 60 seconds ([lead credit policy](https://pro.porch.com/pro/lead-credit-policy)).

The cost of Vetted status is not on the pages we opened. One third-party guide mentions an annual fee, but we could not confirm it with Porch, so check it before you apply.

## How does HomeAdvisor charge contractors?

HomeAdvisor says what you pay for a lead varies by the type of work and by region, and that the current price is reviewed when you join. You set a spend target, an estimated amount for a 28-day period. It is not a hard cap, some lead types are charged outside it, and you can exceed it ([HomeAdvisor, How it works](https://www.homeadvisor.com/spa/how-it-works)).

The same page says HomeAdvisor does not provide or guarantee jobs, and you are charged for each lead whether or not you win the job. HomeAdvisor's lead business now runs under the Angi Leads name, covered in our [Angi Ads vs HomeAdvisor comparison](/blog/angi-ads-vs-homeadvisor-contractors).

## What did the FTC find about HomeAdvisor leads?

In March 2022 the FTC alleged that HomeAdvisor made false or unsubstantiated claims about lead quality and source, including leads that did not match a provider's services or area. The FTC's January 2023 release said providers generally paid an annual membership fee of $287.99 plus a fee per lead ([FTC, Jan 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-order-requires-homeadvisor-pay-72-million-stop-deceptively-marketing-its-leads-home-improvement)).

The FTC finalized the consent order on April 21, 2023, with redress of up to $7.2 million ([FTC, Apr 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/04/ftc-approves-final-order-against-homeadvisor-inc-deceptively-marketing-its-leads-home-improvement)). The fee is historical and may have changed. The order concerns HomeAdvisor only; it says nothing about Porch.

## What does a lead cost per signed job look like?

Both platforms sell leads, so compare them on cost per signed job. The numbers below are an **assumption** for illustration, not Porch or HomeAdvisor prices.

Assumption: you spend $500 in a month on one platform. Lead price averages $50, so you get 10 leads. Two are credit-eligible and credited, leaving 8 paid leads, or $400 net. You reach 5 homeowners, quote 3 and win 1. Cost per signed job is $400, and the job must be worth well above that after materials and labor.

Change the inputs to your own: if lead price is $80 and you win 2 of 8, the same $640 buys a job at $320 each. Win rate and credits move the result more than the sticker price.

## Which one should a contractor test first?

Start with the platform whose credit rules you can read before paying. Porch's policy is public, so you can check whether your lead problems would qualify. For HomeAdvisor, ask for the lead credit terms in writing when you call to join.

Set a small cap, log every lead, and track calls, quotes and signed jobs. Answer fast, because Porch's automatic credit depends on documented calls and messages. ## FAQ

### Does Porch publish its lead prices for contractors?

No. The Porch pro pages we opened show how you can pay (individually, a monthly budget, or both) but no dollar figures. Prices appear inside the account.

### Can I get a refund for a bad Porch lead?

You can request a credit, not a refund. Credits go back to your Porch balance, and Porch does not refund to your credit card. Requests need proof that you reached out, and none is granted after 30 days.

### Do I pay for HomeAdvisor leads I do not win?

Yes. HomeAdvisor states that it does not guarantee jobs and that you are charged for each lead whether or not you win the job.

### Is there a contract or minimum term?

We could not open a Porch or HomeAdvisor contract. Porch's reviews page says canceling is easy, which is a claim by Porch itself. Ask both for their Terms of Use and cancellation steps in writing before you fund an account.

### Are Porch leads exclusive?

Porch's pro pages make no statement about exclusivity. The credit policy covers duplicates within 30 days, which suggests you should plan for homeowners contacting several pros.

### Which is cheaper, Porch or HomeAdvisor?

We cannot say, because neither company publishes lead prices. Compare the net cost per signed job over the same period.
